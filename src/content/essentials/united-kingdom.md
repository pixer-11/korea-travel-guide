**Quick answer:** Most visitors now need a paid UK Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) or a Standard Visitor visa before flying to the UK, London transport is easiest with a contactless bank card or phone, and 999 (or 112) is the number to know in any emergency.

## Visa & entry

Nationals of more than 85 visa-exempt countries, including the United States, Canada, Australia and all EU member states, need an ETA to visit the UK, and this fee change affects 85+ visa-exempt countries including the United States, Canada, Australia, and EU nations. The scheme has applied to these travellers since 25 February 2026, and if you need one and arrive without it, your airline is likely to refuse boarding.

The ETA costs £20 per person: the UK raised the fee for its Electronic Travel Authorisation to £20 from £16 on April 8, 2026, a 25% increase. Once approved, it is valid for two years, or until your passport expires, whichever comes first, and allows unlimited trips to the UK during that period, with each stay lasting up to six months. An ETA is permission to travel, not a guarantee of entry; Border Force still decides admission on arrival, and there is no right of appeal if it is refused, though you may reapply or apply for a visa instead. Apply only through the official GOV.UK site or the official UK ETA app, using the passport you intend to travel on.

If your nationality is on the UK's visa-required list (this includes India, Pakistan, and many countries across Asia, Africa and the Middle East), an ETA will not work for you and you must apply for a Standard Visitor visa instead. This can be applied for up to three months before travel, and the fee is £135, raised from £127 on 8 April 2026, for stays of up to six months.

British and Irish citizens, and eligible Ireland residents arriving from within the Common Travel Area, do not need an ETA. Almost everyone else does, including infants and children.

**Rules change often, so before booking or travelling, always confirm your exact requirement (ETA, visa, or exempt) on the official GOV.UK visa checker, not on third-party sites.**

## Getting around

London's public transport runs on tap-and-go payment: use an Oyster card, a Visitor Oyster card, or simply a contactless bank card or phone wallet, touching in (and out, except on buses and trams) on the yellow readers across the Tube, DLR, Elizabeth line, Overground and most National Rail services. For most visitors a foreign contactless card or phone is simpler than buying a physical card, since fares and daily/weekly caps are identical and the system automatically applies the cheapest combination. Each traveller needs their own card or device, as each is capped separately. If your card charges fees on every tap abroad, check with your bank first, since a physical Oyster card can still make sense in that case.

Fares rose modestly in March 2026 while caps were frozen: single contactless and Oyster fares on the Tube and TfL rail rose by around 6% from 1 March 2026, though the daily and weekly caps themselves were frozen. As a guide, a Zone 1 peak single is £3.10 and off-peak is £3.00, bus and tram journeys cost £1.75, with a £5.25 daily cap, and the Zones 1-2 daily cap is £8.90, the same at peak and off-peak times.

Outside London, intercity travel is mainly by National Rail trains, long-distance coaches (National Express, Megabus) and domestic flights for Scotland, Northern Ireland and longer hops. Booking rail tickets ahead and travelling off-peak brings the biggest savings. Driving is on the left; city-centre driving is often discouraged by congestion charges and low-emission zones, so trains, buses and rideshare or taxi apps are generally easiest for visitors.

## Money & costs

The currency is the pound sterling (£, GBP). Contactless debit/credit cards and mobile wallets are accepted almost everywhere, from shops and restaurants to transport and many market stalls, so carrying large amounts of cash is rarely necessary, though a small amount is wise for emergencies or cash-only vendors.

Costs vary hugely by city and season. Accommodation, food and entertainment are cheapest outside London and during quieter months, while London itself runs notably higher for hotels, dining and attractions. Many major museums and galleries remain free to enter, which helps offset higher accommodation and dining costs elsewhere. Booking ahead pays off: London hotel rooms are typically far cheaper in November than in July, and reserving accommodation and transport a few months out often saves significantly compared with last-minute bookings. Tipping is optional and modest, rounding up or adding around 10% in restaurants where service isn't already included, but it is not expected in pubs, cafés or taxis in the way it is elsewhere.

## Best time to visit

Late spring (May) and early autumn (September) are generally the best times to visit, when mild weather, long daylight hours and lighter crowds suit city wandering, countryside rambles and coastal exploring. Summer (June to August) brings warmth, festivals and long evenings, from Glastonbury and Wimbledon to the Edinburgh Fringe and Notting Hill Carnival, but also peak prices and crowded attractions. Winter (December to February) is colder and darker, but good for cosy pubs, Christmas markets and quieter sightseeing. Whatever the season, pack for rain and layers, since British weather is famously changeable year-round.

## Emergencies & safety

For any life-threatening situation (police, ambulance, fire or coastguard), dial 999, the UK's emergency number; calls to the EU's 112 are also answered by the same operators and work identically, with no priority between the two numbers, and both are free to call.

For non-life-threatening issues, use the separate non-emergency lines: 101 for police, 111 (NHS 111) for urgent medical advice, 105 for power cuts, and 0800 111 999 to report a suspected gas leak. Emergency operators can access interpreter services if English isn't your first language. If you can only text, you can use the emergencySMS service, but you must register your phone in advance by texting "register" to 999.

The UK is generally safe for tourists; take ordinary precautions around busy transport hubs and tourist sites, keep your ETA/visa confirmation and travel insurance accessible, and note your embassy or consulate's location in case you need consular help.
