**Quick answer:** UK, Japanese and South Korean passport holders, plus citizens of a specific list of European countries (not every EU state), can enter Vietnam visa-free for up to 45 days, while US, Canadian and Australian citizens need an e-visa valid for up to 90 days (US$25 single entry, US$50 multiple entry) applied for online in advance; always double-check your specific nationality's rules on the official government portals before booking flights.

## Visa & entry

Vietnam's entry rules depend heavily on your passport, so treat the summary below as a starting point, not a guarantee.

Vietnam grants visa exemptions through bilateral agreements with individual countries and unilateral decisions made by the Vietnamese government, and this reflects the current policy as of 2026. The 45-day exemption covers a specific list of countries rather than the whole EU. Specifically, citizens of Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the UK, Russia, Japan, South Korea, Denmark, Sweden, Norway and Finland enjoy a 45-day exemption (Resolution 44/NQ-CP, valid to 14 March 2028), and from 15 August 2025 to 14 August 2028 Vietnam extended the same 45 days to Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Hungary, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia and Switzerland (Resolution 229/NQ-CP). Other EU states, such as Austria, Ireland, Portugal and Greece, are not covered and need an e-visa. Regional neighbours have shorter windows: Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Laos and Cambodia get 30 days visa-free, the Philippines gets 21 days, and Brunei and Myanmar get 14 days. By contrast, Australia, Canada, and the United States have no exemption, so travellers from those countries must apply for an e-visa for every trip. China and India are not exempt either; travelers from both countries, as well as from Taiwan and Hong Kong, need an e-visa for any visit.

For anyone who doesn't qualify for visa-free entry, or who wants to stay longer, the e-visa program remains the most practical option for most nationalities in 2026, since citizens of all countries and territories have been able to apply online since 15 August 2023 for an e-visa valid for up to 90 days, costing 25 USD for single entry or 50 USD for multiple entry. Applications are processed online and typically take 3-5 working days. Whichever route you take, your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from your date of entry into Vietnam, so check the expiry date and renew it if needed. Note too that British National Overseas (BNO) passport holders are not eligible for the UK exemption and need an e-visa.

A newer wrinkle for 2026: travellers arriving by air should be aware of Vietnam's Pre-Arrival Information Declaration, introduced during 2026, which encourages foreign travellers at major airports to complete a declaration that can be filed within three days before arrival or upon arrival.

**Confirm before you fly:** visa policy in Vietnam changes with little notice, and exemption lists have been expanded more than once in recent years. Check your exact nationality on the official Immigration Department and e-visa portals listed below before booking non-refundable flights.

## Getting around

Vietnam's cities are dense, motorbike-heavy and increasingly served by app-based transport. Vietnam offers five primary public transport categories: metro systems, city buses, ride-hailing apps (Grab, Be, Xanh SM), intercity trains, and metered taxis. Ride-hailing is the easiest option for most visitors: ride-hailing apps dominate urban transport with Grab holding a large market share, Be around a third, and Xanh SM close behind. Grab requires a local SIM or a foreign number, so it helps to register before arriving if possible.

Both major cities now have limited metro service. Metro systems operate in Hanoi (Line 2A with 12 stations) and Ho Chi Minh City (Line 1 with 14 stations). Ho Chi Minh City's Line 1 opened on 22 December 2024 from Bến Thành Market to New Eastern Bus Terminus, and coverage is expanding but still doesn't reach most tourist areas. City buses remain the cheapest option; the free BusMap app provides live bus locations, route maps, and arrival times in English for Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Da Nang, Binh Duong, and Can Tho, and has exceeded 44 million downloads.

For airport transfers, typical costs are: from Hanoi's Noi Bai airport, Grab to the Old Quarter runs about 45 minutes for USD 10-15, while Grab from Tan Son Nhat to District 1 in Ho Chi Minh City takes 20-40 minutes depending on traffic and costs USD 4-8.

For long-distance travel, the 1,726-kilometer Reunification Express train connects Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City, and long-distance travel between the two cities offers three primary options: domestic flights (2 hours), sleeper trains (30-34 hours), and sleeper buses (35-40 hours). Renting a motorbike is popular for exploring; semi-automatic bikes cost USD 5-10/day, automatics USD 8-15/day, and full manuals USD 7-12/day, with an international driving permit and basic riding experience strongly recommended.

## Money & costs

The local currency is the Vietnamese Dong (VND). Cash is still king for street food, markets, motorbike taxis and small guesthouses, while cards are increasingly accepted at hotels, malls and city restaurants. ATMs are widespread in cities and tourist towns, though most Vietnamese banks charge a withdrawal fee and cap single withdrawals, so plan for a few transactions rather than one large one. Carrying a mix of dong and a backup international card is the safest approach; note also that some payment gateways for online services like the e-visa run through local processors, so payment can be made with credit/debit cards via a local payment processor.

Prices swing sharply with the season and around major holidays. During peak weeks, a mid-range four-star hotel room in Hoi An that runs around $70 in the off-season can climb to roughly $140 during late December, and Ha Long Bay overnight cruises follow the same pattern, from about $125 per person in shoulder season to around $215 at peak. Domestic flights and hotel rates also spike sharply around Tet (Lunar New Year), when domestic flights double and many family-run businesses close for about a week.

## Best time to visit

Vietnam's long, narrow shape means there is no single "best" month everywhere at once. Vietnam's climate varies significantly across its three main regions, the north, central, and south, with the general dry-season windows being south Nov-Feb, central Feb-early May, and north late Sep-Nov and Mar-Apr. Broadly, February to April and September to November offer the best balance of weather across most regions, and March comes closest to a month when the whole country has good weather, with late February and early April also working for most travellers, though the south heats up sharply by mid-April.

Be aware of the wet season: most of the country has one rainy season from May to September when humidity is at its highest and conditions can be sultry and oppressive. Central Vietnam has its own pattern, with a typhoon season from September to November that adds unpredictability and frequently causes flight delays and cancellations. Around Lunar New Year (Tet, typically late January or February), expect higher prices, fully booked transport and many small businesses closed for family celebrations, as noted above.

## Emergencies & safety

Vietnam does not use a single 911-style number. Emergency contacts are 113 for police, 114 for fire, 115 for medical emergencies, and 112 for search and rescue, and these hotlines are available 24/7 nationwide. However, these official services function exclusively in Vietnamese, according to UK Foreign Office travel advice, so having a Vietnamese-speaking friend, hotel staff member, or translation app on hand is invaluable in a real emergency. In major cities, hospitals with English-speaking staff exist, but for minor issues taking a taxi or ride-hailing car directly to a reputable hospital can be faster than waiting for an ambulance.

Note that 112 connects callers to the 113, 114, and 115 services rather than replacing them, and as of late 2026 all three numbers still function, so keep all three saved. Standard travel-safety precautions apply: watch for pickpocketing and bag-snatching in tourist areas, use registered taxis or ride-hailing apps at night, take extra care crossing streets and riding motorbikes given heavy traffic, and invest in travel insurance that covers medical expenses, evacuation, theft, and other emergencies. Register your trip with your embassy and save its 24-hour emergency line before you travel.
