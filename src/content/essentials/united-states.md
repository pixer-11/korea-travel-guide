**Quick answer:** Most leisure travelers from Visa Waiver Program countries can still enter the US for up to 90 days using an ESTA instead of a visa, get around by rideshare, subway, or Amtrak in and between major cities, pay mostly by contactless card in US dollars, and dial 911 for any emergency; always verify entry rules on the official CBP/State Department sites before you fly, since fees and country lists change often.

## Visa & entry

The Visa Waiver Program (VWP), run by the Department of Homeland Security together with the State Department, still lets citizens of participating countries visit the US for business or tourism without a consular visa, using an online ESTA (Electronic System for Travel Authorization) instead.

Key 2026 details:

- The official CBP/ESTA country list currently names Andorra, Australia, Austria, Belgium, Brunei, Chile, Croatia, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Israel, Italy, Japan, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Republic of Malta, Monaco, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Qatar, San Marino, Singapore, Slovakia, Slovenia, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Taiwan, and the United Kingdom, 42 countries in total. Romania is not currently listed on CBP's own ESTA page, even though some third‑party sites claim it rejoined in 2026, so check the live CBP list yourself rather than a secondhand one.
- An approved ESTA grants a max stay of 90 days per visit, with validity of 2 years or until your passport expires.
- The ESTA fee has risen in stages: $40 starting September 30, 2025; $40.27 since January 1, 2026; $40.62 starting October 16, 2026, with the total made up of three components, $17 + $10.62 + $13 = $40.62.
- CBP and most guides still recommend applying as soon as you book, and in any case at least 72 hours before departure.
- Losing ESTA eligibility: travelers who have visited certain high-risk countries can be disqualified. People who have traveled to Iran, Iraq, Libya, North Korea, Somalia, Sudan, Syria, or Yemen during or after March 1, 2011, or who were in Cuba on or after January 12, 2021, or dual nationals of North Korea, Iran, Iraq, Sudan, or Syria are no longer eligible for an ESTA and must apply for a regular B‑1/B‑2 visitor visa instead.
- Land-border travelers pay separately: the total fee to apply for a CBP Form I‑94 at a land border port of entry for FY 2026 continues to be $30, consisting of the $6 land border fee and the $24 HR‑1 fee; air and sea arrivals don't pay this.
- Non‑VWP citizens need a traditional B‑1/B‑2 visitor visa, which carries the standard $185 application fee, plus a newly legislated $250 Visa Integrity Fee per visa issuance, from which Visa Waiver Program/ESTA travelers are exempt; as of late October 2026 some trackers reported this fee was legislated but not yet showing as a collected line item on the published State Department fee schedule, so confirm current status with your consulate.

**Bottom line:** confirm your country's eligibility, passport requirements and the exact fee on the official ESTA portal and CBP site before booking, since these details are revised through the year.

## Getting around

The US is huge and car-centric outside big cities, but visitors relying on public transport have solid options:

- **Cities:** New York, Washington DC, Chicago, Boston and San Francisco have subway/metro systems plus buses. New York's old paper MetroCard is gone: MetroCard is retiring, and as of January 1, 2026, you can no longer buy or refill one. Instead, tap a contactless bank card, phone, or an OMNY card at the turnstile; you only pay for the subway and local bus rides you take up to a cap of $35 in a 7-day period, as long as you use the same device or card. Other cities use their own contactless or local transit cards, and some buses still want exact change.
- **Intercity trains:** Amtrak is the national rail operator. The headline multi-city product is the USA Rail Pass: 10 segments of travel in Coach within a 30-day window for $499, with no blackout dates and access to most of the Amtrak network, and the pass itself must be used within 120 days following purchase, with 30 days to complete travel once you start. Amtrak periodically runs flash sales that cut the price; one dropped it to $250 for a short window in January 2026. On the Northeast Corridor, premium Acela service is pricier than the regular Northeast Regional trains, so compare fares before booking.
- **Long-distance buses** (Greyhound and similar) remain a budget alternative to trains on many routes.
- **Rideshare apps** (Uber, Lyft) are widely available in cities and suburbs; taxis remain common in a few dense metros like New York.
- **Rental cars** are practical, often essential, for national parks, small towns and road trips; an International Driving Permit alongside your home license is recommended.
- Navigation apps like Transit or Citymapper help when navigating unfamiliar US transit systems.

## Money & costs

The currency is the US dollar (USD). Contactless credit and debit cards are accepted almost everywhere in cities, but keep some cash handy, since food trucks, small stores and rural businesses may still prefer it.

Tipping remains a core part of US service culture: 15–20% is standard at sit-down restaurants on the pre-tax bill, $1–2 per drink at a bar, a few dollars per day for hotel housekeeping, and roughly 15–20% for taxis or rideshares.

Sales tax is added at checkout and varies by state and city, from 0% in some states to over 10% in others, so sticker prices are never final. Daily budgets vary widely: budget travelers can often manage on $50–$100 per day using hostels and public transit, while mid-range visitors typically spend $150–$250 daily. Use a card with no foreign transaction fees where possible, and buy travel insurance, since medical costs in the US can be very high and even minor injuries may lead to large bills.

## Best time to visit

There's no single "best" month for a country this large and climatically diverse, but shoulder seasons, roughly late March to late May and mid‑September to late October, generally offer the best balance of mild weather, lower prices and fewer crowds than peak summer, along with spring blooms or autumn colour depending on the region.

Summer (June–August) is peak tourist season, with warm-to-hot weather nearly everywhere, ideal conditions for beaches, national parks and city sightseeing, but also the highest prices and biggest crowds at major attractions. Winter can be a bargain outside the north: northern regions suit skiing and holiday markets, while southern destinations like Florida, Arizona and California offer mild, sunny weather even as northern states turn cold. If visiting the Southeast or Gulf Coast, note that Atlantic hurricane season runs from June through November.

## Emergencies & safety

Dial **911** from any phone for police, fire or medical emergencies; it is the standard nationwide number in the US. Basic safety tips for visitors:

- Watch for pickpockets in crowded tourist areas and keep bags zipped; on the road, obey posted speed limits, wear seat belts, and never drive under the influence.
- On transit, use standard urban-safety habits: keep your phone out of sight on crowded cars, and prefer cars with more riders late at night.
- Healthcare is excellent but expensive, so travel insurance covering medical treatment and medical evacuation is strongly advised.
- Mobile coverage is generally reliable in cities and towns but can be patchy in remote national parks and mountainous areas, so download offline maps in advance.
