**Quick answer:** Since 15 September 2026, nationals of roughly 60 countries and territories (including the US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia and New Zealand) get a 30-day visa-free tourist entry, not the old 60 days, and everyone must file a free Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) online before landing; always verify your specific passport's rule on the official immigration site before you fly.

## Visa & entry

Thailand overhauled its entry system in 2026. Thailand's visa exemption rules took effect on 15 September 2026, with final Ministry of Interior notices published in the Royal Gazette on 31 August 2026, replacing the special 60-day exemption introduced in 2024. The 30-day group includes all 27 EU Member States, as well as Australia, Canada, India, Japan, New Zealand, Switzerland, the United Kingdom, the United States and other countries and territories listed in the final Royal Gazette notice. A separate 15-day exemption applies to a couple of nationalities: the 15-day visa exemption covers Mauritius and Seychelles. A few nationalities that previously used visa-free entry now need a Visa on Arrival instead: current guidance lists Visa on Arrival as now limited to Azerbaijan, Belarus and Serbia, for a stay of up to 15 days.

Important restrictions to know before you plan: they should not be treated as permission to work in Thailand or as a substitute for an appropriate business, employment or long-stay visa. If you plan to enter and exit repeatedly overland, note that for travellers using the new 30-day exemption, entry through a land-border checkpoint or a port adjoining a land border is generally limited to two times per calendar year, with an exemption for citizens of Brunei, Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore. A visa-exempt stay can usually be extended once inside the country: travelers eligible for the 30-day exemption may request a one-time extension of up to an additional 30 days at a local immigration office, subject to approval and the standard ฿1,900 extension fee.

Regardless of visa status, every traveler now needs an arrival declaration. The Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) is an arrival card system required for all foreign nationals entering Thailand by land, air, and sea since 1 May 2025. Everyone files the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card within three days of arrival, and it is single-use: every entry into Thailand, including a border run and back, needs a new card. A TDAC does not admit you to Thailand; you still need to be on the 30-day exemption list, hold an e-Visa, or be one of the three Visa on Arrival nationalities. Submit it only at the official portal, tdac.immigration.go.th, since Thai authorities have warned travelers about fake Thailand Digital Arrival Card websites.

Because these rules changed abruptly in 2026 and could change again, treat the above as a starting point only. Confirm your exact allowed stay, required documents, and any new fees on the official Thai Immigration Bureau and e-Visa websites before booking flights.

## Getting around

Bangkok's elevated BTS Skytrain and underground MRT remain the easiest way to dodge the city's notorious traffic. Payment differs by line, which trips up many first-timers. Since June 1, 2026, MRT's Blue and Purple lines fully accept contactless payments: any Visa, Mastercard, or UnionPay card with a contactless chip works directly at the turnstile, and the same now applies to the Airport Rail Link, SRT Red Line, and the Pink and Yellow monorails. The BTS is the exception: contactless Visa and Mastercard payments work on the MRT Blue, Purple, Yellow, and Pink Lines and the Airport Rail Link, but the BTS Skytrain (Green and Gold Lines) still requires a Rabbit Card or a single-journey ticket, with a unified system expected to join the contactless network as part of a rollout targeted for 2027.

A Rabbit Card is easy for foreigners to get but is not free: it costs ฿200 at any BTS ticket booth, of which ฿100 is a non-refundable issuing fee and ฿100 is travel credit, and you show your passport because the card is registered to you. The stored value expires two years after the last transaction, and the card can hold up to 4,000 baht at a time. Fares are modest: BTS single tickets run roughly 17 to 65 baht per adult journey, while MRT Blue and Purple fares depend on the stations you pick.

From Suvarnabhumi Airport, the Airport Rail Link remains the quickest low-cost route into the center, with its own ticketing separate from Rabbit or MRT cards; you can now tap a contactless bank card directly at ARL gates, or buy a dedicated token from the vending machines. Don Mueang Airport has no rail connection, so from DMK your options are a metered taxi plus expressway toll, Grab, or a public bus into the city. Outside Bangkok, buses, domestic flights, overnight trains, and ride-hailing apps such as Grab cover most journeys between cities, beaches, and northern hill towns.

## Luggage storage

At Suvarnabhumi Airport, Bellugg operates a staffed luggage storage service with counters on Level 2 and Level 4. Reservations should be made at least an hour in advance. Bags are charged by size and by 24 hour period: smaller pieces, up to 22 inches, cost 100 baht per piece per day; medium pieces, between 22 and 26 inches, cost 120 baht per piece per day; and larger pieces, over 26 inches, cost 150 baht per piece per day. If storage is needed beyond the booked period, travellers are asked to contact the service counter directly to arrange an extension.

Beyond the airport, travellers in Thailand have other options for storing bags for a few hours or overnight, including lockers at transit stations and shopping centres, staffed counters run by private companies, and hotels, which will often hold luggage at reception both after checkout and before check-in. Anyone relying on these alternatives should confirm opening hours, accepted payment methods and any size limits directly with the provider, since terms vary by location and are not standardised across the city.

## Money & costs

The currency is the Thai baht (THB). Thailand still runs largely on cash outside malls and hotels, with Visa and Mastercard widely accepted in tourist areas but cash still needed for markets, street food, and transport.

ATMs are everywhere, but foreign cards pay a flat surcharge. Almost every Thai bank charges ฿220 per withdrawal on a foreign card, on top of whatever your own bank charges for overseas ATM use and currency conversion, with some machines or card types showing 250 THB or more. Because the fee is flat, it's a flat fee, so the maths is simple: take the maximum each time. If you want the lowest local fee, AEON Bank has the lowest fee in the country at 150 Baht. At any machine, always choose to be charged in baht rather than accepting "dynamic currency conversion," since that lets the ATM apply its own exchange rate, which is a bad deal.

Thailand's local QR system, PromptPay, is everywhere but generally needs a Thai bank account, so as a visitor you'll typically pay cash or card instead. A reasonable daily cash budget for markets, transport, and casual meals is roughly 1,000 to 2,000 baht, topped up with card payments for hotels and larger restaurants.

## Best time to visit

Thailand's climate splits into three broad seasons. The cool, dry season from around November to February brings the lowest humidity and rainfall, making it the most popular and comfortable window for both Bangkok and the beaches. March through May turns hot and increasingly humid, with April typically the hottest month and the Songkran (Thai New Year) water festival in mid-April. The southwest monsoon runs roughly June to October, bringing afternoon downpours, especially on the Andaman coast, while the Gulf coast islands (like Koh Samui) follow a slightly different rain pattern and can stay drier into autumn. For most first-time visitors chasing sunshine, November to February remains the safest bet, though it is also peak season for prices and crowds.

## Emergencies & safety

Thailand does not have one single number for every emergency, so it is worth saving several before you travel. Call 191 for police, 1669 for an ambulance, 199 for fire and 1155 for the English-speaking Tourist Police. The Tourism Authority of Thailand's non-emergency info line is 1672.

The Tourist Police exist specifically to help foreign visitors: the Tourist Police have English speakers, can come to hotels and tourist areas, and can act as a go-between with a shop, taxi driver or jet ski operator. Many visitors also use the dedicated app, since you can contact the tourist police through the Thailand Tourist Police App, which is useful for tourists without a Thai SIM card. If you need an official report for an insurance claim, remember theft should also be reported to a local police station, though Tourist Police can help with that process. Emergency response is generally best in Bangkok and major tourist areas like Phuket, Chiang Mai, and Pattaya, while response in rural and island areas may be slower, so consider travel insurance with medical evacuation cover if you plan to venture off the main tourist trail.
