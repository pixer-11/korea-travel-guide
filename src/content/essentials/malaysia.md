**Quick answer:** Most visitors from the US, UK, EU, Australia and many other countries can enter Malaysia visa-free for up to 90 days, pay in Malaysian Ringgit (RM) with widely accepted cards plus a Touch 'n Go card for transit, and dial 999 for any emergency; always verify visa specifics on Malaysia's official immigration site before you fly.

## Visa & entry

Malaysia's entry rules depend heavily on your passport, so treat the summary below as a starting point only. Malaysia allows citizens of many countries to enter without a visa for periods of 90 days, 30 days, or 14 days depending on nationality, and holders of an APEC Business Travel Card with the "MYS" code can enter visa-free for business for up to 60 days. Chinese and Indian travellers, who previously used dedicated eVisa or electronic registration (eNTRI) schemes, can now enter visa-free for up to 30 days: Indian citizens under a visa-free arrangement running until 31 December 2026, and Chinese citizens under a mutual visa exemption in force since 17 July 2025. Rules differ by passport type, so check the current requirement for yours rather than assuming a blanket rule.

A few practical points apply almost universally: most countries require at least six months of passport validity beyond your return date, so renew early if yours is close to expiring, and immigration officers may ask for proof of an onward or return ticket even for visa-free entry, so have one ready or carry a flexible booking. Crucially, visa-free status does not guarantee admission; border officials can refuse entry if you do not meet documentation requirements or if rules have changed. Because visa policy and eVisa/ETA arrangements are revised periodically, confirm your specific requirements on the Malaysian Immigration Department's website and the official eVisa/eNTRI portal (both linked below) before booking, and again a few days before departure.

## Getting around

Kuala Lumpur and the surrounding Klang Valley have an extensive urban rail network combining LRT, MRT, Monorail and KTM Komuter lines, spanning seven lines in 2026, with one Touch 'n Go card covering MRT, LRT, and buses. Since 2025, Prasarana completed a unified fare integration under the MyRapid system, meaning one card now works across all those modes without penalty transfers, though KTM remains a separate system that accepts the same Touch 'n Go card, just not at integrated fare rates.

The city has moved decisively toward cashless payment for local transit. Feeder buses to MRT and LRT stations now only accept fares via a Touch 'n Go card, and the GoKL CityBus service now costs RM1 per ride, payable by Touch 'n Go card or contactless credit/debit card, with cash not accepted. For trains, you can still buy single tickets at station machines, which accept cash and Visa or Mastercard, though UnionPay is not accepted. The simplest approach for visitors is to get a physical Touch 'n Go card, which you tap at rail gates and on buses; it works on MRT, LRT, Monorail and Rapid KL buses, and can be bought and reloaded at station counters or convenience stores like 7-Eleven. Note that the Touch 'n Go eWallet phone app is not what the rail gates read: gates take the physical card (or a single-journey token, or a contactless bank card where supported), so treat the app as a payment wallet rather than a transit ticket.

From Kuala Lumpur International Airport, KLIA Ekspres trains reach KL Sentral in 28 minutes for RM55 one way, while the cheaper Skybus shuttle costs RM14 one way but takes 60 to 90 minutes. Ride-hailing is popular and convenient: Grab charges roughly RM8 to RM25 for typical journeys and operates 24/7 with upfront pricing. Beyond the capital, domestic flights, long-distance coaches, and ferries (for island destinations like Langkawi and the Perhentians) are the main ways to cover longer distances; Sabah and Sarawak in Borneo rely more on flights and road/river transport given the terrain.

## Money & costs

The currency is the Malaysian Ringgit (MYR), symbolised as RM, with banknotes in RM1, RM5, RM10, RM20, RM50 and RM100 denominations, and coins in 5, 10, 20 and 50 sen. Most hotels, malls, restaurants and shops accept international credit cards such as Visa, MasterCard, American Express and Diners Club, and digital payment methods including Touch 'n Go, GrabPay and Boost are widely used by locals and tourists alike.

That said, do not rely on cards everywhere. Cities are increasingly cashless in malls, restaurants and hotels, but cash is still king at hawker centres, night markets, small shops, rural areas, and especially in East Malaysia on Borneo. ATMs are plentiful in cities, shopping malls, airports and petrol stations, with major networks including Maybank, CIMB, Public Bank, Hong Leong and RHB accepting international Visa, Mastercard and Cirrus/Plus cards, though coverage thins out in remote areas, so withdraw cash before heading off the beaten track.

For exchanging money, licensed money changers in shopping districts almost always offer better exchange rates than airport counters or hotel desks. One legal detail worth knowing: you may carry ringgit cash only up to the equivalent of USD 10,000 in or out of the country, and while foreign currency has no limit, any cash above the USD 10,000 equivalent must be declared, so convert leftover ringgit before you leave. Budget travellers can get by on modest daily transport costs, since getting around by rail and bus can cost roughly MYR 6 to 12 a day.

## Best time to visit

Malaysia is tropical and warm year round, but rainfall patterns differ sharply between the west and east coasts and Borneo, largely driven by the monsoon cycle. The most disruptive weather window is the north-east monsoon, the main flood season for the east coast and Borneo, which ran from about mid-November 2025 to late March 2026, so travellers heading to the Perhentian Islands, Redang, Tioman's eastern approaches, or interior Borneo are generally better off avoiding that stretch, when heavy rain, rough seas and some resort closures are common. The west coast, including Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi, is comparatively drier during this period and can be a good choice if you are visiting between December and February.

Outside the north-east monsoon window, roughly April through October, conditions are more favourable across most of the peninsula, though flash floods can hit the Klang Valley and other areas after heavy rain at almost any time, so it pays to check forecasts regardless of season. Haze from regional land-clearing fires can also affect air quality in some years, typically in the mid-year dry season, so if you are sensitive to air quality it is worth checking conditions shortly before you travel.

## Emergencies & safety

999 is Malaysia's unified national emergency number for police, ambulance and fire, reachable free from any landline or mobile phone; 112 also works from mobile phones. Every call through the 999 line is free, and operators generally speak both Malay and English. Response times vary by location: the system draws on nearly 2,000 Health Ministry ambulances plus support from St John Ambulance and the Malaysian Red Crescent, with typical response times of 15 to 30 minutes depending on location and demand, and can be faster in cities and slower in remote areas.

It is worth downloading the official emergency app before you need it: SaveME999 is Malaysia's official emergency response app that sends your GPS location with the call. Note that misuse carries real penalties: a false emergency call can be fined RM50,000 or result in imprisonment for one year, or both, under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998, so reserve 999 for genuine emergencies and use your local police station for minor, non-urgent matters. For non-emergency health questions or suspected disease outbreaks, Malaysia's Ministry of Health Crisis Preparedness and Response Centre can be reached at 03-8881 0200. Travel insurance is strongly recommended, since while Malaysia does not require travel insurance for entry, tourists must pay for medical treatment at the time of service.
