**Quick answer:** Most Western passport holders get 90 days visa-free in Malaysia but must still file the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online before flying; getting around is cheap via Touch 'n Go-enabled trains, buses, and Grab, the currency is the Ringgit (MYR), and the national emergency number is 999 (or 112 from any mobile). Always double-check entry rules on official sites before you travel, since visa policies change frequently.

## Visa & entry

Entry rules depend heavily on your nationality, so treat the summary below as a starting point, not the final word.

- **Visa-free stays:** Most Western countries (US, UK, Canada, EU, Australia, New Zealand) can visit Malaysia visa-free for up to 90 days for tourism or business. No advance application is needed for visa-free travelers — just present your passport and return ticket at immigration, making Malaysia extremely accessible for short-term visits. Some nationalities, including China and India, currently benefit from a separate 30-day visa-free facility for citizens of China and India, extended through 2026 by the Malaysian government.
- **e-Visa/visa-on-arrival:** For nationalities that require a visa, Malaysia offers convenient e-Visa applications online, allowing you to apply, pay, and receive your visa via email, typically within 24-48 hours.
- **Mandatory Digital Arrival Card (MDAC):** Regardless of visa status, the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) is an official electronic form required of most foreign travellers to Malaysia, administered by the Immigration Department to streamline border processing. It must be submitted online no more than three days before arrival by most foreign visitors, and exemptions include citizens of Singapore, Malaysian citizens and permanent residents, diplomats, and a few cross-border pass holders. Submit it only through the official government portal — the official MDAC at imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main is 100% free, and any website charging money for MDAC is a scam.
- **Passport & funds:** Carry a passport valid well beyond your stay and be ready to show proof of onward travel and sufficient funds if asked.

⚠️ **Always confirm the current visa-free duration and MDAC requirements for your specific nationality on the official Malaysian Immigration Department website before booking flights** — these policies are periodically revised.

## Getting around

Malaysia's public transport is inexpensive and improving, especially in the Klang Valley (Kuala Lumpur region).

- **Airport to city:** The fastest option is the KLIA Ekspres — 28 minutes to KLIA, 33 minutes to KLIA2, RM35 each way; buy tickets at the KLIA Ekspres counters inside KL Sentral. Alternatively, a Grab car is the door-to-door choice if you have heavy bags, a group, or you land late — follow the airport signs to the e-hailing pickup zone rather than accepting an unmetered offer from a tout. A Grab ride into central KL typically costs about RM60 to RM100, more during surge.
- **City rail network:** Rapid KL (operated by Prasarana) runs the LRT lines, MRT lines, KL Monorail, and Rapid KL bus, while KTM runs suburban commuter and intercity trains, and ERL runs the KLIA Ekspres/Transit — each company has its own fares, though all accept Touch 'n Go payment. KL Sentral is the main interchange hub where KTM Komuter, Kelana Jaya LRT, KL Monorail, KLIA Ekspres, and ERL all connect.
- **Tickets & passes:** The Rapid Kembara Pass is RM25 for one day and RM55 for three days, effective 1 January 2026, and it covers Rapid KL LRT, MRT, Monorail, BRT, buses and MRT feeder buses. A reusable Touch n Go card (about RM10) is the simplest option for casual riders, and open contactless payment (tapping a credit or debit card, QR or phone directly at the gate) is being introduced across Rapid KL rail and buses. Note that city buses (Rapid KL, Go KL) do not accept cash or cards — the only way to pay is the Touch 'n Go transport card.
- **Ride-hailing & taxis:** Grab is the default app-based option nationwide and generally more reliable than street taxis.
- **Beyond the city:** A foreign licence written in English lets you drive for up to 90 days, with all highway tolls cashless through Touch n Go, and intercity travel is easy by KTM ETS train, express bus from TBS, ferry to the islands, or a short domestic flight to Sabah and Sarawak.

## Money & costs

- **Currency:** Malaysia's official currency is the Malaysian Ringgit (MYR), commonly written as RM, with banknote denominations of 1, 5, 10, 20, 50, and 100.
- **Cards vs. cash:** The Malaysian Ringgit (MYR) is the official currency, and while cards are widely accepted in urban areas, cash is still king in rural regions, night markets, and small eateries. Specifically, a notable exception is the hawker centers, street food stalls, and kopitiam establishments — there, it's cash only.
- **ATMs:** ATMs are everywhere in Malaysian cities — shopping malls, airports, petrol stations, 7-Elevens, and on most urban streets, and Maybank (yellow ATMs), CIMB (red ATMs), Public Bank, RHB, Hong Leong, HSBC, and OCBC all accept international Visa, Mastercard, Cirrus, Maestro, and Plus cards. Expect an extra ATM fee — check with your home bank beforehand.
- **Card fees:** Some merchants and banks add a transaction fee of between 2-4% on foreign credit cards, so it's worth asking before you pay.
- **Digital wallets:** Mobile wallets (Touch 'n Go eWallet, GrabPay, Boost) are popular locally, and Apple Pay and Google Pay are accepted at select merchants.
- **Tip:** Notify your bank of your travel dates and decline dynamic currency conversion — declining lets your home bank handle the conversion at their much better rate; the same applies at merchant card terminals — always choose to pay in ringgit, never in your home currency.

## Best time to visit

Malaysia is hot and humid year-round, so timing depends on which region you're visiting rather than a single "best month."

- **West coast (Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Langkawi):** The ideal time to tour Malaysia's west coast is between December and March, which marks the dry season, during which the west coast largely escapes the north-eastern monsoon.
- **East coast (Perhentian, Redang, Tioman):** The east coast gets shut down by the Northeast Monsoon from November through February or March, when many island resorts close completely, so aim for roughly March/April through September instead, when the east coast islands like Tioman and Perhentian are best visited during the dry season from March to September, ideal for island hopping, snorkelling, and enjoying the pristine beaches without interruption.
- **Borneo (Sabah & Sarawak):** The best time to visit Borneo is from March to October when it is relatively drier, as the Northeast monsoon season affects the rest of the year.
- **General pattern:** The south-west monsoon generally starts in late May and stays until September, while the north-east monsoon, which is typically a lot stronger, begins around November and lasts until March. Since the coasts alternate, there's always somewhere in Malaysia where you can enjoy a beach holiday away from the monsoons.

## Emergencies & safety

- **Universal emergency number:** Dial 999 for any emergency in Malaysia (police, fire, ambulance, civil defence) — it is free, and from a mobile 112 also reaches the same operators even with no SIM or credit.
- **Free and traceable:** Every call through the 999 emergency line is free, and the 999 emergency call reception system is able to identify every call made, which can help prevent dropouts when emergency calls are made as well as monitor for false calls.
- **Works without a local SIM:** Emergency calls to 999 and 112 work from any GSM-compatible phone, even without a local SIM card, without credit, or from a locked phone, and all emergency calls are free in Malaysia.
- **Emergency app:** Consider installing the SaveME 999 app before you need it — it sends your GPS location and details to the response centre and supports video, text and multimedia when you cannot speak.
- **Health & insurance:** Travel insurance is strongly advised — while Malaysia doesn't require travel insurance for entry, tourists must pay for medical treatment at the time of service.
- **Seasonal hazards:** Be weather-aware, especially floods, which are Malaysia's most frequent large-scale hazard — the north-east monsoon, the main flood season for the east coast and Borneo, runs roughly mid-November to late March. Flash flooding can also occur elsewhere after heavy rain.
- **General safety:** Malaysia is generally safe for tourists; use common-sense precautions (watch belongings in crowded areas, use registered Grab/taxi services, and avoid unlicensed money changers).
