**Quick answer:** Most visitors from the US, UK, EU, Australia, Canada and many other countries can still enter Malaysia visa-free for up to 90 days (shorter for some nationalities), pay in Malaysian Ringgit (RM) with widely accepted cards plus a Touch 'n Go card for local transit, and dial 999 (or 112 from a mobile) for any emergency; always verify your specific visa and arrival-card requirements on Malaysia's official immigration site before you fly.

## Visa & entry

Malaysia's entry rules depend entirely on your passport, so treat this as a starting point, not a guarantee. Most visitors to Malaysia are granted visa-free entry for a period of 90, 30, or 14 days respectively, while nationals from some countries must first obtain a visa from one of the Malaysian diplomatic missions. Broadly, citizens of the United States, United Kingdom, European Union member states, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and most ASEAN nations get 90 days visa-free, while citizens of China, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Saudi Arabia get 30 days, with a smaller group limited to 14 days.

China and India are special cases worth noting. The government extended the visa exemption for Indian nationals, initially set to end on Dec 31, 2024, for an additional two years until Dec 31, 2026, allowing visa-free visits of up to 30 days. Chinese nationals had their allowance briefly raised to 90 days in 2025, but as of February 2026, Chinese nationals eligible for this visa exemption can now only stay in Malaysia for up to 30 days, with a maximum stay of 90 days within any 180‑day period, and this exemption also runs through 31 December 2026. Both groups must still obtain a separate visa for work or study.

A requirement that now applies to almost everyone is the arrival card: all travellers except residents and Singapore citizens must complete the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) within 3 days before arrival. Do this online before you fly rather than at the airport.

A few practical points apply broadly: most nationalities need at least six months of passport validity beyond your return date; immigration officers may ask for proof of an onward or return ticket, hotel bookings, or funds even for visa-free entry; and visa-free entry is intended for tourism and short visits. Visa-free status does not guarantee admission, border officials can refuse entry if documentation is incomplete. Because these arrangements change periodically (as the China/India example shows), confirm your exact requirements on the Immigration Department's website and the official eVisa/MDAC portal before booking, and again a few days before departure.

## Getting around

Kuala Lumpur and the Klang Valley have an extensive urban rail network combining LRT, MRT, Monorail and KTM Komuter lines, with one Touch 'n Go card usable across MRT, LRT, Monorail and most buses. The city has moved decisively toward cashless local transit: payment on Rapid KL and Go KL city buses does not accept cash or cards; the only way to pay is the Touch 'n Go card. The GoKL CityBus is free for Malaysian citizens, but foreign visitors pay RM1 per ride via Touch 'n Go, credit or debit card. For intercity trains you can still buy tokens with cash at station machines, though UnionPay is generally not accepted on city buses or rail gates.

The simplest approach for visitors is a physical Touch 'n Go card: buy it for a small non-refundable fee at stations, kiosks or 7-Eleven, and top it up with cash. Note the card, not the eWallet phone app, is what gates read.

From Kuala Lumpur International Airport, the standard one-way adult fare for KLIA Ekspres and KLIA Transit between KL Sentral and KLIA is RM55 as of November 2026, but starting January 1, 2027, this will rise to RM65, the first revision since 2016. Travellers flying in or out around the New Year should budget for the new fare. Ride-hailing via Grab remains popular and convenient, operating 24/7 with upfront pricing. Beyond the capital, domestic flights, long-distance coaches and ferries are the main ways to cover longer distances; Sabah and Sarawak rely more on flights and road or river transport given the terrain.

## Money & costs

The currency is the Malaysian Ringgit (MYR, symbolised RM), with banknotes from RM1 to RM100 and coins of 5, 10, 20 and 50 sen. Most hotels, malls, restaurants and shops accept Visa, Mastercard, American Express and Diners Club, and digital wallets like Touch 'n Go, GrabPay and Boost are widely used.

Do not rely on cards everywhere, though: cash is still essential at hawker centres, night markets, small shops, rural areas, and especially in East Malaysia on Borneo. ATMs are plentiful in cities, malls, airports and petrol stations, but coverage thins out off the beaten track, so withdraw cash before heading to remote islands or interior Borneo. Licensed money changers in shopping districts typically beat airport or hotel exchange rates. Malaysia also applies cash-declaration rules for large sums carried in or out of the country; check Bank Negara Malaysia's current thresholds before travelling with significant cash, as limits and reporting rules can be revised.

## Best time to visit

Malaysia is tropical and warm year-round, but rainfall differs sharply by region, driven by the monsoon cycle. The 2026/2027 Northeast Monsoon season is expected to start around October 27, 2026 and continue until March 2027. Its initial phase, from November 2026 to January 2027, is expected to bring three to five episodes of continuous heavy rain, potentially causing flooding in areas near rivers and low-lying locations, chiefly affecting the east coast (Perhentian, Redang, Tioman) and parts of Borneo. The west coast, including Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Langkawi, is comparatively drier during this window.

In the monsoon's final phase, from January to March 2027, rainfall is expected to decrease, particularly in the northern peninsula and Sabah, bringing hotter, drier weather with potential for more extreme heatwaves amid a strong El Niño. Outside the monsoon window, roughly April through September, conditions are generally more favourable across most of the peninsula, though flash floods can still hit the Klang Valley after heavy rain at almost any time. Seasonal transboundary haze can also affect air quality in some years, so check conditions shortly before travelling if you are sensitive to it.

## Emergencies & safety

Malaysia operates a unified emergency number, 999 (or 112 from a mobile phone), that routes callers through to police, ambulance, fire, and civil defence agencies. From a mobile phone, 112 is equally valid and functions even without airtime credit or a PIN unlock, connecting directly to the same 999 response centre. Operators generally handle both Malay and English. The MERS 999 (Malaysian Emergency Response Services) system handles all emergency calls, drawing on police, fire and rescue (Bomba), and ambulance services; response times vary by location and are generally faster in cities than in remote areas.

Misuse carries real penalties: a person can be charged and fined RM50,000 or sentenced to 1 year in prison or both if convicted of a false emergency call to 999. Reserve 999 for genuine emergencies and use your local police station for minor, non-urgent matters. Travel insurance is strongly recommended: Malaysia does not require it for entry, but tourists must pay for medical treatment at the time of service.
