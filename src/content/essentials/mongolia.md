**Quick answer:** Most Western travellers can currently enter Mongolia visa-free for up to 30 days (90 for US citizens) thanks to a temporary policy running through the end of 2026, but rules are nationality-specific and genuinely shift year to year, so verify your exact status on the official Immigration Agency and e-visa sites before booking.

## Visa & entry

Mongolia's entry rules changed again in 2026, and most of the policies now in force are explicitly temporary, so this is one area where double-checking really matters. As things stand, citizens of sixty countries, including the U.S., Canada, most of Europe, Australia, and New Zealand, may travel to Mongolia visa-free through the end of 2026. Within that group, the temporary visa-free policy for Europeans scheduled to expire at the end of 2025 has been extended, and through the end of 2026, citizens of most European countries may visit Mongolia visa-free for up to thirty days. The same thirty-day extension applies to Australians and New Zealanders, though beginning in 2027, Australians and New Zealanders will need to obtain an e-visa prior to their visit using the evisa.mn website, so the window is closing. US passport holders have it easier long-term: US passport holders can visit Mongolia visa-free for up to 90 days for tourism, thanks to a long-standing bilateral agreement, and Hong Kong and Macau SAR passport holders get 14 days visa-free.

If your country is not on the exempt list, Mongolia now runs a genuinely convenient e-visa system. Citizens of the countries on this list can apply for Mongolian e-visa from anywhere in the world, with tourist (K2), event/cultural (K4), and transit (K6) categories available. Processing is quick: applications are reviewed by Immigration Agency officers and processed within 3 working days, or 72 hours, with approval sent as a printable Electronic Travel Authorization. Regardless of visa type, all visitors must have a passport valid for at least 6 months from the date of arrival, and anyone planning to stay more than 30 days must register with the Mongolia Immigration Agency in Ulaanbaatar within the first seven days of arrival. Border officers also commonly check for onward or return tickets. Because exemptions, durations, and registration windows are tied to bilateral agreements that expire or renew annually, always confirm your specific nationality's current rules directly on immigration.gov.mn and evisa.mn before you fly.

## Getting around

Ulaanbaatar's public transport runs on buses and trolleybuses paid for with a rechargeable smart card rather than cash. Cash payment is not accepted many times, so you pay by using the U Money bus card, which you load with a minimum of MNT 500 and a maximum of MNT 90,000. Fares are flat-rate and cheap, and transfers within a short window are free if you tap correctly. For trip planning, locals and visitors alike use apps: the most useful apps for travelers in Ulaanbaatar are Google Maps and MAPS.ME for navigation, UBCab for taxis, and UB Smart Bus for public transport. There is no metro yet: construction of Ulaanbaatar's first line was only confirmed to begin in mid-2026, with construction of the Ulaanbaatar Metro set to begin in June 2026, consisting of a single 19.4-kilometer line, so it will not be operational for visitors in the near term.

Beyond the capital, travel is mostly by road or domestic flight. Mongolia runs a regional bus network from Ulaanbaatar to different provinces, organizing around 1,200 vehicles daily to roughly 75 destinations, which is economical compared with other options. Paved highways connect major hubs like the Gobi gateway town of Dalanzadgad, but much of the countryside is unpaved track, so organized Jeep tours or hired drivers are the normal way to reach national parks and ger camps. Licensed taxis and ride apps operate in Ulaanbaatar, while in rural areas private drivers often double as informal taxis.

## Money & costs

Mongolia's currency is the tugrik (MNT, symbol ₮), and besides the Mongolian tugrik, no other currency is officially accepted in Mongolia. In practice, cash remains essential outside the capital: cash remains the most widely used payment method, especially outside major cities, while credit cards are accepted in Ulaanbaatar and at international hotels, but less so in rural areas. Visa and Mastercard are the safest bet at ATMs; ATMs commonly accept Visa, Mastercard, Maestro, Plus, and Cirrus cards, while Amex, JCB, UnionPay, Diners, Discover, and Rupay cards are not accepted. Expect per-transaction limits: ATMs in Mongolia have a maximum withdrawal limit per transaction of roughly ₮400,000 to ₮800,000, and there are no fee-free ATMs, with bank ATMs typically charging ₮500 per transaction to foreign card holders. When an ATM asks whether to "accept conversion," decline it, since accepting lets the ATM convert at its own exchange rate with an extra fee, while declining uses your own bank's rate without the markup. Carry a reasonable cash buffer in tugrik (and crisp, recent-series US dollars as backup) for markets, countryside ger camps, and small-town purchases.

## Best time to visit

Summer is the clear high season. Summer is the best time of year to visit Mongolia and is considered peak season, with June, July, and August offering a great deal to see and experience. The centerpiece is Naadam, Mongolia's national festival of wrestling, archery, and horse racing; in 2026 the national Naadam Festival will run from Saturday, July 11 to Sunday, July 13, in Ulaanbaatar, with the traditional flag-raising ceremony on the morning of July 11. Book well ahead if you want to be in the capital for it, since hotel rates in Ulaanbaatar are known to double in price between July 10 and 12, when the Opening Ceremony takes place. If you prefer smaller crowds, autumn is excellent too: autumn is the time to visit western Mongolia and experience the thrilling eagle festivals of Bayan-Ölgii, alongside golden steppe colors. Winter is for specialists only, given Mongolia's extreme continental climate, and the Gobi can be brutally hot in midsummer, so pack and plan regionally.

## Emergencies & safety

Mongolia uses separate three-digit numbers rather than a single unified line. Ambulance is 103, Fire is 101, and Police is 102 (which also includes traffic police). Note that the European-style 112 is not a reliable fallback here: 112 may not reliably work in Mongolia, so save the local numbers directly. Medical care has real limits outside Ulaanbaatar, and travelers should be aware that there are no emergency helicopter evacuation services available in the country, which makes comprehensive travel insurance with medical evacuation coverage important for anyone heading into the Gobi or far countryside. Violent crime against tourists is uncommon, but register your trip with your embassy if you're heading off the beaten path, carry printed emergency contacts since mobile coverage is patchy in rural areas, and keep your embassy's and insurer's numbers saved before you leave connectivity behind.
