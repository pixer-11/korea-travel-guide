**Quick answer:** Around 50 nationalities, including the UK, most of the EU, Australia, Canada, and New Zealand (but not the US), can enter mainland China visa-free for up to 30 days through 31 December 2026; pay with a phone (Alipay or WeChat Pay linked to a foreign card) plus a little cash, ride the metro and high-speed rail with ease, and remember there is no single "911": dial 110 for police, 120 for ambulance, 119 for fire, and 122 for traffic accidents.

## Visa & entry
China's unilateral visa-free policy has expanded steadily. China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs published an updated list of 50 countries whose nationals can enter China without a visa for up to 30 days, effective 17 February 2026. That update was the day the UK and Canada became able to enter China visa-free for up to 30 days, bringing the total number of countries eligible for the unilateral visa-free entry policy to 50. The policy is in force until 24:00 Beijing time on 31 December 2026. The scheme covers business, tourism, family visits, exchange visits, and transit, and the United States is not on it, so American passport holders still need a standard visa.

There is also a separate transit route: China has added Kyrgyzstan and Vietnam to its 240-hour visa-free transit program, bringing the total number of countries eligible for this policy to 57, immigration authorities announced on Aug 20, 2026. Effective from Aug 20, citizens of these countries holding ordinary passports and interline passenger tickets with confirmed dates and itineraries bound for a third country or region may enter China through any of the 65 ports across the country and stay up to 240 hours, or 10 days, without a visa. This 10-day window covers tourism and family visits but tourism, business, exchange visits and family visits, but not employment, study or journalistic activities, which still require appropriate visas.

Practical entry notes:
- Your passport should be valid for at least six months with blank pages, and non-hotel accommodation (homestays, rentals) generally requires registering with the local Public Security Bureau within 24 hours.
- Overstaying is taken seriously. Under Article 78 of China's Exit and Entry Administration Law, the standard fine for each day of overstay is CNY 500, and a maximum ceiling of CNY 10,000 applies to the total amount, and serious cases can bring detention and an entry ban ranging from 1 to 10 years depending on the severity of your violation.
- Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan have their own entry regimes and are not covered by this mainland policy.

Because eligibility lists and dates shift, always **confirm your specific nationality's status, exact permitted stay, and required documents directly with the National Immigration Administration or your nearest Chinese embassy/consulate before booking flights.**

## Getting around
Domestic transport in China is fast, cheap by Western comparison, and increasingly foreigner-friendly.

- **High-speed rail** is the backbone of long-distance travel. Book through the official 12306 platform or app; in the normal system, official presale is 15 days including the travel day, and each station posts its own release clock, so you should read the date and time shown in the app. A limited pilot on the Beijing-Shanghai corridor now also lets travellers submit earlier reservation requests, which the system then tries to fulfil closer to departure, so check the app for your specific route.
- **City metros** in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, and other major cities are extensive, English-signed, and accept mobile QR payment.
- **Ride-hailing**: Didi is the dominant app and the standard way to book a car, especially at busy times.
- Set up a payment app before you need transport; many small restaurants and some metro ticket machines still favour Chinese payment apps over foreign cards or cash.
- For Golden Week or Spring Festival, book international flights and hotels three to six months ahead.

## Money & costs
The currency is the Renminbi (RMB/yuan). China is largely a mobile-payment society, and the barriers for foreign visitors have dropped further in 2026.

- Foreigners can verify and use both WeChat Pay and Alipay in 2026 with just a passport and an international credit card, no Chinese bank account or phone number required.
- Fees are manageable for typical tourist spending: transactions of ¥200 RMB or less are free, but every single transaction above ¥200 is subject to a 3% international-card fee on the full transaction amount, which covers most meals, coffee, metro rides, and taxi fares without a surcharge. Your own bank may add its own foreign-transaction fee on top, so a no-FX-fee card helps.
- Limits for verified foreign users are generous: after passport verification, the standard caps are about US$5,000 per single transaction and US$50,000 per year on Alipay, with broadly similar figures on WeChat Pay, far more than most tourists will ever need.
- Despite the cashless culture, keep a small cash buffer of 100 to 300 RMB for small stalls, taxi edge cases, or network failures.
- International Visa/Mastercard plastic still works at high-end hotels, chain tourist restaurants, and larger shops, but is far less accepted at small local places, so a working mobile wallet remains worth the setup time.

Costs vary hugely by city and season; Golden Week and Spring Festival push hotel and flight prices up sharply, while shoulder-season travel is markedly cheaper.

## Best time to visit
China's size means "best time" depends on region, but two windows are consistently recommended nationwide: spring (April to May) and autumn (September to October), for clear skies and comfortable walking weather. Mid-October, once Golden Week crowds clear, is widely considered a sweet spot for the big eastern cities.

Avoid, or plan carefully around, China's two mass travel holidays:
- **National Day "Golden Week,"** 1 to 7 October, is a settled seven-day block and is widely considered the single worst time to visit China as a tourist, with heavy crowds, higher prices, and transport strain. Arriving on 8 October or later gets you autumn weather with post-holiday emptiness.
- **Spring Festival (Chinese New Year)**, which moves with the lunar calendar, sees similarly enormous domestic travel volumes and sold-out trains; the 2027 Spring Festival travel rush is expected to run across several weeks around the holiday.

Summer (July to August) brings serious heat and humidity to the eastern plains, including Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangzhou. Winter suits northern snow destinations (Harbin) and southern beach escapes (Sanya) rather than general sightseeing in the big eastern cities.

## Emergencies & safety
China does not use a single unified emergency number like 911 or 112; instead, dial the specific service you need:

- **Police: 110**
- **Ambulance (medical): 120**
- **Fire: 119**
- **Traffic accidents: 122**

110, 120 and 119 are free calls, and 110 may be used for all emergencies; it is the first number to call if you are unsure which service you need. These lines work on any phone, even without a SIM card.

English-language support is inconsistent outside major cities, though Shanghai's 110 police line is answered in English, Japanese, Russian, German, French, Spanish, Arabic and Korean, with 119 and 120 also offering English support. For general (non-emergency) tourist help, the 24-hour China Tourist Helpline on 12301 has English-speaking operators, and China's consular protection and assistance hotline is 12308. Practical tips: have hotel staff call on your behalf if needed, use a translation app, and keep your embassy's emergency contact saved offline. For a lost or stolen passport, report to local police (110) first to get a loss report, then contact your embassy.

General safety in China is good by international standards for violent crime, though petty scams around tourist sites and taxi or ride-hailing overcharging do occur, so standard travel precautions apply.
