**Quick answer:** Most nationalities need an e-Visa (Electronic Travel Authorisation) arranged online before arrival, the rupee is a closed currency so bring/withdraw cash locally, and October to March is the most comfortable season to visit most of the country.

## Visa & entry

Most travellers arrive on India's e-Visa (e-Tourist visa), applied for entirely online with no embassy visit needed. citizens from over 180 countries/territories are now able to apply for an Indian e-Tourist Visa before arriving in India, including America, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, etc. The e-Tourist visa comes in three tiers: a 30-day visa with two entries, and 1-year and 5-year multiple-entry visas capped at 180 days of total stay per calendar year. Standard 30-day e-Tourist fees run $10 (Apr–Jun) or $25 (Jul–Mar), with the 1-year option at $40, plus a bank charge of 2.5% will be charged additionally on the applicable e-Tourist Visa fee.

Practical points: the portal works to a minimum four-day processing buffer before arrival, and you can apply between 4 and 120 days before your travel date. Your approval arrives as an ETA, and you will need to have a printout of the ETA with you when you arrive in India, and present it at the immigration counter at the airport or seaport, where an immigration officer will stamp your passport. Entry is restricted to designated points: entry on a tourist e-Visa is limited to 33 airports and 9 seaports, and land border crossings are not allowed for e-Tourist entries. Note that the tourist e-Visa is non-extendable, and overstaying often results in fines and a formal "regularization" process with immigration authorities. Passport rules require a valid passport with a minimum of 6 months' validity from the date of travel in India, and a minimum of 2 blank pages. Travellers arriving from yellow-fever-risk countries should note that travellers from yellow fever risk countries must carry a valid yellow fever vaccination certificate or face up to 6 days of quarantine.

**Always confirm current fees, eligible nationalities, entry ports and document requirements on the official government e-Visa portal before booking flights, as rules and fees change without much notice.**

## Getting around

**City transport:** Metro systems (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad and others) are the cheapest and most reliable way to beat traffic; Delhi Metro tickets can even be booked in advance via IRCTC. App-based cabs and auto-rickshaws (Uber, Ola, Namma Yatri) are widely used in cities alongside traditional metered taxis.

**Trains:** Indian Railways is the backbone of long-distance travel. Foreigners can register on the official booking site and use the Foreign Tourist Quota (FTQ), which is separate from the regular quota. Foreign tourists can book up to 365 days in advance under the FT Quota; for most other passengers, the Advance Reservation Period is now 60 days, so the FT Quota window is a real advantage for planning a trip early. This is especially useful on busy tourist routes. Indian Railways sets aside a dedicated number of berths on popular routes like Delhi to Agra, Jaipur, Varanasi, or Mumbai, specifically to help international visitors get confirmed seats, and while regular tickets can only be booked 60 days in advance, the FT quota opens a full 365 days before departure for air-conditioned classes. Foreign card payments on the IRCTC site can be glitchy; if declined, third-party agents or your hotel's travel desk can help, or visit an International Tourist Bureau such as the one at New Delhi Railway Station. Note that since mid-2025, Tatkal (last-minute) booking now requires Aadhaar authentication, which foreign visitors cannot complete, meaning planning ahead is not optional but essential.

**Domestic flights** connect all major cities quickly and are often price-competitive with premium train classes for longer distances. **Intercity buses** (state-run and private) fill in gaps where rail isn't convenient, especially in hill regions.

## Money & costs

India's currency is the Indian Rupee (₹/INR); as one guide notes, India uses the Indian Rupee, abbreviated INR and symbolized as ₹, and all official pricing, payments, and transactions within India are denominated in Indian Rupees; foreign currencies are not accepted for everyday transactions anywhere. Cards work well in cities: Visa and Mastercard are accepted at most ATMs across India and at hotels, larger restaurants, established shops, and most tourist-facing businesses in cities, while American Express is accepted at a limited number of ATMs and merchants.

Digital QR payments via UPI dominate daily life, and India now offers a way in for visitors: in 2023, India's National Payments Corporation (NPCI) launched UPI One World, a prepaid wallet system that lets foreign tourists use UPI without an Indian bank account or phone number. This matters because most small vendors, street food stalls, and local markets accept QR payments only. That said, cash is still essential outside big cities: many smaller vendors, local transport operators, and rural areas do not accept digital payments, and personal transfers (as opposed to registered-merchant payments) generally aren't available to foreign UPI wallets.

For getting rupees, a practical approach is to get some cash at the airport for immediate needs (at a poor exchange rate), then withdraw a larger amount from an ATM on day one for better rates. Keep some backup cash on hand at all times, and carry two different card networks in case one is declined; tell your bank you're travelling to India to prevent card blocks, write down your bank's customer service number separately from your phone, and bring two different payment cards (Visa/Mastercard, not American Express only). Tipping is customary though not mandatory: roughly 5–10% at restaurants (check if a service charge is already added), ₹50–₹100 per bag for bellboys, ₹100–₹300 for a full day's driver service, and ₹200–₹500 for guides.

## Best time to visit

For most travelers, October through March is the best time to visit India, as temperatures are cooler and more comfortable across much of the country, making this the ideal season for sightseeing, wildlife viewing, luxury train journeys, and cultural exploration. This is also peak season: this is also India's busiest tourism season, particularly from late November through February, so book flights, trains and hotels well ahead, especially around Diwali, Christmas and New Year.

Regional nuance matters. The best time to visit India's southern states is from November onward, once the monsoon has ended, while the best time to visit Ladakh and the Indian Himalayas is from May through September, when mountain roads reopen and temperatures become more accessible, a season that overlaps with the rest of the country's monsoon. Avoid the pre-monsoon build-up if you dislike extreme heat: intense summer heat (April-June) can exceed 45°C in the north, and from June to September, the monsoon winds bring heavy monsoon rains across the entire subcontinent, and during peak monsoon, the jungle regions and low-lying delta lands in West Bengal often start flooding.

## Emergencies & safety

India's single, unified emergency number is **112**, covering police, fire and medical services nationwide. The Emergency Response Support System is a Pan-India single number (112) based emergency response system, and each State/UT designates a dedicated Emergency Response Centre to handle requests. It can be reached multiple ways: 112 emergency number can be connected through phone call, SMS, e-mail, panic button and '112 India' mobile app, and the emergency numbers are free and available 24h/7.

Older single-service numbers still work in parallel and are worth knowing: the main emergency numbers in India include 112 (national emergency services number), 100 (police helpline number), 108 (ambulance number), and 101 (fire services). Women travellers can also note the dedicated Women Helpline. For rail-specific problems, the Railway Enquiry helpline (139) is useful. As with any destination, avoid unlicensed street "agents," keep copies of your passport and visa/ETA separately from the originals, and share your live location with someone you trust when travelling solo or at night.
