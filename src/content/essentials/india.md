**Quick answer:** Most nationalities need an e-Visa/ETA arranged online before arrival, India's roads/rail/metro are extensive but chaotic, cash plus UPI/cards work well, October–March is the most comfortable season, and 112 is the single national emergency number.

## Visa & entry

Nearly all foreign visitors need a visa before landing in India — there is no general visa-on-arrival for leisure tourists. The easiest route for most nationalities is the **e-Visa (Electronic Travel Authorization)**, applied for online in advance. It is one of 5 sub-categories under the e-Visa scheme, along with e-Business Visa, e-Medical Visa, e-Medical Attendant Visa, and e-Conference Visa, and there are 3 types of e-Tourist Visas available, with a validity period of 30 days, one year, and five years. For e-Tourist Visa (01 year / 05 years), continuous stay during each visit shall not exceed 90 days except nationals of USA, UK, Canada and Japan, who can stay up to 180 days per visit. The Indian e-Tourist Visa is valid for entry into India only through the designated 31 international airports and 6 major Indian seaports, so overland entries may need a different visa type — check before finalizing your itinerary.

Practical requirements: your passport will need to be valid for at least 6 months from the date of arrival in India, and there should be at least 2 blank pages for stamping by the Immigration Officer. Normally it will take about 3 working days for processing, though it can be applied for well in advance (up to 120 days for 30-day e-Tourist Visas). Note that foreigners of Pakistani origin or having a Pakistani passport, or those whose parents or grandparents were born in or permanently resident in Pakistan, are not eligible for the e-Visa and must apply through a regular embassy visa. Some newer categories exist too — the e-Ayush Visa is now available for all foreigners seeking treatment through Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy systems, useful for wellness travelers.

Apply only through the official portal at indianvisaonline.gov.in — third-party "visa agent" sites often overcharge or mishandle data. **Visa rules, fees, eligible-country lists, and validity change periodically, so always confirm current details on the official government e-Visa site and your nearest Indian embassy/consulate before booking flights.**

## Getting around

India's transport network is vast and improving fast, but expect crowding and some chaos.

- **Air:** Domestic flights are the fastest way between major cities and generally affordable if booked ahead.
- **Trains:** Indian Railways is the backbone of long-distance travel. Foreign visitors can use the **Foreign Tourist Quota**, a special reserved-berth allocation. Under this quota, Foreign Tourists (FT) and Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) holding a valid passport can book train tickets through the official IRCTC website, and this quota provides earmarked berths in select trains and allows booking up to 365 days in advance. Tickets can also be bought in person at designated **International Tourist Bureaus** in cities like New Delhi, Agra Cantonment and Varanasi, bringing your original passport and visa.
- **Metro:** Most major cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata, Chennai, Hyderabad) have modern, air-conditioned metro systems. Delhi Metro sells a dedicated **Tourist Smart Card** — available in One-Day and Three-Day validity, offering unlimited rides, priced at ₹200 for one day and ₹500 for three days (including a refundable ₹50 security deposit).
- **Local transport:** App-based cabs (Uber, Ola) and auto-rickshaws are widely available in cities; agree on/insist on the meter or app fare to avoid overcharging.
- **Payments on transport:** Many metros and buses now accept contactless national transit cards alongside cash tokens — carry small change as backup.

## Money & costs

India's currency is the **Indian Rupee (INR/₹)**. ATMs are widely available in cities and towns and accept most international Visa/Mastercard debit cards; carry a mix of cash and cards, especially outside metro areas.

Digital payment via **UPI (Unified Payments Interface)** QR codes is now ubiquitous — from taxis to street stalls — and India has begun opening this system to tourists. UPI One World is a prepaid wallet specifically designed for inbound travelers from G20 countries and others, launched by the NPCI. For a tourist visit of a few weeks, the UPI One World prepaid wallet works — you can load it at an airport kiosk on arrival at cities like Delhi, Mumbai, and Bangalore, using your foreign credit/debit card or by exchanging cash at the counter, then scan any Google Pay, Paytm, or PhonePe QR code in the country. Note that any unused balance can be refunded to your original payment source when you leave, but only if you complete the refund process at the counter before your flight out. As of mid-2026 this rollout is still expanding, so availability at your entry airport should be verified on arrival, and a physical foreign-transaction-fee-free debit/credit card remains a reliable backup.

Budget travel is generally inexpensive by Western standards (hostels, street food, local trains), while mid-range hotels, private drivers, and domestic flights push costs up quickly — build a flexible daily budget depending on your travel style.

## Best time to visit

For many visitors, the best overall time is from roughly late October to early March, when much of the country experiences cooler, drier and generally more comfortable weather, making it easier to combine several regions in one trip. The hottest period in many parts of India is typically from late April through June, especially in northern and central regions, when daytime temperatures can become very high. The summer **monsoon** brings heavy rain to most of the country roughly from June to September/October, causing transport delays and localized flooding, though it's a low-season bargain period. Ladakh, located in the far north, is most accessible between June and September, when the rest of the country experiences monsoon season — the opposite pattern to the rest of India. If you want festivals, the best time to visit India for festivals is between October and March, with the famous Diwali (Festival of Lights) taking place in October, sometimes November.

## Emergencies & safety

India has consolidated most emergency services under one number: in an emergency, dial 112 first — it is India's single national emergency number under the Emergency Response Support System (ERSS), connecting you to police, fire, ambulance, and other emergency services. 112 can be connected through phone call, SMS, e-mail, panic button and the '112 India' mobile app. Older dedicated numbers still work as backups: Police 100, Fire 101, Ambulance 102, Tourist helpline 1363, Road Accident 1073, Disaster management 108, Air Accident 1071, Women in Distress 1091, Medical helpline 104, Indian Railway Security Helpline 1322.

General safety tips: keep photocopies (digital and paper) of your passport and visa; use registered taxis/apps after dark, especially solo travelers; drink bottled/filtered water; be alert for common tourist scams around major sights and train stations; and register serious complaints with local police (a First Information Report/FIR) if property is stolen, which is usually required for travel-insurance claims. Solo women travelers should take extra precautions with transport and accommodation choices, and can use the dedicated women's helpline (1091) if needed.
