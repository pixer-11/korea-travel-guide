## Three days in a Chonburi valley

Tomorrowland has picked the Thai coast. From December 11 to 13, 2026, the festival takes over Wisdom Valley, a purpose-built event site in Chonburi province. That puts it about two hours' drive south of Bangkok, in the same province as Pattaya and the port city of Si Racha.

This is the brand's first full Asian outing. Until now, fans in the region have mostly known it through livestreams, the original festival in Boom, Belgium, and the editions in Brazil and the French Alps. Thailand gets the complete three-day format, not a one-night spin-off.

## What the Tomorrowland name means on the ground

The festival started in Boom in 2005. Brothers Manu and Michiel Beers founded it, and it built its name on two things: big electronic lineups and stage design that looks like theatre sets. Main stages are built around a story that changes from year to year. Fans fly in from more than a hundred countries.

The Thai edition sits on that reputation. Expect a festival that cares about design as much as headliners, and a crowd that travels for it. Nobody has given us the Thailand lineup or the stage list, so we don't print either here.

## Tickets, dates and where the details live

The official sources are simple to find:

- **Dates:** December 11–13, 2026, three days
- **Venue:** Wisdom Valley, Chonburi province
- **Tickets and lineup:** the organiser publishes ticket links, the lineup and any packages on the official Tomorrowland website
- **Prices:** shown on the official ticket pages, not repeated here

Tomorrowland usually sells several kinds of pass at its flagship events, from day tickets to full-weekend passes and travel bundles. In Belgium, demand has always outrun supply. Fans of the brand usually register on the official site and buy only through official channels, which avoids the resale scams that follow every big festival.

## From Bangkok to the valley

Most international visitors fly into Suvarnabhumi Airport. It sits on the eastern edge of Bangkok, which is already the Chonburi side of the city. Motorway 7 runs from there toward Pattaya and the eastern seaboard, and it is the road most festival traffic uses.

Ways people usually make the trip:

- **Grab or a private car** from Bangkok or the airport, the easiest door-to-door choice
- **Intercity buses** from Bangkok's Ekkamai (Eastern) bus terminal to Chonburi and Pattaya
- **Domestic flights to U-Tapao**, the airport in neighbouring Rayong that serves the Pattaya area

Festival-organised shuttles are common at Tomorrowland events, and the official site carries the transport options for each edition. Weekend traffic on Motorway 7 is heavy at the best of times. Many travellers leave Bangkok well before the gates open.

## Where attendees base themselves

There are three usual choices for a bed. Pattaya has the most hotel rooms in Chonburi, from beach towers to small guesthouses on the side streets. Si Racha is quieter and has a strong Japanese expat dining scene. Bangkok suits people who want a city break as well and don't mind the drive.

December is the start of Thailand's cool, dry season. Days are still hot and sunny on the coast, but evenings ease off and rain is unusual. That makes it one of the busiest months for tourism on the eastern seaboard. Hotel rooms near the venue are under pressure from festival crowds and holidaymakers at the same time.

## Festival manners, Thai style

A few local habits make the weekend go more smoothly. Thailand runs on mobile payments. Thai QR codes (PromptPay) are everywhere, and Grab handles most rides outside the festival. Cash in baht is still useful for street food and small shops in Pattaya and Si Racha.

Thai law on drugs is among the strictest in Asia. Police often run checks around big events. That is the single most costly mistake a visitor can make here, and the official festival rules don't soften it.

Respect for the monarchy is a legal matter, not just a social one. Leave royal images and the national anthem out of jokes and costumes. A smile and a small *wai* (palms together, slight bow) to hotel and venue staff goes a long way. Saying *khop khun krap* (men) or *khop khun ka* (women) for "thank you" gets an instant warm reaction.

Tipping isn't required. Drivers and servers appreciate rounding up, though. Sunscreen, a refillable bottle and a light layer for the evening are what regulars at Thai outdoor events usually carry.
