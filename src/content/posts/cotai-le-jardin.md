## A garden on the Strip

Cotai was built for indoor life. Air-conditioned malls, casino floors and covered walkways link one resort to the next. Le Jardin sits outside all that on Estrada do Istmo, the road where City of Dreams, The Parisian and The Londoner have their addresses.

It's a park, so you go for the open air. Few visitors know it's there, and that's the reason to go: it gives you a short, quiet break from the Strip's noise and crowds. Treat it as a pause between other plans. It isn't a destination for a whole afternoon.

## Getting to Estrada do Istmo

Most visitors arrive the Cotai way, through the resorts:

- **Free resort shuttles** run from the Macau Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal, Taipa Ferry Terminal and the Border Gate to the big Cotai properties. Get off at whichever resort is closest on Estrada do Istmo and walk the rest.
- **The Macau LRT Taipa Line** has stations along Cotai. Check the route map for the stop nearest your resort.
- **Taxis** know Estrada do Istmo. Show the address in writing, since not all drivers speak English.

On foot, the resort walkways make the Strip feel smaller than it is. At street level the distances are long and the crossings are wide, so give yourself extra time.

## Timing your stop

Google currently lists the hours as 10am–8pm daily. Park hours can change with the season and on holidays, so check before a special trip.

- **Morning, after 10am:** Cotai's casinos and malls are quietest early, and so is the street outside.
- **Late afternoon:** in Macau's hot, humid summer (roughly May to September), wait until the sun is lower before sitting outdoors.
- **Before 8pm:** the resort façades along Estrada do Istmo light up after dark. Arrive in the hour before closing and you get the change from daylight to neon.

Autumn and early winter, from about October to December, bring drier, milder air. That's the most comfortable time of year for anything outdoors in Macau.

## Using Le Jardin the Cotai way

Fit the park in between other things. The usual pattern is a mall or museum visit inside one resort, then a short walk outdoors, then dinner in the next resort.

- **Bring water.** The shops and food courts are inside the resorts, not out in the park.
- **Carry an umbrella in summer.** Afternoon downpours and typhoon-season storms come in fast. Covered resort entrances are your nearest shelter.
- **Keep your voice down.** People come here to get away from the casino-floor noise.
- **Pay with Hong Kong dollars or patacas.** Both are accepted across Cotai, and cards work in the resorts. Small shops and taxis tend to prefer cash or local e-wallets like MPay.
- **Check the official listing** if you want to know about events, closures or any entry charge. We don't have current details.

Afterwards, cross back to the resorts. The Parisian's half-scale Eiffel Tower and The Londoner's re-created Westminster-style façade are both on the same road, so you can finish your stop with a look at the Strip's set-piece architecture.
