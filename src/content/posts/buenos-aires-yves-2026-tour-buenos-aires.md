## Yves on Avenida Corrientes

Yves is coming to Buenos Aires on her own. Ha Soo-young sang in LOONA, the twelve-member group built one solo release at a time, and her own chapter in that rollout was the single "new". In 2026 she tours as a soloist, and Buenos Aires is one of her stops.

The show is at C Art Media, a concert hall on Avenida Corrientes in Chacarita, northwest of Palermo. Argentina has one of the biggest K-pop fanbases in South America. Expect a crowd that knows every cue.

## Dates, tickets and the venue

The facts we have give the year, 2026, and not the exact night. The tour promoter and C Art Media's own channels hold the date, door time and ticket links. Argentine concert tickets are usually sold online in pesos through one ticketing platform per show. The venue's box office is the other route.

What fans usually carry on the night:

- the ticket as a QR code on their phone, plus the ID the platform asks for
- a charged phone, because the ticket and the ride home both depend on it
- a light layer, since Buenos Aires gets cold at night from May to September
- any fan-project items: banners, slogan cards, the LOONA lightstick

## Getting to Chacarita and back

Subte Line B runs directly under Avenida Corrientes. Dorrego and Federico Lacroze are the closest stations, and Lacroze sits by the Chacarita train terminal. Line B also takes you straight back to Microcentro and the Obelisco, so the venue is easy to reach from a hotel in the centre.

The Subte and the colectivos (city buses) both take the rechargeable SUBE card, sold at kiosks and stations. Contactless bank cards also work on most of the network. Colectivos run all night. When a show finishes after the Subte has stopped, people take a bus or book a car through Cabify or Uber.

Plenty of visitors stay in Palermo or Villa Crespo, where the walk or taxi home is short. The Cementerio de la Chacarita is just up Avenida Corrientes, and it is worth a daytime visit.

## How the fan line works at C Art Media

Argentine K-pop fans organise. Fan accounts usually hand out free slogan cards, stickers and small gifts near the entrance before doors, and travelling fans are welcome to take them. Queues for general-admission shows in Buenos Aires tend to form hours before doors. People bring folding stools, mate and snacks for the wait.

Inside, the crowd does the fan chants in Korean and the songs in full. A few things are good to know:

- Spanish is the language of the line, and a "hola" and "gracias" are always welcome
- Argentines greet friends with one kiss on the cheek; with strangers in a queue, a nod is enough
- most ticketing and food stalls take cards or Mercado Pago, but small street vendors outside often want cash

The Argentine night runs late. Dinner at ten is normal, so after the show the pizzerias and parrillas along Corrientes and around Federico Lacroze are still serving.
