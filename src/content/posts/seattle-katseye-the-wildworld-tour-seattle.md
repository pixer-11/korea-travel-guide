## A pop group built on camera, playing under a 1962 roof

KATSEYE's Seattle date was set for November 17, 2026. The stop was part of the group's North American run of The Wildworld Tour, and the venue was Climate Pledge Arena, the city's largest indoor arena.

The group came together through *The Debut: Dream Academy*, the HYBE and Geffen Records competition series. That origin shapes its fan base. Plenty of fans have followed the members since their first audition clips, and at the group's shows the singing along tends to start with the opening song.

KATSEYE is known for the choreography-heavy, all-sung-live style behind tracks like "Gnarly" and "Touch".

## Inside Climate Pledge Arena

The arena sits beneath the sloped, landmark roof designed for the 1962 Seattle World's Fair. Most of the bowl is underground, dug below street level.

Visitors enter at ground level and then head down to the seats. That gives the room steeper, closer sightlines than the outside suggests. On other nights it is home to the Seattle Kraken (NHL) and the Seattle Storm (WNBA).

Some points about the venue:

- **Payment:** the arena is cashless, so concessions and merch take cards and mobile wallets.
- **Tickets:** entry is by mobile ticket on a phone.
- **Address:** 334 1st Ave N, on the north edge of Seattle Center.
- **Merch:** stands typically open with the doors, and lines at pop shows are often longest before the first act.

## Getting to Seattle Center without the parking fight

Event-night traffic jams Mercer Street and the garages around Seattle Center fill fast. Most concert-goers come by transit.

- **Monorail:** the Seattle Center Monorail runs from Westlake Center downtown to Seattle Center in about two minutes. Its terminal is a short walk from the arena.
- **Link light rail:** the 1 Line stops at Westlake, directly below the monorail terminal. That links Sea-Tac Airport, Capitol Hill and the University District to the arena.
- **On foot:** from Belltown or Lower Queen Anne, it's a 10–20 minute walk on flat streets.

After a concert, the monorail queue usually builds quickly. Many people walk the 20 minutes or so back downtown along 5th Avenue instead.

## Doors, bags and a November night in Seattle

Fans of K-pop-adjacent acts usually line up well before doors, especially for general admission floor spots. Mid-November in Seattle means early darkness and drizzle, so outdoor waits are usually done in rain jackets, not under umbrellas that block the people behind.

Climate Pledge Arena sets its own bag rules, and small or clear bags get through security fastest. Coat checks are not always available, so people tend to wear layers they can stuff under a seat.

Seattle Center makes the hours before a show easy to fill:

- **Space Needle:** its observation deck is a few minutes' walk from the arena.
- **Chihuly Garden and Glass:** sits at the Needle's base.
- **Museum of Pop Culture (MoPOP):** the Frank Gehry building the monorail runs through.

For dinner, concert-goers usually head to Lower Queen Anne's restaurants along Queen Anne Avenue North and Roy Street, just west of the arena.
