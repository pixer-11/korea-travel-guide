## Two nights at Homebush

BTS brings the ARIRANG world tour to Sydney for two nights. The shows take place on Saturday, February 20 and Sunday, February 21, 2027, at Accor Stadium. The tour takes its name from "Arirang", Korea's best-known folk song.

The stadium sits in Sydney Olympic Park at Homebush, roughly 14km west of the CBD. It was built as the main venue of the 2000 Olympic Games. Its tiered bowl rises steeply on all sides, so the upper levels still look straight down onto the stage.

The organiser publishes the dates, venue details and ticket links on its official site. Set times and the support line-up are not part of the facts here, so this guide leaves them out.

## Getting to Sydney Olympic Park

The rail line runs right into the park. Olympic Park station is a few minutes' walk from the stadium along Olympic Boulevard, the wide pedestrian spine that links the venues.

- **Train:** On ordinary days the T7 shuttle runs from Lidcombe. For major events, Sydney Trains adds special services that run direct from Central and other hubs.
- **Ferry:** Parramatta River ferries stop at Sydney Olympic Park wharf, and a bus connects the wharf to the park.
- **Car:** The park has multi-storey car parks, though stadium-sized crowds make leaving slow.

After a stadium show, the queues for trains are managed. Crowd marshals hold and release batches of fans onto the platforms, and most people reach the CBD within an hour of the final song.

## How ARMY does a stadium night at Homebush

Sydney's transport is tap-and-go. An Opal card, a contactless bank card or a phone wallet works on trains, ferries and buses. Tap on and tap off every time, or you pay the default fare.

What fans usually do:

- **Carry the ARMY Bomb.** The official light stick syncs to the show's lighting. Fans pair it to their seat before the first song so the colour patterns run across the whole bowl.
- **Learn the fanchants.** Korean fan chants shout members' names in set places during the songs. Australian ARMY usually knows them, and the stands carry them.
- **Treat merch as a separate trip.** Merch queues at stadium shows often form hours before doors, and plenty of fans shop on the first day and come back for the show.
- **Pack light.** Stadium bag rules limit size. Most attendees bring a small clear or soft bag and leave large camera gear behind.

Both nights draw fans from across Australia, New Zealand and Asia. Exchanging photocards and freebies along Olympic Boulevard before the show is part of the ritual.

## Summer heat, and where fans stay

Late February is high summer in Sydney. Days are often hot and humid, with the chance of an afternoon thunderstorm. The stadium has a partial roof, but many lower seats and the floor are open to the sky. Fans usually bring a refillable water bottle and a light rain poncho rather than an umbrella.

Interstate and overseas visitors split between two bases. Some stay in the CBD for the harbour, the Opera House and the most dining choice, then take the event train out. Others book the hotels inside Sydney Olympic Park or nearby Parramatta. That keeps the walk home short when the trains are busy.

With shows on consecutive nights, many fans attend both or spend the second day at Bondi or Manly before the Sunday show.
