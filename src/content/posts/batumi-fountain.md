## A fountain with no other name

Batumi has plenty of water on show. The Black Sea runs along Batumi Boulevard, and the town squares have their own fountains. This one sits apart from those. It's a place you come across on a walk, so you don't need to plan a day around it.

## Timing your stop

Right now Google lists it as open 24 hours every day. That reflects an outdoor public space with no gate. It doesn't promise the water is running at every hour. Public fountains are often switched off overnight, in cold months or for maintenance, so treat a dry basin as a possibility.

Batumi's climate shapes the visit more than any timetable does. Summers are hot and humid, and a shaded path near moving water is at its best in the early morning or after sunset. Autumn brings heavy rain on the Adjara coast, so pack a light shell from September onward. Winter is mild but damp, and the city empties out after the beach season.

## How to treat a neighbourhood fountain

People here use it as an ordinary public space. It's somewhere to sit for a minute, let children look at the water, or meet a friend halfway. That sets the etiquette:

- **Don't wade or climb.** Unless there are clear splash-pad signs, the basin isn't for paddling.
- **Keep the path clear.** It's a working pedestrian route, so step to the side for photos.
- **Carry small lari.** Cards are accepted across Batumi, but kiosks and street vendors near footpaths often prefer cash.
- **Say hello.** A simple *gamarjoba* (hello) and *madloba* (thank you) go a long way with Georgians, especially older residents.

Because so few visitors make the trip, you'll often get the benches with almost no competition. Pair the stop with a longer walk along the seafront, where the Alphabetic Tower and the moving Ali and Nino statue stand on Batumi Boulevard. That way the fountain becomes a quiet break between the city's busier sights.
