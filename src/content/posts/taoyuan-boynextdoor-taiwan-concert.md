## Six members, one arena on a hill

BOYNEXTDOOR are a six-member boy group from KOZ Entertainment, a label under HYBE, and they debuted in 2023. Sungho, Riwoo, Jaehyun, Taesan, Leehan and Woonhak built their name on bright, conversational songs like "But Sometimes" and "One and Only". The group writes a lot of their own material, and that gives the songs the feel of a neighbourhood friend talking to you.

For their Taiwan date in December 2026, the group plays Linkou Arena in Taoyuan City. Taiwan's fandom calls itself ONEDOOR like everyone else's, and the room will be full of them singing the Korean lyrics back.

## Where Linkou Arena sits

The venue stands on the plateau between Taipei and Taoyuan. It is on the campus of National Taiwan Sport University in Guishan District, and fans usually call it Linkou Arena. It is an indoor bowl, the standard size for K-pop arena tours in northern Taiwan. You get a raised stage at one end, a standing or seated floor in front, and tiered seating curving around the other three sides.

The surroundings feel more like a university town than a city. There are wide roads and sports fields, and the slope rises toward the hospital district at Chang Gung. It is quiet on an ordinary day and crowded with lightsticks on a show night.

## Getting there on the Airport MRT

The Taoyuan Airport MRT is the line that matters. It runs between Taipei Main Station and Taoyuan International Airport, and the arena's station is **A7 Taoyuan Sports University**.

- **From Taipei:** board at Taipei Main Station (A1) and take a **commuter** train, the blue one. Express trains, the purple ones, do not stop at A7.
- **From the airport:** ride the commuter train back toward Taipei. A7 is only a few stops east of the terminals.
- **From A7:** the arena is a walk uphill through the campus area. Fans typically follow the stream of people wearing merch.

Taxis and ride-hailing are an option too, but the roads around the campus are narrow at the pinch points. On concert nights the MRT is usually the less stressful choice.

## What the night inside looks like

The members announce the details for each show themselves: setlist, run time and any special stages. None of that is given here. What every K-pop arena night in Taiwan shares is the build-up outside. Merch queues snake along the plaza, fans pose with banners, and phones go up at the first notes of the opening track.

Inside, the official lightstick is the main light source once the house lights drop. The fan chants are learned in Korean, and Taiwanese fans are known for knowing them cold. Members usually try a few lines of Mandarin between songs, and the cheer that follows is reliably one of the loudest of the night.

## Freebies, ID and the queue at the gate

Taiwan's concert crowds have their own routines.

- **Freebies (應援物):** fans hand out free stickers, photocards and slogans on the plaza before the show. Taking one is normal. A thank-you in Mandarin ("xièxie") is the usual response.
- **Real-name tickets:** many K-pop shows in Taiwan use real-name ticketing. Fans typically carry the passport or ID that matches the name on the ticket.
- **Payment:** an EasyCard covers the MRT and most convenience stores. Merch booths and street stalls vary, so attendees usually carry some cash as well as a card.
- **The ride home:** A7 is a small station that empties a full arena. The queue on the platform stretches after the encore, and many fans wait out the first wave on the plaza.

## Where fans stay and eat

Most international visitors base themselves in Taipei, around Taipei Main Station. From there, one commuter ride covers the trip out and back. Others stay near Chang Gung Memorial Hospital (A8) or Linkou (A9). Those stations put you a short ride from the arena and away from the late-night crowds downtown.

Mitsui Outlet Park Linkou sits right at A9 Linkou Station. Fans use its food court as a meeting point before the show. If you are flying out the next morning, the airport is only minutes further down the same line. That makes Taoyuan one of the easier arena trips in Asia for anyone arriving from abroad.
