## A sandstone front on Hyde Park's edge

Start across the road in Hyde Park. From the corner of College and William Streets, the museum's sandstone front faces the fig trees. St Mary's Cathedral stands a short walk north along College Street.

This is Australia's oldest museum, founded in 1827. It has spent almost two centuries gathering zoological and anthropological collections. The building shows that history: old stone galleries at the core, newer glass and open floor space around them.

## The rooms to prioritise

The Long Gallery is the place to begin. It opened in the 1850s and is the museum's oldest exhibition space. It now holds 200 Treasures, a single long room of objects, specimens and portraits. The pieces are chosen to tell the story of the country and of the museum itself.

After that, head for the rooms most visitors come back for:

- **Dinosaurs**: full skeletons and casts, plus Eric, an opalised pliosaur from Coober Pedy whose bones turned to opal.
- **Minerals**: cases of crystals and gemstones under careful lighting, with a strong Australian focus.
- **Wild Planet**: a big, crowded run of animal specimens grouped to show biodiversity.
- **Garrigarrang: Sea Country**: a gallery on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander connections to coast and sea.
- **Skeletons**: a small, slightly eerie room of articulated skeletons, humans included.

Touring exhibitions change through the year and are usually ticketed separately. The official website lists what's on and how to book.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

The museum opens at 10am and closes at 5pm, seven days a week. There's no late night, so that window is all you get.

Weekdays are quiet right through opening hours, 10am to 5pm. That makes any weekday easier than any weekend. If Saturday or Sunday is your only option, arrive at 10am and see the busy rooms first. Weekends between 12pm and 4pm are the peak: families pack the dinosaur hall and queues form at the ticketed shows.

Most people spend two to three hours inside. If you're coming for a single gallery or a touring show, 90 minutes is enough. If you read every label, you'll use the whole day.

## Getting in without the fuss

General admission has been free since the museum reopened in 2020 after a major renovation. Special exhibitions are paid, and their current prices are on the official site. If a touring show is the reason you're coming, book a timed ticket online. On weekends those slots can sell out before you reach the desk.

A few habits make the day easier:

- Pay by card or phone. Tapping is standard across Sydney, and nobody expects a tip at the desk or the café.
- Large bags may need to be left at the cloakroom. Pack light so you can walk straight in.
- Flash photography is generally discouraged near fragile specimens. Check the signs in each gallery.
- School groups tend to arrive mid-morning on weekdays. Their noise moves around, so if one fills a gallery, come back to it later.

## Arriving by train, bus or on foot

The nearest train stations are Museum and St James, both on the City Circle line. From Museum Station it's a short walk across the southern end of Hyde Park to William Street. From St James you walk down the park's eastern side, past the ANZAC Memorial.

Buses along Oxford Street and Elizabeth Street stop within a few minutes' walk. From Circular Quay the whole walk takes around half an hour, going through the Domain or along Macquarie Street. Street parking nearby is tight and metered, so public transport is the easier choice.

## Before and after

The museum sits where the CBD meets the inner east, so it fits easily into a walking day. Some pairings:

- **Hyde Park and the ANZAC Memorial**: right across the road, and a good place to sit after a few hours inside.
- **St Mary's Cathedral**: a few minutes north along College Street, with a Gothic Revival sandstone front.
- **Art Gallery of New South Wales**: a walk through the Domain, if you still have energy for another museum.
- **Oxford Street and Darlinghurst**: east along William and Oxford Streets for cafés, bars and a meal once the museum closes at 5pm.
