Jingshan Park is an artificial hill built from the earth dug out to create the Forbidden City's moats, piled up directly behind the palace's north gate. That's the whole trick of the place: climb roughly 45 meters and the entire Forbidden City unrolls below in a straight axis of yellow roof tiles.

## The hill and the view

Five peaks run across the ridge, each topped with a pavilion built in the Qing dynasty. Wanchun Pavilion, the central and tallest one, is the photo everyone comes for: Forbidden City in the foreground, CBD skyline behind it, hills faintly visible on clear days. The climb itself is short, a series of stone stairs and switchbacks, but steep enough that it counts as the workout of the visit.

Skip the side pavilions if you're short on time. Wanchun alone gives the full panorama, and it's directly on the central north-south axis with the palace.

## Getting there and layout

The park's south gate faces the Forbidden City's Shenwumen (North Gate) directly across the street, making it the natural next stop after a palace visit. Line 8 subway's Shichahai station is a 15-minute walk northwest; Line 6's Beihai North station is closer, about 10 minutes.

The park covers 57 acres, laid out symmetrically around the central hill. Beyond the peaks, look for:

- Chonghua Palace and Fuxiang Pavilion, former imperial garden buildings on the east side
- The locust tree site near the east gate, marked as the spot where the last Ming emperor is said to have hanged himself in 1644
- Peony and lilac gardens flanking the lower slopes, best in April and May

## Timing the crowds

Weekday and weekend mornings between 7am and 11am are the quiet window, before tour groups arrive from the palace next door. Weekends turn busy fast, with crowds building from noon and holding until 10pm.

Sunrise and sunset are popular for photos precisely because the light hits the golden roofs at an angle, so expect company at the summit even in the early morning slot. Arrive right at opening if you want the pavilion platform to yourself for even a few minutes.

## Buying in and moving through

Entry is a small fee, paid at gates on the south, north, east or west sides; bring cash or be ready to scan a QR code, as most Beijing park ticket booths now favor mobile payment (Alipay or WeChat Pay) over card. Bag checks are routine at every gate, same as at the Forbidden City.

Most visitors enter from the south gate and exit north, or reverse it depending on which subway station they're headed to next. The park is well-visited enough that pairing it with an early Forbidden City visit, rather than treating it as a standalone trip, is how most people fit it in without doubling back across the city.

Locals use the park for morning tai chi and fan dancing near the lower gardens, mostly before 8am; it's worth a look even if the summit is your main goal.
