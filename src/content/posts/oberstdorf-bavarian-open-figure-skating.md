## Six days of skating at the end of the line

The 2027 edition runs from January 19 to 24. Oberstdorf is Germany's southernmost market town, ringed by the Allgäu Alps, and the railway simply stops here. In January the town is under snow, and skiers and skaters share the same streets.

The Bavarian Open is an international figure skating competition on the ISU calendar, ranked just below the Challenger Series. In earlier editions it drew skaters from across Europe and beyond across several age categories, many of them chasing qualifying scores. The organisers publish the official schedule, entries and ticket links on the event's website.

## The rink by the ski jump

Oberstdorf has trained figure skaters for decades. Its Eissportzentrum is a national performance centre for the sport, with several indoor ice surfaces under one roof. That setup makes the town a natural host: practice sessions and competition can run in neighbouring halls on the same day.

The ice centre sits on the south side of town, close to the Schattenberg ski jump used in the Four Hills Tournament. In the stands the air is rink-cold. Seats are close to the boards, so you hear the blades bite on every take-off and landing.

## Getting to Oberstdorf

There is no airport in town, so most visitors arrive by rail or road:

- **By train:** regional services run to Oberstdorf station via Immenstadt, with connections from Kempten and Munich. The trip from Munich takes roughly two and a half to three hours.
- **From the station:** the ice centre is about a kilometre south, a flat walk through the centre.
- **By air:** Memmingen is the closest airport. Munich and Zurich are bigger hubs, a few hours away by road or rail.
- **By car:** the B19 runs south from the A7 at Kempten. Winter tyres are a legal requirement on snowy German roads.

Visitors usually stay in Oberstdorf itself, where guesthouses and hotels are within walking distance of the rink. Those on a tighter budget often stay further down the valley in Fischen or Sonthofen.

## Watching like the regulars

Skating crowds have their own customs, and Oberstdorf's spectators follow them closely:

- **Applause:** people clap for jumps as they land and stay quiet in the stillness before a programme starts.
- **Throwing:** after a skate, fans throw flowers and soft toys onto the ice, wrapped so nothing breaks or sheds. Young skaters, often local club members, sweep them up.
- **Photos:** regulars switch off their flash during programmes, because sudden light can put a skater off mid-jump.
- **Clothing:** people sit in down jackets with a thermos and a cushion through long junior sessions.
- **Paying:** card is widely accepted in Germany, but small Allgäu cafés and snack counters still prefer cash, so a few euros in coins helps.

Between sessions, attendees often take the Nebelhornbahn cable car up for alpine views. Others walk the frozen Breitachklamm gorge, a short bus ride south, where waterfalls turn to ice columns in midwinter. Cross-country trails start at the edge of town. The pedestrian centre around the Marktplatz is where people usually eat Kässpatzen, the Allgäu's cheese noodles, after the evening free skates finish.
