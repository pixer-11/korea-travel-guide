## Wood, brass and a driver with a microphone

A restored tram rolls down Worcester Street at walking pace, its bell clanging as it nears a stop. Inside you sit on wooden seats under a painted ceiling, with windows that open onto the street. Up front, the driver works the controls and talks you through the city as it passes.

This is the Christchurch Tram, a heritage line that circles the central city. Trams first ran in Christchurch in the 1880s. The network shut in the 1950s, and the heritage loop opened in 1995. It closed after the 2011 earthquakes and came back in 2013, and the route has since been extended through the rebuilt centre.

## The loop and what you see from it

The full circuit takes roughly an hour if you stay on board. It passes the places most visitors want to see in the centre anyway. Highlights from the window include:

- **Cathedral Square**, with the earthquake-damaged ChristChurch Cathedral, which is being restored
- **Worcester Boulevard**, the Gothic Revival stretch toward the Arts Centre
- **The Arts Centre and Canterbury Museum**, both close to the Botanic Gardens on Rolleston Avenue
- **New Regent Street**, the pastel row of 1930s shops
- **The rebuilt retail blocks** around Cashel Street and the Riverside Market area

The commentary is live and changes from driver to driver. Expect earthquake stories next to Victorian history, plus tips on where to get off. It suits a first day in the city, because you get your bearings before you choose where to spend time on foot.

## New Regent Street: the narrowest stretch of the route

The tram's best moment is New Regent Street. This short pedestrian street was built in the early 1930s in the Spanish Mission style. Its two facing rows of small shops are painted in soft colours, with curved gables and tiled detailing.

The rails run straight down the middle, so the tram slides between café tables and shopfronts with not much room to spare. Pedestrians step aside and the bell rings. Most people photograph the street from inside the tram, so get off at the nearby stop and take your shot from the pavement. Then sit down for a coffee and wait for the next tram to come through the frame.

## Catching the 5pm–6pm lull

Timing changes this ride more than almost anything else. The measured quiet window is 5pm–6pm, on weekdays and weekends alike. Seats are easier to get then, and late light falls along Worcester Boulevard.

Try not to board between 10am and 5pm on weekends. That's the busiest stretch, when carriages fill and you can end up standing at the back, away from the narration. Weekday mornings are a reasonable alternative if evenings don't suit.

There's one catch. The tram currently runs 8:30am–6:30pm, and a full loop takes about an hour, so the last complete circuit leaves before closing. Check the last departure time at the stop or on the official site before you plan around 5pm. These hours are this week's schedule and can shift, especially on public holidays.

## Hopping on and off without fuss

The ticket is a hop-on, hop-off pass. Most people make the mistake of riding the loop once and walking away. Buy your ticket through the official Christchurch Tram website or where it's sold at the main stops. Current fares are posted there, and they're also where you'll learn how long a ticket stays valid.

A few habits make the day easier:

- **Do one full loop first.** Listen to the whole commentary, then use later rides to get between the places you picked.
- **Wait at the marked stops.** The tram won't pick up between them, so find the signed stop nearest to you.
- **Keep your ticket handy.** You'll show it each time you get back on.
- **Let people off first.** The doors and aisles are narrow, and the crew will ask you to step clear.
- **Sit near the front for the narration.** Toward the back, the street noise takes over.

Tipping isn't expected in New Zealand. A thank-you to the driver as you get off is normal. Card and contactless payments are standard across the city, though it's worth checking what the tram accepts when you buy.

## Where to get off

Each stop puts you within a short walk of somewhere worth an hour or more. Good places to break the ride:

- **Canterbury Museum and the Botanic Gardens**, side by side on Rolleston Avenue
- **The Arts Centre**, a precinct of restored Gothic Revival stone buildings with courtyards, shops and galleries
- **Cathedral Square**, for the cathedral restoration and the city's central library, Tūranga
- **The Avon River (Ōtākaro)**, which you can walk beside, or punt on from near the Antigua Boatsheds
- **Riverside Market**, an indoor food hall that works well for lunch

A sensible day might go like this. Ride the loop in the morning. Get off at the museum and walk through the Botanic Gardens. Pick the tram back up for lunch near the market, then go for a last spin in the quieter late afternoon.

## Planning the ride

Allow about an hour for a single loop. Give it half a day or more if you're using the pass properly. The tram runs year-round, but the open windows make it a draughty ride in a Canterbury winter, so bring a layer from May to August.

The Worcester Street stop at number 109 is close to Cathedral Square, so it's an easy place to start if you're staying in the centre. Central Christchurch is flat and compact. Between the tram and your own feet, you won't need a car for this part of the city.
