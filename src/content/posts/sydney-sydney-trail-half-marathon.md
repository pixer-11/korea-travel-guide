## A half marathon in the bush, inside the suburbs

Parramatta Lake surprises most visitors. It is a reservoir held back by a curved stone dam from the 1850s, with sandstone ridges and eucalypt bushland closing in on the water. Parramatta's office towers sit only a few kilometres south.

The Sydney Trail Half Marathon is held here on 12–13 December 2026, a Saturday and Sunday. Runners come from interstate and abroad for a trail race that stays inside metropolitan Sydney. The reserve's tracks follow the lakeshore and the Hunts Creek valley, so you get dirt, rock and roots rather than tarmac.

## Getting to Lake Parade

The reserve's main entrance is on Lake Parade, North Parramatta. Parramatta Station is the nearest rail hub, served by Sydney Trains' T1 Western Line and other lines, and it sits about 3km south of the lake.

- **From the city:** trains from Central to Parramatta take roughly 25–35 minutes, depending on the service.
- **From Parramatta Station:** most people take a taxi or rideshare, or walk about 40 minutes north through North Parramatta.
- **By car:** the reserve has parking off Lake Parade. It fills quickly on busy summer weekends, so runners usually car-pool.

The organiser publishes the venue details and race logistics on its official site.

## Running in a Sydney December

December is early summer in Sydney. Heat and humidity shape the race more than the distance does. Bushland trails trap warmth once the sun clears the ridges, and shade comes and goes along the sandstone sections.

Runners at summer trail events here usually pack:

- a soft flask or hydration vest, even if the course has aid stations
- trail shoes with grip for sandstone, which turns slick when damp
- sunscreen and a cap
- electrolytes, because humid mornings drain salt fast

Spectators tend to gather near the lake's edge, close to the picnic areas and the summer swimming spot. That area has the clearest view of runners coming in.

## Race-weekend habits at Lake Parramatta

Trail-running culture in Sydney is relaxed but strict about the bush. Runners stay on marked tracks, carry their rubbish out and step aside on narrow single-track so faster runners can pass. On these trails people call "on your right" as they come past.

Interstate and overseas runners usually base themselves in Parramatta rather than the CBD. Hotels sit near Church Street and the river, and the late-night food strip along Church Street covers carb-loading the evening before.

The reserve stays open to the public on race weekend, so expect walkers, families and swimmers alongside the event. Locals often stay on after the finish to cool off in the lake's designated swimming area during summer. Payment around Parramatta is almost entirely by card or phone. Tipping is not expected at cafés.

Afterwards, many visitors walk the 3km south to Parramatta Park, a World Heritage-listed site where Old Government House stands. It is an easy recovery stroll with history attached.
