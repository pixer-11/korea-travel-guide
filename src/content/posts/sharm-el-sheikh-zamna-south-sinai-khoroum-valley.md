## Getting to Sharm El Sheikh

Most international visitors arrive through Sharm El Sheikh International Airport (SSH). It sits north of the main resort strip and has direct flights from many European cities. From Cairo, people usually take a short domestic flight rather than the long road trip across Sinai.

Where people usually stay:

- **Naama Bay:** the walkable centre, with a seafront promenade, bars and late-night food.
- **Sharks Bay and Nabq:** quieter resort areas closer to the airport.
- **Hadaba and the Old Market area:** further south, near the Old Market's mosque and souk lanes.

Attendees usually get from their hotel to the valley by hotel transfer, private driver or taxi. Desert festivals of this kind often add their own shuttles from the main hotel areas.

## Dressing for a desert night in November

November is one of the most comfortable months in Sharm. Days are warm and sunny, often warm enough to swim. Once the sun goes down the desert cools quickly, and the interior valleys get noticeably colder than the coast.

What festival-goers usually pack:

- A warm layer or light jacket for after dark
- Closed shoes that can handle sand and loose stones
- Sunglasses, sunscreen and a hat for daytime
- A scarf or buff against dust when the wind picks up
- A refillable water bottle, since the dry air dehydrates people quickly

## Getting around Sharm on a Zamna weekend

A few local habits make a festival weekend easier. Taxis in Sharm often don't use meters, so locals and regulars agree the fare before the car moves. Hotel desks can arrange drivers at a set rate, and for a late return from the desert that is what most visitors do.

Egypt runs on the Egyptian pound. Resorts and larger restaurants take cards, but drivers, porters and small cafés mostly deal in cash. Tipping, known locally as baksheesh, is normal for drivers, hotel staff and anyone who carries bags, so small notes are useful.

Sharm is relaxed about beachwear and festival clothes inside resorts and at events. In the Old Market and around the mosque, people cover their shoulders and knees. Greeting someone with "salaam alaikum" or saying "shukran" (thank you) goes a long way.

## Making a week of it

Visitors often build a festival into a longer stay, and Sharm makes that easy. Ras Mohammed National Park, at the southern tip of the peninsula, has some of the Red Sea's best-known reefs and is a standard day trip by boat or road. Tiran Island's reefs are a regular snorkelling and diving trip from Sharm's marinas.

For those who prefer the interior, St Catherine's Monastery and Mount Sinai are a few hours' drive into the mountains. Visitors there traditionally climb Mount Sinai overnight to reach the summit for sunrise. It's the same granite landscape as Khoroum, seen at dawn instead of under festival lights.
