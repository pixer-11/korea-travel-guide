## What you're looking at

The building itself is the first exhibit: a sweeping black-gold-blue roofline meant to evoke calligraphy brushstrokes, designed by Hong Kong architect Kris Yao. It sits on reclaimed wetland in Taibao City, Chiayi County, ringed by a constructed lake and terraced gardens.

Inside, the focus is pan-Asian rather than purely Chinese. Permanent galleries rotate through Buddhist sculpture, Ming and Qing ceramics, Tibetan Buddhist art, tea culture, and textiles from India, Persia and Southeast Asia. This is the sister site to Taipei's National Palace Museum, built to spread visitors and artifacts outside the capital.

Give yourself two to three hours minimum; the permanent collection alone spans multiple floors, and special exhibitions often add another wing.

## Planning your visit

Hours run Tuesday through Sunday, 9am to 5pm on weekdays and until 6pm on Saturday and Sunday. The museum is closed every Monday, no exceptions.

It's a very popular stop on the Chiayi circuit, so weekends draw real crowds between 11am and 6pm. Arriving at opening, or coming on a weekday, gets you the galleries with breathing room.

- Address: No. 888, Gugong Blvd, Dongshi Village, Taibao City, Chiayi County
- Closed Mondays year-round
- Weekend hours extend one hour later than weekdays

## Getting there without a car

Chiayi High Speed Rail (HSR) Station is the practical gateway; the museum is roughly 15 minutes away by road. A free or low-cost shuttle bus typically runs between the HSR station and the museum grounds, timed loosely to train arrivals.

Taxis from the HSR station are quick and inexpensive given the short distance. If you're coming from Chiayi's older Train Station (the TRA line into the city center), factor in extra transfer time to reach the HSR station first, since the two Chiayi stations sit apart from each other.

Drivers will find parking on site; the museum's grounds were built with car and tour-bus access in mind.

## Making the most of the grounds

This isn't a building to duck into and out of. The surrounding Story Land wetland park and lake paths are landscaped for a slow loop, and locals and visitors alike use them for photos of the roofline reflected in the water.

Because the site draws heavy weekend footfall, treat lunch strategically: the on-site restaurant and nearby vendor areas get busy in the same 11am–6pm window as the galleries. Eating slightly earlier or later smooths that out.

Photography is generally allowed in most permanent galleries but restricted in special loan exhibitions, so check signage at each entrance rather than assuming blanket rules. The gift shop near the main entrance stocks reproductions and design goods worth a slower browse if you've got a layover before an HSR train.
