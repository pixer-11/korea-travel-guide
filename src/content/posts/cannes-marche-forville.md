## Under the roof on Rue du Marché Forville

Cannes shops for dinner here before most visitors have had breakfast. Marché Forville is one long covered hall, with rows of stalls running under the roof and trestle tables stacked with whatever Provence is growing that week.

The produce and flowers are the main event. Around them you'll find fishmongers with the morning's catch on ice, cheese counters, olives, and stalls selling herbs by the bunch. It's one of the most-reviewed places in Cannes and consistently well rated, so don't expect to have it to yourself.

## Timing the morning

Plan around lunch, because that's when the market packs up. At the moment it runs:

- **Monday:** 8am–4pm
- **Tuesday–Friday:** 8am–12:30pm
- **Saturday–Sunday:** 8am–1:30pm

On weekdays it's calmest from 8am to 9am. Monday is the only day it stays open into the afternoon, and 1pm–2pm on a Monday is also quiet. Try not to arrive between 10am and 1pm at the weekend. That's when the aisles fill and the queues at the popular stalls stretch out.

Sixty to ninety minutes is enough to walk every row, buy picnic supplies and get a coffee nearby. If you come late you'll find the stalls half empty and the best fish already sold, especially Tuesday to Friday.

## Buying like the regulars

Forville runs on the same habits as any Provençal market, and following them changes how vendors treat you.

- **Greet first.** Say "Bonjour" before you point at anything. Skipping it reads as rude.
- **Don't handle the produce.** At most stalls the vendor picks for you. Say how many, or ask for "un kilo" or "une barquette".
- **Look for "producteur."** These signs mark growers selling their own crops, as opposed to traders reselling stock.
- **Carry a bag and some coins.** Some stalls take cards, but many smaller sellers prefer cash. Prices are written on the labels or chalkboards at each stall.
- **Ask for a taste.** Cheese and olive sellers will often give you a sliver before you buy.

Shopping for later? Ask for "pour ce soir" (for tonight) or "pour demain" (for tomorrow). Fruit sellers will pick ripeness to match.

## Around the market

The hall sits where the old town meets the modern centre, so it's easy to fit into a morning walk.

- **Le Suquet:** Climb the lanes above the market to the old castle and Notre-Dame-de-l'Espérance for views over the bay and the Lérins Islands.
- **Rue Meynadier:** This pedestrian shopping street starts close by and has delicatessens and bakeries.
- **Vieux Port and the Palais des Festivals:** Both are a short walk downhill. Take your market food to the benches along the harbour.
- **Gare de Cannes:** The train station is roughly a 10–15 minute walk east through the centre.

Coming by car, use the public car parks near the port rather than looking for a space on the streets around the market.
