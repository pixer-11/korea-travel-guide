## Why this show matters

Twenty years after their debut, G-Dragon, Taeyang, and Daesung open their anniversary tour at home. Goyang Stadium hosts all three opening nights — August 21, 22, and 23, 2026 — before the tour moves on to 31 stadium dates worldwide.

For fans, this isn't just another stop. It's the first stop, in the city where it all restarts.

## Getting to Goyang Stadium

Goyang sits just northwest of Seoul, easily reached by subway or bus. The stadium is officially known as Goyang Stadium (also called Goyang Sports Complex), and it regularly hosts large-scale concerts and sporting events.

- Take Seoul Subway Line 3 toward Daehwa and get off near the stadium area, then transfer to a local bus or taxi for the final stretch.
- On concert nights, expect shuttle buses and extra bus routes laid on for crowd control — check local transit apps closer to the date.
- Driving in is possible but parking fills fast; arriving 2-3 hours early is wise if you're set on a spot.

Because exact gate numbers and shuttle details usually surface only weeks before a show, treat any pre-2026 transit advice as a starting point, not gospel.

## Tickets and how to book

Ticketing for a tour of this size typically opens in waves — a fan-club presale first, then a general public sale. With three nights at one venue, expect tiered pricing based on seat block (floor standing versus stadium seating).

Do not buy from secondary resellers before the official on-sale. Watch YG Entertainment's official social channels and the tour's official ticketing partner for South Korea, since dates, prices, and seating maps have not been finalized publicly as of this writing.

## Where to stay and eat nearby

Goyang has its own hotel and guesthouse options, but many international fans base themselves in Seoul and commute in for the show, since Goyang is only a short subway or bus ride from the capital.

If you'd rather stay closer:

- Ilsan (the district containing the stadium) has mid-range hotels and serviced apartments within a short ride of the venue.
- Lake Park (Ilsan Lake Park) is a pleasant daytime detour if you're in town early — good for killing pre-concert hours.
- Restaurants around Ilsan's commercial streets serve Korean barbecue, fried chicken, and late-night snacks — useful if you're leaving the stadium hungry after a night show.

## When to go and how long to stay

Plan for a minimum of three to four days if you want to catch multiple nights or simply build in a buffer, since stadium concerts can run late and public transit thins out afterward. August in Goyang is hot and humid, so pack for outdoor heat if the show has an open-air component.

Arrive in the city at least a day before your first show to sort out transit logistics and ticket collection, especially if you're new to the area.

## How to visit like a local

Korean concertgoers tend to arrive early, but not absurdly so — most fans start queuing outside 2-3 hours before doors, not at dawn, unless it's a numbered fan-club entry line. Payment at the venue and nearby vendors is almost entirely card or mobile pay (Korean mobile wallets and major international cards both work), so cash isn't essential.

Merchandise lines move separately from entry lines — decide ahead of time whether you're queuing for goods or for your seat, since doing both same-day means picking a priority. Light sticks (official fan-light versions) are commonly used at K-pop stadium shows, and buying one in advance saves an on-site queue.

The most common mistake tourists make is assuming Goyang is remote from Seoul — it isn't. Treat it as a regular subway commute rather than budgeting a whole travel day for it. Also confirm your specific show date and gate assignment the week of the concert, since large fan-club presales can shift general seating logistics even after tickets are secured.
