## What Merci actually is

Merci is not another Uzbek teahouse with tapestries and low tables. It's a fusion restaurant built around two cuisines that rarely share a menu here: European plates and Japanese sushi, served under a ceiling and walls painted in saturated blue and bubblegum pink.

That color scheme is the reason half the photos of this place circulate online before the food does. Locals mention it as a landmark description on its own: "the blue-pink place," not the street address.

The menu pairs steak cuts with sushi rolls, an unusual combination in a country where fresh raw fish is not a culinary tradition. That contrast, rather than any single dish, is what people are actually talking about.

## Why it stands out in Samarkand

Samarkand's dining scene runs heavily toward Registan-adjacent teahouses and Silk Road-themed interiors aimed at tour groups. Merci breaks from that formula entirely.

There's no attempt at "traditional" decor here. The bold color palette and the fusion menu read as deliberately modern, aimed at a local and regional clientele as much as visitors.

- Interior: saturated blue walls, pink accents, a look built for photos
- Menu concept: European dishes and Japanese sushi on the same table
- Signature draw: steak and sushi pairings, unusual for the region
- Crowd: younger, trend-driven diners rather than tour buses

This is the kind of place that spreads through word of mouth and social feeds rather than guidebook listings, which is exactly how it's built its name.

## Booking a table

Merci is new enough, and buzzy enough, that walk-ins are a gamble on weekend nights. Call ahead or book through whatever platform the restaurant currently lists, and reconfirm your reservation the day before.

Hours, contact details, and menu pricing for a recently opened spot like this can shift quickly. Check the restaurant's current listing or call directly before you set out, rather than relying on secondhand information.

If you're combining it with sightseeing, treat it as a dinner stop after a day at Registan Square or Gur-e-Amir, not a lunch break between monuments; fusion tasting menus like this reward a slower, evening pace.

## Ordering and etiquette here

Treat Merci like a modern fusion restaurant anywhere, not a traditional Uzbek eatery. There's no floor seating or communal tea ritual to navigate.

- Expect table service and a printed or digital menu split between European and Japanese sections
- Card payment is standard at newer Samarkand restaurants catering to this crowd, but carry some cash as backup
- A modest tip, if not already included in the bill, is appreciated but not obligatory in Uzbekistan
- Dress a little sharper than you would for a teahouse; the interior invites it, and so does the clientele

Ask staff to recommend which sushi rolls pair well with the steak dishes if you're unsure; that pairing is the restaurant's whole identity, and the people working there know the combinations that actually work.
