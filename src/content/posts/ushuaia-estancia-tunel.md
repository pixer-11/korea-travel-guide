## A ranch at the end of the coast path

Estancia Túnel sits where Ushuaia's eastern edge gives way to open shoreline. It is a historic sheep-farming estancia from Tierra del Fuego's settler era, and it now gets listed as a historical landmark. It does not work like a ticketed museum. What you come for is the walk out to it, along the north shore of the Beagle Channel.

It gets far fewer visitors than the Ushuaia waterfront or Tierra del Fuego National Park. On the trail you mostly meet other hikers, not tour groups.

## Walking out from Playa Larga

Most people start at Playa Larga, a pebble beach a short taxi or drive east of the city centre. From there a marked footpath follows the coast toward the estancia. It runs through low southern beech forest and comes out onto open headlands above the water.

What you'll see along the way:

- Wind-bent lenga and guindo trees, with the trunks leaning away from the channel
- Rocky coves with kelp washing up at the tideline
- Views across the Beagle Channel to the mountains of Chile's Isla Navarino
- Seabirds on the rocks offshore, which you'll see most often near the points

Parts of the path are churned peat and roots, and they stay wet long after rain. Expect mud on most days, not only after a storm.

## Getting your timing right

Give the out-and-back walk half a day, and more if you stop often for photos or birds. The trail is fairly level but uneven underfoot, so it suits anyone used to forest walking.

Ushuaia's long summer days, from roughly December to February, give you the most room. In winter the light goes early, and snow or ice can cover the path. In any season the weather on the channel shifts fast, so pack layers and a windproof shell even if the morning starts calm.

No opening hours are published for the estancia itself. Ask at Ushuaia's tourist office on the waterfront about current trail conditions and access before you go.

## Walking private land the Fuegian way

People here treat estancias as working properties first. You are crossing land someone owns, so the norms are simple and they matter:

- **Stay on the marked path.** Gates and fences are there for livestock. Close any gate you open.
- **Don't go into the buildings or yards** unless someone invites you in. A historic estancia is still somebody's home and workplace.
- **Carry everything out.** There are no bins along the coast, and the wind scatters litter into the water.
- **Bring your own food and water.** You won't find a café on the trail, so stock up in Ushuaia.
- **Greet anyone you meet** with a plain "buen día". It goes a long way in rural Tierra del Fuego.

Some local operators run kayak or guided outings along this part of the coast. They are worth a look if you'd rather see the estancia from the channel. Ask in town about who is running trips this season, because offers change from year to year.
