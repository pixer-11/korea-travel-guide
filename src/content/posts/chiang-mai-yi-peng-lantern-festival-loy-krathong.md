## Two nights under the full moon

Chiang Mai lights up from the river to the sky. On the water, thousands of small krathongs drift south on the Ping: rafts cut from banana trunk, folded from banana leaf, and finished with marigolds, a candle and three sticks of incense. Overhead, paper khom loi rise slowly, each one a hot-air balloon lit by a fuel ring of wax-soaked paper.

The festival was set for **November 24-25, 2026**. The lunar calendar sets the dates, so they shift every year.

## Yi Peng and Loy Krathong are not the same thing

Two festivals share the same nights. Loy Krathong is celebrated across Thailand on the full moon of the twelfth Thai lunar month. People float krathongs to thank the water spirits and to let go of bad luck.

Yi Peng is the northern Lanna version. The name means "full moon of the second month", because the old Lanna calendar counts differently from the central Thai one. Chiang Mai marks it with lanterns of every kind:

- **Khom loi**: the sky lanterns that rise
- **Khom kwaen**: hanging lanterns strung over lanes and temple gates
- **Khom thue**: lanterns carried on a stick in processions

The Old City's lanes and temple grounds are traditionally strung with lanterns, with parades and fireworks along the river.

## Where the lanterns go up

Most of the festival is free and happens in public spaces. These are the main places:

- **Wat Phan Tao**, on Phra Pokklao Road beside Wat Chedi Luang: a teak viharn whose grounds are lit with candles and lanterns. Monks often lead lantern ceremonies here.
- **Tha Phae Gate**: the brick east gate of the Old City, the main gathering point and a focus for stage events.
- **Nawarat Bridge and the Iron Bridge (Khua Lek)**: the best spots to watch krathongs on the Ping and lanterns over the water.
- **Tha Phae Road**: the route that links the gate to the river and fills with parade traffic.

The largest krathong parade traditionally runs on the final night. Its floats are lit with bulbs and carry dancers in Lanna dress.

## Tickets: free streets versus paid releases

The city celebrations need no ticket. Ticketed events are separate, privately run mass releases held on open grounds outside the city, historically north of town around Mae Jo, San Sai and Doi Saket. Thousands of people let their lanterns go at the same signal, and that is the scene in most of the famous photographs.

These packages usually bundle transport, a seat, a lantern and a meal. Sellers vary in quality. Each organiser set its own date, venue, pickup point and prices.

## Launching a khom loi without starting a fire

This is the skill nobody explains. In recent years the city has limited lantern releases to set zones and time windows, because lanterns land on roofs and temples and drift into flight paths.

A safe launch follows a few simple rules:

- Two people work together: one holds the paper top up while the other lights the fuel ring.
- The lantern is let go only once it pulls upward on its own. Releasing it early drops it into the crowd.
- Trees, power lines and thatched roofs catch lanterns easily, so open sky overhead matters.
- Lanterns are not launched in a strong wind, and never near Chiang Mai International Airport.

Many people make a wish and say it silently before they let go.

## Floating your krathong the Chiang Mai way

Vendors set up along the riverbanks selling krathongs. Banana trunk and bread versions break down in the water; foam ones do not. Street vendors usually take cash.

The candle and incense are lit first, at the top of the bank. At the water's edge, people crouch and set the krathong down gently rather than tossing it. Some locals tuck in a coin, a hair or a nail clipping as a token of letting go. Couples often float one together.

Temple etiquette is the same as on any other day. Shoulders and knees are covered, and shoes come off at the door of a viharn. Voices stay low near monks during chanting, and people step aside when a procession comes through.

## Getting around a city at standstill

Walking is the easiest way around. Traffic in the Old City and along the river slows to a crawl on festival nights, and many roads close for parades. The Old City is compact, and it is a straightforward walk from Tha Phae Gate east to the Ping.

Red songthaew trucks and ride-hail cars such as Grab work well before dusk, but waits are long afterwards. Rooms inside the moat or near the river are within walking distance of the main sites.

Flights at Chiang Mai International Airport are usually rescheduled or cancelled during the main release windows. Rooms across the city typically fill early for these nights.
