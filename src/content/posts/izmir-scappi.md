Nine floors above Izmir's waterfront, inside the Swissotel Büyük Efes, a dining room named after a sixteenth-century Italian chef has become the reservation everyone in the city is suddenly trying to make.

Bartolomeo Scappi cooked for popes in Renaissance Rome and wrote one of the first great printed cookbooks, the kind of figure whose name a hotel restaurant borrows when it wants to signal ambition rather than novelty. That's the joke and the pitch of Scappi: old-world Italian cooking, delivered from a room with a very modern, very Aegean view.

## The room and the view

Scappi sits on the ninth floor of the Büyük Efes, one of Izmir's landmark hotels overlooking the Gulf of Izmir. The floor-to-ceiling outlook over the bay is the restaurant's defining feature, and it's what people are posting about as word spreads.

Sunset is the obvious hour to angle for. As the light drops over the water and the city's waterfront promenade lights up below, the dining room is doing exactly what its ninth-floor position was built for. Ask for a window-facing table when you book, since not every seat gets the same angle on the gulf.

## Why the name matters

Naming an Italian restaurant after Bartolomeo Scappi is a deliberate flex. He was chef to Pope Pius V and author of Opera, a landmark Renaissance cookbook that documented Italian court cooking in unusual technical detail for its time.

A hotel restaurant that reaches back to him is telling you it wants to be judged on technique and history, not just on having "Italian" in the description. Whether the kitchen fully lives up to that reference is worth judging for yourself, but the ambition is the point of the branding.

Given the buzz and the limited number of window tables on a single ninth floor, reservations matter here more than they would at a larger, longer-established restaurant.

- Ask specifically for sunset timing when you call, since that's the window everyone wants and it will book out first.

The Swissotel Büyük Efes sits centrally in Izmir, within easy reach of the city's Konak waterfront district and the broader Alsancak area, both worth combining with a Scappi dinner if you're spending an evening downtown.
