## A dome on the hill

The ride up takes five minutes. The Wellington Cable Car climbs from Lambton Quay to Kelburn, and from the top station a short path through the Botanic Garden brings you to the observatory's white dome at 40 Salamanca Road.

Carter Observatory has stood here since 1941. Today it runs as Space Place, an astronomy museum with a planetarium, hands-on exhibits and a historic telescope. It is consistently well rated, and it is one of the city's best-known evening outings, so plan for company.

## Inside: planetarium, exhibits, the Cooke

The planetarium is the core of the visit. You recline under the dome while the southern sky is projected overhead. Shows typically cover:

- the Southern Cross and the Magellanic Clouds, which you can't see from most of the northern hemisphere
- Matariki, the Māori name for the Pleiades, whose rising marks the Māori New Year
- the planets and missions beyond the solar system

The exhibit floor has interactive displays on the solar system, space travel and Polynesian wayfinding by the stars. The centrepiece is the Thomas Cooke refracting telescope, a 19th-century instrument still used for public viewing when the sky is clear. Allow 1.5–2 hours, including one show.

## Timing your night under the southern sky

Evenings are the point here. Currently Space Place opens 5pm–10:30pm on Tuesday and Wednesday, 10am–10:30pm on Thursday, and 10am–11pm on Friday and Saturday. Sunday it closes earlier, at 5pm, so there is no night viewing. It is closed on Monday. Museum hours shift through the year and on holidays, so check the official site for this month's times.

Measured foot traffic shows weekends are busiest from 10am to 8pm. Try not to arrive then if you want unhurried time at the exhibits. A weekday evening gets you both the dark and the telescope.

Wellington's weather decides the telescope session. If cloud or wind comes in off Cook Strait, the planetarium still runs, but there may be no live viewing.

## Getting the dome seat you want

Planetarium shows run at set times and seats are limited, so book a session online before you ride up. Weekend shows are the first to sell out. Tickets and current prices are on the official Space Place website and at the front desk.

- **Paying:** card and contactless are normal in New Zealand. Tipping isn't expected.
- **Getting there:** take the Cable Car up from Cable Car Lane, off Lambton Quay. After a late session, walk back down through the Botanic Garden or take the Cable Car if it is still running. Check the last car before you go in.
- **Driving:** there is limited parking on Salamanca Road and around Kelburn, and the Cable Car is simpler.
- **Pair it with:** the Cable Car Museum next to the top station, the Kelburn lookout over the harbour, or a daylight walk down through the garden to the Lady Norwood Rose Garden.

Bring a warm layer even in summer. The hilltop is exposed, and telescope viewing means standing outside in the evening air.
