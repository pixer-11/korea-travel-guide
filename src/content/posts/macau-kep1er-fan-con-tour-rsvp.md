## A fan-con under Big Ben

The Londoner Macao is hard to miss. Its frontage copies the Houses of Parliament, clock tower and all, and it faces the Cotai Strip across from The Venetian. On November 21, 2026, Kep1er brings the 'RSVP: Respond Our Code' fan-con tour to The Londoner Theatre inside the resort.

The tour name works like an invitation, and a fan-con is a more personal kind of night than a stadium show. The usual format mixes stage performances with talk segments, games and moments played straight to the crowd. Kep1ians who know the fan chants tend to get the most out of it.

## Who Kep1er are

Kep1er came out of Mnet's survival show *Girls Planet 999* in 2021. They debuted in January 2022 with "Wa Da Da", a bright, drum-heavy single that remains a staple of their setlists and their fans' chant repertoire.

The group draws strongly across Greater China and Japan, which makes Macau a natural stop. Fans travel in from Hong Kong, Zhuhai and Guangdong as well as from within the city. Expect plenty of Cantonese and Mandarin in the foyer, and plenty of light sticks.

## Reaching Cotai

Macau has two halves for visitors. The old peninsula has the Ruins of St. Paul's and Senado Square. Cotai is the reclaimed strip between Taipa and Coloane where the big integrated resorts stand shoulder to shoulder, and The Londoner sits right among them.

Most international fans arrive one of these ways:

- **From Hong Kong by ferry:** boats run to the Taipa Ferry Terminal, the closest ferry port to Cotai.
- **From Hong Kong by road:** buses cross the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge to the Macau port on the artificial island.
- **From Zhuhai:** on foot through the Border Gate or the Lotus checkpoint at Hengqin.
- **From Macau International Airport:** a short taxi or bus ride, since the airport is on Taipa.

The Sands resorts, The Londoner included, have long run free shuttle buses from the ferry terminals and border crossings. The Macau Light Rapid Transit Taipa line also runs through Cotai, with stations within walking distance of the resort strip. Taxis queue at the resort's own rank.

## Kep1ian etiquette inside The Londoner

A few habits make the night smoother for everyone.

**Under 21? Mind the casino floor.** Macau's legal age for entering gaming areas is 21, and staff enforce it. Younger fans walk the shopping arcades and corridors instead, which connect most of the resort without crossing the gaming floor.

**Cameras stay away from the tables.** Photographs on casino floors are not allowed. The Big Ben façade, the red phone boxes and the Crystal Palace-style atrium are all fair game, which is where fan selfies usually happen.

**Recording rules belong to the show.** Fan-cons set their own phone and camera policy. Staff and on-screen notices at the doors state what applies, and most fans keep phones down during talk segments out of courtesy.

**Paying is easy.** The Macau pataca is the local currency, and Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted. Inside the resorts, cards and mobile wallets such as Alipay, WeChat Pay and MPay work almost everywhere. Small Taipa eateries tend to prefer cash.

**Freebies and fan projects.** Fan-run gift tables and banner projects are a familiar sight at K-pop fan-cons. Fans usually organise these through fan-club social accounts and hand items out away from the venue entrance to keep doorways clear.

## The Cotai walk after the encore

This is where Macau differs from most tour stops. When the show ends, you don't leave the venue for a dark car park. You walk straight into a resort district that stays lit and open late.

Covered footbridges link The Londoner with The Parisian Macao and its half-scale Eiffel Tower, and with The Venetian's canal-side shopping arcade. The resort food courts are the default after-show stop for big groups. They sit near the cheaper end of Cotai dining, with noodles, Macanese pork chop buns and Portuguese egg tarts.

Fans with more time head to Taipa Village, a short ride away. Its lanes of low pastel houses and Rua do Cunha snack stalls are the classic place for almond cookies and serradura. These Macanese treats double as souvenirs for the trip home.

## Making a weekend of it

November 21, 2026 falls on a Saturday. Many travelling fans make a full weekend of it. November is one of Macau's more comfortable months, generally drier and milder than the humid summer.

The Macau Grand Prix usually runs in mid-November on the peninsula's Guia street circuit, and hotels across the city fill up around it. Fans who stay in Cotai keep the theatre within walking distance. Those who stay on the peninsula save money but add a bus or taxi across the bridges.

A second day fits the peninsula well:

- **Ruins of St. Paul's** and the Mount Fortress beside it
- **Senado Square** and its black-and-white wave paving
- **A-Ma Temple**, one of the city's oldest
- **Macau Tower**, for the view back over Cotai
