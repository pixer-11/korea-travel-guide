## Tiles, towers and a bridge over 4 Street

Look up before you walk in. The building is clad in glazed terracotta tiles that curve like the body of a guitar. Allied Works Architecture designed it, and it opened in 2016 as the permanent home of the National Music Centre.

There are two towers. A skybridge over 4 Street SE links them, and it connects to the restored King Edward Hotel. The King Eddy was a well-known Calgary blues bar for decades, and it is now part of the complex.

## What fills the five floors

The editorial line is simple: exhibits on Canadian music history, hands-on instrument areas and concerts. In practice that means:

- **TONTO**, the giant analog synthesizer from the 1970s whose name stands for The Original New Timbral Orchestra
- **The Rolling Stones Mobile Studio**, the recording truck used on landmark rock albums
- **Hall of fame galleries** covering Canadian music, country music and songwriters
- **Instrument zones** where you can play drums, keys and guitars, plus booths to try your voice

The galleries climb through the towers, so a full circuit means stairs, elevators and the bridge. Leave two to three hours if you plan to play anything.

## Playing the instruments, not just reading the labels

The hands-on rooms are the reason to come, so treat them that way.

- **Book online.** Tickets are sold through the official National Music Centre site, and that is where the current price is posted.
- **Go early in your visit to the play areas.** School groups often come on weekday mornings during term, so starting with the instrument rooms beats a class arriving.
- **Take turns at the stations.** One person plays and others wait, so a short go is the norm.
- **Use headphones where provided.** Several stations are set up for private listening.
- **Check the event calendar.** Concerts and talks happen in the performance spaces. Evening music at the King Eddy runs separately from museum hours, so confirm times before you go.

At the moment the museum opens 10am–5pm all week, with no short days. Arrive by early afternoon at the latest. A late start means hurrying past the parts you came to play.

## Getting there and the blocks around it

Studio Bell is a short walk east of downtown. The closest CTrain stop is City Hall / Bow Valley College, served by the Red and Blue lines on 7 Avenue SE. From there, walk south and east into East Village.

East Village is worth pairing with your visit:

- **Calgary Central Library**, the Snøhetta-designed building a few blocks north, free to walk through
- **The Bow River RiverWalk**, a paved path along the water north of the museum
- **St. Patrick's Island**, a park reached by a footbridge off the RiverWalk

If you are coming for the Calgary Stampede in July, Stampede Park is just south across the Elbow River. Expect the whole area to be much busier that week.
