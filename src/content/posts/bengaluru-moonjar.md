## Eight drinks, one moon

The menu follows a lunar cycle. MoonJar's idea is the pull between the moon and the ocean, the same force that drives the tides. It builds its cocktail list around that theme: eight drinks, each inspired by a phase of the moon.

Astronomers count eight phases, from new moon through crescent, quarter and gibbous to full and back again. Reading the list in order works like a small story. The bar hasn't published a fixed menu we can quote, so let the staff walk you through the cycle when you sit down.

## Why low-ABV matters here

These drinks are lighter than a typical cocktail. The base spirits are soju, vermouth and wine, not the gin, rum or whisky most Bengaluru bars lead with. Soju is the clear Korean spirit, softer than vodka. Vermouth is a fortified, aromatised wine. Both carry flavour without much alcohol.

In practice, you can work through two or three phases of the moon and still hold a conversation. It also suits a city where most evenings start late and run long. If you plan to try more than a couple of drinks, low-ABV is the format that lets you do it.

## Finding the door in Cambridge Layout

Look for Nila first. MoonJar sits on the floor above Chef Rahul Sharma's restaurant, so the restaurant is your landmark. Cambridge Layout is a residential pocket of east Bengaluru, wedged between Halasuru (Ulsoor) and Indiranagar.

- **Metro:** Halasuru station on the Purple Line is the closest stop. Indiranagar station, one stop east, is an option if you're coming from that direction.
- **By road:** autos and cabs booked through Uber, Ola or Namma Yatri are the easiest way in after dark. Share your pin, because the lanes here can confuse drivers.
- **Nearby:** Ulsoor Lake is a short ride away, and 100 Feet Road in Indiranagar is close enough for dinner before or after.

Bengaluru traffic peaks in the early evening. A trip that looks like ten minutes on the map can take far longer around 6pm to 8pm, so leave a buffer.

## Booking a seat at a 28-seat bar

Twenty-eight seats fill fast. MoonJar is newly opened, and new bars in east Bengaluru get a lot of attention in their first months. A small room like this can be full on a weekend night before most people have finished work.

- **Reserve ahead.** Check MoonJar's or Nila's official channels for how bookings work. We haven't been given hours, so confirm them there too.
- **Carry photo ID.** The legal drinking age in Karnataka is 21, and bars do check.
- **Watch for dry days.** Karnataka stops alcohol sales on election days and some public holidays, and bars close or serve food only.
- **Read the bill.** Card and UPI payments are standard in Bengaluru bars. Some places add a service charge, so check before you leave an extra tip.

Turning up as a walk-in group of six is the riskiest way to visit. Pairs and small groups have a much better chance of getting in without a booking.

## Pairing it with Nila downstairs

You don't have to leave the building. Since MoonJar sits directly above Nila, the obvious plan is dinner downstairs and drinks upstairs, or the other way round. Booking both at once saves you from doing it twice.

Lighter drinks also go well with a full meal. A vermouth or wine-based cocktail sits more easily alongside a long dinner than a strong spirit-forward pour. Ask when you book whether the two venues coordinate tables. Policies at new openings change quickly.

## Who it suits

MoonJar is built for conversation. A 28-seat room with an eight-drink list is not a party bar. It works best for a date, a catch-up between two or three friends, or a solo drinker who likes asking bartenders how a drink was made.

If you want loud music, a big crowd and a long spirits list, Indiranagar's larger bars are minutes away. Come here when you want to taste something unusual, work through a few phases of the moon and still remember them the next morning.
