Most visitors to Bukit Timah beeline for the main Nature Reserve entrance and its steep summit trail. Rifle Range Nature Park is the quieter cousin next door, a restored patch of secondary forest and old quarry land that used to be a British Army rifle range and later a series of granite quarries before it was rehabilitated into parkland.

That history is still visible if you know where to look. The old quarry has been turned into a wetland with a boardwalk running through it, and the park's design leans hard into water: retention ponds, a series of terraced pools, and channels that were engineered to slow runoff before it reaches the reservoir catchment downstream.

## Finding the entrance on Rifle Range Road

The park address is simply Rifle Range Rd, Singapore 589322, and the entrance is easy to miss if you're driving past at speed. There's a small car park near the entrance, though it fills up fast on weekends.

Public transport works well too: the nearest MRT is Hillview on the Downtown Line, from which it's a walk of around 15 to 20 minutes along Hillview Road and then Rifle Range Road. Taxis and ride-hailing apps can drop you directly at the entrance gate.

## What the boardwalk actually shows you

The signature feature is the elevated boardwalk that loops through the old quarry wetland, keeping your feet dry while you look down into ponds thick with reeds and the occasional monitor lizard sunning on the bank. Further in, the trail climbs gently into secondary rainforest that's been left to regenerate since the quarrying stopped decades ago.

Keep an eye out for:

- Long-tailed macaques, usually more interested in each other than in you
- Dragonflies and damselflies over the wetland pools, especially mid-morning
- Squirrels and the occasional monitor lizard along the quieter forest paths
- A three-storey timber tower structure near the park's edge, built as a lookout over the wetland

The park connects directly into the Bukit Timah Nature Reserve trail network, so if you're feeling ambitious you can walk straight through and tackle the reserve's hillier paths without backtracking to a car.

## When to time your visit

Rifle Range Nature Park is open every day from 7am to 7pm, and the quietest stretch is 7am to 8am on weekdays, when it's often just you, a few joggers, and the macaques. Weekends are a different story: crowds build steadily from 8am and stay heavy until around 1pm as families and hiking groups arrive.

If you want the boardwalk without company, a weekday dawn start is the move. Arriving right at 7am also means cooler air, which matters here since there's very little shade over the open wetland sections.

## Getting the most from a short visit

A loop through the wetland boardwalk and back takes about 30 to 45 minutes at an easy pace, enough to see the ponds, the lookout tower, and a stretch of regenerated forest. Budget 1.5 to 2 hours if you plan to continue on into the main Bukit Timah Nature Reserve trails.

Wear proper walking shoes rather than sandals. The boardwalk itself is flat and easy, but any onward trail into the reserve has exposed roots, loose granite gravel, and can turn slick after rain.

## Blending in on the boardwalk

This isn't a park with food stalls, vendors, or anything to buy, so there's no tipping or transaction etiquette to navigate. Bring your own water and snacks; the nearest shops are back toward Hillview MRT.

Because it's still relatively under-the-radar compared to the main Bukit Timah entrance, you won't find big tour groups here, mostly local runners, birdwatchers with long lenses, and families with young kids on the flat boardwalk sections. Keep noise down near the ponds if you're hoping to spot wildlife, and don't feed the macaques; it's both discouraged and the fastest way to have your snacks stolen.

Toilets and basic signage exist near the entrance, but this remains a functional nature park rather than a polished visitor attraction, which is exactly why it stays calm even on a Saturday afternoon.
