## Twelve on one stage

The title spells out the idea. MIRROR is the 12-member Cantopop group formed through ViuTV's talent show *King Maker* in 2018. It became the biggest homegrown act Hong Kong had produced in a generation. "12 AS 1" points to the group as a unit, not to its members' solo careers.

The run covers December 11-20, 2026, a ten-day window at the end of the year. Expect a stage built for scale: wide LED screens, choreography for a dozen performers, and an arena filled with fans holding lightsticks in a single colour.

## AsiaWorld-Expo and the ride out

The venue is a long way from the city centre. AsiaWorld-Expo sits at Chek Lap Kok, on the airport island off Lantau. Concerts are held in its large convention halls, which are flat-floored and hangar-sized, not tiered stadium bowls. Sightlines depend heavily on the seating plan, and the big screens matter more than they would at the Hong Kong Coliseum.

Getting there:

- **Airport Express:** from Hong Kong Station (Central) via Kowloon and Tsing Yi, to AsiaWorld-Expo Station, one stop past Airport. The exit leads straight into the venue.
- **Tung Chung Line:** cheaper, but you finish at Tung Chung and take a bus or taxi across to the airport island.
- **Taxi:** Lantau (blue) taxis and urban (red) taxis both serve the airport area. Queues build at the stand after the show.

From Central, the Airport Express ride takes roughly half an hour.

## Sleeping and eating near Chek Lap Kok

Out-of-town fans often skip the trek back to Kowloon. The Hong Kong SkyCity Marriott is the closest hotel to AsiaWorld-Expo. The Regal Airport Hotel and the hotels at Tung Chung are one stop or a short ride away.

Within the venue, food is limited to concession stands. Most attendees eat beforehand:

- **Hong Kong International Airport terminals:** a full spread of cha chaan teng, noodle and fast-food counters.
- **Citygate Outlets, Tung Chung:** the mall's restaurant floors and food court, next to Tung Chung MTR.
- **Tung Chung town:** local dim sum and noodle shops on the estates around the station.

## Moving with the Mirrorer crowd

MIRROR fans call themselves Mirrorers, and the crowd is organised. Fan clubs set up support stands and hand out free merch such as banners, fans and stickers near the entrance. Many attendees come in time to collect these before doors. Chants and member-name calls happen in Cantonese, and they follow cues in the songs.

Practical habits regulars follow:

- **Lightsticks:** the official MIRROR lightstick is the standard. Many fans also carry slogan towels for individual members.
- **Octopus card:** most fans use it for MTR fares, convenience-store snacks and taxis. Load it in the city, because the machines at AsiaWorld-Expo Station get crowded after a show.
- **The exit surge:** thousands of people head for the same platform at once. Some fans stay in their seats a few minutes for the crowd to thin. Others walk out during the final song.
- **Photography rules:** these come from the organiser and are printed with ticket terms. Phones raised for long stretches block the view for rows behind on a flat floor.

Bag checks happen at entry. Large bags slow the line, so fans who travel light get in faster.
