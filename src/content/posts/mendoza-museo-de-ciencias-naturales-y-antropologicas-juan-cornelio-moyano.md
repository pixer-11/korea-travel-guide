## A museum at the end of a lakeside walk

Mendoza's natural history museum is part of a park outing. It stands on Av. las Tipas inside Parque General San Martín, the huge planted park on the city's western side. The lake is right beside it, with the Rosedal rose garden close by.

Most visitors arrive on foot under the park's shade trees, often with a picnic or a bike ride planned for later. The museum is named after Juan Cornelio Moyano. It is one of the most-reviewed attractions in Mendoza and consistently well rated, so expect company, especially families.

## What's behind the doors

The collection covers two big subjects, and both are rooted in the region.

- **Natural sciences:** fossils and paleontology, plus the animals and plants of the Andes and the dry Cuyo plains around Mendoza.
- **Anthropology and archaeology:** the peoples who lived in these valleys before and after the Spanish arrived.
- **Displays for all ages:** the building is modern and the exhibits are pitched at school groups as much as at adults, so children keep up.

Most labels are in Spanish. A translation app with camera mode is the easiest fix if you don't read it. If you are planning a trip into the high Andes, the regional fauna rooms are a useful primer for what you might see out there.

## Getting to Av. las Tipas

The museum is about 3–4 km west of Plaza Independencia, past the park's ornate wrought-iron gates, the Portones del Parque. You have a few options:

- **Taxi or ride-hailing app:** the simplest choice. Ask for "el Museo Moyano, en el Parque, al lado del lago."
- **Bicycle:** Mendoza is flat, and bike paths run through the park.
- **Walking:** from downtown, head west along Avenida Emilio Civit and keep going through the gates into the park. It takes roughly 45 minutes.
- **Local bus:** several lines run to the park. You pay with a SUBE card, which you buy and top up at kiosks, not on board.

## Timing your visit

Check the day before you set off. The museum is currently closed on Mondays, and on Sunday it opens only at 2pm, so a Sunday morning plan won't work. Tuesday to Saturday it runs 9am–7pm.

Museum hours shift through the year and on public holidays, so look at the official site for this month's schedule. Weekday mornings are when school trips usually come through. Arriving at 9am or in the mid-afternoon often means fewer groups in the galleries.

Give yourself 1.5–2 hours for the exhibits. Mendoza summers are hot and dry, and an air-conditioned museum at the hottest part of the day makes sense before you go back out to the lake at dusk.

## Doing it the Mendocino way

The museum is one stop in a day spent in the park, and it works best planned that way.

- **Greet staff on the way in.** A "buen día" or "buenas tardes" at the desk is expected in Argentina.
- **Check the admission price at the entrance.** It is posted there and on the official site. Ask which payment methods are accepted before you queue, and carry some pesos in case.
- **Bring a mate thermos for afterwards, not inside.** Locals sip mate on the lawns around the lake. Leave food and drink outside the galleries.
- **Follow the siesta rhythm.** Many shops in town close for a few hours after lunch. The museum stays open through the afternoon from Tuesday to Saturday, which makes it a good place to spend that stretch.
- **Expect noise from school groups.** If a class fills a room, skip ahead and come back to it.

## Around the lake and up the hill

The rest of the day is a short walk away. The Rosedal, the park's formal rose garden, sits by the lake, and the shoreline paths are where Mendoza jogs, rows and walks its dogs.

Farther west, Cerro de la Gloria rises above the park. It is topped by the bronze Monumento al Ejército de los Andes, which honours San Martín's crossing of the Andes. The road climbs steeply, so most people take a taxi up and walk down.

The Ecoparque, the former city zoo, and the Estadio Malvinas Argentinas are also inside the park. Together they can fill an easy half day without leaving the trees.
