## A designer's table on Thonglor

Thonglor runs north from Sukhumvit as one long, busy road. Jakawaan sits just off Soi 9, one of the side lanes branching from it. The street is known for cocktail bars, Japanese restaurants and community malls. A small Thai kitchen led by a food stylist is a different reason to make the trip.

The person behind it is Vipittichak "Nick" Pitthayanont, who works as a food stylist and designer. Food styling means deciding how a dish looks before anyone eats it. Expect that eye to shape how plates are put together and how a set arrives at the table. Jakawaan opened recently and people have been talking about it quietly, mostly by word of mouth.

## What samrub means, and why it matters here

A *samrub* (สำรับ) is the traditional way of serving a Thai meal. Instead of one main per person, rice comes with a set of dishes that balance one another. A typical spread mixes:

- something rich, like a curry
- something sharp or sour, often a soup
- a relish such as *nam prik* with vegetables for dipping
- something fried or stir-fried for texture

Jakawaan builds structured samrub set menus around this idea. That makes it a good choice if you want to see how a Thai meal fits together, rather than ordering pad thai and green curry by habit. We weren't given the dishes in the current sets, and they may change. Ask what's in the set when you sit down.

## Small plates or the full set

The menu offers two ways to eat. The tapas-style bites suit a lighter evening. You can share a few between two people, perhaps before moving on to a Thonglor bar. The samrub sets are the more considered choice: a complete meal planned so that each dish plays against the others.

If it's your first visit, the set is the better introduction to what the kitchen is trying to do. Groups of three or four can try both, ordering a set and adding some small plates around it.

## Getting up Thonglor

The nearest rail stop is **Thong Lo station** on the BTS Sukhumvit Line. Leave the station on the north side of Sukhumvit Road and head up Thonglor, which is Sukhumvit Soi 55. The side lanes are numbered as you go north, so Soi 9 is some way up the road and not right beside the station.

- **Walk** if the heat allows. The pavement is uneven in places, so allow extra time.
- **Motorbike taxis** in coloured vests wait at the mouth of Thonglor and will run you up the road fast.
- **Grab or a metered taxi** is easiest at night or in the rain. Evening traffic on Thonglor can crawl, though, so leave room in your schedule.

## Booking, ordering and paying at Jakawaan

Before you go, look up the current hours and how reservations work. New places in Bangkok often change their schedule in the first months, and a small dining room can fill up with a single large group. Most new Bangkok restaurants post updates and take bookings through social media or messaging apps. Look there first and send a message a day or two ahead.

At the table, eat the Thai way. Hold the spoon in your right hand and the fork in your left, and use the fork to push food onto the spoon. Dishes in a samrub are shared. Take a little from one plate at a time and eat it with rice, instead of piling everything on at once. If you're unsure about spice levels, ask the staff before ordering. Thai kitchens are used to that question.

Some Thonglor restaurants add a service charge to the bill, so read it before you tip. Leaving small change on top is a polite, common gesture, not an obligation. Check how they take payment when you book. Thai QR payments are everywhere, but not every foreign card works with them.

## Making an evening of the neighbourhood

Jakawaan is a natural first stop for a night on Thonglor. Further up the road are two community malls. **J Avenue** sits by Soi 15 and **The Commons** by Soi 17, a terraced complex of food stalls and bars where people linger late. Both are a short hop north of Soi 9.

If you'd rather keep the night quiet, the parallel road of **Ekkamai** to the east has its own cafés and bars. A taxi gets you there in minutes. Plan around two hours for an unhurried samrub dinner, a little less if you only stop for small plates.
