## What's on and where

Art Basel Paris takes over the Grand Palais from October 23 to 25, 2026. The venue is the restored 19th-century glass-and-steel hall on Avenue Winston Churchill, between the Champs-Élysées and the Seine.

Expect modern and contemporary galleries from across the world showing painting, sculpture, photography and installation under the Grand Palais's vast iron-and-glass nave. The fair's Paris edition has grown into one of the anchor stops on the international art calendar, alongside Basel and Miami Beach.

Public days typically follow invitation-only preview days, a pattern the fair has kept in prior editions. Confirm the exact split between preview and public days on the official site closer to your visit.

## Getting there and getting in

The Grand Palais sits a short walk from Champs-Élysées–Clemenceau, served by Metro lines 1 and 13. RER C stops at Invalides, roughly ten minutes on foot across the Pont Alexandre III.

- Nearest metro: Champs-Élysées–Clemenceau (lines 1, 13)
- Alternate stop: Invalides (RER C, lines 8, 13)
- Bus: several lines stop along Avenue Winston Churchill and Cours la Reine
- Driving is not recommended; the surrounding streets close for pedestrian flow during fair days

Tickets are sold by timed entry slot in past editions, with separate categories for preview and public days. Buy directly through the official Art Basel Paris site; resale tickets carry no guarantee of entry.

## Planning your visit

Two to three hours covers a focused walk through the main galleries; serious collectors and researchers often return across multiple days. The Grand Palais also runs a public program of talks and film alongside the gallery booths in past editions, worth checking on arrival.

Cloakrooms handle coats and bags above a certain size; large backpacks and rolling luggage are typically restricted from the floor. Wear shoes you can stand in for hours: the nave floor is stone and there are few seating areas inside the booths themselves.

October in Paris runs cool and often wet, so a layer for the walk from the metro matters more than what you wear inside.

## Moving through the Grand Palais without losing the day

Galleries cluster by section, and the fair map (available at entry and online) groups exhibitors by sector rather than alphabetically, so decide your priorities before you're inside. Mornings on public days tend to move slower than the final afternoon, when browsing thins out and dealers are more open to conversation.

- Check the official Art Basel Paris site for the current floor plan and exhibitor list before you go
- Book timed tickets in advance; the fair does not guarantee walk-up entry
- Cash isn't needed inside; card and contactless cover the on-site cafés and bookshop
- If you want to speak with a gallery representative at length, midweek public hours are calmer than the closing day

Confirm timing, ticket categories, and any last-minute venue changes on the official Art Basel Paris site before finalizing plans.
