## What's on and where

Art Basel Paris was set for the Grand Palais from October 23 to 25, 2026. The venue is the restored 19th-century glass-and-steel hall on Avenue Winston Churchill, between the Champs-Élysées and the Seine.

The fair brings modern and contemporary galleries from across the world, showing painting, sculpture, photography and installation under the Grand Palais's vast iron-and-glass nave. The fair's Paris edition has grown into one of the anchor stops on the international art calendar, alongside Basel and Miami Beach.

Public days typically follow invitation-only preview days, a pattern the fair has kept in prior editions.

## Getting there and getting in

The Grand Palais sits a short walk from Champs-Élysées–Clemenceau, served by Metro lines 1 and 13. RER C stops at Invalides, roughly ten minutes on foot across the Pont Alexandre III.

- Nearest metro: Champs-Élysées–Clemenceau (lines 1, 13)
- Alternate stop: Invalides (RER C; Metro lines 8, 13)
- Bus: several lines stop along Avenue Winston Churchill and Cours la Reine
- Driving is impractical; the surrounding streets close for pedestrian flow during fair days

In past editions, tickets were sold by timed entry slot, with separate categories for preview and public days. Tickets for 2026 were sold through the official Art Basel Paris site; resale tickets carry no guarantee of entry.

## Planning your visit

Two to three hours covers a focused walk through the main galleries; serious collectors and researchers often return across multiple days. Past editions have included a public program of talks and film alongside the gallery booths.

Cloakrooms typically handle coats and bags above a certain size, and large backpacks and rolling luggage are usually restricted from the floor. Comfortable shoes matter: the nave floor is stone and there are few seating areas inside the booths themselves.

October in Paris runs cool and often wet, so a layer for the walk from the metro matters more than what you wear inside.

## Moving through the Grand Palais without losing the day

Galleries cluster by section, and the fair map (available at entry and online) groups exhibitors by sector rather than alphabetically. Mornings on public days tend to move slower than the final afternoon, when browsing thins out and dealers are more open to conversation.

- The floor plan and exhibitor list are the quickest way to set priorities in a fair this size
- Timed tickets are the norm; the fair has not guaranteed walk-up entry
- Cash isn't needed inside; card and contactless cover the on-site cafés and bookshop
- Midweek public hours are typically calmer than the closing day for longer conversations with gallery representatives

With a clear sense of which sectors matter most, a single afternoon covers the highlights of the nave.
