## What you're actually looking at

This is a working retail fish counter, not a theater set. Since the 1930s it has sold salmon, crab, halibut and shellfish from a stall at 86 Pike St, the corner where Pike Place Market's main arcade meets the street.

The famous bit: when a customer orders a whole salmon, a monger yells the order across the counter and another catches it, bare hands, over the ice display. It happens dozens of times a day, not on a schedule. Stand near the scale end of the counter, that's where the catches land.

## Why the crowds show up

At 4.6 stars across nearly 4,500 reviews, this is one of the most consistently well-rated stalls in the market, and one of its most photographed corners. It's well-known citywide, not an insider spot, so don't expect to have it to yourself.

Weekends from 10am to 5pm are the peak: tour groups, cruise-ship crowds, and everyone with a phone out for the throw. If you just want the photo and the noise, that's prime time. If you want to actually see the counter and talk to a monger, it isn't.

## Getting the quiet version

The market itself opens at 7am and the fish counter runs the same 7am to 5pm hours daily, all week. Weekday mornings between 7am and 10am are the calmest stretch; weekend mornings tighten that window to 7am and 9am before it fills in.

Arrive at opening on a weekday if you can. You'll get:

- A clear line of sight to the ice display and the scale
- Mongers who have time to talk about what's fresh
- No shoulder-to-shoulder crowd blocking the arcade behind you

By late morning on any day, especially weekends, the aisle in front of the stall narrows to a shuffle.

## Buying, shipping, and not just gawking

Nobody owes you a show for free; the fish on ice is for sale, and buying something, even a pound of smoked salmon or a crab cake to eat on the spot, is the polite way to justify camping out with a camera.

- The stall packs fish for checked-bag travel and can ship fresh or frozen seafood to addresses in the US
- Card and cash are both accepted at the counter
- If you want the throw, ask what's fresh and order a whole fish rather than a filet; whole fish is what gets tossed
- Give the crew room to work; the space behind the counter is a real workstation, not a stage

Pair the visit with the rest of Pike Place Market around it, the produce stalls, the original Starbucks a block away, and the lower levels' shops, since the fish market is one stop among many in a market built for a slow wander, not a five-minute photo stop.
