## A whole industry in one building

This museum is about one tree and everything people took from it. Kauri, pronounced roughly "KOH-ree", once covered much of Northland. The exhibits trace how pioneer settlers felled it, milled it and dug up its fossilised resin. The timber became houses, boats and furniture. The gum went into varnish, and some of it was carved.

Expect these highlights:

- **Giant trunk sections** that show the full width of a mature kauri
- **The gum collection**, with polished amber-coloured kauri gum, some pieces lit from behind
- **Sawmilling machinery** and displays on bush logging
- **Kauri furniture and pioneer domestic rooms**, showing what settler life in Matakohe looked like

The museum began as a local centennial project in 1962. That's why it reads as a community's own record of its history.

## Getting to Matakohe from the Bay of Islands

Matakohe is a small settlement just off State Highway 12, between Brynderwyn and Dargaville. From Paihia, take SH1 south past Whangārei. Then go west on SH12 at Brynderwyn, or cut across through Dargaville. Either way, allow around two hours each way.

No practical public transport serves the village, so you need a car or a tour. Church Road runs off the highway, and the museum is signposted. Matakohe makes a natural stop on a loop between Auckland and Northland. Many visitors break the Paihia–Auckland drive here instead of making a separate day trip.

## Timing your visit in Matakohe

The crowd data is consistent across the week. It's calmest from 9am–10am and from 3pm–5pm, on weekdays and weekends alike. Try not to arrive between 10am and 2pm on weekends. That's the peak, when coaches and families pack the gum room and the machinery halls.

The opening hours change through the year, so check the official site for this month's times before you go. At the moment it opens 9am–5pm every day. If you arrive at 3pm, you get two quiet hours, enough for the main halls but tight for the whole collection. If you arrive at 9am, you're done before the weekend rush peaks.

Some practical habits:

- Buy tickets at the front desk. Current prices are on the museum's official website. Cards are standard across New Zealand.
- Tipping isn't expected in New Zealand.
- Read the panels in the gum room. The difference between dug gum and bled gum is what explains the industry.
- Leave time for the shop, which stocks kauri wood and gum pieces.

## Carrying on up the Kauri Coast

The museum sits at the southern end of what Northland calls the Kauri Coast. Continue north on SH12 through Dargaville to Waipoua Forest. There you can see Tāne Mahuta, the largest living kauri, after the museum has explained what you're looking at. That stretch adds roughly another hour and a half.

Near Dargaville, Kai Iwi Lakes is a swimming and camping stop along the same route. From Waipoua, the road winds on to the Hokianga Harbour and Ōpononi. From there you can loop back to Paihia through Kaikohe, which makes a full circuit of the region in one long day or two easier ones.
