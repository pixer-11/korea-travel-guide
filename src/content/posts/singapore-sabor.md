## Why Sabor is worth knowing about

Chijmes has hosted restaurants and bars for years inside its former convent chapel and courtyard, but Sabor gives the space a new identity: Spanish tapas, served communal-style, from the same team behind Duomo Ristorante. That pedigree matters in Singapore's restaurant scene — Duomo has built a reputation for polished Italian dining, and Sabor is their bet that the same hospitality instincts translate well to sherry, jamón, and small plates meant for sharing. Having opened in April 2026, it's still finding its rhythm, which is part of the appeal: the energy of a genuinely new opening rather than an established institution coasting on reputation.

## Where it sits, and why the setting matters

Sabor occupies a spot within Chijmes, the restored 19th-century convent-turned-lifestyle complex on Victoria Street in the Civic District, a short walk from City Hall and Bras Basah MRT stations (both served by multiple lines, including the North-South and East-West lines via City Hall). The neo-Gothic chapel building, with its vaulted ceilings and stained glass, anchors a courtyard that's long been one of downtown Singapore's more atmospheric al fresco settings — string lights, old stone walls, and a hush that feels removed from the traffic on Bras Basah Road just outside the gates. Sabor's alfresco seating leans into that setting, making the space itself as much a draw as the food.

## What to expect at the table

Expect classic Spanish tapas built for sharing: think the kind of small-plate repertoire — croquetas, jamón, pintxos-style bites, grilled seafood, patatas — that rewards ordering several dishes across a table rather than one main each. The communal-dining format is deliberate, encouraging groups to order widely and pass plates around, which suits Chijmes' social, evening-out atmosphere. Because the menu and pricing are still settling in as a new opening, it's worth checking Sabor's own channels or calling ahead for the current lineup and any signature dishes that emerge as house favorites — new Spanish restaurants in Singapore often refine their most-ordered plates within the first few months.

## Getting there

The easiest approach is on foot from Bras Basah or City Hall MRT stations, both a five-to-ten-minute walk away. Grab or taxi drop-off is straightforward given Chijmes' central location, and there's metered parking and nearby car parks for those driving in, though weekend evenings around the Civic District can mean a short walk from the car regardless. Chijmes itself has several entrances; the Victoria Street gate is the most recognizable, opening into the courtyard where Sabor's alfresco tables are set.

As a newly opened, trending spot, expect weekend nights to draw a crowd quickly; a weekday dinner offers a calmer, more conversational pace at the table. ## How to visit like a local

Book ahead rather than walking in, especially for dinner on a Friday or Saturday — new, buzzy openings in prominent locations like Chijmes tend to fill up fast, and a reservation avoids a wasted trip. If you can't get a table, an early weekday dinner (around opening, before the after-work crowd arrives) is typically the quietest window. Order tapas-style as intended: three to four dishes per two people is a reasonable starting point, adding more as the table decides rather than committing to a fixed multi-course meal upfront. Payment in Singapore restaurants is overwhelmingly cashless — card and mobile payment (PayNow, Apple Pay) are standard, and service charge is usually already built into the bill, so additional tipping isn't expected or necessary. The most common visitor mistake at Chijmes venues generally is treating it purely as a photo backdrop and rushing the meal — the courtyard rewards lingering over the tapas format, so budget more time than you would for a quick dinner elsewhere in the Civic District.
