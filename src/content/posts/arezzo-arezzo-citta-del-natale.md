## Three venues on one hill

Arezzo's Christmas festival climbs the town. Città del Natale runs from November 14, 2026 to January 6, 2027. It uses three spaces stacked up the hill of the old centre: Piazza Grande, Parco Il Prato and the Fortezza Medicea at the top.

That layout shapes the visit. Most people do it as one uphill walk, starting among the market stalls and finishing at the fortress walls. It covers less than a kilometre, but most of it is uphill.

## Piazza Grande and its Alpine chalets

Piazza Grande is the centre of the festival. It is a sloping, uneven square, paved in stone and noticeably steeper at one end. Giorgio Vasari's long arcaded Palazzo delle Logge closes off the top side. The stone apse of the Pieve di Santa Maria backs onto the lower corner.

The Tyrolean-style market brings a little South Tyrol into Tuscany. Markets in this style usually mean:

- wooden chalet stalls set in rows
- mountain food such as speck, strudel and sausages
- mulled wine, called *vin brulé* in Italian
- handmade decorations and gifts

The square's slope means the stalls sit on a tilt, and the view down the rows changes at every step. Visitors also spot the arcades and medieval tower houses from the Oscar-winning film *Life Is Beautiful*, which used this square as a location.

## Up to the Fortezza and Il Prato

Parco Il Prato is the open green space on the hilltop. It lies between Arezzo Cathedral and the fortress. In summer it's a lawn for picnics. During the festival it becomes part of the walk from the market up to the Fortezza Medicea, and it adds open ground for light displays.

The Fortezza Medicea is a 16th-century Medici fortress with angled, star-shaped bastions. Antonio da Sangallo the Younger, one of the leading military architects of his day, was involved in its design. Its ramparts look over the Val di Chiana and the hills of eastern Tuscany. Light shows on its brick walls and arched gateways are the reason many visitors save the climb for after dark.

The cathedral is on the way and worth a stop. It holds Piero della Francesca's fresco of Mary Magdalene.

## Getting up the hill

Arezzo station sits on the main Florence–Rome railway line. Regional trains from Florence Santa Maria Novella take roughly an hour, and many visitors come as a day trip from Florence. From the station, Corso Italia climbs straight up through the shopping streets to the old centre. Piazza Grande is a short turn off its upper end.

Drivers mostly avoid the restricted traffic zone, the ZTL, that covers the historic centre. Many use the Pietri car park instead. From there, a chain of public escalators carries you up inside the hill to the cathedral area, close to Il Prato and the Fortezza. That makes a top-down route easy: start at the fortress and walk down to the market.

## Moving through the chalets like an Aretino

Christmas markets in Italy are as much about the evening stroll, the *passeggiata*, as about shopping. Families from Arezzo and the surrounding valley do slow laps of the square. They stop for a cup of *vin brulé* and then carry on towards the fortress. Keep to the flow along the rows of stalls rather than stopping in the middle.

A few habits make it easier:

- **Paying:** card and phone payments are common, but small food stalls handle quick cash sales more easily.
- **Ordering:** say what you want in plain words. *Un vin brulé, per favore* works at any drinks counter.
- **Cups:** some markets charge a returnable deposit on a souvenir mug. Hand it back at the stall, or keep it as a memento.
- **Footing:** the stone of Piazza Grande gets slippery when it's wet, especially on the steep upper side.

Weekends in December bring day-trippers from across Tuscany and Umbria. Weekday evenings, in comparison, tend to feel more local. The festival continues after Christmas, and the days up to Epiphany on January 6 have a festive, holiday feel. For dinner, the streets around Piazza Grande have long served Aretine dishes such as *pici* and grilled meats.

## Dates, tickets and what's free to wander

The 2026–27 edition runs from November 14, 2026 to January 6, 2027. The three venues are Piazza Grande, Parco Il Prato and the Fortezza Medicea. The market spaces are public squares and parkland in the middle of town.

For any attractions that charge entry, the organiser publishes ticket details on the festival's official site. Daily opening times for the market and light shows are listed there too, so this guide doesn't print them. Most visitors give the festival an afternoon and an evening. That allows time for the market in daylight and the fortress after dark.
