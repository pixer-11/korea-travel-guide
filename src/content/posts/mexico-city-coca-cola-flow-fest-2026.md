## Reggaeton on a racetrack

This festival takes over a Formula 1 circuit. Coca-Cola Flow Fest is Mexico City's large gathering for reggaeton, Latin urban music and hip-hop. The 2026 edition takes place in November at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, the same track that hosts the Mexico City Grand Prix.

The grounds are flat and very large, with asphalt, paddock areas and long walkways between stages. The circuit also holds the stadium long known as Foro Sol. Distance is part of the experience here: moving between stages takes real walking, so people pick their spot for the headline sets well before those sets begin.

## Dates, tickets and the November double bill

Flow Fest 2026 runs in November 2026. It shares the Autódromo with Corona Capital, the city's indie and rock festival, in the same month. That gives Mexico City two very different crowds on the same circuit within a few weeks.

OCESA, the promoter behind the big Autódromo festivals, sells its tickets through Ticketmaster México. The festival's own site and social channels carry the dates, the lineup and the ticket tiers. This guide names no artists or prices, because both belong to the organiser, not to us.

## Reaching the Autódromo

The circuit sits inside the Magdalena Mixiuhca sports complex in Iztacalco, east of the city centre. **Metro Line 9** (the brown line) runs along its northern edge, so it is the obvious way in.

- **Ciudad Deportiva** and **Velódromo** stations (Line 9) are the closest stops for festival gates.
- **Puebla** station (Line 9) is another walkable option.
- Line 9 crosses Line 1 at **Pantitlán** and connects toward Line 2 and Line 3 at **Chabacano** and **Centro Médico**.

Ride-hailing apps such as Uber and DiDi work across the city. After the final set, demand jumps and nearby streets clog. Many attendees walk a few blocks away from the circuit before ordering a car, or they take the Metro while it is still running.

## Dressing for a November night at altitude

Mexico City sits at about 2,240 metres. Festival days in November usually start bright and dry, and the high-altitude sun burns faster than visitors expect. Once the sun sets, the temperature falls quickly.

Regulars usually bring:

- a light jacket or hoodie they can tie around their waist
- sunglasses and a cap for the afternoon sets
- shoes that can handle hours on asphalt and packed grass
- a fully charged phone, since phones double as tickets and wallets

The thin air makes a long day harder than it would be at sea level. Fans who drink water between sets and eat properly usually last through the headliners.

## How the crowd moves at Flow Fest

The atmosphere comes from the crowd. Reggaeton audiences in Mexico City sing every word, and the big stages fill up long before the main acts. Fans who want a spot near the barrier usually claim it several sets early and stay there, with friends taking turns on food and bathroom runs.

Practical habits that make the day easier:

- **Paying:** card and contactless payment are standard at large OCESA venues, and many people keep a little cash for the street stalls outside.
- **Meeting points:** phone signal often weakens in big crowds, so groups pick a fixed landmark, such as a sign or a stage screen, before they split up.
- **Language:** basic Spanish helps at the bars and with security. "¿Dónde está la salida?" means "where is the exit?" and is worth knowing at night.
- **Bags:** large festivals here usually limit bag size and search everyone at the gate. The festival site sets out what is allowed.

## Where fans base themselves

Hardly anyone stays next to the circuit. The area around it is sports grounds and residential streets with few hotels. Visitors usually stay in **Roma** or **Condesa**, which are full of restaurants and bars. Others choose the **Centro Histórico**, close to Line 2 and the connection to Line 9.

The weekend leaves time for the city. Before an afternoon start, many visitors fit in a morning in **Coyoacán** or at the **Museo Nacional de Antropología** in Chapultepec. Late tacos al pastor in Roma or Condesa are how a lot of fans end the night.
