## Six voices, one capital city

NMIXX's first world tour comes to Madrid. *Episode 1: Zero Frontier* is the JYP Entertainment group's debut headline tour, and in 2026 it takes in Spain as part of a European leg that also covers France, Germany and the UK.

For Spanish fans, that changes the trip. A Madrid date means no flight to Paris or London: just a high-speed train from Barcelona, Valencia or Seville, or a Metro ride across town.

## Who you're actually seeing

NMIXX debuted in February 2022 with "O.O". From day one they pitched a sound they call "MIXX pop": songs that switch genre partway through, so a trap verse can drop into a bright pop chorus without warning. For a first-timer that's the main thing to know. Their songs don't sit still, and the choreography changes along with the music.

The current line-up is six members:

- Lily
- Haewon
- Sullyoon
- Bae
- Jiwoo
- Kyujin

Their reputation rests on vocals. Lily and Haewon in particular are known for the high, sustained notes that fans clip and share after every live stage. Songs that built the group's following include "DICE", "Love Me Like This", "Party O'Clock" and "Dash". The fandom is called NSWER, and you'll see that word on banners, fan-made slogans and T-shirts all around the venue.

## "Soñar" and the Spanish thread

Few K-pop groups have a song with a Spanish title. NMIXX do. "Soñar (Breaker)" comes from their 2024 release *Fe3O4: Break*, and *soñar* means "to dream".

Nobody outside the production knows the setlist, so this guide won't promise the song is on it. Still, it's the one track in their catalogue that already speaks the local language. If it shows up in Madrid, a Spanish-speaking room has a hook ready to shout back. Fans who know the song usually learn the title line before the trip.

## Getting across Madrid on show night

Madrid runs on its Metro. The network is dense, the trains are frequent, and almost every central neighbourhood sits within a short walk of a station.

Getting in from out of town:

- **From Adolfo Suárez Madrid-Barajas Airport:** Metro Line 8 (pink) runs from the terminals to Nuevos Ministerios, where it connects with Lines 6 and 10 and Cercanías commuter trains.
- **By high-speed train:** AVE and other long-distance services arrive at Madrid Puerta de Atocha or Madrid Chamartín, and both connect to the Metro and Cercanías.
- **Paying:** contactless cards work at Metro barriers, and the rechargeable Tarjeta Multi travel card is sold at station machines.

After a big show, the stations nearest the arena fill up all at once. Plenty of attendees walk ten or fifteen minutes to the next station along the line, where the platforms are calmer.

## Lightsticks, photocards and the NSWER queue

K-pop concerts in Europe run on a few unwritten rules, and Madrid follows them.

The official NMIXX lightstick is the main accessory. Many fans pair it through the official app so it syncs with the venue's light show, which only works if the stick is set up before the lights go down. Merch stands usually draw the longest lines of the day, and lots of fans head straight there and pick up their lightstick before anything else.

Around the queue, you'll see the side events of fan culture:

- **Freebies:** fans hand out stickers, slogans and small printed cards to strangers, often with a sign saying what they're giving away. Taking one is normal, and a "gracias" covers it.
- **Photocard trading:** binders open on the pavement and people swap duplicates. Ask before you flip through someone's binder.
- **Fan chants:** NMIXX songs come with official chant guides that fans learn in advance. Joining the chant is part of the show, not an interruption.

Inside, phones go up for the famous high notes, and Spanish crowds sing loudly. Holding your phone at chest height rather than over your head is the courtesy fans usually expect from each other.

## Eating on Madrid time

Spain eats late, and that suits concert night. Dinner in Madrid usually starts around 9pm or later, so kitchens are often still busy when the crowd spills out.

Fans usually split the evening like this:

- **Before:** a quick *bocadillo* (filled baguette) or a round of tapas near the venue. A *caña*, a small draught beer, or a soft drink is the standard order at the bar.
- **After:** a proper sit-down meal, or the Madrid classic of *chocolate con churros*, served late in plenty of cafés across the centre.

Card payments are accepted almost everywhere. Tipping is modest in Spain: rounding up or leaving small change is normal and nobody expects a percentage. In busy tapas bars you order at the bar, and catching the server's eye with a clear "*por favor*" works better than waiting at a table.

For visitors from outside Spain, one small trick helps. Eat your main meal around the show, not at a northern-European hour. A restaurant that's empty at 7pm isn't failing; Madrid just hasn't arrived yet.
