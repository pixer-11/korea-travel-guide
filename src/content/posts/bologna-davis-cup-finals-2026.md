## Eight nations, six days, one trophy

Spain arrives in Bologna as top seed. Seven other nations join them, South Korea among them, for the last stage of men's tennis's biggest team competition. Play runs from November 24 to 29, 2026, at the SuperTennis Arena.

The Finals use a straight knockout of quarterfinals, semifinals and a final. Each tie is a best-of-three: two singles rubbers, then a doubles if the score sits at one-all. That last rubber is often where a tie swings, so crowds tend to stay to the end.

Italy has strong recent form here, having won the trophy in 2023 and 2024. That gives a Bologna crowd every reason to fill the stands for any home tie.

## Tickets and the session format

The ITF is the organiser. It publishes the dates, venue details and ticket links on its official Davis Cup site, and that is where current prices sit. This guide prints no figures.

Tickets are usually sold per session, and one session normally covers one full tie. The quarterfinals spread across the early days. The weekend carries the semifinals and the final, and those seats are the most sought after. Fans following one nation often buy a quarterfinal ticket and add later rounds as their team advances.

## Reaching Bologna in late November

Bologna is one of Italy's easiest cities to reach by rail. From Bologna Centrale, high-speed trains reach:

- Florence in roughly 35 minutes
- Milan in about an hour
- Rome in a little over two hours

Guglielmo Marconi Airport is just outside the city. The Marconi Express monorail connects it straight to Centrale.

Late November is cold and damp in the Po Valley, and fog is common in the mornings. The city's arcaded porticoes are UNESCO-listed and run for kilometres, so you can walk most of the historic centre under cover. Visitors usually stay inside the old walls, near Piazza Maggiore or the station.

## Courtside manners and evenings after match point

Davis Cup is louder than tour tennis. Fans wear team colours, bring flags and chant between points. The arena still goes quiet once a player gets ready to serve. Noise during a rally or a first serve draws hisses from the stands and a word from the umpire. Movement to and from seats usually happens only at changeovers.

After play, the evening belongs to the old town. In the Quadrilatero, the narrow market streets beside Piazza Maggiore, people stand at counters for mortadella, squacquerone cheese and a glass of Lambrusco. At dinner, the Bologna dishes to look for are:

- tortellini in brodo, the winter classic
- tagliatelle al ragù (no one here calls it "spaghetti bolognese")
- lasagne verde, made with spinach pasta

Restaurant bills often add a coperto, a per-person cover charge, and tipping beyond it is optional. Coffee is usually drunk standing at the bar. Cards are widely accepted, though small bakeries sometimes prefer coins.
