## Two nights at Homebush

Sydney gets a weekend of Harry. On Saturday 12 and Sunday 13 December 2026, Harry Styles plays Accor Stadium as part of his world tour. These are the only two Sydney dates.

The stadium sits in Sydney Olympic Park, in the suburb of Homebush, roughly 16 kilometres west of the city centre. It was built as Stadium Australia for the 2000 Olympics. It is still the biggest stage in the city, used for State of Origin, NRL grand finals and the largest touring concerts.

His last Australian stadium run was the Love On Tour leg in 2023. Plenty of people in these crowds will be coming back for a second time.

## Tickets for the Accor Stadium shows

The ticket links live in one place. Harry Styles' official website lists the tour dates and the official ticket links for both Sydney nights.

New South Wales law limits ticket resale to no more than 10 per cent above the original price. Resale platforms advertising seats well above that are worth treating with suspicion. Fans who miss out on one night usually look at the other date, since it is the same show in the same bowl.

The stadium's conditions of entry, including bag rules and prohibited items, are set out on the Accor Stadium website.

## Getting to Sydney Olympic Park

Most of the crowd comes by train. Olympic Park station sits at the end of a short spur line from Lidcombe, and on major event nights extra services run to and from Central. From the station, Olympic Boulevard leads straight down to the stadium in a few minutes.

Your other options:

- **Ferry:** Sydney Olympic Park wharf is on the F3 Parramatta River route from Circular Quay. It is slower but scenic, and a connecting walk or bus takes you to the precinct.
- **Driving:** the precinct has large car parks, but leaving after a sold-out stadium show is slow.
- **Rideshare:** there are designated pick-up zones, and surge pricing after the encore is normal.

Sydney's public transport takes Opal cards, and you can tap on and off with a contactless bank card or phone. That is how most visitors pay.

## Sequins on Olympic Boulevard

The walk from the station is part of the show. Olympic Boulevard is a wide pedestrian avenue lined with flagpoles and light towers, built to move Olympic crowds. On concert nights it fills with feather boas, cowboy hats, glitter and sequinned flares.

Dressing up is the culture at a Harry Styles show. Fans trade friendship bracelets, hold up handmade signs and wear outfits nodding to particular eras of his music. Nobody will look twice at a pink sparkly jumpsuit on the 6pm train from Lidcombe.

Merchandise stands go up around the stadium concourse. On a two-night run, fans attending Sunday often buy on Saturday's walk-past.

## Inside the bowl

Accor Stadium is an oval bowl of tiered seating. For concerts the field becomes a standing floor in front of the stage, with seated sections climbing up the levels around it.

The floor is closest to the action, and you stand for the whole night. The lower tier gives a clearer sightline over the crowd. The upper levels are steep and high, and giant screens carry the detail.

It is a huge space. Mid-December in Sydney is high summer, and the sun sets around 8pm, so the opening part of the evening usually happens in daylight. Expect it to be warm on the floor even after dark.

## Saturday and Sunday the Sydney way

The rhythm of the night is predictable, and regulars plan around it.

- **Arrival:** floor ticket holders usually come well before doors to secure a spot near the front. Seated fans tend to arrive in time for the support set.
- **Paying:** card and phone payments are the norm at the bars and food outlets. Contactless works on transport too.
- **Summer kit:** sunscreen, a hat for the queue and a refillable bottle, within the venue's entry conditions, are standard for a December show.
- **Leaving:** after the encore, staff marshal the crowd into holding lines for trains at Olympic Park. It moves steadily but takes time. Many fans walk the boulevard slowly or stop for food in the precinct while the first wave clears.

Locals tend to tap off at Lidcombe or Strathfield and change there, rather than waiting for a direct Central service.

## Where fans stay and what's nearby

Staying close cuts the late journey home. Sydney Olympic Park has a cluster of hotels within walking distance of the stadium, and these are usually the first to fill on big event weekends.

Parramatta, the city's second centre, is a short train or ferry ride west. It has more rooms and plenty of late-night places to eat around Church Street. Staying in the CBD puts you near Circular Quay, the Opera House and the harbour for the rest of the weekend.

The precinct has room for more than the show. Bicentennial Park and its wetland boardwalks are next to the stadium, and the Parramatta River foreshore runs along the northern edge. Both are good for a walk on Sunday morning before night two.
