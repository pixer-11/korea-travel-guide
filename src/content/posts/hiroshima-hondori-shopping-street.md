Hondori doesn't look like a tourist attraction, and that's exactly the point. It's a glass-roofed arcade lined with shops, cafes and small chain outlets, the kind of street locals actually use to get somewhere, not a recreated "old town" built for visitors.

## Where it sits in Hiroshima

Hondori runs east-west through Naka Ward, a few minutes' walk from Hiroshima's Peace Memorial Park and the Atomic Bomb Dome. Hondori Station, on the Hiroshima Electric Railway (streetcar) network, sits right at its edge, and Hondori is also a stop name on several tram lines that cross the city centre.

That location matters: most people who come to Hiroshima already walk near here on the way to the Peace Park, but few treat the street itself as a stop. At 4.4 stars from a modest 189 reviews, it hasn't built up the reputation of the park or the dome, even though it sees plenty of foot traffic simply by being in the way.

## What the street is actually like

The arcade's roof keeps it dry and shaded whatever the weather is doing outside, which matters in Hiroshima's humid summers and wet winters. Storefronts run the usual mix for a Japanese covered shopping street:

- Clothing chains and shoe shops
- Drugstores and cosmetics counters
- Bakeries, cafes and a handful of casual eateries
- Stationery and daily-goods stores

Nothing here is dressed up for tourists. Signage is mostly in Japanese, prices are posted on shelves and racks, and the pace is closer to a weekday errand run than a sightseeing walk.

## When to go

The street keeps the same hours every day, 10am to 8pm, so there's no early-morning or late-night surprise to plan around. Weekdays between 10am and 11am are the quietest stretch, right after opening and before lunchtime traffic builds.

Weekends are different. The whole day from 10am to 8pm stays comparatively even, but the window from 1pm to 5pm is the busiest stretch, when shoppers and visitors overlap. If you want the arcade closer to empty, aim for a weekday morning or a Sunday evening after 6pm.

## Walking it without a plan

Treat Hondori as a corridor, not a destination in itself. Enter from either end, walk the full 600 metres or so, and let side alleys pull you off toward Hiroshima's other central shopping streets, several of which connect directly into the arcade.

Because it's under-the-radar for a shopping street this central, you won't find much English signage or tourist infrastructure, which is part of what keeps it feeling like a working street rather than a stage set. Pair it with the Peace Park visit: walk one, then cut through here on your way to lunch or back to your hotel.

## Paying and moving like a regular

Most shops and cafes on Hondori take IC transit cards (Suica, PASPY and similar) and standard credit cards, though smaller stalls may still be cash-preferred; carrying some yen avoids any awkward moment at a till.

- Streetcars stop directly at Hondori, so there's no need for a taxi
- Shops post fixed prices on shelves; haggling isn't part of the culture here
- Bikes aren't ridden through the arcade, so the covered walkway is pedestrian-only and easy to navigate with luggage or a stroller
- If a shop looks closed but the lights are on, check the hours sign by the door rather than assuming; most keep to the 10am–8pm window listed for the street generally

There's no ticket, no gate and no set route. The best approach is simply to walk through slowly on your way between the Peace Park and wherever you're headed next, and let whatever storefront catches your eye be the reason you stop.
