## First sight of the dome

You enter through one of three gates: East, West, or South, all funneling into the red sandstone Great Gate, the Darwaza-i-Rauza. Through its arch, the white marble dome appears framed like a photograph. Built in the 17th century by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan as a mausoleum for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, the complex took roughly two decades to complete.

The symmetry is the whole point. Four minarets lean slightly outward at the corners, an engineering choice so they'd fall away from the tomb in an earthquake. The gardens split into quadrants by raised marble water channels, a Persian charbagh layout meant to mirror paradise.

## Inside the marble

Close up, the marble isn't blank white. It's inlaid with semi-precious stones, carnelian, jasper, turquoise, in floral patterns using a technique called pietra dura. Run your eyes along the dado panels and you'll spot the same motif repeated with tiny variations, no two exactly alike.

The inner chamber holds the cenotaphs of Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal, though their actual tombs sit in a quieter crypt below, closed to the public. Entry to this inner sanctum usually requires an extra ticket and a separate queue that moves slowly.

Across the river, the unfinished Mehtab Bagh garden gives a flat, uncluttered view of the Taj's rear facade, especially good near sunset.

## Practical logistics

- Foreign visitor tickets cost more than domestic ones; buy online in advance through the official ASI portal to skip the ticket-counter line.
- Security is strict: no food, tripods, or large bags; only small clear bags and phones/cameras are generally allowed.
- Closed on Fridays, historically kept for prayer at the on-site mosque; confirm this before planning your trip.
- The nearest train station is Agra Cantt, about 6 km away; auto-rickshaws and prepaid taxis run to Tajganj.
- Shoe covers are provided (or you go barefoot) before stepping onto the marble platform.

Set aside two to three hours minimum. Photographers linger longer for the light shifting off the dome between dawn and mid-morning.

## How to visit like a local

Skip the idea of finding a quiet moment here; this is one of the most visited monuments on earth, so plan around the crowds rather than hoping to avoid them. Sunrise entry, right at gate opening, is the closest you'll get to breathing room before tour buses arrive mid-morning.

Guides cluster near the ticket gates; agree on a price before starting, and know that ASI-certified guides wear identification badges. Tipping small amounts in cash for guides and shoe-cover attendants is standard practice.

The most common tourist mistake is skipping the West Gate approach and missing the classic sunset photo angle, or arriving without printed or downloaded e-tickets and getting stuck at manual verification lines. Cash still helps for small vendor purchases outside the complex, though ticketing itself is cashless online.
