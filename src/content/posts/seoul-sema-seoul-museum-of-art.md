Walk up Deoksugung-gil past the palace's grey stone wall and you'll hit a red-brick building that used to be the Gyeongseong District Court. That colonial-era facade is now the front door to SeMA, Seoul's main city-run art museum, and the contrast between the old courthouse shell and the white-cube galleries inside is part of what makes a visit here different from the private museums a few blocks away.

## Getting there and getting in

The museum sits at 61 Deoksugung-gil in Jung District, a short walk from City Hall Station (Line 1 or 2) or Seodaemun Station (Line 5). Most visitors combine it with the Deoksugung Palace stone wall road, which runs right alongside it and is one of Seoul's better-known walking routes.

There's no admission charge for the permanent building entry in most cases, though special exhibitions sometimes carry their own ticket, so check SeMA's website for whichever show is running before you go.

## When to actually show up

SeMA is closed every Monday, which trips up a lot of first-time visitors planning a weekday itinerary around it. Tuesday through Thursday it runs 10am to 8pm, Friday it stays open until 9pm, and weekends close earlier at 7pm.

If you want the galleries close to yourself, the quietest window measured is 8pm to 9pm on weekdays, right before closing. Weekend crowds build steadily and peak between 1pm and 5pm, so a Saturday or Sunday morning right at the 10am opening is a better bet than an afternoon drop-in.

## What's inside

SeMA doesn't hold a single fixed permanent collection the way older institutions do; its identity is built around rotating exhibitions of modern and contemporary Korean and international art, spread across several floors connected by a central atrium. Expect a mix of painting, photography, media art and installation work, with shows changing every few months.

Because the building started life as a courtroom, some of the original structural bones, high ceilings, load-bearing columns, the general proportions of the halls, are still visible under the museum fit-out. It gives the newer, more experimental installations an odd formality they wouldn't have in a purpose-built gallery.

The museum also runs a busy schedule of talks, film screenings and workshops tied to current exhibitions, usually listed on-site and online a few weeks ahead.

## Moving through the galleries without a plan

There's no single prescribed route through SeMA, and that's part of the point. The atrium works as a natural hub: pick a floor, work through it, come back down rather than trying to snake through every wing in one pass.

Signage is bilingual in Korean and English throughout, and gallery attendants stationed at each floor can point you toward whichever exhibition is drawing the most attention that season. Photography rules vary by show, some pieces are marked no-photo, so glance for signage before raising a phone.

## Around the museum

Deoksugung Palace itself is a two-minute walk downhill, and its stone wall path is popular for a slow loop either before or after the museum, especially in autumn when the ginkgo trees along it turn yellow. Jung District also puts you within reach of Seoul City Hall Plaza and the Cheonggyecheon stream, both walkable extensions to a museum morning.

If you're building a half-day around art, SeMA pairs naturally with the nearby Seoul Museum of History, a short walk further west, for anyone who wants a second stop without changing subway lines.

## Making the most of a visit

Budget around 1.5 to 2 hours for a normal visit, longer if a major touring exhibition is on and drawing dense crowds through the upper floors. Coat check and lockers are available near the entrance, worth using if you're carrying bags from a day of walking.

Since it's well known rather than an under-the-radar spot, don't expect to have it to yourself on weekend afternoons; the 1pm to 5pm stretch is when tour groups and family visits overlap. A Friday evening visit, taking advantage of the extended 9pm closing, is one of the easiest ways to see the collection without the daytime density.
