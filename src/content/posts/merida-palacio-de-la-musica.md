## A city's soundtrack, under one roof

Mérida plays live music in its squares every week. Palacio de la Música tells you where those songs came from. It opened in 2018 as a national centre for Mexican music, and its galleries move from pre-Hispanic instruments through colonial church music to the radio hits and boleros of the twentieth century.

The heart of the collection is the trova yucateca. This is the romantic guitar-and-voice style that grew up in Yucatán and still fills the city's plazas. It is consistently one of the best-rated museums in Mérida, and it's well known. Don't expect it to be empty.

## Finding it on Calle 58

The address is C. 58 in Centro, beside Parque Santa Lucía. Mérida's old centre is a grid. Even-numbered streets run north to south and odd-numbered streets run east to west, so the address alone gets you there.

- **From Plaza Grande:** walk north a few blocks. It takes about ten minutes on the narrow colonial pavements.
- **From Paseo de Montejo hotels:** head south toward Centro on Calle 58 or 60. A short Uber or Didi ride saves you a sweaty walk.
- **Driving:** street parking in Centro is tight. Paid lots (*estacionamientos*) on the surrounding blocks are the easier option.

## Inside the galleries

The building is contemporary, set into a centre full of colonial stone. Inside, the route runs as a chronological sequence of rooms. Much of the experience is listening, and recordings sit alongside the instruments and photographs.

Expect a lot of the wall text in Spanish. A translation app with a camera mode helps in the denser rooms. The recordings need no translation at all.

What to look out for:

- **Pre-Hispanic sound-makers:** drums, flutes and whistles that open the story.
- **The trova rooms:** the Yucatecan composers and singers whose songs you'll hear later in the plazas.
- **Popular music of the last century:** the boleros, film songs and radio stars that spread Mexican music abroad.

The building also has a concert hall that hosts performances separately from the museum. Check the official listings if you'd like to hear something live while you're in town.

## Timing the visit around the heat

The schedule is short, and you need to plan for it. Doors open at 10am and close at 4pm, Wednesday through Sunday. It is **closed every Monday and Tuesday**, which catches out visitors who arrive early in the week.

Mérida's afternoons are hot for most of the year. An indoor, air-conditioned museum is a sensible way to spend late morning. Arriving around opening also gives you the rooms before later visitors arrive.

Don't turn up after 3pm. With a 4pm close, you won't have time for the listening stations, and they are the reason to come.

## Doing it the Mérida way

Buy your entry at the ticket desk inside. The current price is posted there and on the official site, so check before you go. Carry some pesos as a backup in case the card reader is having a bad day, which happens anywhere in Centro.

Some etiquette helps in the listening rooms:

- **Keep your phone on silent.** Other people are wearing headphones or leaning in to hear.
- **Ask before filming performances** if a concert or event is on.
- **Greet the staff** with a "buenos días" on the way in. It's ordinary politeness in Yucatán and gets you friendlier directions.

The best way to use the museum is as a primer. On Thursday evenings, Parque Santa Lucía next door hosts the long-running Serenata Yucateca, with trova and folk dance in the open air. The museum is closed by then, so visit earlier in the week's opening days. That evening you'll recognise the songs.

## Pairing it with the rest of Centro

Several of the old city's best stops are within a few blocks:

- **Parque Santa Lucía:** the arcaded square right beside the museum, lined with restaurants.
- **Teatro José Peón Contreras:** the grand early-1900s theatre a short walk west on Calle 60.
- **Plaza Grande:** the main square, home to the Catedral de San Ildefonso and the Casa de Montejo.
- **Paseo de Montejo:** the boulevard of mansions to the north, best seen later in the day once the museum has closed.

A good half-day goes museum at 10am, then lunch around Santa Lucía, then the cathedral and Plaza Grande. Come back on a Thursday night for the serenata.
