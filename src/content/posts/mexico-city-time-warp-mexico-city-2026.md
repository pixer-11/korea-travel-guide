## Two nights of German techno, out west

Time Warp is a German techno festival that grew up in Mannheim. The brand has spent decades putting dancers in dark halls under huge light rigs, and over the years it has taken that format to cities in South America and to New York. In Mexico City it moves indoors for two nights, November 20 and 21, 2026.

The draw has always been the production as much as the bookings. Floors are built around big lighting and laser structures rather than one stage at the end of a field. A night here means long sets, low light and a crowd that came to dance, not to watch.

## Expo Santa Fe and the drive to get there

Santa Fe surprises people who know only the old centre. It's a district of glass office towers, corporate headquarters and steep ravines in the hills at the western edge of the city. Expo Santa Fe is its convention centre, a complex of large exhibition halls by the Centro Santa Fe shopping mall.

The Metro doesn't reach this far west. Most festival-goers travel by Uber or DiDi, or a pre-booked taxi. From Roma, Condesa or Polanco the ride crosses a big chunk of the city. Friday-evening traffic heading out of town can easily stretch it to an hour or more.

## Tickets and tiers

There are several ticket price tiers. The organiser publishes the dates, venue details and ticket links on its official site. That is also where you'll find the current price of each tier, so this guide gives no figures.

Two-night festivals like this usually sell single-night entry and full-weekend passes as separate products. The weekend option generally works out better for anyone travelling to Mexico City for the event.

## Rides, pesos and the long night in Santa Fe

How locals handle a night out in Santa Fe comes down to logistics.

- **Rideshare over street taxis.** Chilangos (Mexico City locals) generally order a car in the app or use an official *sitio* taxi stand instead of hailing a cab on the street. That's the normal way home at the end of the night.
- **Walk before you call.** At closing time, thousands of phones request a car at once and prices surge. Many people walk a few blocks from the main exit before ordering, or wait out the first rush.
- **Ride together.** Groups usually share one car home, which splits the fare and means fewer people standing around at 5am.
- **Mixed payment.** Inside venues, cards and tap-to-pay are common. Outside, the taco stand and the corner shop often take pesos only.
- **Tipping.** Around 10–15% is normal at restaurants and bars. Rideshare tips go through the app.
- **Language.** A few words of Spanish go a long way at the bar: *una agua, por favor* gets you water.

## Altitude and November air

Mexico City sits about 2,240 metres above sea level, and Santa Fe is higher than the city centre. The thin air makes hours of dancing feel harder than they would at sea level. Alcohol also hits faster. Regulars drink plenty of water between drinks, especially on the first night.

November falls in the dry season. Days are usually mild and sunny, but nights in the western hills turn properly cold. Inside a packed hall that won't matter. It will matter on the pavement at dawn, which is why attendees often bring a light jacket they can tie around the waist.

## Where people stay

Hotels in Santa Fe itself are mostly business hotels next to the office towers. They make the trip home short, but there's little to do nearby by day. Many visitors stay in Roma, Condesa or Polanco instead. They accept the longer ride in return for walkable streets, cafés and the city's restaurants.

Polanco is on the western side of the city, which shortens the trip a little. Roma and Condesa are better if you plan to spend the days between the two nights at taquerías and in Parque México. Some visitors split their stay: central Mexico City for the trip overall, and one night in Santa Fe for the festival.
