## A lane barely wider than a delivery van

Haji Lane is short and narrow. Two rows of low shophouses face each other across a strip of tarmac. Their shutters, side walls and upper storeys are covered in paint: murals, lettering and colour blocks that change as shops come and go.

At ground level you walk past small independent boutiques, vintage racks, cafés and bars. Upstairs are shuttered windows and old timber louvres. You can walk the whole thing in a few minutes. The point is to go slowly and duck into doorways.

## Why a pilgrims' lane became a design street

The name comes from the Haj. Kampong Glam was the Malay and Arab quarter of colonial Singapore. This lane was where pilgrims are said to have lodged before sailing to Mecca. The shophouses date from that era, low and narrow-fronted, built close together.

The creative scene came much later. Independent fashion labels, record and lifestyle shops and small bars moved into the ground floors. The street art followed. Today the lane draws less traffic than Bugis Street a few minutes away, and that is the reason to come: you get room to look around.

## Getting there from Bugis

The simplest route is the MRT to **Bugis Station**, on the East-West Line and the Downtown Line. Come up toward Victoria Street and walk north-east past Bugis Junction. The lane is about ten minutes on foot.

- **Nicoll Highway Station** (Circle Line) is a similar walk from the Beach Road side.
- Haji Lane runs parallel to **Arab Street**, between North Bridge Road and Beach Road.
- Taxis and ride-hailing drivers know it by name. The verified address is 69 Haji Ln, Singapore 189262.

## Daylight shopping, after-dark drinks

The lane never closes, since it is a public street. The businesses along it keep their own hours, and those vary shop by shop. Boutiques tend to open late morning. Bars come into their own in the evening, when tables and stools spill toward the gutter.

For murals without many people in the frame, early in the day works best, before most shutters roll up. Some of the best paintwork is on the shutters themselves, so that's also when you see it. Come back after dark for a drink. Singapore's heat makes the middle of the afternoon the hardest time to linger outdoors.

## Walking Haji Lane without getting in the way

The lane is small, and the people who work here deal with cameras all day. A few habits help:

- **Keep doorways clear.** Shopfronts are popular backdrops. Shoot, then step aside so customers can get in.
- **Ask before photographing staff** or the inside of a shop. Some boutiques don't allow photos of their stock.
- **Watch for vans.** Deliveries still come through, so move to the side when you hear an engine.
- **Paying is easy.** Cards, contactless and PayNow-style QR codes are common in Singapore. Carry a little cash for the smallest shops.
- **Don't tip.** It isn't customary here. Many sit-down places add a service charge to the bill.

Kampong Glam is still a Muslim neighbourhood with a working mosque at its centre. Dress for that if you plan to step inside Sultan Mosque. The mosque lends robes at the entrance, and visitor hours pause around prayer times, especially at midday on Friday. Its official site lists the current visiting times.

## Around the corner in Kampong Glam

Haji Lane makes more sense as part of a loop through the district.

- **Sultan Mosque (Masjid Sultan):** the golden-domed mosque at the end of Bussorah Street, the heart of the quarter's history.
- **Bussorah Street:** a palm-lined pedestrian street with a direct view of the mosque's domes.
- **Arab Street:** textile, batik and carpet shops, one block over.
- **Malay Heritage Centre:** housed in the former Istana Kampong Gelam, the old sultan's palace.
- **North Bridge Road:** long-running eateries known for murtabak and biryani, facing the mosque.
- **Bali Lane:** a smaller, quieter lane close by with a similar shophouse scale.

An easy plan is a late-morning walk down Haji Lane, lunch on North Bridge Road, the mosque and heritage centre in the afternoon, then back to the lane for an evening drink.
