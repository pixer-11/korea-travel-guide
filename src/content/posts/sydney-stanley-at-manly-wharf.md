## A Brisbane name lands on Manly Cove

Stanley started in Brisbane, where it earned a reputation as the city's most celebrated Chinese fine-diner. Chef Louis Tikaram has now brought it to Sydney, on Manly Wharf. That puts it on the calm harbour side of Manly, facing Manly Cove rather than the surf.

The Sydney room is big: 250 seats. The focus is Cantonese seafood, a cooking tradition built on steaming, quick wok work and keeping the fish at the centre of the plate. With a menu that new and that busy, check the official site for what is being served now.

## The ferry is the best part of the commute

Get to Manly by water. Ferries run from Circular Quay to Manly Wharf, and you step off right at the restaurant's front door. The standard Manly ferry takes roughly 30 minutes. It passes the Opera House, the Harbour Bridge behind you and the cliffs of North and South Head.

- **Paying:** tap on and off with an Opal card or a contactless bank card or phone.
- **Faster option:** the private fast ferry also runs Circular Quay to Manly, ticketed separately.
- **By road:** Manly is a slow drive through the northern beaches traffic, and parking near the wharf is limited.

If you plan an evening booking, a late-afternoon ferry gets you there in daylight. On the way back, the last legs cross the harbour after dark with the city lit up.

## Booking a table at Stanley

The first rule here is simple: book. The restaurant is filling up fast, and 250 seats still go quickly. Reserve online as early as you can, and confirm opening hours when you book, because a new opening can change its schedule in its first months.

- **Order to share.** Cantonese meals arrive as shared dishes for the whole table. Order across the table rather than one main each.
- **Bring a group.** A larger room usually suits bigger parties better than a tiny bistro would, but say how many are coming when you book.
- **Paying the bill.** Australian restaurants take card and tap almost everywhere. Some add a surcharge for cards or on weekends and public holidays, and this is shown on the menu or the bill.
- **Tipping.** You don't have to tip in Australia, because staff earn award wages. Rounding up or leaving around a tenth for great service is a kind gesture, not something anyone expects.

Ask about the seafood options when you sit down. With Cantonese seafood, what is available often depends on the catch, and staff can steer you to the dishes worth ordering that day.

## Making a day of Manly

Arrive early and build the meal into a longer day. The Corso, Manly's pedestrian strip, runs straight from the wharf to Manly Beach on the ocean side in about five minutes on foot.

- **Shelly Beach:** follow the path south from Manly Beach to a sheltered cove popular with snorkellers.
- **North Head:** sandstone headland lookouts over the harbour entrance, reachable on foot or by a short bus.
- **Spit Bridge to Manly walk:** a coastal track of about 10 km through bushland and small beaches, ending near the wharf. It's a good walk for working up an appetite before a long lunch.

Plan for two or three hours at the table. With the ferry each way, Stanley fills half a day by itself.
