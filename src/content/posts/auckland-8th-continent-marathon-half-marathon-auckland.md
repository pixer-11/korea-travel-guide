## Race day on a volcano

The race is held on Sunday, January 17, 2027. Its base is Auckland Domain, a park laid over Pukekawa, one of the city's dormant volcanic cones. It offers both a full marathon and a half marathon, and it draws distance runners from around New Zealand and from abroad.

The Domain is not flat. Its lawns sit in the old crater bowl, and its roads climb toward the Auckland War Memorial Museum, the white neoclassical building on the summit. Runners used to waterfront courses should expect gradients around the park.

## Getting to Auckland Domain

Rail is the easiest way in. Grafton Station is on the Domain's southwestern side and Parnell Station is on its eastern edge. From both, the walk into the park takes a few minutes.

- **From the CBD:** you can walk up from Britomart or Queen Street through Grafton Gully or Parnell in about 20–30 minutes.
- **Driving:** there are car parks inside the Domain, but spaces are limited, and road closures around a race usually cut access further.
- **Staying nearby:** visiting runners often book in Parnell, Newmarket or the CBD, since all three are an easy walk to the park.

The organiser publishes the start area, course map and race-day logistics on its official site, along with the entry links.

## Mid-January conditions

January is high summer in Auckland. Mornings are usually warm and often humid, and the sun gets strong quickly. Distance runners here tend to carry or plan for more fluids than they would at a cooler European or North American race.

Much of the Domain is open grass with scattered stands of mature trees. That gives spectators patches of shade but leaves stretches of exposed ground. Supporters usually bring hats and sunscreen and pick a spot under the big trees.

## Finish-line habits in the Domain

There are a few Auckland norms worth knowing on race morning:

- **Paying:** card and contactless payment are standard across the city, including at most cafés in Parnell and Newmarket. Tipping is not expected in New Zealand.
- **Getting around:** the AT HOP card or contactless bank cards cover Auckland trains and buses, and they spare you the queue at the ticket machine after the race.
- **Cheering:** spectators usually spread along the Domain's internal roads, not only at the finish, because those roads give long views of runners climbing the slopes.
- **Recovery walk:** finishers often loop past the Domain Wintergarden, the pair of glasshouses facing a sunken courtyard pond. It's a short stroll from the museum lawn.

Parnell Road has the nearest run of cafés and brunch spots. Most groups head there or down to Newmarket's Broadway once the medals are handed out.

If you're in town for a few days, the War Memorial Museum is right in the park and has Māori and Pacific galleries. On the way back to the city, the Auckland Botanic Gardens are too far, but Albert Park and the Auckland Art Gallery are both a short walk from the Domain's western side.
