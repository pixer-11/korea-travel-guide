You order a drink at Socio the way you'd order at any Soho bar, except the bartender might mention that the vodka in your glass was clarified through crushed oyster shells from a seafood restaurant two streets over. That's the whole premise here: cocktails built from what other kitchens and bars in the neighbourhood were about to throw away.

## What makes Socio different

Soho is thick with cocktail bars chasing the same imported bitters and the same rotating gin list. Socio does something else. Its bartenders work a supply chain of surplus ingredients pulled from nearby restaurants and bars, then figure out how to turn scraps into something pourable.

The two drinks people talk about most are the oyster-shell-infused vodka, made using shells that would otherwise go into the bin after a seafood service, and a banana-peel whiskey highball, which uses the part of the fruit almost every other bar and kitchen discards. Expect the exact lineup to shift as the available surplus shifts.

## Finding it in Soho

Socio sits within Hong Kong's Soho district, the tangle of streets south of Hollywood Road on Hong Kong Island that's long been the city's densest cluster of small bars and restaurants. The area is walkable from the Mid-Levels escalator, which cuts through Soho and is the easiest way to arrive without hunting for parking.

Because the bar's whole concept depends on relationships with surrounding kitchens, its closest neighbours aren't just backdrop, they're effectively suppliers. That density of restaurants and bars within a few minutes' walk is exactly what makes the surplus-sourcing model work here rather than in a more spread-out part of town.

## What to expect on the menu

Don't go in expecting a fixed, printed list you can look up in advance. A bar built around surplus ingredients means the menu is inherently responsive: what's on it depends on what nearby kititchens had too much of that week.

That's part of the appeal rather than a limitation. Ask the bartender what's currently being reworked and why; the explanation is usually more interesting than the drink name on its own. If you have an ingredient you're squeamish about, it's worth asking what's in a cocktail before you order rather than after.

## Ordering and settling in

Socio works best if you treat it as a conversation with whoever's behind the bar rather than a checklist to get through.

- Ask what's new that week rather than requesting a drink you had somewhere else
- Expect small-batch results, since surplus ingredients arrive in limited, inconsistent quantities
- Sit at the bar itself if seats are available; that's where the explanations happen
- Card and mobile payment are standard across Soho bars, though it's sensible to carry some cash as backup

Because this is a newer, trending spot, table turnover and seating can vary night to night. Confirm current opening hours and whether reservations are needed before you go, since neither has been independently verified here.

## Making it part of a Soho evening

Socio rewards being one stop in a longer night rather than the only destination. Soho's grid means you can walk from bar to bar in a few minutes, so treat it as a stretch of the evening rather than the whole plan.

Arriving earlier in the evening generally means more room to actually talk to the bartender about what's in your glass, since later hours in Soho tend to fill up fast as bar-hoppers move through the district. If sustainability and provenance genuinely interest you, that quieter early window is when you'll get the fullest version of the story behind each drink.

## Before you go

Treat Socio as a spot worth building an evening around rather than a quick stop you can plan down to the minute, given how much of its identity depends on what happened in nearby kitchens that day. Check opening hours, reservation policy and any current menu details directly with the bar shortly before your visit, since a surplus-driven concept like this changes faster than most listings can keep up with.
