## A ranking event you can sit right next to

You are close enough to hear the ball. That is the main draw of a WTT Feeder event. Players here are chasing world ranking points on the first rung of the World Table Tennis ladder, and the sport is played in a university sports hall, not a stadium.

WTT Feeder Chennai 2026 takes place from October 28 to November 1, 2026. The venue is Tamil Nadu Physical Education and Sports University, the state's dedicated sports university. The series also includes Contender, Star Contender, Champions and Grand Smash events. Feeders sit at the bottom of that pyramid, and the young players and fringe-ranked professionals who enter them often turn up at higher-tier events within a season or two.

World Table Tennis runs the series. Its event pages on worldtabletennis.com carry draws, live scores and results. Entry arrangements and any ticket links for this edition are found through the organiser, and this guide does not name a price.

## Five days, fewer tables

The shape of the week is this event's real quirk. On the opening days the hall holds several tables at once, each with its own court barriers and umpire's chair. Matches start back to back, and you can turn your head and follow three rallies at a time.

As the draw narrows, the tables are taken away. By the final day the floor usually holds one show court for the finals. The early days give you volume: more players and more upsets, and you can wander from court to court. The last day gives you focus, with one match at a time and the whole room watching it.

What the schedule typically covers at Feeder level:

- Men's and women's singles, which usually include qualifying rounds before the main draw
- Men's and women's doubles
- Mixed doubles
- Matches played best of five games, each game to 11 points

Spectators who want a feel for the sport usually come on a midweek early-round day. Those who want a result to care about come for the finals.

## Getting out to Melakottaiyur

The university is not in central Chennai. TNPESU's campus is in Melakottaiyur, off the Vandalur–Kelambakkam Road. That puts it on the semi-rural southern fringe of the metropolitan area, between GST Road and the IT corridor along Old Mahabalipuram Road.

Your main options:

- **Suburban train:** Vandalur station is on the Chennai Beach–Chengalpattu suburban line. From there it is a short auto-rickshaw or cab ride east to the campus.
- **Cab apps:** Uber, Ola and Rapido all operate here. They are the simplest choice from the airport or from hotels along GST Road and OMR.
- **Intercity bus:** The Kilambakkam bus terminus near Vandalur handles long-distance services heading south. That helps if you are combining the tournament with a trip down the Tamil Nadu coast.
- **From the airport:** Chennai International Airport is on GST Road at Meenambakkam. It sits on the same southern side of the city as the venue, so you don't have to cross the centre.

Return cabs from the campus can take a while to find late in the evening. Spectators commonly wait for the auto drivers who gather near the university gate or at Vandalur station.

## Watching from the stands

Table tennis has quiet manners. The crowd goes still while a player bounces the ball before serving. People cheer the point once it is over, never during the rally. Hold onto that and you will fit in at once.

How spectators usually behave in the hall:

- You move between seats only between points, or better, between games. On an early-round day you may change courts often, so this matters.
- Flash photography stays off. A white burst near a ball travelling this fast distracts players, and umpires will say so.
- Phones go on silent. The hall is quiet enough that a ringtone carries across several courts.
- Applause goes to both players. Indian crowds back their own players loudly, but a long rally won by a visiting player still gets clapped.

Payment around the venue and across Chennai runs mostly on UPI, scanned from a QR code at the counter. International visitors without an Indian UPI app usually carry some cash for autos, tea stalls and small food vendors. Cards work at hotels and larger restaurants.

## Northeast monsoon week

Late October to early November is the heart of Chennai's northeast monsoon. Most of the city's annual rain falls in this season, often in heavy afternoon or overnight bursts. The tournament is indoors, but the journey there is not.

What that means in practice:

- Roads on the southern fringe, including stretches around Vandalur, are prone to waterlogging after heavy rain.
- Cab prices on the apps tend to rise and waits get longer during downpours.
- A compact umbrella and footwear that can handle puddles are more useful than anything else in your bag.

On the plus side, the rain brings the temperature down from the summer peaks. Humidity stays high all the same.

## Where to stay and what's around

Most visitors who aren't attached to a team stay in one of two places.

- **GST Road corridor (Tambaram, Guduvanchery, Vandalur):** closest to the venue and on the suburban rail line. Plenty of mid-range business hotels.
- **OMR (Sholinganallur, Navalur, Kelambakkam):** the IT corridor, with larger hotels and plenty of restaurants. It is a cab ride west along the Vandalur–Kelambakkam Road to the campus.

Central Chennai neighbourhoods such as T. Nagar, Mylapore or Egmore give you more of the city. They also add a long daily commute south.

Close to the venue, Arignar Anna Zoological Park in Vandalur is one of the largest zoos in India. It is an easy half-day add-on on a rest day. Further down the coast, the UNESCO-listed shore temple and rock-cut monuments of Mamallapuram are reachable from OMR and the East Coast Road.

## Eating between sessions

Food around a university sports campus is practical, not fancy. Small "mess"-style eateries and tea stalls around Vandalur and Kelambakkam serve the Tamil basics all day:

- Idli and dosa with sambar and chutneys, the standard morning meal
- Meals on a banana leaf at lunchtime: rice with rotating vegetable dishes, rasam and curd
- Filter coffee, served in a steel tumbler set inside a small bowl called a dabarah

Spectators staying for a full day of sessions often eat at one of these between matches rather than travel back into the city. On OMR you will find a wider choice of restaurants, including chains and international options, for evening meals after the last match.
