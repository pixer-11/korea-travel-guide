## Carved stone off an Etobicoke service road

The approach doesn't prepare you for it. Claireville Drive is a strip of warehouses and truck traffic near Highway 427, and at the end of it stands a white domed mandir, its stone carved into pinnacles and spires. It opened in 2007 as a traditional hand-carved Hindu temple of the BAPS Swaminarayan tradition. The outside is carved limestone and the inner sanctum is pale marble.

Inside, almost every surface is worked by hand:

- columns carved with deities and floral bands
- domed ceilings made up of rings of carved figures
- shrines holding murtis (sacred images), dressed in fabric that changes through the day

It is one of the most-reviewed places of worship in the Toronto area. You will share it with worshippers and visitors most days.

## Getting to Claireville Drive

The mandir sits in northwest Etobicoke, close to the Brampton border and a short drive from Toronto Pearson Airport. That makes it an easy stop on the way to or from a flight. Driving is the simplest option because it is right off Highway 427 near Finch Avenue West. On transit, plan a subway trip and then a TTC bus, and check the route in a trip planner beforehand.

Allow 60–90 minutes. That covers the mandir, the grounds and the adjoining complex, which includes an exhibition on Indian heritage. If you want to see that part too, give yourself the longer end.

## Shoes, shoulders and silence: the mandir's etiquette

This is a working temple, and the rules are enforced kindly but firmly.

- **Dress:** cover your shoulders and knees. If you arrive in shorts or a sleeveless top, you may be offered a wrap.
- **Shoes:** take them off before you enter the mandir and leave them on the racks provided.
- **Photography:** it isn't allowed inside the mandir itself. Take your photos outside on the grounds.
- **Voice:** keep it low. People will be praying in front of the shrines.
- **Arti:** the lamp-waving ritual happens at set times during the day. Visitors may stand quietly at the back. Ask at the front desk for today's times.

Entry is through the main complex, where volunteers usually direct you. Don't bring food into the sanctum.

## Timing around the weekend rush

Weekend afternoons are the time to avoid. From 2pm to 7pm on Saturdays and Sundays, families and tour groups arrive and the shoe area and sanctum get crowded. If you can only come at the weekend, get there not long after the 9am opening and you'll be leaving as the rush begins.

On weekdays there are no weekend family visits, so the mandir generally feels calmer, especially in the morning. Google currently shows 9am–7pm every day, but temple hours can change for festivals. Check the official BAPS Toronto site before you go.

Diwali and the Hindu New Year (Annakut) bring big crowds and elaborate displays of food offerings. Come then if you want to see the mandir at its busiest and most decorated, and expect a long wait.
