Two volcanic crater lakes sit inside old-growth forest at roughly 900 meters elevation, about 45 minutes to an hour by road from Dumaguete through Sibulan. The park protects one of the last significant rainforest patches on Negros, and it shows: the air is noticeably cooler than the coast, thick with birdsong, and the canopy overhead blocks out most direct sun on the trail down.

## Getting there from Dumaguete

Most visitors start in Sibulan, catching a habal-habal (motorbike taxi) up the mountain road toward the park entrance. The road is paved for stretches then turns to rough dirt and gravel closer to the top, so a habal-habal or a hired van with a driver used to the route works better than a rental car with low clearance.

There's a barangay checkpoint before the park proper where you register and where local guides are typically arranged. Guides aren't strictly optional here: the trail network isn't signposted for casual wandering, and a guide knows which sections turn slick after rain.

## The walk down to the lake

From the ranger station, it's a steep descent, roughly 20 to 30 minutes down a dirt and stone path, to reach the shore of Lake Balinsasayao. The return climb takes noticeably longer and will leave most people out of breath, so pace yourself and take the handrails on the steeper switchbacks.

The lake itself is a flat sheet of dark green water ringed by forest, with no development on the shoreline beyond a small viewing area. Swimming is generally not allowed, as the lake is a protected water source, but you can rent a small paddle boat or bamboo raft to get out on the water.

The second lake, Danao, sits further along a rougher trail and sees far fewer visitors; ask your guide whether it's accessible on the day you go, since conditions and permissions can vary.

## What actually lives here

This forest is one of the last strongholds on Negros for species found nowhere else, including several endemic birds that draw serious birdwatchers with long lenses and early alarms. Listen for hornbills moving through the canopy and keep an eye on the water's edge for monitor lizards.

The undergrowth is dense enough that you're unlikely to see much wildlife up close without a guide who knows where to look and when. Early morning, closer to the 8am opening, gives you the best chance before the heat of midday pushes animals deeper into cover.

## Timing your visit

The park keeps the same hours every day, 8am to 4pm, with no late admission since the trail down and back needs daylight and dry footing. Because it's still under the radar compared to Dumaguete's coastal attractions, you can often have long stretches of the lakeside viewing area to yourself, especially on a weekday.

Rain changes everything here: the descent trail turns slippery and the lake's mirror surface disappears under cloud and drizzle. Aim for the dry season months and an early start, since afternoon cloud is common in this part of Negros regardless of season.

## Registering and paying at the gate

Expect to pay entrance, environmental, and guide fees in cash at the barangay checkpoint; card machines and ATMs do not exist on this mountain. Fees are modest but change periodically, so bring more cash than you think you'll need and treat any figure you've read online as a rough guide, not a quote.

Guides are paid per group rather than per person in most cases, so traveling with two or three others brings the per-head cost down. A small tip for your guide, on top of the set fee, is customary and appreciated given how physically demanding the terrain is for them too.

## Practical notes for the day

Wear real shoes with grip, not sandals; the descent path is uneven stone and packed dirt that gets slick fast.

- Bring a dry bag or plastic sleeve for phones and cameras near the boat area
- Pack water and a light snack, since there are no shops past the checkpoint
- Bring insect repellent for the forested sections
- Wear layers; it's cooler here than in coastal Dumaguete
- Start as early in the 8am to 4pm window as your transport allows

Combine the trip with a stop in Sibulan town on the way back, since it sits directly on the route between the park and Dumaguete proper.
