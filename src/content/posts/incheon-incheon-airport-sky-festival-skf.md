## Three nights of K-pop beside the runways

The 2026 edition was set for November 7 to 9. The programme put exclusive K-pop concerts at the country's main international gateway, for an audience of travellers as much as locals. The timing suited visitors fitting a show around a flight in or out.

Incheon International Airport stands on reclaimed land on Yeongjong Island, west of the Incheon city centre. The ground is flat and coastal, the skies are wide, and aircraft pass overhead on approach. The festival takes its name from that setting. It was organised in connection with the airport itself.

## Reaching Yeongjong Island from Seoul

The festival offered transport packages from Seoul for out-of-town fans. Without a package, the usual route is the train.

- **AREX all-stop train:** leaves from Seoul Station and stops at Hongik University and Gimpo Airport on the way. It continues to Incheon Airport Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 stations.
- **AREX express:** goes nonstop from Seoul Station to the airport. It is a separately ticketed service and is faster than the all-stop.
- **Airport limousine buses:** connect the terminals with most Seoul districts and with hotels.
- **By car:** the route crosses either the Yeongjong Bridge or the Incheon Bridge. Both are tolled.

Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 are a few kilometres apart and linked by a free shuttle bus. The walk between them is long, so the terminal nearest each concert area matters.

## Staying near the airport

Many people at late events here sleep on the island rather than take the late train back to Seoul. Paradise City and other resort hotels sit right beside the terminals. Business hotels near Unseo Station on the AREX line are a cheaper option.

Daytime visitors often head to Eurwangni Beach on the island's west coast for seafood restaurants and a walk on the sand. November is off-season there, and the beach is usually quiet.

November nights on the Yellow Sea coast are cold and windy. Outdoor shows at this time of year are a coat-and-gloves affair.

## Concert habits at SKF

Korean concert crowds follow a few habits, and knowing them makes a night out easier.

- **Lightsticks:** fans bring each group's official lightstick, and the crowd is often a sea of matching colours.
- **Fan chants:** many songs have set chants, shouted during instrumental breaks. Groups' fandoms post them online.
- **Paying:** cards and mobile payments work almost everywhere at the airport. A T-money card covers the AREX all-stop and the buses with a tap.
- **Leaving:** after the final act at big concerts, crowds tend to pack the train platforms. Many people wait in the terminal food courts until it clears.

The terminals have baggage storage counters, a common first stop for international fans at airport events. Since it's an airport, those counters are part of the building.
