## What you're walking into

Eastern Market has run continuously since 1873, making it the oldest of DC's original public markets. The brick building on 7th Street SE holds the indoor food hall: butchers, a fishmonger, bakers, a produce stand. Outside on weekends, vendor tables spread along 7th Street and into the adjacent lot for the arts and crafts market and a farm line.

It's rated 4.5 from well over 4,000 reviews, and it's a known destination, not a secret; Capitol Hill residents and tourists both fill it on Saturdays.

## Navigating the building and the street

Inside, the layout is simple: one long room, counters on both sides, a shared row of tables in the middle for eating. Franklin's, the fishmonger, and the bakery counters draw the longest lines. Outside:

- Saturdays: full farmers line plus arts and crafts vendors along 7th Street
- Sundays: a smaller flea-market and crafts lineup, hours run 8am-5pm (an hour shorter than Saturday)
- Weekdays (Tue-Fri, 8am-6pm): indoor food hall only, no outdoor market

Blue crabs, when in season, are sold by the pound at the seafood counter and are the thing regulars queue for.

## Timing it right

Weekends draw crowds steadily from 9am to 5pm, so a 10am Saturday arrival means hunting for a table. The market's own quiet window is the last hour, 5pm-6pm on Saturday, when vendors are still open but the crowd has thinned. Weekdays are calmer across the board since the outdoor stalls don't set up.

If brunch is the goal, the crowded window is also the point: the smell of griddle cakes and coffee at Market Lunch's counter is part of why people wait. If you just need groceries or crab cakes to go, a weekday morning is faster and the counters aren't backed up.

## Eating and buying at the counters

There's no table service. Order at a counter, take a number or wait for your name, then find a seat at the communal tables or eat standing.

- Cash and card both work at most stalls, though smaller crafts vendors outside may prefer cash
- Tipping a dollar or two at counter service is common but not expected
- Lines move fastest right at opening (8am) on weekdays; weekend counter lines can run long by mid-morning

Don't expect to browse quietly on a Saturday; treat the crowd as the point, not an obstacle. The Eastern Market Metro stop (Blue, Orange, Silver lines) sits one block away, making this an easy add-on to a Capitol Hill walk that includes the Capitol grounds or Barracks Row a few blocks south on 8th Street.
