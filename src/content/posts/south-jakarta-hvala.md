## A Singapore matcha name crosses the Java Sea

Hvala built its following in Singapore. Its reputation rests on one thing: green tea taken seriously, in drinks and in desserts. South Jakarta is the first place the brand has chosen to open outside its home city.

That choice makes sense. South Jakarta is where the capital's café culture is most concentrated, and matcha has become one of the drinks Jakartans queue for. The name helps, too: *hvala* means "thank you" in Croatian, and it's easy to remember.

## What lands on the table

Matcha is the reason to come. Expect the menu to be built around it: vivid green drinks, plus small, carefully finished desserts meant to go alongside a cup rather than replace a meal.

We haven't been given the full Jakarta menu, and a new branch often adjusts its line-up during the first months. Look at the board or the posted menu when you arrive rather than going in set on one item. Prices are on the menu too.

A few things are true of good matcha anywhere, and they help you judge a cup:

- **Colour:** a bright, clean green usually means fresher, higher-grade powder. A dull olive tone suggests the opposite.
- **Texture:** properly whisked matcha should be smooth, with no gritty clumps at the bottom.
- **Taste:** good matcha tastes grassy and savoury, with only a gentle bitterness.

## Finding it in South Jakarta

South Jakarta (Jakarta Selatan) covers a large area. Its café scene spreads across neighbourhoods such as Senopati, Kemang and the streets around Blok M. Check Hvala's official listing for the exact street address before setting off, because "South Jakarta" alone won't get a driver to the right door.

Most visitors get around by ride-hailing app; Gojek and Grab both run cars and motorbike taxis. If the café turns out to be near the MRT Jakarta North–South line, which runs through Blok M and Senayan, the train lets you skip the worst of the road traffic.

## Ordering matcha the Jakarta way

Paying is straightforward in Jakarta's newer cafés. Card readers are common, and QRIS, Indonesia's national QR payment standard, is everywhere. If you have a local e-wallet or a banking app that supports it, you can scan and pay. Ask at the counter which methods this branch accepts.

Tipping isn't expected in Indonesia. Many cafés add tax, and sometimes a service charge, to the bill, so check the receipt before you leave anything extra.

A few habits will make the visit easier:

- **Specify your sugar level.** Indonesian cafés often sweeten drinks generously. If you want to taste the tea, ask for less sugar (*kurang manis*).
- **Stir before you sip.** Matcha settles in iced drinks, and the first sip is usually milkier than the last.
- **Ask before you camp.** Popular new cafés sometimes put time limits on tables at busy times. Check before you open a laptop.
- **Photograph quickly.** Layered matcha drinks look best in the first minute, before the ice melts and the colours blur.

## Picking your moment

There is no measured crowd data for this branch yet. What we do know is that new openings in Jakarta's café districts draw their biggest crowds at weekends, when people come to try the newest thing. A weekday visit is the safer bet if you'd rather not wait.

Jakarta's traffic matters as much as the queue. Weekday rush hours make crosstown trips slow, so a mid-afternoon visit usually means an easier ride both ways. From roughly November to March, heavy afternoon downpours are common. Leave yourself extra time, because ride-hailing demand jumps the moment the rain starts.

Confirm the current opening hours on Hvala's official social media before you go. A café in its first weeks may change its schedule.

## Turning one cup into an afternoon

A matcha and a dessert is a short stop, so plan on about 45 minutes to an hour. That leaves room to make a loop of South Jakarta's café streets. Senopati is good for another round of coffee or dinner afterwards. Kemang's lanes are more spread out, with older bars and galleries.

If the café puts you near Blok M, M Bloc Space is worth the walk. Its converted former government buildings now hold small shops and food stalls. The streets around Blok M are also known for Japanese restaurants, which fits well after an afternoon of green tea.

Whichever neighbourhood you're in, Hvala works as a calm start before South Jakarta's evening crowds come out.
