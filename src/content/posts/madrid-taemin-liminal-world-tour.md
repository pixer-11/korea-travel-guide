## A solo stage, one night in Madrid

This is a dance-first show. On December 3, 2026, Taemin brings his 2026–27 LiMiNaL World Tour to Palacio Vistalegre, the round arena in Madrid's southwestern Carabanchel district. It is a solo tour, separate from SHINee, the group he joined as its youngest member in 2008.

Taemin's solo career began in 2014 with "Danger." Then "Move" (2017) made slow, controlled choreography his signature. "Criminal" and "Guilty" pushed the concept darker and more theatrical. His concerts tend to centre on dancing, staging and costume as much as vocals, so a seat with a clear view of the whole stage is worth more than a close one at an awkward angle.

## The arena in the old bullring

The shape tells you what the building used to be. Palacio Vistalegre stands where the Vista Alegre bullring once stood, and it reopened in 2000 as a covered venue for concerts, sport and events. The seating still rises steeply in a ring around a circular floor, so even the upper tiers look down onto the stage instead of along a long hall.

Spanish ticket terms worth knowing:

- **Pista**: the floor area
- **Grada**: the tiered seating around it
- **Puerta**: the gate number printed on your ticket
- **Fila / Asiento**: row / seat

## Riding Line 5 to Vista Alegre

The Metro is the obvious way in. **Vista Alegre** station (Line 5) is a few minutes' walk from the arena. **Oporto** (Lines 5 and 6) is a short walk the other way and connects to the Line 6 circular. From the centre, Line 5 runs directly from Callao, Ópera and La Latina with no change.

When the show ends, the Vista Alegre platform fills fast. Plenty of attendees walk ten minutes to Oporto or south to Carabanchel station, both on the same line, to skip the crush. Single tickets and the rechargeable Tarjeta Multi are sold at machines in every station.

## How Madrid's Shawol do show night

The crowd brings its own habits. Floor-ticket holders at K-pop arena shows in Europe often queue outside for hours, and Madrid's fans are no exception. Pearl aqua, SHINee's official colour, turns up on banners, lightsticks and clothing all through the stands. Many fans learn the Korean fan chants from the official chant guides that circulate online, then shout them during the intros and bridges.

A few local norms help:

- **Pay by card.** Contactless cards are normal in Madrid bars and shops, so small purchases rarely need cash.
- **Eat on Madrid time.** Dinner usually starts around 9pm or later, so the bars stay busy after the encore. Attendees typically have a late lunch or an early tapas stop before the arena.
- **Speak a little Spanish.** "Una caña" gets you a small draught beer and "la cuenta, por favor" gets you the bill. Tipping is optional; rounding up is usual.
- **Mind the bag.** Arenas in Spain usually search bags at the gate, so fans tend to bring a small one.

## Eating and staying nearby

Carabanchel is a residential district, not a hotel area. Calle General Ricardos, near Oporto, has a long run of everyday bars and cafés serving tapas, bocadillos and coffee. It is where most of the pre-show eating happens.

Most visitors stay in the centre around Sol, Ópera or La Latina. Line 5 puts them back at their hotel without a change. Madrid Río, the riverside park, is a short ride north at Marqués de Vadillo if you have a free afternoon on the day.

## Madrid in early December

The evenings are cold. Madrid is dry and sunny in December, but temperatures drop sharply after dark, so the walk from the station and the outdoor queue call for a proper coat.

The city's Christmas lights are already up by this point. The Christmas market fills Plaza Mayor, and the light displays along Gran Vía and around Sol make the trip back into town worth a detour. December 6 (Constitution Day) and December 8 (Immaculate Conception) are national holidays. Many Spaniards take the days between them off for the "puente de diciembre," so central hotels and trains are busy that week.
