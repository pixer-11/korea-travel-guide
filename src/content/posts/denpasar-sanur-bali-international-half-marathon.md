## Three distances on Bali's east coast

Sanur faces sunrise. This stretch of Denpasar looks east across the Badung Strait toward Nusa Penida. It is flatter and slower than Kuta or Canggu, which suits a road race.

The Sanur Bali International Half Marathon takes place on February 7, 2027. It is an annual international race with three distances:

- **5K**: the entry point for first-timers and families
- **10K**: the mid-distance most travelling runners pair with a holiday
- **Half marathon (21.1K)**: the headline race

The organiser publishes registration, route and start-time details on the event's official site.

## Heat, humidity and February

February falls in Bali's wet season. Mornings are humid even before the sun clears the horizon, and heavy showers usually arrive in the afternoon. Road races in Bali typically start before dawn, which keeps the half marathon field out of the worst heat.

Runners coming from colder climates usually treat 21K here as harder than the same distance at home. Many pick a target pace slower than their personal best. Hydration matters more than kit. Lightweight, quick-drying clothing copes with both sweat and a passing downpour.

## Getting to Sanur and staying close

I Gusti Ngurah Rai International Airport is roughly 15 to 20 km south of Sanur. The drive runs along the Ngurah Rai Bypass and usually takes 30 to 45 minutes, depending on traffic. Grab and Gojek both operate in the area, along with metered Bluebird taxis.

Most international runners stay in Sanur itself, so they can walk to the start. This avoids pre-dawn traffic and road closures. Accommodation runs the length of Jalan Danau Tamblingan, from guesthouses to the beachfront resorts near the Grand Inna Bali Beach, which was Bali's first high-rise hotel when it opened in the 1960s.

## Race-weekend habits in Sanur

Sanur goes to bed early and wakes early. On ordinary mornings the paved beach path fills with walkers and cyclists before 7am, and race morning starts even earlier.

- **Watch your feet.** Small palm-leaf offerings called *canang sari*, with flowers and incense, sit on pavements and doorsteps every day. Step around them.
- **Carry some rupiah.** Card payment works in hotels and larger cafés. Beachfront warung and street stalls usually want cash.
- **Ride-hailing pickups.** Roads close around a race, so drivers typically meet passengers on a side street off the bypass. Gojek motorbike taxis get through traffic faster than cars.
- **Cover up off the course.** Shoulders and knees should be covered at temples, and Balinese temples provide a sarong and sash at the entrance.

After the race, runners often walk the beach path north toward the Le Mayeur Museum, the former home of the Belgian painter Adrien-Jean Le Mayeur. In the evening, the Sindhu night market serves cheap Balinese and Indonesian dishes.

Some runners stay on for an extra day. Fast boats leave Sanur's beach for Nusa Lembongan and Nusa Penida, which makes the island trip a common recovery outing.
