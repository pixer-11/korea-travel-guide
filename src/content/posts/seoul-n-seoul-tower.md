## Getting up the mountain

N Seoul Tower stands on Namsan Mountain, at 105 Namsangongwon-gil in Yongsan District. Three ways up: the Namsan cable car from Myeong-dong station (Line 4), a free shuttle bus from Myeong-dong or Jongno, or the hiking paths through Namsan Park.

Cable car lines form fast on clear weekends. Hiking takes 30–40 minutes from the base and costs nothing but legs; the trail passes the old fortress wall sections still standing from the Joseon Dynasty.

Built in 1980, the tower predates most of Seoul's skyline. It was originally a broadcast relay tower before opening to the public as an observation deck.

## What's actually up there

The main draw is the observation deck, glassed in and wrapped around the tower's upper section, giving 360-degree views over the Han River and the city grid below. A revolving restaurant sits above the deck, rotating slowly enough that a full meal completes one turn.

Below the tower, the terrace is famous for its fences of padlocks, thousands clipped on by couples, a tradition specific to this spot rather than a citywide custom. Bring your own lock if you want to add one; vendors nearby sell them too.

Sunset and after-dark are the two moments worth timing for. The tower lights up at night and the city view below turns into a grid of light, which is the version most photos show.

## When to go

This is one of Seoul's most visited sites, so expect crowds; it is not an off-the-radar spot and weekends run heavy.

- Weekdays: steady traffic 10am to 10pm, no sharp lull
- Weekends: busiest 11am–9pm, avoid this window if you can
- Weekend quiet window: 10pm–11pm, right before closing
- Weekday closing: 10:30pm; weekend closing: 11pm

Arriving right at the 10am opening on a weekday is the surest way to get the deck to yourself. If you can only go on a Saturday or Sunday, aim for the last hour before close instead of midday.

## Buying tickets and moving through

Ticket counters sit at the tower base and also inside the cable car station; buying at the base skips one line if the cable car queue is already long.

- Combined cable car + observatory tickets save a second wait in line
- The observation deck ticket is separate from the restaurant, which takes its own reservation
- Padlocks can be bought streetside near the terrace if you don't bring one
- Namsan Park at the base has walking paths worth an hour if you arrive early and the tower isn't open yet

Check current hours before heading up on a public holiday, since holiday scheduling sometimes shifts. Most visitors spend 60–90 minutes total: enough for the deck, the padlock terrace, and photos, not counting a restaurant meal.
