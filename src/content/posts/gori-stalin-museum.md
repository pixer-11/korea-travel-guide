## A palace built for one man

Gori kept its most famous son on its main street. The museum's sandstone hall, completed in 1957 in the grand Stalinist style, faces Stalin Avenue with a clock tower and arched loggias. It reads more like a ministry than a museum, and that is deliberate.

In front, a columned stone pavilion shelters the small brick house where Ioseb Jughashvili was born in 1878. His family rented one room here. The canopy turns a cobbler's lodging into a shrine, and the contrast sets the tone for everything inside.

## Inside the halls

A red-carpeted staircase climbs to the first floor, where a white statue of Stalin waits at the top. The rooms run in chronological order:

- **Early life:** school records, seminary years in Tbilisi, revolutionary mugshots.
- **Power and war:** portraits, gifts from foreign delegations, Yalta and Tehran conference material.
- **The death mask:** displayed in a darkened circular hall, the visit's most theatrical moment.

The tone is reverent, and the curation has barely changed since Soviet times. Critics point out how little it says about the purges, famine and Gulag. A small room on Soviet-era repression has been added, but it is easy to miss, so ask staff where it is. Go expecting a Soviet artefact as much as a biography.

Outside, on the east side, stands Stalin's green armoured Pullman carriage. He used it for travel, including to the 1945 Yalta Conference. You can step inside to see the compact bathroom, the bunk and the conference room.

## Touring the museum without missing the point

This is one of Georgia's most-visited museums, so it is never a quiet find. Make the most of it this way:

- **Join a guided tour.** Guides lead groups through the halls in English and Russian, among other languages. Their framing is often the most interesting part, as some guides add pointed commentary of their own. Ask at the ticket desk when the next English group starts.
- **Check what your ticket covers.** The carriage interior and photography rules have changed over time. Confirm both at the desk; prices are posted there too.
- **Arrive in the morning.** Day-trippers from Tbilisi usually get to Gori by late morning, so a 10am start often means smaller groups.
- **Keep your voice down.** Some visitors, mostly older ones from the former Soviet Union, come to pay respects. The staff treat the place with matching formality.
- **Leave time for the shop.** It sells Stalin busts, wine and souvenirs, an odd exhibit in its own right.

## Getting to Gori and what's nearby

Marshrutkas (shared minibuses) to Gori leave from Tbilisi's Didube bus station throughout the day. The trip takes about an hour. There are also trains from Tbilisi Central Station. From Gori's centre the museum is a short walk down Stalin Avenue.

Pair the museum with:

- **Gori Fortress (Goristsikhe):** the medieval citadel on the hill above town, a 15-minute walk away, with views over the Kartli plain.
- **Uplistsikhe:** a rock-cut cave town about 10 km east, reachable by taxi. Most people combine it with the museum in one day.
- **Mtskheta:** an easy stop on the drive back towards Tbilisi.

A full day covers Gori and Uplistsikhe comfortably, with the museum taking up the morning.
