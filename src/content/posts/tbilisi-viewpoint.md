Most visitors to Tbilisi do their looking from Narikala or Mtatsminda. This spot faces the other way. It's marked on Google simply as "Viewpoint", plus code MQW2+XR, and it sits on the green slopes at the city's western edge. From there the city spreads away from you to the east, and the dense blocks of Vake and Saburtalo fill the foreground.

It has only a modest number of reviews, and they are strongly positive. That's typical of a place people find by chance and are pleased they did.

## A lookout with no ticket booth

Keep your expectations right. This is a vantage point, not a monument. Google lists no gate, no entry desk and no fixed schedule, and it shows the spot as open 24 hours on every day of the current week.

What you get is distance and height. Downtown Tbilisi sits in a river valley with hills on both sides. From the western slopes you look across the whole bowl, toward the ridges on the far side of the Mtkvari.

- **Time to spend:** 30–60 minutes, or longer if you bring something to eat
- **Facilities:** don't count on any. Bring water and a layer for the wind.
- **Footwear:** the hills here are unpaved in places, so wear shoes with grip

## Finding MQW2+XR

A generic name causes generic search results. If you type "viewpoint Tbilisi" into a map, you'll get a dozen lookouts. Type the plus code **MQW2+XR, Tbilisi** instead. Google Maps and most other map apps will drop a pin on the exact spot.

The code places it on the hills above Vake, in the same area as Turtle Lake (Kus Tba) and the wooded slopes behind Vake Park. Ride-hailing apps such as Bolt are the usual way Tbilisi residents get around, and they accept a dropped pin. Check the pin against your map before you confirm the ride. Cars may only be able to get close, so expect to walk the last stretch.

## Pairing it with Vake and Turtle Lake

The area around it is reason enough to go. Vake Park has a long tree-lined central avenue and a war memorial, and its main entrance is on Chavchavadze Avenue. Above the park is Turtle Lake, a small lake with a walking path around it. A cable car runs up to the lake from the Vake side.

Further along the same hills is the Open Air Museum of Ethnography. Traditional houses from across Georgia were moved there and rebuilt on the slope. A loop could look like this:

- Vake Park in the morning or early afternoon
- Up to Turtle Lake for a slow walk around the water
- The viewpoint as the light starts to drop
- Back down to Chavchavadze Avenue for dinner in Vake

## Reading the light

Because you're looking east, late afternoon works well here. The setting sun lights the city in front of you instead of shining into your camera. On clear days in spring and autumn, the far ridges look sharp. In midsummer the valley often holds a haze.

Winter has short days and colder wind on the ridge. Even so, a clear day after rain can give the cleanest views of the year.

The 24-hour listing doesn't mean the path is lit or easy after dark. If you stay for the city lights, bring a phone torch and know your route down before you start. Go with someone else if you can.

## Up here at dusk

This is a quieter find than the Old Town lookouts, and that changes how it feels. Nobody is selling anything at the top and there's no stream of tour groups. Treat it like a neighbourhood hillside, because that's what it is.

- **Paying:** nothing to pay at the lookout. Card and ride-hailing apps cover your transport. Carry a few lari for a kiosk or a bus on the way down.
- **Etiquette:** Georgians often bring food and drink up to green spaces. If you join in, take your bottles and wrappers back down with you.
- **Language:** a viewpoint is *gadasakhedi* (გადასახედი). Showing the plus code on your screen is easier than pronouncing it.
- **Getting back:** taxi pickups from the hills can take a while. Book the ride before you leave the top, not once you reach the road.

The mistake that costs people most here is a pin dropped on the wrong "Viewpoint". Check the plus code before you set off.
