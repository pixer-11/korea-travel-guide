## A square under a church tower

Market Hill sits right beside the tower of Great St Mary's, the University Church. Rows of canvas-roofed stalls fill the open space between the church and the Guildhall. Narrow lanes run between them, and in the middle stands a stone fountain where people perch with paper trays of lunch.

People have traded on this spot since the Middle Ages, which is why the city's streets all seem to lead here. It isn't a secret. It's one of the best-known stops in the city centre and draws a steady flow of visitors every day. The trick is choosing when you go.

## What's on the stalls

The mix changes from stall to stall and day to day, but these are the main groups:

- **Hot food and street food**, the stalls that draw the lunch crowd
- **Gourmet produce**: cheeses, baked goods and fresh fruit and vegetables
- **Cambridge University clothing and souvenirs**: hoodies, scarves and college-crest gifts
- **Odds and ends** like books, flowers and household bits, depending on who's trading

Sundays have usually leaned towards arts, crafts and local produce. If you're after handmade things rather than lunch, a Sunday walk round is worth it.

## Beating the crowds on Market Hill

Get there early on a weekday. Measured foot traffic shows the square is quietest between 10am and 11am on weekdays. That's also right at opening, so traders are still setting out and nobody is queuing for food yet.

Weekends are a different story. Between 11am and 4pm on Saturdays and Sundays the lanes fill up, the food queues get longer and you'll struggle to find space on the fountain steps. If a weekend is your only option, come at 10am and be finished before the rush.

Note the early close. At the moment the market runs 10am–4pm, so a late-afternoon visit after punting will usually mean stalls packing away.

## Eating the way Cambridge does it

There's no proper seating, so think of it as street food in the old sense. Order at the stall front, wait close by for your name or number, then take your food somewhere else. Locals spill out to:

- The fountain steps in the middle of the square
- The low walls around Great St Mary's
- The lawns of Parker's Piece, a ten-minute walk south-east, when it's dry

Most traders take contactless cards. A few small stalls may still prefer cash, so carry a little as backup. Tipping isn't expected at a market stall. Queue in a single line along the stall front, and step back to the side once you've ordered so the next person can reach the counter.

Bikes are everywhere in central Cambridge. When you cross from the square towards King's Parade, look both ways for cyclists as well as cars.

## Reaching Market Hill

The square is in the middle of the city centre, and almost all of it is easy to walk. From Cambridge railway station it's roughly a 25-minute walk north-west along Hills Road and Regent Street. Plenty of city buses also run from the station towards the centre.

If you're driving, the city centre is tight and parking is limited. Cambridge's Park & Ride sites on the edge of the city have buses into the centre, which saves you circling the narrow streets. From Drummer Street bus station, the square is under ten minutes on foot.

## Around the corner

The market is a good starting point because so much is close by:

- **Great St Mary's Church**: climb the tower for a view straight down over the stall roofs and across to King's College Chapel
- **King's Parade and King's College**: a couple of minutes west
- **The Guildhall**: on the square's southern side
- **The Corn Exchange**: a short walk south, for evening shows
- **The Backs and the river**: about ten minutes west, where the punts set off

Give the market itself 45 minutes to an hour, longer if you're stopping for lunch. Then head for the colleges while the morning is still young.
