## A pagoda roof over a Counter-Strike stage

Look up as you walk in. The Singapore Indoor Stadium is the Kenzo Tange building with the sweeping, pagoda-like roof, and it has stood on the edge of the Kallang Basin since 1989. For four days in December 2026, its seated bowl surrounds a single stage built for the PGL Major Singapore 2026 Playoffs.

This is a Counter-Strike 2 Major, the top tier of the CS2 calendar. The playoffs are the closing stretch, after the earlier stages have cut the field down. Each round eliminates teams until one lifts the trophy. Expect an arena crowd reacting to every clutch round together, which is the reason to come instead of watching the stream at home.

## Four days, 10 to 13 December 2026

The playoffs run from 10 to 13 December 2026. Majors usually narrow from more matches on the early days to one deciding series at the end, so the last day tends to be the most sought-after ticket.

Start times, the match order and the team list are not given here. Confirm the schedule and the bracket on PGL's official channels. Plan for long sessions, because a best-of series in CS2 can easily run several hours.

December falls in Singapore's northeast monsoon season. Heavy afternoon and evening showers are common. Keep a compact umbrella with you for the walk between the MRT and the arena doors.

## Getting to Kallang by MRT

The train beats a taxi on event days. Your options:

- **Stadium MRT (Circle Line, CC6):** the closest station. Follow the signs for the Sports Hub, and it is a short covered walk to the Indoor Stadium.
- **Kallang MRT (East-West Line, EW10):** a longer walk along the riverside, handy if you are coming from the city centre or Changi Airport via the green line.
- **Ride-hailing:** Grab and taxis drop off around the Sports Hub, but expect queues when a session lets out.

The arena shares the precinct with the National Stadium, Kallang Wave Mall and the OCBC Aquatic Centre. If the National Stadium has its own event on the same night, the station gets crowded fast.

## Inside the bowl: how a Singapore event night works

Singapore runs on contactless payment. Tap a Visa or Mastercard bank card or a phone wallet straight on the MRT gates; there is no need to buy a separate ticket. Inside the arena and the mall, cards and mobile payments are the norm.

A few local habits keep the night smooth:

- **No eating or drinking on the MRT.** Enforcement is real, so finish your drink on the platform side before you tap in.
- **Tipping isn't expected.** Sit-down restaurants usually add a service charge to the bill already.
- **Queue in lines.** Singaporeans queue patiently for food and train doors, and pushing in draws looks.
- **Bring a layer.** Indoor air conditioning is set cold, a sharp contrast to the humidity outside.

Plan to arrive early on the final day. It's the session most people want, and getting through security and the merch lines takes time.

## Eating before and after the matches

Kallang Wave Mall sits right beside the Indoor Stadium. It has a food court and chain restaurants, which makes it the easiest place to eat between sessions.

For a proper hawker meal, ride the Circle Line two stops to Dakota MRT (CC8). From there, walk to Old Airport Road Food Centre, one of the city's best-known hawker centres, with rows of stalls under one roof. Order at the stall, pay there, and carry your tray to any free table. A packet of tissues left on a seat means it is taken.

If you have a free morning, walk along the Kallang Basin waterfront. You get views of the arena roof from outside before you head in for the evening's matches.
