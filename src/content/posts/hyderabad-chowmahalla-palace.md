## Why go

You come to Chowmahalla for the moment you step through the gate and the noise of Khilwath Road just falls away. Four courtyards unfold in sequence, each one quieter and more formal than the last, until you reach the Khilwat Mubarak — the durbar hall where the Nizams of Hyderabad once held court under rows of chandeliers. The pale yellow arcades, the reflecting pools running down the center of the marble floor, the sheer scale of the place: this is what remains of a dynasty that ruled one of pre-independence India's richest princely states, and it still reads as a working seat of power rather than a museum piece.

## Getting there

Chowmahalla Palace sits in the old city at 20-4-236, Khilwath Road, in the Khilwat neighborhood, a few minutes' walk south of Charminar and Laad Bazaar. Most visitors combine the two in one outing — you can walk it in under ten minutes through the bangle-shop lanes, or take a short autorickshaw ride if you're coming from further out, say Hussain Sagar or Banjara Hills.

The old city's streets narrow considerably near the palace, and parking is tight, so an auto or a walk beats driving yourself. There's no direct metro stop at the palace itself; the closest useful station is on the Old City side, but honestly, an auto from wherever you're staying is the simplest way in.

## What to see inside

Start in the Khilwat Mubarak, the palace's centerpiece, where the Nizam once received guests and where his marble throne still sits beneath a ceiling of Belgian chandeliers — count them and you'll lose track well before you reach the far wall. From there the route takes you through the Council Hall, a clock tower that's kept remarkably good time since the 19th century, and a costume and textile gallery with the brocades and turbans the royal household actually wore.

Don't skip the courtyard housing the vintage car collection — a fleet of the Nizam's old Rolls-Royces and other classics parked as if waiting for him to step out. The Bara Imam courtyard, the palace's oldest surviving section, has a stillness to it that the busier front rooms don't; the light falls differently through its arches in late afternoon.

## When to go

Chowmahalla draws heavy year-round footfall — this is one of Hyderabad's most-visited monuments, with tour groups and school trips filling the courtyards by mid-morning. Arrive close to opening time on a weekday if you want the durbar hall to yourself for even a few minutes; by midday, especially on weekends, the courtyards fill and the photo queues at the throne room lengthen noticeably.

Winter mornings (November through February) are the most comfortable time weather-wise — Hyderabad's summer sun turns the open courtyards into a furnace by 11am, so plan around that if you're visiting between March and June. The palace is typically closed one day a week (Fridays, historically), so confirm current opening days before you go.

## How to visit like a local

Buy your ticket at the counter near the entrance — cash and card are both generally accepted, but carry some cash as a backup since card machines at heritage sites can be unreliable. Photography passes for cameras (not phones) usually cost extra, so decide before you queue whether you're bringing a proper camera in. Because this is a heavily trafficked stop on every Hyderabad itinerary, the single biggest mistake visitors make is treating it as a quick 20-minute add-on to Charminar — rushing the Khilwat Mubarak means missing the textile gallery and the car courtyard entirely, both of which reward slower viewing.

Wear something that covers shoulders and knees comfortably; it's not strictly enforced everywhere, but the palace still functions as a heritage and event space and dressing modestly fits the setting. Give yourself a full two hours, go early, and save Laad Bazaar's bangle shopping for after — you'll want the energy for both.

## Nearby to combine

Charminar and Laad Bazaar are a short walk away, and Mecca Masjid, one of India's largest mosques, sits right beside Charminar too. Together they make a natural half-day loop through the old city: palace in the morning, mosque and monument at midday, bazaar for the afternoon.
