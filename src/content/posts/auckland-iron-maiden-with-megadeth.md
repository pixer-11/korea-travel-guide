## Two nights on Mahuhu Crescent

Spark Arena stands at the end of Mahuhu Crescent, below Parnell, with the railway lines and the port behind it. On November 7 and 8, 2026, Iron Maiden plays that bowl with Megadeth on the same bill. Both nights are in Auckland, at the same venue.

The pairing has some history behind it. Iron Maiden formed in Leyton, East London, in 1975 under bassist Steve Harris, so these shows fall just past the band's half-century. Megadeth started in Los Angeles in 1983, after Dave Mustaine was fired from Metallica. Mustaine has since said the band's latest album is its last and has called the tour that follows it a farewell.

## Fifty years of Eddie, a last lap for Megadeth

Expect black shirts everywhere: tour designs from the 1980s next to new ones, and Eddie, the band's undead mascot, on half the chests in the queue. Eddie has turned up in some form at Maiden shows for decades. He is the reason first-timers keep their eyes on the stage between songs.

Megadeth fans tend to come for the guitar work. That means fast, technical thrash, and Mustaine's snarl over it.

The two crowds overlap a great deal. Many people at Spark Arena saw both bands decades ago, and a fair number bring the kids they raised on them. A double bill like this works as one long night of metal, not a headliner with a warm-up act.

## Inside Spark Arena

The arena opened in 2007 as Vector Arena. It holds around 12,000 people for concerts and is Auckland's main indoor venue for big touring acts. The layout is a standard bowl:

- **The floor**: usually standing general admission for rock shows, with the densest crowd at the front.
- **Lower tiers**: raked seating close to the stage, with a clear sightline over the floor.
- **Upper tiers**: steeper seating higher up, where you see the whole lighting rig and stage set.

Seated tickets suit anyone who wants to see Maiden's stage changes in full. The floor is the place for the crush and the noise. Concourses ring the bowl with food, drink and merchandise stands. Merch queues at metal shows usually peak as doors open, then shorten once the first band starts.

## Walking in from Britomart

Britomart is the simplest way in. The city's main rail hub sits in the downtown Britomart precinct, which is served by Auckland's train lines, many bus routes and the Downtown Ferry Terminal across Quay Street.

From the station it is a flat walk east along Quay Street or Beach Road towards Quay Park, roughly 10–15 minutes. On show nights you only need to follow the crowd in band shirts. Auckland Transport fares take an AT HOP card, and contactless bank cards work at the readers too.

Driving is the harder option. Parking near the arena is limited and the roads around Quay Park clog after the encore. Many locals skip it: they park further out and take the train in, or get dropped off. Rideshare pick-ups after the show tend to be slow while the arena empties.

## Gigs, gates and the Auckland way of doing it

- **Earplugs**: regular gig-goers carry them, because two loud bands in an enclosed bowl add up.
- **Layers**: November is late spring in Auckland. Evenings can turn cool by the harbour, while the arena floor gets hot.
- **Meeting point**: groups usually pick a spot outside, since phone signal drops when the bowl is full.
- **Floor etiquette**: mosh pits form at thrash shows, and the custom is to pick up anyone who falls.

## Before the doors and after the encore

The streets around the arena give you choices on both sides of the show. Britomart's lanes, between Customs Street and Quay Street, are packed with bars and restaurants. That makes the precinct the usual pre-show meeting spot for people coming in by train.

Up the hill, Parnell has its own strip of cafés and restaurants along Parnell Road. Beyond it are the Auckland Domain and the Auckland War Memorial Museum, which visitors in town for the weekend often fit in on the daytime between the two nights. Viaduct Harbour, a short walk west of Britomart, stays busy late and is where many people end up after the show.

Visitors from outside Auckland usually stay in the CBD or around the Viaduct, so they can walk back after the encore. Ferries from the Downtown Ferry Terminal also run out to Devonport and Waiheke Island for a slower day between shows.
