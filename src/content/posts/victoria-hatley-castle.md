## A coal fortune in stone

Hatley Castle was built for money, not defence. James Dunsmuir, son of a Vancouver Island coal baron and later premier of British Columbia, commissioned it in 1908. Victoria architect Samuel Maclure designed it, crenellated tower and all.

You approach through parkland. The castle sits above lawns that slope toward Esquimalt Lagoon. Peacocks wander the grounds and the terraces, and they do not move for anyone.

After the Dunsmuirs, the federal government bought the estate. It became a naval training establishment in 1940, then Royal Roads Military College. Since 1995 it has housed Royal Roads University, so you are walking through a working campus.

## Gardens, museum and the screen

The grounds are the reason most people come. Formal sections are laid out in sequence:

- **Italian Garden**: clipped hedges and stonework below the castle walls
- **Japanese Garden**: ponds, bridges and maples, best in autumn colour
- **Rose Garden**: peak bloom runs through early summer
- **Old-growth forest trails**: these lead off toward the lagoon

The **Hatley Park Museum** in the castle's lower level covers the Dunsmuir family, the estate staff and the military college years. Plan 30–45 minutes there.

Film fans will recognise the facade. It stood in for Xavier's School for Gifted Youngsters in several X-Men films, and for the Luthor mansion in *Smallville*. Allow two to three hours for gardens, museum and a tour together.

## Getting to Colwood

The address reads 2005 Sooke Road, but the castle is in Colwood on the West Shore, not in Victoria proper. Driving from downtown takes about 25 minutes via the Trans-Canada Highway and Old Island Highway, or take Sooke Road. BC Transit buses serve the campus; check the trip planner for current routes.

Pair it with **Fort Rodd Hill and Fisgard Lighthouse**, a national historic site a few minutes away. Or walk the spit at **Esquimalt Lagoon**, directly below the estate, for views back to the Olympic Mountains.

## Timing a campus visit

At the moment, the site opens 10am–4pm every day this week. Garden and castle hours shift through the year and on holidays, so confirm on the Hatley Park official site before you set out.

The calmest window on weekdays is 10am–11am, so arrive at opening. On weekends, avoid 11am–4pm if you can: that whole stretch is the busy period, and paths around the Italian Garden fill up. If Saturday is your only day, be at the gate at 10am.

A few habits help on a working campus:

- **Book castle tours ahead.** Interior access is by guided tour only, and slots sell out in summer. Buy through the official site.
- **Check parking signage.** Lots are campus lots with posted rules and payment; the current price is shown at the machines.
- **Stay out of university buildings.** Classes run year-round, and graduation and wedding events sometimes close sections of the grounds.
- **Give the peacocks room.** Do not feed them.

Bring a layer even in summer. Breeze off the lagoon cools the lower gardens quickly.
