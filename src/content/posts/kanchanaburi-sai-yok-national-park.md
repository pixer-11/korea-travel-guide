## What's actually here

Sai Yok National Park covers forested limestone hills either side of the Khwae Noi River, roughly two hours by road from Kanchanaburi town. The signature sight is Sai Yok Yai waterfall, a short, wide cascade that drops directly into the river beside a suspension bridge, so you can watch it from the water or walk right up to the base.

Inland, Daowadueng Cave requires a steep 20-30 minute climb but rewards with large limestone chambers; bring a flashlight if you don't want to rely on the on-site lighting. Diamond Cave (Tham Phet) is a flatter, easier alternative near the visitor center.

The park also protects Kitti's hog-nosed bat, the world's smallest mammal, first documented in caves here in the 1970s.

## Getting there and getting around

Most visitors drive or hire a car from Kanchanaburi town, about a 2-hour trip on Highway 323. Public songthaews run from Kanchanaburi's bus terminal toward Sai Yok but are infrequent; a private driver or organized tour is far more reliable for a single-day visit.

Once inside, the park road connects the visitor center, waterfall, and cave trailheads; expect to walk between them, as there's no shuttle. Raft houses along the river are usually booked as overnight packages that include the boat transfer from a nearby pier.

The park is open daily, 7am to 5pm; arrive well before mid-afternoon to leave time for both the waterfall and a cave hike before closing.

## Raft houses and overnight stays

The floating raft houses moored along the Khwae Noi are the park's other calling card, simple wooden bungalows lashed together and towed to a quiet stretch of river. They're basic: no air-con on most, shared or simple ensuite bathrooms, and evenings lit by lantern rather than streetlight.

Staying overnight buys you the river at dawn, when mist sits on the water and the forest across the bank is loudest with birdsong, well before day-trip crowds arrive by road. Book raft-house packages through a Kanchanaburi tour operator or guesthouse rather than trying to arrange one independently on arrival.

## Making the most of a day trip

This is a well-known stop on the Kanchanaburi circuit, often paired with the Hellfire Pass Memorial or Erawan National Park, so don't expect solitude at the main waterfall on weekends and holidays.

- Wear sandals or water shoes if you plan to wade at Sai Yok Yai; the rocks stay slick.
- Bring a headlamp for Daowadueng Cave rather than relying only on installed lights.
- Pack a dry bag for cameras and phones if you're arriving by longtail boat to a raft house.
- Combine the visit with Hellfire Pass, about 20km further north, to make the drive worthwhile.

Weekday mornings, right after the 7am opening, are the quietest window before tour groups and school trips build through midday.
