## A town hall inside a national park

Most visitors never think about who runs Banff. The answer sits at 110 Bear St, one block west of the crowds on Banff Avenue. This is the seat of the Town of Banff, the municipal government of the townsite.

Banff is unusual: it is a self-governing town that sits entirely within Banff National Park. Parks Canada still holds authority over land use, so the town operates inside federal rules that most Canadian municipalities never face. For a curious traveller, this building is where that arrangement takes physical form.

## When it's open and why you'd go

Plan this for a weekday. Town hall currently keeps office hours:

- **Monday to Friday:** 8:30am–4:30pm
- **Saturday and Sunday:** closed

People who aren't residents usually come for practical reasons rather than sightseeing:

- sorting out a municipal parking matter
- asking about town permits or bylaws, including rules for short-term rentals and businesses
- picking up municipal information on transit, recycling and wildlife-aware waste rules

If you're interested in local politics, council meetings are open to the public. You'll see a park town argue over housing, traffic and growth limits. The town's official website posts the schedule and agendas, so check it before you plan around a meeting.

## Doing business at the counter

Treat this as a working office, not an attraction. Staff serve residents and businesses first, so arrive with a specific question and the paperwork you need. A vague "what's here?" won't get you far.

- **Come early in the day.** Arriving close to 8:30am leaves room for a follow-up before the 4:30pm close.
- **Don't leave a weekend problem for the weekend.** A parking issue on Saturday waits until Monday, because the building is shut. Online services are your only option then.
- **Paying:** expect standard Canadian options, such as debit or credit card. Confirm current methods on the town's site before you go.
- **Getting here:** walk. Downtown Banff is compact, and Bear Street is a short block from Banff Avenue. Roam Transit, the local bus network, stops nearby. Driving in the park also requires a valid Parks Canada pass.

The reception is polite and direct, in the usual Canadian civic style. Line up, wait to be called, and say clearly what you need.

## The rest of Bear Street

This block is the quieter cultural side of downtown. Once you're done at the counter, there's plenty within a few minutes' walk:

- **Whyte Museum of the Canadian Rockies**, across the street, covers mountain art, archives and early Banff history.
- **Banff Public Library**, at the Bear Street end of the block, is a calm place to check email or browse.
- **Central Park and the Bow River**, a few minutes south, has riverside paths and benches.
- **Banff Avenue**, one block east, is lined with shops and restaurants and looks north to Cascade Mountain.

Give the town hall itself 15–30 minutes, depending on your errand. Then spend an hour or two on the museum and the river, and you've seen a side of Banff that most day-trippers drive straight past.
