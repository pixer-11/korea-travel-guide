## Three nights of Jay Chou in Tianhe

Picture an open-air bowl full of tens of thousands of phone screens. Then thousands of voices come in on the first line of "Qing Hua Ci" before Jay Chou reaches the second. Jay Chou Carnival II World Tour - Guangzhou plays Tianhe Sports Center Stadium on December 4, 5 and 6, 2026.

Jay Chou has been the biggest name in Mandopop for more than two decades. He writes his own songs and mixes R&B, hip-hop and Chinese classical sounds in a way nobody else does. A stadium crowd in Guangzhou knows the back catalogue by heart, from "Nunchucks" and "Daoxiang" to the later hit "Mojito". Many fans have been singing along since secondary school.

## A stadium in the middle of the new city

Tianhe Sports Center was built for China's 1987 National Games. It is a large, flat block of green space, arenas and wide plazas, ringed by some of Guangzhou's busiest shopping streets. The stadium sits at the centre of the complex, so you walk across open paving to reach it.

The location is what makes these shows easy. Guangzhou East Railway Station is just to the north. The skyscrapers of Zhujiang New Town line up to the south, ending at the Pearl River and Canton Tower. That means you can get to a concert here from almost anywhere in the city without needing a car.

## Riding the metro to Tianhe Sports Center

The metro is how most of the crowd arrives. Driving makes little sense: roads around the complex are slow on an ordinary evening, never mind a concert night.

- **Tiyu Zhongxin station (Line 1):** sits right against the sports centre and is the closest stop.
- **Tiyu Xilu station (Lines 1 and 3):** a major interchange on the western edge, a short walk away. It suits anyone coming from the airport on Line 3.
- **Guangzhou East Railway Station:** high-speed and intercity trains arrive here, a walk or one metro stop north.

After the show, the stations nearest the stadium fill up as tens of thousands of people head home together. Many concertgoers walk a stop further, or wait out the first rush at a restaurant before heading underground.

## Getting through the gates on show night

Stadium concerts in mainland China run on real-name ticketing. The name on the ticket matches the holder's ID. At the entrance, staff usually scan each attendee's ID card or passport, and then comes the security check. International visitors carry their passport for this reason. A photocopy or a photo on a phone is not the same document.

Security works much like airport screening, with bag checks and walk-through scanners. Large professional cameras, tripods and selfie sticks are normally turned away at events of this size. Lighters and big power banks often get extra attention too.

Inside, the culture is all about joining in. Fans bring light sticks and fan-made banners, and the whole stadium sings full choruses with Jay. On ballads like "Simple Love" ("Jiandan Ai"), nobody is hiding their voice. Scalpers hang around the plazas, but with tickets tied to names, buying from them can leave you stuck outside.

## Eating and staying around the stadium

Malls packed with food surround the sports centre. Teemall (Tianhecheng) faces it on Tianhe Road. Grandview Mall is just to the east, and both have floor after floor of restaurants, from Cantonese roast meats to hotpot and bubble tea. Before a show these food courts are full of people in tour merchandise.

Visitors from outside the city usually stay in Tianhe or Zhujiang New Town, within one or two metro stops of the stadium. Hotels near Guangzhou East Railway Station suit fans coming in by high-speed train from Shenzhen, Hong Kong or elsewhere in Guangdong. They also make leaving the next morning simple.

For Cantonese food the next day, try a dim sum breakfast of har gow, cheung fun and char siu bao. It is a Guangzhou ritual and an easy way to recover after three hours of singing.

## December in Guangzhou

December is one of the most comfortable months in Guangzhou. The summer humidity has passed, and days are usually mild and fairly dry. Evenings in an open-air stadium can feel cool, so most attendees bring a light jacket for after the encore.

The weather also makes it easy to tie the concert into a longer trip. Shamian Island's colonial-era streets, the Chen Clan Ancestral Hall and an evening walk along the Pearl River below Canton Tower all fit around a night or two at Tianhe.
