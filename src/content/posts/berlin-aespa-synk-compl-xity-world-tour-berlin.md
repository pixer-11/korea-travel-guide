## One night on the Spree

Aespa plays Berlin on January 26, 2027. The stop is part of the 'Synk: Complæxity' world tour, and the venue is Uber Arena, the city's largest indoor hall. It sits on Mercedes-Platz in Friedrichshain, a few minutes from the river.

The group is Karina, Giselle, Winter and Ningning, an SM Entertainment act that debuted in 2020 with "Black Mamba". Their earlier tours carried the same "Synk" naming: Synk: Hyper Line and Synk: Parallel Line. The setlist for this run is not part of the facts here, so expect nothing specific beyond the group's catalogue, from "Next Level" and "Savage" to "Supernova" and "Whiplash".

## Inside Uber Arena

For years the arena carried the name Mercedes-Benz Arena. It hosts ice hockey club Eisbären Berlin and basketball side Alba Berlin, so the bowl is steep and the upper tiers look straight down onto the floor.

Concert layouts usually split into a standing floor and tiered seating on three sides. Seats in the side blocks closest to the stage often give a clearer view than the back of the floor. The arena's own website carries its event calendar, seating plans and ticket links, and ticket prices appear there rather than in this guide.

## Getting to Mercedes-Platz

Rail is the easiest route, and most fans take it.

- **Warschauer Straße:** S-Bahn lines S3, S5, S7 and S9, plus U-Bahn U1 and U3. A walk of roughly ten minutes south across the plaza.
- **Ostbahnhof:** S-Bahn and regional trains, about ten minutes west along Mühlenstraße.
- **On foot from the river:** the East Side Gallery, the longest surviving stretch of the Berlin Wall, runs along the Spree right beside the arena.

Mercedes-Platz itself is ringed with restaurants, a cinema and a bowling alley. Fans tend to fill these in the hours before the show, so a sit-down meal right outside the arena often means a wait.

## How MY fans do a Berlin arena night

Aespa's fandom is called MY, and Berlin draws fans from across Germany, Poland and the Netherlands. A few habits make the night smoother.

- **Lightsticks:** fans usually bring the official Aespa lightstick and pair it via the app so it syncs with the show.
- **Merch queues:** stands near the entrances draw long lines. Many fans buy merch in the afternoon, then leave for food.
- **Layers:** late January in Berlin usually sits around freezing. Standing-floor fans often queue outside for long stretches, and many check their coats once they get inside.
- **Payment:** cards are widely accepted inside the arena, though small cash is handy for kiosks on the walk from the station.
- **Cup deposit:** German venues commonly charge a deposit (Pfand) on drinks cups, refunded when you return the cup.
- **Transit tickets:** a BVG single or day ticket covers zones A and B. Validate it on the platform; inspectors fine unvalidated paper tickets.

After the encore, the crowd splits between Warschauer Straße and Ostbahnhof. Ostbahnhof is usually the calmer of the two, and walking there takes a few extra minutes. Friedrichshain's bars along Revaler Straße and Simon-Dach-Straße stay open late for anyone staying out.
