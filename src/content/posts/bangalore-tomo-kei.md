## Japanese technique, Peruvian appetite

Nikkei cooking started with Japanese immigrants in Peru. It joins Japanese precision with Peruvian flavours: citrus cures, chillies, corn, raw fish handled the way a sushi counter would. Tomo Kei brings that tradition to Bangalore under Chef Bobby T. Recto. Very few restaurants in the city cook it seriously.

The menu changes, and we haven't seen the current one, so don't arrive set on one particular dish. Come curious about the style and ask the staff what the kitchen is proudest of right now.

## Why Whitefield is the story

For years, Whitefield was a place you worked rather than ate. It's the IT corridor on the city's eastern edge, built around ITPL, tech parks and long commutes. Serious dining sat in Indiranagar, Koramangala and central Bangalore. Tomo Kei's arrival has got people talking because it puts an ambitious kitchen in a neighbourhood the city's dining map long overlooked.

That works in your favour if you're staying or working out east. You no longer have to fight cross-city traffic for a destination meal. If you're coming from the centre, the trip is the price of entry, so plan for it.

## Reaching the Sheraton Grand

The restaurant is inside the Sheraton Grand hotel in Whitefield. That means you walk in through a hotel lobby, not off the street.

- **By metro:** the Namma Metro Purple Line now runs out through Whitefield. Check a map for the station closest to the hotel, then take a short auto or cab ride.
- **By cab:** Ola, Uber and Rapido all cover Whitefield. Give the hotel name, not the restaurant's, because drivers know the Sheraton.
- **Timing the drive:** Whitefield's evening rush on the Outer Ring Road and ITPL Main Road is notoriously slow. From central Bangalore, allow plenty of extra time.
- **Driving yourself:** large hotels in Bangalore usually have a guarded driveway and parking. Expect a vehicle check at the gate.

## Getting a table at Tomo Kei

With 52 seats and a lot of attention since opening, walking in is a gamble. Reserve through the hotel or the restaurant's own channels, and confirm the hours when you do. We weren't given a schedule, and new restaurants often adjust theirs in the first months.

A few things that make the evening go smoothly:

- **Hotel security:** most large Bangalore hotels screen bags at the entrance. Leave a few spare minutes before your booking.
- **Paying:** cards and UPI are standard at hotel restaurants in India. You won't need to carry much cash.
- **Tipping:** check the bill first. Hotel restaurants often add a service charge, and if one is there, any extra tip is up to you.
- **Dress:** smart casual fits a hotel dining room. Office clothes from a Whitefield workday are fine.
- **Ordering:** Nikkei food is built for sharing. Order several plates for the table rather than one main each, and tell the server about any spice limits or dietary needs up front.

Treat Tomo Kei as a dinner you plan for. Book it, give the traffic its due, and give yourself a full evening for it.
