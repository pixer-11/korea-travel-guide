## Getting a seat in a Seoul cocktail bar

Plan ahead, especially now the bar has a 50 Best ranking. These are the habits that work here:

- **Confirm before you go.** The venue is new and the hours may still change. Check its official social media for the current schedule and for when the café shift ends and the bar begins.
- **Look for a booking option.** Many Seoul bars take reservations through apps such as CatchTable or through Instagram messages. If M+MS Bar does, book. If it only takes walk-ins, arriving early in the evening usually beats the post-dinner rush.
- **Bring your passport at night.** The legal drinking age in Korea is 19, and foreign visitors who look young are often asked for ID.
- **Don't tip.** Tipping isn't part of Korean bar culture, and the price on the menu is what you pay. Cards are accepted almost everywhere in Seoul, and contactless payment is normal.

Once you're seated, talk to the bartender. Seoul's serious cocktail bars are bartender-led. Telling them what you usually drink and asking for a recommendation is normal, and you're more likely to try something experimental that way than by choosing at random.
