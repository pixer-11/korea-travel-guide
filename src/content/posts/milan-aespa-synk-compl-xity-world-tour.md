## Italy's first aespa night

aespa's first show in Italy is on January 29, 2027. The SYNK: COMPLÆXITY world tour brings SM Entertainment's four-member group (Karina, Giselle, Winter and Ningning) to Milan's Unipol Dome for that single date. A debut country tends to draw MY, the fandom, from well beyond Lombardy. Expect fans in from Turin, Bologna, Rome and across the Swiss border.

## The arena at Santa Giulia

The Unipol Dome is new. It was built in Santa Giulia, a redevelopment district on Milan's south-east edge beside Rogoredo, as the ice hockey arena for the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics. Its capacity is roughly 16,000. Seating rises in tiers around a flat central floor, so standing and seated sections sit close together.

The neighbourhood is mostly offices, new apartment blocks and wide roads. It is not a district of bars and trattorias, so most visitors eat before they come, near Porta Romana or in the centre.

## Getting to Rogoredo

Almost everyone arrives through Milano Rogoredo, a combined metro and railway station that is walkable from the arena.

- **Metro:** the M3 yellow line runs straight to Rogoredo FS from Milano Centrale and from Duomo, with no changes.
- **Rail:** Frecciarossa and Italo high-speed trains stop at Rogoredo, and so do suburban S-line services. Fans coming from other Italian cities can step off without crossing the city.
- **From Malpensa:** take the Malpensa Express to Centrale, then the M3 south.
- **Payment:** the ATM metro turnstiles accept contactless bank cards, so nobody needs to queue at ticket machines after the show.

Out-of-town fans usually stay near Centrale or along the M3. That keeps the trip home to one metro ride.

## Concert night with MY in Milan

European K-pop shows have their own routine, and this one follows it.

- **Lightsticks:** most of the floor carries the official aespa lightstick. At K-pop arena shows these are typically synced by Bluetooth to the stage lighting, so the whole bowl changes colour together.
- **Fanchants:** attendees learn the official fanchants for the singles in advance. Expect the room to shout member names on cue during "Next Level", "Savage" and "Supernova".
- **Freebies:** fans hand out free photocards, stickers and printed slogan banners outside before doors. Trading is friendly, and people usually take one and say thanks.
- **Merch:** the official merchandise stands draw the longest outdoor queues of the day. They are often busier than the entry lines.
- **Weather:** late-January evenings in Milan hover near freezing, and Po Valley fog is common. Fans queuing outside layer up and carry a bag small enough for arena security.

When the show ends, roughly 16,000 people head for Rogoredo at once. The M3 platforms are the pinch point. Fans staying centrally usually walk straight down to the metro rather than stopping for photos outside, which beats the worst of the crush.
