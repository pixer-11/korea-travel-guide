You step out of the elevator on the 19th floor and the noise of Jakarta's street level — the honking, the motorbikes, the hawker carts — is already a memory. Li Lian sits inside Park Hyatt Jakarta, and the room announces itself quietly: lacquered surfaces, warm lighting, the kind of hush that comes from thick carpet and good acoustic design.

Through the windows, the city spreads out in a haze of tower blocks and, at dusk, a grid of headlights and neon that makes the whole dining room feel like it's floating above the traffic rather than sitting in it.

## Why Li Lian stands out

This is Park Hyatt Jakarta's first Chinese restaurant, and that "first" matters — it signals a hotel putting real weight behind the concept rather than treating it as an afterthought banquet room. The kitchen works in contemporary Chinese cuisine, with a dim sum program that's clearly the showcase: delicate, kitchen-forward versions of the classics rather than trolley-service quantity. The setting is deliberately dual-natured — opulent enough for a celebration, but composed in a way that still feels intimate rather than cavernous.

Private dining rooms are part of the design, which tells you this is a place built as much for business lunches and family celebrations as for a casual date night.

## Getting there

Li Lian is inside Park Hyatt Jakarta, in the Thamrin corridor of Central Jakarta — the same general area as Plaza Indonesia and the Bundaran HI (Hotel Indonesia roundabout), one of the city's most recognizable landmarks. If you're using the Jakarta MRT, Bundaran HI Station on the north–south line puts you within easy walking distance of this cluster of hotels and malls; a taxi or ride-hail app (Grab or Gojek) from most Central Jakarta hotels should take well under 20 minutes outside of rush hour.

Given the hotel's high-rise setting and the restaurant's 19th-floor position, allow a few extra minutes for lobby check-in and elevator access compared to a street-level restaurant.

## What to expect at the table

Go in expecting a dim sum-led menu alongside broader contemporary Chinese cooking — think refined, plated interpretations rather than a rowdy, cart-pushed dim sum hall. The room is styled for lingering: expect a proper multi-course sit-down rather than a quick in-and-out lunch, especially if you book one of the private rooms for a group. Because this is a newer opening still building its reputation, exact dishes, set menus and price points are worth confirming directly with the hotel when you reserve, rather than assuming a fixed menu — kitchens at this stage often adjust and refine.

## When to go

Skyline dining rewards timing. A sunset arrival lets you watch Jakarta's light shift from hazy gold to a dense field of lights, which is arguably the single best visual argument for eating on the 19th floor rather than at street level. Given its status as a trending, recently-opened spot inside a five-star hotel, weekend evenings are likely to draw both hotel guests and Jakarta diners looking to try the new opening — a weekday evening or an early seating may be calmer if you'd rather have the view without a wait.

## How to visit like a local

Book ahead. In Jakarta, hotel fine-dining restaurants — especially newly opened, buzzed-about ones — fill up on weekend evenings and around celebrations, and Li Lian's private dining rooms suggest it's already positioning itself for group bookings and business entertaining, so calling or booking online rather than walking in is the norm here, not the exception. Smart-casual dress is the safe default for a five-star hotel restaurant in Jakarta; you won't need a jacket, but flip-flops and beach shorts will feel out of place.

Payment is by card as standard at this level of hotel dining — don't expect a cash-only setup. If you're unsure about dietary notes, portion sizes for shared dim sum, or whether a set menu is running, ask when you reserve rather than guessing; hotel restaurants in Jakarta are generally responsive to that kind of question ahead of your visit.

And because opening hours, seating times and menu details for a newly launched restaurant can shift as the kitchen settles in, treat anything beyond these broad strokes — including exact hours — as something to reconfirm with Park Hyatt Jakarta directly before you set out, especially if you're planning around sunset or booking a private room for a special occasion.
