## Wood, glass and a tall old room

The furniture is the first draw. Cafe Gandolfi opened in 1979 inside a building on Albion Street that once belonged to Glasgow's old cheese market. The chairs, tables and benches were made by the Borders sculptor Tim Stead, in heavy, organic-looking timber. Every surface looks carved rather than assembled.

Look up and you get high ceilings and coloured glass in the windows. The look is old-world bistro, not polished design hotel. There's a full bar, and vegetarians can choose properly rather than settle for one token plate. Prices are mid-range for central Glasgow, and the menu with current prices is posted at the door.

## Finding Albion Street

Albion Street runs south through the Merchant City toward the Trongate. Getting there:

- **Glasgow Queen Street station**: roughly a 10-minute walk via George Square
- **Argyle Street station**: a few minutes' walk east along Argyle Street
- **Buchanan Street or St Enoch subway**: either is a short walk west of the Merchant City grid

Nearby you have the Tron Theatre on Trongate, the Gallery of Modern Art on Royal Exchange Square and the City Chambers on George Square. That makes it easy to slot in before a show or after a gallery.

## Picking your hour

The hours shape the visit more than anything else does:

- **Tuesday–Saturday**: 8am–11pm, so breakfast, lunch and dinner all work
- **Monday**: 8am–4pm, so daytime only
- **Sunday**: 9am–4pm, which suits brunch but not dinner

That means no evening meal on Sundays or Mondays. Weekend crowds are the other thing to plan around. Try not to turn up between 2pm and 10pm on a weekend, because that's when the room is fullest and walk-ins wait longest.

On a Saturday the quietest window is 4pm–5pm. It falls between the lunch and dinner sittings, and it's the easiest time to get a table without planning ahead. Sunday closes at 4pm, so that gap doesn't exist on Sundays. Go earlier, or pick a Saturday.

A weekday breakfast from 8am is the calmest way to see the Stead furniture properly, before the lunch trade from nearby offices arrives. For lunch or a coffee, plan on an hour. For dinner with drinks, allow closer to two.

## Booking, paying and the bar

This is a long-established Glasgow name, not a secret, so treat it like a busy restaurant:

- **Book ahead** for Friday or Saturday dinner and for weekend lunch. Walking in works best at breakfast or in that Saturday 4pm–5pm gap.
- **Pay by card.** It's normal across Glasgow, and contactless is the default.
- **Tipping:** around 10% is customary for table service in the UK. Check the bill first, because some places add a service charge and you shouldn't pay it twice.
- **The full bar** means you don't have to order food. A drink at a Stead table before a show at the Tron is a fair use of the room.

If you're splitting the bill, say so when you ask for it. Staff will bring the card machine to the table rather than send you to a till.
