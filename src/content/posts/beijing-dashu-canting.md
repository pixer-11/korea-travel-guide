## What Dashu Canting actually is

Dashu Canting translates roughly as "Big Tree Restaurant," and the name points to its design conceit: a dining room built as an immersive, tree-themed art installation rather than a standard Cantonese spot. The concept fuses Cantonese savory cooking with a dessert-and-tea program, so a single visit can move from a hot dish to a plated dessert to a pot of tea without changing rooms. That layout is unusual for Beijing, where tea houses, dessert cafes and Cantonese kitchens are usually separate businesses.

The interior design has picked up industry recognition, which is part of why it circulates on Chinese social platforms like Xiaohongshu and Douyin. Diners come as much for the room, the plating, and the ambiance as for the food itself.

## The dish everyone photographs

The signature order is curry chicken served with bread, a Hong Kong-cafe staple reworked here into a more theatrical presentation. It's the dish most tied to the restaurant's viral reputation and the one first-time visitors are told to order.

- Curry chicken with bread: the headline dish, built for dipping and for photos
- Tea service: brewed and served as its own course, not an afterthought
- Dessert plates: positioned as a distinct stage of the meal, echoing a Cantonese dim sum-and-dessert rhythm

Because the menu leans on visual presentation, portions and pacing are built around lingering rather than turning tables quickly.

## Eating and paying like a regular

Cantonese dining in Beijing is typically shared-plate style: order a main, split the curry chicken and bread, then let dessert and tea close the meal rather than arriving alongside it. Payment across Beijing restaurants is almost universally by mobile app, WeChat Pay or Alipay, with cash accepted but rarely used. Tipping isn't customary in Chinese restaurants, including upscale or design-forward ones like this.
