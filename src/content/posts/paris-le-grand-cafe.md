Grand Palais reopened after years of restoration, and Paris Society moved fast to claim a corner of it. Le Grand Café is their answer to that address: part restaurant, part bar, built for a crowd that treats dinner as the opening act of the night.

This is the same group behind Loulou at the Louvre, Girafe near the Eiffel Tower, and Perruche on the rooftop of Printemps. Each of those spots trades on a similar formula: a landmark building, a photogenic room, a menu secondary to the scene. Le Grand Café follows the pattern, but the address here outranks the rest. Few dining rooms in Paris sit under the iron-and-glass vault of a monument this size.

## Where it sits and why the location matters

The Grand Palais faces the Seine on the Right Bank, a short walk from the Louvre and the Champs-Élysées. Le Grand Café occupies part of that newly renovated shell, so the setting itself, soaring glazed ceilings and restored ironwork, does a lot of the work before a single plate arrives.

Expect the crowd to skew dressed-up and camera-ready. This is a place people choose specifically to be seen in, not just fed in.

## The bar and what to expect from it

The cocktail bar draws its own crowd separate from the dinner service, built around creative, seasonally shifting drinks rather than a fixed classics list. It works well as a standalone stop if you only want a drink and a look at the room without committing to a full meal.

Because the venue is new, exact hours, seating policy and menu details weren't confirmed at time of writing. Check Le Grand Café's own booking channel or the Paris Society site before you go, and reconfirm the same day if you can, since recently opened venues often adjust hours in the first months.

## Booking a table without the scramble

Trendy Paris Society openings fill up fast, and this one sits inside a monument, which only sharpens demand.

- Book as far ahead as the reservation system allows, ideally a couple of weeks out for weekend evenings
- Ask specifically for a table with a view of the glass roof if that's the draw for you
- If fully booked, try the bar for walk-in drinks rather than waiting on a cancellation
- Dress the part; smart, put-together outfits fit the room better than casual wear

## Making the most of the visit

Pair it with a walk through the Grand Palais itself if there's an exhibition running, since the building often hosts art and design shows alongside its restaurants. The Louvre and Tuileries Garden are close enough to fold into the same afternoon.

Arrive a little early for a drink at the bar before your table is called. It's the easiest way to take in the room without feeling rushed, and it gives you a second look at a space that photographs very differently once evening light hits the glass.
