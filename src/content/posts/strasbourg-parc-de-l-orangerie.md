## What you're actually walking into

Parc de l'Orangerie dates to 1692, laid out on the orders of Marshal Vauban and later reshaped in the English landscape style for Empress Josephine's visit in 1804. The pavilion at its center still carries her name. Storks nest here year-round in open-air enclosures, a deliberate nod to Alsace's regional symbol, and you'll hear them clacking their beaks before you spot the nests in the treetops.

The park spreads across roughly 26 hectares, with a boating lake at its heart where you can rent a rowboat by the hour. Water birds, mostly ducks and swans, cluster along the banks near the café terrace. A small farm with goats and rabbits sits toward the northern end, popular with families and free to enter.

## Getting there and getting your bearings

The park sits at the northeastern edge of the European quarter, a 10-minute walk from the European Parliament and Council of Europe buildings. Tram E or C to Droits de l'Homme, or Tram E to Parlement Européen, both drop you within a few minutes' walk.

- Main entrance: near Place de Bordeaux, closest to trams
- Boating lake: center of the park, rentals typically run spring through early autumn
- Stork enclosures: scattered along the main paths, no ticket needed
- Terrace restaurant (Buerehiesel area): sit-down option if you want a proper meal

It's open every day, 7am to 8pm, with no seasonal variation listed, so plan around those hours regardless of the day of the week.

## Timing it right

This is one of Strasbourg's most visited green spaces, so the difference between a calm walk and a crowded one comes down to timing. Both weekdays and weekends are quietest from 7am to 11am, when joggers and dog walkers outnumber tourists.

Weekend afternoons flip that entirely: 1pm to 7pm is the busiest stretch, with families, picnickers, and stroller traffic filling the main lawns near the lake. If you're set on a weekend visit, arrive before 11am or wait until after 7pm for a quieter lap, keeping in mind the park closes at 8pm.

Spring brings the flower beds near the Josephine pavilion into full bloom, matching the "flower-filled" reputation the park is known for. Storks are visible year-round, so there's no bad season for that particular draw.

## Making the most of a short visit

Locals treat this as a park for routine, not a special outing, so don't expect hushed reverence: expect joggers, families feeding the ducks, and office workers from the nearby EU buildings eating lunch on benches.

- Bring coins or a card for the boat rental kiosk; it's not always cash-only but confirm on site
- The terrace restaurant near Buerehiesel gets busy at lunch; arrive early or plan for a wait
- Paths are flat and stroller-friendly, looping around the lake in under 30 minutes if you skip stops
- Combine with the nearby European Parliament exterior or Palais de l'Europe for a half-day itinerary, since both are a short walk away

Given how popular this park is, don't expect solitude on a sunny weekend afternoon. The early morning window is your best bet for the postcard version without the crowds.
