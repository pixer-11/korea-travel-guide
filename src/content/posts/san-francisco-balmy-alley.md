## A Block Painted Wall to Wall

The whole alley is the gallery. Balmy runs one short block from 24th Street south to 25th Street, between Treat Avenue and Harrison Street. On both sides, garage doors, wooden fences and the back walls of houses are covered in paint from pavement to roofline.

Painting here started in the early 1970s. The Mujeres Muralistas, a Latina women's collective, were among the first artists. In 1984 a project called PLACA covered the alley in murals protesting US intervention in Central America. That political thread still runs through the block, next to newer work on housing, gentrification and Mission identity.

Walls get repainted as murals fade, so the collection keeps changing. What you see won't exactly match older photos online.

## Getting to 24th and Balmy

It's a flat walk from the nearest station.

- **BART:** 24th St Mission station. Walk east along 24th Street for roughly ten minutes, past Folsom, to Balmy between Treat and Harrison.
- **Muni:** the 48 bus runs along 24th Street and stops close to the alley's north end.
- **By car:** street parking is residential and tight. Read the curb signs for street-cleaning days.

The 24th Street walk is part of the trip. The corridor is lined with taquerias, panaderías and Latino-owned shops. Precita Eyes Mural Arts Center, at 2981 24th Street, sits a few doors from the alley's entrance.

## Timing Your Walk Through

Twenty to forty minutes covers the block properly. The alley is open at all hours, but the murals need daylight to read well.

On weekdays it's calmest between 7am and 10am. Morning light falls evenly down the lane and there's space to step back for a full-wall photo. Try not to come on weekends between 10am and 11pm. That's when walking groups and photographers stack up in a lane barely wider than a car.

Even so, Balmy is still a quieter find than the big San Francisco sights. Pair it with a lunch stop on 24th Street, or take the 20-minute walk north to Clarion Alley, the Mission's other mural lane, between 17th and 18th Streets.

## Walking Balmy Without Being a Nuisance

Remember that people live here. The painted garage doors belong to working garages, so keep clear of driveways and move aside when a car needs through. Don't lean on the walls or touch the paint. Many of these pieces are decades old and fragile.

To understand what you're looking at, join a guided walk run by Precita Eyes. Muralists lead them and explain who painted each wall and why. Tour times and current prices are listed on the Precita Eyes website and at their 24th Street storefront.

Photograph the art freely, but ask before photographing residents or anyone working on a wall. Credit the artist when you post: most murals carry a painted signature in a lower corner. If you want to support the work directly, buying prints or books at Precita Eyes funds mural restoration across the neighbourhood.
