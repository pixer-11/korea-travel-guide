## What you're actually visiting

Parc de Merlet sits on a forested shelf above Les Houches, at 2495 Chemin de Merlet, facing the Mont Blanc massif across the valley. It's not a zoo in the caged sense: marmots, chamois, ibex and deer live in large enclosed meadows and forest plots that follow the natural slope. Paths thread between them, so you're often within a few metres of a marmot sunning on a rock.

The site is rated 4.6 from over 4,000 reviews, and it's well-known rather than obscure. Expect other visitors, especially families, on any clear-weather day in summer.

## Walking the reserve

Trails loop through mixed terrain: open meadow, spruce forest, rocky outcrops. Total walking time runs 1.5 to 2.5 hours if you take the full network rather than the short version.

- Wear proper shoes; paths are unpaved and slope steadily
- Bring water; there's little shade in the meadow sections
- Binoculars help for chamois and ibex, which keep more distance than the marmots
- The upper viewpoints give a direct sightline to Mont Blanc and the Bossons glacier

Marmots are the easiest sighting, often right beside the path in the lower enclosures. Deer and chamois tend to hold back near the treeline, more visible in early morning or toward closing.

## Hours and getting there

The park opens Tuesday through Sunday, 10am to 6pm, and is closed all day Monday. There's no shortened day among the rest of the week, so any Tuesday-to-Sunday visit gets the full window.

By car, it's a signed drive up from Les Houches on the narrow Chemin de Merlet; the road switchbacks through forest and has limited passing space, so go slowly and expect to give way. Parking is on site but fills on peak summer afternoons. There's no direct train or bus link to the park gate itself; Les Houches has its own train station on the Mont Blanc Express line, but you'll still need a car or taxi for the final climb.

Confirm current admission prices before you go, since they're adjusted periodically and aren't listed here.

## Timing your visit like a regular

This is a popular, well-reviewed stop, not a quiet find, so plan around the crowd rather than expecting to avoid it. Reasoning from how the day structures itself: tour buses and family groups tend to arrive mid-morning once they've had breakfast in the valley, so being at the gate close to the 10am opening usually means quieter paths for the first hour.

Late afternoon, in the last couple of hours before the 6pm close, is the other calm window, since day-trippers have usually moved on by then. Bring cash or a card; French rural attractions increasingly take both, but a small cash reserve is still sensible outside town centres. Photos of the animals are best taken with a long lens rather than approaching enclosures, both for your shot and for the animals' space.

Pair the visit with a walk in Les Houches itself afterward, or with the Bellevue cable car nearby if you want to continue upward into higher alpine terrain the same day.
