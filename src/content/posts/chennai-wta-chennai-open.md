Chennai has staged professional tennis for decades, mostly in one concrete bowl just off Nungambakkam's busy roads. You sit in tiered stands around an outdoor hard court, and the air is thick with coastal humidity. Every point is played in front of a crowd that knows its tennis. The WTA Chennai Open brings women's tour players to that setting for one week in November.

## A tour week on Chennai's hard courts

This is a professional women's tournament on the WTA calendar. It draws international players along with Indian fans, and the most famous names are not always the reason to go. A week like this puts spectators much closer to tour-level tennis than the big Slam venues usually do.

The city has a long record with the sport. The men's ATP Chennai Open was held here every year from 1997 to 2017, then moved to Pune. The women's event arrived later. Earlier editions in 2022 and 2025 were played in the WTA 250 tier, the level where rising players and seasoned regulars often meet in the same draw.

## Dates: November 2-8, 2026

The 2026 tournament runs from Monday 2 November to Sunday 8 November. Like most WTA events, it works as a knockout week:

- **Early days:** the widest spread of matches, with several courts in use and the most tennis per hour.
- **Midweek:** the draw narrows through the second round and quarterfinals.
- **The weekend:** semifinals and finals, when the stands are usually fullest.

The organiser publishes the order of play and session details on its official site. The draw itself is made by the tour, so player names are not listed here. Many fans treat the early rounds as the best value of the week. There are a lot of matches, there's room to move between courts, and spectators often catch players warming up close by.

## The stadium in Nungambakkam

Earlier editions were played at the SDAT Tennis Stadium, run by the Sports Development Authority of Tamil Nadu. It sits in Nungambakkam, a central neighbourhood of offices, colleges, consulates and shopping streets. The main court is an outdoor hard court with banked seating on all sides, and smaller outside courts sit nearby.

The organiser's official site lists the venue for each edition. If 2026 stays at the SDAT stadium, you are in one of the easiest parts of the city to reach. That makes it simple to drop in for a single session rather than committing to a full day.

## Getting to Nungambakkam

Most spectators get there by app cab or auto-rickshaw. Uber, Ola and Rapido all work across Chennai, and autos are everywhere on the main roads. Traffic around Nungambakkam gets heavy at weekday rush hours, so fans heading to an evening session usually leave a margin for it.

- **From the airport:** Chennai International Airport is in Meenambakkam, south of the centre. The drive is usually under an hour outside peak traffic.
- **By suburban rail:** Nungambakkam has its own station on the Chennai Beach–Tambaram suburban line.
- **Where to stay:** visitors often base themselves in Nungambakkam, T. Nagar or along Anna Salai (Mount Road), all a short ride away.

## Tennis in monsoon season

Chennai's calendar has one quirk that matters here. The city gets most of its yearly rain from the northeast monsoon, roughly October to December. That means early November is the wettest stretch of the year, and November 2026 sits right inside it. Downpours can be sudden and heavy.

Outdoor hard courts cannot be played on when wet. Rain delays and reshuffled schedules are a normal part of the week, and a session can pause, resume late or move to another day. Regulars tend to carry a compact umbrella or poncho, and they keep their evenings flexible.

Between showers it's warm and humid. Light cotton clothes, a cap and a water bottle are standard in the stands. Deepavali, one of Tamil Nadu's biggest festivals, also falls in this week in 2026, which brings busier roads, festive crowds and fireworks across the city.

## Courtside manners and paying your way

Tennis has its own etiquette, and Chennai's crowd follows it. Spectators stay seated and quiet while a point is being played. People move only at changeovers, which come after the first game of a set and then every two games. Ushers usually hold people at the aisle entrance until the break.

- **Applause:** clapping comes after the point ends, never during the rally. Calling out between serves is fine; calling out during a serve is not.
- **Phones:** silent mode, and no flash photography during play.
- **Paying:** UPI (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm) is the default way to pay in Chennai, and cards are widely accepted. A little cash is still handy for autos and street stalls.
- **Language:** Tamil is the local language, and English works fine at the venue and in hotels. *Vanakkam* (hello) and *nandri* (thank you) are always well received.

## Between sessions in Nungambakkam

When play stops, the neighbourhood around the stadium is easy to explore. Khader Nawaz Khan Road, a short ride away, is a compact street of boutiques and cafés. Valluvar Kottam, the chariot-shaped memorial to the Tamil poet Thiruvalluvar, sits a little further west towards Kodambakkam.

For food, south Indian vegetarian "tiffin" places are the local habit. Idli, dosa and vada come on a banana leaf or a steel plate, with sambar and chutneys, then strong filter coffee in a steel tumbler and *davara* (saucer). Chains like Saravana Bhavan have branches across central Chennai. They're a reliable fallback when a rain delay leaves you with a spare hour.
