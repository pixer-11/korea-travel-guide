## Breakfast on Park Street since 1927

Park Street wakes up slowly, and Flurys opens before most of it. At the moment the doors open at 6am, which puts it ahead of the restaurants and bars the street is better known for. A Swiss couple, Joseph and Rose Flury, founded it in 1927. Kolkata has kept coming back ever since, for one cake box or one plate of eggs at a time.

It calls itself a tearoom, and that is the right word. Seated diners and takeaway customers share the same address at 18A Park Street. Pastries, cakes and savouries sit in glass cases near the entrance, and the dining room is for lingering over a pot of tea. The editorial line Google uses is "Indian & European cakes/pastries, plus light fare". In practice that means a menu that moves between a full English breakfast and a cream-filled pastry without any embarrassment.

## What to put on the table

Ordering here is easy if you know the menu's habits. Flurys is best known for these:

- **The English breakfast**: eggs, toast, sausages and the rest, which is the reason many people book an early slot
- **Rum balls**, a dense chocolate sweet that regulars buy by the box
- **Pastries and cream cakes** from the front counter, eaten in or carried out
- **Savoury patties and sandwiches** for a lighter mid-morning stop
- **Plum cake** in December, when Park Street's Christmas season brings a crowd of its own
- **A pot of tea**, the drink that makes sense of the word "tearoom"

The price level is on the pricier side for Kolkata, and the menu inside has current prices. You are paying for the address and the history as much as the eggs. Most visitors think that is a fair trade for one breakfast.

## Timing your table

The crowd pattern here is clear, so use it. On both weekdays and weekends, Flurys is calmest from 7am to 10am. That suits the breakfast menu, so the early hours are both the quietest and the most typical way to eat here.

Avoid weekend afternoons and evenings. From 1pm to 11pm on Saturdays and Sundays the room is at its busiest, and the waits get longer as the day goes on. If a weekend is your only option, get there by mid-morning. Hours apply to this week only and can change around holidays, so check Google before you set off.

## Getting a seat without the scrum

Flurys is very popular, and every regular knows how the place works. Here is how to avoid waiting around:

- **Decide between counter and table first.** If you only want rum balls or a cake to take away, go to the display cases. Don't join the queue for a table.
- **Expect to wait at peak times.** Staff seat people from the door, so give them your party size and stand to one side.
- **Pay as Kolkata does.** Cards and UPI mobile payments are normal at places like this. Check the bill for a service charge before you add a tip.
- **Go even earlier in December.** Park Street is lit up for Christmas, and plum cake buyers fill the counter. A 7am breakfast avoids most of that rush.

## Park Street around the door

Flurys sits on a stretch of Park Street that has been Kolkata's dining strip for decades. Park Street Metro station on Line 1, the Blue Line, is a short walk away. That makes it easy to reach from Esplanade or from the south of the city without fighting the traffic on Chowringhee.

Walk a few doors in either direction and the city's old restaurant names begin. Trincas, Mocambo and Peter Cat are all on or just off the street. The Oxford Bookstore, a long-running Park Street fixture, is a good place to spend half an hour after breakfast.

## Making a morning of it

Plan on 45 minutes to an hour for a sit-down breakfast. If you are only collecting cakes, ten minutes is enough. Flurys works best as the first stop of a central Kolkata day rather than an outing of its own.

From Park Street, the South Park Street Cemetery is a short ride east. Its colonial-era tombs are calmest early in the day. The Indian Museum on Jawaharlal Nehru Road is close by, and the Victoria Memorial and the Maidan are a brief taxi ride south-west. Eat at Flurys at 7am and you can be at any of them before the heat and the traffic build.
