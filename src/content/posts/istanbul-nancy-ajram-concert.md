## A Beirut voice, one night before New Year

The date matters here. Nancy Ajram plays Ora Arena in Istanbul on December 30, 2026, one night before the city fills Taksim and the Bosphorus shoreline for New Year's Eve. Many in the room are in town for the whole holiday week, and the show works as its first big night out.

Expect a crowd that knows the words. Arabic-speaking visitors from the Gulf, Lebanon and Egypt come to Istanbul in large numbers over the winter holidays. Turkish fans of Arabic pop join them, and the singing from the floor often carries as far as the stage.

## The songs the room is waiting for

Ajram has been a headline name in Arabic pop for more than two decades. Her breakthrough came in 2003 with "Akhasmak Ah", and the hits kept coming. A typical Nancy setlist leans on songs the audience has carried around for years:

- "Akhasmak Ah", the early hit that made her famous
- "Ah W Noss", a playful, sing-along favourite
- "Ya Tabtab", one of her best-loved tracks
- "Inta Eih", the slower ballad that brings out the phone torches

Many people also remember her from judging *Arab Idol*. Others know her from the Arabic version of "Wavin' Flag" for the 2010 World Cup. No setlist has been published for this date, so treat that list as her catalogue, not a promise.

## Ora Arena and getting across the city

The show is held at Ora Arena. Istanbul is huge, and its traffic is famous for bad reasons. Concert-goers usually allow far more time for the journey than the map app suggests, especially on a winter weekday evening.

Most visitors get around this way:

- **İstanbulkart**: the rechargeable card works on metro, tram, Marmaray, buses and ferries, and one card can pay for several people.
- **Rail first**: the metro and the Marmaray line under the Bosphorus avoid the worst of the road jams.
- **Taxis and apps**: yellow taxis and the BiTaksi app work well for the last stretch. Leaving after the encore takes longer, though, because thousands of people want a ride at once.

Late December in Istanbul is usually cold, damp and windy, particularly near the water. A layer that packs small makes sense for the queue outside, since arena cloakrooms vary.

## Doors, bags and the floor: how a Turkish arena night runs

Security screening is standard at Istanbul venues. Bags go through a check and sometimes an X-ray, and people walk through a metal detector. Fans usually travel light and leave large bags, umbrellas with metal tips and outside drinks at the hotel. Those tend to cause the slowest moments at the gate.

Signs at the venue are mostly in Turkish. These are the words to know:

- *Giriş*: entrance
- *Çıkış*: exit
- *Tuvalet*: toilets
- *Bilet*: ticket

Phones come out constantly at Arabic pop shows, and nobody minds. Big ballads turn the room into a sea of screen lights. Dancing in the aisles during the faster songs is normal too, and staff mostly let it happen. Cards and contactless payment work at most arena counters. Cash in Turkish lira still helps with street vendors and smaller taxis, where people usually round up the fare.

## Making a week of it

The concert falls in Istanbul's busiest hotel week of the year. Visitors often stay near a metro or Marmaray stop instead of chasing a Bosphorus view. That makes the late-night trip back simpler.

Before or after the show, the classic winter stops are close to the rail network:

- **Sultanahmet**: Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque, reached by the T1 tram
- **Galata and Karaköy**: cafés, the Galata Tower and ferry piers
- **Kadıköy**: the Asian-side market streets, a short ferry ride across the water

A warm *salep* or a simit from a street cart is the usual way to deal with the cold on the walk afterwards.
