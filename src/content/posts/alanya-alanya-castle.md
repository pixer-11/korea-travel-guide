## Why go

The first thing you notice climbing toward Alanya Castle is how the road keeps switching back on itself, the sea dropping away on both sides. This isn't one building but a whole fortified peninsula, walls snaking nearly 6.5km around a rocky headland the Seljuks fortified in the 13th century.

By the time you reach the upper gates, the Mediterranean is spread out in a blue that seems to wrap the whole horizon. The red tower — Kızıl Kule — anchors the harbor below, its brick the color of dried clay against the water.

## Getting there

Alanya Castle sits at Hisariçi, Kale Cd. No:12, high above the modern town. Most visitors take the small municipal buses or dolmuş marked "Kale" from the town center — they wind up the switchback road and drop you near the main gate.

A taxi from the harbor takes about 15–20 minutes depending on traffic on the hairpin turns. If you're driving, parking near the top is limited and fills fast by mid-morning.

## What to see inside

Inside the walls, the ruins spread across a surprisingly large plateau — this was a working citadel, not a single monument. Look for:

- The Byzantine-era church with faded frescoes still visible on the ceiling
- Ehmedek fortress section, with sweeping views over the harbor and old town
- Remains of roughly 400 cisterns that once supplied the garrison's water
- The Adam Atacağı cliff, where the drop to the sea below is dizzying

Give yourself at least half a day. The site is large, the paths are uneven cobble and dirt, and the heat by midday can be punishing in summer.

## When to go

The castle is open every day from 8:00 AM to 8:30 PM, so you have a long window to work with. Spring and autumn mornings are the most comfortable — summer sun on exposed stone paths gets brutal by noon.

Sunset here is genuinely worth planning around. The light turns the red tower and the bay below into deep gold, though this is also when crowds cluster near the best viewpoints.

## How to visit like a local

Because this is one of the busiest attractions on the Turkish Riviera, the smart move isn't finding a secret entrance — it's timing your arrival. Get there right at 8am opening, before the tour buses climb up from the coastal hotels.

Alternatively, aim for the two hours before closing, around 6:30–8:30pm, when day-trip groups have already left. Midday, especially between 11am and 3pm, is when the paths and viewpoints get genuinely congested.

Ticket entry is typically by card or cash at the gate, though prices and exact ticketing setups change often enough that you should confirm current entry fees locally before you go. Wear proper walking shoes — sandals struggle on the loose cobblestones — and carry water, since shaded rest points inside are limited.

Most visitors combine the castle with the cable car (teleferik) that runs up from the harbor area, which saves your legs for the walking inside rather than the climb up. If you're short on time, prioritize Kızıl Kule and Ehmedek over trying to walk the full length of the walls.

## Nearby to combine with your visit

The castle pairs naturally with a wander through Alanya's old town below, where narrow lanes lead down toward the harbor and Cleopatra Beach. The Red Tower itself, right at the waterfront, is worth the short separate stop if you skip climbing all the way up.

Boat tours departing from the harbor often loop past the base of the castle peninsula, giving you the view from the water that most land visitors never see. It's a good way to close out the day after the climb.
