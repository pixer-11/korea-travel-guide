## Why this tournament matters

The BWF World Championships is the sport's marquee individual event outside the Olympics, contested across five disciplines — men's and women's singles, men's and women's doubles, and mixed doubles. Winning it is one of the two or three career-defining achievements in badminton, alongside Olympic gold and the Thomas/Uber Cup. India hosting the 2026 edition in New Delhi is a significant moment for the sport in South Asia, a region that has produced world-class talents like P.V. Sindhu, Saina Nehwal, Kidambi Srikanth, and the Satwiksairaj Rankireddy–Chirag Shetty doubles pair, all of whom have a realistic shot at medals on home soil.

Expect a week of high-intensity matches, from opening-round upsets to a finals weekend (likely the final two days of the August 17–23 window) where medals are decided. Worlds editions typically run six or seven days: early rounds thin the draw quickly, and by the semifinals the arena atmosphere becomes considerably more charged.

## Getting to New Delhi

New Delhi is served by Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL), one of Asia's busiest hubs, with direct long-haul connections from Europe, East Asia, the Gulf, and North America. From the airport, the Delhi Metro Airport Express Line runs directly into the city center (New Delhi Station) in about 20 minutes, a far faster option than road transport during August's monsoon traffic. Once in the city, the wider Delhi Metro network (multiple color-coded lines) is the most reliable way to reach most major indoor sports venues, which are typically well connected — plan to confirm the exact host arena once BWF and the Badminton Association of India announce it, since Delhi has hosted top-tier badminton before at venues like the Siri Fort Sports Complex and the K.D. Jadhav Indoor Hall at the Indira Gandhi Sports Complex.

## What to expect at the venue

Championship-level badminton halls are set up with a central show court surrounded by tiered seating, plus one or two side courts for concurrent early-round matches — arrive early in the tournament week if you want to see multiple nations' players across several courts in a single session. Sessions generally run in two blocks, afternoon and evening, with the evening sessions carrying marquee matches once the draw narrows. Bring or rent binoculars if you're seated high in the stands, since shuttlecock rallies move fast and are easiest to appreciate from lower-tier seats or courtside standing areas where permitted.

## When to go and what to pack

Mid-to-late August in Delhi is deep in monsoon season: expect heat in the low-to-mid 30s°C, high humidity, and sudden downpours. Pack a light rain jacket or a compact umbrella, breathable clothing, and comfortable shoes for walking between metro stations and gates. Indoor arenas are air-conditioned, so a light layer for the seating bowl is worth carrying even in summer heat.

## Tickets and planning ahead

As of this writing, official ticketing details, exact venue confirmation, and daily play schedules for the 2026 Championships had not been locked in publicly. Do not rely on secondary resellers for tickets to a tournament of this profile — badminton's biggest events are prone to scalping around semifinal and final sessions. Check the official BWF website (bwfbadminton.com) and the Badminton Association of India's channels directly for confirmed dates, ticket release windows, and venue announcements before booking flights or hotels.

## How to visit like a local

Delhi's badminton fan culture leans knowledgeable and vocal rather than rowdy — expect informed applause for clever net play and deception shots, not just smashes. If you want the calmest experience, go to early-round afternoon sessions on the first two or three days, when tickets are easiest to get and multiple courts run simultaneously; the crowd swells and prices/scarcity tighten by quarterfinal weekend. Most local fans pay by UPI or card rather than cash at concessions and merchandise counters, so carrying a chip-enabled international card or a mobile payment app linked to a local-friendly network is more useful than cash alone. If you're unfamiliar with scoring, matches are best-of-three games to 21 points (with extension rules past 20-20) — knowing this avoids the common tourist mistake of leaving mid-match thinking a game has ended when it's only game one. Dress modestly if you plan to combine the trip with visits to nearby religious or heritage sites on non-match days, and always double-check gate times, as security screening at major Indian sporting events can take longer than expected.
