## A hill on the city's western edge

The road to Matsuyama General Park climbs. Most visitors to Matsuyama never come up here, because they're busy with the castle and Dogo Onsen. That's exactly why the park feels so roomy. It's spread across a slope in Asahigaoka, on the western side of the city.

What you get for the climb is space. There are lawns, wooded paths, a playground built for whole families and a tower at the top. On weekdays you may have big stretches of it nearly to yourself.

## The view from the tower

The observation tower is the reason to keep walking uphill. From the top you can see the whole Matsuyama plain spread below you. Matsuyama Castle sits on its own wooded hill in the middle of the city.

Look the other way on a clear day and you'll see the Seto Inland Sea, with islands lined up along the coast. It's the best way to understand how this city fits between the mountains and the water. Come up on your first day and the rest of your trip will make more sense on the map.

Allow about 20 minutes at the top, longer if you're taking photos. Haze cuts the long views, so pick a crisp, dry day if your schedule lets you.

## Slides, lawns and the cherry grove

The playground is the park's other big draw. It's large and built for climbing, and children tend to disappear into it for an hour while parents sit on the edges. If you're travelling with kids, plan your day around this rather than the tower.

The cherry grove changes the park's mood each spring. Around blossom season, usually late March into early April in Ehime, the trees turn the slope pink. Groups come up with picnic sheets to sit under them.

Outside that window, the trees still give shade along the paths. In early summer and autumn the park is mostly about the walk and the view.

## When to come up

It's open 9:00 AM–5:00 PM every day of the week. That rules out sunset and night views, so plan a daytime visit.

- **Weekdays:** quiet the whole time it's open, 9am–5pm. Any weekday slot works.
- **Weekends:** busiest from 10am to 4pm. Try not to arrive in the middle of that window, because that's when families fill the playground and the paths.
- **Weekend workaround:** arrive right at 9am and you'll get an hour or so before the crowd builds.

For a full visit with the tower, a loop of the paths and a sit on the lawn, plan on two to three hours. With children and the playground, it can easily take half a day.

## Getting up the hill

The address is 1-chōme Asahigaoka, Matsuyama, Ehime. The park is outside the compact centre where the trams run. A car or taxi from central Matsuyama is the easiest option, and a taxi saves you the climb.

If you'd rather use public transport, check current Iyotetsu bus routes toward Asahigaoka before you set out. Expect an uphill walk from the stop. Drivers will find parking at the park.

It works well as a half-day trip combined with Matsuyama Castle. See the castle from below in the morning, then look back at it from the tower in the afternoon. Just make sure you're through the gates well before 5pm.

## Picnic manners on the slope

Japanese parks run on a few unwritten rules, and they're easy to follow:

- **Take your rubbish home.** Bins are scarce in Japanese parks. Bring a bag and carry everything back down.
- **Buy food before you climb.** Pick up onigiri, bento and drinks at a convenience store in town. Don't count on finding much up the hill.
- **Shoes off on picnic sheets.** If a group invites you onto their tarp during blossom season, leave your shoes at the edge.
- **Don't shake the branches.** Photograph the cherry blossom, but leave the branches alone. Pulling them down for a selfie gets disapproving looks.
- **Keep the noise down on weekdays.** The quiet is the point. Weekday visitors are mostly walkers and parents with toddlers.

Say "konnichiwa" to people you pass on the paths. Most park users will nod back.
