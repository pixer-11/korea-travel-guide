## The whole coast from one ridge

Kaikōura is easier to understand once you're up here. From the top of Scarborough Street the town shrinks into a strip of roofs along the shore. The Kaikōura Peninsula pushes out into the Pacific, with North Bay on one side and South Bay on the other.

Behind you, the Seaward Kaikōura Range rises sharply from the coastal flats. Snow often caps it from winter into spring. This view of mountains dropping almost straight into the ocean is the one the town is famous for.

It's a short stop. Allow 15–30 minutes, or longer if you've brought a camera and want the light to change.

## Reaching Scarborough Street

The lookout is at 274 Scarborough Street, South Bay, Kaikōura 7300. That's a short drive uphill from the town centre and the Esplanade.

- **By car:** Kaikōura sits on State Highway 1, roughly two and a half hours north of Christchurch and about two hours south of Picton. Follow the signs up Scarborough Street from town.
- **By train:** the Coastal Pacific stops at Kaikōura station. From there it's a steep walk uphill, or a short taxi ride.
- **On foot:** if you're fit, walking up from the Esplanade gives you views that open up as you climb.

## Choosing your hour

The lookout is currently listed as open 24 hours, so the only question is the light and the crowd. On weekends it's calmest between 7am and 9am. Early morning also suits the view, because the sun comes up over the Pacific and lights the mountain faces.

Avoid weekends from 12pm to 11pm. That's the busiest window, when campervans and day-trippers fill the small parking area. We have no weekday crowd figures, but outside school holidays the lookout is a much lower-profile stop than the whale-watching boats in town.

Clear, still mornings are the best bet. Cloud often settles on the range, so check the sky before you drive up.

## Visiting a residential hillside

Scarborough Street is a residential hill road, and how you behave up here matters.

- **Park only in the marked space.** Never block a driveway or stop on the narrow bends.
- **Keep your voice down at dawn.** People live close by, and your 7am visit is their morning too.
- **Take your rubbish with you.** Expect no bins or toilets. Use the ones in town first.
- **No drones over houses.** Check Civil Aviation Authority rules before you fly anywhere near the town.

The lookout pairs well with the Kaikōura Peninsula Walkway. Drive or walk down to Point Kean at the end of the peninsula road. There you'll find kekeno (New Zealand fur seals) on the rocks: stay at least 20 metres away and never get between a seal and the sea. Fyffe House, the town's oldest surviving building, is on the way.

Locals say the town's name the Māori way: "Kai-kō-ura", meaning a meal of crayfish. Once you've seen the peninsula from above, the roadside crayfish caravans along the coast make sense.
