## Down Through the Rock

It starts with an elevator. The car drops about 70 metres through the cliff face of the Niagara Gorge. When the doors open, a tunnel bored through the rock carries you out to the river's edge.

What waits at the end of that tunnel is the loudest water in Niagara. These aren't the falls. They're the Whirlpool Rapids, where the whole Niagara River squeezes through a narrow stretch of gorge. The water stands up in waves and moves at roughly 48 km/h, and the rapids are rated Class 6, which means they can't be navigated.

## The Boardwalk Beside the Rapids

The boardwalk runs for about 300 metres along the base of the gorge. It sits only a few metres above the water. The cliffs rise on either side, and the river below is a churning green-white mess that never settles.

Along the way you pass:

- Interpretive panels on the gorge's geology and the daredevils who tried to ride these rapids
- Viewing platforms that jut out closer to the water
- Views up to the Whirlpool Rapids Bridge, the railway and road crossing into the United States just upstream

Most people walk to the end, turn around and do it again slowly. The second pass is when you start picking out individual standing waves and the layers of rock in the gorge walls, laid down over hundreds of millions of years.

## Timing Your Visit

The current schedule is 10am to 5pm every day. Treat that as this week's schedule, not a fixed rule. White Water Walk is seasonal. It shuts for winter, and its hours shift through spring, summer and autumn. The Niagara Parks website lists this month's times.

The last elevator down runs some time before closing, so don't turn up right at 5pm. Arriving soon after the 10am opening usually puts you ahead of the tour groups working their way along the Parkway from the falls. In summer, a late-afternoon visit means the sun lights up the spray on the gorge walls.

Allow 45 minutes to an hour. That covers the elevator, the walk out and back, and some time just watching the water.

## Getting to River Road

The attraction sits at 4330 River Rd, on the Niagara Parkway north of downtown. It's about 3 km from the Horseshoe Falls and roughly 2 km from the Clifton Hill strip. If you're fit, you can walk it along the riverside path, which follows the gorge rim.

Niagara Parks' WEGO bus stops at White Water Walk. That's the easiest way to combine it with other stops along the river without driving. If you do drive, there's parking on site, though rates are posted at the lot rather than published here.

## Doing the Gorge Like a Regular

Locals and repeat visitors tend to treat White Water Walk as one stop on a Parkway day, not a trip on its own. A few habits help:

- **Buy online or get a pass.** Niagara Parks sells single tickets and combination passes that bundle several of its attractions. Check the official site to see which passes currently include White Water Walk. Buying ahead means you skip the ticket counter.
- **Pay by card.** The counter takes cards like every other Niagara Parks site, so you don't need to dig for cash.
- **Plan for the noise.** Inside the gorge, the rapids drown out normal conversation. Agree on a meeting point before you go down.
- **Hold on to phones near the rail.** Anything dropped over the railing is gone for good.
- **Wear shoes with grip.** Spray can leave the boardwalk damp, and some side platforms have steps.

This is one of the Parkway's busiest attractions. The main way to beat the crowds is to arrive early, not to hunt for some secret entrance.

## Pairing It With the Rest of the Parkway

North of the walk, the Niagara River bends into a huge whirlpool. You can see it from above on the Whirlpool Aero Car, a cable car that crosses the basin. Seeing the rapids at water level and then the whirlpool from the air shows you the full force of the river.

Further downriver on the Parkway are the Niagara Parks Botanical Gardens and the Floral Clock, both easy WEGO or car stops. Heading back south brings you to Clifton Hill and then Table Rock at the brink of the Horseshoe Falls. Doing the falls in the morning and the gorge in the afternoon makes a full, well-paced day.
