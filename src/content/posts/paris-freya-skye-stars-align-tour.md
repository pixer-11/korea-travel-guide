Salle Pleyel doesn't usually turn up on a British pop tour itinerary. It's the room Paris built for orchestras in 1927, all Art Deco lines and a famously precise acoustic, which makes Freya Skye's stop here on November 13, 2026 an unusual pairing of artist and venue.

## Why Salle Pleyel is the story here

This is the first French date of the Stars Align Tour, and the choice of room says something about how the show is being pitched. Salle Pleyel seats its audience rather than packing a standing floor, so the sightlines and sound are built for listening, not just for a wall of noise near the stage.

If you've seen Freya Skye at a festival or an arena before, expect a different register of show. Ballad-heavy stretches of a pop set tend to land harder in a hall like this than they do outdoors.

## Finding Salle Pleyel

The venue sits on Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré in the 8th arrondissement, a short walk from Parc Monceau and the shopping streets around Rue de Courcelles.

- Nearest metro: Ternes or Courcelles, both on Line 2
- Also walkable from Miromesnil, Line 9 and Line 13
- The neighbourhood is residential and quiet at night, so plan your route out before the show ends rather than after

Because this is a concert hall rather than a stadium complex, there's no sprawling forecourt or long approach road. You're often on the doorstep within a couple of minutes of leaving the metro.

## Getting there and settling in

Salle Pleyel keeps a formal-ish house style compared to a typical pop venue: coats get checked, seats are numbered and ushers will ask you to take yours rather than clustering near the stage. Arrive with enough time to find your row without rushing.

- Doors typically open well before a stated start time at halls like this; build in a buffer
- Bars inside serve drinks pre-show and during intervals, but you take them into the auditorium only where allowed
- Phone and camera policies at classical-leaning venues tend to be stricter than at arena pop shows, so check the venue's own rules rather than assuming festival norms apply

## Eating and staying nearby before the show

The 8th arrondissement isn't a late-night eating district the way the Marais or Bastille are, so a pre-show dinner works better than trying to find something after.

Rue de Lévis, a covered market street a short walk north, has bakeries, cheese shops and small bistros that suit an early dinner before a concert-hall start. Parc Monceau itself is worth a slow lap beforehand if you arrive early and the weather holds; its gates close at dusk, so this is a pre-show detour, not a post-show one.

## Getting home afterward

Concert-hall shows in this part of Paris tend to let out in one bloc, so the Ternes and Courcelles metro entrances can see a short surge right after the final song. Walking a block or two before descending into the metro often beats waiting out the initial crush at the closest entrance.

Taxis and ride-hail cars have nowhere obvious to queue outside a hall on a residential street like this, so treat the metro as the more reliable option on the night rather than counting on hailing a car outside the doors.
