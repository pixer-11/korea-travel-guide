## Eleven days of table tennis under one roof

Most tennis Grand Slams have a table tennis equivalent. This is it.

WTT Singapore Smash is a Grand Smash, the highest tier on the World Table Tennis calendar. For eleven days, from Thursday 18 February to Sunday 28 February 2027, the world's top players fill OCBC Arena at the Singapore Sports Hub. Everything happens indoors, in an air-conditioned hall, while Kallang sits in the February heat outside.

Grand Smash events cover the full set of disciplines:

- Men's and women's singles
- Men's and women's doubles
- Mixed doubles

That breadth is why the event runs so long. The draw starts wide and narrows each day until only the title matches remain.

## OCBC Arena and the Kallang precinct

The venue sits on the waterfront. OCBC Arena is the indoor hall within the Singapore Sports Hub, the sports complex built around the domed National Stadium on the edge of the Kallang Basin.

Around the arena you have the stadium's huge roof, the OCBC Aquatic Centre and Kallang Wave Mall, all joined by covered walkways. The Singapore Indoor Stadium is a short walk along the water. Table tennis fits a hall like this well: seats rise steeply on every side, so even upper rows look down onto the blue table.

## Getting to the Sports Hub

The MRT does most of the work. Stadium station (CC6) on the Circle Line opens straight onto the Sports Hub grounds, and the walk to the arena stays mostly under cover.

Kallang station (EW10) on the East-West Line also works if you are coming from the city centre or Changi. From there it is a longer walk across to the Sports Hub, roughly 10 to 15 minutes.

- **Paying for the train:** Singapore's MRT gates take contactless bank cards and mobile wallets, so visitors rarely need a separate transit card.
- **By taxi or ride-hail:** drivers know "Sports Hub" and "Kallang Wave Mall" as drop-off points.
- **From Marina Bay:** Stadium is a short ride on the Circle Line, and many visitors base themselves around Bugis, City Hall or Marina Bay for that reason.

## Early rounds versus the final weekend

Each day of the eleven feels different. In the opening days, several matches usually run on multiple tables at once, with players warming up nearby and scoreboards ticking over all over the hall. It is the best time to watch many players in one session, including the ones you have not heard of.

As the draw narrows, the tables go. By the end, one table sits under the lights and the whole arena watches the same rally.

The closing weekend, Saturday 27 and Sunday 28 February, is where the event ends, and the title matches sit at the end of the draw. Fans who want medal matches usually go then. Fans who want the most table tennis per hour usually pick an early weekday.

## Courtside manners at a table tennis match

Table tennis crowds work differently from football crowds. The ball is light, rallies last seconds, and players read spin by sight, so the arena goes quiet the moment someone steps up to serve.

- **Noise:** spectators cheer between points, not during them. The roar after a long rally is part of the show; shouting during a serve is not.
- **Moving seats:** people usually wait for the point to finish, or for a break between games, before walking along a row.
- **Photos:** flash is a distraction at table level, and regulars keep it switched off.
- **Reading the score:** a game goes to 11 points, two clear. Matches are best of several games, so a player down two games is never out of it.

Singapore pays by card and phone nearly everywhere, the arena concessions included, and tipping is not expected. Fans usually come prepared for a cold hall: after the humidity outside, the air-conditioning hits quickly, so many bring a light layer.

## Eating around Kallang between sessions

The quickest food is next door. Kallang Wave Mall connects to the Sports Hub and has a food court and casual restaurants, which is where most spectators go between sessions.

For a proper hawker meal, Old Airport Road Food Centre is two stops east on the Circle Line at Dakota station (CC8). It is one of Singapore's best-known hawker centres, a long open-sided hall with rows of stalls selling dishes such as:

- char kway teow
- rojak
- satay
- fried Hokkien mee

The usual system: reserve a seat with a packet of tissues or an umbrella, then queue at the stall and pay there.

## Making a trip of it

Eleven days leaves room for the rest of the city. Kallang is a few minutes from Marina Bay by MRT, which puts Gardens by the Bay and the bayfront promenade within easy reach in the evening.

The Kallang Basin itself has a waterfront path linking the Sports Hub toward the city. In the cooler evening it is a pleasant walk after the last match of a session.

World Table Tennis organises the Smash, and its official site carries the match schedule, session times and ticket links.
