Parco Sempione is 38.6 hectares of landscaped green wedged between Sforza Castle and the Arco della Pace, laid out by Emilio Alemagna in 1893 on former ducal hunting grounds and parade fields. It's the biggest park in central Milan and one of the most reviewed attractions in the city.

## The layout, gate to gate

Enter from Castello Sforzesco on the south side and you're facing the park's spine: a straight sightline north to the Arco della Pace, a triumphal arch built for Napoleon and finished under Austrian rule in 1838.

Along the way sit the park's landmarks:

- **Torre Branca**, a 108.6-metre steel observation tower by Gio Ponti (1933), open seasonally for city views
- **Palazzo dell'Arte**, home to the Triennale design museum
- **Civic Aquarium**, Italy's oldest surviving aquarium building, from the 1906 World Fair
- **Arena Civica**, a neoclassical amphitheater used for public events
- An artificial lake with rowboats near the castle end

Paved paths loop the whole park, so a full circuit on foot takes about an hour; add 30 minutes to detour into the Triennale or aquarium.

## When to go

Hours run 6:30am to midnight every day, among the longest of any Milan park, so early joggers and late-evening walkers both have full access. Crowd patterns are steep here: weekends from 11am to 9pm draw the heaviest foot traffic, with families, tour groups from the castle, and picnic crowds filling the lawns near the lake.

Both weekday and weekend mornings from 7am stay noticeably calmer, and that quiet holds until late evening. If you want the lake and lawns without jostling for space, arrive before 11am or come back after 9pm when the light-lit paths empty out.

Spring and early summer are best for the lawns; the plane trees and horse chestnuts give heavy shade by June, useful since Milan summers run hot and humid.

## Getting there and getting around

The park has no single main entrance; gates open on all sides.

- **Cairoli (M1)**: exits onto Castello Sforzesco, the classic approach through the castle courtyards
- **Cadorna (M1/M2)**: a 10-minute walk via Foro Buonaparte
- **Lanza (M2)**: closer to the park's eastern paths and the Arena

Trams 1, 4, and 19 stop near the castle side. There's no vehicle parking inside the park; street parking around Foro Buonaparte and Corso Sempione is metered and limited, so public transit is the practical choice.

## Moving through it like a regular

Milanese cut through Sempione on foot or bike as a shortcut between the Brera and Sempione neighborhoods, not as a destination to linger at on a Saturday afternoon. Given the park's very-popular status, weekend midday visits mean shared lawns and busy paths near the lake and Arco della Pace.

Bikes and scooters are common on the wider paved paths; stick right and expect fast-moving commuters even inside the park. Entry is free and there are no ticket gates, but the Torre Branca, aquarium, and Triennale each charge separate admission and keep their own shorter hours, so check those before planning around them specifically.

Bins are placed regularly along main paths; benches near the lake fill first on sunny weekends, so the castle-facing lawns further south are your fallback for open grass.
