A stone tower rises above the rooftops of Rockland, and that is where you're headed. Craigdarroch sits at the top of Joan Crescent, a short curved road climbing off Fort Street. The house is all turrets, steep roofs and heavy stone, more Scottish laird than Canadian banker. Then you step inside and it is wood almost everywhere you look.

## The house a coal fortune built

Robert Dunsmuir never got to live in it. The Scottish-born coal baron made his fortune in Vancouver Island's mines and started building in 1887. He died in 1889, before the house was finished in 1890.

His widow Joan moved in and stayed until her death in 1908. After that the building went through several institutional lives:

- a military hospital after the First World War
- Victoria College, the forerunner of the University of Victoria
- school board offices
- a music conservatory

Each tenant left its mark. Restoring the interiors to their Dunsmuir-era look has taken decades of work by the museum society that runs the castle today.

## Four floors of oak, glass and stairs

The visit runs upward. On the ground floor, dark carved panelling lines the hall and the formal rooms, and stained and leaded glass catches the light at the windows. The bedrooms and family rooms are above, furnished with period pieces, and the panels on the walls explain who slept where.

The climb ends at the dance hall on the top floor. One more flight takes you into the tower. From there you can see across Victoria's rooftops, and on a clear day the Olympic Mountains stand out across the water.

The upper floors are reached by staircase only. Anyone who struggles with stairs should check the official site's accessibility information before buying a ticket.

## Reaching Rockland from downtown

The castle is about 2km east of the Inner Harbour. The walk takes roughly 30 minutes: head up Fort Street, then turn uphill onto Joan Crescent.

Several BC Transit buses run along Fort Street. Get off near Joan Crescent and walk the final few minutes up the hill. Drivers will find a small car park at the top, and it fills quickly on busy days. If it's full, you can usually find street parking in the residential lanes nearby.

## Moving through the castle without the crush

The tour is self-guided. You follow a set route through rooms that were built for one family, not a few hundred visitors. Hallways get narrow and staircases bottleneck. On a crowded afternoon you'll shuffle, so let the slow groups go ahead on a landing.

- **Pick your day.** The castle is busiest on weekends from 10am to 4pm, which covers almost all of its open hours. Wednesday to Friday is the better bet.
- **Mind the closed days.** At the moment it closes Mondays and Tuesdays. Hours change through the year, so check the official site for this month's schedule.
- **Get tickets sorted first.** Current admission is listed on the official website and at the entrance. Booking ahead saves time at the door when it's busy.
- **Arrive early.** Last entry comes before the 4:30pm close. Turning up near the end means rushing the top floors, and the tower is the best part.
- **Look up and down.** Ceilings, newel posts and the windows on the stair landings hold some of the finest detail.

Plan on 60 to 90 minutes. Most people spend longer on the stairs and in the tower than they expected.

## Pairing it with the rest of Rockland

Rockland is old-money Victoria. Big houses and mature gardens line its streets, and the castle sits right among them.

A short walk away is Government House, the official residence of British Columbia's Lieutenant Governor. Its gardens are open to the public, including a rose garden and native Garry oak meadows. Together they make an easy half day.

Fort Street's Antique Row is on the way back downtown, with shops selling furniture, silver and curios. It suits the mood after an afternoon of Victorian rooms. Keep walking and you reach the Inner Harbour, with the Royal BC Museum and the Parliament Buildings.
