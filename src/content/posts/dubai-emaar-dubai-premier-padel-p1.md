## Eight Days Behind the Glass

A padel court is a box. It measures twenty metres by ten, with glass walls at each end and mesh along the sides. The ball stays in play after it hits the glass, so rallies come back from shots that look finished. For anyone who has only watched tennis, that's the surprise.

The Emaar Dubai Premier Padel P1 was set to bring that box to Dubai from November 8 to 15, 2026. Every match on the programme was doubles. Sitting close to a padel court, you hear the low, flat pop of a solid racket with no strings, and you see players chase lobs off the back wall and turn them into smashes.

The name tells you who the sponsor is. Emaar is the Dubai developer behind the Burj Khalifa and The Dubai Mall, and it put its name on the city's stop of the world's top professional padel circuit.

## Where a P1 Sits on the Tour

Premier Padel is the elite professional tour. It launched in 2022, has the backing of Qatar Sports Investments and is sanctioned by the International Padel Federation (FIP). Its events come in tiers. The four Majors are at the top, the P1 tournaments sit just below them, and the P2 events come after.

For spectators, that tier matters. A P1 offers serious ranking points, so the top-ranked men's and women's pairs usually play. The draws are deep enough to include the established stars and also the pairs pushing up the rankings, sometimes on the same day.

## Reading the Week: Early Rounds to Sunday Final

Eight days is a long event. The 8th and the 15th were the bookends, and the week was planned around the usual shape of a professional tournament.

- **The opening days:** qualifying and the first main-draw rounds. Lots of matches, lots of courts in use, crowds spread thin.
- **Midweek:** the draw narrows and the top seeds play. Plenty of fans think this is the best value, with several strong matches in one session.
- **The closing weekend:** semi-finals and then the men's and women's finals. Demand for tickets is highest and the arena feels the most like a stadium.

People who mostly want atmosphere usually go for the final weekend. People who mostly want padel often go midweek, when there are more matches per seat. The order of play at these events is set day by day as the draw progresses.

## Tickets and the Venue

Premier Padel's official site is where the tour keeps its event pages, ticket links and venue information. Tournament tickets are usually sold by session, and the later rounds are priced separately from the early ones. Prices differ by session and seating area.

Big padel events build their show court as a temporary arena, and a stop can move from one year to the next, so older articles about the Dubai event don't always match a given edition. Most attendees get to Dubai venues by taxi, ride-hail or the Metro, all of which are described below.

## Courtside Manners at a Padel Final

Padel crowds make more noise than tennis crowds. The quiet moments still matter.

- **During a point:** spectators stay seated and quiet, especially near the glass. Players can see movement through the walls.
- **Between points:** applause, chanting and calling players' names are normal. Spanish and Argentine fans tend to set the volume.
- **Changing seats:** stewards usually hold people at the entrances until the changeover after odd-numbered games, the same as in tennis.
- **Paying:** card and mobile payments are standard in Dubai, so most visitors carry little cash.
- **Dress:** smart-casual is fine. Shoulders and knees covered is the polite default in Dubai's public spaces, even at sporting events.

Players often stay to sign autographs or take photos after matches, particularly in the early rounds. Fans who want a signed ball usually wait near the player exit, not by the court gate.

## Dubai in Mid-November

November is when Dubai becomes comfortable outdoors again. The summer heat has gone. Days are warm but bearable, typically around 30°C in early November, and evenings are pleasant enough to sit out. That's one reason so many international sports events cluster in this part of the year.

The sun sets early, around half past five, so evening sessions are played under lights. Air conditioning in malls, taxis and the Metro is still strong, so a light layer is useful indoors. Sunscreen and a hat help for any session in daylight.

## Getting Around Dubai That Week

Most visitors use three ways of getting around.

- **Dubai Metro:** the Red Line runs from Dubai International Airport through Deira and along Sheikh Zayed Road to the Marina. Fares are paid with a rechargeable Nol card, and eating and drinking on board is not allowed.
- **Taxis:** the cream RTA taxis are metered and easy to find.
- **Ride-hail:** Careem and Uber both operate across the city and are popular for late finishes.

When matches end at night, a lot of fans tend to leave together. Many order a ride-hail from a spot a little away from the main exit rather than waiting in the taxi queue.

Emaar's best-known landmarks make an easy add-on on a free day. Downtown Dubai has the Burj Khalifa, the Dubai Fountain shows on Burj Khalifa Lake and The Dubai Mall, all within walking distance of one another. Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall Metro Station on the Red Line connects to the mall by an air-conditioned walkway.
