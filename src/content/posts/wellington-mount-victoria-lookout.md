## A hill you can see the whole city from

Matairangi, the Māori name for Mount Victoria, rises about 196 metres above the inner city. From the top it feels like a model of Wellington laid out on a table. The CBD towers crowd the waterfront below you, and Oriental Bay curves away to one side. On the far side, planes line up on the Rongotai runway, so close you can follow one from the turn to touchdown.

The summit is a paved viewing area with railings, information boards and benches. Grassy slopes drop away on every side. Across the harbour you can pick out Matiu/Somes Island, the Miramar Peninsula and the Hutt Valley, with the Remutaka Range behind on clear days.

## What's up on the summit

The lookout is small, and you can walk around all of it in a few minutes. Look out for these:

- **The Byrd Memorial**, a monument to the American polar explorer Admiral Richard E. Byrd, who flew to Antarctica through this part of the world.
- **Orientation boards** that name the peaks, suburbs and islands in each direction.
- **The grass slopes** below the platform, which people use as an informal picnic spot.
- **The airport view** to the southeast, which aviation fans come for.

Allow 20–40 minutes just for the views. Add an hour or more if you walk up through the Town Belt forest instead of driving.

## The Town Belt on foot

Pine forest covers Mount Victoria's lower slopes as part of Wellington's Town Belt. A network of tracks runs from the edges of Mount Victoria suburb and Oriental Bay up to the summit. The climb is steady, and from Courtenay Place most people take 30–45 minutes.

Lord of the Rings fans know these woods. Peter Jackson filmed early Hobbit scenes here, including the hobbits hiding from a Black Rider beside the road. Several signposted tracks pass close to the spot. You walk on packed dirt and pine needles, and it gets muddy after rain.

## Getting up there

Driving is the easiest way, but it's not relaxing. Lookout Road winds up narrow and steep, and the car park at the top is small. Pull in carefully, because walkers and cyclists use the same road.

Metlink buses serve the Mount Victoria area. Use the Metlink journey planner to see which route gets closest on the day you go, as services change. A taxi or rideshare to the top, then walking down through the forest to Oriental Bay, makes a good half-day loop.

The lookout is currently listed as open 24 hours. This is a public reserve, though, so check Wellington City Council notices for any road closures or works before you go.

## Beating the weekend crush

This is one of the city's best-known viewpoints, and tour vans and cruise passengers come here too. It's calmest between 7am and 8am, and again between 10pm and 11pm. That holds on weekdays and weekends alike. Weekends are the real problem: from 9am to 10pm the platform stays busy and parking runs out.

Some habits that help:

- **Go at 7am** for empty railings and low light over the harbour before the city wakes up.
- **Come after 10pm** for the lit-up CBD and the airport lights, with only a few cars in the car park.
- **Bring a layer whatever the forecast says.** Wellington's wind hits the summit hard, and it feels several degrees colder than the waterfront.
- **Give photographers room** at the railings. People take turns at the best spots, so step back once you have your shot.
- **Take your rubbish down with you.** There are few bins at the top, and the gusts carry litter into the bush.

Sunset is the most popular moment, so expect company then. If you want that light without the crowd, the morning window gives you similar colour over the eastern hills.

## Pairing it with the rest of the day

The lookout fits easily into a walk around the inner city. Head down the forest tracks to Oriental Bay for the beach promenade and the waterfront walk to Te Papa, the national museum. Or drop into Mount Victoria suburb and its steep streets of wooden villas, then on to the bars and restaurants of Courtenay Place.

Planning a few days in Wellington? Go up here first. Ten minutes on the summit shows you how the harbour, the hills and the suburbs fit together, and the rest of the city is easier to find your way around afterwards.
