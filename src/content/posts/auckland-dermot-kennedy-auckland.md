## One night at Quay Park

Dermot Kennedy plays Spark Arena on Saturday, November 28, 2026. The Irish singer-songwriter grew up in Rathcoole, on the edge of Dublin. He started out busking on Grafton Street before his debut album *Without Fear* (2019) went to number one in Ireland and the UK.

His follow-up *Sonder* came out in 2022. His sets lean on huge sing-along choruses, so an arena crowd sings back "Outnumbered", "Power Over Me" and "Giants" almost word for word. Don't expect a quiet folk recital. His raw, full-throated vocal sits over big drums and piano, and the songs are built for a room this size.

## Inside Spark Arena

Spark Arena opened in 2007 as Vector Arena. It is Auckland's main indoor concert venue, holding roughly 12,000 people for a standing-floor show. The building sits at 42 Mahuhu Crescent, between the rail yards and the Parnell side of the CBD.

The layout is a rectangle:

- **General admission floor** in front of the stage, where the singing is loudest
- **Tiered seating** on three sides, with sightlines that stay clear even from the upper rows
- **Concourses** behind the seating, with food and drink counters and toilets

Late November is early summer in Auckland. Evenings are mild and usually light until around 8pm, so the queue outside is rarely cold. The venue's event page carries the ticket links and the entry conditions for this show.

## Getting to Mahuhu Crescent

Public transport is the easiest way in. Waitematā station (Britomart), at the bottom of Queen Street, is the hub for Auckland's rail network. From there, the walk east along Beach Road or Quay Street to the arena takes about 10 minutes.

- **Train:** every suburban line ends at Waitematā; The Strand station is also within walking distance
- **Ferry:** the downtown ferry terminal sits opposite Britomart, handy for anyone coming from Devonport or Waiheke
- **Bus:** many routes stop along Customs Street and Quay Street
- **Car:** parking around Quay Park is limited and gets snarled after shows

Fans usually eat beforehand around Britomart's lanes or the Viaduct Harbour waterfront, both a short walk away. Parnell's restaurants are uphill to the south-east.

## Getting out after the encore

Everyone leaves at the same moment, and that rush is what shapes the end of the night. Most locals walk straight back to Waitematā rather than wait in the ride-share crush on Mahuhu Crescent. Pickup points there clog quickly with traffic.

How Aucklanders handle it:

- **Paying for transit:** trains, buses and ferries take an AT HOP card or a contactless bank card or phone, tapped on and off
- **Ride-share:** people often walk a few blocks towards Britomart or Parnell before calling a car, away from the arena gridlock
- **GA floor:** fans who want the front barrier usually queue well before doors, because the floor fills from the front
- **Bars:** the late-opening bars around Britomart and the Viaduct take much of the post-show crowd

New Zealand has no tipping culture, so nobody expects extra at the bar or from drivers. Card and phone payments are standard across the city centre.
