You don't expect a cocktail bar to sit inside one of the last freestanding Teochew mansions in Singapore, but that's exactly the setup on Penang Road.

## The building does the talking

The House of Tan Yeok Nee is one of only a handful of grand pre-war Chinese courtyard houses left standing in the city, built for a 19th-century Teochew merchant. Its granite columns, sweeping roof and enclosed courtyards have survived decades of Singapore's redevelopment while entire neighbourhoods around it were rebuilt.

Loca Niru and Bar Kap now occupy that shell, with the original brick and timber left exposed against contemporary bar counters, low lighting rigs and modern furniture. The contrast is the whole point: century-old structure, current-day drinks culture.

## Two bars, one compound

Loca Niru and Bar Kap operate as a linked pair within the same restored mansion, rather than as separate destinations you'd need to book into individually.

- **Loca Niru**: the newer of the two concepts, built around the mansion's cocktail-bar identity
- **Bar Kap**: its companion space in the same compound
- **Jing Studio**: a sister concept on-site focused on Chinese tea, for anyone wanting a non-alcoholic counterpoint

Because all three sit under one roof, you can move between a tea pour and a cocktail without leaving the building. That flexibility is part of why the complex has been picking up attention quickly since opening.

## Finding it on Penang Road

The House of Tan Yeok Nee stands on Penang Road, in the Museum Planning Area near Dhoby Ghaut, one of the more walkable heritage corners of central Singapore. It's a short walk from the Dhoby Ghaut MRT interchange, which sits on the North-South, North East and Circle lines, so you can reach it without a car.

Because this is a recently opened complex, opening hours, reservation policy and any dress code haven't settled into fixed public record yet. Check directly with the venue or its listed channels before you go, especially if you're planning a weekend evening visit when a new, buzzy opening is more likely to fill up.

## Reading the room like a regular

This is a heritage building repurposed for drinks, not a museum with a bar attached, so treat it with the same casual confidence you'd bring to any new-opening bar crawl stop.

- Walk in and ask which of the linked spaces (Loca Niru, Bar Kap, or Jing Studio) suits what you're after before you sit
- Expect the crowd and buzz of a fresh opening; arriving earlier in the evening likely means an easier time getting seated before it fills
- Because it's inside a conserved monument, don't expect much flexibility on moving furniture or wandering into closed-off courtyard sections
- Confirm reservation policy ahead, since new bars in landmark buildings in Singapore often fill by word of mouth long before wider awareness catches up

Pair the visit with a walk around the rest of the Museum district, since Dhoby Ghaut puts you within easy reach of Fort Canning Park and the Singapore Art Museum area for a fuller afternoon before the bars open.
