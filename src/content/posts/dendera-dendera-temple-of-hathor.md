## Under Nut's Blue Body

Look up as soon as you step inside. In the hypostyle hall, 24 columns rise to capitals carved with Hathor's face, cow ears and all. Above them the ceiling is painted in deep blue, with the sky goddess Nut stretched across it, vultures with spread wings and the boats of the sun and moon.

Most of what you see dates from the Ptolemaic and Roman periods. The temple was built over a much older sacred site. Its late date explains why it is intact: roof, stairways and inner chapels still stand where Karnak shows open sky.

## Rooms Most Visitors Rush Past

The main hall holds people longest. The rest of the complex rewards anyone who keeps walking:

- **The roof stairs.** Two staircases carry reliefs of priests in procession, climbing on one side and descending on the other, as they did at the New Year festival.
- **The Osiris chapel on the roof.** It has a cast of the circular Dendera zodiac. The original ceiling was removed in the 19th century and is now in the Louvre.
- **The crypts.** These narrow passages in the walls hold the much-discussed "Dendera light" reliefs. Bring a torch.
- **The rear outer wall.** Cleopatra VII and her son Caesarion appear here in large relief, making offerings.
- **The grounds.** Look for the birth houses (mammisi), a sanatorium and the sunken sacred lake, now planted with palms.

Allow two to three hours. Go longer if you climb to the roof and walk the outer walls.

## Reaching Dendera from Luxor or Qena

Most visitors come as a day trip from Luxor by private car or taxi, and the drive takes about 1.5 hours. Many pair it with Abydos and the Temple of Seti I further north, but that makes for a long day. Start early if you plan to do both.

Trains run from Luxor to Qena. From the station it is a short taxi ride across the Nile bridge to the temple. Some Nile cruises also stop nearby.

The current schedule is 7am–5pm. Ancient sites in Egypt can change their hours by season and on holidays, so check the official antiquities site before you set out.

## Timing It Around the Tour Vans

Dendera is well known and sits on the Luxor day-trip circuit. It is busiest on weekends between 8am and 2pm, when the minibuses from Luxor tend to arrive together. Try not to land in the hypostyle hall in that window.

Arrive between 7am and 8am, or come between 4pm and 5pm. Either way you can usually stand under the ceiling with only the guards for company.

Practical habits that help here:

- Buy tickets at the gate office. The current price and any photography permit rules are posted there.
- Carry small Egyptian pound notes. Guardians often unlock a side room or point a torch at a relief, and they expect a tip for it. Agree to it or decline politely before they start.
- Shade on the open grounds is thin. Take water, and see the outer walls first in the early morning, then move indoors as the heat builds.
- Climb the roof stairs slowly. The steps are worn smooth, and the light is dim between landings.
