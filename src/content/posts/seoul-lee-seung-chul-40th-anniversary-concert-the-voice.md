## Forty years of one voice

The anniversary is the debut year: 1986. Lee Seung-chul first came to wide attention as lead singer of the rock band Boohwal, then built a solo career on power ballads. Korean audiences know him as one of the country's strongest live vocalists.

Over three nights, December 4-6, 2026, **The Voice** marks four decades of that career. Solo standards like "Girls' Generation" (the 1989 song the K-pop group later took its name from) and "Mallikkot" sit alongside his Boohwal years. A younger generation also remembers him as a judge on the audition show *Superstar K*.

## KSPO Dome, Olympic Park

The venue is a covered arena in Songpa-gu, built for the gymnastics events of the 1988 Seoul Olympics. It sits inside Olympic Park, a wide open space of lawns, sculptures and the earthen walls of Mongchon Fortress, a Baekje-era earthwork.

Getting there by subway:

- **Olympic Park Station (Line 5), Exit 3**: the most direct walk to the dome.
- **Mongchontoseong Station (Line 8)**: across the park, a useful alternative when Line 5 platforms clog after the show.
- **Jamsil**, with Lotte World Tower and its malls, is one stop-cluster away for dinner before the show.

The arena's seating rises steeply from a central floor. Upper-tier seats look down at a sharp angle but have clear views of the stage.

## Tickets and seating

Seat maps, ticket links and the start time for each night sit with the concert's organiser and its ticketing partner. This guide gives no prices because none are confirmed here. Korean concert tickets are usually priced by section, with floor seats at the top and upper-tier seats at the bottom.

Three nights give fans some choice. Domestic fans often go for the final night, December 6, on the assumption that the closing show carries the extra encore.

## How a Seoul arena night runs

Korean arena concerts follow a fairly fixed routine:

- **ID at will-call.** Ticket pickup usually requires photo ID matching the booking name, and foreign visitors normally show a passport.
- **Merch first.** Official merchandise booths sit outside the arena, and their queues form well before doors. Fans typically arrive an hour or more ahead to shop and still get seated.
- **Phones down.** Korean arena shows commonly ban filming and photography during the set, and staff enforce it with signs and torches.
- **Sing when he points.** A veteran crowd like this one stays seated for the ballads, then stands and sings the choruses when he holds out the microphone.
- **Transport cards.** Most people tap in at the subway with T-money or a contactless card, and few queue for paper tickets.

Early December nights in Seoul are near freezing, and the park walk from the station is exposed to the wind. Attendees usually carry a coat they can fold under the seat. After the encore, many people walk ten minutes to Mongchontoseong or Bangi Station instead of queuing at Olympic Park Station.
