## Two planned cities, one night course

This race runs after dark. The Twincity Marathon is an annual night road race tying together Cyberjaya and Putrajaya, two planned cities next to each other in the Klang Valley, roughly 30km south of central Kuala Lumpur.

The pairing is what gives the race its name. Cyberjaya is Malaysia's tech-park city of office blocks and campuses. Putrajaya is the federal administrative capital, built around a man-made lake with the pink-domed Putra Mosque and the Perdana Putra complex on its shoreline. Both are laid out on wide, multi-lane boulevards, and that layout is what makes closing roads for thousands of runners workable.

## Date and distances for 2027

The 2027 edition takes place on Saturday night, January 24, 2027. It offers five distances:

- **Full marathon** (42.195km)
- **Half marathon** (21.1km)
- **12km**
- **10km**
- **5km**

The 5km and 10km categories draw first-timers and families. The 12km is an unusual distance that sits between a standard 10km and a half. Start times, course maps and registration fees appear on the organiser's own race pages; this guide doesn't reproduce them.

## Reaching Cyberjaya on race night

Rail gets you to the area, but the late finish changes things. The KLIA Transit line stops at Putrajaya & Cyberjaya station, and the MRT Putrajaya Line serves stations including Cyberjaya City Centre and Putrajaya Sentral. Those trains work well for reaching the area in daylight.

A night race can finish after the last trains have gone, so most participants drive or use Grab. Ride-hail pickups near race villages tend to pile up after the final finishers come in. For that reason, out-of-town runners usually stay in a Cyberjaya or Putrajaya hotel rather than in central Kuala Lumpur, which means a short walk or ride back.

## Race-night habits in the Klang Valley

The tropical night sets the pace more than the course does. January evenings in the Klang Valley stay warm and humid after sunset, and a passing shower is normal at this time of year. Experienced local runners treat the night start as relief from the sun, not from the heat.

What participants typically do:

- **Collect race kits beforehand.** Malaysian road races usually hand out bibs and timing chips at a separate kit-collection session in the days before the race.
- **Drink at every station.** Water and isotonic drinks are standard at Malaysian race aid stations, and seasoned runners take both even in the shorter categories.
- **Wear light or reflective kit.** The course runs under streetlights, and spectators find their runners more easily by a bright shirt.
- **Pay digitally.** Grab and the Touch 'n Go eWallet cover transport and most post-race food stalls, and Malaysians don't generally tip for either.

Etiquette on the start line is simple. Each distance lines up in its own pen, faster runners go to the front, and walkers keep to the left. Finishers who stay on to cheer the marathoners home are part of the night race tradition here.
