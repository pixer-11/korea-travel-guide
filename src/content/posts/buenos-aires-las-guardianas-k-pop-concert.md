## A K-pop tribute on Corrientes

On Monday, December 21, 2026, LAS GUARDIANAS brings its K-pop tribute and dance concert to Argentina for the first time. It plays Teatro Gran Rex, a single big hall on Avenida Corrientes. The audience is the city's K-pop following plus regional fans, and the show is built around the choreography and songs of the genre.

Expect a tribute production. The billing describes a dance concert that celebrates K-pop culture, so it is not a tour by a Korean act. The organiser publishes the dates, venue details and ticket links on its official site.

## Inside Teatro Gran Rex

You'll find the Gran Rex at Avenida Corrientes 857, between Suipacha and Esmeralda. Teatro Ópera stands almost directly across the avenue. Alberto Prebisch, who also designed the Obelisco, drew up the building, and it opened in 1937. Its plain white modernist front was a sharp break from the ornate theatres around it.

Inside, a single wide auditorium seats more than 3,000 people across three levels:

- **Platea**: the stalls, flat and closest to the stage, best for following the formations.
- **Pullman**: the first balcony, with a clean overhead view of the choreography.
- **Super Pullman**: the steep top tier. It is the furthest from the stage, but you still see the whole stage.

For a dance show, the balconies are an advantage. Patterns that look crowded from the stalls read clearly from above.

## Getting to the Obelisco block

The theatre is a few minutes' walk from the Obelisco, and three Subte lines meet underneath it:

- **Line B, Carlos Pellegrini**: runs under Corrientes itself. This is the closest stop.
- **Line C, Diagonal Norte**: connects from Constitución and Retiro.
- **Line D, 9 de Julio**: comes in from Palermo and Belgrano.

All three stations link underground, and you pay for every ride with a SUBE card. Many city bus lines also run along Corrientes and Avenida 9 de Julio.

December 21 is the summer solstice in Buenos Aires. Evenings are long and warm, and the Corrientes theatre strip stays lit and busy late on weeknights. After the show, crowds usually head for the Subte entrances by the Obelisco, or walk west towards the pizzerias.

## Doing Corrientes the porteño way

Before a theatre night on Corrientes, the classic move is pizza. Güerrín, at Corrientes 1368, serves thick *pizza al molde* and *fugazzeta* by the slice. Many people eat standing at the front counter, which is much faster than waiting for a table in the back. El Cuartito, on Talcahuano a block off the avenue, is the other long-established choice.

- **Ordering by the slice:** at Güerrín you pay at the register first, then hand over the ticket at the counter.
- **Tipping:** in sit-down restaurants, leaving about 10% in cash is normal. Card machines don't always offer a way to add a tip.
- **Dinner hours:** porteños eat late. Fans often have a quick slice before the show and a proper dinner afterwards.

Fans of tribute and dance shows here often wear group colours and bring lightsticks. They also sing the Korean lyrics along with the dancers. Fans typically gather on the pavement outside the Gran Rex well before doors open, since queues for its few entrances spread along the Corrientes sidewalk.
