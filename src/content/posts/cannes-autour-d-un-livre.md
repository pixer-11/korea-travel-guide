## A bookshop on the station street

Most visitors to Cannes never look at Rue Jean Jaures. They come out of the train station, cross it, and head south for the Croisette. That leaves this street of everyday shops to the people who live and work here, plus anyone who stops long enough to look up.

Autour d'un livre... sits at number 17. The name means "around a book", and the trailing dots are part of it. Reviewers rate it consistently well, though few tourists have found it yet. It's a quieter find in a town better known for red carpets than reading.

## Finding 17 Rue Jean Jaures

The easiest landmark is Gare de Cannes, the main SNCF station, which opens onto Rue Jean Jaures. If you arrive by TER from Nice or Antibes, you can be browsing within minutes of stepping off the platform.

From the seafront, walk north from the Palais des Festivals through any of the cross streets. You'll cross Rue d'Antibes, the long shopping street, and reach Rue Jean Jaures one block later. The walk is flat, short and easy in sandals.

- **By train:** Gare de Cannes, then along Rue Jean Jaures
- **On foot from the Croisette:** north past Rue d'Antibes
- **By bus:** most Palm Bus lines through the centre stop near the station

## Timing around the French lunch break

This shop keeps traditional French hours, and they catch people out. At the moment it opens Tuesday to Saturday from 10am to 12:30pm, then again from 1:30 to 6:30pm. Turn up at 12:45 and you'll find the door locked.

Monday is afternoon only, from 2 to 6:30pm. Sunday it's closed. Hours can change, so check the current listing before you make a special trip.

The lunch gap works in your favour, though. Spend a late morning browsing, have lunch nearby, then come back when it reopens at 1:30 for the book you couldn't stop thinking about.

## Browsing the way a French bookshop expects

A French *librairie* has its own small rituals. Knowing them turns a polite browse into a proper conversation.

- **Say "Bonjour" as you walk in.** It's expected in any small French shop. Skip it and you'll come across as cold. Say "Au revoir, merci" as you leave.
- **Ask for a recommendation.** Independent booksellers in France are known for hand-selling. "Vous avez un conseil ?" (any recommendation?) is a good way to start.
- **Expect French-language stock first.** That's normal for a French bookshop. If you want something in English, ask before you go hunting.
- **Don't haggle, and don't expect deep discounts.** French law fixes book prices, so a new novel costs about the same here as at a chain. The price is printed on the cover.
- **Ask for a gift wrap.** Many French bookshops will wrap a book if you say "C'est pour offrir". Ask rather than assume.

Most small shops in France take cards. Contactless is everywhere, though carrying a little change never hurts at small counters.

## Who will get the most from it

This isn't a place to tick off. It suits travellers who build a trip around small pleasures. Think of a paperback for the beach, a French children's book for a niece, or a novel set on the Riviera to read on the train home.

It also suits French learners. Buying a book in French from someone who can tell you what it's about is better practice than any app. If your French is rusty, a short question in French usually gets you a patient reply.

## Making an afternoon of the neighbourhood

The shop works best as one stop on a slow loop through the old side of town. Walk west and the streets get older and narrower.

- **Rue Meynadier:** a pedestrian street of food shops and boutiques that leads towards the old quarter.
- **Marché Forville:** the covered market, mostly fruit, vegetables and fish, liveliest in the morning.
- **Le Suquet:** the hilltop old town. Climb to the church of Notre-Dame d'Espérance and the Musée des Explorations du Monde for views over the bay.
- **Rue d'Antibes:** one block south, for mainstream shopping.

Here's one plan that works. Take the morning for Forville and the Suquet climb, then have lunch. Get to Rue Jean Jaures for the 1:30pm reopening, and finish on a bench along the Croisette with whatever you bought.
