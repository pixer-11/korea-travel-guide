Look up from the Small Palace Courtyard and you see a lattice of steel and translucent cushions where the sky used to be. That roof is the domed courtyard people photograph. It is also the hinge of the whole building: ticket desks, cloakroom and the doors to a dozen collections all open off it.

The palace you are standing in was a burnt-out shell after the bombing of 13 February 1945. Its reconstruction ran for decades. What you walk through today is a mix of faithful rebuilding and modern museum design, holding the state art collections of the Saxon electors.

## A palace with the treasury still inside

The Residenzschloss was the seat of Saxony's rulers for centuries. Augustus the Strong, elector and king of Poland, filled it with objects meant to dazzle visiting princes. Many of those objects never left the building, or came back once it was rebuilt.

That is the draw here. You are seeing royal treasure in the rooms designed to show it off, not in a neutral gallery. The palace is run by the Staatliche Kunstsammlungen Dresden (SKD), the state museum body that also runs the neighbouring Zwinger.

## Two Green Vaults, two very different visits

The name confuses almost everyone. There are two separate Green Vaults, ticketed separately.

- **Historic Green Vault (Historisches Grünes Gewölbe):** on the ground floor, the baroque treasure chamber as Augustus had it built. Mirrored walls, gilded consoles and objects set out in open displays, room after room. Entry is by timed ticket in small numbers, and you pass through a climate lock to get in.
- **New Green Vault (Neues Grünes Gewölbe):** upstairs, a modern museum layout with objects in individual cases. This is where you get close to the fine craftsmanship, including the Dresden Green Diamond and Johann Melchior Dinglinger's miniature court of the Grand Mogul.

If you can only do one, the Historic Green Vault is the experience nobody else has. The New Green Vault is better for studying individual pieces. The Historic Green Vault was the target of a high-profile jewel theft in 2019, and most of the stolen pieces were later recovered.

## Beyond the jewels

Plan time for the other collections too. Several are as good as the treasuries and far less crowded.

- **Giant's Hall (Riesensaal):** the Armoury's long ceremonial hall, lined with armour for horse and rider plus tournament gear.
- **Turkish Chamber (Türckische Cammer):** Ottoman tents, weapons and horse trappings collected by the Saxon court. The vast silk tent is the centrepiece.
- **Coin Cabinet (Münzkabinett):** coins, medals and orders spanning centuries.
- **Kupferstich-Kabinett:** prints, drawings and photographs, shown in rotating exhibitions.
- **State apartments:** the reconstructed parade rooms of Augustus the Strong.

For a focused visit to one Green Vault plus the Armoury, allow two to three hours. Seeing everything properly is a full day, and doing it in one session is tiring.

## Under the glass roof: the Small Palace Courtyard

The courtyard roof was added during the rebuild to turn an open yard into a covered foyer. Light comes through it evenly, so the sandstone facades read clearly even on a grey Dresden afternoon.

Stand in the middle and turn slowly. You can see where reconstructed walls meet surviving stonework. The courtyard is also a sensible meeting point if your group splits between collections, since every route eventually brings you back here.

## Getting in without the weekend crush

The doors open at 10am. On Saturdays and Sundays the building is busy from 11am right through to closing at 5pm, so there is no quiet afternoon to wait for. Turn up at 10am and do your most popular room first.

**Book the Historic Green Vault slot online.** Timed tickets are released through the SKD website and popular slots go quickly, especially on weekends. Prices are listed there too. A combined palace ticket does not always guarantee a Historic Green Vault slot, so read what your ticket includes.

**Remember Tuesday.** The palace is closed on Tuesdays. Every other day it keeps the same 10am to 5pm hours, Mondays included, which is handy because some other museums close that day.

**Travel light.** Larger bags and coats go into lockers or the cloakroom off the courtyard before you enter the collections. Photography rules differ from room to room, and the Historic Green Vault is stricter than the rest. Follow the signs at each entrance.

**Start late, lose rooms.** With a 5pm close, arriving after lunch leaves too little time for more than one major collection. Do the Green Vaults in the morning and keep the Armoury or Coin Cabinet for after lunch.

## The Fürstenzug and the walk around the walls

Some of the best palace sights cost nothing. On Augustusstraße, the outer wall of the Stallhof (the old stable courtyard) carries the Fürstenzug. It is a procession of Saxon rulers on horseback, more than a hundred metres long, made of Meissen porcelain tiles. It survived the 1945 bombing largely intact.

Walk its length from the Schlossplatz end to see the rulers in chronological order. Morning light is kinder for photos. From there the Frauenkirche and Neumarkt are a few minutes away.

## Getting there and what is next door

The palace sits in the Altstadt between the Zwinger, the Semperoper and the Catholic Court Church (Hofkirche). Everything in historic Dresden is walkable from here.

- **Tram:** the Theaterplatz and Postplatz stops are each a short walk from the palace.
- **On foot from Dresden Hauptbahnhof:** about 20 minutes north along Prager Straße and past the Altmarkt.
- **From the Elbe:** climb the steps up from Schlossplatz to the Brühl Terrace for the riverside promenade.

A full day pairs well: the Residenzschloss in the morning, then the Zwinger's Old Masters gallery or a walk across the Augustus Bridge to the Neustadt. The Taschenbergpalais, the baroque palace built for Augustus's mistress and now a hotel, stands directly opposite the entrance on Taschenberg.
