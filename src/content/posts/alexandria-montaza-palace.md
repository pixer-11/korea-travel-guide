## A palace you circle, not enter

Come for the grounds. Montaza Palace is a 1930s royal residence built in a blend of Turkish and Florentine styles, and it now serves as a public park. Its rooms are generally not open to visitors.

That changes how you plan the day. You walk the lawns, the palm avenues and the shoreline, and the palace stays a backdrop. The facade, the tower and the long terraces facing the sea are what you photograph.

For many Alexandrians this is simply the city's big garden. Families spread blankets under the trees, couples walk the sea wall, and children run between the flowerbeds. You join in rather than tour it.

## Reading the Haramlik

The main building is the Haramlik, completed in 1932 for King Fuad I. It was later the summer base of King Farouk, Egypt's last king. Look up at the tall tower first: its striped arches and loggias show the Florentine side of the design.

Lower down, the Ottoman influence shows in the deep balconies and the arcades along the ground floor. The stone has a warm, sandy tone that turns golden in late-afternoon light. Stand on the lawn on the seaward side for the full length of the facade.

Elsewhere in the grounds is the smaller Salamlik. This is an older royal lodge from the 1890s, built under Khedive Abbas II Hilmi. Together the two buildings span the last decades of Egypt's monarchy.

## Coves, causeway and the sea wall

The gardens run right down to the Mediterranean. A sheltered bay curves along the edge of the estate, with small beaches tucked into it. The water inside is calmer than the open sea off the central Corniche.

Out in the bay, a slender bridge leads to a small islet with a pavilion. It is one of the most photographed spots in the park. Walk out for a view back across the water to the palace tower.

Inside the gates, expect:

- Long palm-lined avenues, wide enough for cars as well as walkers
- Open lawns where families picnic
- Rocky stretches of sea wall where people sit and fish
- Beach areas with their own access arrangements, which you should check at each entrance

## Getting out to the eastern end of the Corniche

Montaza is a long way from downtown. It sits at the eastern tip of the Corniche on El-Gaish Road, in Al Mandarah Bahri. The drive from the centre follows the seafront the whole way and passes Stanley Bridge.

A taxi or a ride-hailing app is the simplest option. Ask for "Qasr el-Montaza" and confirm which gate you want, because the estate has more than one entrance. Microbuses also run along the Corniche if you're comfortable flagging one down. Traffic on the coast road builds on summer evenings, so allow extra time.

## Gates, tickets and the Friday crowd

This is a very popular park. Expect company, especially on Fridays and Saturdays, which make up Egypt's weekend. Ticket booths are at the gates. The current admission is posted there, and cars pay a separate entry, so have small notes ready.

Some practical habits that help here:

- Weekday mornings are reasoned to be calmest, before school and work let out.
- On Fridays, families often arrive after the midday prayer, so get there early in the day.
- Dress modestly on the lawns. Locals swim in a range of styles, but shoulders-covered is the norm away from the beaches.
- Walk the grounds anticlockwise from the main gate to reach the bay before the paths fill up.
- Pick a gate by what you want to see. Some are closer to the beaches and the islet, others to the palace lawns.

Keep your picnic tidy and stay off roped-off lawns. Guards do ask people to move. Photographing the palace exterior is normal, but don't point cameras at security staff.

## Late hours on the lawns

Montaza keeps unusually late hours. As listed this week, the gates open at 8am and close at midnight, and Friday shows as open 24 hours. Treat this as the current schedule, not a permanent one. Parks change their hours with the season and on holidays.

The long evenings shape how locals use the place. Once the summer heat breaks, the lawns and sea wall fill with families who stay for hours. If you prefer quiet, come in the morning. If you want to see the park at its liveliest, come in the evening.

## Pairing it with the rest of Alexandria

Montaza is far enough from the centre that it's worth building a half-day around it. Mamoura beach lies just to the east, a short hop along the coast. Further east, Abu Qir is known for its seafood restaurants on the bay.

Heading back west along the Corniche takes you past Stanley Bridge towards downtown. There, the Bibliotheca Alexandrina and the Citadel of Qaitbay sit at the opposite end of the seafront. A morning at Montaza followed by sunset at Qaitbay covers both ends of the city's coastline in one day.
