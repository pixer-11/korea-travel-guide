## What Sutēki actually is

This is a Japanese concept from AlphaMind Group, the same kind of hospitality group behind Dubai's wave of concept-driven restaurants that lean hard into a single idea rather than a sprawling menu. Sutēki's idea is wagyu, treated as the main event rather than one line item among many.

The framing is Tokyo-inspired: think counter seating oriented around the cooking action, a room designed to put the beef and the grill at the centre of the experience rather than tucked into an open kitchen at the back. That format rewards guests who book seats at or near the counter if that option exists, since it's the closest equivalent to watching a Ginza steakhouse chef work.

## The wagyu-first format, and why it matters

Most Japanese steak concepts in Dubai split their menu across wagyu, Australian beef, seafood teppanyaki and a handful of non-beef dishes to widen the appeal. Sutēki's summary points the other way: a concept "centred almost entirely" on premium wagyu, which suggests a tighter, more focused menu than the typical teppanyaki house.

That focus is the whole pitch. If you're the kind of diner who skips past the chicken and shrimp sections of a teppanyaki menu anyway, waiting for the wagyu course, this format removes the detour. It also means portion, grading and sourcing of the beef likely matter more here than at a broader-menu competitor, so it's worth asking directly what grade and origin of wagyu is being served on the night you visit, since that kind of detail shifts and isn't something to assume from a general concept description.

## Ordering at the grill

Treat a wagyu-focused Japanese steak counter the way you would any specialist restaurant: let the kitchen's specialty set the pace of the meal instead of trying to steer it elsewhere. A concept like this is generally structured around a few grades or cuts of wagyu, often with a small selection of starters or sides meant to complement rather than compete with the beef.

Ask your server to talk you through grading and cut before ordering if you're unfamiliar with Japanese wagyu classifications; at a specialist restaurant like this, staff are typically well versed in explaining marbling grade and cooking style. Pace yourself, since wagyu courses at counters like this are usually rich and served in smaller, more concentrated portions than a Western steakhouse cut.
