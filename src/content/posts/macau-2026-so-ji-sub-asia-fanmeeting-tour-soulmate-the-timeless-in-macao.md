## Saturday night under a fake Big Ben

The Macau stop falls on a Saturday. On October 24, 2026, Korean actor So Ji-sub brought his Asia fanmeeting tour, *Soulmate The Timeless*, to The Londoner Theatre in Cotai.

To reach the theatre you walk past a replica of Elizabeth Tower and a Houses of Parliament facade, both built on reclaimed land between Taipa and Coloane. The Londoner is Sands China's London-themed rebuild of what used to be Sands Cotai Central. Inside, it is all carpeted casino corridors, shopping arcades and a long run of restaurants.

## Who you are there to see

So Ji-sub's fans go back two decades. Plenty of them first saw him as the doomed Cha Moo-hyuk in *I'm Sorry, I Love You* (2004). Others found him later in *The Master's Sun*, *Oh My Venus* or *My Secret Terrius*. He also has a following for his film roles, *Rough Cut* and *Always* among them, and for the rap tracks he has put out over the years.

A fanmeeting is a different thing from a concert. Korean fanmeetings usually combine:

- talk segments with an MC and a Mandarin or Cantonese interpreter
- games and Q&A built around fan submissions
- one or two songs
- photo-taking moments or small fan events, depending on the ticket tier

The exact running order and any ticket-tier benefits belong to the organiser. This guide doesn't list them.

## Getting to The Londoner

Cotai is flat and its resorts sit close together. The Londoner is on Estrada do Istmo, a short walk from The Venetian Macao and The Parisian Macao, and its own Eiffel Tower replica makes it easy to get your bearings.

How visitors usually arrive:

- **From Hong Kong:** the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge shuttle bus to the Macau Port, then a taxi or resort shuttle to Cotai.
- **By ferry:** boats to Taipa Ferry Terminal, which is the closest ferry terminal to the Strip.
- **From Zhuhai:** through the Border Gate or Hengqin crossing, then a taxi south.
- **Within Macau:** the Light Rapid Transit's Taipa Line runs along Cotai, and taxis queue outside every resort.

Hong Kong passport holders, mainland visitors with the right permits and many foreign nationals go through separate immigration lanes at each port. On a Saturday those lanes are often the slowest part of the trip.

## Inside the theatre on the night

The Londoner Theatre is a proper seated hall. It isn't a converted ballroom, so every seat has a direct sightline to the stage. Fans usually gather in the lobby well before doors open, swapping photocards, posing with banners and queuing at any merchandise counter the organiser runs.

Korean fanmeetings in Macau almost always ban professional cameras. Many also restrict phone recording during the show, and staff hold up signs to enforce it. Fan support projects are common at events like this: coordinated light-up moments, slogan banners and a shared message board. Fan clubs arrange these on social media, not the venue.

## How fans in Macau handle the day

Most people coming from overseas or Hong Kong make a weekend of it. They stay at The Londoner itself or at one of the linked Cotai resorts, so the walk back after the show is under ten minutes and stays indoors.

A few habits that make the day easier:

- **Money:** Macau patacas and Hong Kong dollars are both accepted across Cotai. Card and mobile payment work in the resorts.
- **Tipping:** you aren't expected to tip at restaurant counters, and many sit-down places add a service charge.
- **Getting out afterwards:** taxi lines at the resort entrances get long the moment a show ends. Many fans eat dinner inside the resort and leave once the queues have gone down.
- **Language:** staff at the theatre speak Cantonese, Mandarin and English, and signs in the resort use all three.

## Filling the hours before the show

The Londoner keeps visitors close by design. Its Crystal Palace atrium, the London-style shopfronts and the costumed guard performances fill an afternoon without you leaving the building. You can walk on to The Venetian's indoor canals and gondolas, or ride up The Parisian's Eiffel Tower replica for a view across the Strip.

If you have a free morning, Taipa Village is a short taxi ride north. Its narrow lanes of Portuguese-era houses and Rua do Cunha's snack shops offer egg tarts, pork chop buns and almond cookies. They are a good antidote to a day spent inside a casino resort.
