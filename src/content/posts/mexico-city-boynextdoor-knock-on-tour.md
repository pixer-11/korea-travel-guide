## Tickets in a crowded market

Mexico City is one of the most competitive ticket markets in the Americas. Major arena shows here usually sell through Ticketmaster México, with a presale tier for fan-club members or certain bank cards before general sale.

Resale scams are a real problem around K-pop dates in Mexico. Fans who lose out on the primary sale typically stick to the official resale channel tied to the original platform. Screenshots of tickets passed around on social media are the classic way people end up turned away at the gate.

Mexican K-pop crowds have a reputation, and it is earned. The singing is loud, the fanchants are rehearsed, and the fan projects are organised weeks in advance through fan accounts on X and Instagram.

- **Freebies:** fans swap photocards, stickers and keychains in the lines outside. Trading is part of the night.
- **Lightsticks:** the official lightstick usually syncs to the show by Bluetooth. Fans pair it at their seat once they're inside.
- **Spanish and Korean:** banners mix both languages. Members tend to try a few words of Spanish from the stage, and the room answers at full volume.

## Getting there and getting home

Traffic is the real headliner on a weeknight. Most attendees use the Metro or Metrobús to get to the show, since they run on fixed tracks and lanes while the avenues jam.

Leaving is harder than arriving. Around every big arena, ride-hail prices on Uber and DiDi spike at the moment thousands of people open the apps at once. Many fans walk a few blocks away from the venue before requesting a car, or ride the Metro while it is still running.

Visitors from outside the city usually stay in Roma, Condesa or Juárez. These neighbourhoods are central, walkable and well connected to most of the arenas listed above.

## Late November in the city

November is dry season in Mexico City. Days tend to be clear and mild, and nights get properly cold, often dipping into single digits Celsius.

The city sits at about 2,240 metres. Visitors flying in for the show sometimes feel the altitude on the first day, especially after hours standing and singing. A layer for the walk out of the arena and plenty of water make a real difference.

If you're staying a few days, the concert pairs easily with the rest of the city. Chapultepec Park, the Museo Nacional de Antropología and the Centro Histórico around the Zócalo are all a short ride from the central neighbourhoods.
