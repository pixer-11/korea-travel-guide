## A festival built around one idea

Every edition has a single theme. The Cairo International Art Festival picks one subject each year and gathers the work around it, so the whole programme answers the same question from different angles.

The stated aim is dialogue. The festival places Cairo as a hub where the Arab art scene and the international one meet, rather than a stop on someone else's touring circuit. That framing shapes the rooms themselves. Work from Beirut, Amman or Casablanca typically hangs alongside work from Europe, often in the same space.

## Cairo's art map: Gezira, Zamalek and Downtown

Most of Cairo's art scene sits within a few kilometres of Tahrir Square, spread across four districts with distinct characters.

**Gezira island, the southern half.** This is the state's cultural compound. The Cairo Opera House grounds hold the Museum of Modern Egyptian Art and the Palace of Arts, a large hall used for national exhibitions. Opera station on Metro Line 2 is a short walk from the Opera gates.

**Zamalek, the northern half of the same island.** This is the private-gallery district. Leafy streets of old apartment blocks hide commercial galleries on ground floors and in converted flats, often marked only by a small brass plate beside the door. People hop between them on foot over an afternoon.

**Downtown (Wust el-Balad).** Here the galleries and independent spaces sit among the belle-époque buildings around Talaat Harb Street, with peeling façades and wrought-iron balconies. Sadat station, the Line 1 and Line 2 interchange under Tahrir Square, sits on its edge.

**Dokki, across the river to the west.** The Mahmoud Khalil Museum holds a well-known collection of French Impressionist and 19th-century European painting in a riverside villa. It makes a useful counterpoint to contemporary festival work.

## Crossing the city between rooms

Cairo traffic is the real timetable. A ride that looks like ten minutes on a map can take forty around 2pm or after dark on a weekday.

The Metro is the reliable option for the Gezira and Downtown legs. Line 2 links Sadat and Opera in a single stop under the river, and the system has women-only carriages near the middle of each train. Ride-hailing apps such as Uber and Careem are widely used, and the price shows before the ride starts, which saves haggling.

Walking works well inside each district but not between them. The bridges over the Nile, Qasr al-Nil from Tahrir to Gezira most of all, are long and exposed. They are pleasant in November and much less so in a jammed rush hour. Visitors who group venues by district usually see far more than those who cross the city twice a day.

## Gallery manners in Cairo

Cairo galleries are relaxed, and a few local habits make a visit smoother.

- **Greetings matter.** A quick "salaam alaykum" or "masa' el-kheir" (good evening) to whoever is at the desk goes a long way. Attendants often walk visitors through the show when they show interest.
- **Photography varies.** Some spaces welcome phones and some exhibitors don't. A gesture towards the camera and a raised eyebrow usually settles it.
- **Street dress is the norm.** Shoulders and knees covered is the easy default for moving through Downtown and between districts.
- **Small Egyptian pound notes are useful.** Cards work in many galleries and cafés, but cash pays for taxis, tea and the baksheesh tips given to doormen and parking attendants.
- **Evenings run late.** Openings in Cairo tend to start after sunset and go on for hours, and the crowd often moves to a nearby café afterwards.

Conversation is part of the point here. Artists and gallerists often attend their own shows, and a question about the work usually gets a long, generous answer.

## November in Cairo

November is one of the most comfortable months to be in the city. Days are warm and dry without summer's heat, and evenings are cool enough that a light layer helps after dark.

The festival dates fell in the main sightseeing season. Many visitors pair a day of galleries with the Giza Plateau or the Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square. Downtown is the practical base for an art-focused stay: it sits between Gezira, Zamalek and the Metro interchange at Sadat.
