## Two days, two age groups

This is France's national title for its youngest snooker players. Over Saturday 16 and Sunday 17 January 2027, the country's best under-18 and under-21 cueists compete for their category crowns in Maromme.

The sport is played on a full-size 12-foot table: 15 reds, six colours and a cue ball on green baize. Matches are frames of potting a red, then a colour, then a red again, until the table is cleared or a frame is conceded. Youth events like this one are where the players who later reach French senior finals and international junior tours tend to appear first.

Snooker in France is governed by the Fédération Française de Billard (FFB). Event information sits with the federation and the host club.

## Reaching Maromme

Maromme is in the Cailly valley, a few kilometres up the river from central Rouen in Seine-Maritime. It is part of the Métropole Rouen Normandie, so the area's Astuce public transport network covers it.

- **By train:** Rouen Rive Droite is the main station, with direct trains from Paris Saint-Lazare. Maromme also has its own small station on the regional TER line towards Le Havre.
- **By bus:** TEOR bus routes run from central Rouen out through the Cailly valley to Maromme.
- **By car:** The town lies just off the main roads leading north-west out of Rouen, so the drive from the city centre is short.

January in Normandy is usually cold and damp. Visitors coming from further afield typically stay in Rouen, where there are far more hotels than in Maromme itself.

## Watching from the rail

The etiquette at a snooker match is strict, and junior players feel every breach of it. Spectators normally follow these conventions:

- **Stay still during a shot.** Nobody walks behind or in the eyeline of a player who is down on the cue. People move between frames or while the referee re-spots balls.
- **Phones on silent, no flash.** A camera flash at the table can cost a player a pot.
- **Applaud at the right moments.** Clapping is for a frame-winning ball, a big break or a difficult escape from a snooker. Gasps and comments while a player is still at the table are frowned on.
- **Keep your voice down.** If several tables are running at once, a cheer at one of them breaks the concentration at the others.

At French billiards events, talk usually happens in French, with snooker terms borrowed from English: "break", "snooker" and "frame" are used as they are. The referee's call "faute" means a foul.

Youth championships draw parents, coaches and club players, and the crowd is small. That makes the hush around the table even more noticeable.

## Between sessions: Rouen next door

Central Rouen is close enough for a meal or an evening between the two days of play. The old town around the **Cathédrale Notre-Dame de Rouen** is the one Monet painted over and over, its Gothic facade facing the square.

A short walk away, the **Gros-Horloge** is a gilded astronomical clock set into an arch over a pedestrian street lined with half-timbered houses. At the far end, **Place du Vieux-Marché** is where Joan of Arc was burned in 1431. The modern church dedicated to her now stands on the square, its roof curving like an upturned hull.

Most visitors combine one day of watching snooker with an afternoon in these streets.
