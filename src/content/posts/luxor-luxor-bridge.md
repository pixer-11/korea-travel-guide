## The crossing south of town

Luxor splits in two along the Nile. The East Bank holds the hotels, Luxor Temple and Karnak. The West Bank holds the tombs and mortuary temples of ancient Thebes, and Luxor Bridge is how you reach it by road.

The bridge sits several kilometres south of the town centre. Its western end comes down near El-Boghdady in Al Qarna. It's a long concrete road span, built for vehicles. Nobody comes here for the bridge itself. What you get is the view from the middle: wide brown water, sugarcane and palms along both banks, and the bare limestone ridge of the Theban hills ahead.

## What the drive gives you

Seen from a car window, the crossing shows you how the valley is laid out. Looking north, the river runs back toward the city. On the West Bank side, green fields run right up to the edge of the desert, and you can see clearly where watered land stops and bare ground begins.

Once you reach the far side, the road runs west through farmland toward:

- **Colossi of Memnon**, the first big stop, right beside the road
- **Medinet Habu**, the temple of Ramesses III, to the south
- **The Ramesseum** and **Deir el-Bahari** (Hatshepsut's temple) further on
- **Valley of the Kings**, up in the hills at the end of the route

Google currently lists the bridge as open 24 hours. Early starts for the tombs mean crossing at first light, when the hills turn pink and the river is usually flat and calm.

## Riding over without the hassle

Since the bridge is so far south, a road trip to the West Bank is a long loop. If you just need to cross, the public ferry from the corniche near Luxor Temple is far more direct. Motorboats wait at the same landing. Use the bridge when you're travelling by car with a driver for a full West Bank day.

How the locals do it:

- **Agree the taxi fare before you get in.** Meters are rarely used in Luxor, so settle the price and whether it's one-way or a full-day hire.
- **Pay in Egyptian pounds, in cash.** Drivers seldom take cards, so carry small notes.
- **Ask before photographing at checkpoints.** Police posts are common on bridge approaches in Egypt. Photos taken through the window while moving are usually fine, but don't point a camera at officers or barriers.
- **Don't plan to walk it.** The span is long, exposed and built for traffic. The ferry is the sensible way to cross on foot.

## Fitting it into a West Bank day

Count on the crossing as part of a 15–30 minute drive from East Bank hotels to the first monuments, depending on where you start. If you've hired a driver, ask them to slow down on the bridge on the way back. Late-afternoon light falls straight down the river from the west, the best view you'll get of it from a road.

Pair the return crossing with sunset on the East Bank corniche, or a felucca trip from the landings near Luxor Temple. That way you see the river from two levels on the same day.
