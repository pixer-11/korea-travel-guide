## Running through the rice bowl

Tianzhong is small, flat and mostly fields. Irrigation channels and narrow farm roads divide the paddies into neat rectangles. The low green ridge of Bagua Mountain sits to the east. On November 7, 2026, this landscape becomes a racecourse for Taiwan's Rice Heaven Tianzhong Marathon.

The race has three distances:

- **Full marathon:** the long tour through the paddies and the villages around the town.
- **Half marathon:** the most popular pick for runners who travel in from Taipei or Kaohsiung.
- **10km:** a shorter loop that suits first-timers and families.

Early November falls around the end of the second rice crop in central Taiwan. Depending on the year, some fields are golden and some are already cut to stubble, and both look like farm country.

## What the course feels like

The roads are mostly level, so you get no punishing hills here. What you do get is a long stretch of open fields with very little shade, and the sun is strong once it climbs. Taiwanese road races generally start before sunrise to stay ahead of the heat. Runners usually plan their pacing around that first cool hour.

This race made its name on its crowds. Earlier editions became known for residents lining the roads, temple drum groups and lion dances at intersections. Local volunteers also handed out food at the aid stations: rice dishes, fruit and other produce from Changhua. That is how the "Rice Heaven" nickname caught on with Taiwanese runners.

## Taking the roadside hospitality in stride

Most of the course's character comes from the people beside it. Here is how runners usually handle it.

- **Learn two words.** "Jiāyóu!" (加油) is the cheer you will hear all morning. "Xièxie" (謝謝, thank you) is the right reply when you take something from a table.
- **Sample, don't feast.** The food tables are generous. Experienced runners take small bites early and leave the real eating for the finish.
- **Keep the road clear.** Spectators often stand right at the edge of farm lanes. Runners usually slow down near aid stations and step aside before stopping to take a photo.
- **Look for the bins.** Volunteers sort cups and wrappers, so runners drop rubbish at the stations and don't throw it into the paddies, which are working farmland.

Spectators usually gather near the start and finish and in the town centre, where the crowds are thickest. Cheering from the field roads means a long walk or a scooter ride.

## Getting to Tianzhong on race weekend

The town is easy to reach by rail.

- **TRA Tianzhong Station** sits on the Western Line, so local and express trains connect it with Changhua, Taichung and points south.
- **THSR Changhua Station** is in Tianzhong Township itself, a short drive from the town centre. That puts the race within easy reach of Taipei and Kaohsiung by high-speed rail.

Taiwanese marathons usually start before trains begin running. Runners who come by rail tend to stay locally the night before, or they arrive by car or chartered bus. Most streets near the course close to traffic on race morning, and parking fills up quickly around the start area.

## Where runners usually stay

Tianzhong has only a few places to stay, and they go fast on race weekend. Many visitors stay in **Changhua City** or **Taichung** instead. Both are a short train ride north, with a wide choice of hotels. Some groups book guesthouses in nearby townships such as **Ershui** or **Yuanlin**.

The day after is a good time to explore Changhua County. A few easy trips:

- **Lukang:** an old port town with temples and narrow brick lanes.
- **Bagua Mountain Great Buddha** in Changhua City.
- **Ershui:** the next town south, known for its historic irrigation canal and the Jiji Line railway that branches off there.
