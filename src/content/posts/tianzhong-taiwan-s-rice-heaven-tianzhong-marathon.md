## What the course feels like

The roads are mostly level, with no punishing hills. Instead there is a long stretch of open fields with very little shade, and the sun is strong once it climbs. Taiwanese road races generally start before sunrise to stay ahead of the heat. Runners usually pace themselves around that first cool hour.

This race made its name on its crowds. Earlier editions became known for residents lining the roads, temple drum groups and lion dances at intersections. Local volunteers also handed out food at the aid stations: rice dishes, fruit and other produce from Changhua. That is how the "Rice Heaven" nickname caught on with Taiwanese runners.

## Taking the roadside hospitality in stride

Most of the course's character comes from the people beside it. This is how runners usually handle it.

- **Two useful words.** "Jiāyóu!" (加油) is the cheer heard all morning at Taiwanese races. "Xièxie" (謝謝, thank you) is the usual reply when taking something from a table.
- **Small bites.** The food tables are generous. Experienced runners take small bites early and leave the real eating for the finish.
- **A clear road.** Spectators often stand right at the edge of farm lanes. Runners usually slow down near aid stations and step aside before stopping for a photo.
- **The bins.** Volunteers sort cups and wrappers, so runners drop rubbish at the stations rather than in the paddies, which are working farmland.

Spectators usually gather near the start and finish and in the town centre, where the crowds are thickest. Cheering from the field roads means a long walk or a scooter ride.

## Getting to Tianzhong on race weekend

The town is easy to reach by rail.

- **TRA Tianzhong Station** sits on the Western Line, so local and express trains connect it with Changhua, Taichung and points south.
- **THSR Changhua Station** is in Tianzhong Township itself, a short drive from the town centre. That puts the town within easy reach of Taipei and Kaohsiung by high-speed rail.

Taiwanese marathons usually start before trains begin running. Runners who come by rail tend to stay locally the night before, or they come by car or chartered bus. Streets near the course usually close to traffic on race morning, and parking around the start area typically fills quickly.

Tianzhong has only a few places to stay, and they usually fill up on race weekends. Many visitors stay in **Changhua City** or **Taichung** instead.
