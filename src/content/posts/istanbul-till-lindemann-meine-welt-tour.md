## A Rammstein voice, without Rammstein

This is Till Lindemann alone, without the band. His solo Meine Welt Tour stops in Istanbul on Sunday, December 6, 2026, at Ülker Sports and Event Hall. It is one date on an arena run that crosses Europe and Asia.

Lindemann sings in German, in the low, rolling baritone that fronted Rammstein for three decades. His solo catalogue runs from the Lindemann project with Peter Tägtgren (*Skills in Pills*, *F & M*) to the 2023 album *Zunge*, released under his own name. The organiser publishes the dates, venue and ticket links on its official site.

## Inside Ülker Sports and Event Hall

The venue is a covered indoor arena in Ataşehir, a district of office towers and residential blocks on Istanbul's Anatolian side. On most winter nights it is home court for Fenerbahçe's basketball teams. For concerts, the floor becomes a standing area and the tiered seating bowl rings it on all sides.

That layout gives you two very different shows:

- **Floor:** you stand close to the stage and feel the bass in your chest, but your sightlines depend on the crowd in front.
- **Tiers:** you get a seat, a full view of the staging and lighting, and an easier exit.

Early December in Istanbul is cool and often wet. Fans usually wear a layer they can tie round the waist, because the inside of a packed arena runs warm.

## Crossing to the Asian side

Most visitors stay on the European side, around Beyoğlu, Taksim or Sultanahmet, so getting there means crossing the Bosphorus. Rail is the steadiest route:

- **Marmaray:** the undersea commuter line runs from Sirkeci or Yenikapı to Ayrılık Çeşmesi on the Asian shore.
- **M4 metro:** at Ayrılık Çeşmesi, the Kadıköy–Tavşantepe line heads east towards Ataşehir. Fans usually cover the last stretch on foot or by short taxi.
- **Ferry:** boats from Eminönü, Karaköy and Beşiktaş reach Kadıköy, where you join the M4.

Taxis across the 15 Temmuz Şehitler Bridge or the Fatih Sultan Mehmet Bridge are an option. Sunday evening traffic at both can turn a short drive into a long one.

## Doing the night like an Istanbul concertgoer

Locals pay for transit with an **İstanbulkart**. It is a reloadable card sold and topped up at machines in metro and Marmaray stations, and one card can be tapped for several people in a group. If you load enough for the return trip on the way out, you skip the queue at the machines after the show.

After big arena shows, the M4 platforms fill quickly. Many fans walk or ride to Kadıköy and wait out the rush over a late *dürüm* or a glass of tea around the Moda and Çarşı streets. After that, the ferry or Marmaray back is calmer.

For taxis, residents mostly use the **BiTaksi** app instead of flagging cars on the street. It shows the driver and route, which helps outside a venue where dozens of people are hailing at once.

Turkish arenas run security checks at the gates, with bag searches. Concertgoers usually carry a small bag or none at all. Ticketed seats are marked by block (*blok*), row (*sıra*) and seat (*koltuk*), the three words you'll see on signs inside.
