## Why Yongdusan Park anchors a Busan visit

You climb out of the alleys of Nampo-dong and the city noise just drops away. Pigeons scatter off a stone plinth, cicadas buzz in summer, and suddenly you're under a canopy of pines instead of neon signs.

The park sits on a low hill in Jung-gu, and it's less a single sight than a layered one: statues, a tower, a folk museum, and a series of terraces that keep unfolding as you climb.

## Getting there and getting up the hill

The easiest approach is Nampo Station (Busan Metro Line 1), Exit 7, which puts you at the base of the hill in central Nampo-dong.

From there you have three options:

- Walk up through the stone stairways and garden paths — 10-15 minutes, shaded most of the way
- Take the outdoor escalators that cut through the hillside, a local shortcut that skips most of the climb
- Ride the free elevator inside the Lotte Department Store Gwangbok branch, which lets out near the top

Because the park never closes, there's no gate and no ticket for the grounds themselves — only Busan Tower has its own admission.

## What to see once you're up top

Busan Tower is the obvious anchor, rising 129 meters and visible from much of the old downtown. The observation deck gives you a sweep of Busan Harbor, the container cranes at the port, and on clear days the outline of Gwangalli and the hills beyond.

Below the tower, the park's paths are dotted with monuments worth slowing down for:

- A large bronze statue of Admiral Yi Sun-sin, the naval commander who repelled Japanese invasions in the 1590s
- The Busan Citizen's Bell, cast to mark the city's civic milestones and rung on New Year's Eve
- Assorted stone markers and small gardens tucked between the main paths

Give the park itself 45 minutes to an hour if you're just wandering the terraces and monuments. Add another 30-40 minutes if you're going up the tower.

## When to go for the best light and fewest crowds

Weekday crowd levels stay manageable across a long window, roughly 7am to 11pm, so there's real flexibility if your schedule is loose.

Weekends are a different story. The park gets genuinely busy from 10am to 9pm, with tour groups and families filling the main terraces and the tower's ticket line.

If you're visiting on a Saturday or Sunday, the only real quiet window is early morning, 7am to 8am, before the crowds arrive and while the light is still soft on the harbor.

Evenings bring a different draw: Busan Tower lit up against the dark, and the surrounding hillside strung with light installations in warmer months.

## How to visit like a local

Yongdusan Park is one of central Busan's most visited attractions, so the goal isn't finding a quiet corner nobody knows — it's timing your visit to dodge the peak crush.

- Go early. Weekday mornings, or that narrow 7am-8am weekend window, are your best shot at empty pathways.
- Skip the stairs if you're short on time — the escalators or the Lotte Department Store elevator save real effort.
- Pay for the tower ticket at the counter; cash and card are both fine, and there's rarely a need to book ahead outside peak holiday weeks.
- Combine it with Nampo-dong and Gukje Market, both within walking distance, so the hill becomes one stop on a bigger loop rather than a separate trip.
- The mistake most visitors make is arriving midday on a weekend expecting a peaceful hilltop walk — it's the busiest stretch of the whole week here.

Because the park is open around the clock, there's genuine freedom to build it around the rest of your Busan day rather than the other way around.
