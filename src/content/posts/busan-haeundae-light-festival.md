## Seven weeks of lights by the sea

This is the festival's 13th edition, and it runs through Christmas and New Year. Dates: **November 29, 2026 to January 18, 2027**. For seven weeks, winter illumination displays light up two connected spaces.

- **Gunam-ro**: the wide pedestrian-friendly road running from Haeundae Station down to the sand, lined with shops, cafés and chain restaurants.
- **Haeundae Beach**: the long crescent of sand between Dongbaekseom island and the Mipo end, with the towers of Marine City and the LCT complex rising behind it.

The two halves feel different. Gunam-ro is a tunnel of light at street level, packed in with neon shopfronts. At the beach, installations sit against the dark open water and the lit hotel facades.

## Getting to Gunam-ro

The festival begins at the subway. Haeundae Station on **Busan Metro Line 2** opens directly onto the top of Gunam-ro. From there it is a straight, gently sloping walk of several hundred metres to the beach.

From central Busan, the ride on Line 2 from Seomyeon takes around half an hour. Trains arriving from Gimhae Airport connect at Sasang or Seomyeon. Nearby parking is limited and the roads clog up on weekend evenings, so most visitors come by metro. A T-money card covers every subway leg.

## Walking the lights the Haeundae way

Most people do the festival as a loop. They walk down Gunam-ro, along the beach promenade, and back up through **Haeundae Market**, the narrow food alley running parallel to the main road. A relaxed loop takes 60–90 minutes. Weekday evenings are usually calmer than Fridays and Saturdays, when people from all over Busan come down.

What locals typically do:

- **Eat in the market alley.** Skewers of *eomuk* (Busan fish cake) in hot broth, *tteokbokki* and fried snacks are the standard winter warm-up. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, and Korea has no tipping culture.
- **Dress for the wind.** Gusts off the sea make the beachfront noticeably colder than the street, so locals wear padded coats, hats and gloves.
- **Keep photos moving.** Gunam-ro gets dense after dark. People step aside for a quick shot rather than setting up tripods in the flow of foot traffic.
- **Head out from Line 2 exits away from the main crowd** if Gunam-ro is jammed. Side streets lead down to the beach as well.

## Before and after the lights

The festival fits into a full Haeundae evening. At the western end of the beach, a wooded coastal trail circles **Dongbaekseom**, passing Nurimaru APEC House and the lighthouse viewpoint. The lit towers of **Marine City** and The Bay 101 lie just beyond.

At the eastern end, **Mipo** is the starting point for the Haeundae Blueline Park beach train and sky capsules, which run along the old coastal rail line toward Cheongsapo. The **LCT** tower above the beach has an observation deck with views over the illuminated shoreline. Many visitors pair the festival with a seafood dinner in Haeundae or the nearby Songjeong area. Hotels along the beachfront and around Haeundae Station put you within a few minutes' walk of the displays.
