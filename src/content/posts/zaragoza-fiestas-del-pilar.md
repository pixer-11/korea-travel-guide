## Nine days in October

On 12 October, people in Aragonese dress walk Zaragoza's old town carrying armfuls of flowers. The men wear black waistcoats and knotted headscarves, and the women wear embroidered shawls. They are all heading for one plaza on the bank of the Ebro.

That walk is the centre of the Fiestas del Pilar, Zaragoza's biggest annual celebration. In 2026 it runs from Saturday 10 October to Sunday 18 October. It is built around the feast of the Virgen del Pilar, the city's patron.

The nine days pack in a lot. Expect religious processions, jota folk dancing, the giant papier-mâché figures of the *gigantes y cabezudos* parading through the streets, concerts, peñas (social clubs) out in force and late nights in the bars.

## October 12 at Plaza del Pilar

The Ofrenda de Flores is the day to plan around. Groups from across Aragón, Spain and Latin America file into Plaza del Pilar, one after another. Each group hands its flowers to volunteers, who work them into a tall frame set up in front of the Basílica del Pilar.

As the hours pass, the flowers build into a huge mantle around the figure of the Virgin. The plaza is long and open, with the basilica's tiled domes along one side. That gives you room to watch from the edges, but the processional route itself is fenced off.

October 12 is also Spain's national holiday, so the city is at its fullest. In 2026 it falls on a Monday, which turns the opening weekend into a long one for Spanish visitors.

## Tickets, venues and what's free

The fiesta happens mostly in the streets. You can watch these without a ticket:

- the Ofrenda de Flores in Plaza del Pilar
- the processions and the *gigantes y cabezudos* parades
- the open-air jota performances
- the closing fireworks over the Ebro

Some events need a ticket. Big-name concerts and the Feria del Pilar bullfights at the Plaza de Toros de la Misericordia are sold separately, with their own prices and conditions. Confirm the programme, venues and tickets on the official Fiestas del Pilar site run by the Ayuntamiento de Zaragoza. Venues and start times can change from one edition to the next.

## Reaching Zaragoza and the old town

Zaragoza sits halfway between Madrid and Barcelona on the high-speed line. AVE trains reach Zaragoza-Delicias station in under 90 minutes from either city. From Delicias, buses and taxis run to the centre. During the fiesta, the centre is easier on foot than by car.

Tram Line 1 runs north to south through the city. It stops at Plaza del Pilar-Murallas, at the western end of the plaza. Expect road closures around the old town for most of the nine days, especially on 12 October.

Book accommodation early. Hotels near the basilica fill quickly over the Pilar weekend, and staying in Delicias or across the river is a common fallback.

## Handling the Ofrenda crowds

The Ofrenda runs from morning into the evening, so you don't need to fight for one moment. Pick a spot away from the main entrance to the plaza. Watching from the side streets near La Seo cathedral is usually calmer than the corner where the groups come in.

A few habits help:

- **Let processions pass.** Groups walk in formation behind banners, so don't cross through a line of dancers or flower-bearers.
- **Visit the mantle afterwards.** The finished mantle of flowers stays in place for days. Locals come back on the 13th and 14th to see it complete, without the crush of the offering itself.
- **Join, don't just watch.** Visitors can take part in the Ofrenda by signing up with a registered group. Traditional dress isn't compulsory, but you'll see it everywhere.
- **Eat late.** Spanish meal times run late, and the fiesta pushes them later still. Bars in El Tubo, the narrow tapas lanes south of the plaza, stay packed well into the night.

Pay by card in most bars and shops. Keep some coins for the small stalls and for street vendors selling the *pañuelo* (scarf) people tie around their necks.

## Beyond the plaza

The fiesta spreads across the whole old town, so build in time for the streets around it. The Puente de Piedra, the stone bridge over the Ebro, gives you the classic view back toward the basilica's towers. The Gothic-Renaissance Lonja exchange building and La Seo cathedral sit on Plaza del Pilar itself.

The Aljafería is a short walk or bus ride west. It's an 11th-century Islamic palace that now houses the Aragonese parliament, and a quiet contrast to the noise of the centre.

Allow at least two full days to catch the Ofrenda and something of the rest of the programme. The final weekend ends with the fireworks over the river, a fitting close to nine days of fiesta.
