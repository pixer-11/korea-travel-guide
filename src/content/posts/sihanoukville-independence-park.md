Independence Park sits on the hill above Occheuteal Beach, built around the stupa-shaped Independence Monument that Cambodia raised to mark its 1953 break from French rule. The monument itself, a tiered concrete tower styled after Angkorian temple spires, is the whole reason the park exists; everything else is grass, paths and a view.

There's no ticket booth and no gate that locks, since it's listed as open 24 hours every day. That also means there's no official cutoff for lighting or upkeep after dark, so most visitors treat it as a daytime and dusk spot rather than a place to linger post-midnight.

## The monument and the view

The tower stands on a rise, which is the point: from the base you get an open sightline down to the Gulf of Thailand and the curve of Sihanoukville's coast. Sunset works best here, when the concrete catches orange light and the haze over the water softens the container port in the distance.

Bring water. There's little shade beyond a few trees near the paths, and the hill has enough exposure that midday visits feel long even though the walk itself is short.

## Getting there and how long to stay

The park sits on Victory Hill (JGJ4+8MW), a short tuk-tuk ride from central Sihanoukville or a walkable climb from the Occheuteal Beach strip if you don't mind the slope. Most drivers know it by the monument's name rather than "Independence Park."

Twenty to thirty minutes covers the monument and a loop of the grounds. Extend that if you're timing a sunset, since the light show over the water is the main draw, not the architecture up close.

- Walk the base of the monument for the carved tiers
- Find the eastern edge of the park for the clearest sea view
- Skip the visit if it's raining; there's no covered shelter to wait it out

## Visiting without the crowds

This isn't a place that draws tour groups the way the beach or the market does. With a rating built on under 400 reviews for a park this central, it stays genuinely quiet even in peak season, which is part of the appeal.

Locals treat it more as a jogging and evening-air spot than a sightseeing stop, so don't expect vendors or guides stationed at the entrance. Bring your own snacks or drinks if you want them; there's no reliable stall setup inside the grounds.

Because it's free and unstaffed, there's no queue system to navigate. Arrive whenever suits your schedule, though early morning avoids the heat and late afternoon sets up the sunset.

Pair it with a walk down to Occheuteal Beach afterward. The hill position means the walk down is easy; the climb up is the part to pace yourself on, especially in the afternoon heat.
