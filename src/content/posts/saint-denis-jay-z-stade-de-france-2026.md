## What's happening on September 10

Jay-Z brings a single stadium date to France in 2026, landing at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis on September 10. The show marks the 30th anniversary of "Reasonable Doubt," the 1996 debut that set up his run as one of hip-hop's defining figures and a artist with well over 100 million albums sold worldwide.

It's billed as a one-off, not a multi-city tour stop, which is part of why it's drawing attention across Europe. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site before you book flights or hotels around it.

## The venue: Stade de France

This is France's national stadium, built for the 1998 World Cup and seating over 80,000 for concerts. It sits in Saint-Denis, just north of central Paris, in a district known more for its Gothic basilica than its nightlife.

The stadium is a concrete-and-steel bowl ringed by wide plazas, built specifically to move huge crowds in and out efficiently. For a hip-hop headliner of Jay-Z's stature, expect the full stadium configuration: pitch-level standing plus tiered seating rising steeply on all sides.

## Getting there and back

Two RER B stations serve the stadium directly: La Plaine-Stade de France and Stade de France-Saint-Denis (also reachable via RER D). Both put you at the stadium's gates within a short walk.

- **RER B**: La Plaine-Stade de France, a few minutes' walk to the north entrances
- **RER D**: Stade de France-Saint-Denis, closer to the south side
- **Metro**: Line 13 to Saint-Denis-Porte de Paris, then a 15-20 minute walk

Trains run frequently on event nights, but they get packed fast. Budget extra time both ways, especially post-show when tens of thousands hit the platforms at once.

## Making a night of it in Saint-Denis

Saint-Denis itself is worth an hour before or after the show. The Basilica of Saint-Denis, burial site of nearly every French king from the 10th century onward, is a short walk from the stadium.

The area around the stadium fills with food trucks and pop-up bars on show nights, but options are limited and lines build fast. Eating in central Paris beforehand, then taking the RER up just ahead of doors, is the more reliable move than hoping to grab dinner near the venue itself.

## Getting in and out like you've done this before

Stadium shows here run on a strict bag-and-security policy: small bags only, no professional cameras, and a search at every gate. Check your ticket for your exact entrance number well before you leave home, since gates correspond to specific seating blocks and walking to the wrong one costs you time.
