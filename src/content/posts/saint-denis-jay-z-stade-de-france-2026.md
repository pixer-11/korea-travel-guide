## What's happening on September 10

Jay-Z was set to bring a single stadium date to France in 2026, at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis on September 10. The date was tied to the 30th anniversary of "Reasonable Doubt," the 1996 debut that set up his run as one of hip-hop's defining figures and an artist with well over 100 million albums sold worldwide.

It was billed as a one-off rather than a multi-city tour stop.

## The venue: Stade de France

This is France's national stadium, built for the 1998 World Cup and seating over 80,000 for concerts. It sits in Saint-Denis, just north of central Paris, in a district known more for its Gothic basilica than its nightlife.

The stadium is a concrete-and-steel bowl ringed by wide plazas, built specifically to move huge crowds in and out efficiently. For a hip-hop headliner of Jay-Z's stature, the full stadium configuration was planned: pitch-level standing plus tiered seating rising steeply on all sides.

## Getting there and back

Two RER B stations serve the stadium directly: La Plaine-Stade de France and Stade de France-Saint-Denis (also reachable via RER D). Both put you at the stadium's gates within a short walk.

- **RER B**: La Plaine-Stade de France, a few minutes' walk to the north entrances
- **RER D**: Stade de France-Saint-Denis, closer to the south side
- **Metro**: Line 13 to Saint-Denis-Porte de Paris, then a 15-20 minute walk

Trains run frequently on event nights, but they get packed fast, especially after a show when tens of thousands hit the platforms at once.

## Buying tickets without getting burned

Tickets for the Stade de France were sold through the venue's official box office and its named ticketing partners.

Third-party resale sites and social media listings are where inflated prices and fake tickets circulate for shows like this. A price that looks too good, or a seller pushing urgency, is the usual warning sign. Pricing tiers and any official resale option were handled through the box office itself.

## Making a night of it in Saint-Denis

Saint-Denis itself is worth an hour before or after a show. The Basilica of Saint-Denis, burial site of nearly every French king from the 10th century onward, is a short walk from the stadium.

The area around the stadium fills with food trucks and pop-up bars on show nights, but options are limited and lines build fast. Many concertgoers eat in central Paris beforehand, then take the RER up just ahead of doors, rather than hoping to grab dinner near the venue itself.

## Getting in and out like you've done this before

Stadium shows here run on a strict bag-and-security policy: small bags only, no professional cameras, and a search at every gate. Gates correspond to specific seating blocks and the entrance number is printed on the ticket, so walking to the wrong one costs time.

Cards and phone wallets are the norm for food and merchandise, and cash isn't always accepted. At the exit, the crowd empties toward both RER stations simultaneously, and the platforms are easiest once that first wave has cleared, which usually means a smoother ride back into Paris.
