## Shade over 42 hectares

You don't come here for flowerbeds. The gardens cover 42 hectares of native and tropical plants, and most of that space is shaded paths under a tall canopy. The point is cover from the Top End sun, plus plants you won't see growing outside in southern Australia.

The site sits at the corner of Gilruth Avenue and Gardens Road, in the suburb named after it, The Gardens. Two features are worth planning around:

- **The orchid house**, a covered space for the tropical orchids that grow well in Darwin's humidity.
- **The cafe**, which suits a cold drink in the middle of a loop or a late breakfast after an early walk.

The gardens are named after George Brown, a former Lord Mayor of Darwin. They have been a public garden since the colonial era.

## Getting to The Gardens

It's an easy trip from the city centre. The gardens are a few kilometres north of the CBD, on the stretch of coast that runs towards Fannie Bay. Cycle paths link the city to this part of town, so riding or walking in the cool of the morning is realistic.

If you drive, use the Gardens Road side. Taxis and rideshares can drop you at the Gilruth Avenue corner. Mindil Beach is right next door, and the Museum and Art Gallery of the Northern Territory is a little further along the coast. Both make a sensible half-day pairing.

## Timing the heat and the weekend rush

Pick the day first. Weekdays are quiet from 7am to 7pm, so a Tuesday visit at almost any hour beats a weekend one. On Saturday and Sunday, 8am to 2pm is the busiest stretch. That's exactly when you'd want the paths to yourself, so try not to visit then.

Next, pick the hour. The gates open at 7am every day, and the first two hours are the coolest you'll get. Locals walk and jog here early, then leave before the heat builds. The gates close at 7pm. In the dry season, a late-afternoon loop runs nicely into sunset at Mindil Beach.

- **Bring:** a refillable water bottle, insect repellent for the shaded gullies, and a hat for the open lawns.
- **Stay on the paths:** in the wet season (roughly November to April), some ground stays soggy for days after a storm.
- **Eat smart:** the cafe is the only food stop on site, so time your break before the weekend late-morning rush.

## How long and when in the year

Allow 1.5 to 2 hours for a relaxed loop with the orchid house and a stop at the cafe. Keen plant people can easily fill a morning. The dry season, May to October, brings lower humidity and clear skies, which makes walking easier.

The wet season is green and lush, but expect afternoon downpours and a sticky heat. Go early in the day and keep the visit short. Whatever the month, start at 7am on a weekday for the quietest, coolest gardens Darwin has.
