## Two nights, one weekend

The dates are November 14 and 15, 2026. That puts LE SSERAFIM's Pureflow Tour at Taipei Arena on a Saturday and a Sunday, two full shows back to back. The weekend timing shapes everything around it. Both nights draw fans from across Taiwan, and the MRT and hotels near the arena feel it.

The group is Kim Chaewon, Sakura, Huh Yunjin, Kazuha and Hong Eunchae. They debuted in 2022 under Source Music, a HYBE label. Their fans call themselves FEARNOT, and in Taipei that name turns up on banners, phone cases and hand-lettered fan cards all along Nanjing East Road.

## Inside the box on Nanjing East Road

Taipei Arena is hard to miss. It is a huge pale cube at the corner of Nanjing East Road and Dunhua North Road, on the edge of Taipei's business district. A broad open forecourt runs out in front of it, and that forecourt is where the concert crowd collects.

Inside, the seating rises steeply on all sides around a flat arena floor. For a concert, the stage usually sits at one end. That means side and upper-tier seats look down at a sharp angle rather than straight ahead. It is Taipei's main indoor venue for big K-pop tours, so most of the crowd will know the layout.

The building also houses an ice rink, Taipei Arena Ice Land. Don't be surprised to see skating signage on the way in.

## Tickets, seats and where the details live

Ticketing for Taipei Arena concerts runs through the promoter and its ticketing partner, not the arena itself. Some practical points that hold for shows here:

- **Two nights, two tickets.** Each date is its own show with its own seating, so many fans hold seats for both.
- **Floor vs. tiers.** The floor puts you closest to the stage. The lower tiers give a clearer view over the heads in front.
- **Named tickets.** Taiwanese K-pop shows often tie tickets to the buyer's identity. Fans usually carry the same ID they bought with.

## Riding the green line to Taipei Arena Station

The MRT stops right at the door. Taipei Arena Station (G16) is on the green Songshan–Xindian Line, and its exits come up on Nanjing East Road beside the forecourt. From Taipei Main Station, the usual route is the blue Bannan Line to Ximen, then the green line east. Other people take the brown Wenhu Line to Nanjing Fuxing and change there. It's one stop to the arena.

Most visitors pay with an EasyCard, the stored-value card sold at station machines and convenience stores. It works for the MRT and buses, and at 7-Eleven and FamilyMart counters too. Eating and drinking are banned inside the MRT paid area, and the fines are real. Finish that bubble tea before the gates.

## FEARNOT rituals on the forecourt

Hours before doors open, the forecourt is already busy. Fans gather around the official merchandise booths, where queues snake back across the plaza. Other fans hand out free gifts, called yingyuan (應援) items: photo stickers, printed cards and small keyrings they made themselves. Taking one is fine. Fans who brought their own often swap on the spot.

The official LE SSERAFIM light stick is what most of the crowd holds once the lights go down. Fans usually pair it through the official app before entry so it syncs with the show. Inside, fans sing along to the fan chants in Korean. The group's Taiwanese fans learn them as carefully as their Korean ones.

A few habits make the night smoother:

- **Escalators.** In the MRT, people stand on the right and walk on the left.
- **Lines.** Merch and entry queues are orderly and strictly first-come, and cutting in draws open disapproval.
- **Trash.** Public bins are scarce in Taipei. Fans carry their wrappers out or use the convenience stores along Nanjing East Road.

## After the encore: three stops to Raohe

When the show ends, thousands of people head for Taipei Arena Station at once. Many fans skip that squeeze and walk one stop west to Nanjing Fuxing, or one stop east to Nanjing Sanmin. Both are a short walk along Nanjing East Road.

Ride the green line east instead and it is two stops to Songshan Station. Raohe Street Night Market starts by Exit 5, under the ornate gate beside Ciyou Temple. It is one long, narrow lane of food stalls running down the middle of the street. Its best-known snack is the black-pepper bun (胡椒餅), baked against the walls of a clay oven near the temple entrance. Expect a queue, and expect the bun to be scalding inside.

For something quieter, Fujin Street in the Minsheng Community is a short taxi ride north of the arena. It is lined with trees and low-rise cafés.

## Staying in Songshan for a November weekend

Most out-of-town fans stay along the green line, so they can ride straight to the arena. Nanjing East Road and the Songshan area around the arena are full of business hotels. Ximending, at the line's western end, has cheaper rooms and late-night food. Songshan Airport sits just north of the arena, so a short domestic or regional flight lands minutes away.

Mid-November in Taipei is usually mild, though the northeast monsoon often brings grey skies and light drizzle. Fans waiting on the open forecourt tend to carry a compact umbrella and a light layer. Inside, the arena's air conditioning and a packed crowd keep things warm once the show starts.
