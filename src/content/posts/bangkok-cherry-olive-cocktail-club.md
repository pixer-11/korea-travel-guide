## Two bars, one marble shell

The name tells you how it's built. Cherry Olive Cocktail Club is two concepts, Cherry and Olive, under one roof. Each half takes its name from a classic cocktail garnish, and the room is finished in retro rose-gold and marble, so it reads more like a polished lounge than a dive.

The bar comes from Supawit "Palm" Muttarattana. It opened recently and was named Best New Bar at the 2026 B.A.D. Awards. A win like that brings attention quickly, so expect a busy room rather than a quiet one.

## Finding Convent Road

Convent Road is a short, leafy street linking Silom Road and Sathorn Road. It takes its name from the Catholic institutions that have lined it for over a century. Saint Joseph Convent school and the Anglican Christ Church both sit along it.

Transit options:
- **BTS Sala Daeng** (Silom Line): the closest Skytrain stop, at the Silom Road end.
- **MRT Si Lom** (Blue Line): connected to Sala Daeng by a walkway, and handy if you're coming from Sukhumvit or Hua Lamphong.
- **Taxi or Grab**: Sathorn Road traffic backs up badly in the evening, so taking the train and walking is usually faster.

From either station you reach Convent Road's mouth on Silom on foot. Lumphini Park is just across Rama IV Road if you want a walk before dark.

## Booking, paying and the house rules

You can't count on walking in, so book ahead. The bar is new and award-winning, which makes it unwise to assume a walk-in table on a Friday. Confirm current hours and reservations through its official social media before heading out. Neither has been published here, and new bars often change both in their first year.

How Bangkok cocktail bars generally work:
- **Prices are often "++"**: a service charge and VAT are added on top of menu prices, so the total on the bill will be higher than the listed figure. Check the menu's small print.
- **Tipping**: because service is usually already on the bill, extra tipping is optional. Leaving the small change is a common courtesy.
- **Cards**: upscale bars in Silom typically take cards, but carry some baht as a backup.
- **ID**: the legal drinking age in Thailand is 20, so bring your passport or a photo copy if you look young.
- **Dress**: smart-casual suits the marble-and-rose-gold room. Beachwear and flip-flops look out of place.

Check the calendar before you plan a night out. Thailand bans alcohol sales on major Buddhist holidays such as Makha Bucha, Visakha Bucha and Asanha Bucha, and on election days. Bars either close or serve only soft drinks.

## Building a night around it

Silom makes a bar crawl easy. Have dinner along Silom or Sathorn first. Then walk over and work through both halves: start in one concept, move to the other, and compare how each uses the same retro design.

Afterwards, the Sala Daeng area has plenty more late-night bars, and Patpong's night market is a few minutes' walk up Silom Road. The BTS and MRT stop running around midnight, so if you stay late, plan on a metered taxi or a ride-hail app to get home.
