Euljiro doesn't look like a nightlife district from the street. Old hardware wholesalers and lighting shops shutter their metal gates by early evening, and the alleys behind them go quiet except for a scatter of unmarked doors with lines forming outside. Chuan Chuan Jiu Bar sits in that world, one more low-key storefront in a neighbourhood better known for its printing presses than its cocktails, which is exactly why finding it feels like being let in on something.

## What "chuan chuan" actually means here

Chuan chuan is Sichuan's answer to hot pot, but it flips the format. Instead of a communal pot everyone dips into, skewers of meat, tofu, mushrooms and vegetables are pre-threaded and simmered, then ordered and eaten stick by stick.

That makes it inherently social and fast-paced: you keep grabbing more skewers as you go rather than waiting for a shared pot to cook everything at once. The broth carries the classic Sichuan combination of mala heat, doubanjiang and Sichuan peppercorn, the kind that leaves a tingling numbness on the lips rather than a straightforward burn.

## Why this address, specifically

Seoul has had Sichuan restaurants for years, but chuan chuan as its own dedicated format is a newer wave, and Chuan Chuan Jiu Bar has become the reference point for it. It's currently regarded as the trendiest spot in the city for this specific style of Sichuanese dining, the place people mean when they say they're "doing chuan chuan" rather than just getting hot pot.

Part of the appeal is the setting. Euljiro's blend of gritty industrial texture and hidden bars has made it Seoul's most talked-about neighbourhood for exactly this kind of discovery, and a Sichuan skewer bar slotted between machine shops fits the district's whole identity.

## Reading the room: how the skewers work

Once you're seated, the mechanics are simple even if you've never done chuan chuan before. Skewers sit refrigerated or displayed near the counter, already prepped and priced individually or by category, and you select what you want as you go rather than ordering a fixed set menu upfront.

The kitchen drops your selections into the simmering communal-style broth, then returns them cooked. You eat continuously, ordering more as your table clears out, which is part of why the format suits a group grazing over a couple of hours rather than a quick solo meal.

Expect the classic building blocks of Sichuan skewer eating: thin-sliced meats, tofu skin, lotus root, quail eggs, mushrooms and leafy greens are typical across chuan chuan bars generally, though you should treat the exact lineup here as something to see menu-side rather than assume.

## Ordering and paying like you've done this before

The skewer format rewards a specific rhythm, and knowing it upfront makes the meal smoother.

- Order in smaller waves rather than one huge opening batch, so nothing overcooks while you work through it
- Keep the empty skewer sticks on the table or in the provided container; some chuan chuan spots calculate your bill by counting them
- Pace the mala broth: the numbing effect from Sichuan peppercorn builds meal-long, so a dish that felt mild early on can taste hotter by your tenth skewer
- Card and mobile payment are standard across Seoul's newer bar and restaurant scene, though carrying some cash is a reasonable backup anywhere in an alley this narrow

Tipping isn't customary in South Korean restaurants and bars, chuan chuan bars included, so the bill is simply the bill.

## Managing the queue on a busy night

Given its current standing as Seoul's top chuan chuan address, the room fills up, and Euljiro's bar-hopping crowd tends to arrive in a wave after work lets out. If you want a table without much of a wait, an early weeknight visit is the more comfortable option than Friday or Saturday evening.

Because opening hours, reservation policy and exact walk-in rules can shift, especially for a newer, fast-trending venue, check current hours and whether bookings are accepted before you go. A quick search or call ahead saves you standing outside a shuttered hardware-shop alley wondering if you've got the wrong night.
