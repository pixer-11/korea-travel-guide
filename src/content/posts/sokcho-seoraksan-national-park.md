Granite spires shoot straight up from forest on the drive into Seorak-dong village, and that's the whole appeal of Seoraksan in one glance. This is Korea's third-highest range, part of the Taebaek mountains, and the national park wraps a huge chunk of Gangwon-do around it.

Sokcho city sits right at its eastern gate, so most visitors base themselves there rather than inside the park. Buses from Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal reach the Seoraksan entrance in about 20-40 minutes. Gates are open 9am to 6pm every day of the week, no exceptions listed, so plan your hike to finish before closing.

## The cable car versus the climb

Most people skip the summit and ride the Gwongeumseong cable car instead. It lifts you to a ridge near Gwongeumseong Fortress, an old Silla-era earthwork, with views over the granite cliffs and, on clear days, the East Sea.

The full trail to Daecheongbong Peak (1,708m) is a different proposition: 7-8 hours round trip from Sogongwon, steep in the final push, and only sensible if you start early and check the forecast. Ulsanbawi Rock is the middle option, a rewarded but demanding stone-staircase climb of about 3-4 hours round trip with a rock face view at the top. Biseondae is the easy choice: a flat, paved 30-40 minute walk to a waterfall and cliff formations, doable in sandals.

## When to actually go

Autumn color usually peaks mid-to-late October, which is also when the park is at its most crowded and buses from Seoul run extra services. Spring brings azaleas on the lower slopes; summer is green but humid, with afternoon thunderstorms common in the mountains. Winter closes some upper trails after snowfall, so check conditions before attempting Daecheongbong outside the warmer months.

The park is well-known nationally, not a quiet secret, but you can still dodge the worst of it. Arriving right at the 9am opening usually beats both the tour buses from Seoul and the domestic hiking groups that build up by mid-morning.

## Getting oriented on arrival

Sinheungsa Temple sits a short walk inside the main Sokcho-side entrance, with a large bronze Buddha statue that most people photograph before heading further in. This is also where trail signage splits toward Biseondae, Ulsanbawi and the cable car station, so it's a natural first stop for bearings.

- Buy cable car tickets at the base station; they can sell out on autumn weekends, so arrive early or check same-day availability before committing to a plan.
- Wear proper hiking shoes for Ulsanbawi or Daecheongbong; the stone steps get slick after rain.
- Bring cash in small bills for trailside snack stalls near Biseondae, which don't reliably take cards.
- Pack a headlamp if attempting Daecheongbong, since a slow group can easily run past sunset on the descent.

Entry fees are modest and typically paid at the gate; confirm the current rate locally since park fees change periodically. Most day-trippers from Sokcho spend 3-4 hours here for the cable car and Biseondae combination, or a full day for anything involving Ulsanbawi or the summit.
