## Running past a 1,700-year-old column

The start area has an ancient landmark. At Çemberlitaş Square, a stack of scorched porphyry drums rises above the tram wires. Constantine the Great raised it in 330 AD, when the city became his new capital. The iron bands holding it together give the square its Turkish name: *çemberli taş*, "the hooped stone".

DUST Night Run uses this square and the streets around the Grand Bazaar as its stage. The race is a night road run on the historic peninsula. That means asphalt and cobbles between some of the oldest commercial streets in Europe. It is a city race after dark, not a trail run.

## A Sunday when the bazaar sleeps

December 20, 2026 falls on a Sunday. That changes how the area feels. The Grand Bazaar, or *Kapalıçarşı*, traditionally closes on Sundays, so its gates and shutters are usually down.

On a weekday evening the streets around the bazaar fill with porters, shop staff and shoppers heading for the tram. On a Sunday night you see the stone gateways, the domed roofline and the slope down towards the Golden Horn far more clearly. You also get the space a road race needs.

The bazaar itself dates from the 1450s and 1460s, under Mehmed II. It grew into a covered maze of roughly 60 streets and thousands of shops. Its gates connect to the streets this race runs through:

- **Nuruosmaniye Gate**, beside the baroque Nuruosmaniye Mosque, a short walk from Çemberlitaş
- **Beyazıt Gate**, which opens towards Beyazıt Square and the monumental gate of Istanbul University
- **Divanyolu Caddesi**, the old imperial processional road, which runs through Çemberlitaş towards Sultanahmet

## December after dark on the historic peninsula

Istanbul in late December is cool and damp, and rain is common. The sun sets before 5pm, so a night race here runs entirely under streetlights and shop lamps. Wet stone takes on an orange sheen under the lights.

Runners at winter night races usually dress in layers they can shed at the start. They bring a dry top for afterwards and wear shoes with some grip, because polished cobbles and tram rails get slippery when wet. Divanyolu is a slight ridge road, and the side streets drop steeply away from it. Expect short climbs rather than a flat course.

## Tram, çay and salep: race-night habits

Most people arrive by tram, which is the simplest way in. The **T1 line** (Kabataş–Bağcılar) runs straight along Divanyolu. **Çemberlitaş** stop sits on the square itself, and **Beyazıt-Kapalıçarşı** is one stop west.

Fares are paid with an **Istanbulkart**, the city's rechargeable transit card. You can top one up at the yellow machines at tram stops. Many visitors share one card within a group, since a single card can pay for several riders.

A few local habits for the evening:

- **Ask for çay.** Tea comes in a small tulip-shaped glass with sugar cubes on the saucer. Small cafés around Çemberlitaş serve it all evening.
- **Try salep in winter.** This hot, thick milk drink made from orchid-root flour, dusted with cinnamon, is the classic cold-weather drink in this part of the city.
- **Round up the bill.** In simple cafés and *lokantas* people usually leave the change or round up rather than add a percentage.
- **Use Turkish street names.** *Kapalıçarşı* for the bazaar and *Çemberlitaş* for the square will get you further with taxi drivers than the English names.

## Where to eat and warm up afterwards

Divanyolu and the lanes off it are lined with kebab shops, *lokantas* serving ready-cooked stews from steam trays, and *büfe* counters for a quick toasted sandwich. These places run on a steady stream of trade, and many stay open into the evening.

For something specific to winter, Vefa Bozacısı is about a 20-minute walk north-west in the Vefa neighbourhood. It has served *boza*, a thick, tangy fermented millet drink, since 1876. It is traditionally drunk in cold months and topped with roasted chickpeas and cinnamon.

## Nearby, if you are staying the weekend

The race area is close to the main sights of the historic peninsula, so many runners plan sightseeing around the date. Within walking distance of Çemberlitaş:

- **Çemberlitaş Hamamı**, a 16th-century bathhouse designed by Mimar Sinan, right on the square
- **Hagia Sophia**, **the Blue Mosque** and **the Hippodrome**, about ten minutes east along Divanyolu
- **Süleymaniye Mosque**, up the hill beyond Beyazıt, with views over the Golden Horn
- **The Spice Bazaar** (Mısır Çarşısı) at Eminönü, downhill through the market lanes from the Grand Bazaar

Visitors who come for the race usually stay in Sultanahmet or around Beyazıt and Laleli. That puts the start within walking distance or one or two tram stops away, which helps on a cold night when you want to get back to a hot shower.
