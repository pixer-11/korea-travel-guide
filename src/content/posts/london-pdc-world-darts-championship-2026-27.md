## A bigger room on the same hill

The darts has a new home inside its old one. Alexandra Palace has staged the PDC World Championship since the 2007/08 edition, always in the smaller West Hall. For 2026/27 the tournament takes the Great Hall, the palace's largest space, for the first time.

It runs from 11 December 2026 to the final on 3 January 2027. That is more than three weeks of sessions across the Christmas and New Year stretch. The PDC publishes the dates, venue details and ticket links on its official site.

The building itself carries real weight. Alexandra Palace opened in 1873, burned down sixteen days later and was rebuilt by 1875. In 1936 the BBC began the world's first regular high-definition television service from its south-east tower. The mast still stands above the roofline.

## What the Great Hall changes

The Great Hall is a vast Victorian space under a high arched roof. At its far end stands the Willis organ, one of the largest pipe organs built in Victorian Britain. Setting a darts stage in that room changes the scale of the whole event.

More seats means more people inside each session than the West Hall ever held. The format inside stays recognisable: a raised stage, two boards side by side, walk-ons through the crowd and the giant screens showing every dart. The noise has a lot more roof to bounce off.

For anyone who has been before, the familiar routes through the palace still apply. The building's east and west wings, the terraces and the park outside are all where they were.

## Getting up Muswell Hill

Ally Pally sits on a ridge in the borough of Haringey, between Wood Green and Muswell Hill. The climb is steep, and every route ends with a short haul uphill.

The main options:

- **Alexandra Palace station**: Great Northern trains from Moorgate and King's Cross stop here, then it is a walk up through the park.
- **Wood Green (Piccadilly line)**: the closest Tube station. The W3 bus runs from here up to the palace.
- **Finsbury Park (Victoria and Piccadilly lines, plus rail)**: the W3 also starts here, which suits anyone coming from the Victoria line.
- **Taxi or rideshare**: common after evening sessions, though the queue on the hill builds once the hall empties.

The path from the station crosses open parkland with little shelter. In December, that walk is cold, wet and dark after evening sessions, so a proper coat earns its place.

## The fancy dress and the singing

Nowhere else in sport looks quite like a darts crowd. Large groups come in matching costumes: superheroes, Christmas elves, Santas, bananas, whole stag parties dressed as one cartoon. It is a long-running Ally Pally tradition, and the TV cameras seek it out.

Home-made signs are part of it too. Fans hold up cardboard messages aimed at the cameras, from player names to jokes and greetings home. A sign that gets on screen counts as a small win.

The music is the other ritual. Between legs and during breaks, the hall sings along to "Chase the Sun" by Planet Funk. Each player's walk-on song is loud and is often chanted for the rest of the match. Experienced fans learn the big walk-ons before they go.

## How a session at Ally Pally works

Tickets are sold per session rather than per day. A session usually covers several matches back to back, with afternoon and evening sessions on many days. Regulars treat each one as a full night out, not a quick visit.

Floor seating is mostly at long shared tables. Drinks get carried back from the bars in stacked cups, and strangers end up singing next to each other. Tiered seating further back gives a cleaner view of the boards but less of the party.

A few habits that regulars follow:

- **Stay seated while a player is throwing.** Stewards hold people at the aisles until the end of a leg, and moving mid-throw gets loud disapproval.
- **Move during breaks.** The gap between sets is when the bar and toilet queues move fastest.
- **Pay by card.** The bars and food stalls inside the venue run largely on card and contactless.
- **Give the big screens your attention.** From most seats the board is tiny, and the screens are how the crowd follows every treble.

The PDC's site sets out the session list and the venue's entry and bag rules.

## Christmas at the darts

Timing is what sets this tournament apart. The early rounds run through December, when offices across Britain hold their Christmas parties. Many groups book an Ally Pally session as theirs, which explains all the Santa hats.

In earlier editions, play stopped for a short break around Christmas Day and then came back for the latter rounds. The closing stretch moves into the New Year, with the 2026/27 final on 3 January 2027.

The crowd changes as the tournament goes on. Early sessions feel like a big party with darts on in the background. By the final rounds the hall is following every dart closely, and the cheers for a nine-darter or a 170 checkout are louder for it.

## Around the palace

Arriving before the doors open is a good excuse to look around the hill. The south terrace in front of the palace has one of the widest free views over London. The towers of the City and Canary Wharf line the horizon.

Alexandra Park spreads down the slope below, with a boating lake on the east side. Muswell Hill's Broadway, a short walk or bus ride west, has pubs and restaurants in Edwardian parades. Many fans eat there before a session. Wood Green, at the bottom of the hill, has more chain restaurants and the Tube.

Visitors from outside London usually stay in central London and travel up by train or Tube. That trip is easy from King's Cross and the Piccadilly line corridor.
