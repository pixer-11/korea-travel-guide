## Why 8ish stands out

Nariman Point isn't Mumbai's usual nightlife postcode — it's the city's old financial spine, all glass towers and government offices that empty out by evening. 8ish leans into that contrast, opening as a stylish drinking den in a part of town better known for boardrooms than bar crawls. It's the latest project from Rachel Goenka, the restaurateur behind some of Mumbai's better-known dining concepts, and it's designed to feel like a considered, grown-up hangout rather than another rooftop lounge chasing a view.

The interior does a lot of the talking: warm amber lighting, aged wood panelling, and a maroon marble bar that anchors the room. The palette is deliberately moody and tactile, built for evenings that start with a drink after work and drift into something later and looser. The cocktail program is billed as "liquid gastronomy," suggesting drinks built with the same technique and layering you'd expect from a tasting menu rather than a standard cocktail list — think unusual infusions, house-made syrups, or savoury elements folded into classic formats.

## Where it is and how to get there

8ish sits in Nariman Point, at the southern tip of Mumbai's business district, close to Marine Drive and the Arabian Sea promenade. If you're coming by train, the nearest suburban rail station is Churchgate on the Western Line, from which it's a walkable stretch south along or near Marine Drive — pleasant in the evening once the sea breeze picks up. Taxis and app-based cabs are the easier option after dark, since parking in Nariman Point can be tight, especially near office hours. Given the area's daytime identity as a business hub, expect quieter streets on weekends until the bar's own evening crowd builds.

## What to expect once you're there

This is a cocktail-forward venue rather than a full dining destination, positioned for the after-work crowd first and the late-night set second — hence the cheeky name. Expect a bar-centric layout where the maroon marble counter is clearly the centerpiece, with seating designed to encourage lingering over a couple of rounds rather than a quick one-and-done stop. Because the space is newly opened, exact hours, reservation policy, and menu pricing weren't confirmed at the time of writing — call ahead or check 8ish's official channels before you go, particularly if you're planning a group visit or want a specific table.

## Best time to visit

The "post-work and late-night" framing is the biggest clue here: arrive between roughly 7 and 9 pm for a calmer, conversation-friendly atmosphere, or come after 10 pm if you want the room at its liveliest. Weeknights are likely to draw the office crowd from the surrounding towers, while weekends may skew toward a dedicated night-out crowd arriving later. As with most new Mumbai openings generating buzz, the first few weeks and months tend to be busiest — going on a Tuesday or Wednesday rather than a Friday is a reasonable way to dodge the peak crush while the novelty is fresh.

## How to visit like a local

Mumbai's drinking culture runs on reservations for anything trending, so call or book online rather than walking in, especially on weekend nights. Payment is almost universally by card or UPI (India's mobile payment system) in bars of this caliber — carrying cash is a backup, not the plan. Tipping isn't legally mandatory but is customary at around 10% if a service charge isn't already added to the bill; check the check before doubling up. Dress smart-casual — Mumbai's newer cocktail bars skew toward collared shirts and dressier footwear rather than shorts and flip-flops, particularly in a business-district setting like Nariman Point. The mistake visitors often make at new, hyped bars is showing up right at opening on a Friday expecting a quiet drink — go earlier in the week, or arrive right when doors open on any night, to get a proper look at the room before it fills in. Since 8ish is still finding its rhythm as a new opening, treat any listed hours online as provisional and reconfirm the same day.

## Nearby to pair with your visit

Marine Drive's sea-facing promenade is a short walk away and worth a stroll before or after your drink, especially near sunset. The wider Nariman Point and Churchgate area also puts you within reach of Mumbai's colonial-era architecture, including landmarks around Churchgate station itself, making it easy to fold 8ish into a broader evening rather than a standalone stop.
