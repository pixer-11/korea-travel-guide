## A race weekend with the airport next door

Sepang sounds like a jet engine and a motorcycle at once. The Petronas Sepang International Circuit sits a few kilometres from Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA), so on a race weekend airliners climb overhead while MotoGP bikes scream down the straights below.

For 2026, the Petronas Grand Prix of Malaysia runs from **October 30 to November 1** and is round 20 of the championship. Coming this late in the season, it often matters for the title. The entry list features riders including Francesco Bagnaia, Marc Márquez and Fabio Quartararo.

The 2025 edition drew a record 190,977 spectators across the weekend. That makes it one of the largest crowds in Southeast Asian sport. Plan for that scale when you think about traffic, food queues and getting a seat.

## How the three days unfold

The standard MotoGP format spreads the action across the weekend. Each day builds on the last.

- **Friday:** practice sessions for MotoGP, Moto2 and Moto3. Crowds are thinnest, so it's the best day to walk the circuit.
- **Saturday:** qualifying, then the MotoGP Sprint, a shorter race worth half points.
- **Sunday:** the main Grand Prix, plus the Moto2 and Moto3 races.

Session times shift from year to year. Confirm the timetable on the official MotoGP or circuit site before you plan your day.

## Choosing where to sit

Sepang was designed by Hermann Tilke and opened in 1999. Its 5.5 km layout pairs two long straights with a tight hairpin, and the main grandstand sits between them. That means one seat looks onto both the start-finish straight and the back straight. Its roof of curved panels, shaped like leaves, gives real shade, and you'll value that by midday.

The other option is the hillstands. These are open grassy banks around corners such as Turn 1, where riders brake hard into the first bend. They're livelier and more casual, but you're exposed to sun and rain.

Grandstands and hillstands come in different categories with different views. Compare them on the official ticketing page, which lists current prices and what each ticket covers. Covered seats tend to sell out first for Sunday.

## Getting to Sepang from Kuala Lumpur

The circuit is around an hour's drive south of central Kuala Lumpur when the roads are clear. On race day, the last stretch into the circuit is slow. These are your realistic options:

- **KLIA Ekspres** from KL Sentral to KLIA, then a ride-hail or any official event shuttle the organisers run.
- **Grab**, Malaysia's main ride-hailing app, direct to the circuit. Agree a pickup point for the return before you split up.
- **Driving yourself**, using the official car parks. Check the circuit site for parking and shuttle arrangements.

Staying near the airport cuts the trip to minutes. Hotels around KLIA and the nearby town of Salak Tinggi fill up for this weekend, so book early.

## Surviving a Sepang race day

Late October in Selangor is hot and humid, and heavy afternoon thunderstorms are common. Pack for both. Rain can come in fast, soak the hillstands and then clear within the hour.

- A compact poncho, which is easier than an umbrella in a crowd
- Sunscreen and a hat for the open banks
- Earplugs, especially for MotoGP sessions
- A power bank, since you'll be using your phone for tickets and Grab

Malaysians pay by card and e-wallet more and more, with Touch 'n Go widely used. Still, keep some ringgit for smaller food stalls. Water and drinks sell fast in the heat. Refill whenever you get the chance rather than waiting until you're thirsty.

Traffic is the thing that catches visitors out. On Sunday, set off well before the morning support races if you want to settle in calmly. After the Grand Prix, many local fans stay for the podium and fan-zone activities while the car parks empty, instead of joining the immediate exodus.

## Making a longer trip of it

You can do the weekend from a Kuala Lumpur base. The city has Petronas Twin Towers, the hawker stalls of Jalan Alor and easy rail links. Putrajaya, the planned administrative capital with its lakeside pink-domed Putra Mosque, sits between KL and Sepang and works as a half-day stop.

If you're flying in, treat the circuit's closeness to KLIA as a gift. Some fans land on Friday, go straight to practice and fly out on Sunday night. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site before you lock in any flights.
