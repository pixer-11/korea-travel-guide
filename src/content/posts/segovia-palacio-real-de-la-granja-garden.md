## What you're walking into

Felipe V built this palace and garden in the 1720s as a Bourbon answer to Versailles, tucked against the Sierra de Guadarrama rather than flat parkland. That slope is the whole point: it lets water drop downhill through 26 fountains and pools by gravity alone, no pumps.

The layout is French formal garden grafted onto Spanish terrain: clipped parterres near the palace facade, then long tree-lined avenues (the "calles") climbing toward wilder woodland. Statues of mythological figures, mostly lead cast in Segovia itself, line the routes between fountains like La Fama, Los Baños de Diana and the Cascada Nueva.

## Getting there and getting in

- Address: Pl. de España, S/N, 40100 Real Sitio de San Ildefonso, Segovia
- From Segovia city, it's roughly a 20-minute drive or a local bus along the CL-601
- Open daily, 10:00 AM to 8:00 PM, year-round
- The garden and palace have separate tickets; the garden alone is the cheaper option and worth doing even if you skip the palace interior

Confirm current prices and any seasonal hour changes on the official site before you go, since royal sites adjust these periodically.

## Timing it around the fountains

The fountains are the main draw and they don't run constantly. Historically they're switched on for shows on specific dates, often royal-related holidays and select summer weekends, with each fountain performing for a set number of minutes before falling silent again.

Check the current fountain calendar in advance; missing it means a beautiful but static garden. On non-show days, come instead for the walk itself: the Andadores paths under the trees stay shaded and cool even in Segovia's hot July afternoons.

This is a well-known stop on the Segovia day-trip circuit, not a quiet find, so weekend show days draw real crowds around the main basins. Arriving at opening, 10am, gives you a window before tour groups from Madrid and Segovia city arrive.

## Making the most of a short visit

Pace yourself: the palace-to-woods gradient means the walk back uphill is the tiring part, not the descent.

- Start at the palace facade and work down through the formal parterres first
- Save the Cascada Nueva and the upper woodland avenues for last, when crowds thin
- Bring water and sun protection; much of the upper garden has little shade
- Wear shoes that handle gravel paths and inclines, not just flat cobblestone

Pair the visit with the town of San Ildefonso itself, which sits right at the garden gates, or continue on to Segovia city for the aqueduct and cathedral the same day. Two to three hours covers the garden properly; add another hour if you're touring the palace rooms too.
