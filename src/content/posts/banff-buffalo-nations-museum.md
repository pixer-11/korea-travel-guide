## A stockade by the river

Walk south down Banff Avenue and cross the Bow River Bridge. On your right, set back from Birch Avenue, is a building with a log palisade, the kind of frontier fort a film would use. That is the Buffalo Nations Museum, still often called by its older name, the Luxton Museum.

Norman Luxton built it in the early 1950s. He was a Banff trader and promoter with close ties to the Stoney Nakoda, and he was tied up with Banff Indian Days, the summer gathering that brought First Nations families into town for decades. The collection grew out of those relationships.

## Inside the log walls

The rooms are small and dim, and full of cases. Expect beadwork, quillwork, clothing, headdresses, weapons and everyday tools of the Plains and northern Rockies peoples. Some of the scenes are laid out as old-style dioramas.

It reads as a mid-century museum rather than a modern interactive one. That's part of what you're seeing: how Banff presented Indigenous life to visitors two or three generations ago. Read the labels with that in mind.

Plan for:
- **45–60 minutes** for most visitors
- **Longer** if you read every case closely
- **Pairing it** with the Banff Park Museum, a log natural-history museum just back across the bridge, and the Cascade Gardens, a short walk up the road behind the museum

Nearby, too: the Whyte Museum of the Canadian Rockies on Bear Street covers the town's settler and mountaineering side.

## Timing your visit around the weekend rush

Currently the museum opens 11am–5pm every day of the week. Museum hours in Banff change with the season, so check the official site for this month's schedule before you walk over. Don't plan to go before 11am.

Weekends are busy for the full 11am–5pm window, so you won't find a quiet hour on a Saturday or Sunday. A weekday is your better chance at a calm visit. It still gets far less attention than Banff Avenue's shops, and that makes it a good choice on a rainy afternoon when the gondola is clouded in.

## Getting in and moving through

- **Admission:** the current price is posted at the entrance and on the museum's website. Check there rather than relying on old figures.
- **Payment:** cards are standard in Banff, but carry a backup in case the till is having a bad day.
- **Photography:** some objects are ceremonial. Follow the posted signs and ask staff if you aren't sure before you raise a camera.
- **Pace:** the rooms are narrow. If a group has stopped at a case, move on and come back to it.
- **Getting there:** it's about a five-minute walk from the bridge end of Banff Avenue, and most downtown hotels are within walking distance. If you're driving, use the town's public lots, since street spaces near the bridge fill fast in summer.

Afterwards, follow the Bow River trail from the bridge toward Bow Falls. It turns an hour indoors into a full half-day on the south side of the river.
