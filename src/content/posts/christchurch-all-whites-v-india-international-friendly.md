## The fixture at a glance

It's New Zealand against India, under a roof. On 15 November 2026, the All Whites host India's national team, the Blue Tigers, in an international friendly at One New Zealand Stadium in central Christchurch.

The timing gives it some weight. New Zealand play this match a few months after their FIFA World Cup 2026 campaign. It is one of the first chances home supporters get to watch the post-World Cup squad up close, and it happens in a city that waited more than a decade for a proper central stadium.

- **Date:** Sunday 15 November 2026
- **Venue:** One New Zealand Stadium (Te Kaha), Christchurch Central City
- **Format:** International friendly, so no qualification points are at stake
- **Teams:** New Zealand (All Whites) v India (Blue Tigers)

## Why Christchurch, and why now

This city has spent years without a central stadium. The old Lancaster Park was badly damaged in the February 2011 earthquake and never reopened. After that, big matches went to a temporary ground in Addington, west of the centre, which was always meant to be a stopgap.

Te Kaha is the replacement. It was built on cleared land in the rebuilt east of the central city and opened in 2026. A national-team football fixture here marks a new stage in the rebuild that has reshaped the streets around it. For local fans, a World Cup squad playing walking distance from Cathedral Square is new.

## Te Kaha: a stadium with a sky-lit roof

The roof is the first thing to know about. Te Kaha has a fully covered, translucent roof over the seating and pitch, which makes it New Zealand's largest roofed stadium. Daylight comes through, and Canterbury's weather stays out.

That matters in mid-November. Late spring in Christchurch can bring a warm, gusty nor'wester one day and a cold southerly change the next. A wind-blown match is far less likely here than at an open ground. A layer for the walk to the stadium and back is still sensible, but you won't need rain gear in your seat.

The bowl seats about 30,000. Because the stands rise steeply on all four sides, sightlines stay close to the touchline, and supporter noise bounces back off the roof. Te Kaha is also the stadium's Māori name. You'll hear both names used around town, and both appear on signage.

## Getting to the central city

The stadium is in the compact, flat central city, so most visitors staying in town walk. The streets between Cathedral Square, the Avon River and the stadium are mostly short city blocks. Cycle lanes run through the grid as well.

- **From the airport:** Metro's Purple line and route 29 connect Christchurch Airport with the central city, and a taxi or rideshare takes around 20–25 minutes.
- **By bus:** The Bus Interchange on Lichfield Street is the main hub, a short walk from the stadium precinct.
- **By car:** Event-day parking near a central stadium is limited, and many fans park further out and walk in.
- **On foot:** From Riverside Market, Te Pae convention centre or New Regent Street, the walk is easy and flat.

Visitors usually stay in the central city hotels around Victoria Street, Cathedral Square and the riverbank. That keeps the stadium, food and the bus interchange all within walking distance.

## Match-day manners at Te Kaha

New Zealand football crowds are relaxed, family-heavy and loud at the right moments. Here is what fans usually do and expect on the day.

- **Anthems first.** New Zealand's anthem is sung in te reo Māori, then English. Most of the crowd stands and many sing both verses. India's anthem, Jana Gana Mana, is played too. Standing quietly through it is the norm.
- **Two sets of colours.** New Zealand has a large Indian community, so expect plenty of blue shirts and tricolour flags among the white. Mixed groups and good-natured banter are typical.
- **Paying.** New Zealand is a card-and-contactless country. EFTPOS, tap-to-pay cards and phone wallets are standard at food stands, and many big venues are moving away from cash.
- **No tipping.** Tipping isn't expected anywhere in New Zealand, including at stadium bars and food outlets.
- **Getting in.** Fans typically arrive well before kickoff to clear security, find their stand and watch the warm-ups. Bag rules at large New Zealand venues usually limit size, and the stadium's own site lists its conditions of entry.

## Before kickoff and after the final whistle

The stadium sits in the part of the city that changed most after 2011, so pre-match and post-match plans are short walks. Most of the rebuilt centre is within about 15 minutes on foot.

- **Riverside Market:** An indoor food hall and produce market by the Avon on Oxford Terrace, popular for a pre-match meal.
- **New Regent Street:** A short row of pastel Spanish Mission-style buildings with small eateries. The heritage tram runs straight down the middle.
- **The Avon River (Ōtākaro):** The riverside promenade runs through the city. Punting on the Avon is a classic Christchurch outing.
- **Cathedral Square:** The city's historic centre, where restoration of ChristChurch Cathedral is ongoing.
- **Margaret Mahy Family Playground:** A large riverside playground, handy for families with younger fans.
- **Hagley Park and the Botanic Gardens:** On the western edge of the centre. On a clear November morning before an evening match, the gardens are a good place to spend a few hours.

After the match, crowds spread through the central city. The streets towards Victoria Street, the Terraces along Oxford Terrace and the bars around the river are where most supporters go for a drink and a post-mortem on the result.
