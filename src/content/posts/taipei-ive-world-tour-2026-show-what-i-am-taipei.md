## Why this stop mattered

IVE's SHOW WHAT I AM World Tour was built around the group's run of chart-topping singles, and Taipei's inclusion as a multi-night stop signaled how deep their fanbase runs in Taiwan. "After Like" and "Love Dive", two of the most inescapable K-pop tracks of the past few years, were expected to anchor the setlist alongside material from their newer EP. A three-night booking at an arena this size is not a small commitment for a touring act, and it put Taipei in the same tier of stops as the tour's other major Asian cities.

## Getting to Taipei Arena

Taipei Arena sits directly above Taipei Arena Station on the Wenhu Line (Brown Line); you can walk straight from the platform into the venue's lower concourse without surfacing on the street, which matters a lot when tens of thousands of people are trying to leave a show at once. The surrounding Songshan District blends office towers, MRT-linked shopping malls, and a dense grid of night-market-adjacent streets, so there is plenty nearby to fill an hour or three. A walk toward the nearby Raohe Street Night Market is an easy way to eat before a show.

## What the venue experience feels like

Inside, Taipei Arena's bowl seating wraps close enough to the stage that even mid-tier sections get a real view of the screens and lighting rigs, not just a speck on a distant platform. It's a large enough venue that a sold-out multi-night run often turns the plaza outside into its own kind of pre-show event, with vendors, fan-run pop-ups, and photo backdrops clustering near the main entrance in the hours before doors.

Security screening was set up as at any major arena show: bag checks, no large flags or professional cameras, and a scan of the ticket QR code at entry.

## Arrival patterns across the three nights

With three consecutive nights, crowd patterns usually shift a little. The first night typically draws the most intense arrival crush, as fans want to be present for the opening show, while the middle and closing nights can feel marginally calmer in the earlier hours, picking up again as showtime nears.

## How to visit like a local

Taiwanese concertgoers overwhelmingly buy tickets through official local ticketing platforms rather than international resellers, and tickets for a run like this typically go on sale in tiered phases: a fan-club presale followed by a general public sale.

Payment at the arena and surrounding vendors is almost entirely by contactless card or Taiwan's EasyCard/iPASS transit cards, though small night-market stalls nearby still favor cash. Lightsticks (the official IVE "Moment" bong) are common but not mandatory; plenty of attendees just bring phone lights. International fans often underestimate how quickly the Wenhu Line platform floods after a show ends, and regulars tend to linger 15–20 minutes on the concourse before heading down to the MRT to miss the worst of the crush.

Queuing at Taipei Arena is orderly and lane-marked well before doors, and joining the line without merchandise or floor tickets is entirely normal.
