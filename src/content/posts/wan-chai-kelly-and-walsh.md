## A bookshop above Queensway

Admiralty runs on glass, escalators and footbridges. Office workers, hotel guests and shoppers move between towers without ever touching the street. Kelly and Walsh sits inside that system at Shop 216, 88 Queensway, the Pacific Place address.

What you find there is an independent bookstore. It stocks specialist titles alongside English-language books and current bestsellers. In a mall built around fashion labels and hotel lobbies, a shop where people stand still and read the backs of books feels like a different pace altogether.

## Why it rewards the detour

It doesn't get much attention. Most visitors to Pacific Place come for the shops and restaurants, so this place stays fairly low-profile. That makes it a calm find in one of the island's busiest transit districts.

The name has a long history in Asia. Kelly & Walsh started as a bookseller and publisher in nineteenth-century Shanghai, and it has been tied to English-language books in the region for generations. That history isn't something you have to go looking for. The draw is simpler: a curated English-language selection you can browse in a few quiet minutes between other plans.

Come here for:

- **Specialist titles** you are unlikely to see in airport newsagents
- **English-language books** if you have run out of reading partway through a trip
- **Current bestsellers** for a flight home
- **A gift** that weighs less than a box of egg rolls and lasts longer

## Finding Shop 216 inside Pacific Place

Getting there is easy. Finding the door takes a little more care. Take the MTR to **Admiralty Station**, where the Island Line, Tsuen Wan Line and South Island Line meet. Follow the signs for Pacific Place; **Exit F** is the usual route into the mall through covered walkways.

When you're inside, use the mall directories near the escalators and look for Shop 216. Pacific Place has a lot of levels and corridors that look alike, so checking the directory saves you a lap. From Central, the walk along Queensway takes about fifteen minutes. From Wan Chai it's about the same, or two MTR stops on the Island Line.

## Timing your browse

The schedule is simple at the moment. Kelly and Walsh currently opens **11am–7:30pm, seven days a week**, which includes Sundays and Mondays. Hours can change on public holidays, so check before you make a special trip.

The crowd forecast gives one clear warning. On weekends the shop is typically busiest **from 11am to 7pm**, which is nearly its whole day. If you want the shelves to yourself, go on a weekday instead. It also pairs well with an early dinner: arrive mid-afternoon and you still have plenty of time before the 7:30pm close.

Plan for about 30–45 minutes. Allow longer if specialist sections are your weakness.

## Browsing manners in a Hong Kong mall shop

Small bookshops here are quieter than the mall around them, so keep phone calls for the corridor. A few habits help:

- **Ask at the counter** if you're after a specific title or subject. In a shop this size, staff can tell you quickly whether it's on the shelf or can be ordered.
- **Handle display copies gently.** In Hong Kong's humid climate, a creased jacket can make a book hard to sell.
- **Paying:** cards are standard in Pacific Place shops, but ask at the till if you plan to use another method, such as an Octopus card or a mobile wallet.
- **Tipping** doesn't apply in Hong Kong retail.
- **Bring your own bag.** Hong Kong charges a levy on plastic shopping bags, so most locals carry one.

## Pairing it with the rest of Admiralty

A bookshop visit fits easily into a half day in the area. **Hong Kong Park** sits just uphill from Pacific Place. It has a walk-through aviary, a conservatory and **Flagstaff House Museum of Tea Ware**, a colonial-era building with a collection of Chinese teaware.

Keep walking up Garden Road and you reach the **Peak Tram** lower terminus, so you could buy a book here and read it on the way up. Back in the other direction, Queensway leads east into Wan Chai proper, with its older street markets and tram line.

If you would rather stay indoors, Pacific Place connects to the hotels above it. Buying a book and reading it over afternoon tea in one of the lobby lounges is one of the most civilised ways to spend an Admiralty afternoon.

## Before you go

Don't expect a grand flagship. This is a mall unit with a focused stock, and that suits it: the selection is chosen, not just piled up. If you have a particular book in mind, it's better to ask than to scan every shelf. Under-the-radar shops like this one depend on that kind of conversation, and staff usually know their range well.
