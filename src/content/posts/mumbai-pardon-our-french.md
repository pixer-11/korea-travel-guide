The name is a pun. The size is not: twenty-two seats, on a tree-shaded lane in Ballard Estate, one of the most orderly corners of south Mumbai. This is pastry chef Pooja Dhingra's café. She runs it with an all-women team, and it plays as a warmer, more grown-up version of the brand she built over the past decade.

## A small room on a stone-built estate

Ballard Estate rewards walking slowly. The streets run in a straight grid. The buildings are pale stone and stucco, with columns and cornices, put up for the Bombay Port Trust in the early twentieth century on reclaimed land. A lot of them were designed by George Wittet, the architect of the Gateway of India.

Most of those buildings are offices, shipping companies and banks. That makes a 22-seat café stand out. Pardon Our French is built for lingering: the space is intimate and romantic, which suits a long coffee or a quiet dinner for two better than a quick takeaway stop.

## The pastry chef behind the pun

You may know Pooja Dhingra's name before you arrive. She trained in pastry in Paris and came home to found Le15 Patisserie. Le15 made French macarons a familiar sight in Mumbai, and she has written cookbooks and become one of the country's best-known pastry chefs.

The café is her own description of where that work has got to: Le15 grown up. Don't go expecting a copy of a Le15 counter. The menu here is its own thing and it is still new, so look at what's offered on the day before you settle on an order.

The French pun in the name is a nod to the Paris training behind everything she makes.

## Twenty-two chairs, and that's the whole room

The seat count shapes your visit more than anything else. With so few tables, one birthday group or a couple of long lunches can fill the room. It's a recently opened, much-discussed place, so expect demand to run ahead of the space for a while.

- **Reserve if you can.** Check the café's official social media or booking channel for how reservations work right now.
- **Confirm hours on the day.** New places often change their schedules in the first months, and we haven't been given fixed hours.
- **Keep groups small.** Two to four people fit a room this size far better than a big party.

## Ballard Estate after the office crowd leaves

The neighbourhood has a rhythm, and the commercial district sets it. On weekdays the lanes fill with office workers, taxis and delivery bikes. Evenings and Sundays are a different place: once the offices empty out, the grid goes noticeably quiet.

That calm suits the café's mood. A late-afternoon or evening visit puts you in a peaceful lane rather than in the weekday lunch rush of Fort. Before you head over on a Sunday, check that the café is open. Some businesses on the estate keep office-district hours.

## Reaching Fort without the traffic headache

Ballard Estate sits on the eastern edge of Fort, close to the docks. Rail is the easiest way to get here.

- **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CSMT)** is the nearest big station, on the Central and Harbour lines. From there it's a short walk east, or a quick taxi ride.
- **Churchgate** on the Western line is a bit further. Most people take a taxi from there to Ballard Estate.
- **Taxis and app cabs** know Ballard Estate well. Give the driver the estate name and the café's name, then follow your map app for the last lane.

During the weekday evening rush, roads around CSMT jam up. If you have a reservation, leave extra time or come by train.

## Booking, paying and settling in

Mumbai's cafés run on a mix of habits, and knowing them saves you an awkward moment at the table.

- **Payment:** UPI is everywhere in Mumbai, and cards are widely accepted. Visitors without an Indian bank account should carry a card, with a little cash as backup.
- **Tipping:** Look at the bill before you tip. Many restaurants add a service charge. If yours doesn't, leaving around ten percent for good service is normal.
- **Pacing:** In a 22-seat room, tables matter. Linger over a reserved table, but if people are visibly waiting, don't stay for hours with an empty cup.
- **Prices:** We haven't been given prices. Check the menu at the café or on its official channels.

The team is all women, and that's part of the café's identity. Treat it as a neighbourhood place: greet the staff, ask what's freshest, and you'll usually get a straight recommendation.

## Building an afternoon around Ballard Estate

The café makes a good anchor for a walk through Fort. Some of the city's most-photographed colonial architecture is within walking distance.

- **Horniman Circle and the Asiatic Society Town Hall:** a circular garden ringed by arcaded buildings, plus the Town Hall's wide white steps, a few minutes west.
- **Kala Ghoda:** the art district, with the Jehangir Art Gallery, galleries and design shops. It's busiest in February during the Kala Ghoda Arts Festival.
- **Gateway of India:** a longer walk south along the waterfront side of Colaba.
- **Britannia & Co.:** the long-running Parsi restaurant, also in Ballard Estate, famous for its berry pulao. Check its hours separately.
- **CSMT:** the Gothic railway terminus is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and worth a look even if you don't arrive by train.

Weather-wise, November to February is the most comfortable time for walking in Mumbai. From June to September the monsoon brings heavy downpours. Then a small, warm room on a sheltered lane is exactly where you want to be.

Plan on an hour or so at the café, longer if it's dinner. Add the walk and you have a half day in Fort.
