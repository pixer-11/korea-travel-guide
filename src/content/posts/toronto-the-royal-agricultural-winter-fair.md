## Ten days of barns indoors

The fair has run in Toronto since 1922. It calls itself the world's largest combined indoor agricultural fair and equestrian competition. For ten days in November, from November 6 to 15, 2026, the exhibition halls of the Enercare Centre at Exhibition Place work like a farm.

Expect rows of pens bedded with straw and wood shavings. You'll find dairy and beef cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, rabbits and poultry, with breeders grooming animals in the aisles. In the judging rings, exhibitors lead animals past judges while spectators lean on the rails.

Between the barns are the competitions the fair is known for:

- **Giant vegetables**, including record-chasing pumpkins weighed and shown on the floor
- **Butter sculpture**, a long-running Royal tradition
- **Field crops, honey, maple syrup and cheese** entered by Ontario producers
- **Food halls and vendor aisles** selling farm products and fair snacks

## The Royal Horse Show in the Coliseum

The equestrian half of the fair takes place next door in the Coca-Cola Coliseum. This is the indoor arena where the Toronto Marlies play hockey for the rest of the season. During the Royal, the ice gives way to footing for the horses.

The programme mixes international show jumping with heavy horse hitch classes, where teams of Clydesdales and Percherons pull show wagons around the ring. In past editions, evening Horse Show sessions have used reserved seating, sold separately from general fair admission. Daytime ring events have typically been open to fairgoers.

## Getting to Exhibition Place

Exhibition Place is on Toronto's waterfront, west of downtown. Most visitors come by rail or streetcar:

- **GO Transit:** Exhibition GO station on the Lakeshore West line is a short walk from the Enercare Centre.
- **TTC 509 Harbourfront streetcar:** runs from Union Station to Exhibition Loop.
- **TTC 511 Bathurst streetcar:** runs south from Bathurst station (Line 2) to Exhibition Loop.
- **Driving:** there are paid lots on the grounds. Parking rates are posted at the lot entrances.

Hotel X Toronto sits on the Exhibition Place grounds. Liberty Village, just north across the rail corridor, has cafés and restaurants for a meal before or after. November on the lakefront is cold and often windy, so most people come in winter coats and leave layers inside once they're in the warm halls.

## Walking the barns without spooking anything

These animals are working show stock, and exhibitors are preparing them for judging. Regulars ask before touching any animal. They keep children's hands out of pens unless invited, and never walk directly behind a horse. Exhibitors are usually happy to talk breeds and farming when they aren't getting ready for the ring.

Barn floors carry straw, water and the occasional manure trail. Fairgoers usually wear closed shoes they don't mind getting scuffed, and strollers handle the wide hall aisles well.

Families typically spend half a day on the barns and competitions. Adding an evening Horse Show session makes it a full day. Weekday mornings in November bring school groups, so many adults pick afternoons for a calmer walk through the livestock.

Cards and tap payments are standard at most vendor stalls, as they are across Toronto. Tipping applies to sit-down service, not to stalls. Ticket types and prices are listed on the fair's official website.
