Rahul Akerkar built his name on Indigo, one of Mumbai's defining restaurants of the 2000s, and Flint feels like a chef returning to the room with a different obsession. This time it's fire. The kitchen is organized around char, smoke and live flame rather than around a single cuisine, so a menu built for grazing through the day makes more sense than one built around a three-course dinner.

## Where it sits inside NCPA

Flint occupies a spot inside the NCPA complex at Nariman Point, the same campus that houses the Tata Theatre and the Experimental Theatre. That location matters more than it might elsewhere in the city.

You can treat it as a standalone destination or fold it into a night at a play or a concert. Nariman Point itself is Mumbai's old financial district, a grid of sea-facing office towers that empties out by evening, so the café-bar energy here comes mostly from the arts crowd rather than passing foot traffic.

## What actually lands on the table

The dish everyone mentions first is the crab-cake Eggs Benedict, a breakfast classic rebuilt around a Mumbai seafood habit rather than the usual ham. It signals what the kitchen is doing across the board: familiar formats, rerouted through smoke and open flame.

Beyond that plate, expect the char to show up in less obvious places too:

- Live-fire technique applied to items you wouldn't expect to see grilled or smoked
- A crab-cake Eggs Benedict as the anchor brunch dish
- A cocktail list built to match the smoke, not just sit beside it

The bar leans into the same idea. The Picante brings heat and citrus together in a way that cuts through richer plates, while the Pandan Colada takes a Southeast Asian pantry staple and runs it through a tropical, coconut-forward format. Both read as house inventions rather than riffs on standard cocktail-menu names.

## Reading the room

This is pitched as an all-day café-bar, which is a different animal from Akerkar's earlier fine-dining work. That structure usually means the kitchen and bar are built to carry you from a late breakfast through a pre-theatre drink into a proper dinner service, without a hard reset in between.

Because it's inside a performing arts complex, expect the room to fill in waves tied to NCPA's own schedule rather than to a typical restaurant's dinner rush. A show letting out can send a burst of people through the door at once.

## Timing your visit around NCPA's schedule

Since Flint sits inside a venue with its own performance calendar, the smartest move is to check what's showing at the Tata Theatre or Experimental Theatre before you book. Arriving an hour or so before curtain gives you time for a cocktail and a starter without rushing the char-driven dishes, which aren't built for a five-minute turnaround.

If you're going purely for the food, a weekend brunch slot lets you try the Eggs Benedict when the kitchen isn't also juggling a pre-show crowd. As with any newly opened restaurant, hours and reservation policy can shift in the first months, so confirm both directly with Flint or NCPA before you plan the evening around it.

## Getting in and around

Nariman Point isn't on Mumbai's metro network, so a cab or auto-rickshaw to the NCPA complex is the practical option. Parking within the complex can tighten up on performance nights, which is another reason to build in a buffer before showtime.

Once you're inside NCPA, Flint should be sign-posted alongside the theatre entrances; ask at the main gate if you're unsure which building it's in, since the complex has multiple performance and dining spaces under one address.

## A few practical notes before you book

Given how new the concept is, treat any menu prices or exact opening hours you find online as provisional. Call ahead or check Flint's own listing close to your visit date rather than relying on a fixed schedule.

If you're coming specifically for the fire-driven dishes rather than the bar program, ask when you book whether the kitchen has a preferred window for those plates; live-flame cooking often runs on its own rhythm within service.
