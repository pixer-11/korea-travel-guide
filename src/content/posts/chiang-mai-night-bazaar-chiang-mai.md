Night Bazaar runs the length of Changklan Road on the east side of the old city moat, a corridor of covered stalls, side arcades and multi-storey market buildings that only wakes up as the sun goes down. By day it's mostly shuttered metal shutters and parked motorbikes; by 5pm the tables are out, the string lights are on, and the whole strip turns into one continuous aisle of vendors.

This isn't a single market but several stacked together: street-level stalls facing the road, the Kalare Night Bazaar food and craft complex set back from the street, and a scatter of smaller arcades with tailors, jewelry counters and electronics stalls that have operated here for decades. Chiang Mai's night bazaar predates the city's other night markets and still anchors the tourist end of Changklan Road.

## What's actually for sale

Expect a mix, not a theme. The bazaar leans souvenir and craft rather than pure street food, though there's plenty of the latter tucked into food courts and side aisles.

- Hill-tribe textiles, silver jewelry, and hand-carved wood pieces
- Lanna-style lanterns, umbrellas, and northern Thai handicrafts
- Counterfeit and licensed clothing, bags, and electronics side by side
- Fresh fruit stalls, grilled skewers, khao soi, and mango sticky rice at the food courts
- Tailors offering same-week suits, a holdover from the bazaar's older trading days

Quality and pricing vary stall to stall, so it pays to walk a full lap before buying anything that isn't food.

## Finding your way around

The bazaar spans roughly a kilometre of Changklan Road, so getting oriented early saves backtracking. The Kalare complex, with its indoor food court and cultural shows, sits roughly midway and makes a useful landmark.

Side streets branching toward Loi Kroh Road hold their own stalls and are usually less crowded than the main drag. If you're overwhelmed, ducking one street over is often enough to reset.

## Bargaining without the awkwardness

Prices on crafts, clothing and souvenirs are starting points, not fixed. Vendors expect a counter-offer; food stalls generally don't haggle.

- Ask the price, then offer noticeably lower and meet in the middle
- Keep it friendly and unhurried; a smile moves things faster than a hard stance
- Cash is standard at most stalls, though larger shops increasingly take cards
- Walking away is a legitimate negotiating tool, not rudeness

Because the bazaar is very popular with visitors, prices at the stalls closest to the main entrances tend to run higher than those further down the strip or in the side arcades.

## Timing your visit

This is one of Chiang Mai's most-visited night markets, and Changklan Road gets genuinely congested in the main evening hours. The market is open nightly from 5pm to midnight, every day of the week, with no weekly closure to plan around.

Arriving right at 5pm, before the tour buses empty out, gives you a clearer run at the stalls. Coming back after 10pm thins the crowd again as families and day-trippers head out, leaving the food stalls and late shoppers.

Middle of the evening, especially on weekends, is when the aisles bottleneck. If crowds bother you, treat 7pm to 9pm as the window to avoid rather than aim for.

## Getting there and moving on

Changklan Road sits just outside the old city's southeast corner, an easy walk or short tuk-tuk ride from Tha Phae Gate. Songthaews (shared red trucks) and ride-hailing apps are the simplest way in if you're not staying nearby, since parking along Changklan Road is limited and traffic backs up once the market opens.

The Ping River and Nawarat Bridge are a few minutes' walk east, worth a stroll if you need a break from the crowd. Wat Chai Mongkol and the riverside restaurants near it make a reasonable stop before or after the bazaar.

Budget at least two hours to see the main strip and one of the indoor complexes properly; three if you plan to eat a full meal and browse the side arcades too.
