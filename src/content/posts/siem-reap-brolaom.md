Coffee in Siem Reap usually means a glass of iced Cambodian coffee thick with condensed milk, sipped fast between temple runs. Brolaom is doing something different. It opened in September 2023 as a specialty-coffee roaster built around 100% arabica beans, brewed with an eye toward flavor rather than sugar, and it's become a reference point for people who care about what's actually in the cup.

## What makes this cup different

Most coffee stands in town serve one style: strong, dark, heavily sweetened. Brolaom instead treats the bean like a variable worth exploring, using different extraction methods to pull out the character already sitting inside the arabica.

That means a filter or pour-over can come out tasting closer to peach or lychee than to the ashy espresso profile most visitors expect from Southeast Asian coffee stops. If you've never tasted coffee described that way, this is a reasonable place to find out what people mean.

## Finding it near Sister Srey

Brolaom sits close to Sister Srey, a well-known café and social enterprise that many visitors already have on their list, so the two make an easy pairing on foot. This puts it in the cluster of small food-and-drink spots that has grown up around that stretch, rather than in the main Pub Street tourist strip.

Because it's a newer addition to that pocket of the city, don't expect a landmark-sized building or a queue out the door. It reads as a small, focused operation: the kind of place you could walk past if you weren't looking for it.

## A roaster's approach, not a lounge

It's worth setting expectations correctly. Brolaom identifies itself as a specialty-coffee roaster, which means the emphasis is on sourcing and preparing arabica beans well, not on building a sprawling all-day café menu or a place to camp out for hours with a laptop.

Go in expecting the coffee itself to be the reason for the visit. If you want a long, shaded lounge session, pair a stop here with time at a nearby café afterward rather than expecting Brolaom to fill that role.

## Reading the flavor notes

Ask whoever is brewing what's on offer that day and what extraction method they'd recommend for it. Different methods (pour-over versus other filter techniques) pull different characteristics out of the same beans, so the same arabica can taste noticeably different cup to cup.

- If peach or stone-fruit notes are mentioned, a lighter filter method usually shows them best
- If lychee or floral notes come up, ask whether that's from a specific single-origin batch
- Don't assume it will taste like the syrupy iced coffee sold elsewhere in town; it's built to taste different, on purpose

## Timing your visit around the neighbourhood's rhythm

Sister Srey and the surrounding cluster of cafés tend to draw a morning breakfast crowd, since many visitors stop there before heading out to temples for the day. A coffee-focused stop like Brolaom is a natural add-on to that same outing, either right before or right after.

Arriving in that same early window usually means catching the coffee at its most attentive, since specialty brewing methods take a bit more time and care per cup than a quick pour of iced coffee. A slower, less rushed late-morning visit, once the earliest breakfast wave has moved on, is a reasonable middle ground if you'd rather not compete for the barista's attention.

## Because it's newly opened

Brolaom is still a relatively new business, having opened in September 2023, so details like exact opening hours, seating, and whether reservations are needed can shift as the operation settles in. Confirm current hours and whether you need to book ahead before making it the centerpiece of your plans, especially if you're working around a tight temple-visiting schedule.

This also means it hasn't accumulated years of fixed routine the way older Siem Reap institutions have. Part of the appeal right now is catching a roaster still actively refining its approach to sourcing and brewing arabica, rather than one that's settled into a static, unchanging menu.

## Making it part of a slower Siem Reap morning

Treat Brolaom as one stop in a walkable loop rather than a destination requiring a special trip. Combine it with Sister Srey and whatever else sits in that same small commercial pocket, and you get a morning that looks nothing like the standard Angkor Wat sunrise itinerary.

For visitors who've already done the temples and are looking for how locals and long-term expats actually spend a slow morning, a coffee stop built around flavor rather than volume is a useful corrective to Pub Street's louder, faster pace.
