## A ten-year band plays Daegu

This is a band show, not a backing track. DAY6 debuted under JYP Entertainment in September 2015, and the four members play their own instruments on stage: Sungjin on guitar, Young K on bass, Wonpil on keyboards and Dowoon on drums.

"The DECADE" takes its name from those ten years. The Daegu date is one stop on the band's 2026 tour, which puts a city known for summer heat and grilled intestines on the same itinerary as the capital's arenas.

For fans outside Seoul, a regional stop like this means a shorter trip and often a different crowd. Many fans travel down from the capital as well, which makes it part concert and part weekend away.

## What "The DECADE" points to

The title is about looking back. A ten-year mark is when a band's whole catalogue comes into play, and DAY6 has plenty of it. The songs fans sing word for word include:

- "Congratulations", the 2015 debut single
- "You Were Beautiful", the ballad that made their name
- "Time of Our Life" (한 페이지가 될 수 있게), the slow-burn chart hit
- "Welcome to the Show" and "Happy" from the post-military-service era

None of that is a setlist. This guide does not know what the band plays in Daegu, and nobody should pretend otherwise. It does explain why the crowd at a DAY6 show tends to know every bridge and every key change.

Korean concert tickets usually sell through a named online platform, often with a fan-club presale before general sale. Fans with a My Day membership, the official fan club, typically get first access.

## Getting to Daegu and around it

Dongdaegu Station is the front door. KTX and SRT trains from Seoul reach it in under two hours, and Daegu International Airport sits a short taxi ride from the station.

From Dongdaegu, the Daegu Metro takes over. It has three lines:

- **Line 1** runs through Dongdaegu and the Jungangno downtown area
- **Line 2** crosses the city east to west, meeting Line 1 at Banwoldang
- **Line 3** is an elevated monorail, useful for Seomun Market and Kim Gwang-seok Street

A T-money card bought in Seoul works on the Daegu Metro and city buses. Taxis are plentiful, and most visitors call them through the Kakao T app. After a large show, taxi queues near any venue are long, so many attendees walk to the nearest metro station instead.

Visitors usually stay near Dongdaegu for the train, or around Dongseongno and Jungangno for food and late-night streets.

## Show-day manners at a Korean concert

Korean concert culture has its own rules. Knowing them saves a bad moment at the gate.

- **ID at the entrance.** Staff commonly match the name on the ticket to a passport or Korean ID. Foreign fans carry their passport, not a photocopy.
- **Fanchants.** DAY6 fans shout set lines during certain songs. Fans usually learn them from fan-made guides online before the show.
- **The lightstick.** The official DAY6 lightstick often syncs to the venue's light show. Fans pair it with the official app on the day.
- **Filming.** Venues usually restrict or ban filming. Staff walk the aisles and will ask phones to go down.
- **Merch lines.** The official merch booth often draws a line hours before doors. Fans who want a specific item tend to queue in the morning.

Fans also trade small handmade gifts, stickers or photo cards, outside the venue. It is friendly and voluntary. A simple "gamsahamnida" (thank you) covers most exchanges.

## Kim Gwang-seok Street: a detour for band people

Daegu has its own singer-songwriter shrine. Kim Gwang-seok-gil is a mural alley next to Bangcheon Market in Jung-gu, dedicated to the folk singer Kim Gwang-seok, who grew up nearby and died in 1996.

The alley runs along a concrete embankment wall covered in paintings of him, lyrics and guitars. A bronze statue of him with a guitar sits on a bench, and visitors sit beside it for photos. Small speakers along the walk play his songs.

For a crowd that follows a band built on lyrics and live guitar, it fits the day. The walk takes around 30 minutes. Line 3's Kyungpook National University Hospital Station is the usual stop for it.

## After the encore: makchang and jjimgalbi

Daegu eats late. The city's signature is **makchang**, grilled beef or pork intestine cooked at the table over charcoal and dipped in a doenjang-based sauce with chopped green chilli.

Two food streets are where most visitors go:

- **Anjirang Gopchang Alley**, near Anjirang Station on Line 1, a long row of gopchang and makchang shops
- **Dongin-dong Jjimgalbi Alley** in Jung-gu, where braised beef short ribs arrive in a dented aluminium pot, red with chilli and garlic

For something lighter, **Seomun Market** (Line 3, Seomun Market Station) is one of Korea's oldest markets. It sells knife-cut noodles and hotteok from stalls in its covered lanes, and it runs a night market on some evenings.

Korean barbecue and makchang shops expect groups to share. Most take cards. Tipping is not part of the culture, and staff often cut the meat for you with scissors.
