## Six days, one building, the whole world selling to China

CIIE turns the usual trade fair around. Most big expos exist so a country can sell its goods abroad. Here, foreign companies come to Shanghai to sell into the Chinese market. The Ministry of Commerce and the Shanghai Municipal Government host it as a statement on global trade and cooperation.

The 9th edition runs **November 5 to 10, 2026**. Like every edition since the first in 2018, it fills the National Exhibition and Convention Center on Shanghai's western edge. Confirm timing, admission rules and any schedule changes on the official CIIE site before you book anything.

## Inside the four-leaf clover

The venue is easiest to understand from above. The NECC is built in the shape of a four-leaf clover. Four huge wings of exhibition halls spread out from a central plaza, and it ranks among the largest single-building exhibition complexes in the world.

That layout shapes your whole day. Halls connect through long internal walkways, and walking from one leaf to the opposite one takes real time. Plan your route by hall number, not by what you happen to pass.

The expo is usually split into two main parts:

- **Country Exhibition**: national pavilions where governments present their economies, culture and flagship industries.
- **Business Exhibition**: company stands grouped by sector. Recent editions have covered food and agricultural products, automobiles, technical equipment, consumer goods, medical equipment and healthcare, and trade in services.

The **Hongqiao International Economic Forum** runs alongside the halls. That is where the policy speeches and panel sessions happen.

## Getting to Qingpu on Line 2

Line 2 of the Shanghai Metro does the work. Get off at **National Exhibition and Convention Center station**, which you may see listed under its old name, Xujing East. Exits lead almost straight to the complex.

The site sits right beside the Hongqiao hub, which helps a lot. Hongqiao Railway Station and Hongqiao Airport Terminal 2 are both a few stops east on Line 2. If you are coming by high-speed rail from Beijing, Hangzhou or Nanjing, you can be at the halls without crossing the city.

From Pudong Airport the trip is far longer, since it runs the full width of Shanghai. Line 2 runs end to end, but give it well over an hour. Taxis and ride-hailing work too. During expo week, though, roads near the venue often see controls and drop-off restrictions, and the metro is usually the more predictable choice.

## Registration, badges and what "tickets" means here

You cannot simply turn up and buy a ticket at the door. CIIE runs on advance **real-name registration**. Exhibitors, professional buyers, media and visitors all apply for credentials through the official channels.

What to have ready:

- **Your passport.** The name on your registration must match your ID exactly, because credentials are checked against it at entry.
- **A company affiliation** if you are registering as a professional visitor or buyer.
- **A recent photo** in the format the registration system asks for.

Whether there is a public-access day, and who qualifies, has differed between editions. Check the official CIIE site for the current categories rather than relying on older articles. Security at the gates works much like an airport, with bag screening and badge checks. Leave space in your morning for it.

## Working the halls without losing a day

The best visitors treat CIIE as a series of meetings, not a stroll. These are the habits that get the most out of it:

- **Book meetings before you arrive.** Exhibitors fill their calendars early. Use the official matchmaking tools or email stands directly, and give each meeting a hall and stand number.
- **Bring plenty of business cards**, ideally with Chinese on the reverse. You will still hand them over with both hands, and you will get through more than you expect.
- **Set up mobile payment.** Venue food outlets and most of Shanghai run on Alipay and WeChat Pay. Both apps now let foreign visitors link an international card, and setting this up before you fly saves trouble at the first café counter.
- **Install a translation app** that works offline. Many stand staff speak English, but partners and suppliers may not.
- **Group your day by leaf.** Cover one wing of the clover in the morning and another after lunch. Zig-zagging between them wastes time.

Arriving close to opening usually means shorter security lines than the mid-morning wave of delegations. The opening day tends to be dominated by the official ceremony and VIP movements, so serious buyers often schedule meetings for the days after.

## Hongqiao week: beds, food and the city beyond

The hotel squeeze comes from geography. Everyone wants to sleep near the NECC, and properties around the Hongqiao business district fill quickly in early November. Book as early as your plans allow.

If Hongqiao is full, stay anywhere along Line 2. Jing'an and People's Square are a straight run west to the venue. The East Nanjing Road area puts you near **the Bund** for evenings, at the cost of a longer commute.

A few easy add-ons once the halls close:

- **Zhujiajiao**, the canal water town, is also in Qingpu District and makes a good half-day before or after the expo.
- **The Bund and Lujiazui** sit at the east end of Line 2 for skyline views across the Huangpu River.
- **Yu Garden** and the old-city lanes around it are a short hop from the Bund.

Early November is generally mild, cool weather in Shanghai, good for walking. The heavy summer humidity is long gone. Pack a layer, though, because you will move between heated halls and cooler evenings outside.
