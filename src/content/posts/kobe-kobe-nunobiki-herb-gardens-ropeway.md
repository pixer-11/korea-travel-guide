## Getting up the mountain

The ropeway station sits a short walk from Shin-Kobe Station, the Shinkansen stop north of downtown Kobe. Gondolas run continuously, no set departure time, and the climb to the top garden takes roughly ten minutes over pine forest and the Nunobiki Falls ravine.

You can also walk up via the falls trail in about 30-40 minutes, then ride the ropeway down, or buy a one-way ticket either direction. Round-trip tickets cost more than one-way but most visitors do the full loop.

Glass-walled cabins give clear views back over Kobe's harbor and the Akashi Kaikyo Bridge on clear days. Hold your bags on your lap; the cabins are compact and fit maybe eight riders comfortably.

## What's actually in the gardens

Twelve themed garden areas terrace down the hillside, connected by paths and a second, shorter ropeway shuttle between upper and lower sections. Expect lavender fields, a rose garden, a glasshouse of tropical herbs, and a Mediterranean-style area with olive trees.

Spring (April-May) brings roses and tulips; summer runs lavender and Mediterranean blooms; autumn shifts to salvia and cosmos. Winter is thinner but the glasshouse stays planted year-round.

A restaurant near the top serves herb-infused dishes and there's a café with garden views. The gift shop sells herb oils, dried lavender, and Kobe-branded herb teas, useful if you want a souvenir that isn't a keychain.

## Hours and when to go

Weekdays the gardens close at 4:45pm, so plan your ropeway ride up by early afternoon at the latest to have real time on-site. Weekends and Sundays the whole complex stays open until 8:15pm, with illuminated evening gardens in warmer months.

Weekend crowds peak 10am-4pm, exactly when tour groups and families arrive. If your schedule allows a Saturday or Sunday evening visit, 5pm-8pm is measurably quieter and you get the extended hours to yourself.

Weekday mornings right after the 9:30am opening are a reasonable alternative if evenings don't work, since the site is very popular and rarely empty.

## Riding and walking like a regular

Buy the ropeway ticket at the base station counter or from the machines; cash and IC cards (Suica, ICOCA) both work at most kiosks in the area. There's no need to reserve gondola seats, just queue and board the next car.

If you're mobility-limited or short on time, ride up and down rather than walking the falls trail, which has stairs and uneven stone steps. Families with strollers should also default to the gondola both ways.

Wear real shoes even if you skip the trail: garden paths are gravel and sloped in sections, not paved flat. Bring a layer even in summer, since the mountainside runs a few degrees cooler than downtown Kobe.

Check the official ropeway and garden hours before visiting, since seasonal closures and maintenance days do occur outside the standard schedule.
