## Sixty-eight floors above Southwark

Glass is everywhere, and that's the point. The Shard, designed by Renzo Piano and finished in 2012, rises about 310 metres from the south bank beside London Bridge station. Its observation floors sit near the top of the spire, where the building narrows to a sharp point of angled panes.

Up there, London stops looking like a series of streets. It becomes a map. The Thames curls past Tower Bridge and HMS Belfast, St Paul's dome sits low to the west, and Canary Wharf's towers cluster to the east. On a clear day the editors' own line holds: views of up to 40 miles.

It's consistently well rated and very popular, so plan around the crowds rather than expecting to wander in.

## Getting to the doors at London Bridge

The Shard's address is 32 London Bridge St, SE1 9SG, and the building sits directly over **London Bridge station**. That makes it one of the easiest major attractions in London to reach.

- **Underground:** Northern line or Jubilee line to London Bridge.
- **National Rail:** Thameslink and Southern trains stop at London Bridge, handy from Gatwick or Brighton.
- **On foot:** about 10 minutes across London Bridge from the City, or along the river from Tower Bridge.

Inside the station, follow the signs for The View. The ticket entrance is at street level, separate from the hotel and office lobbies.

## The weather gamble and the visibility guarantee

London weather decides your visit. Low cloud can swallow the top of the Shard completely, and from inside you see grey on grey.

The View offers a **good visibility guarantee**: if the view is poor, you can come back again for free. Check the current terms on the official site before you go, since the details of how to claim matter.

Practical habit: look up at the spire from the street or a webcam before your slot. If the point is hidden in cloud, that's your answer. Keeping your plans flexible across two or three days in London gives the guarantee room to work.

## Floors 68, 69 and 72

The lifts carry you up in two stages, and your ears will notice. You step out into indoor galleries on **floors 68 and 69**, wrapped in floor-to-ceiling glass on every side.

Walk the full loop, because each side tells a different story:

- **North:** the City's towers, St Paul's Cathedral, and the Thames bridges.
- **East:** Tower Bridge, the Tower of London, and Canary Wharf further out.
- **South and west:** rooftops rolling towards the hills, plus the London Eye and Westminster downriver.

Then climb to **floor 72**, the highest public level. It is partly open to the air, with the glass shards of the spire rising above you. Wind and city noise come through, which makes it feel genuinely high. Bring a layer.

## Timing your slot

Hours shift through the year and on holidays, so check the official site for this month. At the moment it's **closed Monday and Tuesday**. Wednesday to Friday and Sunday it runs 11am–7pm, and Saturday stretches from 10am to 10pm.

Crowd forecasts are clear. On weekdays it's usually calmest between **11am and 2pm**. At weekends the quiet window is **10am to 12pm**, which in practice means Saturday morning, since Sunday doors currently open at 11am.

Try not to book a weekend slot after 12pm. From then until 10pm is the busiest stretch, and the glass edges fill two deep. Saturday's late opening is the time to see the city lights come on, but expect company.

## Getting up without the wait

Treat this like a short flight. There's airport-style security screening before the lifts, so bags go through a scanner and liquids may be checked.

- **Book online** for a timed entry slot, especially weekends. Prices are on the official site; walk-up availability isn't guaranteed.
- **Arrive a little early** for your slot so security doesn't eat into it.
- **Travel light.** Big luggage slows screening; leave it at your hotel.
- **Pay by card or phone.** Contactless is the norm in London, and the ticket desk follows suit.

Once you're up there's no strict time limit on the galleries, so don't rush the first lap. Most people spend about an hour. Photographers at dusk tend to linger longer, and on a crowded Saturday evening patience at the glass gets you a front spot eventually.

## Before and after: Borough and the river

You don't need to go far afterwards. **Borough Market** is a few minutes' walk west, with food stalls under Victorian iron roofs; it gets packed at weekend lunchtimes. **Southwark Cathedral** sits right beside it, quieter than you'd expect.

Head east along the riverside path and you pass **HMS Belfast**, moored in the Thames, before reaching **Tower Bridge**. Cross it for the **Tower of London**. Go west instead and the Thames Path leads to the Globe and Tate Modern.

A good order: market first, Shard in your quiet window, river walk to finish. Having just seen it from the top, you'll recognise the route.
