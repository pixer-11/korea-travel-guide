You reach it by crossing the Genil river south of the old town, past the university faculties and the leafy Paseo del Salón, until a low white-and-glass complex opens up with its distinctive angular tower jutting above the treeline. That tower is your landmark from most of southern Granada — a 50-metre observation deck that on a clear day lines up the red-tiled rooftops of the Albaicín against the snow-streaked ridge of the Sierra Nevada.

Before you've even bought a ticket, the building itself signals what's inside: this isn't a museum of glass cases and wall text, it's a place designed for touching, cranking, and testing things with your own hands.

## Why go

Parque de las Ciencias is one of the most visited attractions in Granada for a reason that has nothing to do with Moorish palaces or flamenco — it's a genuinely large, well-funded science park, the kind of place where school groups spend entire mornings pressing buttons and where adults find themselves lingering at exhibits about perception and physics longer than planned.

The permanent collection covers biology, technology, the human body, and the physics of everyday phenomena through working models rather than static displays. Add a planetarium screening a rotating slate of astronomy shows, and a separate tropical butterfly house — a warm, humid glasshouse where dozens of free-flying species drift past ferns and flowering plants — and you have a stop that easily fills an afternoon.

## Getting there

The address is Av. de la Ciencia, s/n, in the Ronda district, on the south side of the Genil river. From Plaza Nueva or the Cathedral it's a flat, unhurried 20-25 minute walk down Acera del Darro and across the river — genuinely one of the more pleasant walking routes in the city, especially in the cooler morning hours. Several city bus lines running along Camino de Ronda and Avenida de la Ciencia stop within a couple of minutes of the entrance if you'd rather ride; ask at your hotel for the current line number, as routes get renumbered. There's also space for cars if you're driving in from the Alhambra or Sierra Nevada side of town.

## What to see and do

Start with the main pavilion's rotating and permanent exhibitions — hands-on stations covering everything from mechanics to the senses, well suited to kids but genuinely engaging for adults too. Set aside 30-45 minutes for a planetarium show if the timing works with your visit; shows run on a schedule separate from general admission, so check what's playing and buy tickets for a specific slot rather than assuming you can walk in.

The Mariposario (butterfly park) is worth the detour to the far end of the complex — go slowly, keep your camera lens away from the humidity for a minute to avoid fogging, and expect butterflies to land closer than you'd think. Finish at the observation tower for the view before you leave; it's an easy, quick climb and the best free panorama the site offers.

## When to go

Mornings tend to be calmer than early afternoon, when local school groups and family outings cluster around midday. Weekdays outside Spanish school holidays are noticeably quieter than weekends and August, when Granada families bring children out of the heat and into air-conditioned halls. Spring and autumn give you comfortable walking weather for the riverside approach; midsummer in Granada gets genuinely hot, so an air-conditioned science museum is a smart midday refuge from the sun regardless of the season.

## How to visit like a local

Buy your general admission and, if you want the planetarium, a timed show ticket at the same time rather than queuing twice — the planetarium sells out its individual slots faster than the main galleries fill up. Most Granada families treat this as a full outing rather than a quick stop, arriving with a packed snack or using the on-site café, so don't expect to breeze through in under an hour.

Payment is standard card or cash at the ticket desk; there's no tipping culture at Spanish museums. The mistake visitors most often make is treating it like a quick add-on between the Alhambra and lunch — the complex is genuinely large, spread across several buildings and outdoor spaces, and rushing means missing the butterfly house or tower entirely. Confirm the current opening hours and planetarium timetable on the official website before you go, since both shift with the season.
