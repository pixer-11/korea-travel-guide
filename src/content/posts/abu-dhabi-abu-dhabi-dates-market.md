## What you're walking into

Don't picture a sprawling souk. The Abu Dhabi Dates Market is one compact shop, shelves stacked to the ceiling with dried fruit, nuts and sweets, inside the Mina Market cluster near Zayed Port.

The date wall is the point: dozens of varieties, from soft Medjool to drier, chewier local picks, often in open trays so you can see and smell before buying. Staff hand out samples on toothpicks or small plates as you move down the counter.

Nuts, dried figs, apricots, and boxed Arabic sweets fill the rest of the space. Packaging is aimed at gifting: many shoppers leave with sealed boxes, not loose bags.

## Getting there and timing it right

It's inside the Mina Zayed port district, near the Zayed Port gate and the wider Mina Market complex, which also holds fish, fruit and vegetable markets worth combining into one trip. A taxi is the simplest route since public transit doesn't drop you close.

Hours run 7am to midnight, every day of the week, which is unusually long for a market stall. That flexibility matters because this place is genuinely busy: it has a very-popular status and a heavy review count for its size.

- Morning (soon after 7am) is quieter, before tour groups and bulk shoppers arrive
- Evenings after dinner draw families stocking up for gifting season
- Combine with a walk through the neighboring fish or produce markets for a fuller Mina Zayed visit

## Tasting before you buy

This is a sample-first shop. Walk the counter, ask for a taste of two or three date varieties, and compare texture and sweetness before committing.

Prices vary by weight and variety, and boxes are priced differently from loose scoops, so check the label or ask staff directly rather than assuming a flat rate. Cash and card are both commonly accepted at markets in this district, but carrying some cash is still sensible for smaller stalls nearby.

Bring an insulated bag if you're flying out soon after; the UAE heat is unkind to soft varieties like Medjool left in a hot car or bag for hours.

## Beating the crowds

With over 10,000 reviews logged, this shop is firmly on the tourist and local shopping circuit alike, not a quiet find. Expect other visitors doing exactly what you're doing: tasting, comparing, filling boxes for relatives back home.

Arriving right after opening at 7am usually beats the mid-morning tour-bus rush. If you're after specific varieties in bulk, ask staff which batches came in freshest that day, since turnover is high given the volume of buyers passing through.

Skip weekend late afternoons if you want elbow room at the counter; that's when the shop fills fastest based on its sheer foot count relative to its size.
