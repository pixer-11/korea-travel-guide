Two pieces of Baroque and Renaissance Rome, a few floors away from Xiongnu gold and Mongol Empire armour. That pairing was the point of this show. The plan was for a portrait attributed to Raphael and a bronze Neptune and Triton by Bernini to leave the Galleria Borghese for one month and travel to the Chinggis Khaan National Museum in Ulaanbaatar.

The exhibition was set for October 1 to November 1, 2026. It was billed as the first time these works would be shown anywhere in Asia.

## The wind off the ocean, explained

The title framed the show. In full it was "The Wind of the Great Ocean Across the Steppe: Raphael and Bernini", which names a meeting of two worlds rather than a simple art loan.

The "great ocean" is the Mediterranean culture of Rome. Bernini's subject reinforces it: Neptune, god of the sea, with Triton, his son. Bringing a sea god to one of the most landlocked capitals on earth was a deliberate contrast. Ulaanbaatar sits far from any coast, ringed by the hills of the Tuul River valley.

## What comes from the Galleria Borghese

The Galleria Borghese is the villa museum in Rome's Villa Borghese gardens. It holds one of the world's great Bernini collections and a celebrated group of Raphael paintings. Loans from it are rare, so its name on a wall label carries weight.

The loan for this show named two works:

- **A portrait attributed to Raphael.** "Attributed" was the curators' own word. It means scholars link the painting to Raphael's hand without treating it as fully documented.
- **A bronze Neptune and Triton by Bernini.** Sea god and son are cast in bronze, so the patina and the modelled surface change as you walk around the piece.

A show with so few objects rewards slow looking. A sculpture in the round rewards a full circuit, and the Triton reads differently from behind than from the front.

## Dates, tickets and where the details live

The announced dates were October 1 to November 1, 2026. The venue was the Chinggis Khaan National Museum, the large modern museum that opened in central Ulaanbaatar in 2022.

The museum's ticket desk is inside the building, where admission prices are posted. Special loan exhibitions often carry their own entry terms, separate from general admission.

## Getting to the Chinggis Khaan National Museum

The museum is in the city centre, a short walk from Sükhbaatar Square, which some maps label Chinggis Square. Most visitors arrive on foot from the hotels around the square or by taxi.

- **From the airport:** Chinggis Khaan International Airport is roughly 50 km south of the city. The drive takes about an hour, and longer at rush hour.
- **By taxi:** ride-hailing apps such as UBCab are widely used. Many visitors find them easier than flagging informal cars on the street.
- **On foot:** central Ulaanbaatar is compact enough to walk. Traffic on Peace Avenue is often gridlocked at peak times, so walking can be quicker.

In Mongolian the museum is Чингис хааны үндэсний музей (Chinggis Khaany Ündesnii Muzei). Drivers know it simply as the Chinggis Khaan museum.

## Paying, coats and gallery manners

A few local habits shape a museum visit here.

- **Paying:** bank cards are widely accepted in central Ulaanbaatar. Mongolians also use QPay, a mobile payment app, all the time. Tipping is not a strong custom.
- **Coats:** in October people go out in heavy jackets. Museums in the city usually have a cloakroom, and visitors leave bulky layers there before entering the galleries.
- **Photography:** loaned masterpieces often come with stricter photo rules than a permanent collection, and gallery signage states what applies. Flash is almost always off-limits near old paintings.
- **Language:** labels in major Ulaanbaatar museums are usually in Mongolian and English. Staff at the front desk generally manage basic English.

The museum's permanent galleries trace the steppe from prehistory through the Mongol Empire and beyond. Paired with a loan show, they easily fill a half day.

## The October turn on the steppe

The show's month had a character of its own. October is when Ulaanbaatar tips from autumn into winter. Daytime can still be bright and crisp, but nights regularly drop below freezing and the first snow often arrives.

The light is a bonus. Mongolia is famous for clear skies, and October afternoons are short and low-angled. October visitors tend to fit outdoor sights into the late morning and early afternoon, then head indoors as the temperature falls.

Hotel rates and crowds in October sit below the summer peak around Naadam in July. Travellers who prefer a quieter capital tend to like this season.

## Around Sükhbaatar Square afterwards

The museum's central location means the next stop is close by.

- **Sükhbaatar Square and the Government Palace:** the huge seated statue of Chinggis Khaan sits on the palace's front, facing the square.
- **Choijin Lama Temple Museum:** a cluster of early 20th-century Buddhist temples surrounded by modern towers, a short walk south of the square.
- **Zanabazar Museum of Fine Arts:** works by Zanabazar, the 17th-century sculptor and religious leader. His gilded bronzes make a strong comparison with Bernini's.
- **Gandan Monastery:** the city's main working monastery, a short taxi ride west, with its tall statue of Migjid Janraisig.

Zanabazar's bronzes made the most rewarding companion to Bernini's. Two sculptors from the same century worked in bronze at opposite ends of Eurasia. Each one shaped the sacred art of his own world.
