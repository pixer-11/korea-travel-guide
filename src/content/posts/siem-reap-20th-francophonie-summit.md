## A summit, not a festival

This is a diplomatic meeting. The Sommet de la Francophonie brings together the heads of state and government of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF), the body linking countries that share French as a language. It is normally held every two years. The 19th edition took place in France in 2024, at Villers-Cotterêts and Paris.

The 20th summit moves to Siem Reap, with November 14, 2026 as the announced date. It sits on Cambodia's 2026 calendar of international events. Hosting it places a provincial temple town, best known as the gateway to Angkor, at the centre of a diplomatic gathering usually held in capitals.

## Tickets, access and the venue

There is no box office. Plenary sessions and closed meetings are open only to accredited people:

- official delegations from member and observer states
- accredited journalists
- invited institutional and civil-society guests

Accreditation runs through the OIF and the host government. The OIF keeps summit information on its official site, francophonie.org. The facts we have name Siem Reap as the host city but not a particular hall, so this page does not name one. Summits like this usually have a few side events, cultural programmes or forums, and some of these can be more open than the main sessions.

## Siem Reap in mid-November

The timing matches the town's busiest stretch. November marks the end of the rainy season and the start of the drier, cooler months, which is when visitor numbers to Angkor climb. Add delegations and press corps, and hotel demand near the river and along the airport road rises sharply.

Most international arrivals land at Siem Reap–Angkor International Airport, which opened in 2023 and sits roughly an hour's drive east of the town centre. Summits of heads of state usually bring motorcades, security cordons and short road closures. That means a tuk-tuk ride that normally takes ten minutes can stretch out while convoys pass.

## Moving around town while delegations are in

Most people get around by tuk-tuk. Many visitors book their rides through PassApp or Grab, both of which work in Siem Reap, because the fare is fixed before the ride starts. US dollars and Cambodian riel are both accepted, and change below a dollar usually comes back in riel.

- **Angkor Pass:** it is sold at the official Angkor Enterprise ticket centre on the road out to the temples, and your photo is printed on the pass. Most visitors buy it at the centre itself, because unofficial resellers are a known problem.
- **Temple dress:** cover your shoulders and knees. Guards turn people away from Angkor Wat's upper level, the Bakan, if they are not covered.
- **Greeting:** the sampeah is palms pressed together at chest height with a slight bow. People return it to monks and older people, and it is welcome everywhere.
- **Language:** signs are in Khmer and English. Some older Cambodians still speak French from the colonial era, but English is what works in shops and restaurants.

During summit week, temple visitors often make their Angkor Wat sunrise trip on a day without major sessions. The temple roads and the town centre then carry only ordinary peak-season traffic. In the evening, the action shifts to Pub Street and the Old Market (Psar Chas), a short walk apart near the Siem Reap River.
