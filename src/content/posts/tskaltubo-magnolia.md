## A late table in a sleepy spa town

Tskaltubo goes to bed early. This is a town built around radon-carbonate springs and the huge sanatoriums Moscow once filled with workers on prescribed holidays. Many of those buildings now stand empty, their colonnades overgrown, and the evenings here are quiet.

Magnolia breaks that pattern. On four nights a week it keeps its doors open around the clock, which is rare anywhere in Imereti outside Kutaisi. It is consistently well rated by the people who find it. For now it remains a quieter find rather than a stop on every day-tripper's list.

Google files it as an Eastern European restaurant, and its prices sit in the mid-range. That puts it in a sensible middle: a proper sit-down meal without the bill of a resort hotel dining room.

## What to look for on the table

We were not given Magnolia's menu, so treat the dishes below as the regional food to ask about, not a list of what is guaranteed. Tskaltubo sits in Imereti, and Imeretian cooking has its own identity within Georgian food.

- **Imeruli khachapuri:** the region's namesake cheese bread, a round, closed flatbread filled with brined Imeretian cheese, unlike the boat-shaped Adjarian version
- **Pkhali:** walnut-and-vegetable pastes, often spinach or beetroot, topped with pomegranate seeds
- **Mtsvadi:** skewered grilled pork or veal, usually served with raw onion
- **Khinkali:** twisted dumplings you eat by hand, holding the knot
- **Lobio:** stewed beans, often brought out in a clay pot

The "Eastern European" label suggests the menu may also go beyond Georgia, which is common in places that serve Russian, Ukrainian and Armenian visitors to the spa. If you can't read the menu, ask the staff what the kitchen does best that day.

## Ordering and paying the Georgian way

Georgian meals are shared. You order several plates for the table, and they arrive as each one is ready, not in courses. Order one more dish than you think you need, because bread and cheese fill the gaps.

- **Menus:** Georgian uses its own Mkhedruli script. Ask politely whether there is a menu in English or Russian; Russian is widely understood by older staff across Imereti.
- **Paying:** card payment is normal in Georgian restaurants, though carrying some lari covers you if a terminal is down late at night.
- **Tipping:** check the bill for a service charge line before adding anything. Many Georgian restaurants include one. If it's missing, rounding up for good service is appreciated.
- **Toasts:** if you share a table with Georgians, expect wine toasts led by one person, the *tamada*. Drink when the toast ends, not before.

A useful word: *gaumarjos* means "cheers", or literally "victory to you". "Madloba" is thank you.

## Finding Paliashvili Street

The address is 1 Zakaria Paliashvili Street, a street named after the composer of Georgia's best-known operas. Tskaltubo is compact, and its centre is laid out around the long Spa Resort Park and its bathhouses, so most central addresses are a short walk or taxi ride apart.

From Kutaisi, the drive takes roughly 20 minutes. Marshrutka minibuses run between the two towns from Kutaisi's central stops during the day. Taxis are the easier option at night, and ride-hailing apps such as Bolt operate in the Kutaisi area. Kutaisi International Airport is closer to Tskaltubo than the city itself is, so a late arrival can come here first.

Give your driver the Georgian address, ზაქარია ფალიაშვილის ქუჩა 1. It saves a lot of pointing at maps.

## Building a day around dinner

Magnolia works best as the end of a day out, and the area gives you plenty to fill one.

- **Spa Resort Park:** the green spine of the town, lined with bathhouses and sanatorium facades from the Soviet era.
- **Bathhouse No. 6:** the ornate bath long associated with Stalin, who is said to have taken the waters here.
- **The abandoned sanatoriums:** buildings like the former Medea and Iveria draw photographers; be careful, since some are still lived in by families displaced in the 1990s.
- **Prometheus Cave:** a big show cave a few kilometres northwest of town, with lit halls of stalactites and an underground boat section.
- **Sataplia Nature Reserve:** dinosaur footprints preserved under a protective dome, plus a cave and a glass viewing platform, on the road toward Kutaisi.

Caves, parks and reserves all change their hours with the season, so check each official site for this month's times. A cave in the afternoon, a soak or a walk through the park, then a long dinner is a natural order. On the 24-hour nights there is no pressure to watch the clock.

## Who it suits

Magnolia suits travellers who land late or stay out late. It also suits anyone using Tskaltubo as a cheaper base for Kutaisi and the caves. Its prices and flexible hours fit a road-trip budget better than resort dining rooms do.

It is not a destination restaurant you cross the country for. It is a dependable, well-reviewed table in a town where options thin out after dark, and that is often the more useful thing to know.
