## A café that's also a ceramics room

Mozmoji spreads over two floors on South Bridge Road. It runs as a café and a ceramics gallery at the same time. The shelves hold handcrafted pieces by Chinese artisans, and they're for sale, so the room is part coffee stop and part shop.

The appeal is the quiet. Outside, South Bridge Road carries steady traffic past shophouse fronts and temple gates. Inside, people sit over pour-overs and plated desserts among the pottery. The place is new, but social media has already made it a well-known Chinatown stop.

## Finding it on South Bridge Road

South Bridge Road is one of the main roads through Chinatown, so you can walk there from two MRT stations:

- **Chinatown MRT** (North East and Downtown lines) is the easier pick if you're coming from the Pagoda Street and Chinatown Street Market end.
- **Maxwell MRT** (Thomson-East Coast Line) comes up near Maxwell Food Centre, at the southern end of the road.

The same street has some of Singapore's best-known religious buildings, so the café fits easily into a walk:

- **Sri Mariamman Temple**, Singapore's oldest Hindu temple, with a sculpted gopuram over the entrance.
- **Jamae Mosque**, one of the earliest mosques on the island.
- **Buddha Tooth Relic Temple**, the large red Tang-style temple by Maxwell.

Look up the exact street number before you set off. The road is long, and a café is easy to miss among the shopfronts.

## What to order and what to look at

People come for the pour-over coffee and the desserts. We weren't given a menu, so check what's on the counter when you arrive. Seasonal items and drinks may change.

Plan for about an hour. That's enough time for a drink, a dessert and a slow look at the ceramics on both floors. If you want to buy a piece, give it extra time. Handmade work varies from piece to piece, so pick each one up and check the glaze and the base.

The ceramics also suit a quieter afternoon around Chinatown. Pair the café with the temples nearby, or with lunch at Maxwell Food Centre if you'd rather have your main meal at a hawker centre.

## Sitting among the pottery

The shop shares the room with the tables, so a few habits help:

- **Handle the pottery gently.** If you're unsure about a fragile piece, ask the staff before you pick it up, and keep bags off the display surfaces.
- **Keep your voice down.** Many people come for the calm, and two quiet floors carry sound easily.
- **Ask before a long photo shoot** of the gallery shelves. A quick phone shot of your coffee is normal. A tripod session among the displays is not.
- **Payment:** Singapore cafés generally take cards and PayNow, and tipping isn't customary. If there's a service charge, it will show on your bill.
- **Booking:** check Mozmoji's own social media or listing for current hours and seating rules first. New, much-photographed cafés often change their opening times and seating rules in the first few months.

Weekends in Chinatown draw more people than weekdays. If you want the quiet that made this place known, a weekday visit is the safer bet.
