Causeway Bay has no shortage of imported restaurant concepts, but Commune Maison arrived with more noise than most. It's the Hong Kong debut of a dining-and-bar brand that built its name on the mainland, and it picked a street-facing unit inside Lee Garden Three to make its entrance, the kind of spot where passersby on Sunning Road or Yun Ping Road can see straight into the room from the pavement.

That visibility is part of the strategy. Lee Garden Three sits within the Lee Gardens cluster of towers that anchors this side of Causeway Bay, a shopping and office district with a steady flow of foot traffic all day. A restaurant with glass frontage and an open, street-level presence there is set up to be noticed rather than tucked away upstairs.

## What's actually on the table

The format is deliberately all-day rather than a single-service restaurant. Neapolitan-style pizza is the anchor, the kind with a blistered, leopard-spotted crust cooked hot and fast, alongside burgers and brunch plates for the parts of the day when pizza feels like the wrong order.

Once evening service takes over, the craft cocktail list becomes the draw, pitched at the same crowd that would linger over dinner rather than rush out after the bill. That range, from midday brunch plate to late cocktail, is what lets the space function as an all-day hangout instead of a lunch-only or dinner-only stop.

## Why it's drawing a crowd

Part of the appeal is simply novelty: a brand with a following on the mainland opening its first Hong Kong outpost gives people a reason to show up in the first weeks specifically to see it. Part of it is the room itself, built for a buzzy, sociable atmosphere rather than quiet conversation, which suits the group bookings and after-work crowd that Causeway Bay's office towers feed into.

Because it's a genuinely new and popular opening, expect the first months to run hotter than a settled, years-old restaurant would. Walk-in waits and shifting hours are normal for a debut like this, so treat any schedule you find online as a starting point rather than gospel.

## Getting in without a wasted trip

Causeway Bay MTR station puts you within a short walk of the Lee Gardens complex; from there, following signage toward Lee Garden Three gets you to the door. Because Commune Maison is a new, high-interest opening, don't assume a Tuesday lunch trip will get you a table on a whim.

Before you go:

- Confirm current opening hours directly, since new venues often adjust them in the first stretch after launch
- Check whether reservations are being taken, and book ahead if so, especially for evening cocktail hours
- Have a backup plan for peak lunch and dinner windows in case of a walk-in wait

## Ordering like you've been here before

Treat it as a full menu, not a pizza-only stop: order a pizza to anchor the table, then let someone else pick a burger or brunch plate so you're not all reaching for the same dish. If you're coming for drinks rather than a full meal, evening is when the cocktail program is clearly the point, so arrive after the daytime brunch crowd has cleared out rather than mid-afternoon when the kitchen is still in its lighter, in-between mode.

Splitting plates works well here given the range on offer, pizza slices alongside a burger and a shared starter, rather than everyone ordering a single individual dish. As with most buzzy openings in Lee Gardens, a large group without a reservation should expect to wait, so calling ahead is the difference between walking straight in and standing on Sunning Road watching the room through the glass.

## Nearby, if you've got time to extend the trip

Lee Garden Three sits inside a wider retail complex, so a stop at Commune Maison pairs easily with browsing the rest of the Lee Gardens towers, or continuing on to Times Square and the denser shopping strip toward Hennessy Road. Causeway Bay's harbourfront and Victoria Park are also within walking distance if you want to work off the pizza afterward.
