## One night in Wembley

The date is 26 October 2026. Jay Park, the Korean-American rapper, singer and dancer, brings his Serenades & Body Rolls World Tour to OVO Arena Wembley, with LNGSHOT as special guests.

The tour title tells you what kind of show it is. Jay Park's catalogue runs from smooth R&B ballads to hard-edged hip-hop, and his reputation was built as much on choreography as on vocals. "Serenades" covers one side of that. "Body rolls" covers the other.

## Who's on stage

Jay Park first became known in Korea as a member of the boy band 2PM. After leaving, he rebuilt his career as a solo artist and label founder. He co-founded AOMG and H1GHR MUSIC, two of the most influential labels in Korean hip-hop, and later launched More Vision.

That history is why his crowds are so mixed:

- K-pop fans who have followed him since his idol days
- Korean hip-hop listeners who know him through AOMG's rappers
- R&B fans drawn by his English-language releases

LNGSHOT open the night as special guests. Fans of the wider Korean hip-hop and R&B scene usually treat the support set as part of the main event, not a reason to linger at the bar.

## The arena itself

OVO Arena Wembley is older than most people expect. It opened in 1934 as the Empire Pool, built for the swimming events of the British Empire Games. Its concrete frame and long, curved roofline still show that past. For decades it was simply Wembley Arena, before sponsorship brought its current name.

Inside, there's a standing floor in front of the stage and tiered seating rising on three sides. Capacity is roughly 12,500. That makes it big enough for a full production, yet small enough that the back rows can still read faces on stage.

## Getting to OVO Arena Wembley

Three stations serve the arena, and most concertgoers use the first:

- **Wembley Park** (Jubilee and Metropolitan lines): the simplest route from central London. Walk down Olympic Way, the wide pedestrian boulevard towards the stadium, and the arena is on your right.
- **Wembley Stadium station**: Chiltern Railways trains from London Marylebone.
- **Wembley Central**: Bakerloo line, London Overground and some national rail services. It's a slightly longer walk.

From Baker Street, the Metropolitan line reaches Wembley Park in a few stops. That's usually faster than the Jubilee line from the West End.

## Doors, queues and the walk home

There's a pattern to arena nights here. Standing ticket holders usually join the queue well before doors so they can get close to the barrier. Seated fans tend to arrive later and grab food on the way.

A few habits regulars follow:

- **Bags:** OVO Arena Wembley operates a strict bag policy, so most people bring a small bag or nothing. The arena's website sets out the current rules.
- **Payment:** the venue is card and contactless almost everywhere, from the bars to the merch stands.
- **Merch:** queues for tour merchandise build up in the foyer once doors open. Fans who want a specific item usually head there first.
- **Fan chants and lightsticks:** Korean-artist audiences often bring handmade banners and phone-light moments. Joining in is expected, not odd.

After the encore, Olympic Way fills shoulder to shoulder. Staff manage the flow into Wembley Park station with barriers, which slows the platform entry. Plenty of fans walk to Wembley Stadium or Wembley Central instead. Others wait out the surge at a nearby bar.

## Around the arena before the show

The streets around the arena have been rebuilt over the past decade as Wembley Park. The area is designed for event crowds, so there's plenty to do before the show:

- **Boxpark Wembley**: shipping-container food stalls and bars right by the station, the usual pre-show meeting point
- **London Designer Outlet**: shops and chain restaurants a couple of minutes from the arena doors
- **Wembley Stadium**: the arch is the landmark everyone uses to find each other afterwards
