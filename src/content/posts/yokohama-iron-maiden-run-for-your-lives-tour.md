## Two nights at K Arena Yokohama

Iron Maiden are back in Japan for two dates. The Run For Your Lives tour plays K Arena Yokohama on November 24 and 25, 2026. The tour marks the band's 50th anniversary, and its setlist leans on the albums of their first decade and a bit.

K Arena opened in 2023 and is built for music rather than sport. Seating rises steeply in a fan shape facing one end stage, so even the upper tiers look straight at the band. Capacity is around 20,000. That makes this one of the larger rooms Maiden play in Japan.

## Getting to Minato Mirai

The arena stands on the Yokohama waterfront, between Yokohama Station and the Minato Mirai towers. Your options:

- **Shin-Takashima Station** (Minatomirai Line): the closest stop, a few minutes on foot.
- **Minatomirai Station** (Minatomirai Line): a slightly longer walk past the Yokohama Landmark Tower.
- **Yokohama Station** (JR, Tokyu, Keikyu, Sotetsu, subway): roughly 15 minutes on foot along the waterfront walkways.
- **From Tokyo**: the Tokyu Toyoko Line from Shibuya runs straight onto the Minatomirai Line, so you need no change of train.

After the show, Shin-Takashima gets packed and the platforms are metered by staff. Walking the extra 15 minutes back to Yokohama Station is often faster, and you get a far wider choice of lines.

## Tickets, entry and the merch line

Big concerts in Japan usually sell through lottery-style presales and then general sale. Tickets are often digital and tied to a named smartphone app. Expect a name or ID check at the door, and be aware that unofficial resale is restricted. Buy only through the channels listed on the official tour page. Prices and gate times are posted there too.

Every seat is assigned, so there is no point queuing for hours to get a good spot. The queue worth joining is the one for merchandise. Tour shirts sell out by size, and the merch line forms well ahead of doors.

## Gig etiquette, Japanese-style

Japanese metal crowds are loud during songs and almost silent between them. Shout along with "Run to the Hills" and stay in your seat area. Moving into the aisles gets you a polite but firm word from staff.

- **Photography**: arenas in Japan generally ban photos and filming unless an announcement says otherwise. Watch the screens and signs.
- **Bags**: large bags and suitcases are usually refused. Leave them in the coin lockers at Yokohama Station before you walk over.
- **Payment**: merch stands and nearby convenience stores take cards and IC transit cards (Suica, PASMO). Carry a little cash as a backup anyway.
- **Rubbish**: bins are rare. Carry your empties out and sort them at the station.
- **Timing**: Japanese shows start on schedule and finish early enough for the last trains. Check the start time on your ticket and get there with time to spare.

For food beforehand, try the restaurant floors at Yokohama World Porters or the shops around Yokohama Station. The red-brick warehouses at Akarenga Soko make a good walk afterwards if you aren't catching the last train. Confirm the dates, venue details and tickets on the official site before you travel.
