## Two nights at K Arena Yokohama

The Run For Your Lives tour was set to bring Iron Maiden to Japan for two dates at K Arena Yokohama, on November 24 and 25, 2026. The tour marked the band's 50th anniversary, and it was billed around the albums of their first decade and a bit.

K Arena opened in 2023 and is built for music rather than sport. Seating rises steeply in a fan shape facing one end stage, so even the upper tiers look straight at the stage. Capacity is around 20,000. That put these dates among the larger rooms Maiden have booked in Japan.

## Getting to Minato Mirai

The arena stands on the Yokohama waterfront, between Yokohama Station and the Minato Mirai towers. The options:

- **Shin-Takashima Station** (Minatomirai Line): the closest stop, a few minutes on foot.
- **Minatomirai Station** (Minatomirai Line): a slightly longer walk past the Yokohama Landmark Tower.
- **Yokohama Station** (JR, Tokyu, Keikyu, Sotetsu, subway): roughly 15 minutes on foot along the waterfront walkways.
- **From Tokyo**: the Tokyu Toyoko Line from Shibuya runs straight onto the Minatomirai Line, with no change of train.

After big shows, Shin-Takashima gets packed and staff meter the platforms. The extra 15-minute walk back to Yokohama Station is often faster, and it offers a far wider choice of lines.

## Tickets, entry and the merch line

Big concerts in Japan usually sell through lottery-style presales and then general sale. Tickets are often digital and tied to a named smartphone app. Name or ID checks at the door are common, and unofficial resale is restricted.

Every seat at K Arena is assigned, so queuing for hours brings no advantage. The merchandise line is different. Tour shirts typically sell out by size, and merch lines at Japanese arena shows usually form well ahead of doors.

## Gig etiquette, Japanese-style

Japanese metal crowds are loud during songs and almost silent between them. Fans shout along with "Run to the Hills" and stay in their seat areas. Moving into the aisles draws a polite but firm word from staff.

- **Photography**: arenas in Japan generally ban photos and filming unless an announcement says otherwise.
- **Bags**: large bags and suitcases are usually refused. The coin lockers at Yokohama Station are the usual place to leave them.
- **Payment**: merch stands and nearby convenience stores take cards and IC transit cards (Suica, PASMO). A little cash is a useful backup.
- **Rubbish**: bins are rare. Empties are usually carried out and sorted at the station.
- **Timing**: Japanese shows generally start on schedule and finish early enough for the last trains.

For food beforehand, the restaurant floors at Yokohama World Porters and the shops around Yokohama Station have the widest choice. The red-brick warehouses at Akarenga Soko make a good walk afterwards for anyone not catching the last train.
