## Seven years away, two nights back

Jason Mraz has not played Japan in seven years. This leg of his Asia tour fixes that with two dates, November 10-11, 2026. One night is at Tokyo Garden Theater and the other at Festival Hall in Osaka.

The announcement gives the pair of dates together without saying which city gets which night. Check the official tour page before you book a hotel or a shinkansen seat. It is a short run, so fans who want both shows will be moving between cities with little time to spare.

## Inside Tokyo Garden Theater

The venue is a modern indoor hall built for concerts. It sits inside Ariake Garden, a shopping and hotel complex on reclaimed land on Tokyo Bay, and opened in 2020. Capacity is around 8,000.

The seating is what you will notice. Tiers climb steeply on three sides of the stage, so the upper levels sit closer to the performer than you would expect in a hall this size. That suits a show built on an acoustic guitar and songs like "I'm Yours", "Lucky" and "I Won't Give Up". A seat high up still feels close.

## Reaching Ariake on a show night

Ariake is on the bay side of Koto ward, past Odaiba. It is easy to reach but has fewer train lines than central Tokyo. You have two main routes:

- **Yurikamome line:** get off at Ariake-tennis-no-mori Station, the closest stop. The driverless train runs from Shimbashi, crosses the Rainbow Bridge and gives you a view of the bay on the way.
- **Rinkai Line:** get off at Kokusai-Tenjijo Station, a slightly longer walk. Some Rinkai trains run through from the JR Saikyo Line, which helps if you are coming from Shinjuku or Shibuya.
- **From Toyosu:** the Yurikamome also runs to Toyosu, so you can reach Ariake from the Yurakucho Line that way.

After the encore, thousands of people leave at once for the same few platforms. Expect queues at the Yurikamome. If you are not in a hurry, eat in Ariake Garden first and let the crowd clear.

## Tickets the Japanese way

Concert ticketing in Japan works differently from most countries, and it trips up a lot of visitors. Many big shows sell through lotteries (抽選, chūsen) on platforms such as e+, Ticket Pia and Lawson Ticket. Only some seats go on a straight first-come basis.

What to know:

- **Use official channels only.** Follow the links on the artist's or promoter's tour page. Confirm dates, venue and ticket options there.
- **Expect names and IDs.** Japanese tickets are often issued in the buyer's name, and staff may check ID at the door. Carry your passport.
- **Digital tickets** usually live in a ticketing app on your phone. Install it and log in before the day, while you have time to sort out problems.
- **Skip unofficial resale.** Resold tickets can be refused at the door. If a show offers an official resale system, that is the safe way to buy a spare.

## Concert etiquette in Ariake

Japanese audiences are attentive, and visitors find that the most surprising part. People clap and sing along on the big choruses, then go quiet for the slower songs. If you talk during a ballad, people will notice.

Filming and photography are often banned at Japanese concerts. Read the venue notices and the rules printed with your ticket before you raise your phone. Staff will enforce them politely but firmly.

Merchandise sales usually open well before doors, and fans queue in lines that staff keep organised. If you want a tour shirt, go to the merch line first. Coin lockers fill up early on show days, so pack light.

## Before and after the show

Arrive early and Ariake gives you plenty to fill the afternoon. Ariake Garden has restaurants and shops under the same roof as the theater, which makes it the easiest place for dinner. Tokyo Big Sight, the exhibition centre with the upside-down pyramids, is a short walk away. Odaiba's waterfront is a few Yurikamome stops back towards the city.

If your tickets are for Osaka, Festival Hall stands in Nakanoshima, the river island in central Osaka. Its nearest stations are Higobashi on the Yotsubashi Line and Watanabebashi on the Keihan Nakanoshima Line. The hall is known for its acoustics and its red-carpeted grand staircase. Confirm timing and tickets for both cities on the official site.
