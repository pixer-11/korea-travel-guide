## December 27, a port city on foot

On a race morning, traffic gives way. Hai Phong is a city of motorbikes, container lorries and wide French-era boulevards. Then for one Sunday-length stretch the roads belong to runners.

VnExpress Marathon Hai Phong takes place on December 27, 2026. It belongs to the VnExpress Marathon series, a set of road races run by VnExpress, the Vietnamese online newspaper. The series stops in different cities across the country through the year, and this is the Hai Phong edition.

## Four distances, one start line culture

The race offers four distances, so a mixed group of friends can all enter:

- **Marathon (42.195km):** the full distance, for runners who have trained through the autumn.
- **Half-marathon (21.1km):** usually the most popular distance at Vietnamese road races.
- **10km:** a step up for newer runners, or a fast effort for experienced ones.
- **5km:** the entry point, often run by families and first-timers.

Longer distances at Vietnamese road races usually start in the dark, before sunrise. That timing keeps runners off the road before the city's traffic picks up. The shorter races then go off in waves after the longer ones.

## Where the venue, course and entries live

The organiser publishes the dates, the start and finish venue, the course map and the entry links on its official VnExpress Marathon site. Race "tickets" here are race entries: you register for a distance, not buy a seat. Entry prices and any category limits appear on that same registration page, and this guide gives no figures.

Spectators need no ticket. Anyone can stand along the open road course. Most supporters gather near the finish area, where runners bunch up and the finisher photos get taken.

## A northern winter morning

December catches visitors from the south off guard. Hai Phong sits in northern Vietnam, which has a real winter. Late-December mornings here are typically cool, grey and damp rather than tropical. A light drizzle the Vietnamese call *mưa phùn* is common through the season.

For runners, that is mostly good news. Cool air suits a marathon far better than the heat of Ho Chi Minh City races. Visitors from warm climates often bring a light layer for the wait at the start, then shed it before the gun. Supporters on the pavement for several hours tend to dress for the chill.

The famous red flamboyant trees that give Hai Phong its nickname, "the city of red flame flowers", bloom in early summer. In December their branches are bare or green, so the course looks quieter than the postcards.

## Getting to Hai Phong

Hai Phong is about 120km east of Hanoi, and most international runners route through the capital. There are three main ways in:

- **By road:** the Hanoi–Hai Phong expressway makes the drive roughly one and a half to two hours. Many runners share a car or take one of the frequent intercity buses and limousine vans.
- **By train:** trains run from Hanoi to Hai Phong Station, close to the old city centre. It is slower than the expressway but delivers you straight into town.
- **By air:** Cat Bi International Airport, on the city's southeastern side, has domestic flights from Ho Chi Minh City, Da Nang and other cities.

Inside the city, ride-hailing apps such as Grab are the easy way to move between hotel, bib collection and the start. Runners usually stay within walking or short-ride distance of the start, since pre-dawn race mornings leave little room for transport.

## Bánh đa cua after the finish line

Hai Phong's signature breakfast is the natural reward. *Bánh đa cua* is a crab noodle soup made with flat, reddish-brown rice noodles. The broth is built on pounded field crab, and the bowl comes topped with greens, fried tofu and often pork. Locals eat it in the morning, which fits a finisher's appetite perfectly.

Two other local snacks are worth finding during race weekend:

- **Bánh mì que:** a thin, stick-shaped baguette with pâté and chilli sauce, small enough to eat while walking.
- **Nem cua bể:** square spring rolls filled with sea crab, a Hai Phong speciality usually shared at the table.

Many visitors stretch the trip to the coast. Do Son beach lies south of the city. Cat Ba Island, on the edge of Lan Ha Bay, is reachable by road and ferry, though December is out of beach season.

## How race weekend works in Hai Phong

Bib collection is the first fixed point. Races in the VnExpress Marathon series typically hand out bibs and race kits at a collection point in the days before race day, with photo ID required. The exact location and hours sit on the organiser's site.

On race morning, runners usually arrive by Grab or on foot while the streets are still dark. Crowds collect at the start corrals by distance, so the longest races line up first.

Paying for things in Hai Phong is easy if you are ready for it:

- Street-food stalls and small cafés mostly take cash in Vietnamese dong.
- Many vendors also accept bank QR transfers, which locals use constantly, but foreign cards rarely work on them.
- Tipping at noodle stalls is not expected.

Along the course, supporters shout *"Cố lên!"* ("keep going!"). Shouting it back at a volunteer or a stranger with a sign usually earns a grin. After the race, finishers in their medals spread across the city's breakfast spots and cafés. The post-race coffee is often *cà phê sữa đá*, iced milk coffee, even in winter.

## Making a weekend of it

Plenty of runners give the city a day before or after the race. The Hai Phong Opera House, a French colonial building from the early 1900s, anchors the old centre. Tam Bac Lake and the riverside streets nearby are pleasant for an easy shakeout walk.

The pace is gentler than Hanoi's, and the food is the main event between races. Hai Phong rewards a slow loop of noodle shops and coffee stops on the day your legs refuse to do anything else.
