## A Tuesday night above the Bosphorus

The date is a weeknight in late November. On Tuesday, November 24, 2026, Caribou plays Zorlu Center, the glass-and-steel complex where Büyükdere Caddesi meets the road up to the Bosphorus Bridge. It takes the evening out of the old-city tourist circuit and drops it among Istanbul's office towers.

Expect a typical late-autumn Istanbul evening: cool, often damp, and you'll want a proper coat. Inside, the music is warm, layered and built for dancing. That contrast suits a Caribou show.

## What Caribou sounds like in a room

Caribou is the project of Dan Snaith, the Canadian producer from Dundas, Ontario. He also releases club records as Daphni. Over almost two decades the project has moved from the sun-bleached psychedelia of *Andorra*, which won the Polaris Music Prize, to the dance-floor records that made it a festival name.

Live, Caribou is a band rather than one man behind a laptop. Snaith has long toured with a four-piece line-up, with a live drummer driving the beat under the synths. The material a set can draw on includes:

- *Swim* (2010), home of "Odessa" and "Sun"
- *Our Love* (2014), with "Can't Do Without You"
- *Suddenly* (2020), with "Home" and "Never Come Back"
- *Honey* (2024), the most club-facing Caribou album so far

No setlist is fixed here. A show built on that catalogue tends to start in headphone-listening territory and finish with the crowd jumping.

## Zorlu Center and its stages

Zorlu Center opened in 2013. It combines a shopping mall, residences, a hotel and Zorlu PSM, the performing arts centre that hosts most of the complex's concerts. You enter through the mall levels, so the walk to the hall takes you past shopfronts and escalators before you reach the venue corridors.

Because this is a mall-and-arts complex rather than a standalone club, the basics are nearby. Restaurants and cafés sit inside the building, toilets are plentiful, and there is underground parking. When the weather turns, you can stay indoors from the metro exit to the hall.

## Getting to Zincirlikuyu on a weeknight

Rail is the sensible choice. Büyükdere Caddesi and the bridge approach are some of the most congested roads in the city at evening rush hour. A Tuesday show lands right in it.

- **M2 metro to Gayrettepe:** the green line running from Taksim through Şişli to Levent. Zorlu Center is a short walk from the station.
- **M11 from Istanbul Airport:** this line ends at Gayrettepe, so anyone flying in on the day can ride the train almost to the door.
- **Metrobüs to Zincirlikuyu:** the rapid bus line along the O-1 highway, useful from the Asian side via the Bosphorus Bridge.
- **From Sabiha Gökçen or Kadıköy:** most people use the Metrobüs or the Marmaray and then switch to the M2.

All of these take the Istanbulkart, the city's reloadable transit card. You can also tap a contactless bank card at many gates.

## Doors, coats and the Zorlu routine

Concert-going at Zorlu follows a familiar pattern. A ticket is a *bilet*, and door opening is *kapı açılış*. Many attendees eat in the building first and then queue at the hall entrance. Bags go through a security check at the mall entrances and again at the venue, so people usually travel light.

- **Payment:** cards and contactless work almost everywhere in the complex. Cash in Turkish lira is handy for taxis and late snacks outside.
- **Coats:** a cloakroom (*vestiyer*) is standard at Turkish venues in winter. People tend to use it rather than dance in a wool coat.
- **Smoking:** it is banned indoors across Turkey, so smokers go outside between sets.
- **Tipping:** in restaurants around ten percent is usual. At bars, people often round up.

The organiser publishes the ticket links, door times and age rules on its official site. That is where the ticket conditions for this show live.

## The last-train question

A Tuesday show means a Tuesday ride home. On weeknights Istanbul's metro does not run through the night; the all-night service runs only at weekends. When a set runs late, the crowd heading for the M2 tends to move fast once the lights come up.

If you miss the last train, take a taxi. Locals usually order one through the BiTaksi app, and Uber also operates in the city. Both are easier than flagging a cab on the bridge approach. Many visitors sidestep the problem by staying in Şişli, Levent or Beşiktaş, where the ride back is short.

## Before and after: Beşiktaş and Ortaköy

Zorlu sits above two of the best evening districts on the European shore. Downhill to the east, Ortaköy has the Ortaköy Mosque on the water, right under the Bosphorus Bridge. Its square is lined with stalls selling *kumpir*, the stuffed baked potato the neighbourhood is known for.

South along the shore is the Beşiktaş çarşı, a tight grid of meyhanes, cheap breakfast places and student bars around the fish market. It is the obvious place for a pre-show dinner or a post-show drink. A taxi from Zorlu covers either in a few minutes when traffic allows.

Anyone with a free afternoon can fit in Dolmabahçe Palace, on the shore road between Beşiktaş and Kabataş.
