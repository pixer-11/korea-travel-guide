## Glass, steel and a forest indoors

Look for the huge blade-like roof. It rises over the northern edge of Carlton Gardens and faces the domed Royal Exhibition Building across the forecourt. The museum dates back to 1854, but this building opened in 2000. The contrast is deliberate: steel and glass one side, 1880 brick and dome on the other.

Inside, the spaces are big and easy to navigate. The Forest Gallery is a living, glass-roofed stand of Victorian forest with real trees and running water, right in the middle of the building. Few museums have one, so go there first.

## What to see, in order of pull

The museum is popular, and a few galleries draw most of the crowds. Plan your route around these:

- **Dinosaur Walk**: full skeletons hung at eye level and overhead, the reason most families come.
- **Phar Lap**: the mounted hide of the 1930s racehorse, still the museum's most famous single object.
- **Bunjilaka Aboriginal Cultural Centre**: First Peoples galleries built with Victoria's Aboriginal communities. Give them real time.
- **The Melbourne Story**: the city's history from the gold rush onward, full of everyday objects.
- **Bugs Alive!**: live insects and spiders behind glass.

Plan on two to three hours. Add another hour if you're booking a film at the IMAX cinema in the same complex.

## Timing the weekend crush

Weekdays are the easy option: the museum stays quiet from 9am to 5pm. Weekends are different. The calm window runs from 9am to 10am, and from 11am to 5pm the busy stretch fills the dinosaur hall and the children's areas.

If Saturday or Sunday is your only chance, be at the door at the 9am opening. Head to Dinosaur Walk while it's clear. Save Bunjilaka and the Melbourne Story for later, since the crowds thin out in those quieter galleries.

Hours are the same every day, 9am to 5pm, with no closing day. Last entry comes before 5pm, so an afternoon arrival leaves you rushing.

## Getting in and around Carlton Gardens

The address is 11 Nicholson St, Carlton. Trams 86 and 96 run along Nicholson Street and stop right by the entrance. From Parliament Station it's roughly a ten-minute walk north through the gardens.

A few habits make the visit easier:

- **Book online.** Buy tickets on the museum's official website, where current prices are posted. You'll skip the ticket counter at the peak weekend hours.
- **Card is normal.** Contactless payment is standard at the desk and the café, and tipping isn't expected.
- **Use the gardens.** Locals bring food out onto the Carlton Gardens lawns rather than queueing indoors at lunch.
- **Pair it up.** The Royal Exhibition Building is next door, and Lygon Street's Italian cafés are a short walk west for coffee afterwards.

Treat the museum as half a day, not a quick stop. The Forest Gallery and Bunjilaka reward slow visitors, and people who rush through at 3pm miss most of what makes the place worth coming to.
