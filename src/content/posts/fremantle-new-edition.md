## A bookshop on Fremantle's oldest shopping street

High Street is a good street for a bookshop. It runs west through Fremantle's West End toward Arthur Head. Most of the facades along it date from the 1890s gold-rush boom: limestone and render, iron-lace verandahs, upper-floor windows with the old business names still painted on them.

New Edition sits at number 41, partway down that stretch. It's an independent bookshop, and that tells you how to treat it. This is a place for slow browsing, not a chain store with a bestseller wall at the door.

It's also easy to walk past. Most visitors heading along High Street are aiming for the Round House at the far end, or cutting across to the markets. Plenty of them never step inside. That's a real reason to go, because the shop is very well rated by the people who do find it.

## Finding 41 High Street

The simplest way here from Perth is the train.

- **By train:** take the Fremantle line to its last stop, Fremantle station. From the station it's an easy walk of under 15 minutes south and west into the West End grid.
- **By bus:** Fremantle's free CAT bus loops the town centre and stops near High Street. It's handy if you're coming from South Terrace or the Esplanade.
- **By car:** drive in and use one of the paid town-centre car parks. Street parking in the West End is short-stay and closely watched, so check the signs before you leave the car.

As you walk west along High Street, the numbers climb toward the ocean end. If you reach the steps up to the Round House, you've gone past it. Turn round and walk back.

## Nine o'clock on High Street

The opening hours are this shop's quirk. At the moment New Edition opens 9am–9pm, seven days a week. For a bookshop in a heritage shopping district, those are long hours. Many shops nearby close in the late afternoon, and a 9pm close on a Monday or Tuesday is unusual.

That changes how you can use it. You don't have to squeeze it into a sightseeing day. Have fish and chips by the harbour or eat on South Terrace, then walk up to High Street afterwards.

Once the day-trippers have gone back to Perth, the West End empties out. A lit shop window and a few shelves are a good way to end the evening. Hours can change, so check the current listing before planning a visit after dark.

## Browsing without hovering

An independent bookshop runs on a few unwritten rules. Following them makes the visit better for everyone.

- **Pay by card or phone.** Tap-and-pay is normal in Australian shops, even for small purchases. Cash is generally fine too.
- **Don't tip.** Tipping isn't part of Australian retail.
- **Ask the person at the counter.** Staff at an independent shop usually know the stock in a way a search box doesn't. If you're after a particular title, ask whether they can order it in.
- **Ask for something local.** If you're travelling, ask what they'd suggest by Western Australian writers. It's a better souvenir than anything on a market stall.
- **Handle books gently.** Don't crack spines, and leave your coffee cup outside.
- **Ask before photographing.** Small shops can be particular about cameras, so check first.

Bags and big backpacks get awkward between shelves. If you've come straight from the markets with shopping, hold it in front of you as you squeeze past.

## Building a West End afternoon around it

New Edition works best as one stop on a walk. Everything worth seeing in the West End is within ten minutes on foot.

- **The Round House:** at the western end of High Street. Built in 1831, it's the oldest surviving public building in Western Australia, looking out over Bathers Beach.
- **Bathers Beach:** just below Arthur Head. It's a small stretch of sand that faces west, so it's a good place to watch the sunset.
- **WA Shipwrecks Museum:** on Cliff Street, close by. It holds the remains of the Dutch ship *Batavia*.
- **Fremantle Markets:** on Henderson Street, a short walk east. Check which days they're trading before you go.
- **South Terrace:** the café strip. Go for coffee before you browse or dinner afterwards.
- **Fremantle Prison:** up the hill on the east side of town. Its tours are popular, so book ahead.

One easy route: start at the Round House, walk east along High Street and stop at number 41. Then carry on toward the markets and South Terrace. The late close means you can also do it the other way round and finish with books.

## When to come, and how long to stay

There's no published foot-traffic forecast for this shop, so plan around the town's own rhythm instead. Fremantle fills up on weekends, especially on market days. Weekday mornings around High Street are generally calmer than Saturday afternoons, and the evening hours give you an easy alternative to the daytime crowds.

Give it 30 minutes if you're just looking around. Allow an hour if you browse the way most book-lovers do, one spine leading to another. Unlike the beach and the Round House lookout, the shop is indoors, so it's a good shelter in Perth's winter rain or a hot summer afternoon.

## Before you set out

Check the current hours before a late visit, as small shops adjust their schedules.
