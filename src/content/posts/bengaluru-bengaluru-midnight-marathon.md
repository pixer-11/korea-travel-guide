## A race that starts when the traffic stops

Bengaluru's roads are usually jammed, which is what makes a night race here unusual. The Bengaluru Midnight Marathon is an annual night road race in Karnataka's capital, and the 2026 edition takes place in early December. Distances range from a 5K to the full 42.2km marathon.

That range shapes the start area. People running their first 5K share the streets with marathoners chasing a time. Running at night means the course gets streets that are usually packed with cars and two-wheelers by day.

## Dates, distances and registration

The basics for this edition:

- **When:** early December 2026, overnight
- **Distances:** from 5K up to the full marathon
- **Where it happens:** on Bengaluru's city roads, with the start venue and route set for each edition
- **Registration and fees:** on the event's official registration page

The organiser publishes the exact date, start venue, race categories and registration links on its official site. Start points can move between editions, so the official race page is the reliable source for the venue rather than older race reports. Entry fees are listed there too, beside each category.

## What a December night on the course is like

Bengaluru sits about 900m above sea level. By December the northeast monsoon has usually ended, so nights are typically dry and cool, often in the mid-teens Celsius. Runners from Chennai or Mumbai find it much less humid than home.

That cool air helps on the long distances. Standing still in the start corral can feel cold, though. Many runners wear an old layer to the start and take it off before the gun. Reflective strips and a headlamp or clip light are common, because some stretches of road are lit more brightly than others.

## How race night usually runs

Night logistics matter here more than the course does. A few patterns hold for most runners:

- **Getting there:** Namma Metro doesn't run all night, so most runners reach the start and get home by app cab (Ola, Uber, Namma Yatri) or by car. Many groups share a car both ways, because cab demand jumps when the race ends.
- **Food and sleep:** runners usually eat an early, plain dinner and nap in the evening. Bengaluru's darshinis (stand-up South Indian canteens) are a common pick for idli or plain dosa.
- **Paying:** UPI is the default across the city for cabs, food stalls and post-race chai. Cards work in most restaurants.
- **Spectators:** friends and family tend to gather near the finish, not along the course. Late at night they are spread thin, so runners often agree on a meeting point beforehand.
- **Bibs and rules:** bib collection details and race rules sit on the official site alongside the category information.

Runners coming from outside the city usually book a room close to the start venue. Then the ride before the race and the ride back to bed are both short, which matters when the finish comes after midnight.
