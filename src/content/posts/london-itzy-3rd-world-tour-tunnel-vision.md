## Inside the old Empire Pool

The building is older than it looks. OVO Arena Wembley opened in 1934 as the Empire Pool, built for the British Empire Games. It also hosted the swimming events at the 1948 London Olympics. Today it is an indoor concert arena holding around 12,500 people.

The layout is simple. A standing floor sits in front of the stage, with tiered seating rising around it in numbered blocks. Floor tickets get you closest to the members. The upper tiers trade distance for the full view: the staging, the screens and the whole room lit up by lightsticks.

The arena is cashless. Food, drink and merchandise stands take card and mobile payment only. The venue's own website is where its bag rules and accessibility information are kept.

## Getting to Wembley Park

Three stations serve the arena, and most attendees come by rail rather than car.

- **Wembley Park** (Jubilee and Metropolitan lines): the closest. You walk down Olympic Way, the wide pedestrian boulevard toward Wembley Stadium, in about ten minutes.
- **Wembley Central** (Bakerloo line, London Overground and National Rail): roughly fifteen minutes on foot along the High Road.
- **Wembley Stadium station** (Chiltern Railways from London Marylebone): a short walk from the stadium side of the complex.

From central London, the Metropolitan line runs fast from Baker Street to Wembley Park. The Jubilee line from Bond Street or Westminster is slower but very frequent.

Driving is the hard option. Event-day parking restrictions cover many of the residential streets around Wembley. At the end of the night, stewards often hold crowds in queue lanes outside Wembley Park station to stop the platforms overfilling. Attendees usually shuffle forward in those lanes for a while before they reach the trains.

## Before and after, around Wembley Park

The area around the arena is built for event crowds. **Boxpark Wembley** is right beside the arena: a food-and-drink hall made of shipping containers, with street food stalls and long shared tables. The **London Designer Outlet**, a few minutes' walk away, has more restaurants and a cinema.

At the top of Olympic Way stands Wembley Stadium, with its arch and the bronze statue of Bobby Moore outside. Fans gather there for group photos before heading down to the arena.

Fans travelling from outside London often stay in one of the chain hotels clustered around Wembley Park. That way the walk home replaces the post-show Tube queue. Fans staying centrally usually ride the Metropolitan line back toward Baker Street, where they can change to other lines.
