## Through the Danby Gateway

The garden opens with a stone arch. The Danby Gateway, finished in the early 1630s, frames the entrance to a walled garden laid out in 1621 as a physic garden. It grew medicinal plants for the University of Oxford's teaching. That makes it Britain's oldest botanic garden, and the original grid of rectangular beds inside the walls still shows its logic.

Magdalen College tower rises just across Rose Lane. The River Cherwell runs along the far edge of the garden. Inside, you'll find:

- **The Walled Garden:** family order beds, where plants are grouped by botanical relationship rather than looks.
- **The glasshouses:** a run of linked houses covering tropical, arid and water-plant collections, including giant water lilies and carnivorous plants.
- **The Lower Garden and riverside:** herbaceous borders, a rock garden and views onto the Cherwell.

## Literary ground

A lot of readers come looking for one bench. At the end of Philip Pullman's *The Amber Spyglass*, Lyra and Will promise to sit on a bench in this garden, each in their own world, once a year. Fans still leave notes on it.

Tolkien also walked here. His favourite tree, a black pine, stood in the garden until it was felled in 2014 after branches failed. Ask the staff where it stood and they can point you to the spot.

## Timing and getting there

It's open every day from 10am to 5pm, with no shorter day in the week. The quietest hour, weekday or weekend, is **10am–11am**. Avoid weekend afternoons from **12pm to 5pm** if you can: that's when the glasshouses fill and the narrow paths between beds slow to single file.

An unhurried loop takes 60 to 90 minutes, or longer if you read the labels. Getting there:

- **On foot:** about 25–30 minutes from Oxford railway station, straight along Queen Street and the High Street.
- **By bus:** city services run along the High Street and stop near Magdalen College.
- **By car:** central Oxford parking is scarce, so use one of the Park & Ride sites and bus in.

Spring bulbs and the high-summer borders give the outdoor beds their showiest months. The glasshouses make a wet or cold day worth the trip too.

## Walking the beds properly

This is a working scientific collection, and the house rules follow from that. Stay on the paths and off the beds. Don't take cuttings or seeds. Dogs aren't allowed, apart from assistance dogs.

Check current admission and any timed-entry booking on the official Oxford Botanic Garden website before you go. On busy weekends, a booked slot saves you standing in the Rose Lane queue.

Card payment is standard at the entrance. Glasshouse doors are kept shut to hold the heat and humidity, so close them behind you. The first house often fogs up camera lenses, so give yours a minute before you shoot.

Pair the visit with the neighbours. Punts go out from the boathouse at Magdalen Bridge, a two-minute walk away. Christ Church Meadow opens off Rose Lane opposite, and its riverside path leads down to the Thames. Harcourt Arboretum, the garden's sister site, lies south of the city if you want more trees than the walls can hold.
