## Why go

MAUTO isn't a shed of shiny cars — it's a design museum that happens to use automobiles as its medium. Turin built the Italian car industry, and this building, opened in 1960 and redone in 2011, tells that story with an eye for staging that rivals any modern art gallery.

You walk in expecting engines and leave thinking about lighting, typography, and the shape of a fender as a cultural artifact.

## What you'll actually see

The collection runs chronologically and thematically across several floors, from 1769 steam carriages up through postwar Italian design icons. Highlights that regulars point to:

- Early Fiats and Lancias tracing Turin's industrial rise
- Bugatti, Isotta Fraschini and other prewar luxury marques
- A dedicated Formula 1 and racing section
- The famous ramp of cars suspended mid-air near the entrance, a favorite photo spot
- Rotating design exhibitions pairing cars with fashion, film, or architecture

Give yourself two to three hours minimum; car enthusiasts often stay longer. There's a café and a design-forward gift shop if you need a break partway through.

## Getting there

The museum sits on Corso Unità d'Italia, on the south bank of the Po, a bit removed from the historic center. Tram line 4 stops close to the entrance, and it's the easiest way in if you're coming from Porta Nuova or the city core.

Drivers will find on-site or street parking nearby, though weekends near the river can get tight. If you're combining it with other sights, it pairs naturally with a longer loop that includes the Parco del Valentino, a pleasant walk or short ride away.

## When to go

MAUTO is genuinely one of Turin's most-visited attractions, and the crowd data backs that up. Weekends between 11am and 6pm are consistently the busiest stretch, with tour groups and families filling the galleries.

For a calmer visit, aim for a weekday morning, specifically the 10am to 11am window right after opening, when the ramp and early galleries are still uncluttered. Note that Monday is a short day — the museum closes at 2pm rather than staying open into the evening. Every other day it runs 10am to 7pm, so a late afternoon visit midweek works well too.

## How to visit like a local

Because this is a heavily visited museum rather than a hidden find, the smart move is timing, not secrecy.

- **Book ahead online.** Skipping the ticket line matters most on weekend afternoons.
- **Go early on a weekday.** The 10–11am slot is measurably the quietest; you'll get the suspended-car ramp mostly to yourself for photos.
- **Avoid Saturday and Sunday 11am–6pm** if you can — that's the peak window locals and repeat visitors know to dodge.
- **Payment is standard card/contactless** at the ticket desk and café, so don't worry about carrying cash.
- **Don't rush the top floor.** Most visitors move fast through the modern design section near the end and miss it — it's where the fashion and film crossover exhibits usually live.
- **Skip the audioguide rush at the entrance** — pick it up, then double back to the first gallery once the entrance crowd has thinned a few minutes in.

The most common mistake is treating this as a quick add-on stop between other sights. The design staging rewards slow walking, and rushing it means missing exactly what makes MAUTO different from a typical transport museum.

## Nearby, if you have time

The riverside setting means you can turn this into a half-day out. Walk north along the Po into Parco del Valentino for greenery and the mock-medieval Borgo Medievale, or continue toward the center for Turin's grand piazzas and cafés. If you're museum-hopping, Turin's Egyptian Museum and the Mole Antonelliana are both easily reachable by tram or a moderate walk, making a car-museum morning and a culture-heavy afternoon an easy combination.
