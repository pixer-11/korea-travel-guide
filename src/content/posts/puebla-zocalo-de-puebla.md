## A square set by the cathedral

Look up first. Puebla Cathedral's twin bell towers fill the south side of the Zócalo, and they're among the tallest in Mexico. The cathedral was consecrated in 1649, and its grey stone sets the tone for the whole plaza.

The middle of the square is laid out in tree-shaded paths and benches. They lead in to the Fountain of San Miguel, with the archangel standing on top. Arcades called *portales* line the other three sides, with cafés and restaurants tucked under their stone arches. The Palacio Municipal sits on the north side. This is the core of a historic centre that UNESCO listed as a World Heritage Site in 1987.

## Hours, and when the crowds come

The square itself is listed as open 24 hours, all week. It's a public plaza with no gate, so you can see the cathedral facade at dawn or after dark.

- **Quietest:** 7am–9am on both weekdays and weekends. This is when you'll get clear photos of the fountain and the towers.
- **Busiest:** weekends from 12pm–11pm. The benches fill and vendors crowd the paths. If you only have a Saturday or Sunday, go early or expect to share the square.

The cathedral sets its own hours, and those change. Check them at the door before you plan a visit inside.

## Sitting under the portales

Weekends draw a lot of local families as well as visitors. These habits will save you some friction:

- **Carry small peso notes and coins.** You'll want them for the balloon sellers, snack carts and shoe-shiners on the square. Card payment is more likely at the sit-down cafés under the arcades.
- **Order a table under the portales.** Coffee or a plate of *chalupas* gets you a seat facing the cathedral. Waiters won't hurry you, so ask for *la cuenta* when you're ready to pay. Tipping table service is the norm in Mexico.
- **Enter the cathedral quietly.** Take your hat off, keep your voice down, and don't use flash during Mass. Shoulders covered is the respectful choice.
- **Watch your phone on weekend afternoons.** That's when the plaza is most packed.

## What's within a few blocks

The Zócalo sits at the centre of a grid of streets, so everything below is walkable.

- **Biblioteca Palafoxiana**: just behind the cathedral. It opened in 1646 and is often called the first public library in the Americas.
- **Templo de Santo Domingo**: a few blocks north along 5 de Mayo. Its Capilla del Rosario is covered floor to ceiling in gilded plaster.
- **Calle de los Dulces (6 Oriente)**: shops selling *camotes* and other traditional Puebla sweets.
- **Callejón de los Sapos and Barrio del Artista**: east of the square, with antiques, Talavera pottery and painters' studios.

To eat local, look for *mole poblano* and *cemitas* (sesame-seed sandwiches). From August into September, look for *chiles en nogada* too.

From Mexico City, buses to Puebla take about two hours and arrive at the CAPU terminal. A taxi or app ride gets you from there to the centro. Give the square and the cathedral about an hour. Plan half a day if you want to take in the nearby blocks as well.
