## A park below the traffic

Merab Kostava Street is one of Tbilisi's loudest roads. Vere Park takes its name from the Vere river valley, which cuts through this part of the city, and it gives you a break from that noise. Under the trees the sound of the street fades, and the city's hills rise up around you.

It's a public city park and not a monument, which is why it's worth an hour. You come here to slow down between sights: a bench, a loop of the paths, somewhere for children to burn off energy. It's well known and well reviewed in Tbilisi, so don't expect to have it to yourself at peak times.

## Getting to Merab Kostava Street

The park sits along Merab Kostava Street. The street runs northwest from the Rustaveli Avenue end of the centre towards Heroes' Square.

- **By metro:** Rustaveli Station on Line 1 is the most practical stop. From there it's a walk along Kostava Street, which climbs a little as you go.
- **By taxi:** Bolt and Yandex Go both work across Tbilisi. Type in "Vere Park" or the plus code PQ5Q+G59 so the driver drops you on the right side of a busy road.
- **On foot:** If you're already on Rustaveli Avenue, walking is often quicker than a taxi in weekday traffic.

Kostava carries heavy traffic in both directions. Use the marked crossings instead of dashing across, as plenty of drivers in Tbilisi don't slow down for pedestrians.

## Timing your visit

It's quiet first thing. Between 7am and 10am, on weekdays and weekends alike, the park is at its calmest. That's the window for a slow walk, a run, or coffee on a bench before the city's sightseeing traffic starts.

Weekend afternoons are a different place. From 1pm to 11pm on Saturdays and Sundays the busiest period runs straight into the night: families, groups of friends, strollers on every path. If you want quiet, avoid that block. If you'd rather watch how Tbilisi spends a weekend evening outdoors, that's exactly when to come.

Summer in Tbilisi gets hot, and a shaded park in the early morning is far more pleasant than the same paths in the afternoon heat. Spring and autumn are milder at any hour. The park doesn't publish opening hours we can confirm, so check locally before planning a late visit.

## Park manners in Tbilisi

A few habits make the visit easier.

- **Greet people in Georgian.** A simple "gamarjoba" (hello) and "madloba" (thank you) goes a long way with kiosk staff or anyone you ask for directions.
- **Carry some lari in small notes.** Cards are widely accepted in central Tbilisi, but small vendors around parks often prefer cash.
- **Give way on the paths.** On weekend evenings the walkways fill with prams and small children. Joggers keep to the edges.
- **Don't count on tipping rules here.** Tipping applies in cafés and restaurants, where a service charge is often already added to the bill. Nobody expects a tip at a snack stand.
- **Photograph people with care.** Families come here with their children. Ask before you point a camera at a group.

Plan on 45 minutes to an hour and a half. That covers a full loop, a sit-down and a drink, without feeling rushed.

## What's around Vere Park

The park works best as a pause in a longer walk across central Tbilisi.

- **Rustaveli Avenue:** The city's grand boulevard, lined with the Georgian National Museum, the Opera and Ballet Theatre and the old Parliament building.
- **Heroes' Square:** The big roundabout at the far end of Kostava, with its memorial to Georgians who died fighting for the country.
- **Tbilisi Zoo:** Close to Heroes' Square, also in the Vere valley. The 2015 flood that tore through the valley badly damaged it.
- **Vake Park:** A bigger, more formal park a short taxi ride west, if you want a second green space the same day.

A good half-day loop: go down Rustaveli Avenue in the morning, cut up Kostava to Vere Park for a rest, then carry on towards Heroes' Square.

## Who it suits

Families travelling with young children will get the most out of it. The park gives them space to run that the Old Town's steep cobbled lanes don't. Runners staying in the Vake or Saburtalo direction can fit an early loop in during the quiet morning window.

If you only have a day in Tbilisi, put the Old Town, Narikala Fortress and the sulphur baths first. On a longer stay, Vere Park is where you see the residential side of the city outdoors, away from the tour routes.
