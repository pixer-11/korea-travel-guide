Chijmes is one of those Singapore addresses that used to be a convent and school, all Gothic arches and a chapel building, now ringed with restaurants and bars behind Victoria Street. Sabòr Cocina Y Tapas has taken up residence in that courtyard, and the setting does a lot of the work before the food even arrives.

## What Sabòr actually is

This is a Spanish tapas restaurant, not a tourist-menu paella house. It's from the team behind Duomo Ristorante, so there's an established restaurant group's hand on the operation rather than a first-time venture.

The kitchen sticks to classic tapas and traditional Spanish dishes: think small plates meant for the table, not individual courses. With around 100 seats split between an air-conditioned interior and the open courtyard, it's built for groups and communal-style eating rather than a solo lunch at the bar.

## The Chijmes setting

Chijmes sits a short walk from City Hall and Bras Basah MRT stations, tucked between Victoria Street and North Bridge Road in the Civic District. The complex's centerpiece is the old chapel hall with its stained glass and buttresses, floodlit at night, and Sabòr's alfresco tables look straight onto that courtyard.

That makes it a genuinely different room depending on the hour: bright and touristy at lunch, atmospheric once the lights come up in the evening. If ambience matters to you, ask for the courtyard rather than the indoor section.

## Why it's drawing attention

New Spanish restaurants aren't rare in Singapore, but a 100-seat tapas operation from an established Italian restaurant team, planted in one of the city's most photographed courtyards, is the kind of combination that gets talked about quickly. It's being discussed as one of the more buzzy openings in the Civic District right now.

That popularity cuts both ways for a visitor. The room looks the part, but a trending new opening in a landmark courtyard fills up fast, especially on weekend evenings.

## Getting a table without the wait

Because this is a recently-opened, in-demand spot, treat it like a reservation-first restaurant rather than a walk-in.

- Book ahead, especially for Friday or Saturday dinner
- If you want the courtyard seats specifically, mention it when reserving
- Call or check the restaurant's own listing for current opening hours before setting off, since these can shift in a venue's first months
- Arriving earlier in the evening, before the main dinner rush builds, is generally the easier way to land a good table without a firm booking

## Eating tapas-style without the awkward pauses

Spanish tapas dining works on shared timing, not individual courses arriving separately.

- Order in rounds rather than all at once, so plates come out at a manageable pace for the table
- Split dishes across the group instead of ordering one each; that's the point of tapas
- Let the table decide on cold plates first, then move to anything cooked to order
- If you're a party of two, ask staff how many plates they'd suggest, since portioning for a full table looks different from portioning for a couple

## Making it part of a Civic District evening

Sabòr's location means it pairs easily with a walk through the rest of Chijmes, past Raffles City and the Esplanade waterfront, or a stop at St. Andrew's Cathedral just across the road. Treat dinner here as one stop on an evening loop through the district rather than a standalone destination, and the courtyard setting will feel earned rather than incidental.
