## Two nights of jazz on the harbour edge

The festival was set for two November nights, Friday 13 and Saturday 14 November 2026, across the West Kowloon Cultural District. Cathay • WestK Jazz Fest is an annual fixture. Its name pairs Cathay, Hong Kong's home airline group, with "WestK", the district's own short name.

Most of the setting is about where it is. West Kowloon is built on reclaimed land that runs out into Victoria Harbour, so the water and the Hong Kong Island skyline sit in front of you. The International Commerce Centre rises behind. It feels quite different from a club gig in a basement in Central.

## Where West Kowloon sits

The district fills the western point of the Kowloon peninsula. It runs from the edge of Tsim Sha Tsui out to the harbour. It was planned as a single arts quarter, and between the buildings there are wide lawns, paved plazas and a long waterfront promenade.

These are the landmarks people use to find their way:

- **M+**, the museum of visual culture, with its tall, slim tower facing the harbour
- **Hong Kong Palace Museum**, near the western end of the district
- **Xiqu Centre**, the Cantonese opera house, on the corner closest to Austin Road and Jordan
- **Art Park**, the big green space along the water
- **Freespace**, the district's contemporary performance venue, at the edge of the park

Distances inside the district are longer than they look on a map. A walk from Xiqu Centre to the far end of Art Park takes a while, so the MTR exit you use makes a real difference.

## Getting there by MTR

A taxi isn't needed here. Three MTR stations serve the district, and the best one depends on which end you are heading for.

- **Kowloon Station** (Tung Chung Line and Airport Express) is under the Elements mall and the ICC. It is the natural stop for M+ and the middle of the district, with signs pointing across.
- **Austin Station** (Tuen Ma Line) is closest to Xiqu Centre and the eastern side.
- **Tsim Sha Tsui Station** (Tsuen Wan Line) and **East Tsim Sha Tsui** (Tuen Ma Line) are further away. From there, people often walk along Canton Road or Austin Road West.

The Airport Express calls at Kowloon Station, so visitors flying in can reach the harbourfront without changing trains. Tsim Sha Tsui and Jordan, both within walking distance of the venue, are popular bases for visitors.

## Tickets and the programme

West Kowloon Cultural District organised the festival's programme, the stage locations and the ticketing. The programme set out which acts were billed for which night and whether each show was ticketed or free to walk into.

Festivals built around a whole arts district often mix ticketed concerts with open-air sets. At events like this, the venue name in a listing matters as much as the act, because stages can sit far apart.

## The skyline as a backdrop

This is the part that makes the festival hard to copy elsewhere. From the West Kowloon waterfront you look straight across Victoria Harbour at Central and Admiralty. Two Taipei-style towers aren't the point here. The point is the whole wall of Hong Kong Island lit up, with the Peak ridge dark behind it.

Mid-November usually falls in Hong Kong's dry autumn season. Evenings are generally mild and the humidity of summer has gone, which is why so many outdoor events in the city cluster in this month. The promenade gets steady harbour wind after dark, so regulars tend to bring a light layer. For lawn sessions, they bring something to sit on too.

## Moving around the district like a regular

A few local habits make an evening in the district easier.

- **Pay with Octopus or contactless.** The Octopus card works on the MTR, at convenience stores and at many food outlets. Most MTR gates also take contactless bank cards and phones.
- **Don't worry about tipping.** It isn't customary at food stalls or counters. Sit-down restaurants in Hong Kong usually add a 10% service charge to the bill.
- **Follow the queue lines.** Hong Kong audiences queue in neat single files. Where there are barriers or staff with signs, people stick to them.
- **Keep the lawn quiet.** At open-air sets, the people on the grass are there to listen. Long conversations usually move to the back or out onto the promenade.
- **Use the mall as your shortcut.** Elements, above Kowloon Station, has toilets, ATMs and indoor walkways. It's a common place to meet before heading out to the harbour.

The MTR is the easy way out of the district after an evening event. Taxis do serve the district, but the last stretch of Austin Road West tends to back up when a large crowd leaves all at once.

## Before and after the music

The district sits close to some of Kowloon's busiest eating streets. Jordan, a short walk east of Austin Station, has cha chaan teng diners and noodle shops. Temple Street Night Market is a little further north for late snacks.

Tsim Sha Tsui is southeast along Canton Road, with Harbour City on the way. Inside the district, M+ and the Hong Kong Palace Museum make a good afternoon before an evening set. Their opening hours change through the year.

The Elements mall has a wide spread of restaurants and a supermarket. That makes it a practical place for a quick meal between the train and the stage.
