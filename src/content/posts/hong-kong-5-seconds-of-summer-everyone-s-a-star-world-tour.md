## The last-train problem

This is the Hong Kong concert issue that catches visitors out. The MTR stops running around midnight and doesn't restart until morning. An encore that runs late, followed by a slow exit from the venue, can leave you at a closed station.

The risk depends on the venue. In Kowloon, missing the last train usually just means a taxi queue and a short ride. AsiaWorld-Expo is different: it's out on Lantau by the airport, and the Airport Express is the easy way back to Kowloon or Central. Look up the last departure from your station on the MTR website or app on the day of the show.

Night buses cover the gaps, and the routes starting with "N" run through the small hours. Red taxis serve the urban areas. Green taxis serve the New Territories and blue ones serve Lantau, and that colour system matters when you're heading back from the airport side.

## Getting home on the MTR after the encore

Hong Kong moves crowds well, but only if everyone follows the system. Here's how people get out of a big show without stress.

- **Load your Octopus card before the show.** Topping up at a machine while the whole stadium leaves at once is a mistake you only make once. Many gates also take contactless bank cards, which saves the step completely.
- **Stand right, walk left** on the escalators. It's taken seriously here, and on long station escalators it keeps people moving.
- **Follow the marshals.** Staff after big events often send people to particular station entrances or hold them back in waves. Push against that and you'll be stuck longer.
- **Don't eat or drink** in the paid area of the MTR. The rule is posted and enforced, so finish your drink before the gates.

Inside the venue, the usual Hong Kong arena customs apply. Your seat is the one printed on your ticket, and staff will move you back to it. Professional cameras are generally banned, though phones are fine. The bag policy is on the ticket page, so read it before you pack.

## What late November is like

Late November is one of the most comfortable times to be in Hong Kong. The summer humidity has gone, the typhoon season is over, and evenings are mild and usually dry. A light layer is useful, especially if you're outdoors at Kai Tak or waiting on an open platform.

That good weather is also why the city is busy then. Hotels fill up for concerts, conferences and regional travel, so book your room as soon as you have a ticket. If the venue is in Kowloon, staying in Tsim Sha Tsui, Jordan or Hung Hom keeps your trip home short.

## Making a weekend of it

A Friday show leaves you the whole weekend. If the gig is in Kowloon, the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront and the Avenue of Stars are an easy walk or a short ride from Hung Hom. The Star Ferry crosses to Central from there, which is the classic way to see the harbour.

Before the show, a cha chaan teng is a quick, filling meal for an evening on your feet. These are Hong Kong-style cafés serving milk tea, pineapple buns and macaroni soup, and you'll find them all over Kowloon. If you're at AsiaWorld-Expo instead, Ngong Ping 360 and the Tian Tan Buddha are on the same island, which makes Saturday on Lantau an easy plan.
