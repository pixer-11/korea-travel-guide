## The last-train problem

This is the Hong Kong concert issue that catches visitors out. The MTR stops running around midnight and doesn't restart until morning. A late encore followed by a slow exit from a venue can leave people at a closed station.

The risk depends on the venue. In Kowloon, missing the last train usually just means a taxi queue and a short ride. AsiaWorld-Expo is different: it's out on Lantau by the airport, and the Airport Express is the easy way back to Kowloon or Central. The MTR website and app show the last departure from every station.

Night buses cover the gaps, and the routes starting with "N" run through the small hours. Red taxis serve the urban areas. Green taxis serve the New Territories and blue ones serve Lantau, and that colour system matters when heading back from the airport side.

## Getting home on the MTR after the encore

Hong Kong moves crowds well, as long as everyone follows the system. This is how a big show usually empties without stress.

- **Octopus cards top up at station machines**, and those machines get long queues when a whole stadium leaves at once. Many gates also take contactless bank cards, which skips the step completely.
- **Stand right, walk left** on the escalators. It's taken seriously here, and on long station escalators it keeps people moving.
- **Marshals direct the flow.** Staff after big events often send people to particular station entrances or hold them back in waves, and pushing against that only slows things down.
- **No eating or drinking** in the paid area of the MTR. The rule is posted and enforced.

Inside Hong Kong arenas, the usual customs apply. Your seat is the one printed on your ticket, and staff will move you back to it. Professional cameras are generally banned, though phones are fine. The bag policy usually sits on the ticket page.

## What late November is like

Late November is one of the most comfortable times to be in Hong Kong. The summer humidity has gone, the typhoon season is over, and evenings are mild and usually dry. A light layer is useful, especially outdoors at Kai Tak or on an open platform.

That good weather is also why the city is busy then. Hotels fill up for concerts, conferences and regional travel. For a Kowloon venue, Tsim Sha Tsui, Jordan and Hung Hom keep the trip home short.

## Making a weekend of it

A Friday show sits right before a free weekend. In Kowloon, the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront and the Avenue of Stars are an easy walk or a short ride from Hung Hom. The Star Ferry crosses to Central from there, which is the classic way to see the harbour.

A cha chaan teng is a quick, filling meal before an evening on your feet. These are Hong Kong-style cafés serving milk tea, pineapple buns and macaroni soup, and they're all over Kowloon. Near AsiaWorld-Expo, Ngong Ping 360 and the Tian Tan Buddha are on the same island, which makes Lantau an easy Saturday.
