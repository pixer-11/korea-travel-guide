## Five houses on an old shoreline

The houses face water that has mostly disappeared. They were built in 1921 for Macanese civil servants and their families, along what was then Taipa's waterfront promenade. Land reclamation pushed the sea back. Today the verandas look across a strip of protected wetland and mangrove to the casino towers of the Cotai Strip.

The houses stand shoulder to shoulder in mint green with white trim. Each has steps up to a raised veranda, and the row is shaded by banyan trees. The address on maps is Estrada de Cacilhas, but you'll find the frontage on the promenade below the church hill.

## What's inside each door

Each house runs as its own small gallery. You can step in and out in any order.

- **The Macanese residence:** furnished rooms showing how a well-off Macanese family lived in the early twentieth century, with dark wood furniture, family portraits and Catholic devotional pieces.
- **Exhibition houses:** rotating displays on Taipa's history, Macanese culture and local craft. Content changes, so check the Macao government museum listings before you go.
- **Shop and event spaces:** the remaining houses host creative pop-ups, small shows and occasional performances.

The rooms are compact, and you can do all five in under an hour. Photography rules can vary between galleries. Look for signs at each door.

## Walking the promenade properly

The best part of a visit is outside, on the paved promenade in front of the houses. Benches sit under the trees. The wetland boardwalk lets you look back at the whole row from the water side, which is where most photos are taken.

Climb the steps behind the houses to **Our Lady of Carmo Church**, a small yellow neoclassical church completed in 1885. Its terrace and garden give you the rooftop view of the row. The church is still a working parish, so dress modestly and stay quiet if a service or wedding is on.

From the church, a few minutes' walk takes you into the lanes of Taipa Village. **Rua do Cunha** is the narrow pedestrian street lined with almond cookie shops, pork chop bun stalls and serradura counters.

## Getting in and timing it

The houses open from 10am to 7pm Tuesday to Sunday and close every Monday. That catches out a lot of day-trippers. If your only free day is Monday, you can still walk the promenade and photograph the exterior, but the doors will be shut.

- **By LRT:** take the Macau Light Rapid Transit Taipa Line to Pai Kok station and walk downhill into the village, about ten minutes.
- **By casino shuttle:** the free hotel buses to the Cotai resorts drop you a short walk away. Cross toward the wetlands to reach the promenade.
- **By public bus:** several routes from the Macau peninsula stop near Taipa Village. Check the route board at your stop.

The houses are well known, and the promenade is a favourite backdrop for wedding shoots. Arriving soon after the 10am opening usually puts you ahead of the tour groups that come through later in the day.

Late afternoon gives softer light on the green facades. You also have until 7pm to see the interiors before heading to Rua do Cunha for dinner. Entry arrangements are posted at the houses and on the official Macao museums site. Card and mobile payment are widely accepted in the village, though a few snack stalls are quicker with cash in patacas or Hong Kong dollars.
