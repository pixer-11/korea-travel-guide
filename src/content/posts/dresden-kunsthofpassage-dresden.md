## A blue wall wired for rain

Look up at the blue facade and you'll see drainpipes. They aren't hidden away. They wind across the wall in loops and funnels, joined by trumpet-shaped spouts and little metal cups.

This is the Hof der Elemente, the Courtyard of the Elements. When it rains, water runs through the whole system and the wall turns into a clattering, gurgling instrument. It's the photo most people come for. It's also only one of five courtyards in the Kunsthofpassage, a passage cut through the backyards of the old tenement blocks at Görlitzer Str. 21-25.

## Five courtyards, five ideas

Each courtyard was given to different artists with its own theme. The complex opened in stages around the turn of the millennium. Expect painted plaster, mosaic and metalwork rather than a museum.

- **Hof der Elemente** (Elements): the singing drainpipe wall, plus a facade of angled metal panels that catch the light.
- **Hof der Fabelwesen** (Mythical Creatures): a turquoise facade with tiled creatures and mosaic figures climbing the walls.
- **Hof der Tiere** (Animals): painted animals on the walls, plus the mood of a small town square.
- **Hof des Lichts** (Light): mirrors and reflective surfaces that bounce daylight into a narrow space.
- **Hof der Metamorphosen** (Metamorphoses): sculpture and changing forms, and the quietest corner on many days.

Between the courtyards you'll find outdoor café tables, artists' studios and independent boutiques. Most visitors spend 30 to 60 minutes here. That stretches if you stop for coffee.

## Getting into the Neustadt

The passage sits in the Äußere Neustadt, Dresden's old student and bar district north of the Elbe. It's a short walk from the Baroque Altstadt across the river, but the feel is very different.

- **By tram:** ride to Albertplatz, a hub for several lines, then walk about ten minutes north along Alaunstraße.
- **On foot from the Altstadt:** cross the Augustusbrücke and continue up Hauptstraße to Albertplatz. Allow roughly 25 minutes.
- **From Dresden-Neustadt station:** about a 15-minute walk east.

There are entrances on both Görlitzer Straße and Alaunstraße. You can walk straight through the courtyards and come out on the other street.

## Timing your walk-through

The courtyards keep long hours. They open at 6am Monday to Friday and at 8am on weekends. They close at 11pm most nights, or midnight on Fridays and Saturdays. Shops and studios inside keep their own, shorter hours, so check those separately.

Weekday mornings between 7am and 10am are the calmest time. That's your window for an empty shot of the drainpipe wall. On weekends, the courtyards are busiest from 12pm to 11pm, so try not to arrive after midday. Turn up at the 8am opening and the courtyards are still mostly yours.

A rainy day isn't bad luck here. It's the only time the Hof der Elemente actually plays.

## Moving through quietly

These are lived-in buildings. Above the painted walls are ordinary flats, and the windows looking down on you belong to residents. The few rules in the courtyards come from that.

- **Keep your voice down,** especially early in the morning and late at night.
- **Don't block the passages** with tripods or group photo shoots. The walkways are narrow, and residents and shop staff still need to get through.
- **Ask before photographing** inside studios or of the artists at work.
- **Carry a little cash.** Germany still runs on it more than visitors expect, and a small café or studio may not take every card. Ask before ordering if it matters.
- **Say "Guten Tag"** when you walk into a shop, and "Tschüss" when you leave. It's normal courtesy in Germany, not optional politeness.

The Kunsthofpassage is one of Dresden's most visited sights, so don't expect a secret. You're sharing a small space with plenty of others, and it works best when everyone remembers someone lives upstairs.

## Around the corner

The streets around the passage deserve the rest of your afternoon.

- **Pfunds Molkerei** on Bautzner Straße: a dairy shop famous for its hand-painted Villeroy & Boch tiles, about ten minutes' walk east.
- **Alaunpark:** the neighbourhood's big lawn, a few blocks north.
- **Louisenstraße and Rothenburger Straße:** bars, second-hand shops and street art. Busiest after dark.
- **Martin-Luther-Kirche:** the brick church at the centre of the quieter Preußisches Viertel side of the district.

Put them together and you get a loop of half a day or so. The Neustadt is at its best when you treat it as a neighbourhood, not a single stop.
