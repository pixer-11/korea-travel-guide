## One day a week, six hours long

Glebe Markets only happens on Saturdays. From 10am to 4pm, the corner of Glebe Point Road and Derby Place fills with stalls. For the other six days there is nothing to see here.

That short window decides how you visit. Turn up on a Sunday and you will find a quiet street. Arrive at 4pm and stallholders will be folding tables. Treat it as a fixed Saturday event, a bit like a weekly festival, and plan the rest of the day around it.

It is well known across Sydney and has run for a long time. Expect company, especially on a fine weekend.

## What the stalls actually sell

Vintage clothing is the market's reputation. Expect rails of second-hand jackets, denim, dresses and shirts, with the browsing done by hand, hanger by hanger. Sizes and condition vary from rack to rack, so look each piece over before you buy.

Around the clothing you will find the usual flea-market spread. Stock changes from week to week because many sellers are independent traders. What you can usually expect to find:

- **Vintage and pre-loved clothes**, the main reason most people come
- **Accessories**: bags, sunglasses, hats, jewellery
- **Bric-a-brac and homewares** from second-hand and craft sellers
- **Street food** from a rotating set of stalls

Don't arrive with one particular item in mind. You'll do better with a loose budget and patience for digging.

## The lawn, the food and the acoustic set

Acoustic music is part of the market's identity. Musicians play while people eat, so the food stalls and the music tend to anchor the same part of the day. Many visitors buy lunch, find a spot to sit, and stay longer than they meant to.

The food is varied street food, the kind you carry in one hand and eat standing or sitting on the ground. Line-ups shift weekly, so do a slow lap before you choose. If you're visiting with a group, split up across several stalls and meet back where the music is playing.

This is the slower half of a Glebe Markets visit. Shopping is the early-morning job; eating and listening fill the middle of the day.

## Getting to Derby Place

The market sits on Glebe Point Road at Derby Place, in Glebe, an inner-west suburb just south-west of the Sydney CBD. The University of Sydney is close by, and Broadway is at the southern end of Glebe Point Road.

Your options:

- **On foot:** walk from Central Station via Broadway, then turn up Glebe Point Road. Allow around half an hour at an easy pace.
- **Bus:** several routes run along Glebe Point Road from the city. Check the Transport NSW Trip Planner for current Saturday services.
- **Light rail:** the Inner West Light Rail stops at Glebe, further down the hill. From there it is an uphill walk to the market.
- **Car:** street parking in Glebe is tight on a Saturday. Public transport is less stressful.

Tap on and off with an Opal card or a contactless bank card on buses and light rail.

## Working the market on a Saturday

There are no tickets and no booking; you just walk in. How the day goes depends on what you came for. Here is how regulars tend to approach it.

**Timing.** Serious vintage hunters usually arrive near the 10am opening, before the best pieces go. People who come mainly to eat and hear music drift in later. Don't count on finding much after mid-afternoon, since stalls start packing up before 4pm.

**Paying.** Most Australian traders take tap-and-go card payments, but small independent stalls sometimes prefer cash. Carry a little for smaller purchases. There's no tipping culture at food stalls, so pay the price on the board.

**Bargaining.** At vintage stalls a polite question about the price is generally fine, especially if you're buying several pieces. Keep it friendly and brief. Food prices are fixed.

**Trying things on.** Fitting space is limited or nonexistent at many clothing stalls. Wear a thin base layer you can pull a jacket or shirt over, and ask before you take anything off the rail to try.

**Carrying.** Bring your own tote bag. Rummaging through racks is easier with your hands free.

## Making a day of Glebe Point Road

The market is a good reason to spend the afternoon in Glebe itself. Glebe Point Road is lined with cafés, pubs and independent shops. Gleebooks, one of Sydney's best-known independent bookshops, is on the same road.

If you'd rather be outdoors, follow Glebe Point Road downhill toward the water. Jubilee Park and the foreshore walk along Blackwattle Bay are at the bottom. Wentworth Park is also nearby.

For a full day, combine the market in the morning with a wander through the University of Sydney's sandstone quadrangle, a short walk away. Then finish with a meal on Glebe Point Road once the stalls close.

## How long to give it

Two hours covers the market comfortably: an hour browsing the clothes, then lunch and some music. Dedicated thrifters can easily fill the whole six hours.

Add another hour or two if you plan to explore Glebe Point Road and the foreshore. Just remember the market only exists on Saturdays, from 10am to 4pm. Missing that window means waiting a week.
