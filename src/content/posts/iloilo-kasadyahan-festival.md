## Saturday belongs to the towns

Dinagyang gets the headlines. Kasadyahan gets the Saturday.

It's a cultural dance competition held the Saturday before Iloilo's Dinagyang Festival. Dance groups from towns across the Western Visayas region bring their own folk performances into Iloilo City. Dinagyang's Sunday is about Ati-inspired warrior tribes and pounding drums. Kasadyahan is broader and more varied, with each contingent drawing on its home town's stories, harvests and local festivals.

The result plays like a sampler of the region. One group might perform a fishing ritual and the next a courtship dance. Costumes run from woven native fabrics to huge, many-layered headdresses. Groups usually carry props and backdrops built to be read from across a wide street.

## Date and where it happens

The 2027 edition takes place on **Saturday, January 23, 2027**. Dinagyang itself always falls on the fourth weekend of January, in honour of the Santo Niño. That makes Kasadyahan the opener of the city's biggest festival weekend.

The performances happen in the streets of Iloilo City. Contingents move along a set parade route and stop at designated judging areas to perform their full routines. The organisers set the exact route and the judging stations for each edition. For a first-timer, the main thing to know is that the judging areas are where you see a complete performance. Everywhere else, you mostly see groups passing through.

## Tickets, seats and the open kerb

There are two ways to watch.

- **Along the route:** open to the public. You stand or sit wherever there's space, and you see the groups as they move between stations.
- **At the judging areas:** this is typically grandstand or bleacher seating, sold as tickets. You see each contingent's full choreography from the angle it was designed for.

The ticket price isn't something we can give you. It's set by the organisers and appears with their seating sales. Families from the city usually treat the kerb as their seat: they bring a mat or a stool and settle in for the whole morning.

## Reaching Iloilo City on festival weekend

Most visitors fly into **Iloilo International Airport** in Cabatuan, about 19 km north of the city. Shuttle vans and taxis run into town from there. From Negros, fast ferries cross the Guimaras Strait from Bacolod to Iloilo in roughly an hour, which makes Kasadyahan easy to combine with a stay in Bacolod.

Rooms fill quickly on Dinagyang weekend, and visitors who leave it late often end up outside the centre. The usual bases are:

- **Iloilo Business Park and Smallville in Mandurriao:** newer hotels, restaurants and taxis, a short ride from downtown.
- **Downtown near Calle Real (J.M. Basa Street):** old heritage façades, and you can walk to the action.

Expect roads near the route to be closed on the day. Jeepneys reroute, and walking the last stretch is normal.

## Watching from the street, Ilonggo style

Kerb space goes to whoever claims it first. Locals typically stake out a stretch of pavement well before the first contingent appears, bringing water, umbrellas against the sun and something to sit on. Shade is the most fought-over thing on the route.

A few habits make the day easier:

- **Carry small cash.** Street vendors selling water, grilled snacks and souvenirs deal in coins and small notes.
- **Respect the performance space.** Groups need the full width of the street. Marshals clear anyone who steps in to take photos, so shoot from the line.
- **Applaud loudly.** Crowds cheer for their own province's contingents, and cheering for the visiting towns goes down well too.
- **Keep valuables zipped.** Packed festival crowds are where phones disappear.

In local speech you'll hear *"Viva Señor Santo Niño!"* around the whole weekend. Shouting it back is welcome.

## Between performances: soup, noodles and the river

Iloilo's food is half the reason to stay the whole weekend. **La Paz batchoy** is the city's signature noodle soup: pork broth, egg noodles, offal and crushed chicharrón. It's named after La Paz district, and the La Paz Public Market is its traditional home. **Pancit molo**, a clear broth with pork dumplings, comes from Molo district. That's also home to the Gothic-style Molo Church, often called the feminist church for its rows of female saints.

When the dancing winds down, many visitors walk the **Iloilo River Esplanade**, a long riverside promenade in Mandurriao lined with mangroves. On Sunday, the city turns to Dinagyang proper. Most people who come for Kasadyahan stay for both days.
