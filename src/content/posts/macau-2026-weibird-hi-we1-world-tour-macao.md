## A Taipei songwriter on the Cotai Strip

WeiBird brings "HI WE1" to Macau on 12 December 2026. The Taiwanese Mandopop singer-songwriter, known in Chinese as Wei Li-an (韋禮安), plays The Londoner Arena that Saturday night. His shows lean on his own writing, with guitar-led ballads and the soft falsetto his fans come for.

The Macao date is one stop on a world tour, so the crowd is mixed. Locals come, and so do Mandarin speakers who cross over from Hong Kong, Guangdong and Taiwan for the weekend. Stage banter is in Mandarin, and the audience sings the choruses back without needing a cue.

## The Londoner Arena and getting to Cotai

The arena sits inside The Londoner Macao, the Sands resort dressed in Victorian London styling, with a Big Ben-style clock tower on its façade. It sits on Cotai, the reclaimed strip between Taipa and Coloane, beside The Venetian and The Parisian. Indoor walkways link all three, so you can walk from one resort to the next without stepping outside.

The usual routes in:

- **Light Rapid Transit:** the Taipa line runs along Cotai, and its stations sit within walking distance of the Sands resorts.
- **Resort shuttles:** free Sands buses run from the Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal, Taipa Ferry Terminal and the Border Gate area.
- **From Hong Kong:** TurboJET and Cotai Water Jet ferries, or the cross-border bus over the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge.
- **From Zhuhai:** the Hengqin crossing lands closest to Cotai.

## Show night around the arena

Concert nights on Cotai follow a familiar pattern. Fans eat first in The Londoner's food courts or the Venetian's shopping canals, then queue at the arena doors in groups wearing tour merch. Merchandise stands usually draw the longest lines before doors open, while the toilets get busiest at the end.

When the show ends, thousands of people leave at once. The shuttle queues and taxi ranks fill quickly. Many visitors stay overnight at the Cotai hotels so they can skip the late ferry back to Hong Kong, which is a sensible choice on a December Saturday.

## How Macau fans do concert night

Macau runs on the pataca, but Hong Kong dollars are accepted almost everywhere on Cotai. Card payments work throughout the resorts. Concertgoers usually keep their e-ticket or collection details on their phone and their passport or ID within reach, since the trip often involves a border crossing.

Inside, the etiquette matches Taiwanese pop shows. People stay seated through the ballads and stand for the upbeat encores. Phones come out for the big singalong songs, and most people lower them for the quiet numbers. Holding a sign up in a way that blocks the people behind you is frowned upon.

Mid-December evenings in Macau are cool and dry, and the arena's air conditioning is strong. A light layer is useful both inside and in the queue for the ferry afterwards.
