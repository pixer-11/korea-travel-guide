## Four December nights in Istanbul

The festival runs Thursday to Sunday. BERGFESTival 2026 takes place in Istanbul from December 3 to 6, 2026, so it fills one full weekend with two weeknights in front of it.

This is the city off-season. Daytime temperatures usually sit in the high single digits to low teens Celsius, rain is common, and the sun sets before 5pm. The upside: shorter queues at Hagia Sophia, the Basilica Cistern and Topkapı Palace for anyone who spends the days sightseeing.

## Getting around a city split by water

Istanbul spreads across the Bosphorus, so where you sleep matters more than in most festival cities. Once the venue is known, people usually pick a hotel on the same side of the water.

- **From Istanbul Airport (IST):** the M11 metro runs to Gayrettepe, where it meets the M2 line toward Taksim and Şişhane.
- **From Sabiha Gökçen (SAW):** the M4 metro runs along the Asian side to Kadıköy.
- **Crossing the Bosphorus:** ferries link Kadıköy with Karaköy and Eminönü. The Marmaray rail tunnel runs under the strait.
- **Late at night:** taxis are the fallback. Most locals hail them through the BiTaksi app rather than flagging one on the street.

Beyoğlu, around İstiklal Avenue, and Kadıköy on the Asian side have the city's densest spread of bars and live music rooms. That makes either one a common base for festival-goers.

## Festival nights the Istanbul way

The whole public network runs on the **Istanbulkart**, a reloadable card sold and topped up at machines in metro stations and ferry piers. Bank cards are tapped directly at many gates too. One card can pay for several people, one tap per person.

Istanbul eats late. Before a show, crowds fill the meyhanes (tavern-style restaurants) of Asmalımescit in Beyoğlu or the market streets of Kadıköy. They work slowly through shared plates of meze and rakı. After midnight the usual move is a street-side stop for a stuffed mussel (midye dolma) or a wet burger (ıslak hamburger) on Taksim Square.

Some small courtesies:

- In restaurants, leave a tip in cash on the table. Card machines often have no tip option.
- A few words go a long way: "teşekkürler" (thank you) and "hesap, lütfen" (the bill, please).
- Most Istanbul music venues have a cloakroom, but queues for coats build at the end of the night in winter. Regulars usually wear layers they can carry.

December rain makes waterproof shoes more useful than an umbrella on the steep, cobbled streets that run down from Galata to Karaköy.
