## A restaurant under a steel cloud

The building does a lot of the talking. BMW Welt is a sweep of glass and steel at the edge of Munich's Olympiapark, designed by the Viennese firm Coop Himmelb(l)au and opened in 2007. Its roof floats over the hall like a flattened cloud. At one corner a double cone of twisted glass and steel drops to the ground.

Inside, new cars sit on the showroom floor, and buyers come here to collect theirs. Most visitors arrive to look at engineering. A fine-dining table is not what they expect to find in the same building, and that contrast is half the appeal of THE CLOUD by Käfer.

The name is no mystery once you have stood under that roof. The restaurant takes its identity from the architecture around it.

## Why the Michelin inspectors moved fast

Most restaurants wait years for a star. This one received two right away. The Michelin Guide Germany also named it the 2026 'Opening of the Year', a title for the debut that impressed the inspectors most across the whole country.

The chef is Jens Madsen, and the operator is Käfer. Munich knows that name well. Feinkost Käfer is the city's old-guard delicatessen and catering house, behind the Käfer-Schänke on Prinzregentenstraße and the Käfer Wiesn-Schänke at Oktoberfest. Putting a two-star kitchen inside BMW Welt is a different kind of bet for the group, and so far it has paid off.

## Culinary nomadism: a new passport each season

The defining idea is movement. Madsen's kitchen calls its concept 'culinary nomadism'. Each season the menu sets off for a different region of the world and explores its cooking.

That has practical consequences for you:

- **The menu you read about online may already be gone.** A review from last season describes a different trip.
- **Repeat visits make sense.** Locals with a two-star habit can come back four times a year and eat a new menu each time.
- **Ask which region is current when you book.** If the season's destination doesn't appeal, you can wait for the next one.

We haven't listed specific dishes here on purpose. They rotate, and a guide that names last season's plates would send you hunting for something that no longer exists. The restaurant itself is the only reliable source for what the current journey looks like.

## Getting to BMW Welt

The U-Bahn is the easiest way in. Take the **U3** to **Olympiazentrum**. From the platform it is a short walk to BMW Welt, which you will see across the road. The BMW headquarters tower, with its four round cylinders, rises right beside it.

From Marienplatz the U3 runs straight through, so no change is needed from the old town. Taxis and ride-hailing cars can drop you at the BMW Welt entrance on Am Olympiapark. If you plan to drive, check the current parking arrangements with BMW Welt or the restaurant before you set off.

The building sits in the Milbertshofen-Am Hart district, north of Schwabing. That puts it a good way outside the tourist core, so allow for the trip home after a long dinner.

## Booking a two-star table in Munich

A two-star dining room expects planning. These habits will make the evening smoother:

- **Reserve, don't walk in.** Tables at newly starred restaurants go fast, and the 'Opening of the Year' title brings extra demand. Book through the restaurant's official channels as early as you can.
- **Confirm the details.** We were not given opening days, service times or menu prices. Check all three on the official site or when you book. Seasonal changeovers may also mean short closures.
- **Mention allergies at booking.** A kitchen cooking a different region every season will need notice to adapt.
- **Dress the part.** Smart-casual is a safe default for German fine dining. Ask if you are unsure.
- **Water isn't automatic.** German restaurants usually serve bottled still or sparkling water, and you order it like any other drink.
- **Tipping is modest but expected.** Germans round up or add roughly ten percent. Tell the server the total you want to pay as you settle, rather than leaving coins on the table.

## Making a day of the Olympiapark side

The neighbourhood fills an afternoon easily. The **BMW Museum**, the bowl-shaped building beside the headquarters tower, is connected to BMW Welt by a footbridge. Car history fills it, and it pairs naturally with a meal next door.

Across the road is the **Olympiapark**, built for the 1972 Summer Olympics. Its tent-like acrylic roofs, designed by Frei Otto and Günter Behnisch, drape over the stadium and arenas. The **Olympiaturm** gives a high view over the city, and on clear days you can see the Alps.

The **Olympiaberg**, a grassy hill made from wartime rubble, is a short climb with a wide view over the park and the lake. A walk there before an evening reservation works well. You will come back to BMW Welt as the light fades and the glass hall starts to glow.

## Who this table is for

This is a special-occasion address and sits firmly at the pricier end of Munich dining. It suits travellers who plan meals as carefully as they plan museums, and food lovers keen to see what an ambitious new kitchen is doing.

If you want roast pork and a litre of Helles, the old town beer halls do that better. If you want to eat somewhere the Michelin inspectors have just picked out above every other opening in Germany, take the U3 north. Book first.
