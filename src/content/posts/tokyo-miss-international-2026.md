## What's happening on November 25

Miss International 2026 lands at Tokyo Dome City Hall on November 25, 2026. It's the 64th running of one of the world's oldest international beauty pageants, founded in Long Beach, California, in 1960 and based in Tokyo since the 1970s. The evening ends the way it always does: the outgoing titleholder crowns her successor on stage.

Contestants from dozens of countries spend weeks in Japan before the final, doing courtesy visits, cultural events, and rehearsals that rarely get press coverage compared to the final night itself. If you're in Tokyo in the lead-up, the final gala is the one fixed, ticketed date on the calendar.

## Tokyo Dome City Hall, the venue

Tokyo Dome City Hall sits inside the Tokyo Dome City entertainment complex in Bunkyo City, next to the Dome stadium itself. It's a mid-size indoor hall built for concerts, conventions, and exactly this kind of televised awards show, not an arena on the scale of the Dome next door.

The complex is easy to find because of the Dome's roofline, visible from several approach streets. Inside Tokyo Dome City you'll also find LaQua, a shopping and spa complex, and an amusement park with a roller coaster that loops around the building, so there's plenty to do if you arrive early or linger after.

**Getting there:**
- Suidobashi Station (JR Chuo-Sobu Line and Toei Mita Line) is the closest stop, a short walk from the venue.
- Korakuen Station (Tokyo Metro Marunouchi and Namboku Lines) exits almost directly into the Tokyo Dome City complex.
- Both stations are one or two stops from major interchanges like Tokyo Station or Otemachi, so it's a workable evening trip from most central hotels.

## Tickets and how to get in

Confirm timing and tickets on the official Miss International website and its social channels; that is where seating categories, entry procedures, and any credential requirements for the final are posted. Because this is a televised production with broadcast cameras and a working stage, expect a bag check and a fixed entry time rather than a rolling door policy.

Don't assume you can buy on the day at the venue. Pageant finals like this one typically sell through official or authorized channels rather than a walk-up box office, so sort your ticket before you fly.

## The crowning moment, and why the order of events matters

The show builds toward a single sequence at the end: swimsuit and evening gown rounds, a Q&A segment for the finalists, then the announcement of placements from lowest to highest. The reigning Miss International appears in her final walk before passing the crown and sash to the new titleholder.

If you only care about that moment, know that it comes last, after a full program of introductions and preliminary rounds. Pageant broadcasts of this size usually run two to three hours; pace your evening (and any dinner plans) around a late finish rather than an early one.

## Around Korakuen: what to do before the show

Tokyo Dome City's own attractions make an easy pre-show stop. The amusement park (Tokyo Dome City Attractions) sells same-day ride passes and sits right against the hall, so a couple of hours there before doors is realistic if you arrive mid-afternoon.

Koishikawa Korakuen, one of Tokyo's oldest formal gardens, is a short walk southwest of the station and a quieter option if you'd rather sit somewhere green than ride a coaster before an evening of stage lighting and applause. Both fit into a single afternoon without rushing.

## Watching outside the hall

Not everyone attending Miss International events is inside for the final. Fan communities gather near hotel entrances and airport arrivals during pageant season, especially when a popular delegate is competing, and some of that spills into the streets around Tokyo Dome City on the day itself.

If you want to catch a glimpse of delegates without a ticket, the plaza areas around Tokyo Dome City and LaQua are the realistic spots, not the hall entrance itself, which is typically managed for ticketed guests and press only.

## Same-day logistics for a Bunkyo City evening

Tokyo Dome City sits between two other Bunkyo City landmarks, Tokyo Dome stadium and the Tokyo Dome Hotel, so the immediate blocks can get busy if a baseball game or concert overlaps with the pageant on the same date. Check what else is scheduled at the Dome that night before you assume a quiet approach.

Restaurants inside LaQua and around Korakuen Station get through-traffic from all three venues, so a pre-show dinner reservation is worth making rather than hoping for a table on arrival. Trains on both connecting lines run frequently into the evening, which makes the trip back into central Tokyo straightforward even after a late finish.
