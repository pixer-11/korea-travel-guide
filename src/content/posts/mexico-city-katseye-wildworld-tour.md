## Two nights under the copper dome

The tour ends in a building made for the Olympics. The Palacio de los Deportes opened for the 1968 Mexico City Games as the basketball arena. Its roof is still the first thing anyone mentions: a dome of copper-clad hyperbolic paraboloids designed by the architect Félix Candela with Antonio Peyri and Enrique Castañeda Tamborrel.

From outside, the dome looks like a ribbed copper shell set down beside the Viaducto. Inside it works as a full concert arena. There is a general-admission floor, which Mexican tickets call *pista*, and raked seating, the *gradas*, running up toward the curved roof.

KATSEYE's Wildworld Tour finishes here, with shows on November 27 and 28, 2026, as announced.

## The group on stage

KATSEYE is a global girl group. It was formed through *The Debut: Dream Academy*, the audition series HYBE and Geffen Records made together. Its six members come from the United States, the Philippines, Switzerland and South Korea, and that mix shows in a crowd full of homemade signs in several languages.

Most fans know the group from songs like "Touch", "Gnarly" and "Gabriela". "Gabriela" has an obvious pull in a Spanish-speaking city. The tour's setlist and any support acts aren't covered here. This guide sticks to what's known about the dates and the venue.

## Dates, tickets and seating

The facts are short:

- **Dates:** Friday, November 27 and Saturday, November 28, 2026
- **Venue:** Palacio de los Deportes, Ciudad Deportiva Magdalena Mixhuca, Iztacalco, Mexico City
- **Tour status:** these are the final dates of the Wildworld Tour

Concerts at the Palacio are run by OCESA, Mexico's largest promoter. Its arena shows are generally sold through Ticketmaster México, and the venue box office is called the *taquilla*. Prices for each zone appear on the ticketing page, so this guide doesn't list them.

On the floor, the experience depends on how far forward you get. In the *gradas*, the curved roof keeps the upper rows closer to the stage than at most arenas this size.

## Getting to Palacio de los Deportes

Metro Line 9 runs right alongside the complex. Two of its stations matter:

- **Velódromo (Line 9):** the stop most concertgoers use, a short walk from the arena gates
- **Ciudad Deportiva (Line 9):** one stop east, handy for the eastern side of the complex and the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez
- **Puebla (Line 9):** another nearby option when the platforms at Velódromo are packed

The Palacio shares the complex with Estadio GNP Seguros, the former Foro Sol, and the Autódromo racetrack. On nights when more than one venue has an event, the surrounding avenues and the Viaducto Río de la Piedad clog quickly. That's why many fans skip cars and ride the Metro.

## Working the gates and the exit at the Palacio

Lines form outside the numbered *accesos* (entry gates), and the gate printed on your ticket is the one that matters. Floor ticket holders usually show up well before doors to get a good spot on the *pista*. Fans with numbered seats tend to come later, because their place is already assigned.

Some habits that help on the night:

- **Payment:** inside the arena, food, drinks and merchandise stands mostly take cards. Outside on the street, vendors selling tacos, water and unofficial merch mostly want cash pesos.
- **Ordering:** a polite *"¿me da…?"* works at any stand. *Pista* means the floor and *gradas* means the seating.
- **Fan projects:** K-pop-style fandoms in Mexico City often hand out free banners or slogans near the gates for coordinated moments during the show. They're given away, not sold.

The exit is the hardest part. Ride-hailing apps like Uber and DiDi struggle with pickups right outside the gates, and surge pricing is normal after a full-arena show. Many attendees walk a few blocks away from the complex and call a car from there. Others go straight into the Velódromo Metro, where staff usually manage the crowd at the turnstiles.

## Staying and eating around the shows

Iztacalco is mostly residential and industrial. Few visitors stay right beside the arena. Most out-of-town fans base themselves in Roma or Condesa, which have dense blocks of hotels, cafés and taquerías. Centro Histórico is another option, with the Zócalo and a direct Metro connection to Line 9 via Chabacano.

From Roma and Condesa, the trip to Velódromo is a short Metro ride with a transfer, or a direct taxi along the Viaducto. Two nights in a row makes this an easy weekend to build around. Fans attending both shows often spend Saturday daytime in Coyoacán, or at the Museo Nacional de Antropología in Chapultepec, and then head back east for the final show.
