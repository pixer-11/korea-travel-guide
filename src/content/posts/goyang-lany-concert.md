## Two nights in Ilsan

LANY was set to play Goyang on October 3 and 4, 2026. The American pop band announced two shows at KINTEX, the Korea International Exhibition Center. It sits in Ilsanseo-gu, the planned new-town side of Goyang, about an hour from central Seoul by subway.

KINTEX is not a purpose-built arena. It is a pair of huge exhibition buildings, Exhibition Center 1 and Exhibition Center 2. Their halls turn into concert floors with a stage at one end, a flat standing area and seating blocks. That layout shapes the night: sightlines depend far more on the section than they would in a raked stadium.

Venue details vary from show to show, including which hall is used, door times and the seating plan.

## Why LANY fills a room like this

The band started in Los Angeles in 2014, and the name is short for "LA to NY". Frontman Paul Klein writes the kind of heartbreak-pop that crowds sing word for word. Songs like these fill a floor:

- "ILYSB"
- "Malibu Nights"
- "Super Far"
- "Thru These Tears"
- "Anything 4 U"

LANY have toured Asia heavily for years, and Korean audiences are famously loud and word-perfect for Western acts. Rooms like this sing the choruses back louder than the PA, and phone lights come up for the ballads. Setlists change between tours and between nights, so no particular song is guaranteed.

## Getting to KINTEX

KINTEX is far enough from Seoul that the route matters. There are three solid options.

- **GTX-A to Kintex Station.** This is the fastest link from Seoul Station and Yeonsinnae. The station sits right beside the exhibition complex.
- **Seoul Subway Line 3 to Daehwa Station.** Daehwa is the western end of Line 3. From there it is a walk or a short bus or taxi ride to KINTEX. Line 3 is slower than GTX-A but connects directly to Jongno, Gyeongbokgung and Gangnam.
- **Taxi.** Kakao T is the standard app. Pickup points get crowded at the end of the night, and many fans set one a few blocks from the exits.

A T-money card, sold and topped up at convenience stores, works on both rail lines. For arrivals by plane, Gimpo Airport is much closer to Ilsan than Incheon. Both airports have limousine bus routes towards the Ilsan area.

## The ID check at the door

This is the part that catches international fans out. Korean concerts commonly match the ticket holder's name against photo ID at entry. For a foreign visitor, that ID is usually a passport. A screenshot of a ticket bought under someone else's name may not get the holder in.

Tickets were sold through the official ticketing platform named in the promoter's announcement. Many Korean platforms have a separate global or English site for overseas buyers. Some issue tickets for pickup at a booth on the day, which means another queue before the main one. Booking confirmations set out the pickup rules and the accepted ID.

## Standing numbers and how the queue works

Standing tickets in Korea usually carry an entry number. Staff call numbers in blocks, so a low number puts a fan near the barrier and a high number puts them further back. Showing up early does not move anyone up the order. Being there when the block is called is what counts.

Here is how fans typically handle the day:

- **Merch.** Merchandise lines at KINTEX shows often open well before doors.
- **Bags.** Bag rules vary by show, and lockers around big venues fill fast. A small crossbody bag saves trouble.
- **Card or phone payment.** Korean card terminals and convenience stores take international cards almost everywhere. A little cash is handy for street food.
- **Queue culture.** Lines are orderly and staff-directed. Pushing forward when blocks are called is frowned on, and staff send people back.

Seated ticket holders have more leeway on timing. Exhibition hall entrances can be a long walk from the main concourse, though.

## The last-train problem

Ilsan's distance from Seoul is the timing quirk that defines a gig here. Two thousand-plus people leave a hall together and head for the same few stations. Most of them are also crossing the city afterwards.

The two departures that matter are the last GTX-A from Kintex Station and the last Line 3 from Daehwa. Korean apps like Naver Map and KakaoMap show them in English. After a late encore, a missed train means a long taxi ride back to Seoul, and surge demand makes taxis hard to find right outside the venue.

One easy fix is sleeping in Ilsan. The Kintex and Lake Park area has business hotels within walking or short taxi distance of the halls, which avoids the scramble entirely and puts the lake on the doorstep in the morning.

## Around KINTEX before and after

The streets around KINTEX are more pleasant than an exhibition district suggests. Ilsan was built as a planned city, with wide avenues, low-rise shopping streets and a big lake at its centre.

- **Ilsan Lake Park (Hosu Park).** A large artificial lake with a walking and cycling loop. It's good for a stroll.
- **La Festa and Western Dom.** Two open-air shopping and dining streets near Jeongbalsan Station. They're packed with Korean barbecue, fried chicken and late-night bars.
- **Hyundai Department Store Kintex.** Right beside the venue, with a food hall on the lower floors. It's useful for a quick meal without leaving the area.
- **One Mount.** A shopping and leisure complex next to KINTEX with indoor water and snow parks.

The Paju side of the region is a short drive north, with the Imjingak area near the border and the book town of Paju Book City.
