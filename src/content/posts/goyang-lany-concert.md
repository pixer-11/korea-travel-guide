## Two nights in Ilsan

LANY plays Goyang on October 3 and 4, 2026. The American pop band has announced two shows at KINTEX, the Korea International Exhibition Center. It sits in Ilsanseo-gu, the planned new-town side of Goyang, about an hour from central Seoul by subway.

KINTEX is not a purpose-built arena. It is a pair of huge exhibition buildings, Exhibition Center 1 and Exhibition Center 2. Their halls turn into concert floors with a stage at one end, a flat standing area and seating blocks. That layout shapes your night. Sightlines depend far more on your section than they would in a raked stadium.

Each venue detail varies by show, including which hall, door times and seating plan. Confirm timing, hall and tickets on the official site and your ticketing platform.

## Why LANY fills a room like this

The band started in Los Angeles in 2014, and the name is short for "LA to NY". Frontman Paul Klein writes the kind of heartbreak-pop that crowds sing word for word. Expect a full-volume floor on songs like these:

- "ILYSB"
- "Malibu Nights"
- "Super Far"
- "Thru These Tears"
- "Anything 4 U"

LANY have toured Asia heavily for years, and Korean audiences are famously loud and word-perfect for Western acts. Plan for a room that sings the choruses back louder than the PA. Phone lights come up for the ballads. Setlists change between tours and between nights, so no particular song is guaranteed.

## Getting to KINTEX

KINTEX is far enough from Seoul that the route matters. You have three solid options.

- **GTX-A to Kintex Station.** This is the fastest link from Seoul Station and Yeonsinnae. The station sits right beside the exhibition complex.
- **Seoul Subway Line 3 to Daehwa Station.** Daehwa is the western end of Line 3. From there it is a walk or a short bus or taxi ride to KINTEX. Line 3 is slower than GTX-A but connects directly to Jongno, Gyeongbokgung and Gangnam.
- **Taxi.** Book through Kakao T. Pickup points get crowded at the end of the night, so set one a few blocks from the exits.

Tap in with a T-money card, sold and topped up at convenience stores. Arriving by plane, Gimpo Airport is much closer to Ilsan than Incheon. Both airports run limousine bus routes towards the Ilsan area.

## The ID check at the door

This is the part that catches international fans out. Korean concerts commonly match the ticket holder's name against photo ID at entry. For a foreign visitor, that ID is usually a passport. A screenshot of a ticket bought under someone else's name may not get you in.

Buy from the official ticketing platform named in the promoter's announcement. Many Korean platforms have a separate global or English site for overseas buyers. Some issue tickets for pickup at a booth on the day, which means another queue before the main one. Read your booking confirmation carefully for pickup rules and accepted ID.

## Standing numbers and how the queue works

Standing tickets in Korea usually carry an entry number. Staff call numbers in blocks, so a low number puts you near the barrier and a high number puts you further back. Arriving early does not move you up. Turning up when your block is called is what counts.

Here is how fans typically handle the day:

- **Merch first.** Merchandise lines at KINTEX shows often open well before doors. Buy early or skip it.
- **Travel light.** Bag rules vary by show, and lockers around big venues fill fast. A small crossbody bag saves trouble.
- **Card or phone payment.** Korean card terminals and convenience stores take international cards almost everywhere. Keep a little cash for street food.
- **Queue culture.** Lines are orderly and staff-directed. Pushing forward when blocks are called is frowned on, and staff will send you back.

Seated ticket holders can arrive closer to the start. Exhibition hall entrances can be a long walk from the main concourse, though, so leave margin.

## The last-train problem

Ilsan's distance from Seoul is the timing quirk that defines this gig. Two thousand-plus people leave a hall together and head for the same few stations. Most of them are also trying to get back across the city.

Before the show, check the last departures for both GTX-A from Kintex Station and Line 3 from Daehwa. Korean apps like Naver Map and KakaoMap show them in English. If the encore runs late, a missed train means a long taxi ride back to Seoul. Surge demand also makes taxis hard to find right outside the venue.

One easy fix is to sleep in Ilsan. The Kintex and Lake Park area has business hotels within walking or short taxi distance of the halls. You avoid the scramble entirely and can wake up near the lake.

## Around KINTEX before and after

The streets around KINTEX are more pleasant than an exhibition district suggests. Ilsan was built as a planned city, with wide avenues, low-rise shopping streets and a big lake at its centre.

- **Ilsan Lake Park (Hosu Park).** A large artificial lake with a walking and cycling loop. It's good for a pre-show stroll.
- **La Festa and Western Dom.** Two open-air shopping and dining streets near Jeongbalsan Station. They're packed with Korean barbecue, fried chicken and late-night bars.
- **Hyundai Department Store Kintex.** Right beside the venue, with a food hall on the lower floors. It's useful for a quick meal without leaving the area.
- **One Mount.** A shopping and leisure complex next to KINTEX with indoor water and snow parks.

For a fuller day, the Paju side of the region is a short drive north. The Imjingak area near the border and the book town of Paju Book City both make a daytime trip before an evening show.
