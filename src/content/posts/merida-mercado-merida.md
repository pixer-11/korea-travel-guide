## A Centro market that keeps market hours

The gates open at 7:00 AM every day. Mexican markets do most of their trade early: produce is unloaded, cooked-food stalls start serving breakfast, and shoppers buy for the day's lunch. By late afternoon the fresh food has thinned out.

Mercado Mérida sits in Centro, the colonial grid around the Plaza Grande and the cathedral. Its listed address is just "Centro, 97000", so drop a pin in your maps app before setting out. Street numbers in Centro won't help you without one.

## What to look for at the stalls

Yucatán cooks differently from the rest of Mexico. The tastes are Maya: achiote, sour orange and habanero. Market food stalls in Mérida usually lean on the regional staples, so these are the names to recognise:

- **Cochinita pibil**: pork marinated in achiote and sour orange, then slow-cooked. It's usually served in tacos or tortas, often in the morning.
- **Panuchos and salbutes**: small fried tortillas with toppings. Panuchos are stuffed with black beans; salbutes are puffy and plain.
- **Sopa de lima**: chicken broth sharpened with the local lime.
- **Recado**: spice pastes sold in blocks, achiote red or charred black. They pack flat and survive a suitcase.
- **Habanero salsa**: it's on the table for a reason. Taste a drop first.

This guide can't vouch for which stalls are inside on any given day. Walk one full lap before you buy, and follow the stools that are already full.

## Ordering at the counter

Cash is the norm at Mexican market stalls, so carry small peso notes and coins. Big notes are a nuisance at a fonda counter first thing in the morning. Prices are usually posted on a board or handwritten sign. If there isn't one, ask "¿Cuánto cuesta?" before you order.

Point and use simple Spanish: "Me da dos de cochinita, por favor" gets you two tacos. Fonda seating is shared, so take any free stool. Saying "provecho" to the people next to you is ordinary courtesy. You pay when you finish, and leaving the coins from your change is appreciated rather than expected.

The market is well known, not a secret. Arriving soon after 7:00 AM usually means more choice, room at the counters and less heat.

## Timing your visit

Mérida is hot for most of the year, and a covered market traps that heat by midday. The morning is the most comfortable time and the best for food. Most stalls run out of their daily batch of cooked dishes once lunch is over.

Watch Sunday: the market closes at 4:00 PM instead of 7:30 PM. Allow 45 minutes to an hour for a lap, a plate and some spice shopping. Then walk back through Centro to the Plaza Grande, the cathedral and the Casa de Montejo.

Bring a cloth bag for produce and recado, and keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket in the narrow aisles.
