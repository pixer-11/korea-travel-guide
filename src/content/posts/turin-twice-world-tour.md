## TWICE in Turin, May 20, 2026

K-pop girl group TWICE plays one night in Turin. The date is May 20, 2026, and the venue is Inalpi Arena, a stop on the group's world tour. TWICE are signed to JYP Entertainment and debuted in Seoul in 2015. Their fandom calls itself ONCE.

The organiser publishes the setlist details, door times and ticket links on its official site. This page sticks to the venue, the city and how the night usually runs.

## Inside Inalpi Arena

Inalpi Arena is the building Turin has known as Palasport Olimpico and later Pala Alpitour. It was built for ice hockey at the 2006 Winter Olympics and designed by Japanese architect Arata Isozaki. From outside it is a long, low steel-and-glass box. Inside, a flat arena floor sits below tiered seating on all sides.

It is one of Italy's largest indoor venues. It also hosts tennis's ATP Finals each November, so the sightlines are built for watching a central floor. For concerts, the stage usually takes one end of the arena, with a standing floor in front of it.

Next door are:

- **Stadio Olimpico Grande Torino**, Torino FC's football ground, across the square
- **Parco Ruffini**, a large park a short walk west, with grass and benches for the wait before doors
- **Corso Sebastopoli and Via Filadelfia**, the main roads around the venue, lined with bars and pizzerias

## Getting to Santa Rita

The arena is in Santa Rita, a residential district roughly 3 km south-west of Torino Porta Nuova station. GTT runs the city's trams and buses from the centre to the stadium area. Taxis wait at Porta Nuova and Porta Susa. Plenty of fans also walk the 40 minutes or so through the Crocetta district.

Out-of-town fans fly into Torino-Caselle airport or arrive by high-speed train from Milan, about an hour away. Fans travelling for the show usually stay near Porta Nuova or Piazza San Carlo, where the hotels, arcades and late cafés cluster. Driving is harder on show nights. Parking around the stadium fills quickly whenever both venues have events.

## Show-night habits at the Olympic arena

ONCE fans follow a few rituals, and most attendees join in:

- **Lightsticks:** Fans bring the official Candybong lightstick, which often syncs to the show through a phone app.
- **Fan chants:** The crowd shouts the members' names in a set order during song intros. Fan videos posted online teach them.
- **Banners and freebies:** Fan groups often hand out free slogans and photocards outside the arena before doors open.
- **Queueing:** Standing-ticket holders usually form lines along the outside of the arena hours before doors. Seated fans tend to arrive later.

For big concerts, Italian tickets are often issued in the buyer's name, and ID checks at the gate are common. Arena bag rules usually turn away large backpacks, so fans tend to carry small bags. Bars inside take cards.

Turin's evening routine fits around a weeknight show. Aperitivo runs from early evening in the bars of San Salvario and Piazza Vittorio Veneto, and a drink buys you a buffet of small plates. The next morning, a bicerin is the city's own drink: layered espresso, chocolate and cream served in a small glass. Al Bicerin, the café facing the Santuario della Consolata, has served it since 1763.
