## Inside the Casino de Paris

The auditorium is smaller than its name suggests. It holds roughly 1,500 people, which puts even the back of the balcony within clear sight of faces on stage. The décor is classic French theatre: red seating, gilded details and curved balcony fronts facing a proscenium stage.

The ground floor, the *fosse*, can be set up as standing or seated depending on the show. That choice shapes the night. A standing floor fills from the front barrier outward, so the front of the queue ends up closest to the stage. Balcony seats give the best view of formation changes, which matters for a group whose choreography is half the show.

## Around the venue before and after

The 9th arrondissement gives you plenty nearby. The Église de la Sainte-Trinité and its small square sit at the bottom of rue de Clichy. The Galeries Lafayette department store and the Palais Garnier opera house are about fifteen minutes' walk south, worth a look if you come into the area in the afternoon.

Up the hill, Place de Clichy leads east along the boulevard to Pigalle and the Moulin Rouge, with Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur above. The streets between Clichy and Saint-Georges are full of brasseries and late-opening bars.
