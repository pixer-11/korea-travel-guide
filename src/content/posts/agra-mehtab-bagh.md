## What you're actually looking at

Mehtab Bagh sits directly across the Yamuna River from the Taj Mahal, on its central north-south axis. Mughal emperor Babur laid out the original garden in the 1500s as one of eleven riverside gardens along this stretch. Shah Jahan later used the site as a viewing point for the Taj, then still under construction. Centuries of flooding buried the formal charbagh layout in silt; archaeologists only excavated and partly restored it in the 1990s.

What remains is a grid of walking paths, an octagonal pool, and low brick foundations marking where pavilions once stood.

## Getting there and getting in

The garden sits in Nagla Devjit, on the eastern bank, roughly a 20-minute drive or auto-rickshaw ride around the river from the Taj's main entrance. There's no direct bridge crossing nearby, so budget time for the loop.

- Open daily, 6am to 6pm
- Entry ticket is separate from the Taj Mahal ticket; buy it at the gate
- Carry ID; foreign nationals typically pay a higher rate than domestic visitors
- No security queues like the Taj; expect a quick bag check

Sunrise is the busiest slot here specifically because it delivers the reflected, front-on Taj shot without the crowds inside the mausoleum grounds. Sunset works too, when the marble catches the fading light from behind.

## Reading the ruins

Walk the central path toward the river and the Taj rises framed by the garden's symmetrical planting, its dome centered on the axis exactly as Shah Jahan intended. The octagonal pool near the riverbank once mirrored the monument; it still does on calm mornings. Low brick walls and plinths scattered through the grounds are unexcavated or partially restored structures, not modern landscaping. Give the site 45 minutes to an hour: it's compact, and most of that time goes to photography rather than walking.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

This garden shows up on every serious Agra photography itinerary now, so it draws real crowds, particularly at sunrise. Getting to the gate at opening, 6am, beats the bulk of tour buses that arrive after breakfast. Weekday mornings run noticeably calmer than weekends. Bring small notes for the entry fee since gate staff often can't break large bills. Pair the visit with Itmad-ud-Daulah's Tomb, sometimes called the Baby Taj, a short drive further along the eastern bank, to make a full riverside morning of it without doubling back through central Agra traffic.
