Your blades scrape over ice that sits directly on a 19th-century canal. Office towers rise on one bank and the Gothic Revival towers of Parliament Hill on the other. Winterlude is the festival Ottawa and Gatineau hold each year around the coldest stretch of the calendar. It treats the cold as the main event.

The festival spreads over two cities and two provinces, Ontario and Quebec. It is linked by bridges over the Ottawa River and by kilometres of frozen canal.

## Dates, venue and where tickets live

Winterlude 2027 runs through January and February 2027. The festival is spread over several weekends rather than a single day. The official Winterlude site carries the exact dates, the site map and links for any activities with their own ticketing.

There is no single venue. The festival takes place across several outdoor sites on both sides of the river:

- **The Rideau Canal Skateway**, running from downtown south toward Dows Lake
- **Downtown Ottawa**, where the ice sculptures traditionally sit
- **Gatineau, Quebec**, across the river, home to the snow play areas and big slides
- **Dows Lake and the canal**, where the Ice Dragon Boat Festival is held

Most of what visitors come for is outdoors and open to walk into. Those events have no turnstile, so "tickets" usually means individual add-ons, not entry to the festival.

## The Skateway: a canal you can skate to work on

The Rideau Canal is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Each winter its downtown section is flooded, groomed and opened as the Rideau Canal Skateway, roughly 7.8 kilometres of continuous ice. The National Capital Commission maintains it, and it is Winterlude's backbone.

The ice depends on the weather, not the festival calendar. In a mild year some stretches stay closed, and in a deep freeze the whole length opens. The NCC posts daily ice conditions on its Skateway page.

Along the route you'll find benches for lacing up, heated rest areas and stands selling hot drinks. The standard canal snack is a BeaverTails pastry: fried dough stretched flat and topped with cinnamon sugar. It comes in a paper sleeve you hold with your mittens on.

## Ice sculptures and the Gatineau side

The carving competitions give Winterlude its sharpest images. Teams shape blocks of clear ice into figures, and at dusk lights set into the bases glow up through them. Downtown Ottawa, around Confederation Park and the pedestrian Sparks Street, has traditionally been where most of them stand.

Across the Portage or Alexandra bridges, Gatineau's Jacques-Cartier Park has long been home to the snow playground. Think towering slides, snow forts and sculptures built from packed snow instead of ice.

French is the first language on the Quebec side, so signs and announcements switch accordingly. A "bonjour" goes a long way.

## Dragon boats on frozen ice

The Ice Dragon Boat Festival is the oddest sight of the season, and it is in the festival's own billing. Crews race dragon boats fitted with blades, pushing them across the frozen surface instead of paddling through water. Drummers keep the beat from the bow, as they would in summer.

Spectators line the edges of the ice in snowsuits. Corporate and community teams in matching gear make up a large part of the field. The race has more noise than polish, and the shouting carries a long way in the cold air.

## Skating with the commuters

Locals treat the Skateway as transport, not a ride. On weekday mornings some people skate to work with a backpack and keep a steady pace. They expect slower skaters to stay to the right, much like a cycle lane.

- **Skate rentals and sharpening** are available at stations along the canal. Many visitors rent on the ice instead of carrying skates around the city.
- **Benches by the access stairs** are the usual place to swap boots for skates. People leave their boots in a backpack or a rental locker, never on the ice.
- **Cards and mobile payment** are standard at food stands and rental huts. A little cash still helps at smaller kiosks.
- **Weekday visits** generally mean thinner crowds on the ice. Weekends, especially Saturday afternoons, are when families and visitors from out of town come out in force.

Many people take the O-Train Line 1 downtown. Rideau and Parliament stations are both a short walk from the canal and the downtown sites. Parking near the canal is scarce during the festival, and residents mostly leave the car at home.

## Dressing for minus twenty

Ottawa is among the coldest national capitals in the world. In late January and February, temperatures below −15°C are common, and the wind chill drops it further. Regulars dress in layers, not in one heavy coat.

- **A thermal base layer** under fleece or wool, with a windproof shell on top
- **Insulated, waterproof boots** for the sculpture sites, where snow is packed underfoot
- **A neck warmer or balaclava**: exposed cheeks numb fast on the open canal
- **Thin gloves inside mittens**, so you can lace skates without freezing your fingers
- **A helmet** for children and new skaters, as plenty of locals wear on the canal

Heated chalets and shopping centres near the canal give everyone somewhere to thaw out. The Rideau Centre, connected to Rideau station, is the closest large indoor stop to the north end of the Skateway.

## How long to give it

A rewarding Winterlude day combines a skate on the canal with the sculptures in the late afternoon. Then cross to Gatineau if you still have the energy. Skating the full canal and back takes most beginners a couple of hours, with stops.

Visitors who come from out of town usually set aside a weekend. That covers both cities and allows for the odd afternoon when the ice is closed and the wind is not cooperating.
