Five nights, one calendar flip, and a town laid out around saltwater lagoons. Recap runs from December 29, 2026 to January 2, 2027, so the countdown to 2027 lands right in the middle of the program. This is the festival's first edition in El Gouna.

The format splits between indoors and sand. More than 30 artists perform across the Gouna Concert Hall and a set of beach venues. Electronic music is the core, with a wider culture side built around it.

## The shape of five nights on the Red Sea

The calendar is simple. Day one is December 29 and the last is January 2, which gives you two nights of build-up before New Year's Eve and two nights after it. Many attendees treat it as a full holiday week rather than a single party.

The two kinds of venue feel very different:

- **Gouna Concert Hall**: a roofed, seated-or-standing space, the place for sets that need a proper sound system and walls around them.
- **Beach venues**: open-air, with sand underfoot and the water a few steps away, where the evening wind off the sea is part of the night.

The organiser publishes the full lineup, set times and venue split on its official festival channels. We don't list artists here because none were given to us.

## A resort town built for this

El Gouna is a privately developed resort town about 25 km north of Hurghada, begun around 1990. It sits on a network of artificial lagoons and small islands, crossed by bridges and footpaths. Low, sand-coloured buildings with domes and arches give it a look closer to a Nubian village than a high-rise strip.

That layout matters for a festival. Distances are short, and most hotels sit on a lagoon or the beach. The main hubs are easy to learn quickly:

- **Downtown (Kafr El Gouna)**: squares, cafés and small shops.
- **Abu Tig Marina**: yachts, restaurants and late-opening bars along the quay.
- **Mangroovy Beach**: the kitesurfing beach on the town's northern edge.

## Getting to El Gouna for Recap

Hurghada International Airport (HRG) is the gateway. Over the winter holidays it gets direct flights from much of Europe plus connections through Cairo. The drive north to El Gouna takes around 30 minutes along the coastal highway. Hotels commonly arrange transfers, and taxis wait outside arrivals.

Many nationalities can get an Egyptian visa on arrival or an e-visa beforehand. The rules depend on your passport, and Egypt's official e-visa portal lists them. Travellers from Cairo sometimes drive the desert highway instead, which takes most of a day.

## Late December weather, honestly

Winter on the Red Sea is mild, but it isn't midsummer. Daytime temperatures in late December usually sit in the low 20s °C, with dry air and steady sun. After dark it drops noticeably, and a beach stage at 2am can feel cold, especially when the wind picks up.

The sea stays swimmable for most people, though it's cooler than it is in summer. Daylight ends early, around 5pm. That leaves long evenings for the program and slow mornings for the lagoon.

## Moving between stages after midnight

Getting around after midnight takes a little know-how. Inside the town, the standard ride is a tuk-tuk. They circulate along the main roads and gather near Downtown and the marina, and people generally agree the fare before setting off. The town also runs a public bus network between the hotels and hubs.

Some things festival-goers in El Gouna usually do:

- **Carry Egyptian pounds in small notes** for tuk-tuks, tips and kiosks. Larger venues and hotels take cards, but small change stops arguments over a big note.
- **Tip as a habit.** Small tips (baksheesh) for porters, servers and drivers are normal across Egypt.
- **Pack a layer.** A light jacket or sweater for beach sets is the most useful thing in the bag.
- **Pace the week.** With five nights in a row, regulars often skip one afternoon for sleep or a slow lagoon swim.

Away from the stages, Egypt is a conservative country. El Gouna itself is relaxed and swimwear is normal at the beaches. Visitors heading out to Hurghada or beyond usually cover up more.

## Filling the daylight hours

The days are yours. Mangroovy Beach is one of the best-known kitesurfing spots on the coast, and its schools rent gear and teach beginners. Boat trips leave from the marinas to snorkel the coral reefs offshore. Giftun Islands trips depart from the Hurghada side.

For a change of scenery, desert excursions into the Eastern Desert are sold all over town. Abu Tig Marina is the usual place for a long lunch with the boats in front of you. Downtown suits a slower coffee-and-wander morning after a late night.
