Stand at the railing behind Customs House and Marina Bay opens up in front of you. Marina Bay Sands' three towers and their ship-shaped SkyPark sit on the far shore. The ArtScience Museum's white petals are to the left of them, and the bay's dark water takes up everything in between.

City View is the name on the map for this stretch of Collyer Quay at number 70. It doesn't get many reviews, but the few it has are very good. It's a quieter find than the Merlion's crowded viewing deck a short walk north, and it looks at the same skyline.

## A former customs post with the best seat on the bay

Customs House is a low waterfront building. As the name says, it was built for the harbour's customs work, back when Collyer Quay was the edge of a working port. Today the quay is a promenade, and the building's position puts you right on the water at the bay's southwestern corner.

That corner matters. From here you see the whole curve of Marina Bay in one frame:

- **Straight ahead:** Marina Bay Sands and its SkyPark
- **Left:** the ArtScience Museum and the Helix Bridge
- **Behind you:** the glass towers of Raffles Place
- **Right, along the quay:** Clifford Pier and the Fullerton Bay Hotel

Google files the spot under Chinatown. In practice you're in the financial district, about a 10–15 minute walk from the shophouse streets around Pagoda Street.

## Getting to Collyer Quay

The MRT is the easiest way in. **Downtown station** (Downtown Line, DT17) is the closest. **Raffles Place** (East-West and North-South Lines) is a few minutes further inland. From either one, head toward the water and follow signs for Collyer Quay or Customs House.

Coming from Chinatown on foot, take Cross Street east and then cut through the Telok Ayer area toward the bay. The walk takes you from temple roofs to office towers in about a quarter of an hour.

Taxis and ride-hailing cars can drop you on Collyer Quay itself. Fares on the MRT are paid by tapping a contactless bank card or phone at the gate, so you don't need a separate ticket.

## Spectra from across the water

Most visitors watch the light show from the wrong side of the bay. Spectra, the free light-and-water show staged at the Event Plaza in front of Marina Bay Sands, is designed to face the water. That puts Customs House close to head-on, with the towers behind the lasers and the reflections running across the bay toward you.

You won't hear the music as well from here as you would on the plaza. You do get the whole picture: fountains, projections and skyline in one view, without standing in the crowd pressed against the Event Plaza steps.

Show times change, so check the Marina Bay Sands website on the day. Get to the railing a few minutes early, because the best spots are on the front edge.

## Day versus night

City View is listed as open 24 hours, and as an open waterfront it's easy to drop by at any time. Each part of the day gives you a different view.

- **Morning:** the light is behind you, so the towers across the bay are lit from the front. Office workers stream along the quay toward Raffles Place.
- **Midday:** the heat and glare off the water are at their worst. There's little shade on the open promenade.
- **Sunset into dusk:** the sky behind Marina Bay Sands goes dark while the building lights come on. Photographers come for this changeover.
- **Late night:** the bay goes quiet, and the skyline stays lit after the crowds have gone home.

Singapore is hot and humid all year. Short, heavy afternoon showers are common, especially in the monsoon months from November to January. An evening visit is the more comfortable choice.

## Sharing the quay after dark

Small habits matter here, because Singapore enforces rules that visitors often don't know about.

- **Drinking outdoors has a curfew.** Under Singapore's liquor laws, drinking alcohol in public places is banned from 10:30pm to 7am. A beer at the railing at 11pm can get you fined, so finish it at a licensed bar.
- **Litter gets fined.** Bins are spread out along the promenade. Hold onto your rubbish until you reach one.
- **Tripods are fine, but keep them out of the way.** Set up against the railing, not across the walkway, because joggers and commuters use the quay as a through route.
- **Leave the front edge to others once you're done.** When the light show starts, it's polite to step back after your photos so the people behind you get a turn.

If you're eating nearby, the hawker-centre custom applies. People "chope" (reserve) a table by leaving a tissue packet on it. Don't sit down at a table marked that way.

## Eating within walking distance

You don't need a restaurant booking to eat well around here.

- **Lau Pa Sat:** the Victorian-era cast-iron market hall on Raffles Quay, a short walk inland. In the evenings, Boon Tat Street beside it closes to traffic and fills with satay stalls grilling over charcoal.
- **Telok Ayer and Amoy Street:** shophouse blocks full of cafés and bars, on the walk back toward Chinatown.
- **Chinatown Complex Food Centre:** further in, on Smith Street. It's a large hawker centre for when you've crossed back into Chinatown proper.

Customs House itself has places to eat facing the water. Their hours and menus change, so check what's open before you plan around them.

## Building a route around it

City View works best as one stop on a walk, not a trip on its own. Here's a loop that takes an evening:

1. Start in Chinatown at the **Buddha Tooth Relic Temple** on South Bridge Road.
2. Walk east past **Thian Hock Keng Temple** on Telok Ayer Street.
3. Eat at **Lau Pa Sat**.
4. Continue to Customs House for the bay view and the light show.
5. Follow the waterfront north past **Clifford Pier** to **Merlion Park**.

Plan on 20–40 minutes at the viewpoint itself, and longer if you're waiting for the show or the sunset. The full loop is about three hours at an easy pace. If you'd rather keep walking, the waterfront promenade carries on around the bay to the Helix Bridge and Marina Bay Sands.
