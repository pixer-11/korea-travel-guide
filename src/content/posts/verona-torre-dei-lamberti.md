You spot it before you reach it — a brick shaft rising well above every rooftop around Piazza delle Erbe, its upper stage a different, paler stone from a later century. That mismatch is the first clue to its story: begun in 1172, finished in fits and starts over 300 years.

## Why go

The reward here is simple: Verona from above, with the Adige River bending around the old town and the Lessini hills on the horizon. On clear days you can pick out the Arena's oval and the hills of Valpolicella beyond the rooftops.

At 84 metres, it's the tallest structure in the historic centre, so nothing blocks the view. Two open-air levels mean you can shuffle around the perimeter without fighting one packed railing.

## Getting there

The tower sits on Via della Costa, tucked at the edge of Piazza delle Erbe, Verona's old Roman forum turned market square. If you're staying anywhere in the centro storico, it's walkable — most hotels are within 15 minutes.

From Porta Nuova train station, take a city bus toward Piazza Bra (lines 11, 12, 13, or 51/52/53) and walk north through Via Mazzini, the pedestrian shopping street that spills directly into the piazza. The tower's entrance is easy to miss: a narrow archway off the square, not a grand front door.

## What you'll find inside

Inside, the climb splits between a lift and a staircase — you can go up one way and down the other, which is worth doing since the stairs pass narrow window slits that frame the city in slices. The steps are worn smooth and uneven in places, a genuine 800-year-old surface underfoot.

At the top, the platform is caged with railings and mesh, practical rather than picturesque, but the view through it is unobstructed. You'll also pass the tower's old bell, Rengo, which once rang out civic announcements and alarms across the city.

Look down and you'll see the geometry of Piazza delle Erbe laid out like a map — striped market umbrellas, the fountain at its centre, and the Case Mazzanti's frescoed facades nearby.

## When to go

The tower keeps different hours depending on the day:

- Monday–Friday: 10am–6pm
- Saturday–Sunday: 11am–7pm

Crowds build steadily through the day. The calmest window is 10am–11am on weekdays, right after opening, before tour groups arrive. Weekend afternoons, especially noon through 7pm, are the busiest stretch — if you can, visit on a weekday morning instead.

Plan for 45 minutes to an hour total, more if you're waiting for the lift during a busy spell.

## How to visit like a local

This is a well-known, well-visited landmark, not a quiet find, so the smart move is timing rather than secrecy.

- Arrive right at weekday opening (10am) for the shortest lines and clearest photos.
- Skip weekend early afternoons entirely if you can shift your visit to a weekday.
- Buy tickets on-site or online in advance during peak season to skip the ticket-window queue — check current prices before you go, as they do change.
- Wear shoes with grip; the staircase treads are polished from centuries of feet.
- If heights or enclosed stairwells bother you, take the lift up and walk down, or vice versa, rather than committing to stairs both ways.
- Combine the visit with the rest of Piazza delle Erbe and the nearby Piazza dei Signori, both a two-minute walk away, so the trip up doesn't feel like an isolated errand.

Most visitors treat the tower as a quick stop between the Arena and the piazzas, but giving it its own unhurried hour — especially in that quiet morning window — is what actually makes the climb feel worth it.
