Six days, five draws, one arena. The 2027 Malaysia Open runs from Tuesday, January 5 to Sunday, January 10. It opens the badminton year at Axiata Arena in Bukit Jalil, the domed indoor hall inside Kuala Lumpur's National Sports Complex.

It is a BWF Super 1000 tournament, the top tier of the World Tour below the season-ending finals. That status brings the leading shuttlers from China, Japan, Korea and Chinese Taipei to Kuala Lumpur, alongside Malaysia's own players.

## A season opener with real weight

Few tournaments in the sport go back this far. The Malaysia Open dates to the 1930s, and Lee Chong Wei won it more times than anyone. That history explains why the stands treat it as a national occasion and not just another week on the tour.

Five titles are decided:

- Men's singles
- Women's singles
- Men's doubles
- Women's doubles
- Mixed doubles

The draw narrows across the week. Early rounds put several courts in play at once, so a single session can show you a dozen matches. By the weekend it comes down to one court and the semi-finals and finals, with the arena lights trained on it.

## Inside Axiata Arena

The arena is a large circular hall with tiered seating rising steeply on every side. Even the upper rows look down on the court from a fairly close angle. The air conditioning runs hard. Plenty of fans carry a light jacket, because January in Kuala Lumpur is hot and humid outside and the change at the door is sharp.

Malaysian crowds are loud. Inflatable clappers, drums and long chants of "Malaysia boleh!" build whenever a home player or pair walks out. In a tight third game the hall can drown out the umpire's call. Neutral fans often say the noise is the reason they come.

## Getting to Bukit Jalil

The rail link is simple:

- **Bukit Jalil LRT station** on the Sri Petaling Line (LRT Line 4) sits beside the National Sports Complex, and a short covered walk brings you to the arena.
- **From KL Sentral**, riders take the Kelana Jaya Line to Masjid Jamek, then change to the Sri Petaling Line heading south.
- **From the airport**, the KLIA Transit train stops at Bandar Tasik Selatan, where you can change to the Sri Petaling Line.

Grab is widely used for the ride, and drivers know the arena by name. The complex has parking. On finals day the roads around Bukit Jalil Stadium next door slow down as the sessions empty out, so many fans just take the train.

## Tickets and sessions

The Badminton Association of Malaysia hosts the tournament and publishes the ticket links, seating plans and session schedule on its official channels. No prices appear in this guide: any figure here would be a guess. The official ticketing page shows the current categories.

Early-round tickets usually cover a session, not a whole day, and those days often run as separate daytime and evening blocks. Weekend semi-final and final sessions are the most sought after. Fans who mainly want to see a lot of badminton often pick a midweek session, when several courts run at once and more seats are free.

## Cheering, seats and the walk home

Clapping between rallies is welcome. During a point, the crowd goes quiet just before serve, and the umpire will ask for silence if it doesn't. Flash photography is off-limits during play because it distracts players tracking a fast shuttle.

A few habits regulars tend to share:

- They move seats or head to the concourse only between games or at the interval at 11 points, not mid-rally.
- They bring a Malaysian flag or clapper if they want to join the home end. Visiting fans with their own colours are common too.
- They pay by card or e-wallet at most food stalls and in the nearby mall. Touch 'n Go cards cover the LRT.
- They go straight for the LRT after evening sessions, since Grab demand jumps once the arena empties.

The Malay name to know is *Kejohanan Badminton Terbuka Malaysia*, which you'll see on local signage and in the Malay-language press.

## Around the arena

Bukit Jalil is a suburban district south of the city centre, and the sports complex dominates it. Pavilion Bukit Jalil, a large shopping mall nearby, is where many fans eat between sessions. Its food court and restaurants handle crowds far better than the arena concourse does.

Bukit Jalil Recreational Park, a green space with walking paths, sits close to the complex for a quiet stretch between sessions. Visitors staying several days usually base themselves in Bukit Bintang or around KL Sentral and ride the LRT out each day. The trip takes roughly half an hour, depending on connections.
