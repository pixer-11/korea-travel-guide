Stand on the footbridge over Estrada do Istmo after sunset and turn slowly. On one side, a half-scale Eiffel Tower is lit gold. Behind you, a replica of Big Ben glows above The Londoner. Across the road, the steel lattice of Morpheus has a hole cut through its middle. None of this land existed a generation ago, and the buildings seem to know it.

## Land that used to be sea

Cotai is reclaimed ground. The name joins **Co**loane and **Tai**pa, the two islands whose shallow channel was filled in from the 1990s onward. The resorts arrived in the 2000s, starting with The Venetian Macao in 2007. Today the strip is one of the densest clusters of integrated resorts anywhere.

That history explains the layout. The road is wide, straight and flat. The resorts are enormous, set back behind driveways, so a building that looks next door can take ten minutes to reach on foot. Treat the strip as a set of linked indoor worlds rather than a street you stroll along.

## Getting there

Most visitors never pay to reach Cotai. The big resorts run free shuttle buses from the Macau Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal, Taipa Ferry Terminal and the Border Gate. You don't need a room booking to ride, though queues at peak ferry arrival times can be long.

Other options:

- **Macau Light Rapid Transit, Taipa Line:** Cotai East and Cotai West stations sit closest to the resorts, with Lotus station near the Lotus Bridge crossing to Hengqin.
- **Taxi:** easy to find at every resort's taxi rank, harder to hail on the road itself.
- **From Macau airport:** a short taxi ride, or the Taipa Line from Airport station.

## The resorts, north to south

You can't see everything in one night, so pick three or four.

- **The Venetian Macao:** indoor canals with gondolas under a painted sky ceiling, lined with shops. It's the anchor of the strip.
- **The Parisian Macao:** the Eiffel Tower replica out front has an observation deck high up, and the views down Estrada do Istmo are the best overview you'll get.
- **The Londoner Macao:** formerly Sands Cotai Central, rebuilt with a Houses of Parliament façade and red-phone-box styling inside.
- **City of Dreams:** home to Morpheus, the Zaha Hadid hotel opened in 2018, with its exposed exoskeleton and three voids through the tower.
- **Galaxy Macau and Studio City:** a little west, worth it if you want the full sweep of the skyline.
- **Wynn Palace:** at the northern end near the Lotus area, known for its performance lake fountain and the SkyCab gondola that glides over it.

## After dark on Estrada do Istmo

The strip makes most sense at night. The editorial line on the place says it plainly: huge hotel casinos, illuminated, lining one buzzing road. Daytime shows you concrete and car parks; after sunset every façade becomes a light display.

Time it like this. Arrive in the late afternoon and eat inside one of the resorts. Then head up to the Parisian's tower deck or a footbridge as the lights come on. Because the strip runs 24 hours, nothing closes on you. The malls and restaurants keep their own hours, so check those before you head out.

Footbridges and covered walkways link several resorts. Using them means you can cross from The Venetian to The Parisian to The Londoner without dealing with traffic. In the humid summer months, that air-conditioned route matters more than any map.

## Casino floors: the rules on the door

Gaming floors run on clear rules, and staff enforce them.

- **Age:** you must be 21 to enter a gaming area. Carry your passport, because security at the entrance may ask for it.
- **Photos:** don't photograph the tables or gaming floor. Shopping arcades, lobbies and façades are fine.
- **Currency:** Macau uses the pataca, and Hong Kong dollars are accepted almost everywhere on the strip. Many tables deal in Hong Kong dollar chips.
- **Tipping:** restaurants usually add a service charge to the bill. Beyond that, tipping is optional.
- **Dress:** smart-casual passes everywhere. Beachwear and bare feet don't.

You don't have to gamble to enjoy any of this. Most people on the strip are there for the shopping, the restaurants, the shows and the spectacle. The casino floors are simply one room among many.

## Escaping the neon

The real Macau is a short walk away. **Taipa Village**, just north of the strip, has the narrow pedestrian lanes around Rua do Cunha, with bakeries selling almond cookies and pork-chop buns. The pastel Taipa Houses-Museum sits nearby along the old waterfront. Going there in daylight, then returning to Cotai for the lights, makes for a well-paced day.

South of the strip, Coloane is quieter still. It has the village square around the Chapel of St Francis Xavier, plus Hac Sa beach. A taxi from the southern end of Cotai gets you there quickly.

## Pacing a visit

Allow three to five hours for a first look at the strip, longer if you're booking a show or a long dinner. A full day covers Taipa Village in the morning, resort interiors through the heat of the afternoon, and the illuminated road after dark.

Summer is hot, humid and in typhoon season, so the indoor links between resorts earn their keep then. Autumn and early winter bring drier, clearer evenings, which suit the observation deck and the footbridges. Weekends and Chinese public holidays bring big crowds from the mainland and Hong Kong. Shuttle queues and restaurant waits grow at those times, so a weekday visit is usually smoother.
