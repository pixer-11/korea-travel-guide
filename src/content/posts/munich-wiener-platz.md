## A market square on the Haidhausen side

Wiener Platz is small, and that is the appeal. A ring of permanent market stalls sits at its centre. They sell fruit, vegetables, cheese, flowers and snacks every season of the year, and the fountain sits among them.

Above them rises a blue-and-white Bavarian maypole, carved with figures that represent local trades. Trams run past along Innere Wiener Straße. Old Haidhausen houses line the edges of the square. It feels like a working neighbourhood market rather than something built for visitors, though plenty of visitors do find it.

## Getting to Wiener Platz

The square is on the east bank of the Isar, a short distance uphill from the Maximilianeum.

- **Tram:** lines 15 and 25 stop at Wiener Platz, directly on the square.
- **U-Bahn:** Max-Weber-Platz (U4/U5) is a walk of a few minutes west.
- **On foot from the old town:** cross the Isar at the Maximiliansbrücke, pass the Maximilianeum and climb Innere Wiener Straße. It takes roughly 25 minutes from Marienplatz.

The square itself is open 24 hours. Each stall keeps its own hours, so if you are coming to buy something, check before you go. Mornings and late afternoons are the obvious times when the stalls are trading.

## Eating, drinking and lingering

In summer the Hofbräukeller beer garden fills the corner of the square under its chestnut trees. It is one of the easiest beer gardens to reach from central Munich. Long wooden tables and litre mugs are the standard set-up.

At the market stalls you can put together the classic beer-garden picnic:

- Obatzda (spiced cheese spread) and radish
- Fresh pretzels
- Cold cuts and seasonal fruit
- Flowers, if you're visiting friends

From the square it's easy to walk to more of Haidhausen:

- **Müllersches Volksbad**, the Art Nouveau swimming hall, down by the river
- **The Gasteig cultural centre**
- **The Isar meadows**, a good place to carry the rest of your picnic
- **Johannisplatz and the church of St. Johann Baptist**, a few streets east

## Beer-garden rules at Wiener Platz

Bavarian beer gardens follow a long-standing custom. At bare, self-service tables you may bring your own food. Tables laid with cloths are for table service, so don't unpack your picnic there. Drinks always come from the garden itself; never bring your own.

Mugs and glasses usually carry a deposit (*Pfand*). At the self-service counters you pick up your drink, pay and return the mug when you're done.

At the market stalls, carry some cash. Smaller vendors often prefer it even when they take cards. Don't handle the produce: say what you want and how much, and the stallholder picks it out. A *Grüß Gott* when you step up is the local greeting.

May Day is the year's big moment on the square. Munich neighbourhoods traditionally put up a new maypole every few years, with music, beer and a crowd. If your visit falls around 1 May, look for local announcements. Otherwise, a warm weekday evening in the beer garden is when the square is at its most sociable.
