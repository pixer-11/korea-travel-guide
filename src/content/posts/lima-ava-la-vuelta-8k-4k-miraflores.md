## Two distances, one Sunday by the Pacific

AVA La Vuelta takes place on Sunday, December 20, 2026, in Miraflores. There are two distances. The 8K is just under five miles. The 4K is the shorter option, and newer runners and walkers usually take it.

Miraflores sits on cliffs above the Pacific, with the Costa Verde highway running along the beach below. The Malecón follows the clifftop past Parque del Amor and the Larcomar shopping centre. Parque Kennedy and Avenida Larco are a few blocks inland.

December marks the start of Lima's summer. The low grey winter cloud usually begins to thin by this point. Mornings can still start overcast, and the sun often appears later in the day.

## Registration and race kits

The start location, start time and entry fees are on the organiser's registration page. This guide gives none of them.

Most Lima road races work the same way. Registration, called *la inscripción*, typically covers:

- a race number (*número* or *dorsal*)
- a timing chip, often attached to the bib
- a race shirt (*polo*, as Peruvians say)

Runners usually collect this at a kit pickup (*entrega de kits*) in the days before the race. On the morning itself, the course is full of matching race shirts.

## Reaching Miraflores on race morning

Lima's traffic is at its lightest on Sunday mornings, so the race-day trip is usually easier than on a weekday.

- **Metropolitano bus rapid transit:** the Ricardo Palma and Benavides stations on the Vía Expresa serve Miraflores. From either one, the clifftop is roughly a 20-minute walk west.
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber, Cabify and InDrive all operate in Lima. When streets around a course are closed, drivers drop riders at the nearest open corner.
- **Staying nearby:** many visitors base themselves in Miraflores or in neighbouring Barranco. From there the start area is close enough to walk.

The district is laid out on a grid, so a walk from the Larco hotels to the coast takes 10–15 minutes.

## How race morning works in Miraflores

Spanish is the language of the race. Marshals at turns call out *derecha* (right) and *izquierda* (left). Water stops are *puntos de hidratación*. Bag drop, if the organiser runs one, is the *guardarropa*.

Lima sits close to the equator, so the December sun is strong even under cloud. Local runners typically wear sunscreen and a cap, and many keep sunglasses on after the cloud lifts.

Most people pay small amounts with phones. The Yape and Plin apps are everywhere in Peru, though they need a local bank account. Visitors tend to carry a few small soles notes for drinks from vendors near the finish. Cafés on Avenida Larco take cards.

After the race, finishers usually walk to breakfast. Avenida Larco and the streets around Parque Kennedy are full of cafés and juice bars. The Malecón is a flat clifftop cool-down walk, with paragliders taking off from Parque Raimondi when the wind is right. Barranco's Puente de los Suspiros is about 30 minutes south on foot.
