## A one-man band at the end of the line

The stage is mostly instruments. FKJ, the French musician Vincent Fenton, performs as French Kiwi Juice. His live sets have a reputation for one thing above all: one person building every song while you listen. He records a guitar riff, loops it, moves to the keys, adds a bassline, then reaches for a saxophone.

On December 3, 2026, that setup comes to AsiaWorld-Expo Hall 10 in Hong Kong. AsiaWorld-Expo is the exhibition and concert complex on Chek Lap Kok, the island that holds the city's airport. You make the trip from the skyline to a big hall at the edge of the runway, and the evening takes much of its shape from that journey.

## What an FKJ set sounds like

FKJ came up through the French house scene from Tours. He sits where nu-jazz, R&B and electronic music overlap. His self-titled debut album, *French Kiwi Juice*, came out in 2017. In the same year, "Tadow", a looping jam with the singer and saxophonist Masego, spread online and introduced him to a crowd well beyond dance music.

Songs a long-time fan tends to know:

- **"Tadow"**, the improvised session with Masego that made his name
- **"Ylang Ylang"**, whose live video was filmed on the salt flats of Bolivia
- **"Skyline"**, the warm instrumental that opens many fans' playlists
- **"Die With A Smile"**, shorter and built around his vocal

## The Airport Express run to Lantau

The train is part of the night out. AsiaWorld-Expo has its own MTR station, the final stop on the **Airport Express**, one stop beyond Airport station. The line starts at Hong Kong Station in Central, with stops at Kowloon and Tsing Yi. From Central the trip takes about half an hour, crossing the Tsing Ma Bridge as the harbour drops away behind you.

When you leave the station, the exhibition halls are directly ahead, on the same level. Hall 10 is one of the numbered exhibition halls, separate from the AsiaWorld-Arena. Signs inside the complex point to each hall by number, so it's easy to find.

Other ways people get there:

- **Octopus card**: most people tap through the Airport Express gates with one
- **Taxi**: urban (red) and Lantau (blue) taxis both serve the airport area
- **Bus**: the Cityflyer and other airport routes link Chek Lap Kok with Kowloon, the New Territories and Hong Kong Island

## Getting home after the encore

The trip back takes the most thought. Thousands of people leave Hall 10 together and walk straight to one station platform. Concertgoers usually expect a queue at the Airport Express gates and let the first train or two go by while the crowd thins.

If you're staying in Tung Chung, it's a short taxi or bus ride from the airport island. If you're heading back to Kowloon or the Island, the overnight "N" airport buses are the backup when trains finish. Some fans skip the trip altogether and stay at the hotels next to the complex.

## Inside the hall, Hong Kong style

Hong Kong concert crowds are polite and react fast. Queues for entry, merchandise and the toilets form in neat lines, and people expect you to join at the back. Cantonese and English signs appear together throughout AsiaWorld-Expo, and staff can handle both languages.

A few habits help you blend in:

- **Paying**: Octopus is accepted almost everywhere on the airport island, cards are common, and nobody expects tips at the bars or stalls
- **Phones**: crowds tend to go quiet during FKJ's slow builds, so a phone held high through a whole loop blocks the view for everyone behind you
- **Layers**: December evenings in Hong Kong are cool and dry, often in the mid-teens Celsius, while the hall itself is warm and air-conditioned

## Food and a bed near Chek Lap Kok

There isn't much around AsiaWorld-Expo, which is why most people eat before the show. **SkyCity** and the airport terminal food courts are one train stop away and serve noodles, dim sum and fast food late into the evening. The **Hong Kong SkyCity Marriott** is next to the expo complex. The **Regal Airport Hotel** is connected to the terminal.

**Tung Chung** is a few minutes away and has more choice. Citygate Outlets and the food streets around Tung Chung MTR station have local restaurants and chains. It's also the base for the Ngong Ping 360 cable car up to the Tian Tan Buddha, an easy way to turn the concert day into a full day on Lantau.

## Making a day of it on Lantau

The concert is in the evening, so the daytime is free. A common route goes up Ngong Ping by cable car in the morning, takes a bus down to Tai O fishing village for its stilt houses, and finishes with an early dinner in Tung Chung. From there, AsiaWorld-Expo is a short ride away.

If you're flying in or out around December 3, this is one of the few big Hong Kong shows where the airport is right next door. Travellers on a stopover sometimes fit in the concert and leave their luggage at the terminal.
