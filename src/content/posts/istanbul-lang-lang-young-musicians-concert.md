## Two December nights on Taksim Square

Taksim Square is Istanbul's busiest open space. One whole side of it belongs to the Atatürk Cultural Centre, the city's flagship performing-arts house, which everyone calls AKM. On December 1-2, 2026, the Chinese pianist Lang Lang shares its stage with young musicians.

That pairing fits him. Lang Lang has spent years championing young players, including through his own music foundation. A concert built around him and a younger generation of performers is a familiar format in his career. No programme or performer list is part of what we know, so this guide sticks to the dates, the venue and the practical side of the evening.

## The pianist at the centre of it

Lang Lang is one of the best-known classical musicians alive. He came to fame as a teenager and went on to play the world's major concert halls and orchestras. He is known for big Romantic repertoire, Liszt, Chopin and Rachmaninoff among it, and for a physical, expressive way of playing that makes his name familiar well beyond regular concertgoers.

That reach shapes the audience. Expect a crowd that mixes Istanbul's committed classical listeners with families and students who came because of the name on the poster.

## Inside the Atatürk Cultural Centre

AKM has stood on the square since the late 1960s. The current building has a long glass front facing Taksim, and behind it you can see the red, rounded shell of the main hall. Coming out of the metro, you see the building before you see much else.

Inside, the foyers run along the glass, so the square is visible during the interval. Doors, seating plans and the hall used for each night are set by the venue. AKM sells tickets for its own programme through its official website and the box office in the building.

## Getting to Taksim on a concert night

The metro is the simplest option. Taksim station on the **M2** line opens onto the square a short walk from AKM's entrance. Other options:

- **From the Old City (Sultanahmet):** take the T1 tram to Kabataş, then the F1 funicular, which climbs straight up to Taksim in a couple of minutes.
- **From the Asian side:** ferries run to Kabataş, where the funicular starts.
- **Paying:** an Istanbulkart, the city's reloadable transit card, works on the metro, tram, funicular and ferries.

Taxis tend to slow to a crawl around the square in the evening. Most concertgoers coming from any distance use the rail lines instead.

## Concert manners at AKM

The etiquette is standard concert-hall practice, and Istanbul audiences follow it closely. Here is what usually happens on the night:

- **Coats:** December evenings in Istanbul are typically cool and damp. Most people leave heavy coats at the cloakroom so they aren't holding them in narrow rows.
- **Arrival:** concertgoers usually reach Taksim with time to clear the entrance security check and find their seats. Latecomers are generally held at the door until a suitable break.
- **Applause:** in multi-movement works, the hall usually waits until the end of the whole piece. When in doubt, follow the people around you.
- **Phones:** photos and filming during the performance are not welcome. Many people take a picture of the hall before the lights go down.
- **Dress:** smart-casual is the norm. You don't need formal wear.

A few Turkish words help at the door and the bar: *bilet* (ticket), *salon* (hall), *teşekkürler* (thank you).

## Before and after: Beyoğlu around the hall

Istiklal Avenue starts at the edge of Taksim Square. It is a long pedestrian street of shops, cafés and side streets full of meyhanes, the Turkish taverns that serve mezze and rakı until late. The red nostalgic tram runs down its middle.

Gezi Park, the green space just behind the square, is a quiet place to walk before a concert. Further down Istiklal you reach Galatasaray and then the Galata Tower area. That walk is a common way to finish the evening, ending above the Golden Horn.

Many visitors who come to Istanbul for a concert stay in Beyoğlu, Cihangir or around Şişhane. From there they can walk home after the encore without dealing with transport.
