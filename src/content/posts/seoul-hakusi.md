## Why Hakusi stands out

Hakusi belongs to Seoul's growing wave of Japanese-influenced tasting-menu restaurants, but its calling card is as much visual as it is culinary. The reopened space in Cheongdam-dong is deliberately composed like a still-life painting — thoughtfully arranged light, ceramics, and framing that make each course feel staged rather than simply served. That design-forward identity, combined with fresh Michelin recognition for 2026, has put it firmly on the radar of Seoul's dining cognoscenti.

A much-cited example of the kitchen's style has been unagi (freshwater eel) paired with sansho pepper and basil — a dish that shows how the chefs lean on Japanese technique while folding in unexpected, almost Mediterranean, herbal notes.

## Where it is and why the location matters

Cheongdam-dong sits in Gangnam-gu, long known as Seoul's epicenter of luxury boutiques, flagship fashion houses, and quiet, expense-account dining rooms. Hakusi's move into a newly designed space here places it alongside some of the city's most polished restaurants and galleries, reinforcing the neighborhood's reputation as a place where design and food are treated with equal seriousness. Expect a discreet, low-signage storefront typical of the area's high-end restaurants — part of the appeal of Cheongdam-dong is that its best addresses rarely shout.

## Getting there

Cheongdam-dong is best reached via Cheongdam Station on Seoul Subway Line 7, with the surrounding blocks also walkable from Apgujeong Rodeo Station on the Bundang Line, another well-known Gangnam dining and shopping hub. Because exact building entrances in this district can be tucked behind boutiques or into upper floors, it's worth pulling up the restaurant's location on a mapping app right before you leave your hotel, and building in a few extra minutes to find the entrance.

## What to expect at the table

Given its Michelin recognition and tasting-menu format, Hakusi should be approached the way you'd approach any serious omakase-style restaurant: expect a set progression of small, technique-driven courses rather than an à la carte menu you can freely customize. The unagi with sansho and basil is a useful signal of the kitchen's overall philosophy — Japanese-rooted seafood and grilling techniques, sharpened with citrusy, peppery, or herbal accents that keep each course feeling current rather than traditional.

## How to visit like a local

Seoul diners treat reservations at tasting-menu restaurants as non-negotiable, not optional — book as early as the restaurant's system allows, and call or message promptly if your plans change so the seat can be released to someone else. Arrive on time rather than early; many small Seoul restaurants have no lounge or waiting area, and space is tightly choreographed around seating times.

Payment is almost always by card or mobile pay (Korean apps like Kakao Pay are common, but international cards are generally accepted at this tier of restaurant); tipping is not customary in South Korea and should not be added. Dress a notch smarter than casual — Cheongdam-dong's dining culture leans polished. The most common visitor mistake is treating a tasting-menu restaurant like a walk-in spot; without a confirmed reservation, you likely won't get a table.
