## What you're looking at

Maha Vihara Maitreya is one of the largest Buddhist temple complexes in Indonesia, built in a Chinese architectural style rather than the Javanese or Balinese forms most visitors expect. The main hall carries the tiered roofline, red pillars and gold trim typical of Chinese temple design, scaled up to a size that makes it a landmark inside the Cemara Asri estate.

Wide courtyards and manicured gardens surround the hall, with statuary and pavilions spread across the grounds rather than crammed into one building. It draws close to 4,600 reviews online at a 4.7 rating, making it one of the better-regarded sights in Deli Serdang district.

## Getting there and around

The address is Jl. Cemara Asri Boulevard Raya No. 8, Medan Estate, in Kecamatan Percut Sei Tuan, part of Kabupaten Deli Serdang just outside Medan city proper. There's no direct rail or busway link, so a car, taxi, or ride-hail app (Gojek or Grab both operate in Medan) is the practical option. From central Medan the drive typically runs 20-30 minutes depending on traffic on Jl.

Yos Sudarso or the ring road. The complex has open grounds for walking once you arrive, so plan for a stroll between the main hall, gardens and outer pavilions rather than a single stop.

## When to go

The temple opens daily at 6am and closes at 8pm, with identical hours all week. Weekday mornings from 7am to 11am are the quietest stretch, and on weekends the calm window is shorter, 7am to 10am. Avoid arriving on a weekend afternoon: traffic through the complex runs heavy from 9am to 8pm as day-trippers and families fill the courtyards. If your schedule allows a weekday, that's the easier visit overall; if not, get there right at 6am on a Saturday or Sunday before the crowd builds.

## Temple etiquette and the vegetarian cafe

This is an active place of worship, not a museum, so dress and behavior matter. A few practical points:

- Cover shoulders and knees; sarongs or wraps aren't always provided, so bring your own layer.
- Keep voice low near the main hall, especially when devotees are praying or lighting incense.
- Photography is generally fine in courtyards and gardens; be more careful and discreet inside the prayer hall itself.
- Footwear rules vary by building; watch for shoe racks at entrances and follow what other visitors are doing.

The complex has a vegetarian cafe on site, a practical stop given there's little food immediately around the temple gates. It serves plant-based dishes in keeping with Buddhist dietary practice, useful if you're timing a visit around a meal. Since it's a well-known destination rather than an under-the-radar one, expect other visitors and tour groups, particularly midday on weekends; the early-morning windows above are your best bet for a quieter walk through the gardens.
