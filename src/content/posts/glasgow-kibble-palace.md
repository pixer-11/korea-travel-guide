## A palace that travelled by water

Kibble Palace was never meant for Glasgow. Engineer John Kibble built it as a conservatory at his home in Coulport, on Loch Long. In 1873 he had it taken apart and shipped up the Clyde to the Botanic Gardens, where it was rebuilt and enlarged.

The structure is wrought iron and curved glass. A large circular dome connects through a short passage to a smaller entrance dome. Between 2003 and 2006 the whole thing was dismantled, its ironwork restored and reglazed, then rebuilt on site.

It also has a civic past. Benjamin Disraeli and William Gladstone both gave University of Glasgow rectorial addresses under this roof, back when the dome doubled as a meeting hall.

## Under the dome

Step inside the main dome and the planting is temperate rather than steamy jungle. The air is cool enough that you can keep your jacket on. The headline collection is tree ferns from Australia and New Zealand, with their shaggy trunks and wide fronds arching overhead.

Paths circle a central pond. Marble statues stand among the ferns, including Scipione Tadolini's *Eve* and George Henry Paulin's *King Robert of Sicily*. Look up and the iron ribs spread outward like spokes from the crown of the dome.

Half an hour covers it. Allow an hour or two if you also want the neighbouring Main Range glasshouses and the gardens' riverside paths down to the Kelvin.

## Getting into the West End gardens

The gardens sit at the junction of Great Western Road and Queen Margaret Drive. The postcode for maps is G12 0UE.

- **Subway:** Hillhead station on the Glasgow Subway circle. Walk north up Byres Road for about ten minutes to the main gate.
- **Bus:** many routes run along Great Western Road and stop at the gates.
- **On foot:** the Kelvin Walkway follows the river through the lower gardens. It connects to Kelvingrove Park and Kelvingrove Art Gallery and Museum.

The University of Glasgow's Gilbert Scott tower is a short walk south. Ashton Lane, the cobbled lane of bars and restaurants just off Byres Road, makes a natural next stop.

## Timing it for a quiet glasshouse

The glasshouse is open daily from 10am to 5:45pm, the same hours every day. Measured foot traffic shows the quietest window is 10am to 11am on weekdays. Arrive at opening and you may have the tree ferns largely to yourself.

Weekends between 11am and 4pm are the time to avoid. That is when families and West End strollers fill the circular paths. The gardens outside close later than the glasshouse, so don't save Kibble for the end of a long afternoon walk.

Despite the name, this is still an under-the-radar stop next to Glasgow's big museums. A few habits help:

- **Keep to the paths.** The beds and pond edges are fragile, and the statues are not for climbing.
- **Photograph without blocking.** The paths are narrow, so step aside for tripods and prams.
- **Check events first.** The dome sometimes hosts concerts and exhibitions. The Glasgow Botanic Gardens pages on the city council site list closures.
- **Pair it sensibly.** Do the glass first, then walk the Kelvin down to Kelvingrove Art Gallery and Museum.
