The Rose formed in Seoul in 2017. The lineup is Woosung (Sammy), Dojoon, Hajoon and Jaehyeong. They write and play their own material, closer to an indie rock band than an idol group, and they built a following through busking and constant touring.

## Concert-night manners, Malaysian style

A few habits make the night smoother.

**Paying.** Malaysia is close to cashless in the city. Cards work almost everywhere, and QR payments through DuitNow or the Touch 'n Go eWallet are standard at food stalls and kiosks. The same Touch 'n Go card pays rail fares. Tipping is not expected, and restaurant bills often include a service charge already.

**Queueing.** Malaysian concert crowds queue in orderly lines for merchandise and entry. Merch lines at K-pop and K-band shows often form hours before doors. Fans who want a particular item usually join the line soon after it starts forming, because popular sizes go first.

**Inside the hall.** Fan projects are common at Korean acts' Malaysian shows: banners, slogans or a coordinated sing-along, often organised by local fan accounts on X and Instagram. Joining in is welcome. Holding a phone or tablet high for the whole set is not, and the people behind you will tell you so.

**Dress.** Kuala Lumpur is a conservative-leaning city. Concert wear is relaxed, but most people pack a layer for the train or the mall afterwards.

## Late-October weather and what to carry

October falls in Kuala Lumpur's inter-monsoon season. Heavy afternoon and evening thunderstorms are routine, and they usually blow over within an hour or two. Heat and humidity stay high even after dark.

What regulars tend to bring:

- a compact umbrella, which doubles as shade in the queue
- a fully charged phone and a power bank, since tickets and Grab both live on it
- a light layer, because air-conditioned halls run cold after a humid walk
- photo ID, in case the tour checks names at entry

The promoter's admission rules list what is banned at the door, and bag rules at arenas tend to be strict.

## Making a weekend of it

Because the show is on a Sunday, out-of-town fans usually arrive on Friday or Saturday. That leaves time to see Kuala Lumpur itself:

- **Petronas Twin Towers and KLCC Park.** The towers' skybridge and the park's fountain shows are the classic first stop.
- **Bukit Bintang.** The shopping strip, plus Jalan Alor's night food street a block away.
- **Batu Caves.** The limestone temple caves north of the city, reached by KTM Komuter train.
- **Kampung Baru.** The Malay village area under the towers, known for nasi lemak.

Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA) links to KL Sentral on the KLIA Ekspres train. Most visitors use it as their way in and out.
