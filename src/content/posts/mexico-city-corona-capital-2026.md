## Three days on a racetrack

Corona Capital 2026 takes place from November 20 to 22, 2026. It is Mexico City's big international rock and indie festival. Since its first edition in 2010 it has filled the grounds of Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, the Formula 1 circuit inside the Magdalena Mixiuhca sports complex.

Picture flat, open ground and tarmac. Stages face each other across wide fields, and the city's skyline sits low on the horizon. Long walks between stages are part of the deal. So is a crowd that sings every word back at the band.

## Who plays this year

The 2026 bill is headed by four acts with very different crowds:

- **Gorillaz**, Damon Albarn's animated project, known for big-screen visuals and rotating guest vocalists
- **Twenty One Pilots**, the Ohio duo whose sets lean on theatrics and crowd participation
- **The Strokes**, the New York band that defined early-2000s garage rock
- **Mumford & Sons**, the English folk-rock group behind stadium-sized banjo singalongs

That mix fits the festival's identity. Corona Capital has always leaned towards British and American alternative headliners, with Mexican and Latin American acts filling the afternoon slots. The full day-by-day schedule sits with OCESA, the organiser, on the festival's official channels.

## Reaching the Autódromo

The circuit sits in the borough of Iztacalco, about 7 km east of the Zócalo. Metro Line 9 (the brown line) runs along its northern edge. Most festivalgoers use one of three stations:

- **Ciudad Deportiva**, the closest stop to the main festival gates on past editions
- **Velódromo**, one stop west, a slightly longer walk but often less packed
- **Puebla**, one stop east, useful if the gates for your entrance sit on that side

From Roma, Condesa or the Centro Histórico, the usual route is to reach Line 9 at Chilpancingo, Centro Médico or Pino Suárez and ride east. Plenty of visitors base themselves in Roma Norte or Condesa. These neighbourhoods have the densest spread of hotels and late-night food, and they are a straightforward Metro or ride-hail trip away.

## Weather, altitude and the long walk

Late November falls in Mexico City's dry season. Days are usually sunny and mild. After dark, temperatures drop quickly, and evenings at the Autódromo can feel properly cold by the headline sets.

The city sits at about 2,240 m. The sun burns faster than visitors from sea level expect, and alcohol hits harder. Regular attendees tend to pack these:

- Sunscreen and a hat for the afternoon sets
- A light jacket or hoodie for after sundown
- A small power bank, since phones double as tickets and maps
- Shoes that can handle dust, grass and several kilometres of walking

## Getting home from Ciudad Deportiva

The exit after the last headliner is the moment that decides whether your night ends well. Tens of thousands of people leave at once. Locals handle it in a few familiar ways.

Many walk to Velódromo or Puebla instead of Ciudad Deportiva, trading ten extra minutes on foot for a less crushed platform. Others leave before the final encore to beat the surge. Ride-hail apps such as Uber and DiDi are widely used in Mexico City, but prices climb and pickups slow right after the festival closes. A common trick is to walk several blocks away from the circuit before requesting a car.

Street taxis hailed outside the gates are the option experienced locals avoid. App-based rides or official *sitio* taxis are the standard advice across the city. Inside, card payment is common at festivals of this size. Even so, a little cash in pesos is useful for street food stands on the walk out.

Crowd etiquette is easy to read. Mexican audiences chant *"¡Otra! ¡Otra!"* for encores, wave phone lights during ballads, and pack tight at the front for headliners. Fans who want a rail spot for The Strokes or Gorillaz usually camp at that stage for the act before.

## Tickets and where the information lives

OCESA runs the festival, and past editions sold through Ticketmaster México. Passes have come in single-day and full-weekend versions, plus premium tiers with separate viewing areas and facilities. The official festival site holds the current ticket types, entry rules and price information for 2026.

Mexican festival tickets are usually linked to the buyer's name or a digital account. Resale outside the official platform has a long history of fake tickets in the city. Most regulars buy only through the authorised channel.
