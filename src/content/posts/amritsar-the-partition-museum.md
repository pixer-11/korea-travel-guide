## What's inside the Town Hall

The museum occupies Amritsar's colonial-era Town Hall, a red-brick building a short walk from the Golden Temple complex. Inside, roughly a dozen galleries trace the 1947 Partition chronologically: British withdrawal, the Radcliffe Line, mass migration, and resettlement. Exhibits lean on oral histories, donated family photographs, refugee-camp documents, and personal objects like a single suitcase or a set of keys carried across the new border.

There's no grand artifact centerpiece here. The power is cumulative: recorded testimonies play on loop in small listening booths, and walls of newsprint reproduce partition-era headlines verbatim. Expect to read a lot. This is a museum built for people willing to slow down, not skim.

## Getting there and timing it right

The address is Hall Rd, Town Hall, Katra Ahluwalia, Amritsar 143006, an easy 10-15 minute walk from the Golden Temple's Heritage Street. Auto-rickshaws from anywhere in the old city cost little and know the Town Hall by name.

- **Open:** Tuesday to Sunday, 10am–6pm
- **Closed:** Mondays, with no exceptions
- **Busiest:** weekends, 11am–6pm, when tour groups stack up in the entry corridor
- **Best window:** weekday mornings right at 10am opening

With a 4.6 rating from over 20,000 reviews, this is one of the most consistently praised museums in Punjab, and it draws crowds accordingly. Don't expect an empty hall on a Saturday afternoon.

## Reading the galleries without rushing

Give it two hours minimum. The exhibits are text- and audio-heavy, and skipping the listening stations defeats the point: much of the collection was assembled directly from survivor interviews and donated family archives, and the recorded voices carry weight the photographs alone don't.

Bring earphones if you want to listen at your own pace at shared audio points; queues form at busy times. The final gallery, sometimes called the Gallery of Hope, shifts tone deliberately after the heavier rooms, so don't rush the ending.

## Visiting without the weekend crush

Come on a weekday, ideally Tuesday through Thursday, and walk in right when doors open at 10am. Weekend crowds build steadily from mid-morning and stay heavy until closing at 6pm, so a Saturday visit after lunch means shuffling through galleries at someone else's pace.

Photography rules and any current entry fee can change, so confirm both at the ticket counter rather than assuming. Most visitors pay at the door; there's no need to book far ahead, though arriving early on weekends still saves you the queue at the entrance itself.

Pair the visit with the Golden Temple and Jallianwala Bagh, both walkable from Hall Rd, and treat the museum as the quiet, sobering counterpoint to those two louder, more crowded sites. Many visitors do all three in a single unhurried day, museum first while galleries are calm, temple and garden after.
