## The highest ground in a flat city

Cambridge has very few hills, so this one counts. Castle Mound rises from the top of Castle Street, a steep grassy cone with a stepped path winding up its side. From the top the town lies below you in a single sweep: college towers, church spires and the green line of the Backs following the river.

The hill was settled long before the colleges arrived. The editorial record describes it as an Iron Age hill fort. The mound you climb today is the motte of the castle William the Conqueror ordered in 1068 to control the river crossing below. Almost none of the castle survives. What remains is the earthwork, worn smooth and grassed over.

## Finding the steps on Castle Street

The address is Castle St, Cambridge CB3 0RG, on the north side of the river. From the Market Square it's a walk of roughly 10–15 minutes. Head along Bridge Street, cross Magdalene Bridge, and keep climbing up Castle Street past Kettle's Yard.

The mound sits back from the road behind the old Shire Hall site. Look for the path leading in from Castle Street. Coming from Cambridge railway station, it's a long walk across the centre, so most visitors take a bus into town or cycle.

## Timing the climb

The climb itself takes a few minutes. Most people stay 15–30 minutes, longer if they bring something to eat. Google currently lists it as open 24 hours, but it's an outdoor public space and access can change, so check before planning a late visit.

- **Quietest:** weekends 7am–9am, when the views are clear and you'll often have the top to yourself.
- **Busiest:** weekends 11am–12pm and 1pm–10pm. Skip these if you want photos without strangers in them.
- **Late afternoon:** low sun from the west lights up the college stone, but expect company at weekends.
- **After rain:** the grass and lower path get slippery, so wear shoes with grip.

## Up the mound the Cambridge way

Few visitors find Castle Mound, so it feels more like a neighbourhood lookout than a tourist sight. Most people treat it as a short stop on a walk, not a destination in its own right. You don't book, there's no gate, and there are no facilities on the hill.

- **Pair it with Kettle's Yard,** the house and gallery a few steps down Castle Street.
- **Drop into the Museum of Cambridge** at the bottom of the hill to learn about everyday life in the town.
- **Walk back via Magdalene Bridge** and Quayside, where punts set off along the Cam.
- **Lock your bike** on Castle Street rather than wheeling it up the steps.
- **Carry your rubbish down**, because there are no bins at the top.

From the summit, try to pick out King's College Chapel and the tower of St John's, then work out your route back down through the streets. It's the easiest way to understand how compact central Cambridge really is.
