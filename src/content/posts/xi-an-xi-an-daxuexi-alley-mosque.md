## A mosque dressed as a temple

Daxuexi Xiang means "Great Learning Lane." The mosque at number 94 is often called the West Great Mosque (Xi Da Si). That name pairs it with the better-known East mosque on Huajue Lane.

Nothing about the gate says Middle East. You walk through timber gateways with upturned eaves and grey tile roofs into courtyards laid out one behind the next, on the plan of a Chinese temple. Arabic calligraphy on the boards is often the only sign you are in a mosque.

Tradition dates the foundation to the Tang dynasty. What stands now reflects later rebuilding. Treat the exact dates as tradition rather than documented fact.

## Finding Daxuexi Xiang

The alley runs north off Xi Dajie (West Street), west of the Drum Tower, in Lianhu District.

- **Metro:** Zhonglou (Bell Tower) Station on Line 2, then walk west past the Drum Tower. It takes roughly 10 to 15 minutes.
- **On foot from the food streets:** Beiyuanmen and the wider Huimin Jie (Muslim Street) area are a few minutes east.
- **Navigation:** Show a driver 大学习巷清真寺, or the full address, 94 Da Xue Xi Xiang.

The lane is narrow and residential. It is easy to walk straight past the entrance, so watch the house numbers.

## Why it's worth the detour

The Great Mosque on Huajue Lane draws tour groups and souvenir stalls. Daxuexi Xiang gets a small share of that traffic. The courtyards here are often close to empty apart from worshippers.

That quiet is the point of coming. You can stand in front of the carved screens and roof brackets for as long as you like, with no groups waiting behind you.

Pair it with the Great Mosque to compare the two, or with the Drum Tower and the Bell Tower, all within a 20-minute walk. The Muslim Quarter food streets make an easy lunch stop before or after.

## Visiting a working prayer space

This is a place of worship first and a sight second. Behave the way you would in any active mosque.

- **Dress:** Cover shoulders and knees. Women should carry a scarf in case staff ask for one.
- **Prayer hall:** Non-Muslims are usually kept to the courtyards. If you are allowed near the hall, take your shoes off before stepping onto carpet.
- **Timing:** Avoid Friday midday, when the congregational prayer fills the site. Visitors are least welcome then.
- **Photos:** Architecture is generally fine. Ask before you photograph anyone praying or washing.
- **Entry:** If there is a charge, it is posted at the gate. Mobile payment (WeChat Pay or Alipay) covers most things in Xi'an, but keep a little cash for small gatekeepers.

Keep your voice low in the courtyards and step aside for worshippers. Speak a few words of Mandarin, or show the Chinese name on your phone, and staff will point you through the right gate.
