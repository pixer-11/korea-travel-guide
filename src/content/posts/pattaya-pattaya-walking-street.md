## Neon from the arch to the pier

Walking Street starts under a lit gateway where Beach Road runs out in South Pattaya. From there it's a single strip of pavement that ends at Bali Hai Pier. Neon signs stack up two and three storeys high on both sides. Bar staff stand in the doorways, music from several sound systems overlaps, and the crowd moves slowly down the middle of the road.

Walking Street is Pattaya's best-known red-light district. It is not a secret and it is not a hidden find. It's among the most-reviewed sights on Thailand's eastern seaboard, and reviewers rate it well for what it is. That means go-go bars, beer bars, nightclubs, seafood restaurants and street snacks, all on the same street.

Know that before you arrive. Plenty of couples and groups of friends walk it once simply to see it. But the go-go bars are its main trade, and nobody here pretends otherwise.

## The 7pm switch-over

The street changes every evening. During the day this is an ordinary road with delivery vans and closed shutters. From 7pm to 3am, seven nights a week, it's closed to vehicles, and that's when it becomes Walking Street.

The first hour is the one to aim for. It's quietest from 7pm to 8pm on weekdays and weekends alike. At that hour bars are still switching on their signs, restaurants are laying out the evening's seafood, and you can walk the whole length without getting stuck behind anyone.

Try not to arrive between 9pm and 11pm on a weekend. That's the busiest window. The middle of the road turns into a slow-moving crowd, and every doorway has someone waving a menu at you.

There's no gate and no ticket. You just walk in from either end.

## Getting there on a baht bus

Pattaya's blue *songthaews*, pickup trucks with two benches in the back, are the usual way to get here. They loop around the centre, running south along Beach Road and north along Second Road. A southbound Beach Road truck takes you straight to the Walking Street arch.

- **To ride:** flag one down at the roadside and climb into the back. No need to tell the driver where you're going if it's on the route.
- **To get off:** press the buzzer on the ceiling.
- **To pay:** hand coins or small notes through the passenger window after you climb down. Ask the driver or your hotel what the current fare is. It's cash only.
- **Heading back:** go to Beach Road and catch a truck heading north.

If you're coming from Jomtien, taxis and ride-hailing apps drop off near the Bali Hai Pier end. That's the less crowded of the two entrances.

## What lines the street

The mix changes along the length of the street, so it's worth walking all of it before you choose where to go.

- **Go-go bars:** these fill the upper floors and the side lanes, with neon signs over staircases that lead up from the street.
- **Open-fronted beer bars:** stools around a bar counter, facing the pavement. These are the easiest places to sit with a drink and watch the crowd.
- **Nightclubs:** most sit toward the pier end, with doormen and bass you can hear out on the street. They fill up late.
- **Seafood restaurants:** several of them have catch laid out on ice by the entrance. You point at what you want and it's weighed before it's cooked.
- **Street carts and 7-Elevens:** skewers, fruit and cold water, which you'll want by midnight.

## Reading the touts and the bill

On a first visit the street can feel aggressive, but there's a simple way to handle it. A smile and a firm "no, thank you" (*mai ao, khop khun*) while you keep walking works almost every time. Don't stop to argue or bargain on the pavement.

- **Pass on the laminated show menus.** Touts offering "ping-pong shows" and taking you upstairs are the street's best-known trap. People regularly report big surprise bills and a hard exit.
- **Check your bill at each round.** Bars keep a running tab, often slipped into a cup on your table. Ask for *check bin* when you're ready to pay, and read every line.
- **Carry cash in small notes.** Smaller bars often don't take cards. Handing over small notes also stops disputes about change.
- **Tip lightly and directly.** Leaving your coins, or a small note for bar staff who have looked after you, is normal. Nobody expects a percentage.
- **Keep your phone away inside venues.** Photographing dancers or staff is a quick way to get thrown out. On the street, keep your phone in a front pocket, because the crowd gets tight.
- **Leave the vape at home.** E-cigarettes are illegal in Thailand, and people caught with them can be fined.

Thailand's legal drinking age is 20. Some clubs check ID at the door, so keep a copy of your passport with you.

## The Bali Hai end, before and after dark

The pier end of the street is worth a look outside nightlife hours. Bali Hai Pier is where the ferries and speedboats leave for Koh Larn, the island visible offshore. A beach morning on Koh Larn works well after a night on Walking Street, provided you're up in time.

From the same end, the road climbs onto Pratumnak Hill. The Pattaya City sign and the Khao Pattaya viewpoint up there look back over the bay. From the viewpoint, the neon strip you walked the night before looks surprisingly small against the curve of the beach.

## Planning the night

Here's a plan that works for most people:

1. Arrive at 7pm.
2. Walk the full length while it's still quiet.
3. Eat seafood at the pier end.
4. Settle into a beer bar before the 9pm weekend crowd arrives.

Two to three hours covers the street properly. Staying until the 3am close is for the clubs, not for sightseeing.

Stick to bottled water and keep an eye on your drink, and the night stays easy. If the heavy crowd between 9pm and 11pm on weekends is the part you want to see, go then. Otherwise, the first hour is when the street is easiest to walk.
