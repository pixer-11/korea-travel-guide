Domes stacked on domes, a nave that swallows sound, and shafts of light falling through high windows onto marble worn smooth by fifteen centuries of feet. That's the first impression inside Hagia Sophia, and it doesn't fade with repetition.

## What you're actually looking at

This building was a Byzantine cathedral for nearly a thousand years, then a mosque, then a museum, then a mosque again since 2020. The scale still shocks: a dome soaring 55 meters up, seemingly unsupported.

Look for the surviving Christian mosaics in the upper galleries, gold-ground figures of Christ and emperors coexisting with Islamic calligraphic roundels the size of cartwheels. Byzantine and Ottoman layers sit side by side rather than one erasing the other. That tension is the whole point of a visit.

## Getting in

It's in Sultanahmet, Fatih district, right beside the Blue Mosque and Topkapı Palace. Nearest stop is Sultanahmet on the T1 tram line, a two-minute walk.

Entry to the ground-floor prayer hall is free. The upper gallery, where most mosaics live, typically requires a separate paid ticket and entrance, so budget extra time and confirm current arrangements before you go, as ticketing setups here have shifted more than once.

Security screening is thorough; arrive expecting a queue and metal detectors.

## When to go

The crowd data is blunt here: weekdays between 7am and 9am are the calmest stretch, and weekends from 7am to 8am offer a similarly quiet slice. Avoid weekends from 10am to 8pm, when tour groups and cruise-day crowds jam the entrance and interior alike.

Because it's a working mosque, the hall closes to sightseers briefly five times a day for prayer, more so around midday Friday. Plan your visit around these gaps rather than against them.

Give yourself 45 minutes to an hour for the ground floor, longer if the upper gallery is open and you want the mosaics.

## How to visit like a local

Dress matters: shoulders and knees covered for everyone, and women need a headscarf, available free or for a small fee at the entrance if you arrive without one.

Shoes come off at the door and go into a plastic bag you carry, so wear something easy to slip off.

- Skip the guided-tour scrum: arrive right at the 7am quiet window instead of mid-morning
- Keep voices low and phones on silent; this is an active place of worship, not just a monument
- Photography is fine in most areas, but not during active prayer times
- Locals often treat the surrounding square, not the mosque itself, as a meeting point; don't expect a local-crowd feel inside, this is squarely a major international draw
- Skip the ground-floor souvenir hawkers near the exit; prices drop a street or two back toward the Grand Bazaar

Given how visited this site is, the single best move is time of day, not day of week. Early morning buys you space to actually look up.
