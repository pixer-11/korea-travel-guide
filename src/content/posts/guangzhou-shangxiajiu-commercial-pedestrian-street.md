Look up before you look at the shop windows. Above the neon signs and racks of discounted trainers, Shangxiajiu runs under a continuous row of qilou: shophouses whose upper floors jut out over the pavement on columns, forming a covered arcade. It was built that way so you could shop through Guangzhou's downpours and midday heat. It still works exactly as designed.

This is old Guangzhou's Xiguan, the "Western Pass" district in Liwan, once the city's merchant quarter. The street is officially a stitched-together run of Shangjiu Road, Xiajiu Road and Dishifu Road, which is where the name comes from. Today it is part fashion bazaar, part snack crawl, part architecture lesson.

## Why this stretch rewards a slow walk

It is a working shopping street. Most of what you pass sells everyday clothing, shoes, bags and phone accessories, plus a few malls tucked behind the arcade fronts. If you came for luxury labels, you are on the wrong side of town; Tianhe is where those live.

What makes it worth your afternoon is the setting. The arcades give the street a rhythm of pillars and shade, and the facades above them carry decades of plaster ornament, shuttered windows and faded painted lettering. Many international visitors skip it for the newer districts, so it still feels like a street built for Guangzhou shoppers rather than for tour groups.

## Reading the qilou overhead

The arcades are the real exhibit. Walk the full length and the facades change as you go:

- **Columns at street level** carry the upper floors and create the covered walkway.
- **Upper storeys** show a mix of Western-influenced plasterwork and Lingnan detailing, a style that grew from Guangzhou's long trade contact with the outside world.
- **Side lanes** branch off the main drag into narrower residential alleys, where the city's older domestic architecture survives.

Step out into the middle of the street to see a facade properly. Under the arcade you only see the shop fronts.

## Eating your way down Dishifu Road

Food is the strongest reason to come hungry. The Dishifu Road end has long been home to some of Guangzhou's best-known old names. Lianxianglou is famous for lotus-seed paste and mooncakes. Taotaoju is a classic dim sum tea house. Both have operated in the Xiguan area for generations.

Dessert is the local speciality. Look for shops selling the two Cantonese milk puddings:

- **Shuang pi nai** (double-skin milk), a chilled, silky steamed milk custard; Nanxin on Dishifu Road is the name most people cite.
- **Jiang zhuang nai** (ginger milk curd), hot milk set by fresh ginger juice, with a sharp, warming bite.

Fill the gaps with street snacks from the counters along the arcade:

- Wonton noodles in clear broth
- Rice noodle rolls (cheung fun)
- Fried fish balls on skewers
- Herbal tea from the dark-liquid tea stalls

## Morning tea the Cantonese way

Yum cha is the ritual this neighbourhood was built around. A traditional tea house morning is slow, loud in a cheerful way, and centred on the pot rather than the food. Older tea houses may still have trolleys, though many now use a tick-box order sheet.

A few habits help:

- **Choose your tea first.** Pu'er and jasmine are the safe defaults.
- **Tap two fingers on the table** when someone tops up your cup; it's a quiet thank-you.
- **Leave the teapot lid ajar** or tip it sideways when you want more hot water. Staff will refill it.
- **Order a few dishes at a time.** Har gow, siu mai and char siu bao are the classic opening round.

## Shopping without the hassle

Mobile payment is how the street pays. WeChat Pay and Alipay are used everywhere, from malls to snack windows, and both now let foreign visitors link an international card. Set them up before you arrive; some small stalls may not take foreign cards at all. Carry a little cash as backup.

Tipping isn't customary in Guangzhou restaurants or shops. Some tea houses add a service or tea charge, shown on the menu. Light bargaining is normal at independent clothing stalls but not in malls or chain stores. A translation app with camera mode makes menus and price tags easy.

Weekends draw more Guangzhou shoppers than weekdays. If you want room to stop and photograph the facades, a weekday visit is the easier bet. Opening hours vary shop by shop, so check individual restaurants before you set out.

## Getting there and what's around it

Metro Line 1 is the simplest route. Get off at Changshou Lu Station and follow signs toward Shangxiajiu; the street starts a short walk south. Huangsha Station, also on Line 1 and on Line 6, works if you're arriving from the Shamian side.

The best plan joins the street to its neighbours:

- **Hualin Temple and the jade market**, just north near Changshou Lu, with stalls of carved jade and stone.
- **Enning Road**, lined with more qilou and home to the Cantonese Opera Art Museum.
- **Liwan Lake Park** and **Lychee Bay**, the waterways at the western edge of Xiguan.
- **Shamian Island**, a short walk south, with colonial-era European buildings along tree-lined avenues by the Pearl River.

A good half-day loop: yum cha near Dishifu Road, a walk along Shangxiajiu under the arcades, then south to Shamian for late afternoon along the river.
