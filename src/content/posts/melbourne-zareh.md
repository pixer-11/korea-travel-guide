## Armenian cooking, briefly

You should arrive knowing roughly what the cuisine is. Armenian cooking comes from the high country between the Black and Caspian seas. It shares ground with Persian, Levantine, Georgian and Turkish food, but keeps its own identity.

Across the cuisine, these are the staples to know:

- **Lavash**, the thin flatbread traditionally slapped onto the wall of a clay tonir oven
- **Khorovats**, the Armenian word for meat grilled over coals
- **Dolma**, filling wrapped in vine or cabbage leaves
- **Basturma**, cured beef with a spice crust
- **Apricots and pomegranates**, both national symbols that show up in sweet and savoury dishes

Zareh calls itself modern Armenian, so don't expect a heritage menu. Read the menu on the night, not an imagined one, and ask staff what the kitchen does with these traditions.

## Getting to Collingwood

Collingwood is an inner-north suburb about 3 kilometres northeast of the CBD, so the trip is short. Look up Zareh's exact street address before you leave and choose your stop from that.

- **Tram:** Route 86 runs the length of Smith Street, Collingwood's western edge and main eating strip. Route 109 runs along Victoria Parade and Victoria Street on the suburb's southern side.
- **Train:** Collingwood Station, on Hoddle Street, is on the Mernda and Hurstbridge lines from Flinders Street and the City Loop.
- **On foot:** From the eastern CBD, walking up through Fitzroy along Gertrude Street takes roughly half an hour.

The CBD's Free Tram Zone does not reach Collingwood, so tap on with a myki card. Rideshare is easy at night. Parking on the side streets is mostly short-term and permit-controlled, so check the signs closely.

## Booking a table after the trophy

In Melbourne, a serious dinner almost always starts with an online booking. Use Zareh's own website or the booking platform it links to. Third-party listings can be out of date, especially while a restaurant is this new to the spotlight.

These are local customs that will help:

- **Card is normal.** Melbourne restaurants mostly take card and contactless payments. Some add a card surcharge, and many add one on Sundays and public holidays. Any surcharge should be printed on the menu or the bill.
- **Tipping is optional.** Hospitality staff in Australia earn award wages. Rounding up or leaving about ten percent for great service is appreciated but never expected.
- **Honour your booking.** Many sought-after restaurants take card details to hold a table and charge a no-show fee. If plans change, cancel early, because someone on the waitlist will take your seat.
- **Say what you can't eat when you book.** Armenian cooking uses a lot of lamb, dairy, nuts and wheat. Raise dietary needs early, not at the table.

## Making a night of Collingwood

The suburb is the second reason to come. Collingwood was a working-class, industrial suburb, and many of its old factories and warehouses now house bars, studios and galleries.

Before or after dinner, these are within easy reach:

- **Smith Street**, the long tram-route strip of bars, pubs and late kitchens on the Collingwood–Fitzroy border
- **Gertrude Street**, a short run of design shops, wine bars and cafés at the southern end of Fitzroy
