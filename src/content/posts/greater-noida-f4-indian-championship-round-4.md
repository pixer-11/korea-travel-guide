## A Formula One track, built for something bigger

Picture a full Grand Prix circuit with junior single-seaters out on it. That is what this round is. The Buddh International Circuit was designed by Hermann Tilke and hosted the Indian Grand Prix from 2011 to 2013. MotoGP came here in 2023 for the Grand Prix of Bharat.

The 5.1 km layout climbs and drops more than its flat surroundings would suggest. Turn 1 is a tight right-hander at the end of the pit straight. Further round, the long back straight is where F4 drivers sit in each other's slipstream and set up passes into the braking zone at the end.

## What Round 4 is

The F4 Indian Championship is the country's entry rung for single-seater racing. Every car is mechanically identical, which puts the result down to the driver. Round 4 takes up the weekend of October 24-25, 2026, and is one stop on the 2026 calendar.

Spec-series grids like this one usually run close. Races tend to be decided by braking points and starts more than by straight-line speed. Watch the first lap. A clean getaway through Turn 1 often decides the podium order.

## Getting out to the Yamuna Expressway

The circuit is in Jaypee Greens Sports City, along the Yamuna Expressway. It is well south of central Noida, so you'll need road transport.

- **From Delhi:** most visitors drive or take an app cab via the DND Flyway or Noida–Greater Noida Expressway, then join the Yamuna Expressway. Count on well over an hour from central Delhi.
- **By metro:** the Noida Metro Aqua Line runs into Greater Noida but stops a long way short of the circuit. Fans who use it finish the trip in an auto or cab.
- **Flying in:** Indira Gandhi International Airport in Delhi is the usual arrival point. Noida International Airport at Jewar sits further down the same expressway.

The expressway is a tolled, fast, open road. Out here there is little within walking distance of the gates.

## Race weekend at Buddh

On a race weekend, the main grandstand faces the pit straight, the start line and the pit garages. That's the seat for starts, pit activity and podium ceremonies. The paddock buildings sit right behind the pit wall.

The circuit has hardly any shade outside the covered stands. Late October in the Delhi region usually means dry, warm days after the monsoon, with cooler air once the sun drops. Haze from the start of the post-harvest season is common, so the far corners can look soft-edged from the stands.

## Making a day of it at the circuit

A few habits make the weekend easier.

- **Paying:** UPI is the standard way to pay for food and drink across India, and most vendors take it. Some cash is still handy if signal is weak at the stalls.
- **Ear protection:** F4 cars are quieter than F1 machinery but still loud at the pit wall. Regulars carry foam earplugs, especially when children come along.
- **Sun cover:** caps, sunscreen and a refillable bottle are what seasoned spectators pack for a full day in the open grandstands.
- **Getting home:** cabs dry up after the last race, when everyone leaves together. Many visitors arrange a return car with their driver for the day rather than relying on an app pickup on the expressway.

Indian race crowds tend to be relaxed and family-friendly. People cheer local drivers loudly, so it helps to know who on the grid is Indian before the lights go out.

## Where to stay and what's nearby

Most visitors stay in Greater Noida or Noida instead of out by the circuit. Pari Chowk and Knowledge Park in Greater Noida have business hotels close to the expressway. Noida's Sector 18 has more restaurants and shopping.

If you want to stay on longer, Agra and the Taj Mahal are further down the Yamuna Expressway. Plenty of visitors from abroad add that trip to a race weekend. Back toward the city, the India Expo Centre & Mart in Knowledge Park hosts trade fairs throughout the year.
