Shopfronts sit close together along a narrow road, and the upper storeys look older than the signs beneath them. The street is known for independent places to eat and drink, which is why a brunch spot fits so naturally here.

## Finding 44 Fossgate

York's compact centre makes this easy. Most of the walk is through pedestrian-friendly streets.

- **From York railway station:** cross Lendal Bridge or Ouse Bridge into the centre, then head for Parliament Street and Pavement. Allow roughly 15 to 20 minutes on foot.
- **From York Minster:** walk south through the old streets towards the Shambles, then continue to Pavement. Fossgate leads off it.
- **From the Shambles:** this is the shortest hop, just a few minutes away.
- **By car:** the medieval centre is not built for driving. Use one of York's Park & Ride services or a city-edge car park and walk in.

Fossgate is one-way and narrow. Keep an eye out for delivery vans in the morning.

## After brunch: Foss Bridge to the Merchant Adventurers' Hall

Fossgate puts you close to several of York's best sights. A brunch here can anchor a whole morning or afternoon on foot.

- **Merchant Adventurers' Hall:** a medieval guildhall with its own entrance off Fossgate, and one of the finest timber-framed buildings in the country. It is the obvious next stop.
- **Foss Bridge and Walmgate:** cross the River Foss at the bottom of the street and carry on into Walmgate, a quieter, less-visited stretch of the old city.
- **The Shambles:** York's overhanging medieval shopping street is minutes away. It is at its most crowded around the middle of the day.
- **Jorvik Viking Centre:** in Coppergate, a short walk south-west, on the site where York's Viking-age remains were excavated.
- **Clifford's Tower:** the stone keep on its mound beyond Coppergate, with views back over the rooftops.

If you are saving Rise. for the quiet 4pm hour, do it the other way round. Spend the day at these sights and finish on Fossgate.
