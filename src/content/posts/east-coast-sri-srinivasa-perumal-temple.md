Walk up Serangoon Road from Little India MRT and the temple announces itself long before the entrance: a five-tiered gopuram crowded with painted deities, stacked in tiers of blue, gold and red against the low shophouse skyline around it. Founded in 1855, it's dedicated to Vishnu in his form as Perumal, and it remains one of the oldest and most active Hindu temples in Singapore.

## Getting oriented on Serangoon Road

The temple sits at 397 Serangoon Rd, a few minutes' walk north of Little India MRT (exit at Race Course Road side, Downtown or North East Line). It's easy to combine with a wander through the surrounding stretch of sari shops, garland stalls and banana-leaf restaurants.

Look for the tower rising above the two-storey shopfronts; you'll spot it well before you reach the gate.

## What you'll see inside

Past the gopuram, the inner courtyard opens onto a shrine hall with a much older, more restrained architectural feel than the tower suggests. The main sanctum holds the image of Perumal, flanked by shrines to his consorts Sridevi and Bhoodevi.

Walk the courtyard barefoot; shoes come off at the entrance and get left on the racks provided. Ceiling paintings and carved pillars reward a slow circuit rather than a quick glance, so give yourself time to look up as well as around.

This is also the starting point for Thaipusam, when kavadi-bearing devotees begin their procession toward Sri Thendayuthapani Temple on Tank Road. If you're in Singapore during that January or February festival, the temple and street outside turn into one of the most striking religious spectacles in the city.

## When to go

The temple keeps a split schedule every day of the week: 6am to 12pm, then 6pm to 9pm. There's no midday access, so don't plan a lunchtime visit; it will be shut.

Weekends draw the heaviest crowds, busy from 8am right through to 9pm, so if you want a calmer visit, aim for an early weekday morning shortly after the 6am opening. Evenings bring a different atmosphere: oil lamps lit, chanting, and a cooler walk around the courtyard after the day's heat has broken.

## Visiting respectfully

This is a working place of worship, well over 5,500 reviews strong and consistently among the highest-rated attractions in the area, so basic temple etiquette matters more here than at a museum.

- Remove shoes before stepping onto the temple grounds
- Dress modestly: covered shoulders and knees for both men and women
- Ask before photographing devotees or rituals in progress; the architecture and gopuram are fair game
- Walk clockwise around the inner shrine, following the flow of other visitors
- Keep voices low near the sanctum during active prayer times

Donation boxes are placed near the main shrine if you'd like to contribute; there's no admission fee.

## Making the most of the visit

Pair the temple with Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple further up Serangoon Road, or the Tekka Centre hawker complex for a South Indian breakfast of dosa or idli before you go. Both are a short walk away and fit naturally into the same morning.

Because of the split hours, plan around the 12pm to 6pm gap: it's a good window to eat, shop for textiles on Campbell Lane, or duck into the nearby Indian Heritage Centre before returning for an evening visit when the lamps are lit inside the sanctum.
