## Brick, Cobbles and a Very Tall Tree

The market fills the lanes of a former whisky works. The Distillery District grew from the Gooderham & Worts distillery, which was once among the largest in the world. Its Victorian industrial buildings of red brick and timber now surround a car-free grid of cobblestone lanes.

From November 12, 2026 to January 3, 2027, those lanes become the Distillery Winter Village. Wooden vendor huts line Trinity Street and Gristmill Lane, and string lights cross overhead between the brick facades. A tall decorated tree stands at the centre and anchors the season's tree lighting. The shopping leans toward gifts, ornaments and crafts, with food and warm drinks sold from the stalls.

## Reaching the District

The site sits east of downtown, south of King Street near the lake. There is no subway station on the doorstep, so most visitors take a streetcar or walk.

- **Streetcar:** the 504A King route ends at Distillery Loop on Cherry Street, a few minutes' walk from the lanes.
- **From King Station (Line 1):** ride the 504 eastbound, or walk south and east for about 25 minutes.
- **On foot from St. Lawrence Market:** roughly 15 minutes east along Front Street or The Esplanade.
- **Driving:** parking nearby is limited and fills fast in December, so many people come by transit.

## Moving Through the Village Like a Regular

Season regulars treat the Village as an evening outing. After dark the lights and the tree do most of the work, and the lanes are at their most photogenic. Weekday visits are generally calmer than Friday nights and weekends, when much of the city has the same idea.

- **Tickets and entry:** the Distillery District publishes this edition's admission arrangements and any timed-entry ticket links on its official website. They have differed between weekdays and weekends in past seasons.
- **Paying:** most vendors and the permanent shops and restaurants take cards and tap payments.
- **Drinks:** in past seasons, alcohol has been sold and consumed only inside designated licensed areas rather than carried through the lanes.
- **Footwear:** cobblestones plus slush make thin soles miserable, so locals wear winter boots with grip.
- **Warming up:** the district's permanent cafés, bakeries and galleries stay open alongside the huts. Ducking indoors is part of the routine.

A full loop of the huts, the tree and a hot drink takes most visitors one and a half to two hours. Shoppers who browse every stall often stay longer.

## Around the Edges

The neighbourhood has more to offer once you've seen the market. A few blocks west, St. Lawrence Market's south hall sells peameal bacon sandwiches and local cheese, and it makes a good first stop before you walk east. To the north-east, the Canary District and Corktown Common add a quieter riverside walk. Sugar Beach and the waterfront sit a short way south-west.

The Village closes for the season on January 3, 2027. The Distillery District's lanes, shops and restaurants are open year-round, so the brick quarter is still worth the trip without the huts.
