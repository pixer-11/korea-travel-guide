## On the pier, over the water

Fish Frenzy does one thing: seafood and chips. It fills part of an old warehouse on Elizabeth Street Pier, and the windows look straight across the Sullivans Cove docks. Chips come in a paper cone. Seafood comes on plates, either battered or grilled, so you choose between crunch and something lighter.

Prices are mid-range for the waterfront. It's well known with visitors and consistently well rated, so expect company. The pier is the main draw: working boats, moored yachts and Mount Wellington rising behind the city.

## Getting to Elizabeth Street Pier

Walk down Elizabeth Street from the CBD and keep going until you reach the water. Franklin Square is a few blocks uphill and has the city's main bus stops on its edges. From there the pier is a short, flat walk.

Nearby, all within a few minutes on foot:

- **Constitution Dock**, next door, with its floating fish punts and fishing boats
- **Brooke Street Pier**, the ferry terminal for MONA
- **Salamanca Place**, the sandstone warehouse strip that hosts Salamanca Market on Saturdays
- **Franklin Wharf**, the promenade running along the cove

Parking around the waterfront is metered and fills up on weekends. Read the signs, or just walk from a CBD car park.

## Timing your fish and chips

The kitchen opens at 11:30am every day. Most days it closes at 7:45pm, and on Fridays and Saturdays it stays open until 8:15pm. There's no weekly closing day.

Weekdays between 3pm and 5pm are the quietest, which suits a late lunch or an early bite after the museums. Try to avoid weekends from 12pm to 8pm. That covers almost every hour the doors are open, and it's when tables are hardest to get. If you're coming on a Saturday with Salamanca Market in the plan, eat first: arriving close to 11:30am puts you ahead of the noon rush.

Allow 45 minutes to an hour to eat. Add time for a walk along the docks afterwards.

## Eating on the waterfront, Hobart style

Australian habits apply here. Card and tap payments are standard across Hobart. Tipping isn't expected; locals round up or leave something for good service, and nobody minds if you don't.

Some practical points for this stretch of pier:

- **Gulls.** Seagulls work the whole Hobart waterfront. If you carry your cone outside, keep it covered and don't leave food on benches.
- **Fried or grilled.** Ordering grilled is the usual way to keep a fish meal light. Ordering a cone of chips to share is an easy way to cover a group.
- **Fish names.** Tasmanian menus often list local species like flathead and blue-eye trevalla. Check the board on the day, because what's offered depends on the catch.
- **Wind.** The cove catches cold air coming off the Derwent, even in summer. If you plan to eat outdoors, bring a layer.

Fish Frenzy is a well-trodden waterfront stop, not a locals-only secret. Your best move is timing: come on a weekday afternoon and you'll get a quiet table looking over the boats.
