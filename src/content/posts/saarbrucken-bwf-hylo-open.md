## Six days of shuttles on the Saar

The 2026 edition runs Tuesday to Sunday. The BWF Hylo Open is held in Saarbrücken from October 27 to November 1, 2026. It is a Super 500 tournament, the tier below the Super 750 and Super 1000 events on the BWF World Tour. Those ranking points are enough to bring in top international players.

There are five draws:

- Men's singles
- Women's singles
- Men's doubles
- Women's doubles
- Mixed doubles

The tournament grew out of the long-running SaarLorLux Open. That name came from the Saarland, Lorraine and Luxembourg border region, and it still explains why the crowd mixes French and Luxembourgish fans with Germans.

## How the week usually unfolds

The early rounds give you the most badminton. At World Tour events, the opening days run several courts side by side, so one session can cover dozens of matches across all five disciplines. Players who will contend at the business end often walk right past the stands between matches.

As the draw narrows, so does the court count. Quarterfinals usually fall on Friday and semifinals on Saturday. Sunday's finals are played on a single show court under full TV lighting. Weekend sessions have fewer matches, but every one of them matters.

Fans after variety tend to pick a midweek day. Fans after drama tend to pick Saturday or Sunday. The tournament's official website carries the session times and ticket links for each day.

## Reaching the hall and the city around it

Saarbrücken is a rail city first. Saarbrücken Hauptbahnhof sits on the high-speed line between Paris and Frankfurt, with direct TGV and ICE trains. That makes a day trip from either city realistic. Earlier editions were held at the Saarlandhalle, the city's main indoor arena, a short bus or taxi ride from the station.

Between sessions, these are all close by:

- **St. Johanner Markt**: the cobbled old-town square, ringed with bars and restaurants that fill up on tournament evenings
- **Ludwigskirche**: the Baroque Protestant church on its own square, a short walk across the Saar
- **Saarbrücken Castle**: on the south bank, with the Historical Museum Saar underneath
- **Völklinger Hütte**: the UNESCO-listed ironworks, a short regional train ride west

## In the stands: badminton manners

Badminton crowds have their own rules. They are quieter than football crowds and louder than tennis crowds, and the noise follows the rhythm of the rally.

- **Silence during the serve.** Applause and shouting start once the shuttle hits the floor.
- **No flash photography.** Players track a fast white shuttle against the lights, and flashes are a real distraction.
- **Seat changes happen between points.** On multi-court days, spectators usually move between courts during breaks, not mid-rally.
- **The interval at 11 points** is when players towel off and coaches step in. It is also the natural moment to grab a drink.

Card payment is widely accepted in Germany, though smaller food stands and old-town bars still often prefer cash. Many visitors stay around St. Johanner Markt, so dinner after an evening session is a short walk away.
