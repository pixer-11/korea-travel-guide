## Four days of food business in Yeşilköy

WorldFood Istanbul is a trade show, not a tasting festival. The 34th edition runs from December 15 to 18, 2026, and fills Istanbul Expo Center with exhibitors from across the international food industry. With more than three decades of editions behind it, the fair is a fixture on Turkey's food trade calendar.

The halls are lined with company stands, not street-food stalls. Exhibitors come to meet buyers, distributors and importers. Talk at the counters turns to shipping terms, packaging and certification as often as flavour. Samples do get passed across, but the real reason to be there is the meetings.

## Inside Istanbul Expo Center

The venue is spread out, so give it a full day. Istanbul Expo Center sits in Yeşilköy, in the Bakırköy district, a short distance inland from the Sea of Marmara shore. It is a cluster of large linked exhibition halls joined by long covered walkways.

Here, hall numbers matter more than they do at a compact venue. Visitors usually sort their meetings by hall using the exhibitor list on the organiser's site, then follow the floor plan at the entrance. Cafés and food counters inside the complex handle lunch. As at most big fairs, those queues are longest in the middle of the day.

## Dates, tickets and registration

Entry works through registration, not ticket booths. The organiser publishes the dates, venue, exhibitor list and visitor registration on its official WorldFood Istanbul site. The fair is aimed at professionals in food and beverage, and trade visitors typically register online for a badge that is scanned at the entrance.

No entry price is given here. The official site is where the current visitor terms are posted. International visitors usually arrange their Turkish visa or e-visa according to the rules for their nationality, and many carry a printed copy of their registration alongside their passport.

## Getting there on the M1A

The metro is the simplest route. The M1A line has a stop called DTM–İstanbul Fuar Merkezi right beside the exhibition grounds. The same line runs east through Zeytinburnu and Aksaray to Yenikapı, where it connects with the Marmaray rail line and the M2 metro.

- **From Sultanahmet:** take the T1 tram to Aksaray, then change to the M1A.
- **From Beyoğlu/Taksim:** take the M2 to Yenikapı, then the M1A.
- **Payment:** one Istanbulkart covers the metro, tram, Marmaray and ferries.
- **Taxis:** practical with heavy sample bags, but the coastal roads clog at rush hour.

Many visitors stay in Yeşilköy, Ataköy or Bakırköy to keep the commute short. Others choose the old city or Beyoğlu for evenings out and accept a longer ride in the morning.

## Working the stands, Turkish style

Fair-floor manners in Istanbul favour time over speed. A handshake opens most conversations, and a meeting that starts standing often ends up seated at the stand's table.

- **Tea:** exhibitors commonly offer çay in small tulip-shaped glasses. Saying yes is normal, and it is often how a quick chat becomes a proper meeting.
- **Business cards:** still widely exchanged, alongside QR codes for LinkedIn or WhatsApp.
- **Language:** English is widely used on the floor. "Merhaba" (hello) and "teşekkür ederim" (thank you) still go a long way.
- **Paying:** cards and contactless payments are accepted across most of Istanbul, so cash is mainly useful for small purchases.
- **Dress:** business clothes, with shoes that can manage several kilometres of hall walking.

Follow-up usually happens fast. Many exhibitors prefer WhatsApp to email for keeping a conversation going after the fair.

## December evenings after the fair

Istanbul in mid-December is cool and damp. Rain is common, and daytime temperatures usually sit somewhere between the single digits and the low teens Celsius. A waterproof layer earns its place in the bag between the metro and the hall doors.

Evenings close to the venue are easy to fill:

- **Yeşilköy:** a quiet seaside neighbourhood with restaurants close to the fairground.
- **Bakırköy:** shopping streets and a long seafront promenade.
- **Kumkapı:** the fish-restaurant quarter, a short taxi ride from Yenikapı.
- **Beyoğlu:** meyhane taverns serving mezes and rakı, reached via the M2.

Visitors with a spare morning before or after the fair often head for the Grand Bazaar or the Spice Bazaar in Eminönü. Both are reachable from Aksaray on the T1 tram. Food-trade visitors in particular tend to find the Spice Bazaar's displays of dried fruit, nuts and lokum interesting after four days of product stands.
