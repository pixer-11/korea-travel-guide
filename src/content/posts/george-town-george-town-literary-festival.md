## Three days of "Cosmopolises"

The 16th edition takes place on 27–29 November 2026. Its theme, "Cosmopolises", suits the host city well. George Town grew as a British free port from 1786, and Hokkien clan houses, Tamil temples, Malay mosques and Armenian Street still sit within a few blocks of each other.

The festival began in 2011. Since then it has become one of Southeast Asia's best-known literary gatherings. Readings, panels and conversations are spread across venues in the city rather than held in one hall, so the streets between sessions become part of the programme.

## Venues, tickets and the free-entry model

The festival remains largely free and open to the public. For most sessions you simply turn up and walk in, with no ticket and no payment. Any event that needs registration is marked on the programme.

Sessions take place across several George Town venues, all inside or near the UNESCO-listed heritage core, which was inscribed in 2008. Distances are short. Most venues are a 10–20 minute walk apart through shophouse streets with covered five-foot ways.

The festival publishes the full programme, venue list and any registration details on its official channels.

## Getting to George Town

Penang is easy to reach for a long weekend.

- **By air:** Penang International Airport in Bayan Lepas, roughly 30–45 minutes by road from George Town.
- **By rail:** KTM ETS trains to Butterworth, then the ferry across the channel to the George Town jetty.
- **By road:** the Penang Bridge or the Sultan Abdul Halim Muadzam Shah Bridge from the mainland.

Inside the heritage zone, Rapid Penang's free CAT (Central Area Transit) bus loops past the main streets. Grab, the ride-hailing app, covers longer hops.

## Between sessions, the Penang way

Free entry shapes how attendees behave. Popular panels fill on a first-come basis, so regulars usually stay put between back-to-back sessions they care about rather than hoping to slip in late. Q&A rounds are a big part of the format, and audiences use them.

Food breaks are short and close by. Penang's hawker culture is the obvious fuel:

- **Char kway teow** and **assam laksa** from hawker stalls and kopitiams, where you order at each stall and pay the stall when the food arrives.
- **Nasi kandar** from the Tamil-Muslim shops around Lebuh Chulia and Little India.
- **Cendol** for the afternoon heat.

Small stalls often take cash only, while larger cafés and bookshops accept cards and e-wallets. Tipping is not expected.

Late November falls at the tail of Penang's wetter months. Heavy afternoon downpours are common, and the five-foot ways keep walkers dry between venues. Several heritage sites sit near festival venues, including Khoo Kongsi, the Cheong Fatt Tze Mansion and Kapitan Keling Mosque. Covered shoulders and knees are expected at religious sites.

Many visitors stay inside the heritage core around Lebuh Chulia, Love Lane and Armenian Street. That keeps every venue walkable and the evening hawker centres within easy reach.
