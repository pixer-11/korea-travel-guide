## One night, one arena

Vaundy's Hong Kong show was set for Saturday, October 3, 2026. It was a stop on his Asia Arena Tour "HORO", and the venue was AsiaWorld-Expo, the city's largest purpose-built exhibition and concert complex.

Vaundy is a Japanese singer-songwriter who writes, arranges and produces his own songs, and he also handles much of his own visual direction. Since debuting online in 2019 he has become one of Japan's most-streamed young artists. Hong Kong audiences tend to know him through anime tie-ins as much as the charts:

- "Kaiju no Hanauta", his breakout singalong
- "Tokyo Flash", the early track that started it all
- "CHAINSAW BLOOD", from the *Chainsaw Man* anime

## Where AsiaWorld-Expo sits

The venue is at Chek Lap Kok, right next to Hong Kong International Airport and a long way from the city's usual entertainment districts. That shapes the whole evening. Nobody strolls over from Tsim Sha Tsui after dinner. A show here means a ride out to the airport island.

Concerts at the venue are staged in AsiaWorld-Arena, a large, flat-floored hall with standing space in front and tiered seating around it. It is a hangar-like room with a big, open floor. That suits a high-energy band show more than an intimate set.

## The ride out and back

The MTR Airport Express runs from Hong Kong Station in Central, stopping at Kowloon, Tsing Yi and Airport. Its final stop, AsiaWorld-Expo station, opens straight onto the venue. From Central the ride takes roughly half an hour.

Tung Chung and the airport hotels, by contrast, are a short hop away. Some travelling fans base themselves there, since the Citygate outlet mall and the Ngong Ping 360 cable car to the Big Buddha make for a full day on Lantau before a show.

October 3 also falls two days after China's National Day on October 1. That week, mainland Golden Week travellers usually fill the city's hotels and transport.

## Getting through the doors and home again

The crowd at AsiaWorld-Expo moves in patterns. Here is how a show night there usually goes:

- **Paying for the train:** most riders tap an Octopus card or a contactless bank card at the Airport Express gates. Some buy a return ticket in the morning so they skip the machines after the show.
- **Merchandise:** official tour merch tables at Hong Kong arena shows draw their longest lines in the hours before doors. Fans who care about sizes typically queue well before the show starts.
- **Standing floor:** standing-floor ticket holders usually enter in numbered batches. Staff call ticket numbers in order, so a low number means a spot nearer the stage.
- **Etiquette:** Hong Kong crowds at Japanese acts tend to sing along in Japanese and keep phones low. Venue rules on professional cameras and recording are posted at the entrance.
- **After the encore:** thousands of people head for one station at once. Some fans hang back for twenty minutes at the venue or walk to the airport terminals for food, then catch a quieter train.
