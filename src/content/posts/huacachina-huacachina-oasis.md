## Water at the bottom of a sand bowl

A green lagoon sits in a hollow, with dunes rising steeply on every side. Palms and huarango trees line the water. A paved promenade loops the shore, and hostels, restaurants and tour desks face the lagoon. You can walk the whole village in about twenty minutes.

The oasis used to appear on the back of Peru's old 50-sol note, so most Peruvians know it on sight. Natural springs that once fed the lagoon have dwindled, and the water level is now kept up artificially. Paddle boats are still for hire on the water. Local legend says a princess bathing here became a mermaid, and you'll see her statue by the shore.

## Getting to Huacachina

Everyone comes through Ica.

- **From Lima:** long-distance buses run the Panamericana Sur to Ica in roughly 4–5 hours. Hop-on, hop-off backpacker buses also stop in Huacachina itself.
- **From Ica:** a taxi or mototaxi from the bus terminals takes about 10–15 minutes. Agree the fare before you get in.
- **Onward:** Paracas and the Ballestas Islands are about an hour north. The Nazca Lines are roughly 2.5 hours south.

Most visitors stay one night. That gives you a sunset on the dunes and the empty early morning that day-trippers miss.

## Buggies, boards and the dune ridge

The dunes are the reason people come. Open-frame dune buggies roar up the faces and drop over the crests. Between runs they stop at the top of long slopes for sandboarding. Most first-timers ride down on their stomachs, head first.

- **Tour length:** typically about two hours, timed to finish at sunset.
- **Walking up:** the ridge right behind the lagoon is free to climb. Expect a steep 20–30 minute slog in soft sand.
- **Bring:** sunglasses or a buff for flying sand, water, and a zip bag for your phone.
- **Nearby:** Ica's pisco and wine bodegas are a short taxi ride away, and many tour desks bundle them into a half-day trip.

The desert cools quickly after dark. Pack a layer even if the afternoon was scorching.

## Beating the crowds on the dunes

This is one of Peru's most visited spots. Planning around the crowds matters more than anything else here.

- **Go early:** the lagoon and the ridge are calmest from 7am to 9am on weekdays and 7am to 8am at weekends. Climb then for photos without people in them.
- **Avoid weekend afternoons:** from 12pm to 9pm on Saturdays and Sundays, Lima weekenders fill the promenade and the dunes. Do your buggy tour on a weekday if you can.
- **Booking:** tour desks and hostels along the promenade all sell the same buggy-and-board trips. Compare a few, and ask whether the dune entry charge at the reserve checkpoint is included. It's paid separately, so check the current amount at the desk.
- **Safety:** check the buggy before you get in. Seatbelts should buckle across both shoulders, and the driver should go at a pace you're happy with. Saying "más despacio, por favor" (slower, please) works.
- **Paying:** carry Peruvian soles in cash for tours, mototaxis and small cafés. Card readers are patchy.

The village has no gates and you can walk in at any hour. Hostel bars run late, so ask for a room away from the promenade if you want to sleep.
