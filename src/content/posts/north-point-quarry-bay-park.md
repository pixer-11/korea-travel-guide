Quarry Bay Park runs in a long strip along the North Point waterfront, squeezed between Kai Yuen Street's typhoon shelter and the harbour itself. It's not a green retreat: it's concrete promenade, chain-link courts, and open sky, built for people who live in the towers behind it rather than for postcard shots.

The park splits into functional zones. At one end, tennis and basketball courts get booked out by regulars most evenings. A soccer field sits mid-park, fenced and floodlit. Toward the water, playgrounds and outdoor exercise equipment, the kind with steel pull-up bars and waist-twist discs, draw retirees doing tai chi at dawn and kids after school.

The real draw is the harbour edge. Railings line a promenade facing directly across to Kowloon and the container terminal traffic beyond. On clear evenings this stretch catches container ships and tugboats moving through the channel, with Kowloon's residential towers lit up opposite. It's a working harbour view, not a scenic one, and that's the appeal.

## Getting there and getting oriented

The park address is Hoi Tai Street, Quarry Bay, on Hong Kong Island. Quarry Bay MTR station (Island and Tseung Kwan O lines) is the nearest stop, about a 10-15 minute walk north through Kai Yuen Street. Tram stops along King's Road also drop you within a similar walk. There's no single grand entrance; you'll filter in from several side streets, so look for the harbour railing rather than a gate.

## When to go

Hours run 7am to 11pm every day, so there's no early-morning or late-night lockout to plan around. Midday is the worst time: little shade, hard sun off the water, and courts occupied by school groups on weekday afternoons. Early morning suits joggers and the tai chi crowd; after 6pm the air cools and the harbour lights come on, which is when the promenade fills with dog walkers and couples.

## Courts, fields and how locals use them

Locals treat this as a neighbourhood sports ground first, view park second. Basketball and tennis courts operate on a rough first-come basis for casual play, though organised bookings through the Leisure and Cultural Services Department can lock out a court for league games, especially at weekends. Expect noise and no ambient calm.

- Bring your own ball or racket; there's no rental on site.
- Court surfaces are outdoor concrete, so grippy sneakers matter more than style.
- The soccer field is fenced; spectators watch through the mesh from the perimeter path.
- Exercise equipment near the water is free and unsupervised, first-come.

Don't expect food stalls or restrooms clustered conveniently; facilities are basic and spread along the length of the park, so scout them on the walk in rather than after you've settled somewhere. Nearby, Quarry Bay's older streets around Tong Chong Street have cafes and the Monday-to-Sunday farmers' market crowd, a short walk south, if you want food after the harbour walk.
