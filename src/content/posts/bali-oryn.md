## Why go

Ubud's restaurant scene tends to split into two camps: raw-food cafés overlooking rice terraces, and warungs slinging nasi campur to a queue of scooters. ORYn doesn't fit either box. It's a restaurant and bar built explicitly around fire — smoke, char, spice, and a spirits list designed to stand up to all three. The kitchen is led by Danial Fitri Yap, who cut his teeth at CÉ LA VI, and that pedigree shows in a menu that reads more like a global tasting map than a Balinese one: kingfish ceviche sitting a few lines away from Mongolian-style tiger prawns. For a town saturated with smoothie bowls, it's a deliberate change of register.

## Where it is

ORYn sits on Monkey Forest Road, Ubud's main artery, the same street that runs down toward the Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary and is lined with galleries, boutiques, and a dense run of restaurants and bars. Being on this stretch means it's walkable from most central Ubud accommodation — you don't need a driver if you're staying anywhere near Ubud Palace, the Ubud Art Market, or the northern end of Monkey Forest Road itself. The location also means foot traffic is heavy in the evenings, so expect a livelier, more see-and-be-seen energy than you'd find at a quiet gang-side warung.

## What to expect on the plate

The open-fire kitchen is the organizing idea here, not a gimmick bolted onto a standard menu. Dishes are built to take on char and smoke: think proteins finished over live flame, sauces designed to cut through that smokiness, and a general leaning toward bold, layered spicing rather than the softer, coconut-forward flavors typical of Balinese home cooking. The kingfish ceviche suggests a kitchen confident with raw and citrus-cured seafood, likely a lighter counterpoint to heavier grilled items like the Mongolian-style tiger prawns, which point to a sweet-savory glaze built for the fire. Given the chef's background at a high-profile bar-and-restaurant concept, expect the drinks list to be treated with the same seriousness as the food — this reads as a spot where a pre-dinner cocktail is part of the plan, not an afterthought.

## When to go

As a recently opened venue, ORYn's specific opening hours, peak nights, and reservation system weren't confirmed at the time of writing — treat any hours you see online as provisional and call or check their official channels before you head over, especially if you're planning a group booking or a specific night out. Generally speaking, fire-and-spirits concepts like this tend to hit their stride after dark, so plan for a dinner or late-evening visit rather than a lunch stop. Monkey Forest Road gets genuinely busy from late afternoon onward as day-trippers finish at the Monkey Forest and shift into dinner mode, so arriving early in the evening, before that wave peaks, is a reasonable hedge against a wait.

## How to visit like a local

Book ahead if a table function exists — new openings on a high-traffic strip like Monkey Forest Road tend to fill fast in their first months, driven by word of mouth and social media rather than long-term reputation, and turning up unannounced on a Friday or Saturday night is a gamble. Dress a notch smarter than typical Ubud daywear; a bar-forward, spirits-led concept like this tends to skew toward evening-out attire rather than beach cover-ups and sandals. Payment across Ubud's restaurant scene is overwhelmingly card-friendly now, though carrying some cash for smaller add-ons or in case of system outages is still sensible. Order across categories rather than sticking to one section of the menu — with a kitchen built around fire and spice, the appeal is in the range, from the cured/raw dishes through to the char-grilled mains, so sharing plates across a table gets you a fuller sense of what the kitchen is doing. Skip the mistake most visitors make on this street: treating it as a quick pit-stop between shops. Budget real time for a fire-cooked meal like this — it's a sit-down, unhurried experience, not a grab-and-go bite.

## Getting there

If you're not already staying within walking distance, any driver or ride-hailing app in Ubud will know Monkey Forest Road by name — it's one of the town's central reference points, alongside Ubud Palace and the Ubud Market. Traffic on the road itself can crawl in early evening, so factor in extra time if you're arriving by car during peak dinner hours, and consider walking the last stretch if you're coming from central Ubud.
