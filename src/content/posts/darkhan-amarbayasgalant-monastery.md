## A Walled City in an Empty Valley

The walls come into view long before anything else. Amarbayasgalant sits alone on the floor of the Iven Valley, below Mount Bürengiin Nuruu. Its symmetrical compound faces south across open grassland, and there are no other buildings to compete with it.

The Yongzheng Emperor of the Qing dynasty commissioned it in 1727 to honour Zanabazar, Mongolia's first Bogd Gegeen. Zanabazar was a revered sculptor and spiritual leader. Today 28 temples survive inside the walls, out of a much larger original layout. Many halls were destroyed during the 1937 purges, and the complex was restored in the decades that followed.

## Reading the Courtyards

The plan is Chinese imperial: a central axis runs north through gate halls and courtyards. Halls with tiled, upturned roofs sit on stone platforms on either side. Walk the axis first, then work outward.

- **Main assembly hall (Tsogchin):** the largest building, set on the central line.
- **Side temples:** smaller halls with painted timber, prayer wheels and butter-lamp altars.
- **The hillside stupa and statue:** climb the slope behind the compound for the only view that shows the whole layout against the steppe.

Expect two to three hours for the temples and the hill. Monks live and study here, so some doors stay shut while rituals are on.

## Getting There from Darkhan

Most people come by road, either as a detour from Darkhan or on the drive between Ulaanbaatar and Erdenet. The paved highway gets you only part of the way. The final approach is a long dirt and gravel track across the valley, and after rain it turns rutted.

- Hire a driver with a 4x4 who knows the turnoff; signage is minimal.
- Carry water and snacks: food options near the monastery are limited.
- Look into the ger camps in the valley if you want to stay overnight, so you catch the place at dawn and dusk.

Summer (June to August) is the practical window, when the tracks are passable and the grass is green. A large religious festival is held here in summer, with dates set by the lunar calendar. Confirm the year's date locally before planning around it.

## Temple Manners at Amarbayasgalant

This is a working monastery, not a museum. Its rules follow Mongolian Buddhist custom.

- **Move clockwise** around temples, stupas and prayer wheels, and spin the wheels with your right hand.
- **Ask before photographing** inside halls or photographing monks. Many interiors don't allow photos.
- **Don't step on door thresholds.** Step over them, a custom observed across Mongolia.
- **Dress modestly:** cover your shoulders and knees, and take off your hat indoors.
- **Never point your feet** at altars or seated monks.

Small offerings left at altars are common, and so are donations toward restoration. Put them in the boxes provided rather than handing them to monks. If chanting is under way, stand quietly at the back and wait to be invited further in.

Staying overnight pays off here. Day visitors arriving from the highway tend to bunch around midday, so the early morning and evening light usually go to the people sleeping in the valley.
