## One night, one stadium

The tour stops in Lima on January 22, 2027. Karol G, the Medellín-born singer her fans call La Bichota, plays Estadio San Marcos that night. It is a single date, so the whole of Peru's fan base comes to one open-air bowl.

January is high summer on the Peruvian coast. The grey winter haze is gone, the evenings stay warm and humid, and rain is rare. Pink hair, pink wigs and pink outfits are the usual uniform at her shows. Expect a crowd that knows every word of "Tusa", "Bichota" and "Provenza".

## Inside Estadio San Marcos

This is an older concrete stadium. It belongs to the Universidad Nacional Mayor de San Marcos, founded in 1551 and one of the oldest universities in the Americas. The ground sits inside the university's walled Ciudad Universitaria campus. That means fences, controlled entrances and a long walk in from the street.

For concerts the field is turned into a standing and floor-seat area facing the stage. The stands rise steeply around it. Stand seats give you a clear view of the full production, and the field puts you close enough to see the dancers' faces. Your ticket category decides which gate and which section you use, so the zone name on it matters.

## Reaching the Ciudad Universitaria

The campus is in Cercado de Lima, along Avenida Universitaria and Avenida Venezuela. Most visitors stay in Miraflores, Barranco or San Isidro, which puts the stadium well across the city. Lima traffic is heavy at the best of times, and a stadium night makes it worse.

How fans usually get there:

- **Ride-hailing apps** (Uber, Cabify and InDrive all run in Lima). Most people use these, because you get a fixed fare and a tracked route.
- **Taxis booked through a hotel**, a common choice for visitors who don't want to haggle in the street.
- **Group transfers**, which fan clubs and tour operators sometimes run from the main hotel districts.

After the show, cars back up around the campus exits. Lots of people walk a few blocks away from the gates before requesting a car, so the driver can actually reach them. Jorge Chávez International Airport in Callao is closer to the stadium than Miraflores is, which suits fans flying in that day.

## Tickets and what to carry

Tickets are sold through the tour's official ticketing partner, and the categories and prices sit on that platform. Resold tickets from strangers are a well-known risk for big Lima concerts. Fakes and duplicated QR codes turn up at the gate, so most fans stick to the official channel.

Stadium security searches everyone. Fans usually bring:

- Their ID or passport. Foreigners carry the passport, since tickets are often tied to a document number.
- A phone with the ticket saved offline, because mobile signal drops when the bowl fills up.
- A light layer for the walk out, when the coastal air cools down.
- A small bag only. Large backpacks, umbrellas and professional cameras are routinely turned away at Lima stadiums.

## How Lima does a stadium night

Peruvian concert crowds are loud and sing along to everything, and the queue is part of the night. Lots of ticket holders line up outside the campus hours before the doors open, especially for the field zones. Street vendors work the line selling water, snacks, glow sticks and pink merchandise.

Most stalls take Yape or Plin, Peru's mobile payment apps. A foreign card won't work on those, so visitors usually carry small soles notes for the street. Inside, the food and drink counters are generally cashless or card-friendly.

Keep your phone in a front pocket or zipped bag. Phone theft in packed stadium crowds is a known problem in Lima. Locals hold their phones tightly while filming and put them away in the crush at the exits.

## Around the show

The neighbourhood around the campus is residential and commercial, and it isn't somewhere visitors stay. Most fans treat the concert as one trip out from Miraflores or Barranco. They spend the day by the ocean and leave for the stadium in the afternoon.

Turning it into a longer stay is easy. In the historic centre, a short drive from the campus, you can see the Plaza Mayor, the Cathedral and the Monastery of San Francisco with its catacombs. Back in Miraflores, the clifftop Malecón and Parque del Amor make a calm walk the morning after. A plate of ceviche at lunch is the usual Lima way to get over a long night of reggaetón.
