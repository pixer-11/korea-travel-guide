Taichung's West District has no shortage of izakaya, but 肉料理・福 is the first in Taiwan to build its entire identity around meat: not just grilled skewers on the side, but Japanese wagyu, offal, and Taiwanese beef treated as the main event across a run of small plates.

The name itself tells you what to expect. 肉料理 simply means "meat cuisine," and 福 (fuku) is the character for good fortune, a common note struck by Japanese-leaning restaurants in Taiwan. Together they signal a shop that isn't hedging: this is a meat izakaya, full stop.

## Why chef Sam Chung's name matters here

The kitchen is run by Sam Chung, the Michelin-starred chef behind Oretachi No Nikuya, one of Taichung's known names in Japanese-style grilled meat. His track record is the reason this opening has drawn attention beyond the neighborhood.

Chefs who've earned a star tend to bring the same discipline to a smaller, cheaper concept: tight sourcing, careful cuts, a real point of view on where the meat comes from. That's the pitch here, translated into an izakaya's more casual, drink-friendly format rather than a formal tasting menu.

## What actually lands on the table

The stated focus is threefold: Japanese wagyu, offal, and Taiwanese beef, served as small plates meant for sharing rather than single large cuts. That's a notably broader range than most yakiniku spots, which tend to shy away from offal or treat it as a novelty item.

Expect the format to reward ordering across categories rather than settling on one protein:

- Japanese wagyu, likely prepared simply to let the marbling do the work
- Offal cuts, the part of the menu that signals real confidence in Taiwanese and Japanese nose-to-tail traditions
- Taiwanese beef, giving a local counterpoint to the imported wagyu

Because this is a small-plates concept, plan to order in rounds rather than picking one dish and stopping. That's the izakaya logic: taste widely, keep the table moving, pace it against drinks.

## Finding it in West District

West District (西區) sits just west of Taichung's old downtown core, an area dense with cafes, boutiques, and a growing cluster of chef-driven restaurants, partly riding the reputation Oretachi No Nikuya already built in the same city. If you know that restaurant, treat 肉料理・福 as its sibling concept rather than a copy.

Because this is a recently opened, trending spot, exact address details and hours are best confirmed the day you plan to go rather than assumed from an old listing. Search the current Chinese name, 肉料理・福, alongside "台中" (Taichung) for the latest map pin and posted hours.

## Booking a table without the guesswork

New openings tied to a known chef's name fill up fast in Taichung, especially on weekends, so treat a reservation as close to mandatory rather than optional.

- Call or book through whatever channel is currently listed on the restaurant's social page, since new restaurants in Taiwan often run reservations through Instagram or Line before a formal booking system is set up
- Confirm opening hours the same day, since newly opened restaurants frequently adjust their schedule in the first months
- Ask about seating format when you book: an izakaya-style room may have counter seats as well as tables, and counter seats often move faster if you're a smaller group

If you can't get through by phone, a walk-by earlier in the day to check hours posted on the door is a reasonable backup, common practice for small, newly opened Taiwanese restaurants that haven't yet caught up with an online presence.

## Pacing the meal like a regular

Order in small waves rather than all at once: two or three plates first, more once you see portion sizes, since izakaya-style meat cuts run smaller than a Western steakhouse cut. Pair with the drinks list rather than treating it as an afterthought, since that pacing is the entire point of the izakaya format.

Don't skip the offal selections out of habit. They're listed alongside wagyu deliberately, and in a kitchen built by a Michelin-starred chef, they're likely where the most distinctive cooking is happening rather than the safest.
