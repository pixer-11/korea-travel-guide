## Stadium songs in a candlelit room

The setlist belongs in stadiums. Here it gets one room.

Candlelight is a long-running international concert series that takes well-known music and rearranges it for a small acoustic group, usually strings. The stage and aisles are lined with clusters of candles, and the house lights stay low. For this edition, the music is by Coldplay and Imagine Dragons. Big choruses built for tens of thousands of voices get played quietly, close up, to a seated audience.

The Istanbul show takes place on Saturday, December 5, 2026. Anyone expecting a lookalike band will be surprised. Nobody here is impersonating Chris Martin or Dan Reynolds. The melodies carry the show on their own.

## Grand Pera Emek Sahnesi, İstiklal Avenue

The venue has a long story behind it. Emek was one of Beyoğlu's best-known cinemas, inside the Cercle d'Orient building on İstiklal Caddesi. For decades it was a centre of the Istanbul Film Festival, and its demolition became one of the city's big cultural arguments. The Grand Pera complex now on the site includes Emek Sahnesi as a performance hall.

It is a proper theatre: tiered seats facing a stage, with good sightlines. That suits a candlelit format, where the light comes from the stage rather than the ceiling. Signs give the venue's full Turkish name, Grand Pera Emek Sahnesi. Taxi drivers know it as "Emek" on İstiklal.

## Reaching Beyoğlu on a December night

İstiklal is pedestrianised, so the last stretch is on foot whichever way you come.

- **Metro:** Take the M2 to Taksim, then walk down İstiklal toward Galatasaray. It takes roughly ten minutes.
- **From the Old City:** Take the T1 tram to Kabataş, then the F1 funicular up to Taksim.
- **From the south end:** Şişhane (M2) and the historic Tünel funicular from Karaköy both come out near the lower end of İstiklal. From there it's a longer walk uphill.
- **The red tram:** The nostalgic tram runs along İstiklal between Tünel and Taksim. It's slow, but it does save your legs.

Istanbul in early December is usually cool and damp, and İstiklal's paving stones get slick in the rain. A jacket you can fold onto your lap helps, since most theatre seating has little room for a coat.

## Seats, phones and candle etiquette

Candlelight tickets are generally sold by seating zone rather than by seat number. Within a zone, seats are usually filled in order of arrival. Attendees who care about a centre view typically turn up well before the start for that reason. Couples and groups who want to sit together tend to arrive together.

A few habits the series' audiences usually follow:

- **Phones stay down during the music.** Filming is commonly allowed only during the final piece. Silent photos in the gaps between songs are the norm.
- **No flash.** It ruins the candlelight for everyone around you, and the musicians see it straight away.
- **Applause between pieces, not during them.** These arrangements often have quiet endings, so people wait for the bows.
- **Late entry is awkward.** In a dim room with a small ensemble, latecomers are noticeable and are usually seated at a pause.

There's no tipping at events in Turkey, and cards are accepted almost everywhere along İstiklal. Ticket holders usually show the QR code on their phone at the door.

## Before and after on İstiklal

The concert's location is half its appeal. You come out onto one of Istanbul's busiest night-time streets.

- **Çiçek Pasajı:** This glass-roofed arcade near Galatasaray is lined with meyhanes serving meze and rakı.
- **Nevizade Sokak:** This lane off the fish market behind Çiçek Pasajı is packed with tables, and the crowd usually spills into the street.
- **Galata Tower:** It's a downhill walk past Şişhane, and the area around it is lit up after dark.
- **Pera Museum and the Pera Palace Hotel:** Both are a few minutes off İstiklal in the Tepebaşı streets, if you're making a full evening of Beyoğlu.

Many concertgoers end the night with something hot from a street cart: roasted chestnuts in winter, or simit. Then they walk back up to Taksim for the metro.
