## Five months of art, all over the city

There is no single front gate. Bangkok Art Biennale 2026 runs from October 29, 2026 to February 28, 2027 across multiple venues around Bangkok. You don't do it in one visit like a museum. You build your own route through the city.

Since the first edition in 2018, the biennale has mixed gallery spaces with places Bangkok uses every day. Earlier editions put installations inside working temple compounds such as Wat Pho and Wat Arun. The art sat a few steps from gilded Buddha images and stupas covered in porcelain mosaic. The Bangkok Art and Culture Centre, with its white spiralling ramps, held the gallery side of past editions.

## Dates, tickets and the venue list

The schedule itself is simple. The exhibition opens on October 29, 2026 and closes on February 28, 2027. The organiser publishes the full venue list, opening days and any ticket links on the official Bangkok Art Biennale site.

Each venue keeps its own hours. A temple, a shopping complex and a public gallery do not open and close together, so a day that takes in several sites runs on several clocks.

Earlier editions kept most venues free to enter. Temple venues charged their normal admission at the gate, which is the same ticket any visitor buys. Each site posts its price at its own entrance.

## Riding the rails and the river between sites

Trains and boats cover most of the distances between venues in past editions:

- **Bangkok Art and Culture Centre:** BTS National Stadium station (Silom Line), linked by skywalk. Siam station is a short walk away.
- **Wat Pho:** MRT Sanam Chai station (Blue Line), a few minutes' walk.
- **Wat Arun:** the short cross-river ferry from Tha Tien pier, next to Wat Pho.
- **Riverside venues:** the Chao Phraya Express Boat. Sathien pier connects to BTS Saphan Taksin.

Road traffic between the old town and the commercial districts can be slow. For that reason, visitors usually take the boat or the train and save Grab rides for the last stretch.

## Timing the visit in the cool season

The biennale falls in Bangkok's cool, dry season. The most comfortable stretch is November to February, when humidity drops, though early afternoons are still hot. Late October sits at the tail of the rainy season and can still bring heavy showers.

Most visitors spread the venues over two or three days and group them by area. The riverside temples make one day and the Siam and Pathumwan galleries make another. Within a cluster, expect a few unhurried hours. Temple courtyards are mostly open-air, so mornings and late afternoons are the gentler times for those.

## Temple manners and gallery habits

At temple venues the art follows temple rules, and the dress code applies to everyone. Shoulders and knees should be covered. Visitors carry a light scarf or wear long trousers rather than renting a wrap at the gate.

- **Shoes:** they come off before you enter an ordination hall or viharn. You'll see them lined up on the steps outside.
- **Feet:** when sitting near a Buddha image, tuck your feet away from it, never pointing at it.
- **Photography:** rules change from artwork to artwork. Small signs beside each piece say whether photos are allowed.
- **Paying for transport:** BTS riders use a Rabbit card or a single-journey ticket. MRT gates take contactless bank cards. Ferry tickets are sold at the pier booths.

Inside the galleries the mood is quieter. Large bags sometimes go to a cloakroom. Each work has a wall text in English and Thai.

## What sits next door

Each cluster of venues comes with neighbours worth an hour of your time. Near Wat Pho, the Grand Palace is a short walk north. The lanes around Tha Tien pier smell of the dried seafood sold in their old shopfronts.

Across from the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre, the MBK Center and Siam's malls are a few minutes away along the skywalk. Jim Thompson House, the teak home of the American silk merchant, is one stop from National Stadium. In the evening, the riverbank by Tha Tien looks straight across to Wat Arun as the lights come on along its spire.
