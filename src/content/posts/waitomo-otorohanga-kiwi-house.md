## Into the dark at midday

Your eyes need a few minutes. The nocturnal kiwi houses flip day and night, so the lighting is kept dim while the sun is up outside. That means the birds forage while you're awake to watch them. Stand still behind the glass and the shapes come together: a round brown body and a long bill pushing through leaf litter.

Kiwi are shy, nocturnal and almost never seen in the wild. That makes this one of the more reliable places on the North Island to watch one actually moving. Daylight runs on the outside paths, where ponds and native planting link the enclosures.

## What fills the rest of the visit

The kiwi are the reason to come, but they aren't the whole visit. The preserve is built around a loop of walkways. Plan for:

- **Nocturnal kiwi houses:** the main event. Go through more than once, because the birds move between hidden spots and visible ones.
- **Talks and feedings:** held through the day. Ask at the entrance for today's times and plan your loop around them.
- **The large aviary:** a walk-through space where native birds fly overhead and perch at eye level.
- **Paths and ponds:** a level, easy circuit between enclosures, fine for children and slower walkers.

One to two hours covers it without rushing. Add time if you want to catch more than one talk.

## Reaching Ōtorohanga from Waitomo

The Kiwi House is on Alex Telfer Drive in Ōtorohanga. The town is roughly 16km north of Waitomo Caves village, and the two pair naturally in a day. Most visitors drive State Highway 3, and the trip takes about 15 minutes from the caves. Hamilton is about an hour north.

Without a car, the Northern Explorer train between Auckland and Wellington stops at Ōtorohanga. The town centre is compact, so you can easily walk the rest. Look out for the Kiwiana displays along the main street on the way.

At the moment the gates open 9am–5pm. Wildlife parks change their hours with the seasons and on public holidays, though, so check the official site for this month's times. Arriving soon after opening leaves room to see the cave tours in the afternoon.

## Keeping quiet in the kiwi houses

How you behave inside changes what you see. Kiwi startle easily, so treat the nocturnal houses like a library:

- **No flash, no torch, no phone screen at full brightness.** Turn your screen down or put the phone away. Most photos come out black anyway.
- **Talk in whispers** and keep children close to the glass rather than running between windows.
- **Give it five minutes.** People who walk straight through often say they saw nothing. People who wait usually spot a bird.
- **Move along gently** when it's busy so others get a turn at the best viewing windows.

Entry prices are posted at the entrance and on the official website. Paying by card and contactless is normal across New Zealand, and tipping isn't expected. Staff at the talks are the best people to ask about individual birds and the kiwi conservation work behind the centre, so save your questions for them.

If you're doing the caves as well, bring a light layer. The kiwi houses are kept cool and dim, a lot like the caves.
