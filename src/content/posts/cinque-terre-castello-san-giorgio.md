## Why bother with a castle outside the villages

Castello San Giorgio stands on a hill above La Spezia, the transport hub most Cinque Terre visitors pass through anyway. The current structure dates to the 14th century, built over an earlier fortification, and was restored after WWII bomb damage.

Inside, the archaeology museum holds Lunigiana statue-stelae, carved anthropomorphic slabs from the Bronze and Iron Ages found across the surrounding valleys. These are the reason serious visitors detour here rather than just changing trains in La Spezia.

## Getting there and what it costs you in time

From La Spezia Centrale station, it's a steep 20-minute walk uphill through the old town, or a short taxi ride if you'd rather save your legs for the Cinque Terre trails. There's no direct bus that drops you at the gate. Address for a map app: Via XXVII Marzo, 36, 19121 La Spezia SP.

Open daily, 10am to 5pm, with no listed closing day, though it's worth confirming before you climb, since Italian museums occasionally shift hours around holidays. Plan on 45 minutes for the exhibits and ramparts, longer if a temporary exhibition has taken over one of the halls.

## The view is the real draw

The terrace and walls give you an unobstructed look down over La Spezia's naval port, cranes and warships included, out to the Gulf of Poets where the coastline bends toward Portovenere. It's a different register of view from the cliffside villages: industrial harbor rather than pastel houses, but genuinely wide and high.

Photographers should aim for late afternoon light, when the sun swings around behind you rather than into the lens. The castle draws a well-known, steady stream of visitors rather than being any kind of secret, so don't expect to have the ramparts to yourself, especially on weekends.

## Making the most of the visit

Buy tickets at the door; this isn't a site that requires advance booking the way Cinque Terre's park trails sometimes do. Cash and card are both generally accepted at Italian municipal museum ticket desks, but carry some cash as backup.

Wear real shoes, not sandals. The approach is cobbled and uphill, and the internal walkways along the ramparts are uneven stone.

Pair the visit with lunch in La Spezia's old town on the way back down; via Prione and the streets around it have trattorias serving the same seafood you'll find in the villages, usually at lower prices. If you're doing Cinque Terre by train, this makes a logical half-day bookend on the day you arrive or leave, rather than a special trip of its own.
