## Watching from the stands

Badminton halls have their own etiquette. Fans and strong air conditioning are usually kept low because moving air pushes a shuttle off line, so halls often run warm. Regulars tend to bring:

- Light, breathable clothing
- A refillable water bottle
- A phone with the order of play open

Spectators stay seated during rallies and move only between points or at the interval. The interval comes when either player reaches 11 points in a game. Flash photography is off-limits because players are tracking a shuttle at speed. Home players usually get the loudest support, and the noise stops on the serve.

Across Bengaluru, people mostly pay by UPI. QR codes are standard at snack counters and in auto-rickshaws, and cards work at larger outlets. Visitors without an Indian bank account usually carry some cash or use a ride app's card payment instead.

## Getting around Bengaluru in November

November is one of Bengaluru's mild months. The southwest monsoon is over, days are warm and evenings cool, and the odd shower blows in from the northeast monsoon.

Kempegowda International Airport is about 40 km north of the city centre. Depending on traffic, the road trip in usually takes an hour or more. Inside the city, Namma Metro's Purple and Green lines cross at Nadaprabhu Kempegowda station (Majestic). Most visitors combine the metro with one of these:

- Uber or Ola
- Namma Yatri, the local auto-rickshaw app
- A metered auto-rickshaw hailed on the street

Weekday rush hours clog the main roads in the morning and evening. The evening sessions on the 2026 schedule, November 3–6, fell within the evening rush.

Badminton fans in town for the week sometimes add a look at the city's badminton culture. Bengaluru is home to the Prakash Padukone Badminton Academy, named after the former All England champion.
