## Why go

Myeongdong, in Jung District, is the most concentrated retail strip in central Seoul — a dense grid of pedestrian lanes lined with Korean cosmetics flagships (Innisfree, Etude, Olive Young, Nature Republic), fast-fashion stores, and duty-free department stores, threaded through with street-food carts. With over 26,000 reviews and a 4.4 rating on Google, it's one of the most consistently visited commercial districts in the city — not an undiscovered spot, but a genuinely essential one for understanding how Seoul shops and eats on the street. Expect crowds; the point isn't solitude, it's the density and energy of it.

## Getting there

The neighborhood sits between two subway lines, so approach from whichever suits your route:

- **Myeongdong Station (Line 4), Exit 6 or 8** drops you directly into the main pedestrian shopping lane.
- **Euljiro 1(il)-ga Station (Line 2), Exit 5 or 6** is a short walk north and useful if you're coming from Euljiro or City Hall.

Both exits lead into a walkable, largely flat grid, so once you're in you can cover most of it on foot in under 20 minutes end to end — though you'll want longer to actually browse.

## What to see and eat

The main street food scene comes alive on the central pedestrian lane, where carts sell **tteokbokki** (spicy rice cakes), **hotteok** (sweet filled pancakes), **gyeran-ppang** (egg bread), skewered meats and giant fried mandu. Prices are usually cash-friendly small bites in the low thousands of won, though it's worth having cards ready too since most stalls now take mobile and card payment.

For shopping, the beauty aisle is the main draw: Korean skincare and cosmetics brands cluster here with multiple storefronts each, often stocked with tourist-exclusive gift sets and frequent promotional giveaways (samples, mini masks) at checkout — it's worth asking, as staff sometimes offer these unprompted to foreign visitors. Bigger anchors like Lotte Department Store Main Branch and Shinsegae Department Store sit at the edges of the district and are useful for duty-free shopping or a break from the street crowd.

A few minutes' walk south brings you to **Myeongdong Cathedral**, Seoul's first Gothic-style Catholic cathedral, completed in 1898 — a quiet architectural counterpoint to the retail bustle and worth 15–20 minutes if you want a change of pace.

## When to go

Myeongdong is busiest in early evening through night, especially Friday and Saturday, when both shoppers and street-food crowds peak together. For a calmer visit, aim for a **weekday afternoon** (roughly 2–5pm), after the lunch rush and before the evening surge. The street stalls typically start setting up in late afternoon and run into the night, so if you want food-cart Myeongdong specifically, early evening (around 5–6pm) hits the sweet spot of stalls being open but lines still short. Winter evenings add lights and a colder-weather appeal to hotteok and skewers; summer keeps stalls running long after dark.

## How to visit like a local

Treat Myeongdong as a fast-moving grazing route rather than a sit-down meal: order one or two items per cart, eat standing at the small counters provided, and keep moving — this is how the flow of the street actually works, and lingering at a single cart during peak hours draws attention. Payment varies by vendor: many street stalls are cash-first but increasingly accept card or mobile pay (Naver Pay, Kakao Pay), so carry some small won notes as backup rather than relying on cards alone. Tipping isn't expected anywhere in Korea, including here. In cosmetics shops, browsing without buying is completely normal and staff won't pressure you, but if you do buy, ask about the tourist discount or VAT refund counter, since many stores process it on the spot with your passport. The most common visitor mistake is arriving on a Friday or Saturday night expecting easy movement — the lanes genuinely bottleneck then, so if crowd-avoidance matters more to you than nightlife energy, shift your visit earlier in the day or midweek.
