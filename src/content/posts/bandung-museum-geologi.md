## What's inside a 1928 Dutch colonial hall

Museum Geologi opened in 1928 as a research institute under Dutch colonial rule, and the building itself, a long Art Deco hall with high ceilings and tall shuttered windows, still does the cooling work air conditioning does elsewhere in Bandung. The ground floor west wing covers geology: rock and mineral specimens, volcanic models, and displays on Indonesia's tectonic setting along the Pacific Ring of Fire.

The east wing holds the paleontology collection: invertebrate fossils, then the vertebrate hall with a cast of a Tyrannosaurus skeleton and fossilized remains of Java Man (Homo erectus), found at Sangiran and Trinil. Upstairs covers mining history and the economic uses of minerals across Indonesia. Labels are in Bahasa Indonesia and English.

## Getting there and around Cihaur Geulis

The address is Jl. Diponegoro No.57, in Cihaur Geulis, Kecamatan Cibeunying Kaler. It sits directly across from Gedung Sate, Bandung's landmark colonial government building, so pair the two on one walk. Grab or Gojek is the simplest way in; parking is limited and street-side.

- Nearest landmark: Gedung Sate (directly opposite)
- Neighborhood: Cihaur Geulis, Cibeunying Kaler
- Typical visit length: 60–90 minutes

## Hours that trip people up

Museum Geologi keeps a shorter week than most Bandung attractions. It's open 9am–3pm Monday through Thursday, then 9am–2pm on Saturday and Sunday, and closed entirely on Friday.

Weekends are the busiest stretch across the whole 9am–2pm window, with school groups and family outings filling the halls early and staying late. If you want breathing room around the dinosaur cast and Java Man fossils, go on a Tuesday or Wednesday morning instead.

## Getting past the school groups

This is one of Bandung's most-visited museums, so plan around the crowds rather than expecting to dodge them entirely. Weekday mornings, right after the 9am opening, are calmer than any weekend hour, since Saturday and Sunday stay packed straight through to closing at 2pm.

Entry has historically been ticketed at a small fixed price at the front desk, cash preferred; confirm the current fee on arrival since it can shift. Guided explanations sometimes run for school groups but individual visitors mostly self-guide with the printed labels.

- Wear layers: the halls are cool but courtyards outside are not
- Photography is generally allowed without flash near fossil casts
- Bags get a quick check at the entrance, standard for Indonesian public museums
- If visiting Friday, redirect to Gedung Sate or Saung Angklung Udjo instead, since the museum is fully closed that day
