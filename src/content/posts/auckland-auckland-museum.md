## A temple on a volcano

The approach tells you what kind of building this is. You cross the lawns of the Auckland Domain and climb toward a pale neoclassical facade with columns across the front. Below it stands the Cenotaph in the Court of Honour, and the harbour opens out behind you.

The building's full name is Auckland War Memorial Museum, and in te reo Māori it is Tāmaki Paenga Hira. It opened in 1929 on Pukekawa, the remains of one of Auckland's volcanic cones. That makes it a museum and a memorial at once. You see both sides of it in every visit.

## Floor by floor

The ground floor holds the Māori and Pacific collections, and it is the reason most people come. These are the pieces to find:

- **Hotunui**, a carved wharenui (meeting house) built in 1878, which you can walk into
- **Te Toki a Tapiri**, a waka taua (war canoe) carved from a single tōtara trunk, the last of its kind
- Pacific Island canoes, tapa cloth and carvings in the neighbouring galleries

The middle floor is natural history. The volcano gallery covers the field Auckland is built on, and its simulated eruption seen from a suburban living room makes the geology personal. The top floor is the memorial: First and Second World War halls, the Hall of Memories with walls of names, and aircraft that include a Spitfire.

## Hotunui etiquette and timing your visit

The museum is very popular, so a little planning pays off. These are the things that make a difference:

- **Book ahead.** Admission is free for New Zealand residents. International visitors pay, and the current price is posted on the official site. Buying online saves time at the desk, and card payment is standard.
- **Treat taonga with respect.** The Māori collection is made up of ancestral treasures. Follow the signs at Hotunui, don't touch the carvings, and keep food and drink out of the galleries.
- **Use the late night.** Tuesday is currently the one evening it stays open until 8:30pm. Weekend days start an hour earlier, at 9am, so arriving at opening gives you the ground floor before families and school groups fill it.
- **See the cultural performance.** The museum runs a Māori cultural performance that is ticketed separately, so check its session times when you book.
- **Plan for the top floor.** People walk the memorial halls quietly, and many visitors leave them until last.

## Getting up the hill

The museum is in Parnell, inside the Auckland Domain, Auckland's oldest park. Grafton and Parnell train stations are both about a 15-minute walk away, uphill through the park. The walk from Parnell Road is shorter and passes cafés along the way. There is parking on the museum grounds, but on busy days it fills up.

Stay in the Domain afterwards. The Wintergarden glasshouses are a few minutes' walk away, and Parnell's shops and restaurants are just downhill. On Anzac Day (25 April), thousands gather at the Cenotaph for the dawn service. Expect large crowds and a changed schedule that day.
