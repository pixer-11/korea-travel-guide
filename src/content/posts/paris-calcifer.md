## The fire is the kitchen

There's no walled-off back-of-house theatre here in the usual sense; the grill is the point. Meats, whole fish and vegetables all go directly over open flame, with char, smoke and the uneven heat of real wood or coal doing the work that a induction burner can't fake. That's the whole premise of Paris's current "braise" moment, a swing back toward primal cooking methods after years of tweezer-plated fine dining, and Calcifer is one of the names driving it into 2026.

Don't expect a menu built on delicate reductions or hidden techniques. The draw is watching ingredients go in raw and come out marked by fire, then land on the table still carrying that smell of scorched wood and rendered fat.

It's a deliberate contrast: a very old cooking method, live fire, staged inside a very current, pop-culture-literate room. That combination is a large part of why it's become one of the talked-about tables of the year rather than just another grill restaurant.
