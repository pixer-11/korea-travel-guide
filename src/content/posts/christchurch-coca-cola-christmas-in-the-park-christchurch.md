## Carols on Hagley Park's grass

This is Christmas in early summer. Coca-Cola Christmas in the Park turns Hagley Park into an outdoor concert ground on November 28, 2026. Around 100,000 people come, which makes it one of the biggest single-night gatherings in the South Island.

Expect a stage facing open lawn, with crowds spreading back across the grass and under the park's old trees. The concert is free and is held every year. Families, groups of friends and older couples all come for the Christmas songs, sung along to with the whole crowd.

## Date, entry and what it costs

The 2026 edition takes place on Saturday, November 28. It is held in Hagley Park, the 164-hectare green belt bordered by Deans Avenue, Riccarton Avenue, Harper Avenue and Rolleston Avenue.

- **Tickets:** none. It's a free event, open to anyone who walks in.
- **Seating:** there isn't any. Seating is whatever you bring.
- **Lineup and start time:** these are set by the organiser each year, and this guide doesn't list them.
- **Food:** most people bring a picnic. Any food stalls set their own prices at the stall.

## Reaching Hagley Park on concert night

Christchurch is flat, compact and easy to walk. That suits a crowd this size. From Cathedral Square, the walk west along Worcester Boulevard to the park's Rolleston Avenue edge takes about 15 minutes. You pass the Christchurch Art Gallery on the way.

The Bus Interchange on Lichfield Street is the main hub for city buses. Routes along Riccarton Road and Deans Avenue drop passengers along the park's southern and western sides. Many locals cycle instead, using the shared paths that cross the park.

Driving is the slow option. Parking around Hagley Park fills quickly, and the roads around it clog when 100,000 people leave at once. Christchurch Airport is roughly a 20-minute drive away. Visitors usually stay in the central city or in Riccarton, both within walking distance.

## Claiming grass among 100,000 people

Regulars treat the evening as a long picnic that ends with a concert. A few habits make the difference:

- **A blanket or low chair.** Low beach chairs keep the view clear for the people behind you, and high chairs get looks.
- **Layers.** Late-November evenings in Canterbury can turn cool once an easterly wind picks up, even after a warm afternoon.
- **Sun protection.** New Zealand's UV is strong, and it's still light well into the evening at this time of year.
- **A meeting point.** Phone signal can strain in a crowd this size, so groups usually agree on a landmark tree or gate.

People generally claim their patch of grass hours before the music starts, and the lawn nearest the stage fills first. The crowd is relaxed and family-heavy. People sing along, children dance near the back, and almost everyone packs their rubbish out at the end.

When everyone leaves at once, the paths get crowded. Locals often wait twenty minutes on the grass to let the crowd thin out. Others walk the Avon River side toward the city instead of joining the main stream.

Spend the next morning nearby. The Christchurch Botanic Gardens sit in the loop of the Avon River at the park's eastern side. Punts leave from the Antigua Boatsheds on Rolleston Avenue. Riccarton Bush, with its stand of old kahikatea forest, is a short walk west.
