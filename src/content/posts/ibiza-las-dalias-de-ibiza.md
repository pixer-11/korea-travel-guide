## Saturday at kilometre 12

Cars pull off onto the verge long before the entrance. People walk the last stretch along the Sant Carles road, carrying bags and waiting to see what fills them. Beyond the gate, the market spreads through a garden of trees and whitewashed walls: stall after stall under awnings, fabric overhead, silver laid out on cloth.

This is Las Dalias de Ibiza, the best-known hippy market on the island. It is not a secret and does not pretend to be. It pulls a large crowd, gets very well reviewed, and runs on the handmade, the scented and the slightly bohemian. A little strategy makes it far more enjoyable.

## What fills the stalls

Makers and resellers work side by side here. Some stallholders sell what they made at home that week, and others bring in stock. Handle things, ask questions, and you will soon tell them apart.

What you can expect to find:

- **Handmade soaps** and natural cosmetics, often sold by the bar or slice
- **Jewellery**, especially silver, beads and stones
- **Clothing**: loose cotton, linen and the white "Adlib" look Ibiza is known for
- **Leather** sandals, belts and bags
- **Homewares**: dreamcatchers, woven baskets, textiles, incense
- **Art and prints** from island-based artists

Between the craft rows you will find food stands and seating, and DJ music runs through the day. Prices are posted at each stall. Treat the food area as a place to pause, not a detour. The market is bigger than it looks from the gate, and you will want to sit down halfway through.

## From roadside bar to hippy institution

Las Dalias started as a roadside bar in 1954. At the time, Sant Carles was a quiet farming parish of carob and almond trees. In the 1960s and 70s the countryside around the village became a magnet for the hippies who settled in Ibiza's northeast. They rented old *fincas* and made the local bars their meeting points.

The market grew out of that scene and began in 1985. The craft stalls, the music and the relaxed, barefoot-adjacent dress code still carry that lineage, even now that the visitors arrive in hire cars rather than on the ferry from Barcelona with a guitar. Knowing this helps explain why Las Dalias sits out here on a country road rather than in Ibiza Town.

## Getting out to Sant Carles

The address is simply *Carretera, Km. 12*, on the road that runs north from Santa Eulària des Riu to the village of Sant Carles de Peralta. By car it is a short drive from Santa Eulària and roughly half an hour from Ibiza Town, depending on traffic.

- **Driving:** parking is in informal areas and along the road nearby. On busy days it fills early, and the walk from a distant spot can be long on a hot verge.
- **Bus:** public buses run between Santa Eulària and Sant Carles. Check the current timetable with the island's bus operator, as frequency changes with the season.
- **Taxi:** handy for the return leg if you have bought more than you meant to. Ranks thin out in the countryside, so agree a pickup time in advance.

Many visitors combine the trip with a stay in Santa Eulària or Es Canar, which puts the market within easy reach of the morning.

## Working the market without the crush

Crowds are the one real downside, and most of the tactics are about sidestepping them. The market's popularity means midday on a peak-season Saturday can feel shoulder to shoulder in the narrow rows.

- **Arrive at opening.** Getting in early usually beats the coach groups and the late-breakfast crowd from the resorts. It also means the pick of the one-off pieces.
- **Walk a full loop before buying.** Similar items turn up at several stalls. A first pass lets you compare quality, and the same design in silver versus plated metal is not the same purchase.
- **Carry both card and cash.** Plenty of stalls take cards, but smaller makers may prefer cash, and a few coins make a quick drink simpler.
- **Don't expect hard haggling.** This is a craft market, not a flea market, and prices are generally as marked. Buying several pieces from one stall is the polite moment to ask whether they can do something.
- **Ask before photographing** a maker's work up close. Designs get copied, and many stallholders are protective of them.
- **Bring a bag.** A tote saves juggling a stack of small paper packages through the crowd.

Dress for heat and dust in summer. The garden gives some shade, but the paths between rows can be bright and warm by late morning.

## Check the calendar before you go

Saturday is the traditional market day, and it runs through much of the year. In summer, Las Dalias has also staged evening markets with a different feel: lights strung through the trees, more music, a later crowd.

Schedules shift from season to season, and special events and concerts come and go. The official Las Dalias website is the place to confirm what is on before you drive out. It is the difference between a full market and a quiet car park.

## Making a day of the northeast

Las Dalias pairs naturally with the rest of this corner of the island, one of the greener and less built-up parts of Ibiza.

- **Sant Carles village:** a short hop up the road, with a whitewashed church and a small square. The old bar there was a hub of the island's hippy years.
- **Cala Llenya and Cala Mastella:** small coves in pine-backed bays to the east, good for a swim after the market.
- **Aigües Blanques:** a longer beach under sandy cliffs to the north, quieter and more exposed.
- **Punta Arabí market in Es Canar:** Ibiza's other big hippy market, held on Wednesdays in season. Handy if you want to compare the two in one week.
- **Santa Eulària des Riu:** the nearest town, with a seafront promenade and the Puig de Missa church on its hill.

A good rhythm is market in the morning, a long lunch, then a cove in the afternoon when the stalls are at their busiest and you are already in the water.
