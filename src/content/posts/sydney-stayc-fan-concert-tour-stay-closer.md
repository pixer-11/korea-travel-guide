## Sunday 18 October: the last stop

Sydney closes the Australian run. STAYC's "Stay Closer" fan concert tour plays Perth on Wednesday, October 14, Melbourne on Friday, October 16, and Sydney on Sunday, October 18, 2026. That's three cities in five days, and Sydney gets the final night.

By the last stop of a tour leg, the members have already played to two Australian crowds. Sydney is the last audience on this side of the trip. Because the show falls on a Sunday, east-coast fans don't have to rush out of work to get there.

## What a fan concert actually is

A fan concert isn't a full arena tour. The format sits between a fan meeting and a gig. There are songs, but there are also talk segments, games and moments built around the audience rather than around the choreography. The tour's name, "Stay Closer", spells out that idea.

STAYC is a six-member group from High Up Entertainment that debuted in November 2020. The members are Sumin, Sieun, Isa, Seeun, Yoon and J, and the fandom is called SWITH. Their bright, punchy sound gets called "teen fresh", and these are the singles fans tend to know by heart:

- "ASAP"
- "So Bad"
- "RUN2U"
- "Teddy Bear"
- "Bubble"

## Merch lines, lightsticks and SWITH manners

The queue for the show is often not the longest one of the day. At K-pop shows in Australia, the merchandise line usually starts long before doors open. Groups of friends typically split up, with one person in the merch line and another holding a place at the entry.

How the crowd usually behaves:

- **Fan freebies.** Fans often hand out free photocards, stickers and printed slogans in the queue. Trading them is part of the day, and people bring a few spares to swap.
- **Fan chants.** Korean fan chants follow a set script. Fans call out the members' names in order during specific songs. Fan accounts circulate chant guides, and the people who know them make up most of the noise in the room.
- **Lightsticks and banners.** Fans who own the official lightstick bring it. Slogan banners come out at agreed moments.
- **Phones.** Each show sets its own photo and recording policy and announces it in the venue. If you hold a phone up for a whole song, the person behind you misses it.
- **Payment.** Australian venues run largely on card and tap-to-pay, and that includes most merch stands.

## Getting there and back on a Sunday night

Every train, Metro, bus, light rail and ferry in Sydney runs on the Opal network. You tap on and off with an Opal card, a contactless credit or debit card, or a phone wallet. Visitors don't need to buy a separate ticket. Weekend fares are capped lower than weekday ones, which helps on a day spent crossing town.

Sunday timetables are thinner than weekday ones. Late trains still run, but the gaps between them get longer as the night goes on. After the encore, most attendees open the Transport for NSW Trip Planner, or an app such as TripView, to find the next departure from the nearest station.

## Koreatown before doors: Pitt Street and Strathfield

Lots of fans warm up with Korean food. In the CBD, Sydney's Koreatown runs along Pitt Street between Liverpool and Bathurst streets, a short walk from Town Hall station. The strip is packed with Korean barbecue grills, fried chicken shops, late-opening pocha-style bars and Korean cafés. Pre-show dinners are easy to sort here, and so are post-show debriefs.

Strathfield, in the inner west, is the other Korean hub. Restaurants, bakeries and grocers cluster around Strathfield station, which sits on several Sydney Trains lines and is one of the city's best-connected interchanges. It suits fans staying out west or anyone after a quieter sit-down meal.

## Following the tour: Perth, Melbourne, Sydney

Some fans do all three stops. Perth to Melbourne is a long domestic flight, roughly four hours. Western Australia doesn't use daylight saving, and NSW and Victoria are on daylight time by mid-October. That puts Perth three hours behind both east-coast cities, and fans flying east lose those hours.

Melbourne to Sydney is one of the busiest air routes in the country, about an hour and a half in the air. The Saturday between the two eastern shows gives a free day in Sydney. Mid-October is spring, with the harbour foreshore and Circular Quay a ferry ride from the city.
