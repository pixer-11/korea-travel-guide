## A Mughal house built in someone else's accent

A carved stone bowl waits on the lawn before the facade. This is the Hauz-i-Jahangiri, cut from a single block of stone for Emperor Jahangir. Behind it runs a long red sandstone front with white marble inlay, domed chhatris on each corner and a deep central gateway.

Akbar built the palace in the late 16th century, mainly for the women of the royal household. It is the largest residence in Agra Fort. Its name comes from Akbar's son and heir.

## Reading the stone

The building mixes styles, and that is the reason to come. Inside, the Mughal symmetry gives way to Rajput and Gujarati-style construction: heavy stone beams, and brackets carved to look like timber. Look for these:

- **The central courtyard**, lined with pillared halls on all four sides
- **Serpentine brackets** holding up flat stone ceilings, a feature of Hindu temple and palace work
- **Jharokha balconies** where the women could watch the courtyard without being seen
- **River-facing rooms** on the east side, with views over the Yamuna toward the Taj Mahal, about 2.5 km downstream

Walk the east terraces to see how this sandstone palace sits beside Shah Jahan's white marble pavilions. The Khas Mahal and the Musamman Burj are a short walk away. Seeing the two generations together makes the shift in taste obvious.

## Timing and the Friday problem

The listing gives 7:00 AM to 6:00 PM on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday. Friday shows as closed. Confirm on the Archaeological Survey of India site before you plan around a Friday, and don't pair the fort with the Taj Mahal that day, because the Taj also closes on Fridays.

Most visitors to the fort head straight for the Diwan-i-Am and the marble terraces. That leaves the palace courtyard quieter than the rest of the complex. Arriving near the 7:00 AM opening, before the day-trip coaches from Delhi arrive, gives you the best chance at an empty courtyard. It also means soft, low light on the sandstone.

## Getting in through Amar Singh Gate

The palace sits inside Agra Fort, so there is no separate entrance. Visitors enter through the Amar Singh Gate on the fort's south side. The Delhi Gate is kept for military use. Agra Fort railway station is close by, and auto-rickshaws and cycle-rickshaws gather along the approach road from the Taj Mahal area.

A few habits make the visit easier:

- **Buy your ticket online** through the ASI portal and keep the QR code on your phone. The counter queue is the slowest part of the morning.
- **Hire guides only at the gate**, and ask to see their ASI licence. Touts on the approach road are not official.
- **Turn right after the gate ramp.** The palace is among the first big buildings you reach, so seeing it first puts you ahead of the crowds heading for the marble pavilions.
- **Bring water and a hat.** The courtyard and lawns have almost no shade, and the stone holds heat by late morning.
- **Don't climb on the Hauz-i-Jahangiri or the carved brackets.** Wardens do tell visitors off for it.

Plan on 30 to 45 minutes for the palace. Allow two to three hours for the whole fort, then carry on to Mehtab Bagh across the river for a late-afternoon view of the Taj.
