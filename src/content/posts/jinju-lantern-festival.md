## Why go

The Jinju Namgang Yudeung Festival is one of Korea's signature light festivals, built around a genuinely moving piece of history rather than a manufactured spectacle. The floating lanterns on the Namgang River commemorate the 1592-93 Siege of Jinju during the Imjin War, when defenders and civilians reportedly used lit lanterns on the river to communicate across the water and to signal for reinforcements.

That historical layer, thousands of paper and silk lanterns drifting past the stone walls of Jinjuseong Fortress, gives the festival a gravity that distinguishes it from purely decorative lantern events elsewhere in Asia. Large-scale lantern sculptures (zodiac animals, historical figures, folk scenes) line the riverbanks and fortress grounds, and the reflections on the water roughly double the visual effect after dark.

## Getting there

Jinju is in South Gyeongsang Province, reachable by KTX or ITX train from Seoul (via Jinju Station, roughly 3–3.5 hours depending on the route and transfers) or by intercity bus from major cities including Seoul, Busan, and Daegu (Busan is the closest major hub, around 1–1.5 hours by bus or car). Jinju has no subway system. From Jinju Station or the intercity bus terminal, a short taxi ride (roughly 10–15 minutes) or a local city bus reaches Jinjuseong Fortress and the Namgang riverside, which is the festival's main stage.

During the festival, designated shuttle buses and extra city bus routes usually run from the station and terminal directly to the festival entrances. The routes are adjusted each year.

## What to see and do

Jinjuseong Fortress itself is worth a daytime visit as well as an evening one. Inside the walls are the Yeongnam Grand Pavilion (Yeongnamnu), the Uigi-sa shrine honoring the gisaeng Nongae (who, according to legend, leapt into the river with an enemy general during the war), and the Jinju National Museum, which has exhibits on the Imjin War siege. As dusk falls during the festival, the fortress and river transform: thousands of lanterns are set floating downstream, large walk-through lantern tunnels and themed installations (often depicting Korean folklore, zodiac signs, or scenes from the siege) light up along both riverbanks, and a pontoon bridge (Jinnamnu Bridge area) typically lets visitors cross the river amid the lanterns.

Wish lanterns, where visitors write messages and either float or hang their own lantern, are usually available for an additional fee within the festival zone. Food stalls along the riverside sell typical festival fare and Jinju specialties, including Jinju bibimbap, a regional variant said to predate Seoul's more famous version, and Jinju-style cold noodles (naengmyeon) from vendors near the fortress.

## When to go

The festival is held annually in October, typically over roughly two weeks, and exact dates shift slightly each year. The 2026 edition was set to close on October 18. Late afternoon is the time to explore the fortress in daylight. The lanterns are lit from sunset into the evening, when crowds are usually largest; lighting hours vary by year but have often been around 6:30–9:30 PM.

Weeknights are noticeably calmer than weekends, and the first and last weekends tend to be busiest. A proper visit takes at least three to four hours, longer to see both the fortress museum and the full riverside lantern route.

## Nearby & tips

A paid ticket is generally required for the core festival zone (the river lantern area and the fortress lantern displays), while some peripheral lantern displays around the city center are free. Pricing and zone boundaries change from year to year. October evenings by the river can be cool, and phone batteries drain fast when shooting long-exposure lantern photos.

The Jinju Fortress night walk, the nearby Jinju Fish Market and the downtown Jungangno shopping street round out a fuller day trip from Busan or Daegu.
