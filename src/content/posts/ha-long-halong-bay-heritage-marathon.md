## A course with a UNESCO view

This race followed the water. The Halong Bay Heritage Marathon was planned along the coast of Halong Bay, which UNESCO lists as a World Heritage Site for its limestone karsts. Roughly two thousand islands and pillars rise out of green water offshore, and the coast road keeps them in sight.

AIMS affiliation was the detail that mattered to serious runners. It linked the race to the Association of International Marathons and Distance Races, the worldwide body for road events. AIMS standards cover course measurement, so finishing times on an affiliated course carry real weight.

## Date, tickets and the start line

The race was set for **November 22, 2026**. The organisers set the start and finish venue, the distances on offer and the registration fees.

Ha Long splits in two across the water:

- **Bai Chay**, on the west side, holds most of the hotels, the waterfront promenade and the cruise port.
- **Hon Gai**, on the east side, is the older city centre and administrative district.
- **Bai Chay Bridge** connects the two over the mouth of Cua Luc Bay.

Which side the start line sits on makes a real difference. A hotel within walking distance spares runners a taxi hunt in the dark on race morning.

## Getting to Ha Long

Most international runners fly into Hanoi's **Noi Bai International Airport**. The Hanoi to Ha Long expressway brings the drive down to about two and a half hours. Limousine vans, shuttle buses and private transfers all run the route.

**Van Don International Airport** in Quang Ninh province sits closer, around an hour from the city, though it handles far fewer flights. Inside Ha Long, the Grab app works for both cars and motorbike taxis. Taxis also wait outside the larger Bai Chay hotels.

Late November falls in northern Vietnam's dry season. Mornings are cooler than in the summer months, and running conditions are usually better than anything mid-year. A light layer helps during the wait at the start, and a cap helps in the later kilometres once the sun is up.

## Race weekend, the Ha Long way

In Vietnam, mass-participation road races usually start very early to beat the heat, often before dawn. Bib collection at races here is normally held in person before race day, and runners show the ID they registered with.

Everyday spending in Ha Long is in Vietnamese dong:

- **Cash** covers street food, phở stalls and small shops.
- **Cards** are accepted at hotels and larger restaurants.
- **Tipping** isn't customary, but rounding up for drivers and guides is appreciated.

Along race courses here, spectators shout *"cố lên!"*, which roughly means "keep going", and runners often shout it back. Bay cruises leave from the Bai Chay side for close views of the karsts, including Sung Sot Cave and Ti Top Island. Several hours on a boat is easy on tired legs.
