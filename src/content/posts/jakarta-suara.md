## A new kitchen built on old ingredients

Suara is a young restaurant. It opened in April 2026, which puts it among the newest serious Indonesian kitchens in Jakarta.

The idea behind it is narrow and clear. The kitchen takes local ingredients that rarely reach restaurant menus and gives them a refined, contemporary treatment, cooked over a wood-fired stove. You will not find a buffet of Padang classics or a list of greatest hits here. The point is the ingredient itself.

That makes Suara a different kind of meal from most Jakarta dining. The city's food runs from warung counters and street carts to hotel rijsttafel and mall food courts. Suara sits apart from all of those. It tries to show how much of the archipelago's pantry never makes it onto a plate in the capital.

## Beras menthik susu: the rice that leads

The ingredient Suara names first is beras menthik susu. It is a heritage rice from Java, traditionally linked to the farming country around Yogyakarta.

*Susu* means milk in Indonesian, and the name describes the grain. It is short, rounded and an opaque, milky white, unlike the translucent long-grain rice on most Jakarta tables. Cooked, it is soft, slightly sticky and fragrant.

Few restaurants cook it because commercial varieties grow faster and more reliably. Choosing it says something about the kitchen. In Indonesia rice is the base of a meal, not a side dish, so changing the rice changes how everything around it tastes.

You may not know the name, so ask your server how it is being used on the day. Treat it as the dish to judge the restaurant by.

## Moringa, from the village fence to the plate

Suara also names moringa leaves. In Indonesia they are called *daun kelor*.

Moringa trees grow all over rural Java, often along fences and yards. Their small, round leaves usually end up in home cooking such as *sayur bening*, a clear vegetable broth. They are cheap, everyday and almost invisible on restaurant menus. That is exactly why they suit Suara's approach.

The leaves taste green and lightly peppery, a little like spinach with some bitterness. Read the menu with that in mind. Suara works with things Indonesian grandmothers cooked and Jakarta restaurants mostly skipped.

## Cooking over wood

The kitchen cooks on a wood-fired stove. That puts Suara in line with the traditional Javanese kitchen, where a wood stove, the *tungku*, was the standard way to cook long before gas.

Wood changes the result. The heat is uneven and harder to control, and food takes on smoke. Rice cooked over wood can develop a toasted crust at the bottom of the pot, which many Indonesians remember from village kitchens. Grilled and charred elements get a depth that a gas burner cannot give.

The facts do not tell us how the dining room is laid out, so we cannot say whether you will see the stove from your table. If you care, ask when you book.

## Booking a table at a new Jakarta opening

New restaurants in Jakarta change fast in their first months, and Suara opened in April 2026. Hours, booking rules and the menu may still be settling. These habits will save you a wasted trip:

- **Confirm before you go.** Check the current address, opening hours and whether reservations are needed, using the restaurant's own channels.
- **Book ahead for dinner.** At an ingredient-led restaurant the kitchen may prepare a limited amount each night, so a reservation is safer than walking in.
- **Budget for Jakarta traffic.** The evening rush can turn a short distance into a long ride. Most visitors use Grab or Gojek, and leaving early is the usual local habit.
- **Expect additions on the bill.** Sit-down restaurants in Jakarta typically add a service charge and the regional restaurant tax to the listed menu prices. Look at the menu and the bill rather than adding a large tip yourself.
- **Paying is simple.** Cards and QRIS mobile payments are widely accepted at Jakarta restaurants of this kind. Ask if you plan to pay any other way.

When you order, ask what the kitchen is cooking over the fire that day and where the ingredients come from. A restaurant built on unusual produce usually enjoys explaining it, and the answer will make the meal clearer.

## Who Suara suits

Suara suits travellers who already know *nasi goreng* and *sate* and want to see where Indonesian cooking can go next. It also suits anyone curious about regional produce, since the menu starts from ingredients rather than famous dishes.

It is less suited to a fast, cheap lunch between sights. For that, Jakarta's warung and street stalls do the job better. Plan Suara as a sit-down evening meal, and pair it with a street-food morning on another day so you see both ends of the city's food.
