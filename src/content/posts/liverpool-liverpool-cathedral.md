## Red sandstone on a ridge

The building is huge. Giles Gilbert Scott designed it, construction began in 1904 and the cathedral was finished in 1978. It is built of red sandstone and sits on St James' Mount above the city. Its central tower rises over 100 metres and can be seen from across the Mersey.

Inside, the nave opens into a single vast space under the highest Gothic arches in the world. Sound carries a long way in it. The organ is the largest in the UK, and the tower holds the highest and heaviest ring of bells anywhere.

## What to find inside

Give yourself around 90 minutes, or longer if you climb the tower. Look for these:

- **The Lady Chapel**: the first part completed, in 1910. Its scale is intimate, with stained glass and a carved reredos.
- **The Great West Window**: the huge glass wall at the Hope Street end.
- **Tracey Emin's "For You"**: a pink neon message installed under the west window in 2008.
- **The Tower**: lifts and then stairs take you up to open views over the Anglican skyline, the docks and the Welsh hills on a clear day.
- **St James' Gardens**: a former quarry and cemetery below the cathedral's east side, reached by a sloping path.

## Timing your visit around worship

This is a working Church of England cathedral, not a museum. Services go on during opening hours, and parts of the building can be roped off while they happen. Walk around quietly during a service, or join in: choral evensong is open to anyone.

The doors are open 10am to 6pm every day of the week. Weekdays are quiet from opening to close, so any weekday slot works well. Try not to arrive between 12pm and 5pm on a weekend, when crowd levels peak and the tower queue is at its longest. On a Saturday or Sunday, be there at 10am.

Photography is generally fine for personal use. Put your phone away during worship and in the Lady Chapel if prayers are going on. The tower needs a ticket, and its current price is shown at the welcome desk and on the cathedral's official website. Donations toward upkeep are invited at the entrance.

## Getting there on foot

The nearest rail stop is Liverpool Central (Merseyrail), about 15 minutes' walk uphill via Bold Street or Renshaw Street. Liverpool Lime Street, the mainline station, is about 20 minutes away.

Walk the full length of Hope Street, about 10 minutes, and you reach the Metropolitan Cathedral, the city's Catholic cathedral, with its circular concrete crown. Seeing both in one morning shows two opposite ideas of a 20th-century cathedral. Along the way you pass the Philharmonic Hall and the restaurants and pubs of the Georgian Quarter.
