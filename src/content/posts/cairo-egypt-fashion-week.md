## Two days, one garden palace

Picture the runway set among old palace walls and planted grounds. Egypt Fashion Week takes place on November 13 and 14, 2026, and this is its third edition. The setting is The Agricultural Museum in Dokki, one of Cairo's more unusual venues for a fashion crowd.

There is no glass convention hall here and no mall atrium. You walk through gates into a walled compound of gardens and period buildings on the west bank of the Nile. The museum still holds its old farm tools, preserved crops and dioramas of rural Egyptian life.

## The Agricultural Museum, explained

The museum opened in 1938. It sits in the former palace of Princess Fatima Ismail, a daughter of Khedive Ismail, and it ranks among the oldest agricultural museums anywhere. Its halls trace farming in Egypt from Pharaonic times onward. There are displays of ancient seeds, irrigation tools and the cotton that built modern Egyptian fortunes.

That backdrop matters for a fashion event. Cotton and linen run through Egyptian textile history, and the grounds give the shows a sense of place that a hotel ballroom never could. The site is large, so expect to cover real distance on foot between buildings and garden paths.

## Getting to Dokki

Dokki is a residential and office district in Giza, directly across the river from Zamalek and downtown. The museum lies close to the Ministry of Agriculture and the western end of the 6th October Bridge.

Your options:

- **Metro:** Dokki station on Line 2 is the nearest stop. From there it is a short taxi hop or a walk through Dokki's streets.
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber and Careem are the default for most visitors in Cairo. Drivers know the museum by its Arabic name, *Al-Mathaf al-Zirai*.
- **From Zamalek or Tahrir:** both are a quick drive across the Nile, traffic permitting.

Cairo traffic is the real variable. Evening rush hour around the bridges can turn a ten-minute ride into a much longer one, so attendees coming from New Cairo or Sheikh Zayed usually leave a generous margin.

## Tickets and getting through the gate

Entry to Egypt Fashion Week is run by the event organiser, not the museum's own ticket office. The museum's regular admission does not cover the shows. Ticket types, guest lists and access rules come from the organiser's own channels.

Fashion weeks generally mix invited guests, press and industry with any public tickets. Guests who arrive with their pass or confirmation ready on their phone tend to clear the gate fastest. Prices are not given here. The organiser is the only source for them.

## Dressing for a Cairo front row

Egyptian event crowds dress up, and a fashion week crowd dresses up even more. People typically treat it as a chance to be photographed. Statement pieces and local designers' work turn up everywhere between shows.

A few habits that help on the day:

- **Footwear:** the grounds involve garden paths and uneven surfaces between buildings, so many guests carry flats for the walk.
- **Layers:** mid-November days in Cairo are usually warm and dry, but evenings cool noticeably, especially outdoors.
- **Modesty off-site:** Dokki is a working neighbourhood. Guests often bring a cover-up for the street or the metro.
- **Cash:** cards work at larger venues, but small notes are handy for kiosks, parking attendants and tips. Tipping (*baksheesh*) is part of daily life here.

Photography inside a runway space follows the organiser's rules, not the museum's. Phones held high block the rows behind, and seasoned guests keep them low during a walk.

## Around Dokki and across the river

The museum makes a good anchor for a longer stay on this side of the Nile. The Mahmoud Khalil Museum, with its French Impressionist paintings, is a short drive south along the river in Giza.

Cross north into Zamalek and you find the Cairo Opera House grounds and the Cairo Tower on Gezira Island. Zamalek's tree-lined streets have galleries, boutiques and cafés where many visitors finish an evening. Downtown and Tahrir Square, with the Egyptian Museum, are a single bridge away.

Visitors in town for the shows often base themselves in Zamalek or Garden City. Both are close to Dokki and to central Cairo's sights.
