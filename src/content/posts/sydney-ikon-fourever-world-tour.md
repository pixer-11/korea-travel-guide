## A Friday night in Moore Park

Six members, one heritage hall, one Sydney night. The iKON Fourever World Tour was set for the Hordern Pavilion on Friday, November 13, 2026. The tour's Melbourne date was the Sunday, two days later.

K-pop nights at the Hordern usually come with their own queue rituals, from photocard trading to lightstick checks against the pavilion's pale wall. The Hordern is a 1920s exhibition hall, so the space inside feels like a broad, high-roofed shed, not a modern arena bowl.

## Who iKON are now

The group's line-up has changed a lot since the start. iKON debuted under YG Entertainment in 2015 and came out of the survival shows WIN and Mix & Match. Their 2018 single "Love Scenario" became one of the biggest K-pop songs of that year in Korea, and kids and office workers alike sang it.

B.I left in 2019. The current six are Jay, Song, Bobby, DK, Ju-ne and Chan, who left YG and signed with 143 Entertainment in 2023. A world tour this far into their career reads as a reunion with longtime fans, many of whom have followed them since the "Rhythm Ta" and "My Type" years. iKON fans call themselves iKONIC.

## The Hordern Pavilion itself

The building is older than you'd expect. The Hordern was built in 1924 for the Royal Agricultural Society, back when the Sydney Royal Easter Show was held on these Moore Park grounds. It's heritage-listed, and from the outside it still looks like a show-ground hall: long, symmetrical and pale-rendered.

Inside, it's one of Sydney's mid-sized concert rooms, with a capacity in the thousands rather than the tens of thousands. That size is why K-pop and touring indie acts like it. Even from the back of the floor, you're close enough to see faces without staring at the screens all night.

## Getting to Driver Avenue

Most people come by light rail. The L2 Randwick and L3 Kingsford lines both stop at Moore Park on Anzac Parade. From there, a short walk through the park leads to the Entertainment Quarter and the Hordern.

Other ways in:
- **On foot from Central:** about 25–30 minutes via Foveaux Street through Surry Hills, or along the Moore Park paths.
- **Bus:** Anzac Parade routes run from Central and the city past Moore Park.
- **Car:** the Entertainment Quarter has parking, but it fills up on nights when the nearby stadiums host games too.

The Sydney Cricket Ground and Allianz Stadium sit right next door. When a match and a concert share a night, the light rail and Anzac Parade get busy in both directions.

## Lightsticks, chants and the Konbat glow

K-pop crowds come with their own customs. iKON's official lightstick, nicknamed the Konbat, is a hammer-shaped wand. A hall full of them turns the Hordern's dark floor into one sheet of colour. Fans who own one tend to put in fresh batteries and pair it with the app before leaving home, because there's no signal to spare once inside.

Fan chants are the other ritual. On the big singles, iKONIC shout members' names in set orders between lines. Many first-timers learn them from fan-made chant guides online in the weeks before a show. Joining in, even badly, is half the experience.

Banner projects, where fans hold up printed slogans during a chosen song, are often organised by Australian fan accounts. Paper slips sometimes get handed out in the queue.

There's plenty within a short walk. The Entertainment Quarter right beside the venue has casual restaurants and bars, and it's the obvious place to eat before a show. Crown Street in Surry Hills is a short walk west and has a much wider choice, including several Korean and Asian places.
