## Five arenas, one December run

Morrissey's December 2026 tour is an arena tour. It visits five UK cities: Brighton, Cardiff, Liverpool, Glasgow and Leeds.

For Leeds, it means a seated-and-standing hall rather than a theatre. The rooms are big, and so is the singer's back catalogue. You get a former Smiths frontman with nearly four decades of solo records behind him, playing to fans who have often followed him since the 1980s.

Leeds is the only Yorkshire stop on the list. Fans from Sheffield, York, Hull and the Pennine towns usually treat it as their home show. Manchester sits an hour away by train, so plenty of Lancashire fans make the trip too.

## The Leeds date and where tickets live

The tour's official ticket listings carry the exact Leeds date, the venue name and the seating plan. They are the reference point for anything that changes, including timings and entry rules.

Leeds has one purpose-built arena: the First Direct Arena on Claypit Lane, at the northern edge of the city centre. Its floor is a flat standing pit. Above it, tiered seating curves around the room in a horseshoe, so every seat faces the stage.

Prices aren't covered here. They appear on the ticket listing itself.

## Reaching the arena side of the city

Leeds is a walkable city, and most people attending a city-centre show get there on foot.

- **From Leeds railway station:** head north through the shopping streets towards the Merrion Centre. The walk takes 15–20 minutes and goes uphill for the last stretch.
- **By bus:** many city routes stop around the Merrion Centre and the Headrow, a few minutes from the arena.
- **By car:** multi-storey car parks around the Merrion Centre and the universities are the usual choice. The streets around the arena clog up at the end of the night.

In December you walk back after dark through a cold city centre. Fans typically bring a coat they're happy to carry, or they use the cloakroom.

## What a Morrissey night sounds like

His setlists change from tour to tour and sometimes from night to night. Nobody can promise you a particular song.

Over the years his solo sets have tended to lean on songs like "Everyday Is Like Sunday", "Suedehead", "First of the Gang to Die" and "Irish Blood, English Heart". Smiths songs turn up in some years and not in others. That uncertainty is part of the ritual, and fans compare setlists online after every show.

Expect a band in matching outfits, a stage backdrop with projected images, and very little chat between songs. When he does talk, it can be pointed. He's an unpredictable performer, and long-time fans know it.

## How the floor behaves at a Morrissey show

The floor works differently from a typical arena gig.

The hardcore fans gather at the barrier and stay there all night. Flowers have been part of his shows since the Smiths years, and you'll often see gladioli waved or thrown towards the stage. Fans also reach for his hand along the front rail. Sometimes one climbs up for a hug before security leads them off.

Some practical points:

- **Standing tickets:** a spot near the front usually means queuing outside in the December cold before doors. Fans who'd rather keep warm hang back by the sound desk, where the mix is often clearest anyway.
- **Seated tickets:** the side tiers of a horseshoe arena sit closer to the stage than they look on a seating plan.
- **Food:** Morrissey is a long-time vegetarian. In the past he has asked venues to stop selling meat during his shows, so don't count on a burger inside.
- **Payment:** UK arenas generally take cards and contactless at the bars and merchandise stands.

## Before and after in December Leeds

The streets around the arena have plenty of food and drink, so most people make an evening of it.

- **Merrion Street and the Northern Quarter strip** have bars and late-opening pubs within a few minutes' walk.
- **Millennium Square**, just south of the arena by the Civic Hall, usually hosts seasonal events in December.
- **Kirkgate Market and Briggate**, nearer the station, suit an afternoon of browsing and food before you head north.

Fans coming from out of town usually stay near the station. It keeps the morning train simple, and the arena walk takes only 15–20 minutes. Fans heading back to Manchester, York or Sheffield the same night tend to leave during the encore to make the last trains.
