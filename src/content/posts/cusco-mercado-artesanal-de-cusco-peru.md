## Down the avenue, away from the square

Most Cusco shopping happens within sight of the cathedral. This market sits well past that, at Av. El Sol 1226. That's the long, straight end of the avenue that runs from the historic centre towards Wanchaq.

Few visitors walk this far, so it sees fewer people than the stalls around the Plaza de Armas or San Pedro Market. That's the reason to come: you can handle the goods, ask questions and compare stalls without a crowd pushing past behind you.

## What you'll be browsing

It's a crafts market, so expect rows of vendor stalls rather than one shop. The stock is typical of Cusco:

- Alpaca and wool sweaters, scarves, ponchos and *chullos* (Andean knit hats)
- Woven textiles, blankets and table runners
- Ceramics and small carved pieces
- Jewellery, bags and souvenirs with Inca motifs

Stock and quality vary from stall to stall, so treat the first one you visit as a reference point, not the place to buy.

## Bargaining at the stalls

Haggling is expected in Cusco's craft markets, and polite back-and-forth is part of buying. Pay in Peruvian soles. Bring small notes, because change for large bills can be hard to find at a single stall.

- **Ask the material directly.** *"¿Es alpaca o acrílico?"* gets you a straight answer more often than a vague "is this good?". "Baby alpaca" and blends are priced very differently.
- **Ask the price before you show interest.** Vendors here post few prices, so the opening number is where talks begin.
- **Buy several items at one stall.** A bundle is the easiest way to get a better deal.
- **Be friendly.** A smile and a *"gracias"* when you walk away is normal. Vendors don't take a "no" badly.
- **Photos:** ask before photographing a vendor or their work.

## Fitting it into your Cusco day

At the moment the market opens 8am–8pm daily, so you can visit before or after your other sightseeing. The easiest pairing is Qorikancha, the Inca sun temple under the Santo Domingo convent. It stands further up Avenida El Sol, so you pass it on the walk down.

Allow 45 minutes to an hour and a half, depending on how seriously you shop. Remember the city sits at roughly 3,400 metres. The walk down is easy, but the climb back to the centre with full bags is slow work. If you're still adjusting to the altitude, take a taxi back.

The market's location suits a visit just before a day trip too. The lower end of El Sol is on the way out towards the airport side of town, so a stop here costs little time.
