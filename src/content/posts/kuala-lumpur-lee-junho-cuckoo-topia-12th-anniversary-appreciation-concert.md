## An anniversary night under the KL sky

This is a thank-you show, and it is outdoors. On Saturday, October 24, 2026, 2PM member Lee Junho brings his "CUCKOO TOPIA" 12th anniversary appreciation concert to Stadium Merdeka in Kuala Lumpur.

An appreciation concert is built for the fans who have stayed. Most people in the seats know him from both sides of his career: the 2PM stages and the acting run that includes *The Red Sleeve* and *King the Land*. Expect a crowd that knows the words.

## Stadium Merdeka, the setting

Few concert venues carry this much weight. Stadium Merdeka is where Tunku Abdul Rahman declared Malaysia's independence on 31 August 1957. The open-air bowl of terraced seating still holds that history, and its name simply means "independence".

Look up from almost any seat and Merdeka 118 fills the sky beside you. The tower is the tallest building in Malaysia and the second tallest in the world. At night its lit spire becomes part of the backdrop. Next door stands Stadium Negara, the country's first indoor arena.

## Tickets and where you sit

Fans generally treat the promoter's official channels as the one reliable source for ticket links, seat maps and entry rules. Resale offers on social media are where most concert scams in the region start. Experienced fans avoid paying anyone outside the official seller.

Because the stage sits inside an open stadium, sightlines vary more than they do in an arena. Floor sections put you close, but you will be standing among the crowd. The raked terraces give a cleaner view of the whole production, and the tower too.

## Getting to the stadium

The train is the easiest way in. Stadium Merdeka sits on the southern edge of the old city, between Chinatown and Jalan Hang Tuah. Your main options:

- **Merdeka MRT station (Kajang Line):** the closest stop, built alongside the Merdeka 118 development.
- **Maharajalela Monorail station:** a short walk from the stadium's southern side.
- **Pasar Seni (LRT and MRT):** a longer walk through Chinatown, handy if you are staying near Petaling Street.

Rail fares are paid with a Touch 'n Go card or contactless bank card at the gates. Grab is the default ride-hailing app in KL. When a stadium crowd leaves all at once, surge pricing and long pickup waits are normal. Many fans walk a few streets away before ordering a car.

## Concert-night habits at Merdeka

October falls in Kuala Lumpur's inter-monsoon season. That usually means heavy late-afternoon or evening thunderstorms, and Merdeka has no roof. Seasoned concertgoers pack a light poncho, because an umbrella blocks the view of everyone behind it.

Fans typically arrive hours before the show to queue for merchandise and trade freebies. Fan-made photocards, stickers and slogan banners are a big part of K-pop concert culture here. Many people bring small stacks to hand out. Official lightsticks are common, and fans often sync chants with the crowd.

Water and a fully charged phone matter more than snacks. The evening heat stays humid long after sunset. The rail stations near the stadium get very crowded after the encore, so plenty of people linger in the stadium grounds for a while first.

## Before and after: the old city on foot

The stadium sits on the edge of KL's oldest districts, so the night extends easily. Petaling Street, the covered market lane of Chinatown, is a ten-minute walk away. Its hawker stalls run late, which makes it the obvious post-show supper stop.

Nearby, within walking distance:

- **Kwai Chai Hong:** a restored back alley of murals off Jalan Panggong.
- **Sri Mahamariamman Temple:** the city's oldest Hindu temple, with a towering gopuram on Jalan Tun H.S. Lee.
- **Central Market (Pasar Seni):** a 1930s Art Deco hall of craft stalls.
- **Merdeka Square:** the colonial-era green framed by the Sultan Abdul Samad Building, one MRT stop or a longer walk north.

Visitors usually stay in Chinatown or Bukit Bintang. Chinatown is close enough to walk back from the show, while Bukit Bintang is two Kajang Line stops from Merdeka station.
