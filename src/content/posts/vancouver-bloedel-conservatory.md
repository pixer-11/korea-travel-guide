## Under the bubble dome

The roof is the first thing to look at. Bloedel's dome is a triodetic frame of aluminium tubing set with clear acrylic bubbles. It opened in 1969 on the summit of Little Mountain, the highest ground in Vancouver, and the outline is easy to spot from the city below.

Inside, one looping path takes you through three climate zones:

- **Tropical rainforest:** dense, humid planting under the tallest part of the dome
- **Subtropical:** flowering shrubs and palms
- **Desert:** cacti and succulents in dry beds

More than 200 birds fly loose overhead, from macaws and parrots down to small finches. More than 500 plant species fill the beds.

## Reaching the summit

The address is 4600 Cambie St. The closest SkyTrain stop is King Edward Station on the Canada Line. From there it's an uphill walk into Queen Elizabeth Park of about 15 minutes. The number 15 bus runs along Cambie Street and saves you some of the climb.

Drivers can use the pay parking loops near the summit plaza. On weekend afternoons they fill quickly.

At the moment the conservatory opens 10am–4pm every day. Hours change through the year and around holidays, so check the Vancouver Park Board site before you go. Ticket prices are posted there and at the entrance.

## Timing around the crowds

The building is small and very popular. When it's full, the path slows to a shuffle. On weekdays the quietest stretch is usually 10am–1pm. Try not to arrive between 12pm and 4pm on a weekend, which is the busiest window.

On a winter weekday, arriving at the 10am opening means warm, humid air and room to stand under the macaws. Most people spend 30–60 minutes inside, so a 3pm entry is fine but leaves no slack before closing.

## Sharing the room with the birds

The birds run this room, and a few habits make the visit easier for everyone:

- **Hands off.** Don't touch or feed the birds, and keep food packed away.
- **Look up, then down.** Birds perch on railings and sometimes land on the path.
- **Dress in layers.** The air inside is warm and humid even in January. Glasses and camera lenses fog up for the first minute.
- **Flash off.** Many of the birds are used to people, but flash and sudden reaching still startle them.
- **Strollers fit,** but the path is narrow. On busy days, a carrier is easier.

Pair the visit with the rest of the summit. Right outside are the plaza fountains and Henry Moore's bronze *Knife Edge Two Piece*. A short walk downhill brings you to the sunken Quarry Garden, built in an old basalt quarry. On clear days the viewpoints look north over downtown to the North Shore mountains.

VanDusen Botanical Garden is a short bus ride west, at Oak Street and 37th Avenue. Together they make a full day of gardens.
