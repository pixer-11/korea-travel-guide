## West 4th, since 1968

The Naam has been serving vegetarian food on West 4th Avenue since 1968. That was Kitsilano's hippie era, when this strip was full of communes and health-food shops, and the restaurant is one of the clearest survivors of those years. These days the street is lined with yoga-wear shops and espresso bars. The Naam stays loyal to its brown rice and tahini roots.

Its front sits at 2724 W 4th Ave, between Macdonald and Stephens. The menu is all vegetarian, and much of it works for vegans too. It's casual, mid-range on price and easy on a mixed group: no one has to argue about the meat-eaters.

## Bowls, fries and gravy

It's a big menu built on grains, beans, tofu and vegetables. The cooking is generous and homestyle rather than plated and precious. A few dishes have followed The Naam for decades and are what most people come for:

- **Sesame fries with miso gravy**, the order most tables share
- **Dragon bowls**: rice or noodles with tofu, vegetables and a choice of sauce
- **Burritos, enchiladas and stir-fries** for something heartier
- **Weekend breakfast** of scrambles and pancakes, served only on Saturday and Sunday when the doors open at 9am

Menus get revised over time, so check the current one when you sit down. Portions are large, and two people can easily split a starter plus fries.

## Art on the walls, music after dark

The editorial line on The Naam is simple. It's a casual place with local art, a garden patio and live music every night. Inside, there are wooden tables and walls hung with work by local artists, so it feels more like a neighbourhood living room than a dining room.

Out back, the garden patio is where people head in the warmer months. Then there's the nightly music. Plan on a solo guitarist or a small acoustic act more than a full band. Dinner turns into a slow evening if you let it.

## Beating the West 4th queue

The weekend queue is the real cost of eating here. BestTime's forecast of typical foot traffic puts the quietest weekday window at 11am–12pm, which is just after opening. If you're going on a weekend, come at 9am–10am, the first hour of breakfast service.

Avoid weekends from 12pm–1pm, and especially the long 2pm–10pm stretch. That's when the busy periods usually stack up and a wait at the door is likely. At the moment The Naam closes at 11pm every day. A late weekday dinner around 9pm still gets you the music without the peak crowd.

## Getting seated and settling up

The Naam is relaxed, but a few habits help:

- **Ask at the door** whether there's a wait. Seating is first-come at busy times, so check how they handle bookings before you show up with a large group.
- **Name your preference** for the patio or indoors when you arrive. The garden fills first on warm evenings.
- **Pay by card or tap**, which is how most Vancouver diners pay. Your server brings the card machine to the table.
- **Tip 15–20%** of the bill. That's the norm in Canadian sit-down restaurants, and the card machine will prompt you.
- **Share plates.** The fries are meant for the middle of the table.

If you're there for the music, sit closer to the performers and keep conversation low during sets. The regulars notice.

## Getting there and staying in Kits

The Naam sits right on West 4th, Kitsilano's main shopping street. TransLink's #4 and #7 buses run along West 4th from downtown and stop within a block. The ride from downtown takes about 20 minutes. Street parking on the side streets is metered and fills up on weekends, so the bus is usually simpler.

Make a half-day of it. Kitsilano Beach and Kits Pool are roughly a 15–20 minute walk northeast, down toward English Bay. Head west along 4th instead and you reach Jericho Beach in about 25 minutes on foot. Weekend breakfast at 9am followed by a beach walk is a good way to spend a Kits Saturday.
