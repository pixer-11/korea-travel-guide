## October 17, Senayan

The date is October 17, 2026. YG Entertainment's seven-member girl group, BABYMONSTER, brings its show to Indonesia Arena in Jakarta. The members are Ruka, Pharita, Asa, Ahyeon, Rami, Rora and Chiquita.

Treat the announced date and venue as your fixed points and plan everything else around them. Confirm the start time, ticket tiers and entry rules on the official promoter's site and BABYMONSTER's official channels. Those are the only sources that count.

## Inside Indonesia Arena

This is Jakarta's newest big indoor venue. Indonesia Arena opened in 2023 to host FIBA Basketball World Cup games. It now handles the city's large indoor concerts and holds around 16,000 people across a flat floor and steep tiers of seats.

The steep tiers are good news if you're in the upper bowl. Sightlines drop straight down to the stage, without the long, shallow view you get in an old stadium. The arena is fully air-conditioned too. In Jakarta that matters, because the queue outside will leave you sweating.

The arena is part of the Gelora Bung Karno complex, usually just called GBK. That is the same sports park as GBK Main Stadium and the older Istora Senayan hall. On a concert night, thousands of fans in lightstick lanyards and group merch walk across one huge campus of plazas and footpaths, all heading to the same doors.

## Getting to GBK without a car

Plan on the train. Jalan Sudirman, the main road past Senayan, is one of Jakarta's most congested roads at the best of times. A sold-out arena show makes it worse. On parts of Sudirman, private cars also fall under the odd-even plate rule (*ganjil-genap*) during set hours.

Your rail and bus options:

- **MRT Jakarta, North–South line:** get off at **Istora Mandiri** station. It's the closest stop to the GBK complex, and from there you walk across the grounds. **Senayan** station, one stop south, also works.
- **TransJakarta Corridor 1:** the bus runs along Sudirman and stops at Gelora Bung Karno.
- **KRL Commuter Line:** **Palmerah** station is on the western edge of GBK. It's handy if you're coming from Tangerang or the south-west suburbs.

Ride-hailing apps work, especially Gojek and Grab. The motorbike option (*GoRide* or *GrabBike*) is the quickest way through stopped traffic. Arrange your pickup point outside the immediate arena area, though. Drivers can get boxed in by the crowd and road closures.

## Tickets, ID and the wristband queue

Buy only through the official ticketing partner named by the promoter. At big K-pop shows in Indonesia, scalper listings on social media and marketplace apps are a familiar problem. Fake e-tickets get turned away at the gate.

Many large concerts in Jakarta use a redemption step. You swap your e-ticket for a physical wristband or pass at a separate booth, sometimes on a different day. Check whether this show does the same before you go. Ticket names are often matched to ID:

- **Indonesian residents:** usually a KTP (national ID card).
- **International visitors:** bring your passport, not a photocopy, unless the official rules say a copy is fine.
- **Transfers:** if a friend bought your ticket, read the official transfer policy carefully. A name that doesn't match is the most common reason fans get stuck at redemption.

The official rules also list banned items. Read them before you pack, because bag checks are standard at GBK venues. Check what kind of power bank, camera and banner are allowed. Your lightstick should be the official MONSTIEZ one, and fully charged.

## Senayan after the encore

Leaving takes planning, just like arriving. When the lights come up, most of the arena heads for the same footpaths at once, and then to the same MRT entrance. The platform at Istora Mandiri fills fast and staff may hold people back in waves.

Seasoned Jakarta concertgoers handle this a few ways:

- **Walk to Senayan station instead.** It's a short stretch along Sudirman, and the crowd thins out as you go.
- **Wait it out.** The shopping malls next to the complex, Senayan City and Plaza Senayan, plus FX Sudirman a little further along, are good places to eat and let the crowd clear.
- **Book your ride-hailing pickup from a mall drop-off point,** not the GBK gates.

Before you go in, check the MRT's last trains on the official MRT Jakarta channels. If the show runs late, you want a backup plan already decided.

## Fan-project etiquette and paying your way

Indonesian K-pop fandoms are known for organised fan projects. Volunteers hand out printed slogans or banners before the show and ask the whole venue to hold them up at a set moment. If someone gives you one, keep it and follow the cue from the people around you.

Fans often trade freebies outside the venue too: photocards, stickers and small gifts. This is friendly and casual. Ask before you take a photo of someone's display or outfit.

For money, have **QRIS** ready. It's Indonesia's national QR payment standard, and most vendors and food stalls around Senayan accept it. Many international visitors can't link a foreign card to Indonesian e-wallets. Carry some rupiah in small notes for street vendors, and check the arena's own payment rules for official merch.

## Planning the day around the show

Mid-October in Jakarta is the start of the rainy season. Humid days and heavy afternoon downpours are common. Pack a compact poncho, because umbrellas are a nuisance in a dense queue and are often banned inside. Keep your ticket and ID in a waterproof pouch.

Official merch lines at Jakarta K-pop shows tend to form early. If merch is a priority, plan to get to GBK well before the official start time. Check the promoter's channels for the booth location and any pre-order system.

For a place to stay, look at hotels along the Sudirman–Senayan corridor or near any station on the North–South MRT line. Then it's one train ride home instead of a cross-city taxi. Hotels within walking distance of GBK fill quickly on big event weekends, so book early.
