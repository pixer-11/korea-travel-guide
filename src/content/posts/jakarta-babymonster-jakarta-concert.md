## October 17, Senayan

The concert was set for October 17, 2026, at Indonesia Arena in Jakarta, with YG Entertainment's seven-member girl group, BABYMONSTER, as the act. The members are Ruka, Pharita, Asa, Ahyeon, Rami, Rora and Chiquita.

The announcement centred on two fixed points: the date and the venue. The promoter handled the start time, ticket tiers and entry rules.

## Inside Indonesia Arena

This is Jakarta's newest big indoor venue. Indonesia Arena opened in 2023 to host FIBA Basketball World Cup games. It now handles the city's large indoor concerts and holds around 16,000 people across a flat floor and steep tiers of seats.

The steep tiers are good news for the upper bowl. Sightlines drop straight down to the stage, without the long, shallow view of an old stadium. The arena is fully air-conditioned too. In Jakarta that matters, because the queues outside are hot and humid.

The arena is part of the Gelora Bung Karno complex, usually just called GBK. That is the same sports park as GBK Main Stadium and the older Istora Senayan hall. On concert nights, thousands of fans in lightstick lanyards and group merch walk across one huge campus of plazas and footpaths, all heading to the same doors.

## Getting to GBK without a car

The train is the practical choice. Jalan Sudirman, the main road past Senayan, is one of Jakarta's most congested roads at the best of times. A full arena show makes it worse. On parts of Sudirman, private cars also fall under the odd-even plate rule (*ganjil-genap*) during set hours.

The rail and bus options:

- **MRT Jakarta, North–South line:** the stop is **Istora Mandiri** station. It's the closest stop to the GBK complex, and from there it's a walk across the grounds. **Senayan** station, one stop south, also works.
- **TransJakarta Corridor 1:** the bus runs along Sudirman and stops at Gelora Bung Karno.
- **KRL Commuter Line:** **Palmerah** station is on the western edge of GBK. It's handy for anyone coming from Tangerang or the south-west suburbs.

Ride-hailing apps work, especially Gojek and Grab. The motorbike option (*GoRide* or *GrabBike*) is the quickest way through stopped traffic. Pickup points outside the immediate arena area work better, because drivers can get boxed in by the crowd and road closures.

## Tickets, ID and the wristband queue

Tickets were sold through the official ticketing partner named by the promoter. At big K-pop shows in Indonesia, scalper listings on social media and marketplace apps are a familiar problem. Fake e-tickets get turned away at the gate.

Many large concerts in Jakarta use a redemption step. Fans swap their e-ticket for a physical wristband or pass at a separate booth, sometimes on a different day. Ticket names are often matched to ID:

- **Indonesian residents:** usually a KTP (national ID card).
- **International visitors:** an original passport, not a photocopy, unless the rules allow a copy.
- **Transfers:** tickets bought by a friend fall under the transfer policy. A name that doesn't match is the most common reason fans get stuck at redemption.

Concert rules at GBK venues also list banned items, and bag checks are standard. Power banks, cameras and banners are the items most often restricted. The official lightstick for this show was the MONSTIEZ one, and fans bring it fully charged.

## Senayan after the encore

Leaving is as crowded as arriving. When the lights come up, most of the arena heads for the same footpaths at once, and then to the same MRT entrance. The platform at Istora Mandiri fills fast and staff may hold people back in waves.

Seasoned Jakarta concertgoers handle this a few ways:

- **Walking to Senayan station instead.** It's a short stretch along Sudirman, and the crowd thins out along the way.
- **Waiting it out.** The shopping malls next to the complex, Senayan City and Plaza Senayan, plus FX Sudirman a little further along, are good places to eat and let the crowd clear.
- **Booking ride-hailing pickups from a mall drop-off point,** not the GBK gates.

The MRT's last trains are the firm limit on a late night. Late finishes and last trains are a common clash at GBK, so regulars keep a backup route in mind.

## Fan-project etiquette and paying your way

Indonesian K-pop fandoms are known for organised fan projects. Volunteers hand out printed slogans or banners before the show and ask the whole venue to hold them up at a set moment. People who receive one keep it and follow the cue from those around them.

Fans often trade freebies outside the venue too: photocards, stickers and small gifts. This is friendly and casual. Asking before photographing someone's display or outfit is the norm.

For money, **QRIS** is the main option. It's Indonesia's national QR payment standard, and most vendors and food stalls around Senayan accept it. Many international visitors can't link a foreign card to Indonesian e-wallets. Small rupiah notes cover street vendors, and official merch booths set their own payment rules.

## Planning the day around the show

Mid-October in Jakarta is the start of the rainy season. Humid days and heavy afternoon downpours are common. Compact ponchos suit dense queues better than umbrellas, which are a nuisance in a crowd and often banned inside. A waterproof pouch keeps tickets and ID dry.

Official merch lines at Jakarta K-pop shows tend to form early, often well before the official start time. Booth locations and pre-order systems vary from show to show.

Hotels along the Sudirman–Senayan corridor or near any station on the North–South MRT line put the arena one train ride away instead of a cross-city taxi. Hotels within walking distance of GBK fill quickly on big event weekends.

Senayan's malls, MRT stations and footpaths make GBK one of the easier big venues in Jakarta to reach without a car.
