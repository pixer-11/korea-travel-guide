Four Sydney-raised musicians who started out posting covers online now headline the arena where Australia crowns its tennis champions. On Monday 2 November 2026, 5 Seconds of Summer play Rod Laver Arena as part of their Everyone's a Star! World Tour. Here is what you need to know about the night, the room and the city around it.

## A homecoming in a tennis bowl

This is a hometown-country show, and that matters. 5 Seconds of Summer formed in western Sydney in 2011. Luke Hemmings, Michael Clifford, Calum Hood and Ashton Irwin made their name on YouTube before touring stadiums as One Direction's support act.

Since then the band have had singles like "She Looks So Perfect" and "Youngblood", along with a run of number-one albums. An Australian leg of a world tour carries a different energy from the overseas dates. Plenty of people in the crowd have followed the band since those early bedroom-cover days.

The band's official site and the venue's event page carry the tour dates and ticket links. Set times and support acts are not part of the facts we have, so we leave them out here rather than guess.

## Inside Rod Laver Arena

Rod Laver Arena opened in 1988. It is named after the Australian who won two calendar-year Grand Slams, and every January it hosts the Australian Open's centre court matches. The building has a retractable roof. For concerts it holds roughly 15,000 people.

On a show night the tennis court is gone. In its place you get a standing or seated floor in front of the stage and steep tiers of seats rising on every side. The bowl is fairly compact for its size, so even the upper levels feel closer to the stage than a stadium seat would.

- **Floor:** closest to the band. You will be on your feet most of the night if it is general admission.
- **Lower tiers:** a raised view over the floor with an actual seat behind you.
- **Upper tiers:** the full sweep of the lighting rig and the crowd, and cheaper by category.

## The Cup Eve factor

The calendar gives this date a quirk. In 2026 the first Tuesday in November falls on 3 November, which makes it Melbourne Cup Day. The concert therefore lands on the eve of the city's biggest racing day, and metropolitan Melbourne has a public holiday the next morning.

That cuts both ways. Local fans have no alarm clock waiting for them, so a Monday-night show feels more like a Saturday. But the Spring Racing Carnival also fills hotels across the city that week. Interstate fans often split their stay between the CBD, Southbank and Richmond.

Cup Day itself is focused on Flemington Racecourse, in the northwest of the city. It is a long way from Melbourne Park, so the two crowds rarely meet. Public transport runs a holiday timetable on the Tuesday.

## Getting to Melbourne Park

The arena sits between the Yarra River and the rail lines, east of the city grid. Melbourne's public transport runs on the myki system, and you tap on and off at the readers.

- **Tram:** Route 70 runs along Flinders Street and Batman Avenue. It stops directly outside the arena at the Rod Laver Arena stop.
- **Train:** Richmond Station is a short walk across the precinct. It is served by most suburban lines in the south and east. Jolimont Station, on the Mernda and Hurstbridge lines, is about the same distance on the northern side.
- **On foot:** from Flinders Street Station, the riverside path past Birrarung Marr takes about 15–20 minutes. It is flat the whole way.

Driving is the slow option. The precinct's parking is limited and shares the roads with AAMI Park and the MCG, which sit right next door.

## Getting in and out of Melbourne Park

Arena shows here follow a familiar rhythm. Fans going for the general admission floor typically gather outside the doors well before they open. Seated ticket holders tend to drift in later and spend that time at the bars around Swan Street in Richmond.

- **Tickets:** they are almost always digital at Rod Laver Arena. People usually have theirs loaded on their phone before they reach the gates, because the signal drops in a crowd of 15,000.
- **Bags:** the Melbourne & Olympic Parks website sets out the venue's conditions of entry. Small bags are the norm, and larger bags slow down the security line.
- **Payment:** card and phone payments are standard at food and merchandise counters. Merch lines are usually shortest during the support set.
- **Tipping:** it isn't expected at the bars or food counters, which is normal for Australia.

The exit is where people lose time. When the lights come up, most of the crowd heads for the Route 70 stop at once. Many locals walk the ten minutes to Richmond Station instead, where trains run more often and take the crowd away faster.

## Before and after the show

The streets around Melbourne Park give you plenty of choice. Swan Street in Richmond has pubs and casual dining a short walk from the arena. It is the most common spot for a pre-show meal. Further along, Bridge Road and the Vietnamese restaurants of Victoria Street are a short tram or taxi ride away.

If you head back toward the city, Flinders Lane and the CBD laneways keep plenty of bars open late. Federation Square and Hosier Lane's street art sit on the walk back from the river. They make an easy stop for out-of-town fans with an hour to spare before the show.

## Making a trip of it

Interstate fans often turn the concert into a long weekend, and the Cup Day holiday makes that easy. The Royal Botanic Gardens are just across the river from Melbourne Park. The National Gallery of Victoria is on St Kilda Road, a short walk on from there. Early November usually brings mild spring weather, though Melbourne's weather can swing within a single afternoon, so most people pack a layer for the walk home.
