## Robusta on Ann Street

Most specialty cafés treat robusta as the bean to apologise for. Black Sheep Coffee built a whole brand on the opposite view. The London-founded chain has spent years making the case for good-quality robusta, and its "leave the herd behind" sheep logo is the joke behind it.

The Belfast branch is at 56-58 Ann St, in the BT1 shopping grid south of High Street. It's consistently well rated, budget-friendly and still fairly under the radar. Plenty of people pass it on the way to Victoria Square without registering it as anything more than another coffee sign.

## A street threaded with Entries

Ann Street is one end of Belfast's oldest walking shortcuts. The Entries are narrow alleys running north from Ann Street to High Street. They date from the town's seventeenth- and eighteenth-century layout, when these lanes held taverns, printers and workshops.

Pottinger's Entry is the one to find. It's a covered passage with the Morning Star pub partway along it, and it brings you out on High Street within a minute or two. Joy's Entry runs parallel to it. Take your cup through one of them and you get a slice of pre-Victorian Belfast before the city centre's chain-store frontages start again.

## The 7am weekend window

The weekend opening hour is this branch's real advantage. Doors open at 7am on Saturday and Sunday, and the quietest stretch runs **7am to 9am**. That's when you get a free seat and a barista with time to talk you through the beans.

From **11am to 6pm** on weekends the picture changes. Shoppers heading between Cornmarket, Castle Court and Victoria Square treat Ann Street as a through route, and the counter fills up for most of the afternoon. If you need a table to work or plan your day, don't arrive in that window.

On weekdays the café opens even earlier, at **6:45am**. That makes it one of the more useful early stops in the centre if you're catching a morning train or starting a walking tour.

## What to get at the counter

The menu follows the chain's standard lineup, so you can expect the familiar espresso range alongside things built for robusta's heavier body. Good first orders:

- **A flat white or latte.** Robusta's body and lower acidity hold up well under milk.
- **A straight espresso.** This is the honest test if you've never tried robusta on purpose.
- **Iced coffee.** It's a staple of the chain's menu, and robusta comes through clearly when it's chilled.
- **A waffle or pastry.** Black Sheep is known for sweet waffles alongside its baked goods; check what's on the board at this branch.

A heads-up on caffeine. Robusta usually carries noticeably more caffeine than arabica. If you're sensitive to it, a single shot will do more than you're used to.

## Ordering at the Ann Street counter

It's counter service: order, pay, then wait to be called. Contactless card and phone payments are the norm in Belfast cafés, and you won't usually be expected to carry cash.

Tipping isn't expected for counter service in the UK. If there's a jar, dropping your change in is a kind gesture and nobody will notice if you don't.

Black Sheep runs its own app for ordering ahead and loyalty points. That's worth setting up if you're in Belfast for several days and will pass through the centre more than once.

When you order, tell the barista "to stay" or "to take away". Then go and wait by the collection point, out of the queue. On a busy Saturday afternoon, standing about in front of the till is the quickest way to annoy everyone behind you.

## Getting to 56-58 Ann St

The café is in the compact core of the city, so you can walk to it from almost anywhere central.

- **From City Hall:** head north up Donegall Place, then turn right at Castle Place and Cornmarket onto Ann Street. It's around ten minutes on foot.
- **From Lanyon Place station:** walk north-west past the Waterfront Hall towards Queen's Square, then cut along to Ann Street. Allow ten to fifteen minutes.
- **From Grand Central Station:** it's the walk to City Hall plus the City Hall route above, about twenty minutes in total. Alternatively, take a Metro bus into the centre.
- **By car:** the Victoria Square car park is the obvious multi-storey, just round the corner.

Hours run 6:45am to 7:30pm Monday to Friday and 7am to 7:30pm on Saturday. Sunday is a little shorter: 7am to 7pm.

## Walking on from Ann Street

A coffee here is best used as a starting point. The surrounding few streets cover a good share of central Belfast.

- **Albert Memorial Clock:** the leaning Victorian clock tower on Queen's Square, a couple of minutes east.
- **Cathedral Quarter:** cross High Street via the Entries to reach St Anne's Cathedral, the bars of Hill Street and the murals around Commercial Court.
- **Victoria Square:** the shopping centre's glass dome has a free viewing platform looking out over the city.
- **St George's Market:** the Victorian covered market is a short walk south towards the river. It trades on Friday, Saturday and Sunday.
- **The Lagan riverfront:** walk east to the Big Fish sculpture and on across the water towards the Titanic Quarter.

A good weekend plan is to arrive at opening, sit through the quiet early hours, then walk to St George's Market as its stalls get going. You'll be out of the café before the 11am rush begins.
