## The group behind the light sticks

ONEUS debuted in January 2019 under the Seoul label RBW with "Valkyrie." They built a following on sharp, theatrical choreography. Their run on the competition show *Road to Kingdom* in 2020 made the point well.

That same year they released "LIT," a track that mixes traditional Korean instruments with electronic production. It became the song people still name when they explain the group to friends. Fans call themselves To Moon, so a tour named "FULL MOON" is a nod to the fandom as much as a title.

## Concert-night etiquette for To Moon

K-pop shows in New York follow habits that will be familiar from Seoul, with a few local touches.

- **Light sticks**: the official ONEUS light stick is the standard accessory. Fans hold it up during ballads rather than waving it across the view of people behind.
- **Fan chants**: To Moon chant members' names at set points in songs. Fans who want to join usually learn the official chant guides beforehand, and the floor gets loud.
- **Freebies**: fans often hand out small free gifts on the sidewalk, such as photocards, stickers or keychains, and trade them in the queue. Accepting one with thanks is normal. Bringing a few to give back is common.
- **Merch**: the merchandise line forms separately from the entry line. Many attendees join it first, because popular items can run out.
- **Bag rules**: the venue sets its own bag and camera policy, and fans who travel light get through security fastest.
- **Phones**: filming is common. Holding a phone up for a whole song blocks the person behind you, so most people film in short bursts.

## Before and after: the blocks around 44th and Broadway

The venue sits in the middle of the Theater District. Pre-show food options fill every direction.

- **Ninth Avenue in Hell's Kitchen**, three blocks west, has rows of Thai, Mexican, ramen and diner spots, and it is calmer than Times Square itself.
- **Koreatown on West 32nd Street**, a short subway ride or a 20-minute walk south, is where many fans go for late Korean barbecue and fried chicken after the show.
- **Bryant Park**, two blocks southeast, usually hosts a winter village with an ice rink and holiday market stalls in December.
- **Rockefeller Center**, about a 10-minute walk northeast, has its Christmas tree up through the holiday season.

Times Square in mid-December is among the most crowded places in the city. Sidewalks around 42nd to 47th Streets move slowly in the evening, so give the last stretch to the door extra time.
