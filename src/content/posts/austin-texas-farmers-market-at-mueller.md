Mueller used to be an airport. Robert Mueller Municipal Airport closed in 1999, and the land in northeast Austin is now a planned neighbourhood of rowhouses, parks and wide pavements. The old control tower still stands. Every Sunday, one piece of that aviation past fills with farm stalls: the Texas Farmers' Market sets up at the Browning Hangar, a restored steel-truss hangar with an open frame.

It's one of Austin's better-rated markets, and it's still a quieter pick than the downtown options visitors usually hear about first. It feels like a neighbourhood errand, not a tourist stop.

## Under the hangar roof

The hangar gives the market its shape. Stalls line up beneath the high trusses, and the open sides let air through, which counts for a lot in the Texas summer. Where the vendors spill out past the roofline, they work under pop-up tents.

What's on the tables depends on the season and on who turns up that week. Expect the usual mix of a Central Texas producer market:

- Vegetables and fruit from farms around the region
- Eggs, meat and dairy sold straight by the producers
- Bread, pastries and other baked goods
- Prepared food and coffee to eat while you browse
- Plants, soaps and small crafts

The market is organised by Texas Farmers' Market, which also runs a Saturday market at Lakeline in Cedar Park. Its focus is on local growers and makers, so it pays to talk to them. Ask what's just come into season and you'll usually get a real answer.

## Four hours, once a week

The window is short. The market opens only on Sundays, from 10am to 2pm, and it's closed the other six days. If your trip runs Monday to Saturday, you'll miss it.

Popular items sell out early. That goes for baked goods, eggs and whatever fruit is at its peak. Arriving close to 10am gives you the widest choice.

Coming later is still fine for a slow wander and lunch from the food vendors. Just expect gaps on some tables by the last hour.

Weather shapes the day too. From June to September, the late morning is noticeably cooler than early afternoon. In spring and autumn, the whole four hours tends to be comfortable.

## Getting to Mueller

Mueller sits about three miles northeast of downtown Austin, east of I-35 and off Airport Boulevard and 51st Street. The market address is 2006 Philomena St, on the edge of Lake Park.

Most people drive or cycle there. The neighbourhood has free street parking, and the spaces nearest the hangar fill first as the morning goes on. The streets are wide and low-traffic, so cycling is easy, and Mueller's paths connect to the surrounding neighbourhoods.

If you're taking public transport, check CapMetro's trip planner for the current bus routes along Airport Boulevard and Aldrich Street.

## Shopping the stalls well

A few habits make the visit smoother:

- **Bring your own bags.** A sturdy tote or a small cooler keeps meat, dairy and soft fruit in good shape on the drive back.
- **Carry some cash, but not only cash.** Many vendors take cards or mobile payment. Smaller stalls sometimes prefer cash, so it's worth having both.
- **Walk the whole market before you buy.** Several farms may sell the same crop at different stages of ripeness.
- **Ask before you handle the produce.** Some growers are happy for you to pick your own. Others would rather bag it for you.
- **Tipping isn't expected** at produce stalls. At coffee and prepared-food stands, a tip jar or a tip screen is normal and optional.

Families are a big part of the Sunday crowd, so the aisles can get tight around strollers. Step to the side of a stall to chat, and leave the walkway clear.

## After the market: Lake Park and beyond

The market's setting is half the reason to come. Lake Park wraps around a small pond directly beside the hangar. It has a walking loop, grassy slopes and shade trees, so it's an easy place to eat what you've just bought.

Just north is the old control tower, a clear reminder of what this land used to be. Families can walk to the Thinkery, Austin's children's museum, which is also in Mueller. Check its hours separately before you plan around it.

Aldrich Street has restaurants and cafés if you want a proper sit-down meal. The Mueller Hike and Bike trails loop the neighbourhood if you'd rather keep moving.

A relaxed visit takes an hour at the market and another hour in the park. It makes a good Sunday morning before heading back into central Austin.
