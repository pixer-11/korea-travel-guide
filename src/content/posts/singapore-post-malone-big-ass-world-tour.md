## Why this show matters

Post Malone had played Singapore before, but only as a surprise guest during the 2023 Formula 1 Singapore Grand Prix weekend concert series. The BIG ASS World Tour date on September 25, 2026 was different: billed as his first proper solo headline concert in the city, at the 55,000-capacity National Stadium rather than on a festival stage. Don Toliver, a regular collaborator and label-mate from the Houston hip-hop/R&B scene, was announced as the opener. The bill paired Post's genre-blending catalogue (pop, hip-hop, country-rock detours) with Toliver's melodic trap sound.

## Getting to National Stadium

National Stadium sits inside the Singapore Sports Hub in Kallang, on the eastern edge of the city centre. The most direct route is Stadium MRT station on the Circle Line. It's built into the Sports Hub complex, so it's a straight walk from the platform to the stadium concourse without crossing major roads. Coming from the Circle Line's other end or from the East-West/North-South lines means transferring at Paya Lebar, Bishan, or Promenade depending on the starting point.

Taxis and ride-hail cars drop off at the Sports Hub, though congestion and long pickup queues after a big show are common, and Mountbatten or Nicoll Highway MRT work as walking alternatives away from the crush. Kallang MRT (Circle Line/East-West interchange) is another decent overflow option a bit further out.

## What to expect inside

The BIG ASS World Tour was built around Post Malone's biggest arena-and-stadium production to date, with a stage designed for the round or thrust configurations used elsewhere on the run: large video walls, pyrotechnics, and a setlist pulling from across his albums, from "Circles" and "Sunflower" to heavier cuts off his more recent rock-inflected work. Doors, support-act start time, and Post's set length were not fixed in advance, as tour schedules shift city to city.

National Stadium concerts in Singapore usually take place with the retractable roof closed, which gives an indoor-arena feel regardless of September weather.

## Food, drink and pre-show plans

The Sports Hub has its own cluster of eateries at OCBC Square and the Kallang Wave Mall next door, an option for a proper sit-down meal rather than concourse concession food. Kallang also borders the Old Airport Road Food Centre, a well-known hawker centre a short ride or 20-minute walk away, and an option for a cheap, fast dinner.

## When to arrive and how long to stay

For a stadium show of this scale, 60–90 minutes before the advertised gate time is a sensible buffer for security screening, bag checks, and the walk from the MRT through the Sports Hub plaza. A full evening with a support act, a headline set, and the post-show exit crush usually means four to five hours on-site. September in Singapore is warm and humid with a real chance of afternoon showers.

## How to visit like a local

Singapore concertgoers overwhelmingly buy and store tickets digitally through the official ticketing app rather than printing anything. Payment inside the Sports Hub is almost entirely cashless: contactless card or mobile wallet (Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayNow-linked apps) covers food, drink, and merchandise stalls.

Tipping isn't expected at concession stands or bars here. A common mistake first-timers make is underestimating exit logistics: rather than fighting the crowd for a taxi or Grab right outside, seasoned local gig-goers walk to a slightly further MRT station (Mountbatten or Kallang) or wait out the first wave over a drink at Kallang Wave Mall.
