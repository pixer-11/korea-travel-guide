## Maná in River Plate's house

Maná plays Estadio Más Monumental on December 10, 2026. It is the first time the Guadalajara band has headlined River Plate's home ground. Locals still call it simply "el Monumental".

The tour takes its name from "Vivir Sin Aire," the ballad from the 1992 album *¿Dónde Jugarán los Niños?*. Argentine audiences sing it word for word. The same goes for the rest of the catalogue Fher Olvera's band has built over three decades:

- "Rayando el Sol"
- "Clavado en un Bar"
- "En el Muelle de San Blas"
- "Labios Compartidos"

The night's setlist is the band's own call, so none of these is guaranteed.

## Reaching the stadium in Núñez

The Monumental stands on Avenida Figueroa Alcorta in Núñez, the northern barrio between Belgrano and the River Plate shoreline. Rail beats road on show nights, because the avenues around it jam.

- **Tren Mitre (Tigre branch):** Núñez station, a short walk from the stands. Trains run from Retiro.
- **Subte Line D:** Congreso de Tucumán, the end of the line. From there it is roughly a 20-minute walk down Avenida Congreso or Avenida Monroe.
- **Colectivos:** several city bus lines run along Avenida del Libertador and Avenida Cabildo.

On big stadium nights, police close the blocks nearest the ground to cars. Taxis and ride-hails drop fans several streets away.

## Getting through the Monumental gates

Stadium shows in Buenos Aires have their own routine, and regulars follow it.

- **Tickets:** the promoter's official listing holds the seating map, sector names and entry rules. Argentine stadium tickets are usually digital and tied to a named account. Resale outside official channels is a common way fans get burned.
- **Sectors:** the field (campo) is standing room. The tiers, such as the San Martín and Belgrano stands, are seated. The gate printed on your ticket matters, because the walk around the outside is long.
- **Bags and searches:** security runs pat-downs at outer cordons and again at the turnstiles. Most people bring a small bag or none.
- **Food:** street grills outside sell choripán. Inside, the kiosks take cards and transfers more readily than they used to, but many fans still carry pesos.

Fans typically come hours before the headline set to claim a spot on the campo. The crowd sings between songs and keeps singing on the walk to the train.

## December nights in Buenos Aires

December is early summer in Buenos Aires. Evenings are usually warm and humid, and a short thunderstorm is common. Regulars at outdoor shows dress in light layers and bring a thin rain jacket rather than an umbrella, which stadiums tend to refuse.

Visitors usually stay in Palermo or Belgrano. Both are a short Mitre or Subte ride from Núñez and have late-opening parrillas for an after-show meal. Avenida Cabildo, a few blocks west of the stadium, is where the post-concert crowd disperses towards buses and taxis.

Leaving takes time. Núñez station and the Line D platforms fill fast after the encore. Many fans walk 15 to 20 minutes towards Cabildo and Belgrano before trying for a car.
