## A harbour built into the lagoons

Hulls and masts fill the view in every direction. Marina El Gouna sits at the edge of El Gouna, a planned resort town on the Red Sea coast. It is built around a web of man-made lagoons, a short drive north of central Hurghada.

The marina is a ring of sheltered water lined with berths. Sailing yachts and motor cruisers tie up stern-first along pale stone quays. Behind them runs a continuous strip of restaurants, cafés and bars with tables pushed out toward the water. The low buildings are painted in the warm sand and ochre tones used across the whole town.

This place is well known, not a secret. The reviews are consistently strong, and for many visitors staying in Hurghada it is the reason to make the trip north at all.

## Walking the quay

The best way in is simply to walk it. The promenade is pedestrian and flat, and a full loop of the water takes well under an hour at an easy pace. That makes it one of the more relaxed walks on this stretch of coast.

What you'll pass along the way:

- Restaurants covering most cuisines, from seafood and Italian to Asian and Egyptian grills
- Cafés where people linger over coffee, shisha and fresh juice
- Small boutiques and beachwear shops
- Dive centres and boat-trip desks
- The yachts themselves, which most people come to look at

Daylight shows off the turquoise water and the white hulls. After dark the restaurant lights reflect off the harbour, and the quay gets livelier as dinner tables fill.

## Getting there from Hurghada

El Gouna lies roughly 20–25 km north of Hurghada along the coastal highway. Hurghada International Airport is about half an hour away by car, so a final evening here before a flight is realistic.

Options for the trip:

- **Taxi or ride-hailing app**: the simplest choice from Hurghada hotels. Agree a fare or check the app price before you set off.
- **Hotel or tour transfers**: many Hurghada resorts sell evening trips to El Gouna.
- **Inside El Gouna**: local shuttle buses and tuk-tuks link the marina with Downtown and the hotels.

Google currently lists the marina as open 24 hours. The quay is a public space, but the individual restaurants, shops and boat desks set their own hours.

## Meals, boats and the evening hours

Most visitors come here for dinner. Prices along the quay are on the pricier side compared with downtown Hurghada. You're paying for the setting, and the menus posted at each entrance show current prices. Fresh fish displayed on ice for you to choose from is a common format on this coast.

The marina also works as a departure point. Day boats head out to the offshore reefs for snorkelling and diving, and private sailing charters leave from the berths. Booking a trip lets you see the harbour from the water before you come back to eat beside it.

If you have a whole day, add Downtown El Gouna (Kafr El Gouna). It's a few minutes away by tuk-tuk and has its own squares and cafés. Then come back to the marina for sunset.

## Doing the marina the El Gouna way

El Gouna runs at a slower pace than Hurghada. The habits here are a little different.

- **Check the town entrance rules.** El Gouna is a gated resort town, and visitors who aren't staying there may be asked to pay a fee at the gate. Ask your hotel or driver what currently applies.
- **Book dinner on busy nights.** Waterfront tables at the popular spots fill up on weekends and holidays. A call or message earlier in the day saves you a wait.
- **Read the bill before tipping.** Egyptian restaurants often add a service charge. A small extra tip in cash for good service is still customary and appreciated.
- **Cards are widely accepted** along the quay. Carry some Egyptian pounds anyway for tuk-tuks and tips.
- **Dress is resort-casual.** Shorts and summer dresses are normal here. Throw on a cover-up rather than walking in straight from the beach in swimwear.

Locals and residents tend to eat late, so the quay often feels busier after 9pm than it does at the start of dinner.

## When the light is right

The marina is pleasant all year, but timing matters. Summer days on the Red Sea are hot, so the sensible plan from June to September is an evening visit. Arrive as the heat breaks and walk the quay before choosing a table.

From late autumn to spring the daytime warmth is comfortable enough for a lunchtime stroll. Evenings can turn breezy, so bring a light layer. In any season, sunset over the moored boats is the moment most people time their visit around.
