## A courthouse turned into a gallery

Columns, a dome and stone lions frame the doors. The Vancouver Art Gallery occupies the former provincial courthouse, a neoclassical block designed by Francis Rattenbury and completed in 1911. Arthur Erickson later reworked the interior for the gallery, which moved in during the 1980s.

Galleries now fill the old courtrooms and corridors. The marble stair and the central rotunda give the place a formality that many modern museums lack. You walk up through the building floor by floor instead of drifting across one open hall.

## Steps, lions and the plaza

Start outside, because downtown Vancouver uses this building as its front porch. The north side faces West Georgia Street, with broad granite steps, a fountain and the stone lions. On any dry day people sit on those steps eating lunch, waiting for friends or watching buskers.

The south side opens onto Robson Square, Erickson's sunken plaza with its terraced steps. In winter part of it becomes a covered public skating rink. Walking the full perimeter takes ten minutes and shows you how the gallery anchors the city's civic centre.

## Carr, cameras and whatever is touring

The gallery is best known for three things. Here is what to look for:

- **Emily Carr.** The gallery holds one of the largest collections of her work anywhere: forests, totem poles and coastal skies from British Columbia, painted in heavy, swirling greens.
- **Photography.** Vancouver's photo-conceptual artists, Jeff Wall among them, are well represented, and photography exhibitions come round regularly.
- **Touring exhibitions.** Large borrowed shows usually take over a full floor or more and change several times a year.

Exhibitions rotate, so check the official site to see what is up before you plan around a particular work. Most visitors spend one and a half to two and a half hours inside. Give it longer if a big touring show is on.

## Reaching 750 Hornby

The entrance address is 750 Hornby St, on the block bounded by West Georgia, Howe and Robson. You do not need a car. Downtown parking is expensive and slow, and three SkyTrain stations are a short walk away:

- **Vancouver City Centre Station** (Canada Line), at Granville and Georgia, about two blocks east.
- **Granville Station** (Expo Line), a few minutes further along Granville Street.
- **Burrard Station** (Expo Line), roughly five blocks northwest.

From the cruise terminal at Canada Place it is a flat walk of about 15 minutes up Howe or Hornby. Many downtown buses also stop on Georgia and Burrard Streets within a block.

## Getting in without the weekend scrum

Plan your timing before anything else. This is one of the most visited cultural stops in the city, and BestTime's forecast says weekend crowds usually peak between 12pm and 5pm. That window covers almost the whole Saturday and Sunday afternoon, so a midday weekend visit usually means shuffling through the big touring show behind other people.

The quietest weekday slot is typically 5pm–6pm. At the moment the gallery stays open past 5pm only on Fridays, when it runs until 8pm. A Friday visit from late afternoon onward is therefore the best way to get the rooms nearly to yourself. On weekends, arriving at the 10am opening usually beats the lunchtime surge.

A few other habits help:

- **Tuesday is currently closed.** Monday is open, which catches out visitors who expect the usual Monday museum closure.
- **Buy entry online** if a blockbuster exhibition is on, so you skip the ticket desk. Current prices are listed on the official site.
- **Start at the top floor and work down.** Most people start on the ground floor, so going the other way keeps you out of the main flow.
- **Card payment is standard** in Vancouver, including tap-to-pay at the desk and the shop.

Gallery hours change for holidays and special exhibitions, so check the official site for this month's schedule.

## Around the block

You do not have to go far afterwards. The Bill Reid Gallery of Northwest Coast Art is a short walk north on Hornby Street. It pairs well with the Carr rooms, since both deal with the Indigenous art of this coast, from different sides.

Robson Street's shops begin at the gallery's southern corner. Christ Church Cathedral, Vancouver's oldest surviving church, stands on Burrard at Georgia, a few minutes' walk away. For a longer stretch, continue north to Coal Harbour and the seawall, which leads toward Stanley Park.
