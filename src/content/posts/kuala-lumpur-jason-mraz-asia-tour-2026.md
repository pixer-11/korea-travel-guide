## A hockey ground, not an arena

The National Hockey Stadium is a field-hockey venue. It is not a purpose-built concert hall, and that shapes the night.

It sits inside the National Sports Complex, the same Bukit Jalil sports precinct as Bukit Jalil National Stadium and Axiata Arena. The stadium was built for the hockey events of the 1998 Commonwealth Games in Kuala Lumpur. For a concert, the stage goes at one end of the pitch, with stands rising along the sides.

Because it is an open-air ground, the sound carries differently from an enclosed arena. Seats in the stands give you height and a clear view of the stage. Standing on the pitch puts you closer, but you will be on your feet for the whole show. Check your ticket category for which area you are in before you arrive.

## Riding the Sri Petaling Line in

Take the train. Everyone else will be trying to drive or catch a ride, and the roads around the complex choke on big event nights.

- **Line:** LRT Sri Petaling Line (Line 4 on the Rapid KL map).
- **Station:** Bukit Jalil, which serves the National Sports Complex directly.
- **From KLCC or Bukit Bintang:** change at Masjid Jamek or Hang Tuah, depending on your route, onto the Sri Petaling Line heading south.
- **From the station:** follow the crowd and the stadium signs across the complex. The hockey stadium is a walk, not a bus ride.

Pay at the gates with a Touch 'n Go card, or tap a contactless bank card where the reader accepts it. Top up before the show. Machines on the way home after a concert are where the longest lines form.

## Planning for October rain

October in Kuala Lumpur falls in the inter-monsoon period. Late-afternoon and evening thunderstorms are common, and they arrive fast.

At an open-air hockey ground, that matters. A downpour can hit while gates are open or during the set, and some seating may not be covered. Rules on umbrellas vary by event, so check what the organiser allows.

- A light poncho packs smaller than an umbrella and won't block anyone's view.
- A zip bag keeps your phone dry, along with your e-ticket if you're showing it on screen.
- Shoes that cope with a wet pitch are a better choice than sandals if you're standing.

The heat stays even after dark. Drink water before you join the entry queue, and check the venue rules on what bottles you can bring in.

When the encore ends, thousands of people head for one LRT station. You have two choices. You can walk straight out and accept the platform crush, or you can let the first wave clear. Sitting twenty minutes in the stands or grabbing a drink nearby often gets you a less packed train.

## Eating before and after

Eat before you arrive. Stadium food lines move slowly, and the music is why you came.

Sri Petaling, one or two stops up the line, has dense streets of Chinese restaurants and mamak eateries. Mamak shops are the Indian-Muslim diners found all over Malaysia, many open late. Order roti canai with dhal, mee goreng mamak, or a teh tarik, the sweet milk tea that is "pulled" between two jugs to foam it. You pay at the counter or the table, often by DuitNow QR or e-wallet as well as cash.

Pavilion Bukit Jalil, the large mall near the sports complex, is the air-conditioned option. It has food courts and chain restaurants, and it is a good place to wait out a storm before gates open. Expect it to be busy on a Saturday.
