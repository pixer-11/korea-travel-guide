Tashkent doesn't have many buildings tall enough to give you a proper skyline view, so when one does, it gets noticed fast. Sette Restaurant and Bar sits on the seventh floor of the Hyatt Regency Tashkent, and that height alone puts it in a small club of places in the city where you can watch the sun go down over the rooftops instead of past a construction hoarding.

## What Sette actually is

Sette is a restaurant and bar combined, split across an indoor dining room and an open-air terrace. The indoor space does the heavy lifting on any night when the weather turns, with the kind of finished, contemporary look you'd expect from a five-star hotel's signature outlet rather than a standalone restaurant.

The terrace is the reason people specifically ask for Sette by name. It faces out over central Tashkent, and on a clear evening the seventh-floor vantage gives you a wide, low-angle view of the city that's hard to get anywhere else without going up a telecom tower.

## The view from the seventh floor

Tashkent's centre is low-rise by regional standards, all wide Soviet-era boulevards and squat government buildings, so a seventh-floor terrace reads as genuinely elevated here. You're not looking down a canyon of skyscrapers; you're looking across a flatter, greener city, with the sprawl thinning toward the horizon.

That makes Sette's terrace a sunset spot in the literal sense, not just a marketing phrase. Arrive with enough daylight left to watch the light change over maybe half an hour, rather than turning up after dark when the terrace becomes just another outdoor seating area.

## The wine list as the other headline act

Alongside the view, Sette's other calling card is an extensive wine list that draws from producers well beyond Uzbekistan and the wider region. For a city where most restaurant wine lists are short and locally weighted, a genuinely global selection is unusual enough to be worth the trip on its own for anyone who cares about what's in the glass.

If wine is your reason for going, it's worth asking staff what's being poured by the glass that night versus what's bottle-only, since an extensive list like this often rotates.

## Getting there and settling in

Sette is inside the Hyatt Regency Tashkent, which sits near Amir Timur Square, one of the most central and recognisable landmarks in the city. That makes it an easy add-on if you're already in the area for the square, the nearby Broadway pedestrian street, or the government and business district that surrounds it.

Because it's a hotel restaurant rather than a walk-in neighbourhood spot, the practical logic is different:

- Head for the hotel lobby first and take the lift to the seventh floor rather than looking for a separate street entrance
- Dress a notch smarter than you would for a casual Tashkent café, in keeping with the hotel setting
- Confirm current opening hours and whether a reservation is needed directly with the Hyatt Regency Tashkent before you go, since hotel restaurant hours and terrace access can shift with the season and with private events

## Golden hour on the terrace

If there's one Sette-specific ritual worth building an evening around, it's timing your arrival to the light rather than to a meal slot. The terrace's whole appeal is the transition from daylight to dusk over the city, so a table booked for that window matters more here than at a restaurant without a view.

Because the terrace is weather-dependent, it's also worth asking at the time of booking whether it's open that evening or whether service has been moved indoors. Wind, dust, or a cold snap can push everything into the dining room, and you'd rather know before you arrive dressed for outdoor seating.

## Reading the room: who Sette is for

As a Hyatt Regency signature outlet with a view terrace and an international wine list, Sette sits at the trendier, more polished end of Tashkent's dining scene rather than the traditional plov-and-shashlik end. It suits a date night, a small celebration, or an evening where you want a drink with a view more than you want a deep dive into Uzbek regional cooking.

Because it's newly established and part of a major international hotel brand, expect a room that skews toward business travellers, visiting diplomats, and Tashkent residents marking an occasion, rather than a backpacker crowd. That's worth knowing going in, since it shapes the pace of service and the general noise level, both usually calmer and more measured than a street-level bar.

## Pairing it with the rest of the neighbourhood

Amir Timur Square and Broadway are both close enough to fold into the same evening. A common pattern is an early walk around the square while the light is still good, then heading up to Sette as the sky starts to turn, and finishing with a wander down Broadway once you're back at street level.

That sequencing also solves the practical problem of Tashkent's limited elevated viewpoints: you get your golden-hour view from the terrace, then spend the rest of the night at ground level where most of the city's other bars and restaurants actually are.
