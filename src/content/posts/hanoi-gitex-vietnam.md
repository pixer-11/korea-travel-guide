## Getting to Hanoi and around it

Most international visitors arrive through Noi Bai International Airport, roughly 25-30 minutes from central Hanoi by car depending on traffic, longer during rush hour. Airport taxis and ride-hailing apps (Grab is the dominant one locally) are the standard way in; the metered or ride-hailing option avoids haggling over a fare.

Within the city, Grab is also the easiest way to reach a conference venue, with drivers working from a dropped pin rather than a street number. Hanoi's traffic is dense and lane discipline is loose, so real journey times run longer than a map estimate suggests, particularly in the morning.

## Badge etiquette and the business-conference rhythm

Business cards still carry real weight at Vietnamese and broader Southeast Asian tech conferences, and the basic courtesy is to present and receive them with both hands. Dress skews business formal or smart business casual for keynote sessions, looser on the exhibition floor itself.

Networking at events like this tends to happen in the gaps: coffee breaks between sessions, the exhibitor floor during lulls, and organised evening receptions where the programme includes one. For business development rather than keynotes, day two floor time is usually the higher-value window, since that is when exhibitors are set up for longer conversations rather than delivering a scheduled talk.

First editions also keep the logistics simple: a single badge desk, one floor plan, and far fewer parallel tracks than the Dubai or Berlin shows, where the programme spans several halls at once.
