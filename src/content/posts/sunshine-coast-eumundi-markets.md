## Two mornings a week on Memorial Drive

The market has run twice a week since 1979. That long run explains its layout: it sits right in the town centre, along the main street of a small hinterland town, not on a purpose-built showground. Stalls fill covered walkways and open lanes under big shade trees off 80 Memorial Drive, and the town's pubs and shops look straight onto them.

The focus is locally made work. You'll find:

- Handmade jewellery, ceramics and leatherwork
- Clothing, hats and textiles
- Art prints, timber pieces and homewares
- Fresh produce and food to take home

Street food runs through the whole site, so breakfast and an early lunch both happen here.

## Timing it around the crowds

Opening hours are short. At the moment the gates open at 7:30am on Wednesday and Saturday and stalls pack down at 2pm. Every other day the site is closed. Check the market's official site before you drive up, because public holidays and seasonal changes can move things.

Saturday is the big day. Measured foot traffic puts the weekend peak at 8am–2pm, which covers almost the whole trading day. If Saturday is your only option, get there at 7:30am and work the lanes before 8am.

Wednesday is the obvious alternative for anyone with weekday flexibility. Arriving late is a real risk: by early afternoon some makers are already packing up, and 2pm is a hard finish.

## Getting to Eumundi

Most visitors drive. Eumundi sits just off the Bruce Highway, about 20 minutes from Noosa Heads and under an hour and a half from Brisbane. On market days the roads into town back up, and paid parking fills the paddocks and side streets around the site.

Eumundi has a station on the Queensland Rail line north of Nambour. Services are infrequent, so check the timetable before relying on it.

Pair the market with the hinterland nearby:

- The Ginger Factory at Yandina, a short drive south
- Cooroy and Pomona to the north
- Noosa's beaches for the afternoon, once stalls close at 2pm

## Shopping the stalls the Eumundi way

Many of the people behind the tables made what's on them. Ask about materials or commissions: makers expect the question, and plenty take custom orders or post items interstate. Card and tap payments are common across Australian markets, but a few smaller stalls may prefer cash, so carry some.

Haggling isn't the norm here. Prices are set by the maker, and Australians don't tip at food stalls. Bring a sturdy bag or basket, because the lanes are long and loose purchases pile up fast.

Do one full loop before you buy. The site is bigger than it looks from the street, and similar crafts turn up in several lanes. Then go back to what stayed with you. Sunscreen and a hat matter even under the trees: plenty of the walking is in full Queensland sun.

On Saturday, eat early. Food queues lengthen right through the peak. A 7:30am coffee and breakfast, then shopping, beats lining up at noon.
