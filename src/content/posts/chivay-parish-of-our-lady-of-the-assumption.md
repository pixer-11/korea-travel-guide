## A church at the top of the valley

Chivay sits at about 3,600 metres. That number shapes everything about a visit here, including a slow walk across the plaza to the parish church.

Most travellers know Chivay as the overnight stop on the road from Arequipa to the Cruz del Cóndor. Tour buses roll in through the afternoon, unload at hotels, and leave before dawn for the condor viewpoint. The Parish of Our Lady of the Assumption sits on the town's Plaza de Armas through all of it, and most visitors walk straight past.

That's the case for going. It's a working parish church, not a ticketed monument. It's consistently well rated by the small number of people who do stop. It also gives you a quiet window into the Colca Valley's Catholic life, which is older than most of the hotels around it.

## Finding it on the Plaza de Armas

You can't miss it. Chivay is compact, and every main street runs toward the plaza where the church stands.

- **From Arequipa:** buses and tour vans take roughly three to four hours. The road climbs over the Patapampa pass at close to 4,900 metres before dropping into the valley.
- **In town:** walk to the Plaza de Armas. The covered market and most cafés are within a few blocks.
- **Plus code:** 997X+98C, Chivay, which drops a pin right on the church if you're using a maps app.

If you're on a two-day Colca tour, you'll usually have free time in Chivay on the first afternoon. That's the easiest time to slip in.

## What you're looking at

Colca was a busy mission territory under Spanish rule. Villages along the valley were reorganised around a plaza and a church from the late sixteenth century onward. Chivay's parish belongs to that pattern, alongside the better-known churches of Yanque, Coporaque, Lari and Maca further down the valley.

Treat Chivay's church as the valley's introduction, not its showpiece. If it catches your interest, the stone church facing Yanque's plaza is a short drive away. Morning tours to the condor viewpoint often pause there.

Inside, expect a long single nave pointing toward the altar, with wooden pews and devotional images along the walls. The Virgin of the Assumption, the parish's patron, takes the place of honour. Look at how the images are dressed and decorated, often by local families.

## The Assumption in mid-August

The church is named for a feast that falls on 15 August, and that date is the parish's own celebration. Across the Andes, a town's patronal feast is the high point of its religious calendar. Expect processions, Masses and music around the plaza rather than a quiet sanctuary.

If you're travelling in mid-August, ask at your hotel what's planned in Chivay that week. Plans shift from year to year and nothing is fixed here, so check locally once you arrive.

The Colca Valley is also home to the Wititi, a courtship dance recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage. Its embroidered skirts and swirling dancers often show up at valley festivities. Seeing it near the church is a matter of luck and timing, not a guarantee.

## Visiting quietly during Mass

This church is used every week by the people of Chivay, so how you behave matters more than when you arrive.

- **Check the door.** Mass times are usually posted at the entrance. No visiting hours are published, so confirm on the spot or ask at your hotel.
- **During a service,** stay at the back, don't walk the aisles, and save your photos for later.
- **Ask before photographing people,** especially women in traditional dress. In Chivay, Collagua women often wear tall white embroidered hats. Many of them earn money posing for photos on the plaza, so asking first is basic courtesy.
- **Dress modestly:** cover your shoulders and take off your hat inside. At this altitude you'll be in layers anyway.
- **Leave something in the offering box** if you'd like to support the parish. Keep small change in soles handy for that and for the market next door.

A greeting in Spanish ("buenos días" before noon, "buenas tardes" after) goes a long way with whoever is looking after the church.

## Pacing yourself at altitude

Chivay is high enough to catch out travellers coming straight up from the coast. Even if Arequipa felt fine, the climb over Patapampa can leave you breathless and headachy on arrival.

Walk slowly across the plaza and up the church steps. Drink plenty of water, and try the coca tea that cafés and hotels around town routinely offer. A church visit is ideal on your first afternoon precisely because it asks so little of your lungs.

## Rounding out an afternoon in Chivay

The church takes half an hour at most, so pair it with nearby stops:

- **Chivay market:** a few blocks from the plaza, with fruit, cheese, quinoa and stalls serving simple local meals.
- **La Calera hot springs:** a short taxi ride outside town. Thermal pools by the Colca River are the classic way to finish a cold valley afternoon.
- **The Inca bridge (Puente Inca):** a walk to the river crossing on the edge of town, with views up the terraced valley.
- **Yanque:** the next village down-valley, with its own colonial church and plaza.

Next morning, most itineraries head out early for the Cruz del Cóndor, roughly one and a half to two hours away by road. So if you want to see the parish church, see it the evening you arrive. You probably won't be back in the plaza before you leave.
