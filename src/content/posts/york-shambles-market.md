## Not on The Shambles, but just behind it

First-time visitors often get this wrong. Shambles Market isn't on The Shambles itself. It fills the open square between that timber-framed lane and Parliament Street, and its address is 5 Silver Street.

Snickelways run between the two. That's the York word for the narrow alleys that cut between buildings. A few paces through one takes you from overhanging medieval shopfronts to rows of canopied stalls.

This is the city's long-established general market, once known as Newgate Market. It's one of the most-reviewed stops in central York, so it's no secret. It works best as a practical break from the sightseeing.

## What's under the canopies

The stalls are outdoors, with roofs over the pitches and open-air aisles between them. Here's what you'll find:

- **Fresh produce:** fruit and vegetables, often sold by the bowl.
- **Crafts and gifts:** handmade items, prints and souvenirs that cost less than on the tourist-facing Shambles shops.
- **Clothing and accessories:** everyday wear, scarves and bags.
- **Street food:** a food-court area at the market's edge, with shared seating.

The traders change through the week, so a stall you liked on Saturday may not be there on Tuesday. If you spot a craft piece you want, buy it then. You may not find it again.

## Working the stalls

Payment is easy. Most traders take contactless cards, but a few small produce and craft stalls prefer cash, so carry some coins. Nobody tips at the stalls.

Haggling isn't the custom here. Prices are fixed, though asking for a deal when you buy several items is polite enough.

At the street-food stalls you order and pay at the counter, then find a seat at the shared tables. Clear your own space when you've finished.

On timing:

- **Arrive near the 9am opening.** You'll usually get in before the day-trippers walk up from York station and the tour groups finish at the Minster.
- **Come back late in the afternoon for produce.** Fruit and veg traders at UK markets often cut prices to clear stock before the 5pm close.
- **Check the weather.** The stalls have cover but the aisles don't, so bring a jacket if it looks like rain.

The snickelways are single-file. Step out into the square before you stop for photos. People stopping in the alleys is what causes most of the jams around here.

## Fitting it into a York day

Everything worth pairing with the market is within a short walk:

- **The Shambles:** right next door, through any of the connecting alleys.
- **York Minster:** about five minutes north via Low Petergate.
- **Jorvik Viking Centre:** about five minutes south on Coppergate.
- **Clifford's Tower:** around ten minutes south, past Jorvik.
- **York railway station:** roughly a 15-minute walk, crossing the River Ouse at Lendal Bridge.

Don't drive into the centre. Streets inside the city walls are narrow and partly restricted to traffic. York's Park & Ride buses drop you within walking distance of the market.

The hours are the same every day, 9am to 5pm, Sundays included. That makes the market an easy fallback when you're in York on a Sunday or Monday and other places have shorter hours or are closed. A good plan is the Minster first thing, the market for lunch, then Jorvik or Clifford's Tower in the afternoon.
