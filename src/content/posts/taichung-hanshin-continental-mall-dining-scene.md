## Why this food court is the talk of Taichung

Taichung has no shortage of shopping malls, but Hanshin Continental Mall's dining floor has managed to do something rare: turn a food court into a destination in its own right. The buzz centers on two concepts. The first is Sangok, a Korean BBQ chain making its Taiwan debut here with an outdoor terrace — reportedly the first of its kind for the brand in the country — positioned to look out over a baseball field, giving diners grilled meat and a stadium-adjacent view in the same sitting. The second is a rotating sushi (kaiten-zushi style) outlet built around a giant touchscreen ordering system, letting guests build an order visually rather than flag down staff or fill in a paper slip. Together they've made the mall's dining scene as much a reason to visit Beitun as any single retail draw.

## Where it is and how to get there

Hanshin Continental Mall sits in Taichung's Beitun District, a fast-growing residential and commercial pocket in the city's north. Beitun doesn't have the dense subway coverage of cities like Taipei, so the most reliable way in is by car, scooter, or taxi/rideshare; check current bus routes serving the mall before you go, since northern Taichung's bus network has been expanding alongside new developments like this one. If you're coming from the Taichung Railway Station area or the High Speed Rail station, budget real time for the crosstown trip — this isn't a quick detour from the old downtown core.

## What to eat: the two anchor draws

Start with Sangok if you want the full spectacle. The appeal isn't just the meat on the grill — it's the terrace seating that opens the dining room up to an outdoor view of a baseball field, an unusual pairing for a Korean BBQ concept and reportedly a first for the brand in Taiwan. Expect this format to draw crowds especially during evening hours and on days when there's activity at the field.

The rotating sushi counter is the other must-try, less for novelty of the format (kaiten-zushi has long history in Taiwan) and more for the giant touchscreen ordering system, which speeds up the process of building a large order and cuts down on the friction of language or menu literacy for out-of-town visitors. It's a good option if you're dining solo or want a faster-paced meal between shopping stops.

Beyond these two headline tenants, expect the food court to carry the broader mix typical of a large new Taiwanese mall — regional Taiwanese snacks, bubble tea counters, and international chains — though the two standouts above are the reason this particular food court has become a topic of conversation citywide.

## When to go

Because both concepts are newly opened and drawing significant attention, peak weekend lunch and dinner hours are likely to mean long waits, especially for the Sangok terrace seats. Weekday afternoons or early evenings on weekdays tend to be calmer at any freshly opened mall dining floor in Taiwan, and that pattern is worth testing here. As with any new opening, hours, seating policy, and whether reservations are accepted can shift in the first months — confirm details directly with the mall or the restaurants before making a special trip.

## How to visit like a local

Treat this like any hyped new opening in a Taiwanese mall: the smart move is to arrive right when the food court opens for lunch or dinner service, before the after-work and weekend crowds arrive, rather than trying to walk in during peak 7-9pm hours. For the Sangok terrace specifically, ask on arrival whether outdoor seats can be requested or if it's first-come — new outdoor terrace concepts in Taiwan food halls often don't take reservations for a specific table type even if the restaurant itself does. Payment across Taiwanese food courts and mall restaurants is overwhelmingly by card or mobile payment now (Line Pay, Apple Pay, EasyCard-linked apps), though carrying some cash is still sensible as a backup at smaller stalls. Tipping is not expected anywhere in this setting. If you want the rotating sushi without a wait, go right after the lunch rush dies down, typically mid-afternoon. And the one mistake newcomers make: assuming a mall food court is a fallback grab-a-bite option — at Hanshin Continental, the anchor restaurants function more like standalone destination restaurants that happen to share a floor, so plan around them rather than treating them as an afterthought.
