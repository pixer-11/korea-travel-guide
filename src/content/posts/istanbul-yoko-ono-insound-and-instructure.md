## What the show covers

This retrospective traces Yoko Ono's career from her early 1960s Fluxus-era instruction pieces through to recent work. Expect the mix she's known for: text-based instructions, participatory installations, film, and objects meant to be touched, stepped on, or completed by the viewer rather than just observed.

The title itself points to the throughline: sound pieces and structural, architectural interventions that turn a gallery room into something you act inside rather than walk past. Ono has spent decades making work that only finishes when a visitor does something, so arrive expecting to participate, not just look.

## Getting the most from a visit

Ono's instruction pieces reward slow reading. Many works are literally a printed instruction (paint a piece of the sky, mend a broken cup) that you are meant to carry out mentally or physically, so rushing through defeats the point.

- Read wall text before touching or acting on a piece; some works have specific rules for participation
- Set aside more time than a typical painting-and-sculpture show; interactive pieces create natural bottlenecks
- Bring a phone or notebook if you want to record your own instruction-piece response, a habit many Ono retrospectives encourage
- Check whether photography is permitted for participatory works specifically, since rules sometimes differ from the rest of the gallery

Pair the visit with whichever Istanbul art district houses the venue. Both Istanbul Modern (Karaköy) and Pera Museum (Beyoğlu) sit near dense clusters of galleries, cafes, and the Bosphorus waterfront, so a half-day itinerary around the show is easy to build using the official venue details.
