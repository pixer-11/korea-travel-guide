You won't find turnstiles or ticket lines here. You'll find a young missionary guide waiting at the door to walk you, personally, through a story most visitors to Old Town have never heard: the longest infantry march in US military history, made by a battalion of Mormon volunteers from Iowa to San Diego in 1846-47 during the Mexican-American War.

## What the visit actually is

This isn't a look-in-the-glass-case kind of museum. The site is a set of themed rooms, each staffed by a guide (often a young missionary from The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints), who leads you through short, interactive stops rather than leaving you to wander.

- Expect period-costumed staff, not just placards
- Rooms cover the recruitment, the 2,000-mile march, and the battalion's arrival in California
- Some stops let you try a hand-cart pull or grind grain, aimed as much at kids as adults
- The whole route takes roughly 45 minutes to an hour, longer if your guide fields questions

Nobody is pushing you along. If you linger at one exhibit, the guide adjusts pace rather than rushing you to the next room.

## Getting there and what it costs

The address is 2510 Juan St, in San Diego's Old Town, close enough to Old Town San Diego State Historic Park that most visitors fold both into the same afternoon. Admission is free, and there's no ticket counter to navigate. Parking is available on-site and on nearby streets, and the Old Town Transit Center (serving the trolley's Blue and Green Lines, plus Amtrak and Coaster rail) is a short walk away if you're coming without a car.

## Hours worth knowing before you go

The schedule has a quirk that trips people up if they don't check first: most days run 10am to 7pm, but Tuesday and Sunday don't open until 1pm.

- Monday, Wednesday–Saturday: 10:00 AM – 7:00 PM
- Tuesday and Sunday: 1:00 – 7:00 PM

If you're building a morning around Old Town and land here on a Tuesday, you'll find the doors still locked at 11am. Plan those two days for the afternoon instead.

## Beating the weekend crowd

Weekends here run busy from 11am straight through to closing at 7pm, largely families working the site into a full day in Old Town. The quieter stretch is 10am to 1pm on weekends, before that afternoon wave builds. If a weekday works for your schedule, you'll generally find smaller groups and more one-on-one time with your guide, since tours here are often shared with whoever else has just walked in.

## The march itself, in the rooms

The narrative backbone of the visit is the 1846 march: roughly 2,000 miles on foot, through desert and unmapped terrain, undertaken by volunteers whose enlistment helped fund their families' migration west. The rooms trace this chronologically, so by the final stop, you understand not just what happened but why a monument to an infantry unit sits in a residential corner of San Diego rather than a battlefield.

A large monument and reflecting area on the grounds mark the battalion's arrival, and it's worth the extra few minutes outside after the indoor rooms.

## Talking with your guide

The guides are unpaid missionaries, mostly young adults, and they're trained to answer questions about both the history and, if you're curious, about their faith, though nobody here pushes the topic. It's normal to ask follow-up questions as you move room to room rather than saving them for the end. Tipping isn't expected and there's no gift shop transaction to navigate, since the whole experience is donation-based rather than ticketed.

If you want a shorter or more in-depth version, mention it at the start; guides commonly adjust the script depending on whether you're a family with young kids or a couple with time to spare.

## Pairing it with Old Town

Because the site sits right at the edge of Old Town San Diego State Historic Park, it's easy to treat this as one stop in a longer walk rather than a standalone destination. Most visitors spend under an hour here, then continue on to the park's adobe buildings, shops, and restaurants a few minutes' walk away. Given the free admission and short duration, it works well as a mid-day break, especially timed to that 10am to 1pm weekend window, before the afternoon crowds settle into both the historic site and the park around it.
