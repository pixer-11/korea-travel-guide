## A round building in a city park

The museum is built in a circle. Its domed main hall stands in the green stretch of Hiroshima Central Park, with Hiroshima Castle's moat a short walk north and the Peace Memorial Park to the south. You come in from the trees and the traffic noise drops away.

Hiroshima Bank founded it in 1978. Its stated idea is that art belongs to a city rebuilt after 1945, and a gentler stop after the Atomic Bomb Dome is what brings many visitors here.

## What hangs on the walls

The permanent collection follows French painting in order. Galleries move from Romanticism and the Barbizon painters through Impressionism and Post-Impressionism into the early 20th century. These are the names to look for:

- **Delacroix, Corot and Millet** in the early rooms
- **Monet, Renoir and Degas** for the Impressionists
- **Van Gogh's *Daubigny's Garden***, one of the two versions he painted in Auvers in his final weeks
- **Cézanne and Gauguin**, then **Matisse, Picasso, Modigliani and Chagall**

A separate section shows modern Japanese painters working in oils and Western styles. Temporary exhibitions run alongside the permanent rooms, so check what is on before you go.

## Moving through the galleries

The rooms make a loop. You walk around the circular plan, and the route itself carries you through the decades. Most people need 60–90 minutes for the permanent collection. Allow more if a special exhibition is on.

The editorial note calls out the quiet courtyard, and it is worth stepping into. Use it as a break between the French rooms and the Japanese section. Benches and open sky give your eyes a rest after the dense canvases indoors.

## Getting there from the tram and the Peace Park

The address is 3-2 Motomachi, Naka Ward. Hiroden streetcars are the easiest way in:

- **Kamiyacho-nishi or Kamiyacho-higashi** tram stops: walk north into the park, about 5–10 minutes
- **Kencho-mae Station** on the Astram Line: a short walk west
- **From the Atomic Bomb Dome**: head north along the river, about 10 minutes on foot

One warning. The **Hiroshima Prefectural Museum of Art** is a different museum, next to Shukkeien garden. Put the full name into your map app, or you may end up on the wrong side of town.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

Google currently lists 9am–5pm every day of the week. Museum hours can change with the season, for exhibition changeovers and over holidays, so check the official site for this month's schedule before you set off.

BestTime's forecast of typical foot traffic points to weekdays. It's usually calmest between 9am and 10am, then again from 4pm to 5pm. Try not to come on a weekend between 11am and 4pm, when the galleries tend to be busiest. On a weekday, go to the Van Gogh first and enjoy it with fewer people around.

## Quiet rules inside the galleries

Japanese museums run on hushed courtesy, and this one is no exception. People talk in near-whispers, keep a step back from the canvases and follow the arrows around the loop. Here's how to fit in:

- **Tickets**: buy at the counter by the entrance. Current prices are posted there and on the museum's official site.
- **Bags**: large bags and umbrellas usually go in lockers or the umbrella stand before you enter.
- **Photos**: photography is generally not allowed in the galleries. Look for signs that mark any exceptions, especially in special exhibitions.
- **Pens**: if you want to sketch or take notes, use a pencil. Ink is commonly banned near the artworks.

For an easy half day, pair the museum with Hiroshima Castle, about 10 minutes north through the park. Or go the other way and walk south along the river to the Peace Memorial Park and finish there.
