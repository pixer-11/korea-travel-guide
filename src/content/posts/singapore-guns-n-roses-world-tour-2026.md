## One November night in Kallang

There is only one Singapore date. Guns N' Roses bring their World Tour 2026 to the National Stadium on 25 November 2026, and this city gets a single show. If you want to be there, this is it.

The setting suits a band built for big rooms. The National Stadium sits on the Kallang Basin as the centrepiece of the Singapore Sports Hub. Its domed roof has a retractable section, and its seating bowl is one of the largest in Southeast Asia. The date also falls in the month the band named one of its best-known ballads after.

## Tickets and official sources

Buy from official channels only. Start from the band's official tour page and the National Stadium or Sports Hub listings, which link to the authorised ticket seller for this show. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site rather than trusting a reposted screenshot.

Resale pages and social media "spares" are where fans get burned at stadium shows. Check these points before you pay:

- The seller is the one linked from the official tour page
- The seating category and gate are shown on the ticket itself
- The name on the booking matches your ID, if the terms require it
- Bag and camera rules are printed on the venue's event page

## Getting to the National Stadium

The MRT is the easy answer. Stadium MRT station on the Circle Line (CC6) is built into the Sports Hub, so you come up from the platform and walk a few minutes along covered paths to the stadium gates.

Kallang MRT on the East-West Line (EW10) is the backup. It's roughly a 10 to 15 minute walk across the bridge and along the river to the stadium. That walk often moves faster than the crowd queuing at Stadium station.

Tap in with a contactless bank card or an EZ-Link card; the gantries take both. Taxis and ride-hailing work too. Expect slow traffic on Nicoll Highway and around Stadium Boulevard as a full house arrives together.

## Around the Sports Hub before the show

Get to Kallang early and you won't be stuck for food. Kallang Wave Mall is attached to the Sports Hub and has food courts, casual restaurants and a supermarket under one roof. Fans usually fill up there before heading to the gates.

The Singapore Indoor Stadium is next door, and the waterfront promenade runs along the Kallang Basin. Looking across the water, you'll see the city skyline. If you'd rather eat local, Old Airport Road Food Centre is one MRT stop away at Dakota (CC8). It's one of the city's best-known hawker centres.

## Surviving the encore exodus

A full stadium all leaves at the same moment. Here's what experienced Sports Hub concertgoers do:

- **Walk to Kallang station.** The East-West Line platform clears faster than Stadium station after a sold-out show.
- **Settle your MRT fare in advance.** Card tapping is fast, but a card with no value holds up the whole line.
- **Finish drinks before the gantry.** Eating and drinking are banned on Singapore's trains and in paid areas of stations, and the fines are real.
- **Pick a meeting point.** Phone signal can falter when tens of thousands of people leave together. Agree on a landmark, like a Kallang Wave Mall entrance.
- **Pack for the season.** November brings the northeast monsoon, with heavy afternoon and evening showers. Whether the roof stays open is the venue's call, so carry a compact poncho if the bag rules allow it.

Stadium staff tend to enforce the posted bag size limits. Read the event page and leave anything bulky at your hotel.

## Planning the night

Treat this as a full evening. The band's recent tours have been known for long sets. Add the queue at the gates and the walk out, and the night runs late, so leave yourself time to get home.

Staying nearby makes the end of the night easier. Hotels around Kallang, Marina Bay and the Bugis area are a short MRT ride or taxi away. If you're flying in, Changi Airport links to the city by the East-West Line and by taxi. Confirm the start time, gate opening and support acts on the official source, then build your day around them.
