## One November night in Kallang

There was only one Singapore date. Guns N' Roses were set to bring their World Tour 2026 to the National Stadium on 25 November 2026, a single show for the city.

The setting suits a band built for big rooms. The National Stadium sits on the Kallang Basin as the centrepiece of the Singapore Sports Hub. Its domed roof has a retractable section, and its seating bowl is one of the largest in Southeast Asia. The date also fell in the month the band named one of its best-known ballads after.

## Tickets and official sources

Tickets were sold through official channels. The band's official tour page and the National Stadium and Sports Hub listings linked to the authorised ticket seller for this show.

Resale pages and social media "spares" are where fans get burned at stadium shows. Genuine tickets usually share a few traits:

- The seller is the one linked from the official tour page
- The seating category and gate are shown on the ticket itself
- The name on the booking matches the holder's ID, where the terms require it
- The venue sets bag and camera rules for each event

## Getting to the National Stadium

The MRT is the easy answer. Stadium MRT station on the Circle Line (CC6) is built into the Sports Hub, so it's a few minutes' walk from the platform along covered paths to the stadium gates.

Kallang MRT on the East-West Line (EW10) is the backup. It's roughly a 10 to 15 minute walk across the bridge and along the river to the stadium. That walk often moves faster than the crowd queuing at Stadium station.

The gantries take both contactless bank cards and EZ-Link cards. Taxis and ride-hailing work too, though traffic on Nicoll Highway and around Stadium Boulevard is usually slow when a full house arrives together.

## Around the Sports Hub before the show

Kallang is well served for food. Kallang Wave Mall is attached to the Sports Hub and has food courts, casual restaurants and a supermarket under one roof. Fans usually fill up there before heading to the gates.

The Singapore Indoor Stadium is next door, and the waterfront promenade runs along the Kallang Basin, with the city skyline across the water. For local food, Old Airport Road Food Centre is one MRT stop away at Dakota (CC8). It's one of the city's best-known hawker centres.

## Surviving the encore exodus

A full stadium all leaves at the same moment. Experienced Sports Hub concertgoers have a few habits:

- **Walking to Kallang station.** The East-West Line platform clears faster than Stadium station after a sold-out show.
- **Settling the MRT fare in advance.** Card tapping is fast, but a card with no value holds up the whole line.
- **Finishing drinks before the gantry.** Eating and drinking are banned on Singapore's trains and in paid areas of stations, and the fines are real.
- **Picking a meeting point.** Phone signal can falter when tens of thousands of people leave together, so a landmark like a Kallang Wave Mall entrance helps.
- **Packing for the season.** November brings the northeast monsoon, with heavy afternoon and evening showers. Whether the roof stays open is the venue's call, which is why compact ponchos are common where bag rules allow them.

Stadium staff tend to enforce the posted bag size limits, and bulky items are often turned away at the gates.

## Planning the night

A stadium concert is a full evening. The band's recent tours have been known for long sets. Add the queue at the gates and the walk out, and a night like this usually ends late.

Staying nearby makes the end of the night easier. Hotels around Kallang, Marina Bay and the Bugis area are a short MRT ride or taxi away. Changi Airport links to the city by the East-West Line and by taxi.
