## A fruit dock turned furniture market

Puerto de Frutos was once a working port. Boats from the islands of the Paraná Delta came in here with citrus, timber and wicker for the Buenos Aires markets. The produce trade has mostly gone. The river frontage, the docks and the delta's craft traditions are still here.

What you walk through now is a sprawl of covered pavilions and open lanes running along the Río Luján. Stalls sell:

- **Mimbre** (wicker) and cane furniture, baskets and lamps, the delta's best-known craft
- Wooden kitchenware, mate gourds and bombillas
- Leather goods, textiles and home décor
- Dried fruit, jams, alfajores and other edible souvenirs

It's one of the most-reviewed attractions in Buenos Aires Province, so don't expect it to yourself. Plan on 1.5–3 hours of browsing.

## Getting there from Buenos Aires

Take the train. The Mitre line runs from Retiro to Tigre station in roughly an hour. From there it's around a 15-minute walk west through town, crossing the Río Tigre, to Sarmiento 160.

Another option is the Tren de la Costa. It follows the river through San Isidro and terminates at Delta station, next to Parque de la Costa. It's slower and more scenic, and it lands you in the same compact centre.

Most visitors combine the market with a ride on the delta. Lanchas colectivas (commuter launches) and tour boats leave from the Estación Fluvial near the train stations. Nearby you'll also find:

- Museo de Arte Tigre, in the former Tigre Club building on Paseo Victorica
- Museo Naval de la Nación
- The riverside promenade along Paseo Victorica

## Timing your visit around the crowds

Crowd data gives a clear answer. It's quietest between 10am and 12pm on weekdays, when you can see the furniture properly and talk with stallholders.

Don't come on a weekend afternoon if you can help it. From 12pm to 8pm on Saturdays and Sundays the lanes are at their busiest, and Porteño day-trippers arrive by the trainload. Opening hours stretch to 8pm at weekends, compared with 6pm on weekdays. If a weekend is your only option, arrive right at 10am.

If you're joining a boat trip, browse the market first and board in the afternoon. That way you skip the peak instead of returning straight into it.

## Paying and bargaining at the stalls

Carry Argentine pesos in cash. Many stallholders give a better price for **efectivo** (cash) than for card. Bank transfers and QR payments through Mercado Pago are also widely accepted. Prices go on handwritten signs or you ask for them, so ask: "¿Cuánto sale?"

Light bargaining is normal when you buy several pieces. Hard haggling over a single basket isn't. Ask "¿Me hacés un precio si llevo dos?" (Will you give me a price if I take two?)

If you fall for a wicker armchair, ask whether the stall ships it or packs it flat before you pay. Carrying bulky furniture onto a crowded Sunday Mitre train is the costly mistake here.

Food stalls and parrilla stands near the water sell choripán and similar quick lunches. Eat before noon at weekends and you'll beat the main rush.
