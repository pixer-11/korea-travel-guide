## A glasshouse on top of the City

The lift doors open and London drops away below you. Sky Garden fills the top floors of 20 Fenchurch Street, the tower Londoners call the Walkie Talkie because it widens as it rises. Up here the floors stack in terraced slopes of planting, with ferns and fig trees, climbing up both sides beneath a great curved roof of glass and steel.

The space is open-plan and layered. Stepped garden beds rise toward the back, and there's a broad viewing floor along the glass. An outdoor terrace faces south over the Thames. Upstairs, the editorial listing notes a top-floor restaurant, and there's a bar where you can sit with the skyline beside you.

## What you're actually looking at

The views are why you queue. The south side looks across the river to the Shard, which feels close enough to touch. Turn east for Tower Bridge and the stone walls of the Tower of London. Westward, the dome of St Paul's Cathedral rises above the office blocks.

On the north side you look into the City's tight cluster of towers, the Gherkin among them. Because the glass wraps around the room, you can make a full circuit and pick out landmarks without craning. Bring a lens cloth: you'll be shooting through glass everywhere except the terrace.

## Booking, security and the lift ride

This is the part that trips people up. Access to the garden itself is free, but you need a timed ticket booked in advance on the Sky Garden website. Slots, especially around sunset, get snapped up well ahead. Walk-up entry is sometimes possible when it's quiet, but don't build a day around it.

At ground level you go through airport-style security, with bags scanned and pockets emptied. Carry photo ID that matches your booking. Arrive close to your slot time, as early arrivals may be held back. A fast lift then carries you up to the garden floors.

Booking a table at one of the restaurants or the bar also gets you in. If you want a meal with the view, that's a separate reservation through the same site.

## Beating the crowds on Fenchurch Street

This is one of London's most-visited free viewpoints, so timing matters more than anything else. On weekdays it's typically quietest 8am–9am and again 9pm–11pm. At weekends, the calm window runs longer in the morning, 8am–11am, with another lull 9pm–11pm.

What that means in practice:

- **Early morning slot:** clear sightlines along the glass and space on the terrace for photos.
- **Late evening slot:** the city lit up, and fewer people packed against the windows.
- **Midday and sunset:** expect people three deep along the south side and a wait for the terrace.

Google currently shows the building open 8am to midnight, and until 1am on Fridays and Saturdays. Those hours cover the bars and restaurants. Public garden times differ and shift through the year, so check this month's schedule on the official site before you book.

## Getting to Sky Garden Walk

The entrance is at 1 Sky Garden Walk, EC3M 8AF, on the Philpot Lane side of the tower. The nearest Tube is **Monument** (District and Circle lines), roughly five minutes on foot. **Bank** (Central, Northern, Waterloo & City and DLR) and **Tower Hill** (District and Circle) are both within easy walking distance. National Rail trains stop at **Fenchurch Street**, just around the corner.

The City is busy with office workers on weekdays and noticeably quieter at weekends. Expect fewer cafés open nearby on a Sunday morning.

## Pairing it with the streets below

You're surrounded by older London here, and it's worth pairing the two. A few minutes north, Leadenhall Market's painted Victorian roof arches over cobbled lanes. Head south toward the river and you'll reach the Monument, Wren's column marking the Great Fire of 1666, which you can climb for a very different view.

Just east is St Dunstan-in-the-East, a bombed-out church whose ruined walls now hold a small public garden. Further on, the Tower of London and Tower Bridge make an easy afternoon. A morning slot at Sky Garden followed by the Tower is a natural loop on foot.
