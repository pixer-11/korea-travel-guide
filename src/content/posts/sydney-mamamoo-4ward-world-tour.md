## Two arenas, four days

Australia gets two nights of the 4WARD tour. MAMAMOO play Melbourne's John Cain Arena on September 22, 2026, then Sydney's TikTok Entertainment Centre on September 25. Some fans fly between the two cities and catch both shows.

MAMAMOO debuted in 2014 under RBW. Solar, Moonbyul, Wheein and Hwasa built their name on live vocals rather than lip-sync, and that is why their concerts lean heavily on harmonies and ad-libs. The tour name refers to the four members moving forward together.

## The Olympic Park arena

The TikTok Entertainment Centre is the indoor arena at Sydney Olympic Park, known for years as Qudos Bank Arena. It sits about 15 km west of the CBD, beside the 2000 Olympic precinct. In arena configuration it holds roughly 21,000 people, the largest indoor concert room in the city.

The seats rise in steep tiers around a flat floor. Floor sections put you closest to the stage. The lower bowl gives a clean, raised sightline to the whole production. Ticketing runs through the venue's official partner, which sells by section using a seat map.

## Getting to Olympic Park

Most concertgoers come by train, and the walk from the station is short. The usual options:

- **Train:** Olympic Park station is on the T7 line, which shuttles from Lidcombe; Lidcombe connects to Central on the T1 and T2 lines. On major event nights, Sydney Trains often adds direct services from Central.
- **Car:** Sydney Olympic Park has large multi-level car parks. Exits back onto the M4 are slow straight after a full-house show.
- **Rideshare:** There is a designated pickup zone in the precinct, and demand climbs sharply as the arena empties.

Many visitors stay in the CBD or around Parramatta. Both have direct rail links and plenty of food open late.

## How a MooMoo night runs

MAMAMOO's fans call themselves MooMoos, and the crowd behaves like a K-pop crowd. Official lightsticks fill the bowl, and fan chants are shouted on cue. That means members' names in the intros and set call-backs during the choruses. Fans usually learn the chants from fan-made guides that circulate online before the tour.
