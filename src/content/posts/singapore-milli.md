## Why Milli, and why now

Rooftop bars in Singapore are not scarce — the skyline practically demands them — but Milli's position sets it apart from the usual Marina Bay Sands or CBD tower crowd. It sits directly above the National Gallery Singapore, the restored former Supreme Court and City Hall buildings, which means the view isn't just glass towers; it's the green sweep of the Padang, the dome of the old Supreme Court, St Andrew's Cathedral's white spire, and the Marina Bay skyline stacking up behind it all.

Having opened in late May 2026, Milli is new enough that the city's dinner-reservation grapevine is still buzzing about it, and the two-storey layout — restaurant below, bar and lounge above, or some variation of that split — gives it more range than a single-concept rooftop bar. You come here as much for the framing of the city as for what's in the glass.

## Getting there

Milli sits in the Civic District, on top of the National Gallery Singapore building at the corner of St Andrew's Road and Coleman Street. The easiest approach by MRT is City Hall station (served by both the North-South and East-West lines), which lets out within a short walk of the Gallery's entrances — follow signs toward the Padang or St Andrew's Cathedral.

Raffles Place and Esplanade stations are also close enough for a ten-minute walk if you're coming from those directions. Because the venue sits inside a national monument, access to the rooftop is generally via a dedicated lift or entrance separate from the museum's ticketed galleries — ask at the ground floor if you're unsure which door to use, since signage at a newly opened venue can still be catching up with foot traffic.

## What to expect once you're up there

Being new, Milli hasn't published a settled menu that's worth repeating here, and pricing at rooftop venues in this category typically sits on the pricier side of Singapore's dining scene — think a special-occasion spot rather than a casual weeknight bite. What you can bank on is the split personality of the space: a proper restaurant floor for a sit-down meal, and a bar/lounge level above it clearly built for evening drinks and skyline-gazing rather than quick pass-through visits.

Dress the part — rooftop venues attached to a national monument in this part of town tend to skew smart-casual to dressy, especially after dark. Given the Gallery's neoclassical bones, expect the design to play with that contrast: colonial architecture underfoot, contemporary bar culture on top.

## When to go

Aim for the transition between late afternoon and early evening, when the sun is dropping behind the CBD towers and the Padang's grass turns that deep amber that Instagram was invented for — you'll get the daylight skyline and then watch it flip to lit-up towers without changing seats. Weeknights are your best shot at a walk-in feel; weekends, especially Friday and Saturday after 7pm, are when a rooftop this new and this well-located fills up fastest.

Because exact opening hours haven't stabilized publicly yet, treat any hours you find online as provisional and double-check directly with Milli or through its booking platform before you plan an evening around it.

## How to visit like a local

Reserve ahead — a rooftop this newly opened, in this location, on top of a national museum, is not the kind of place you turn up to on spec and expect a table with a view. If you can, book for the golden-hour slot rather than peak dinner rush; you'll get better light and a calmer room before the after-work crowd arrives.

Payment across Singapore's bar and restaurant scene is almost universally cashless — tap-to-pay cards and mobile wallets are standard, and a service charge plus GST is typically already built into the bill, so additional tipping isn't expected the way it is in the US. The mistake newcomers make at Civic District rooftops is confusing the museum's opening hours with the rooftop venue's — Milli operates as an independent restaurant/bar business, so its hours, entrance, and lift access run separately from National Gallery ticketing.

Confirm the correct entrance when you arrive rather than joining a gallery queue by mistake, and if you want a rail-side table for photos, mention it when booking rather than hoping for it on arrival.

## Nearby, before or after

Milli's location does the pairing for you. The National Gallery's own atrium and cafés are downstairs if you want to fold in some art before your reservation, and the Padang, St Andrew's Cathedral, and the old Parliament House are all a short stroll away for a pre-dinner walk. Raffles Hotel and the Esplanade waterfront are close enough to combine into one Civic District evening, bookended by Milli's view from above.
