## Daimyo treasures on the castle side

This is a museum for close looking. The Hayashibara Museum of Art keeps one of Okayama's most focused collections of samurai-era craft: traditional weaponry, Noh costumes and Japanese lacquer pieces. Much of it is tied to the Ikeda family, the feudal lords who held Okayama Castle for most of the Edo period.

The founder was Hayashibara Ichirō, an industrialist who built up the collection. The museum opened in the 1960s on land that once belonged to the castle grounds. Its address is 2-chōme-7-15 Marunouchi, Kita Ward, and the castle keep is a few minutes away on foot.

## What fills the cases

Expect small galleries and objects you have to lean in to read. The collection is broad, but because textiles and lacquer fade under light, only part of it is on display at any time. Each exhibition picks a theme from the holdings, so what you see depends on when you visit.

Things to look out for:

- **Noh costumes**: heavy silk robes woven with gold and coloured thread, made for a stage seen in dim light.
- **Lacquerware**: maki-e boxes and trays with gold powder set into black lacquer, often carrying family crests.
- **Swords and fittings**: blades shown bare under glass, plus tsuba (hand guards) worked as miniature metal art.
- **Armour and daimyo household goods**: pieces made to show rank as much as to protect.

Labels are mostly in Japanese. A translation app's camera mode helps with the longer panels.

## Reaching Marunouchi

Getting there is easy. From JR Okayama Station, take the Okayama Electric Tramway (Higashiyama line) to **Shiroshita** stop. The walk east toward the castle takes about five minutes. If you'd rather walk the whole way from the station, it takes roughly 20–25 minutes along Momotarō-ōdōri.

The museum sits in the city's main cultural cluster, which makes a half-day loop simple:

- **Okayama Castle**, the black-walled "Crow Castle", just across the moat.
- **Kōrakuen**, one of Japan's three great landscape gardens, over the Asahi River from the castle.
- **Okayama Prefectural Museum of Art** and **Okayama Orient Museum**, both a short walk north.

## Visiting a rotating-exhibition museum

The calendar matters more than the clock. The museum closes for days while one exhibition comes down and the next goes up. Check the current show and this month's hours on the official website before you set out. Hours change through the year and around holidays, so don't count on the times you see in a listing.

Inside, the usual Japanese museum etiquette applies:

- Photography is generally not allowed in the galleries. Look for signs at the entrance and ask staff if you're unsure.
- Keep your voice low. In small rooms, a conversation carries.
- Stow large bags in a locker if there is one, and leave space at the cases for the next visitor.
- Pens are often discouraged near exhibits. Use a pencil if you want to sketch or take notes.

The current admission price is posted at the entrance and on the official site. Pair your visit with the castle first, then cross to Kōrakuen. That order keeps the walking in one direction and takes you through three centuries of Ikeda history in a single morning.
