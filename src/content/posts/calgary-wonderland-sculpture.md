## A face made of air

From half a block away, the head looks solid. A young girl sits with her eyes closed, calm and enormous, at the foot of a glass skyscraper. Walk closer and she starts to come apart. The face is a grid of bent white steel wire, and you can see straight through her cheeks to the tower, the traffic and the sky.

This is *Wonderland*, by the Barcelona-based sculptor Jaume Plensa. It was installed in 2013 on the plaza of The Bow, the curved, diagrid-braced office tower designed by Foster + Partners. The editorial line calls it a 12-metre bent-wire girl's head, and that's accurate. What the line leaves out is how much it changes with distance, light and season.

## Where it sits in downtown Calgary

The sculpture is at 110 6 Ave SE, on the east side of the downtown core. The Bow rises behind it like a bent sail, and its crescent of glass mirrors the sky, which makes a soft backdrop for the wire.

Getting there is easy:

- **CTrain:** City Hall / Bow Valley College station on 7 Ave SE is a short walk south. Red and Blue Line trains both stop there.
- **On foot from Stephen Avenue:** walk north from 8 Ave SW to 6 Ave and turn east. It takes a few minutes.
- **From the Calgary Tower:** head north on Centre Street.

Locals know Plensa's work, but plenty of visitors walk past the plaza on their way somewhere else. If you come at the right time, it still feels like a quieter find.

## Stepping inside the head

You don't just look at *Wonderland*. You can walk into it. There are openings at the base, and once you step through, the hollow of the skull curves up over you like a lattice dome.

Look up from inside and the wire grid frames the upper floors of The Bow. The criss-cross of the mesh lines up loosely with the tower's own diagonal bracing. It's the shot most people come for, and it works best with a wide lens or a phone's ultra-wide setting.

Inside, sound changes very little because the head is mostly air. You still feel cut off from the plaza, though: the wire lines your field of view in every direction.

## Light, snow and the hour you choose

The plaza is outdoors and currently listed as open 24 hours, so timing is up to you. Each part of the day suits a different visit.

- **Morning:** low sun throws the shadow of the mesh across the paving. On weekends, 7am–11am is the quietest window, and you may get the inside of the head to yourself.
- **Late afternoon into evening:** on weekends, 4pm–11pm is the busiest stretch. Expect queues of people waiting for their turn inside the frame.
- **Winter:** snow catches on the wire and picks out the face against a grey sky. Calgary cold is real, so plan for a short stop.

Because the plaza belongs to a working office building, maintenance or events can sometimes limit access. Check before you plan a dawn shoot around it.

## Sharing the plaza with the office crowd

*Wonderland* sits on the forecourt of a working tower, so people here use the space as well as photograph it. A few habits make the visit smoother for you and everyone around you.

- **Take turns inside.** People naturally line up for the interior shot. Take yours, step out, and let the next group in rather than staging a long shoot.
- **Keep the entrances clear.** The paths across the plaza are how office workers reach The Bow. Shoot from the side, not across the doors.
- **Don't climb the wire.** The mesh is art, not a jungle gym. Touching it lightly as you pass is one thing. Hauling yourself up it is another.
- **Tripods:** in busy hours, a tripod blocks the flow. Save it for the weekend 7am–11am window.

There's nothing to pay and no one to tip. Your only cost is patience in the evening rush.

## How long to stay

Most people spend 15–30 minutes here. That covers a walk around the outside, a few minutes inside and a step back to frame the head against the tower.

Photographers chasing a particular light could stay longer, but the sculpture rewards a short, well-timed visit more than a long one. Treat it as one stop on a downtown walk rather than a destination you need to clear half a day for.

## Building a walk around it

The sculpture sits within easy walking distance of several downtown sights, so it slots neatly into a loop.

- **Stephen Avenue (8 Ave SW):** the pedestrian street with sandstone facades, shops and patios, a few blocks south.
- **Calgary Tower:** the observation tower on Centre Street, close enough to pair with Wonderland on the same morning.
- **East Village and Studio Bell:** head east toward the riverside neighbourhood and the National Music Centre's curved, tiled building.
- **Chinatown:** a short walk north, a good choice for a meal after an early visit.
- **The Bow River pathways:** follow the river west toward Prince's Island Park if you want to stretch the walk out.

A good sequence: Wonderland at weekend opening light, coffee and breakfast in Chinatown, then the river path while the plaza fills up behind you.
