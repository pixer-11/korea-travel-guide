## Two towers, one surviving gate

Porta Soprana is what's left of Genoa's 12th-century defensive walls: a pair of tall brick towers flanking a stone archway on Via di Porta Soprana. It was built around 1155, part of a ring of fortifications thrown up when the maritime republic needed to protect its landward flank. The current look, crenellations and all, comes from a 19th- and 20th-century restoration rather than untouched medieval stonework, so what you see is a careful reconstruction layered over genuine foundations.

The gate straddles a small rise above the old port, and the two cylindrical towers still frame the road exactly as they did for carts and merchants entering the city centuries ago. Walk through the arch itself; it costs nothing and it's the whole point of the stop.

## What's stacked around it

The site rewards five extra minutes of looking sideways, not just up.

- **Casa di Colombo**: a stone house right beside the gate, traditionally identified as Christopher Columbus's childhood home (the claim is debated, but it's the accepted local marker).
- **Cloister of Sant'Andrea**: a quiet 12th-century cloister with paired columns, tucked just downhill.
- **Piazza Dante**: the modern square below, ringed by 1930s office blocks that make the medieval gate look even more marooned in time.

None of these charge admission, and all three sit within a two-minute walk of each other.

## When to come

Porta Soprana has no gate hours because it's an open monument, not a ticketed one: you can walk through at 7am or midnight and find it unlocked and unstaffed. Weekend evenings between 5pm and 11pm are the busiest stretch, when the area fills with people heading to nearby bars and the Sant'Andrea steps become a informal hangout spot. If you want the towers to yourself for photos, aim for a weekday morning instead, before the aperitivo crowd arrives.

There's no ticket booth and no queue system: this is a public street monument, so you simply walk under the arch like any local commuter.

## Finding it on foot

Porta Soprana sits an easy walk from Genoa's main square, and the approach is part of the experience.

- From Piazza De Ferrari, walk south on Via Dante for about 5 minutes; the towers appear at the top of a short rise.
- The gate marks the boundary between the old city and the modern quarter, so it's a natural stop between Genoa's cathedral district and the Porto Antico waterfront.
- No parking directly at the site; use the Sottoripa or De Ferrari garages and walk in.

Locals treat the gate as a through-route rather than a destination, cutting under the arch on the way to the metro or a coffee bar, so don't expect ceremony. Follow that habit: walk through rather than around, glance up at the towers, and keep moving toward Sant'Andrea's cloister or down into the old town's caruggi, the narrow lanes that start just beyond the gate.
