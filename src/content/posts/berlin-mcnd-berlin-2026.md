## Getting to Hole 44

Hole 44 is on Hermannstraße in northern Neukölln. The U8 runs straight underneath that street, so the trip is simple from most of the city.

- **U8 Leinestraße**: the closest stop, a few minutes' walk along Hermannstraße.
- **U8 Boddinstraße**: one stop north, also walkable.
- **S+U Hermannstraße**: the Ringbahn (S41/S42) meets the U8 here, a little further south. This is the easiest change if you're staying anywhere on the Ring.
- **From Alexanderplatz**: the U8 runs direct, no change needed.

Neukölln and Kreuzberg are both within walking distance or one U8 stop of the venue. Visitors who want a short trip home after the encore usually stay around there.

## The Nikolaustag night: what December 6 means in Berlin

December 6 is Nikolaustag, St Nicholas Day. German children put out a boot the night before and find it filled with sweets in the morning. You'll see chocolate Nikoläuse stacked in every supermarket that week.

The date also falls during Christmas market season. The markets usually open in late November, so in early December the stalls at Gendarmenmarkt, Alexanderplatz and outside Charlottenburg Palace are all running. Out-of-town fans often spend the afternoon at one before heading south to Neukölln.

The sun sets before 4pm in a Berlin December. So the evening is properly dark long before any club doors open. It's cold enough that the coat question below matters.

## Sunday rules that catch visitors out

A Sunday concert in Germany comes with a few quirks worth knowing.

Most shops are shut on Sundays by law. Supermarkets, drugstores and clothing shops close, and that includes the place where you meant to buy batteries for your lightstick. Station shops inside major hubs like Hauptbahnhof and Friedrichstraße, plus the corner Spätis (late-night kiosks) common across Neukölln, are where Berliners go for snacks, water and drinks on a Sunday.

The U-Bahn runs through the night only on Friday and Saturday nights. After a Sunday show, the late U8 thins out and is eventually replaced by the N8 night bus along roughly the same route. Fans heading back to hotels in the west usually look up the night-bus connection on the BVG app before the encore.

Inside, a few habits make the night go smoothly:

- **Coats**: a Berlin club cloakroom (Garderobe) is usually a separate, paid counter. Many Berlin venues still lean towards cash at small counters, so fans tend to carry a few coins as well as a card.
- **Cups and bottles**: Germany runs a deposit system called Pfand. Returning a cup or bottle to the bar gets the deposit back.
- **Lightsticks and fan chants**: the official lightstick and the chants are part of the show, and German K-pop crowds take them seriously. Learning the chants for the singles is the easiest way to feel part of the room.
- **Phones**: at a small venue, a phone held high blocks someone shorter behind you. Regulars usually film a song or two, then put the phone down.
