## Holiday stalls on Front Street

This is a free outdoor holiday market. It sets up at St. Lawrence Market, the 19th-century food hall at Front and Jarvis Streets in Toronto's Old Town. It runs Thursdays through Sundays from November 19, and the season ends on December 22, 2026.

The setting carries much of the appeal. The South Market's red-brick front faces Front Street, and inside it is one long hall of butchers, cheesemongers and bakers. An outdoor market beside it makes the food hall and the holiday stalls one stop.

## Dates, tickets and venue

There is no ticket. Admission is free, and you walk in from the street.

- **Dates:** November 19 – December 22, 2026
- **Days:** Thursdays through Sundays
- **Venue:** St. Lawrence Market, 93 Front Street East, Old Town Toronto
- **Admission:** free

St. Lawrence Market posts its events and vendor news on its official website. Individual stalls set their own prices, and most show them at the booth.

## Reaching Old Town

The closest subway stop is King Station on Line 1. From there it is roughly a 10-minute walk east along King Street to Jarvis, then two blocks south to Front.

Union Station, also on Line 1 and served by GO Transit and UP Express, is about 15 minutes west along Front Street. The 504 King streetcar stops at Jarvis Street. Visitors from outside the city usually arrive by GO train rather than drive, because street parking in Old Town is scarce on weekends.

Within a few minutes' walk you'll find:

- **Berczy Park**, with its dog fountain, just west of the market
- **The Gooderham Building**, the narrow 1892 "Flatiron" at Front and Wellington
- **St. James Cathedral**, its spire visible up Church Street
- **St. Lawrence Hall**, the domed 1850 hall at King and Jarvis
- **The Distillery District**, about 15 minutes east on foot, which has its own holiday market

## Eating your way through the market

Regulars treat the winter market and the South Market as one outing. They usually eat indoors and shop outside. The dish people come for is the peameal bacon sandwich: cornmeal-rolled back bacon on a soft bun, a Toronto staple sold in the South Market hall. The South Market keeps its own hours, separate from the outdoor stalls.

Payment works like the rest of Canada. Most vendors take tap payment by debit or credit card. Tipping isn't expected for goods bought at a stall, though a hot drink from a counter with a tip prompt is a judgement call. Most visitors bring a tote or backpack, since many market vendors charge for bags.

Toronto in December means near-freezing air off the lake, and the stalls are outdoors. Locals tend to browse the stalls first, then warm up in the South Market hall. Thursdays and Fridays are workdays for the office towers around Union. Weekends draw more families and visitors from outside the city.
