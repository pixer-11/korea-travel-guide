## Two mountains, depending on the month

The ridge earns its name from its spires. Jagged granite towers line the summit crest, and early settlers thought they looked like a Gothic cathedral. Below them, lifts climb from the base village, Villa Catedral, to slopes topping out above 2,000 metres.

In winter it is one of the largest ski areas in South America. Runs face the lakes, so from the upper lifts you look down on Lago Gutiérrez on one side and the long arms of Nahuel Huapi on the other. On clear days the snow cone of Cerro Tronador shows up to the west.

In summer the snow pulls back and the same lifts carry walkers and mountain bikers. Trails head out across scree and lenga forest. The editorial line on the mountain is accurate: a sizable winter ski centre, summertime trails, lake views throughout.

## Getting up from Bariloche

The drive is short. Take Avenida Bustillo west out of town, then turn south toward Lago Gutiérrez and climb to Villa Catedral. Count on about 30 minutes from the Centro Cívico without snow traffic.

- **Bus:** Mi Bus line 55 runs from downtown to the base. You pay with a SUBE card, not cash, so load one in town first.
- **Car:** Winter rules can require chains on the access road. Rental desks in Bariloche will tell you whether yours includes them.
- **Shuttles:** Many hotels and ski-rental shops run morning transfers in season.

## Passes, queues and the July crush

Start with the calendar. Argentina's school winter holidays fall in July, and Brazilian visitors arrive in numbers across July and August. Those weeks bring the longest lift lines and the fullest rental shops. Late August and September usually mean fewer families on the slopes, though conditions vary year to year.

- **Buy passes online.** The resort sells lift tickets and season prices on its official site, and buying ahead skips the ticket-window line at the base.
- **Rent in town the evening before.** Shops along the road to Catedral and in central Bariloche fit boots at night, so you are not queuing at the base in the morning.
- **Eat on Argentine time.** Mountain restaurants fill around 1–2pm. A late lunch is easier, or an early one.
- **Share the mate.** Groups pass the gourd around on chairlifts and terraces. If someone offers, drink it all and hand it back. Say "gracias" only when you want no more.

## Refugio Frey and the lakes below

This is the summer reason to come. The trail to Refugio Frey starts near the base of Catedral and follows the shore of Lago Gutiérrez before climbing to a stone hut beside Laguna Toncek. The granite spires stand right above the water. Most walkers need about four hours each way, so start in the morning.

You can stay overnight, but space at the refugio is limited in January and February. Contact the Club Andino Bariloche before you go. It also has current trail conditions, and you should register your route there.

Without the full trek, ride the lifts up for the ridge views and walk the upper paths. After that, drive down to the beaches of Lago Gutiérrez, where the water is cold even in January. Back toward town, Cerro Campanario and the Circuito Chico loop round out the day.

Lift schedules and summer operating dates shift with the season and the snow. Check the resort's official site for this month's timetable before you set out.
