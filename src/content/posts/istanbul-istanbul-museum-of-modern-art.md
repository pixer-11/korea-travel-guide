## A gallery with the Bosphorus for a backdrop

Istanbul Modern stands right at the water's edge. Its building, designed by Renzo Piano, opened in 2023 on the same site where the museum first opened in 2004. Now it anchors the southern end of the Galataport waterfront at Tophane. Cruise ships dock nearby, and the Historic Peninsula's skyline fills the view across the water.

The address is Tophane İskele Caddesi, in the Kılıçali Paşa quarter of Beyoğlu. The quarter takes its name from the Kılıç Ali Paşa Mosque, a Mimar Sinan work just up the road. You can pair a mosque and a museum in one short walk.

## What hangs on the walls

The collection covers Turkish and international modern and contemporary art. It traces how artists in Turkey worked through the 20th century and into the present. Alongside the permanent collection, the museum runs rotating temporary exhibitions, so check the official website for what is on.

Photography has long been a strength here, and there's also a cinema. It screens films on a separate programme from the galleries. If you want to catch a screening, check the schedule before you go and plan your gallery time around it.

Things worth building into your visit:

- The rooftop terrace, which looks across the Bosphorus to the Old City
- The temporary exhibition floor, which changes through the year
- The cinema programme
- The museum shop on your way out

## Getting to Tophane

The easiest route is the T1 tram, which runs along the waterfront between Kabataş and the Old City. Get off at Tophane and walk a few minutes toward the water. The Karaköy tram stop is also close, if you'd like a walk through Karaköy's streets first.

Coming from the Asian side, take a ferry into Karaköy pier and walk north along the Galataport promenade. From Sultanahmet, the T1 crosses the Galata Bridge and reaches Tophane in roughly 15 minutes. Galata Tower is uphill from the museum, about a 15-minute climb.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

This is one of the city's best-known museums, and it fills up. At weekends, try not to arrive between 1pm and 6pm. That's the busiest stretch, and it runs right up to closing time.

At weekends, the calm hour is 10am to 11am, straight after the doors open. On weekdays the quietest window is 6pm to 7pm. The museum closes at 6pm Tuesday to Thursday, though, so that hour only works on Friday, when it stays open until 8pm.

The Friday evening slot is the one to aim for. You get emptier galleries, and the light over the Old City starts to change as you reach the terrace. The museum is closed every Monday, which catches out plenty of visitors on short trips. Plan on two to three hours for the galleries and terrace, and longer if you add a film.

## Moving through İstanbul Modern like a regular

Buy your ticket online through the official İstanbul Modern website, or at the desk on arrival. Current prices and any discounts are posted in both places. Cards are accepted as standard in this part of Beyoğlu, so you won't need a stack of lira for the museum itself.

Start at the top and work down if the temporary show is what drew you. Weekend groups tend to cluster around the newest exhibition, so seeing it first while it's quieter pays off. Leave the terrace for the end, when you want to sit.

In Turkish, locals just call it "İstanbul Modern," and taxi drivers know it by that name or by "Galataport." Staff at the desk generally speak English. As in any gallery, keep a respectful distance from the works and ask before photographing anything marked otherwise.

## Around the waterfront afterwards

Walking out, you're in one of the city's most walkable corners. Galataport's promenade runs north along the water toward the Nusretiye Mosque, with its slender Ottoman Baroque minarets beside the Tophane tram stop.

Head south instead and you reach Karaköy's lanes of cafés and the Galata Bridge. Uphill, the streets climb to Galata Tower and on to İstiklal Avenue. The Kılıç Ali Paşa Mosque sits across the tram tracks. It's closed to sightseers during prayer times, so step in between them.
