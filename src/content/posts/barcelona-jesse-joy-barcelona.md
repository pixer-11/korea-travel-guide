This is a mid-sized hall, not an arena. It stands at number 62 on Avinguda del Paral·lel, the old theatre avenue that runs from the port up to Plaça d'Espanya. The venue used to be called BARTS, and longtime Barcelona concertgoers often still use that name.

The size is why people go. Even the back of the room is close to the stage, which suits a duo built on harmonies and acoustic guitar. Tickets for the night were sold through the duo's tour page and the venue's website.

## Getting to Paral·lel

There are three easy options:

- **Metro:** Paral·lel station (L2 and L3) is a short walk along the avenue. Poble Sec station (L3) is the other nearby stop.
- **On foot:** from the Raval or Sant Antoni the walk is flat, about 15–20 minutes from the bottom of La Rambla.
- **Taxi:** drop-offs work directly on the avenue. After shows, cabs gather along Paral·lel.

November 11, 2026 fell on a Wednesday. On weeknights the metro usually stops running around midnight. After that, NitBus night buses cover the city, and several run along Paral·lel or close to it.

Close to the venue are the Funicular de Montjuïc at Paral·lel station and El Molino, the old cabaret with its red windmill. Montjuïc's museums are uphill from there.

## Dinner, drinks and show-night habits

Barcelona eats late. Locals have dinner well after 9pm. Concertgoers usually eat before a show, early by local standards, or wait until after it ends.

Carrer de Blai, one block into Poble-sec, is the usual choice. It is a pedestrian street of pintxo bars where small bites sit on bread with a toothpick. You take what you want, and the staff count the sticks to work out your bill. People order at the bar in Spanish or Catalan ("una canya" for a small beer), and most are understood either way.

Card and contactless payment are normal at bars, the venue and the metro machines. Tipping is small and optional: people usually round up or leave a few coins. Fans often wear the duo's tour shirts.
