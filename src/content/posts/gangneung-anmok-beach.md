## Why go

Anmok Beach isn't Gangneung's most dramatic coastline, but it's the one everyone ends up at anyway. The draw isn't the water — it's the wide band of pale sand backed by a road of glass-fronted coffee shops, each with a deck angled toward the East Sea. You come here to walk, drink something, and watch the horizon do its thing.

This stretch of Gangwon-do coast is flat, open, and easy on the eyes rather than dramatic. Pine trees break up the skyline behind the cafe strip, and the sand is soft enough to walk barefoot even in shoes-off winter light.

## The coffee street

Gangneung calls itself Korea's coffee capital, and Anmok's cafe street is the reason why. Coffee Cupper Bohemian, one of the pioneers of Gangneung's coffee-roasting scene, opened its original branch right along this beach.

Dozens of shops have followed, ranging from minimalist third-wave roasters to multi-floor cafes with rooftop seating. Grab a cup and take it down onto the sand — nobody blinks at coffee on the beach here.

- Order a hand-drip single-origin if you want what the street is actually known for
- Window seats and rooftop decks face the water directly
- Prices are mid-range for Korea, more café than convenience store

## What to see and do

Beyond the coffee, Anmok is a walking beach. The sand is wide enough for a proper stroll even at high tide, and a paved promenade runs parallel for joggers and cyclists.

Look for the small pier area near Anmok Port, where local fishing boats still come in — a reminder this stretch has a working-harbor side, not just a café one. The beach also connects north along the coast toward Gyeongpo Beach, Gangneung's larger and more resort-style neighbor, via a walkable shoreline path.

Swimming is possible in summer but not the main event; currents and water clarity are ordinary East Sea conditions, not turquoise-postcard stuff.

## Getting there

Anmok Beach sits on Gangneung's coast, a short taxi or bus ride from Gangneung Station, the KTX terminus that put this city within a two-and-a-half-hour direct train ride of Seoul.

Local buses run from downtown Gangneung directly to the Anmok area — ask your accommodation for the current route number, since city bus numbering shifts occasionally. A taxi from the station takes roughly 15 minutes depending on traffic.

The beach itself is open 24 hours every day, so there's no gate or ticket to worry about, only the cafés' own hours.

## When to go

Anmok is a very popular stop on the Gangneung coastal circuit, so expect company, especially on summer weekends and around sunrise photo hours. Early morning — just after sunrise — is when the sand is calmest and the light on the water is softest.

Midday through early evening on weekends brings the heaviest cafe-street foot traffic. Weekday mornings or the tail end of a weekday afternoon are your best bet for a quieter walk and an actual seat at a window table.

Autumn and winter strip away the crowds almost entirely, leaving a cold, clear, walkable beach and cafés that are far easier to get into.

## How to visit like a local

Don't treat Anmok as a swim stop — treat it as a walk-and-coffee stop, which is how most people actually use it.

- **Timing:** go early morning or on a weekday afternoon; weekend midday is when the coffee street gets genuinely crowded
- **Paying:** cards and mobile pay (Naver Pay, Kakao Pay) are accepted everywhere along the strip; cash isn't necessary
- **Ordering:** cafés use standard Korean coffee-shop ordering — pick up a number or order at the counter, no table service in most spots
- **Etiquette:** taking coffee onto the sand is completely normal; just pack out your cup
- **Common mistake:** visitors often stop at the first café they see near the bus stop and never walk the full strip — the better-regarded roasters are often a few minutes further down

Combine it with Gyeongpo Beach and the coffee museum nearby for a full half-day loop rather than treating Anmok as a standalone stop.
