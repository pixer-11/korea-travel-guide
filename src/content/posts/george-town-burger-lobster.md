## A London chain lands on a shophouse street

Lebuh Kimberley is a working street of trishaw repair stalls, kopitiams and pre-war shoplots, not a dining district built for tourists. Burger & Lobster's arrival here, its third Malaysian branch after openings elsewhere in the country, is notable precisely because of that mismatch. The building keeps its original shoplot shell: narrow frontage, deep floor plan, upper-floor shutters typical of George Town's UNESCO-listed core.

That heritage skin matters more here than at a typical branch. You're eating a London-born lobster-roll concept under beams and party walls that predate the brand by a century.

## What's actually on offer

The draw is the brand's signature format: lobster rolls and whole Atlantic lobster, prepared the way the UK original made its name. Menu items and prices aren't fixed publicly enough to quote reliably, and both can shift between openings and cities.

- Confirm the current menu and pricing directly before you go
- Ask whether lobster is flown in fresh or frozen that week, since availability affects both
- Check if the shoplot format means limited seating versus the brand's larger outlets

## Finding it on Lebuh Kimberley

Lebuh Kimberley runs through George Town's old commercial quarter, walkable from Lebuh Chulia and the clan jetties area. It's a street best reached on foot or by short trishaw or Grab ride from central heritage hotels; parking directly outside is scarce, as with most shoplot rows in the zone.

Because this is a recent opening, treat the practical details as fluid:

- Verify opening hours and whether the kitchen runs continuous service or splits lunch and dinner
- Call or check the brand's official channels for reservations, especially for dinner
- Have a backup plan for a table if you arrive without booking

## Eating well on a heritage shoplot street

Lebuh Kimberley itself sets the pace: this is a street where hawker stalls open early and close by mid-afternoon, so a dinner-hour restaurant like this one is filling a gap rather than competing with the street's own rhythm. Expect the room to skew toward a younger, Instagram-aware crowd drawn by the brand name as much as the setting.

Dress is casual, as with the UK originals. Payment at Malaysian branches of international chains is almost always by card or e-wallet as well as cash, but carry some ringgit regardless, since older shoplot-street businesses nearby often don't take cards.

If you want the heritage building without the queue, arrive right as service starts rather than at the peak dinner window; new openings in George Town tend to draw the heaviest crowds on Friday and Saturday nights. Pair the visit with a walk down Lebuh Kimberley itself, since the trishaw workshops and old signage are worth ten minutes before or after your meal.
