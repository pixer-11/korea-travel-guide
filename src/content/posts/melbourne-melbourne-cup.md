## A 3,200-metre race, a whole city on holiday

It's called "the race that stops a nation," and that's close to literal. On Tuesday, 3 November 2026, a field of stayers runs 3,200 metres around Flemington Racecourse. In metropolitan Melbourne, Cup Day is a public holiday. Across the rest of Australia, offices run sweeps and pubs put the race on every screen.

The Cup was first run in 1861. It is a handicap, which means horses carry different weights. That's why outsiders win often enough to make the betting interesting. The race is the centrepiece of the Spring Racing Carnival. Derby Day falls on the Saturday before it, Oaks Day on the Thursday after, and Stakes Day closes the week.

## Reaching Flemington Racecourse

Flemington sits beside the Maribyrnong River in the inner northwest. On race days it has its own railway platform.

- **Train:** race-day services run from Southern Cross and Flinders Street to Flemington Racecourse station, right at the gates. Tap on with a myki card.
- **Tram:** route 57 runs along Racecourse Road. From the stop it's a short walk.
- **Car:** parking is limited and the roads out jam after the last race. Don't drive if you plan to bet and drink.
- **Taxi/rideshare:** use the marked set-down zones. Expect a long wait to leave.

## Choosing where you stand

Your ticket decides your day. Ticket types include general admission, reserved seating and hospitality packages. The Members' Reserve is for VRC members and their guests. The Birdcage holds invitation-only marquees.

General admission puts you on the lawns and along the rail. The famous rose gardens are along the track there too, and on Cup Day the roses are usually in bloom. For a view of the finish, you need a spot near the winning post early. Big screens around the course carry the race wherever you end up.

Prices and ticket types are listed on the official Flemington/VRC site, and that's the only place to check them. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site.

## Cup Day dress codes and betting

Fashion matters on Cup Day. Fashions on the Field runs as a formal competition, and the members' areas enforce a dress code. Many racegoers wear suits, and fascinators or hats are common. General admission is more relaxed, but most people still dress up.

How betting works:
- **On course:** place bets at the TAB windows and with the bookmakers in the betting ring.
- **Apps:** most locals bet on their phone through licensed wagering apps.
- **Sweeps:** the office tradition. You draw a horse at random for a small stake.

Things worth knowing:
- Flemington is very exposed in spring, so bring a hat and sunscreen. Pack a layer too, because Melbourne weather changes fast.
- Look up the gate's rules on what you can bring in, including chairs, eskies and alcohol, before you arrive.
- Once the Cup is run, the crowd heads for the station. If you're staying on for the last race, ride the train back later.
