## A 3,200-metre race, a whole city on holiday

It's called "the race that stops a nation," and that's close to literal. The 2026 Cup was set for Tuesday, 3 November 2026, with a field of stayers over 3,200 metres around Flemington Racecourse. In metropolitan Melbourne, Cup Day is a public holiday. Across the rest of Australia, offices run sweeps and pubs put the race on every screen.

The Cup was first run in 1861. It is a handicap, which means horses carry different weights. That's why outsiders win often enough to make the betting interesting. The race is the centrepiece of the Spring Racing Carnival. Derby Day falls on the Saturday before it, Oaks Day on the Thursday after, and Stakes Day closes the week.

## Reaching Flemington Racecourse

Flemington sits beside the Maribyrnong River in the inner northwest. On race days it has its own railway platform.

- **Train:** race-day services go from Southern Cross and Flinders Street to Flemington Racecourse station, right at the gates. Fares are paid by tapping on with a myki card.
- **Tram:** route 57 runs along Racecourse Road. From the stop it's a short walk.
- **Car:** parking is limited and the roads out jam after the last race. Driving is a poor fit for a day of betting and drinking.
- **Taxi/rideshare:** there are marked set-down zones, and the wait to leave is usually long.

## Choosing where you stand

The ticket decides the day. Ticket types included general admission, reserved seating and hospitality packages. The Members' Reserve is for VRC members and their guests. The Birdcage holds invitation-only marquees.

General admission covers the lawns and the rail. The famous rose gardens are along the track there too, and on Cup Day the roses are usually in bloom. Spots near the winning post, with a view of the finish, tend to go early. Big screens around the course carry the race to every part of the grounds.

Prices differed by ticket type, and tickets were sold through the official Flemington/VRC site. General admission was the cheapest way in, with reserved seating and hospitality priced above it.

## Cup Day dress codes and betting

Fashion matters on Cup Day. Fashions on the Field is a formal competition, and the members' areas enforce a dress code. Many racegoers wear suits, and fascinators or hats are common. General admission is more relaxed, but most people still dress up.

How betting works:
- **On course:** bets are placed at the TAB windows and with the bookmakers in the betting ring.
- **Apps:** most locals bet on their phone through licensed wagering apps.
- **Sweeps:** the office tradition. Each person draws a horse at random for a small stake.

Things worth knowing:
- Flemington is very exposed in spring, so hats and sunscreen are standard. Melbourne weather changes fast, and many racegoers carry an extra layer.
- The gates have rules on what can be brought in, including chairs, eskies and alcohol.
- Once the Cup is run, the crowd usually heads for the station. Trains back are quieter after the last race.
