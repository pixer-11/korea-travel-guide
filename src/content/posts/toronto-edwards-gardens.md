## A garden that drops into a ravine

At the Lawrence Avenue East entrance, Edwards Gardens looks like flat lawn and flower beds. Then the ground falls away. Paths step down past rock gardens into the valley of Wilket Creek, and the traffic noise from the road above fades out.

That drop is what makes the place. The top is formal and open. The bottom is shaded, damp and crossed by small footbridges. Most people work their way down slowly and then puff back up.

## From private estate to public park

This was once the country estate of Rupert Edwards, a Toronto industrialist. Metropolitan Toronto bought it in the 1950s and opened it as a public park. The bones of a private garden are still easy to read: stone walls, terraced beds and water features laid out around views, not around sports fields.

Google's short description sums it up well: a former estate garden with wildflowers, perennials and fountains. Beside it sits the Toronto Botanical Garden, which has themed demonstration gardens and a gift shop. You can walk from one to the other without noticing the join.

## What you'll actually walk through

The layout runs roughly from the road down to the creek. Expect:

- **Perennial and annual beds** near the upper level, replanted through the growing season
- **The rockery**, stone-edged terraces of alpine and trailing plants on the slope
- **Fountains and ponds**, popular spots for photos and a natural pause point
- **Wilket Creek and its footbridges**, at the bottom of the valley
- **The Toronto Botanical Garden**, at the corner of Lawrence and Leslie, with its own smaller themed plots

Late spring brings rhododendrons and flowering trees. Midsummer is roses and perennials at full height. Autumn colour in the ravine is a separate reason to come. In winter the beds are bare, but the valley walk still works.

## The wedding-photo weekend

On summer weekends Edwards Gardens fills with bridal parties. Couples in full dress queue for the same bridges and fountain backdrops, with photographers directing traffic. It's cheerful to watch, but it changes the place: the best viewpoints get booked out in slow rotation.

This lines up with the forecast crowd pattern. Weekends are busiest from 11am to 7pm, which covers almost the whole open day. If you want the bridges to yourself, a weekend afternoon is the wrong time.

## Getting there and around

The address is 755 Lawrence Ave E, North York. The gardens sit at the corner of Lawrence Avenue East and Leslie Street, northeast of the city centre.

- **By car:** there's a parking lot off Lawrence. It fills early on fine weekends, so arrive near opening.
- **By TTC:** buses run along both Lawrence Avenue East and Leslie Street and stop at the corner. Use the TTC trip planner to pick your connection from Line 1.
- **On foot or bike:** the Wilket Creek trail heads south from the bottom of the gardens through Wilket Creek Park. It links into the wider Don Valley trail system and towards Sunnybrook Park.

The valley paths are steep in places. Strollers and wheelchairs manage the upper level more easily than the descent to the creek.

## Beating the crowds at Edwards Gardens

Timing makes the biggest difference here. These habits make the visit easier:

- **Go on a weekday.** The calmest windows are 10am–12pm and 6pm–8pm. A weekday evening, with low light in the ravine, is the best version of this place.
- **On weekends, come at 10am.** Busy hours start at 11am, so the first hour is your only quiet one.
- **Check hours before you go.** Google currently shows 10am to 8:30pm daily, but park and garden hours shift with the season. The City of Toronto and Toronto Botanical Garden sites list the current times.
- **Mind the beds.** Stay on paths and off the planted terraces, even for a photo. Staff and volunteers keep these beds up by hand.
- **Professional shoots need a permit.** The City of Toronto issues photography permits for the park. If you're hiring a photographer, ask them about it.
- **Leave dogs leashed** and expect busy paths at the bridges.

The costly mistake is arriving at 1pm on a sunny Saturday expecting a quiet stroll. You'll circle for parking and share every bridge with a wedding party.

## Pairing it with the rest of the day

The gardens work best as one stop in a longer walk. Head south along Wilket Creek and you are quickly in wooded valley rather than a garden. The trail continues towards Sunnybrook Park, with open fields and more ravine paths, so you can make a half-day of it.

If you'd rather stay put, finish at the Toronto Botanical Garden's gift shop and its smaller themed gardens at the Leslie corner. Allow 1.5–2 hours for the gardens alone, or 3–4 if you walk the creek south and back.
