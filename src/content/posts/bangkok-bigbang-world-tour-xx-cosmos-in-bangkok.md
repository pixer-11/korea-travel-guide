## Twenty years, one stadium night

The tour title carries a number. "XX" is the Roman numeral for twenty, and BIGBANG debuted in 2006, two decades before this tour. On November 7, 2026, the group brings the 2026-2027 World Tour [XX : COSMOS] to Rajamangala National Stadium, Thailand's national stadium.

This is a stadium show, not an arena date. Bangkok's K-pop crowds usually fill indoor halls like Impact Arena in Muang Thong Thani. Booking Rajamangala instead signals how large BIGBANG's Thai following is. The fandom is called VIP, and those fans have sung "Fantastic Baby", "Bang Bang Bang" and "Haru Haru" for most of their adult lives.

## Inside Rajamangala

The stadium is a concrete open-air bowl. It was built for the 1998 Asian Games and holds roughly 50,000 people. The pitch is normally home to the Thai national football team. On concert nights it becomes standing zones and floor seating in front of the stage, with tiered stands curving around the other three sides.

Because the bowl is open to the sky, the evening air is part of the show. November falls at the start of Bangkok's cool, dry season, so evenings tend to be warm rather than steamy. Most of the crowd wears light clothing and carries a small fan or towel. From the upper stands, the screens matter as much as the stage, and fans in those seats tend to bring binoculars.

## Getting to Hua Mak and back out

Rajamangala is in Bang Kapi district, inside the Hua Mak Sports Complex and next to Ramkhamhaeng University. No BTS or MRT station sits at the gates. Most concertgoers use one of these routes:

- **Airport Rail Link** to Ramkhamhaeng or Hua Mak station, then a taxi, Grab or motorbike taxi for the last stretch
- **Grab or a metered taxi** all the way from central Bangkok, which costs time on Ramkhamhaeng Road at peak hours
- **Chartered vans** run by fan groups from other provinces

The trip out is harder than the trip in. When 50,000 people leave at once, ride-hailing apps slow down and traffic on Ramkhamhaeng Road stops moving. Many fans walk a few blocks away from the complex and order a car from there. Others wait out the rush at street-food stalls near the university.

## Lightsticks, freebies and the gate routine

Thai fan culture has its own habits, and this show follows them. Outside the gates you will see fan clubs handing out free stickers, slogan banners, photo cards and snacks. These are fan-funded gifts, known as freebies. People queue politely for them, take one item each and usually say thank you in Thai: *khop khun ka* (women) or *khop khun khrap* (men).

The crown-shaped BIGBANG lightstick is the one piece of kit nearly everyone carries. At many Thai K-pop shows the lightsticks sync to the stage, so the whole bowl changes colour together. A few other habits are standard at large Bangkok concerts:

- The name on the ticket often has to match the holder's photo ID, so fans keep a passport or Thai ID card on them
- Professional cameras and detachable lenses are typically refused at the gate
- Fans learn the fan chants for the bigger songs, and the crowd shouts them in Korean

## Where the tickets and details live

The show's promoter publishes the Bangkok date, venue, seating map and ticket links on its official channels. That is the only reliable source for zone layouts and entry rules. Secondhand ticket sales on social media are a known scam route at high-demand Bangkok concerts. Thai fans generally buy only from the official sale or from verified transfer systems.

Seating is sold by zone, from standing areas on the pitch to numbered seats in the stands. The zone names and stage shape vary from tour to tour. The seating plan for this show is the reference for exactly where each zone sits.

## Staying and eating near the stadium

Most out-of-town fans stay somewhere with an easy rail connection rather than beside the stadium. The Airport Rail Link corridor works well: Makkasan connects to MRT Phetchaburi, and Phaya Thai connects to the BTS Sukhumvit Line. Both offer a straight line back towards Ramkhamhaeng after the show.

Around the stadium, the streets by Ramkhamhaeng University are lined with cheap student eateries and night stalls. Fans fill up there before the show on noodles, grilled pork skewers and iced Thai tea. Concert nights also bring food carts along the approach roads. Most fans take a refillable water bottle into the evening heat.
