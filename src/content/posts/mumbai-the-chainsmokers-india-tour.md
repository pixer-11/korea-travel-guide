## Three nights, three cities, Mumbai first

Mumbai opens the run. The Chainsmokers' India tour starts here on December 18, 2026, then goes to Delhi on December 19 and Bengaluru on December 20. That means three cities on three nights in a row.

The New York duo of Alex Pall and Drew Taggart built their sets on big singalong drops. Expect a crowd that knows every word of "Closer", "Don't Let Me Down" and "Something Just Like This". The exact setlist, support acts and stage times aren't in the details we have. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site.

## Finding the venue and tickets

The Mumbai venue appears on the official ticket listing. Check it there before you book anything around the show. This matters more in Mumbai than in most cities. A stadium in Navi Mumbai and an arena in Worli or Bandra Kurla Complex can be over an hour apart in evening traffic.

When you buy, check a few things:

- **Ticket tier and entry gate.** Big Indian shows often split standing, fan-pit and seated sections, each with its own entrance.
- **Age rules.** Many concerts serving alcohol set a minimum age, and some check ID at the gate.
- **Bag policy.** Outside food, power banks and large bags are often restricted.
- **Name on the ticket.** Some platforms ask for matching photo ID at entry.

Only buy through the official ticketing partner. Resale screenshots shared on social media are a well-known scam during big tours.

## December in Mumbai

December is the best month for an outdoor show in Mumbai. The monsoon is long over and the air is dry. Evenings are noticeably cooler than the sticky months, though still warm enough for a T-shirt.

A light layer helps for the late journey home, especially on an open-air ground near the sea. Hydration still matters. A packed general-admission crowd in a coastal city gets hot, whatever the month.

## Getting there and getting home again

Leave earlier than you think you need to. Mumbai's evening rush on the Western Express Highway, the Eastern Freeway and the Bandra-Worli Sea Link can easily double a journey. Show traffic adds to that.

Rail is often the faster option:

- **Mumbai Suburban Railway.** The Western, Central and Harbour lines carry most of the city. Check which station is nearest your venue, and when the last trains leave.
- **Mumbai Metro.** Line 1 runs Versova to Ghatkopar through Andheri. Lines 2A and 7 serve the western suburbs.
- **Ride-hailing.** Uber and Ola work across the city, but prices surge and pickups jam right after an encore.

If you're coming from Delhi or Bengaluru to see more than one date, remember the shows are on consecutive nights. Book morning flights out of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport, not tight same-day connections.

## Getting home after the encore

How you leave decides how the night ends. Before the show, agree on a meeting point that isn't the main gate, like a landmark or a shop a few minutes' walk away. Mobile signal often drops when thousands of phones are in one place. A fixed spot works better than "call me when you're out."

Expect to pay by UPI. Mumbai runs on it, from venue food stalls to the auto-rickshaw outside, and scanning a QR code is normal. Carry some small cash anyway, in case the network struggles under the crowd.

Ask for change at food counters. Walk a block or two away from the venue before you book a cab. Your driver can then reach you without sitting in the exit queue.

If the venue is in Navi Mumbai or another outer area, staying nearby for the night can be easier than crossing the city after midnight. Bandra, Andheri and Lower Parel all have plenty of hotels and stay busy late.

## Making a weekend of it

A Friday-night concert leaves the weekend free. Marine Drive is busiest at sunset, with the curve of the Queen's Necklace lighting up after dark. Colaba Causeway and the Gateway of India are a short drive south.

Bandra's Carter Road promenade and the lanes around Hill Road are good for a slow, late brunch the morning after. If you're doing the full tour, Delhi is the next night. Bengaluru follows on December 20.
