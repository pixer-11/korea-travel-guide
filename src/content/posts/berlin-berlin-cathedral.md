## A green dome over the Lustgarten

Start out on the lawn. From the Lustgarten, the cathedral covers the whole eastern edge of the square. It is a heavy neo-Renaissance pile of grey stone under a ribbed dome of oxidised green copper. To your left is the colonnade of the Altes Museum. Behind you stands the rebuilt palace front of the Humboldt Forum.

The building is newer than it looks. Julius Raschdorff designed it for the Prussian royal house, and it went up between 1894 and 1905. Wartime bombing gutted it, and restoration dragged on through the divided-city decades. The main church reopened only in 1993.

It is still a working Protestant church. It is also one of the most-reviewed sights in Berlin, so it is never quiet by accident. You get a quiet visit by choosing your hour.

## Reaching Museum Island

The easiest way in is the U5. Get off at **Museumsinsel**, which opened in 2021 with a deep blue, star-studded ceiling. You come up a few minutes' walk from the cathedral steps.

Other options:

- **S-Bahn:** Hackescher Markt (S3, S5, S7, S9), then roughly a ten-minute walk over the Spree.
- **Bus 100 or 300:** both stop at Lustgarten, in front of the building.
- **On foot:** follow Unter den Linden east from the Brandenburg Gate and you end up at the Lustgarten. It's a long but easy walk.

The official address is Am Lustgarten, 10178 Berlin. The visitor entrance faces the square.

## Under the 7,269 pipes

The main room is the Sermon Church (Predigtkirche). It is round, gilded and very tall. Mosaics fill the inside of the dome, and the space feels more like a court theatre than a parish church. Take a minute in the pews before you look at anything in detail.

The organ above the entrance is the one to see. Wilhelm Sauer built it for the opening in 1905, and it has 7,269 pipes. That makes it one of the largest instruments of its period in Germany. Ranks of pipes rise behind a carved case, and most visitors miss it at first because they walk in facing away from it.

Near the altar stand the gilded sarcophagi of Frederick I, the first King of Prussia, and his queen Sophie Charlotte. Andreas Schlüter designed both. They give you a preview of what waits in the crypt below.

## The 270 steps to the dome walkway

This climb is what sets the Dom apart from Berlin's other churches. The route to the dome is 270 steps. It is a plain staircase with no lift for the final stretch, and parts of it are narrow. Along the way you pass displays on the building's history and models of the designs that came before it.

At the top, a walkway runs around the outside of the dome. The views take in:

- the Fernsehturm, just across the Spree to the east;
- the Humboldt Forum and the Spree below you;
- the museum roofs and colonnades of Museum Island to the north;
- the line of Unter den Linden heading west.

Allow 20 to 30 minutes for the climb and the walk around. If you struggle with heights or stairs, skip the dome and spend longer downstairs.

## Down among the Hohenzollerns

The crypt sits under the main floor. It holds the family tomb of the Hohenzollerns, who ruled Brandenburg, Prussia and finally the German Empire. Roughly 90 coffins and sarcophagi lie here. They range from richly decorated metal caskets to small, plain ones for children who died young.

It is low, dim and quieter than the church above. Most people walk through in 15 minutes. Stay longer to read the labels and you will see the dynasty's fortunes change across the centuries. In total, the church, dome and crypt take most visitors one and a half to two hours.

## Ticket, timing and the Sunday service

These are the practical rules that matter here.

**Time it by the crowd figures.** Weekdays are calmest from 9am to 11am, and Saturdays from 9am to 10am. Don't plan a weekend visit for 11am to 5pm. Those are the busiest hours, and on Sunday that is the entire visiting day.

**Know the hours.** It opens 9am–6pm Monday to Friday and 9am–5pm on Saturday. On Sunday it opens only from 12pm to 5pm, because the morning belongs to worship. On a two-day weekend trip, go on Saturday at 9am.

**Buy before you queue.** Tickets are sold at the entrance and on the cathedral's official website, which also lists current prices. Booking ahead means you join the entry line rather than the ticket line.

**Behave as you would in church.** Services, prayers and concerts take place throughout the year. Parts of the building may close to sightseers while they happen, so check the official calendar on the day. Keep your voice down in the Sermon Church, and take your hat off.

**Consider a concert instead.** The Sauer organ is played at services and at organ recitals. Hearing it fill the dome tells you more than any photograph of the pipes.

## After the Dom: the island on your doorstep

Once you're out, you are already in the middle of a UNESCO World Heritage site. The **Altes Museum** sits across the lawn, with the **Neues Museum** and the **Alte Nationalgalerie** a short walk north. The grass and fountain of the **Lustgarten** are the obvious place to sit down after the climb.

South of the square, the **Humboldt Forum** fills the rebuilt shell of the Berlin Palace. It has its own rooftop terrace and a different view back at the dome. Over the Spree bridge, the **DDR Museum** sits on the riverbank opposite. The cathedral's green dome stays in sight from almost every corner of the island.
