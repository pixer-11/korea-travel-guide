## What's actually inside

The market occupies a multi-story building at 2-chōme-10-1 Matsuo, Naha, a short walk off Kokusai Street. Ground-floor stalls sell fish, pork cuts, seaweed and Okinawan produce you won't see on mainland Japan: purple sweet potato, goya (bitter melon), rafute pork belly ready-cooked. Upstairs holds small eateries where staff cook the fish or meat you just bought downstairs for a set cooking charge. That "buy downstairs, cook upstairs" system is the whole point of visiting; it's called mise-age.

Look for tanks of ikasumi (squid ink) preparations, whole reef fish like irabucha, and Okinawan pork parts rarely sold elsewhere, pig ear, pig face, blocks of pig knuckle. Vendors will fillet or portion on request. Reviewers consistently flag the seafood counters and the upstairs restaurants as the reason to come, not souvenirs.

## When to go

Hours run 8am to 10pm every day of the week, no midday closure. Crowd patterns show weekends busiest from 11am to 8pm, so arrive before 11am to shop stalls without queuing. Weekday mornings stay calm through the whole 8am-to-10pm window. Evenings after 8pm thin out as stall owners start packing perishables.

This is a well-known stop on Naha itineraries, not an undiscovered spot, so tour groups and cruise-ship visitors do pass through midday. Plan around the 11am–8pm weekend crush rather than expecting to have it to yourself.

## Buying downstairs, eating upstairs

Here's how the mise-age system actually works:

- Pick your fish, shellfish or meat from a ground-floor stall and pay the vendor directly.
- Ask the stall to write or mark what you bought; they'll direct you to a specific upstairs restaurant partnered with them.
- Head upstairs and hand your purchase to the kitchen; they charge a separate cooking fee per dish, sashimi, grilled or fried.
- Cash is the safer bet at the small stalls, though some now take IC cards; carry small bills for the fish counters regardless.
- Point and gesture if you don't speak Japanese; vendors are used to tourists and will hold up fingers for quantities.

Don't wander upstairs first hoping to order off a menu without buying anything below; that's the one sequence that confuses first-timers and slows the whole floor down.

## Getting there and around

The market is walkable from central Naha; Kokusai Street's covered arcades feed directly into the building's side entrances. If you're coming by monorail, the nearest Yui Rail stations are Makishi or Asahibashi, both a short walk. Combine the visit with a stroll down Kokusai Street itself, Naha's main shopping strip, before or after.

Bring a reusable bag if you're buying dried goods or produce to take back to your hotel; stalls typically bag fresh seafood in ice but won't necessarily supply extra carriers for a full haul. Wear shoes you don't mind smelling faintly of fish afterward, the ground floor is a working seafood market first, tourist stop second.
