## Four days of late-November design

The Seoul Design Festival takes place from November 26 to 29, 2026. That is a Thursday-to-Sunday run, which gives the event two weekdays and a full weekend. Seoul is properly cold by late November, so expect coats on the subway and a cloakroom mood inside.

The festival is a design exhibition organised around content that DESIGN magazine has gathered since 1976. You are walking through a print archive turned into a trade floor. Its booths, displays and talks come out of five decades of the magazine covering Korean products, graphics and interiors.

## Fifty years of a magazine, laid out in halls

The magazine is the event's reason for being. DESIGN began publishing in 1976, which makes 2026 its fiftieth year. That anniversary is the arithmetic behind this edition, whatever the halls end up holding.

This background shapes the event. A fair run by a publication tends to be curated like an issue, with sections, featured names and an editorial eye on who gets floor space. You will find less of a sprawling expo and more of a magazine you can walk around in, page by page.

For visitors from abroad, it works as a quick survey of what Korean designers are making right now. The usual mix runs from furniture and lighting to stationery, ceramics, textiles and packaging. Expect a lot of small studios alongside a few larger brands.

## Where it happens: Samseong-dong and COEX

Recent editions of the Seoul Design Festival were held at COEX, the convention and exhibition complex in Samseong-dong, Gangnam-gu. The organiser publishes this year's venue and hall details on its official site.

COEX is easy to reach by subway:

- **Samseong Station (Line 2):** exits 5 and 6 lead into the complex, much of it underground.
- **Bongeunsa Station (Line 9):** exit 7 puts you on the COEX side of Bongeunsa-ro.

The underground link from Samseong Station matters in late November. You can walk from the platform to the exhibition halls without stepping outside, past the shops and food courts of COEX Mall.

## Moving through the booths

Korean design fairs run smoothly, and a few habits make the day easier.

- **Payment:** cards and Korean mobile payments are accepted almost everywhere in Seoul, including at small makers' stands. Contactless foreign cards usually work too.
- **Getting there:** a T-money card covers the subway and buses. Most visitors top one up at a station machine.
- **Photos:** many exhibitors are independent designers showing original work. Visitors usually ask before photographing products up close, and a nod toward the item with your phone raised is enough.
- **Business cards and brochures:** these sit on most stands. Taking one counts as polite interest, not a commitment.

Weekends at exhibitions like this draw students and young families on top of trade visitors. So the Thursday and Friday usually make for easier browsing. Attendees who plan to buy often bring a tote bag, since some smaller stands keep their packaging minimal.

A few Korean words go a long way: *annyeonghaseyo* (hello), *gamsahamnida* (thank you), and *igeo eolmayeyo?* if you want to ask about an item. English is common among designers and staff at Gangnam venues, but greeting in Korean is the norm.

## Tickets and the official programme

The organiser publishes the ticket links, the exhibitor list and the talk schedule on the official Seoul Design Festival site. DESIGN magazine's own channels carry festival coverage too. This guide does not list a lineup or prices because none were given to us. The festival's own pages hold those details.

Ticketed design fairs in Seoul usually offer online purchase through Korean ticketing platforms as well as sales at the venue. Foreign visitors sometimes find the Korean-only purchase pages slow going. A browser translation tool usually handles them well enough.

## How long to give it

Most visitors spend a half day on the halls. Designers, buyers and students who sit in on talks often stay most of the day. Exhibition floors at COEX are large and hard-surfaced, and the booth layout encourages slow zigzagging rather than a straight walk.

The building handles breaks well. COEX Mall's underground food courts and cafés sit steps from the exhibition entrances. So a lunch break doesn't mean leaving the complex or going out into the cold.

## Around COEX before or after

The neighbourhood fills out a design-minded day without much travel.

- **Starfield Library:** the double-height reading space inside COEX Mall, lined with tall bookshelves. It is a short walk through the mall.
- **Bongeunsa:** a Buddhist temple founded in the Silla period, directly across Bongeunsa-ro from COEX. Its giant Maitreya Buddha statue faces the Gangnam towers.
- **Garosu-gil and Apgujeong:** a few subway stops away, with design shops, concept stores and fashion flagships for anyone who wants more after the halls.
- **Seonjeongneung:** Joseon royal tombs in a wooded park near Seolleung Station, one stop west of Samseong on Line 2.

Late November means short days, and the city gets dark early. That suits Gangnam's lit-up shopping streets after the halls more than outdoor sightseeing.
