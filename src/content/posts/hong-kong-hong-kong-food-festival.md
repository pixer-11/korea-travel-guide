## Five days of eating over Christmas

The festival covers Christmas week. It runs from Thursday, December 24 to Monday, December 28, 2026. That takes in Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and the Saturday and Sunday after, so most of the city is off work for at least part of it.

The setting is Wan Chai, the harbourfront district between Central and Causeway Bay where Hong Kong holds most of its big consumer fairs. The organiser publishes the dates, ticket links and exact venue on its official site. Opening hours and admission prices are posted there too, and this guide does not repeat them.

## Reaching Wan Chai by MTR

The MTR is the simplest way in. Three stations sit within walking distance of the Wan Chai waterfront:

- **Wan Chai Station** (Island Line): Exit A5 links to the covered footbridge network running north towards the harbour.
- **Exhibition Centre Station** (East Rail Line): the closest stop to the convention district, one stop from Admiralty, with a direct ride from the New Territories.
- **Admiralty Station** (Island, Tsuen Wan, South Island and East Rail lines): the interchange most visitors use when coming from Kowloon.

Visitors coming from Tsim Sha Tsui often take the Star Ferry to Wan Chai Pier instead. The crossing is short and lands right on the waterfront. The trams on Hennessy Road also run through the district, if you'd rather go slowly.

## Working the aisles like a Hongkonger

Locals treat food fairs as shopping trips as much as tastings. Many bring a foldable trolley or a large tote and leave with dried goods, sauces and snacks for the Lunar New Year cupboard, which comes only weeks later. Here is how they usually handle it:

- **Payment:** in Hong Kong this usually means an Octopus card, contactless bank cards, cash or mobile wallets (PayMe, Alipay HK, WeChat Pay HK). Smaller stalls tend to favour Octopus or cash.
- **Sampling:** stallholders hand out tasters freely. Taking one carries no obligation, but hovering for seconds without buying gets noticed.
- **Queues:** Hong Kong queues in tidy single files. Popular stalls sometimes run separate lines for paying and for collecting.
- **Language:** stall staff speak Cantonese first, though most know enough English for prices and quantities. "M̀h'gōi" (唔該) covers both "please" and "thank you".

Regulars often go on a weekday afternoon rather than Christmas Day or the weekend. Simple logic says the public-holiday dates draw the biggest crowds. Seasoned fair-goers also walk the whole floor once before buying anything.

## Around Wan Chai after the festival

The district rewards an extra hour. Right on the waterfront, Golden Bauhinia Square has the gilded flower statue marking the 1997 handover. The harbour promenade runs west from there with views across to Tsim Sha Tsui.

South of Hennessy Road, old Wan Chai packs into a few blocks:

- **Tai Yuen Street and Cross Street:** street market lanes of produce, dried goods and toy shops.
- **Blue House** on Stone Nullah Lane: a cobalt-painted 1920s tenement, one of the last of its kind on the island.
- **Lockhart Road:** bars and late-night restaurants for dinner after the fair.
- **Hopewell Centre:** its external glass lift climbs above Queen's Road East.

All of these are within a 15-minute walk of Wan Chai Station.
