## Stone, Mound and Cell Block

Nine centuries of locked doors share one site. The Normans raised a timber castle here in 1071 under Robert D'Oyly, then rebuilt it in stone. St George's Tower, a squat block of rubble stone with very few windows, is one of the oldest standing structures in Oxford.

The castle later became a prison, and it held inmates until 1996. Part of the Victorian wing is now the Malmaison hotel, so you'll see guests crossing the same courtyard where tour groups gather. The grassy motte rises behind it all. It's the earthwork you spot from the street before you find the entrance.

## Inside the Tour

You can't wander the interior alone. Entry is by guided tour, and the guides stay in character as figures from the castle's past, telling the stories in first person. Expect:

- **St George's Tower:** a climb up narrow, steep stairs to a rooftop view across Oxford's spires.
- **The crypt:** the 900-year-old vaulted chamber of the castle's chapel of St George, low, cool and lit dimly.
- **The prison wing:** cells, the Debtors' Tower and the story of how Oxford punished its offenders.
- **Empress Matilda:** the tale of her escape in 1142, when she slipped out during a winter siege dressed in white against the snow.

Afterwards you can climb the mound on your own. It's a short, steep path, and from the top you can see the Westgate Centre and the city's rooftops.

## Getting Tickets and Timing It

It's one of Oxford's most-visited attractions, so tours sell out. Book a timed slot on the official website rather than queueing at the desk. That matters most on weekends and in school holidays.

- **Hours:** at the moment it opens 10am–5:30pm, but times change with the season and on some holidays. Check this month's schedule online.
- **Length:** the tour runs roughly an hour. Allow extra time for the mound and the exhibition rooms.
- **Access:** the tower stairs and crypt steps are tight. Ask the desk about step-free options before you book if you need them.
- **Early start:** first-slot tours usually come before the day-trip coach groups that arrive from London.

## Around the Castle Quarter

You're at the western edge of the city centre, so the walk in from the station is flat and easy. From Oxford railway station, head east along Park End Street to New Road, about 10 minutes. Gloucester Green coach station, where the London coaches stop, is a similar walk to the north.

The cobbled castle courtyard has cafés and restaurants that work well for lunch after the tour. Westgate Centre is a few minutes south, and its rooftop terrace looks back over the castle. Carfax Tower and Cornmarket Street are a five-minute walk east. Christ Church is about ten minutes beyond them, so you can see two of Oxford's oldest towers on the same morning.
