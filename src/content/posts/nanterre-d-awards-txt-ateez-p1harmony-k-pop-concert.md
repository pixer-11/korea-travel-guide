## Two nights, three fandoms, one roof

This is a stacked bill for a French arena. The D Awards brings TXT, ATEEZ and P1Harmony to the Plenitude Arena in Nanterre on January 15 and 16, 2027. That makes two nights of K-pop on the western edge of Paris.

The arena sits just beyond the Grande Arche, the hollow white cube at the far end of La Défense. Glass office towers rise on one side of the esplanade. On the other, the low streets of Nanterre begin. Inside, the venue is fully enclosed, so the January cold ends at the doors.

## Who is on the bill

Each act has its own fandom and its own lightstick. Expect the arena to split visibly between them.

- **TXT (Tomorrow X Together)**: Big Hit Music, part of HYBE, debuted in 2019. Fans are called MOA.
- **ATEEZ**: KQ Entertainment, debuted in 2018. The group built much of its following through heavy touring in Europe and the Americas. Fans are called ATINY.
- **P1Harmony**: FNC Entertainment, debuted in 2020. Fans are called P1ece.

The event is billed under an awards name rather than as a single group's tour. The organiser publishes the dates, venue and ticket links on its official site, and that is where the night-by-night details live.

## Getting there: the walk past the Grande Arche

Most concertgoers come from central Paris to **La Défense** station. The options all stop there:

- **Metro Line 1**: direct from Châtelet, Concorde and Champs-Élysées–Clemenceau
- **RER A**: direct from Châtelet–Les Halles and Charles de Gaulle–Étoile
- **Transilien L and U**, plus **tram T2**, for the western suburbs

From the station, the crowd climbs up to the esplanade and walks past the Grande Arche. The arena is on the far side, roughly 10–15 minutes on foot. The route is signposted, and on show nights you only need to follow the lightsticks.

**Nanterre-Préfecture**, the next stop west on RER A, is a second approach from the Nanterre side. Fans often use it after the show to avoid the crush at La Défense.

## Inside the Plenitude Arena

This is a large, multi-purpose indoor arena. It also serves as the home ground of the Racing 92 rugby club. For concerts the stage takes one end, and the seats rise in steep tiers around the floor.

The upper tiers are steep and a long way from the stage. Fans in those sections rely on a good view of the full lighting rig rather than of the members' faces. The trade-off works in your favour when the arena fills with lightsticks, because that sea of colour looks best from above.

Seating and standing layouts vary from show to show. The ticketing pages are where each category is described.

## The mid-January factor

The dates matter here. In mid-January it is dark in Paris by late afternoon, and the cold is damp rather than dramatic. The La Défense esplanade is notorious for wind funnelling between the towers. Anyone waiting outside on that open stone plaza feels it.

Fans who spend the afternoon by the arena usually come in layers, with gloves that still work a phone screen. Hand warmers come out in the merch line. The upside is that two winter nights give the out-of-town crowd a whole weekend's rhythm. Plenty of people treat the 15th and 16th as one trip and see both nights.

## Lightsticks, fanchants and the merch line

K-pop concerts in Europe follow their own etiquette, and French arenas are no exception.

- **Fanchants**: fans shout the members' names, and set lines, at fixed points in songs. Most people learn them from the guide videos the fandoms circulate, and the chants for a three-act bill sound noticeably different.
- **Lightsticks**: each group has an official lightstick. At many K-pop arena shows these sync to the stage lighting, so a cheap replica stays dark while the official ones change colour together.
- **Merch**: official merchandise queues form outside long before doors. The fans at the front tend to be the ones who came for a specific member's photocard.
- **Freebies**: fans hand out home-made stickers, photocards and banners outside the venue. Taking one is fine, and a thank-you is all that's expected.
- **Slogans**: printed fan slogans come up during specific songs, coordinated by the fandoms in advance.

Contactless card is the normal way to pay around La Défense and Nanterre. A "bonjour" or "bonsoir" to security staff before the bag check goes a long way in France. It's the expected greeting, not just politeness.

## Food, hotels and the ride home

**Westfield Les 4 Temps**, the large shopping centre built over La Défense station, is where most concertgoers eat beforehand. It has chain restaurants, a supermarket and plenty of indoor space to wait in out of the wind. The Grande Arche area also has cafés aimed at the office crowd, and some of these keep shorter hours at weekends.

Visitors coming from abroad usually stay either at La Défense itself or in neighbouring Courbevoie and Puteaux. Both are walkable to the arena. Staying central along Metro Line 1 also works, since the line runs straight back into Paris.

After the encore, the La Défense platforms fill fast. Many fans walk the extra distance to Nanterre-Préfecture, or wait 20 minutes in the mall for the first rush to clear. Fans on the second night with an early flight usually plan the RER A link toward Châtelet–Les Halles, where the RER B to Charles de Gaulle airport begins.

## Dates and tickets

The D Awards with TXT, ATEEZ and P1Harmony takes place on **January 15 and 16, 2027** at the **Plenitude Arena, Nanterre**. The organiser publishes the dates and ticket links on its official site.

One French rule is worth knowing. Reselling event tickets regularly without the organiser's authorisation is a criminal offence in France. Fans who can't attend usually go through the official resale channel, if one is offered, rather than through private sellers.
