## Concert-night habits in Hong Kong

Hong Kong concertgoers run on the MTR. Most people tap in with an Octopus card or a contactless bank card at the gates, so they skip the ticket machines when the crowd leaves at the end. Visitors usually load an Octopus card at the airport's Airport Express counter when they land.

A few things regulars tend to do at Korean artist shows here:

- **Merch first.** The official merchandise booth usually draws its longest line before doors, and popular items can sell out before the encore.
- **Fan projects.** Fan clubs often hand out printed slogans or cards to hold up during a particular song. If someone offers you one, take it and follow the row around you.

## Coming from Macau or across the border

The "Hong Kong / Macau Area" label suits the geography, since the two cities are close. TurboJET ferries link the Macau Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal with the Hong Kong-Macau Ferry Terminal in Sheung Wan. The trip takes about an hour, and Sheung Wan MTR station (Island Line) is a short walk from the pier.

The other route is the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge. Shuttle buses cross from Macau to the Hong Kong Port near Tung Chung. From there, local buses connect to Tung Chung MTR station on the Tung Chung Line. Visitors from the mainland usually come in through the Shenzhen crossings at Lo Wu or Lok Ma Chau, both on the East Rail Line.

Whichever way you come, you clear immigration, so you'll need your passport or travel permit. Late-night ferries and bridge buses run less often than daytime ones. That's why many Macau fans book a night in Hong Kong instead of making the return trip after the show.

## Making a weekend of late November

November is one of Hong Kong's easiest months to visit. It sits in the dry season, after the typhoon months, with mild days and cooler evenings. A light layer is useful, because air conditioning in Hong Kong halls and malls is famously strong.

If you have the morning free, the Star Ferry between Tsim Sha Tsui and Central is short, cheap and a good way to see the harbour by day. Fans often spend the afternoon in Mong Kok, where the shops along Sai Yeung Choi Street South sell Korean albums and goods. Afterwards, Temple Street Night Market in Jordan stays busy late, with clay-pot rice stalls that make a good post-concert meal.
