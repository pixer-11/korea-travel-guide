The Greater Caucasus does most of the work here. The range rises almost straight up from the far shore of Ilia Lake, covered in forest and close enough that its ridges fill the top half of the view. In front of it is a calm sheet of reservoir water. Behind you, the Alazani Valley spreads out flat and green with vineyards.

It's a small place and a quiet one. Not many international visitors make it this far into eastern Kakheti, and most who do come for the wine. That's exactly why the lake is worth an unhurried morning.

## A lake at the foot of the mountains

Ilia Lake is a man-made reservoir. It sits on the northern edge of Kvareli, where the valley floor stops and the slopes start. Its full Google address is just a plus code, XQ4W+3FC, on an unnamed road, which tells you how low-key it is.

Most visitors walk the shoreline, sit on a bench facing the mountains, and take the photograph everyone comes for: still water with the ridge reflected in it. The water stays calmest early in the day, so morning gives you the best chance of a sharp reflection.

This is a stroll, not a hike. Budget one to two hours here, more if you bring lunch or just want to sit facing the slopes for a while.

## Named for the man from Kvareli

The lake is named after Ilia Chavchavadze. He was the 19th-century writer and public figure Georgians call the father of the modern nation, and the Georgian Orthodox Church canonised him as Saint Ilia the Righteous. He was born in Kvareli, and the town hasn't forgotten it.

His family home in the town centre is now a house museum. It makes a natural second stop after the lake. The two together show you why Georgians drive out here and why the town carries his name the way it does.

In Georgian the lake is **ილიას ტბა** (*Ilias tba*, "Ilia's lake"). Drivers and locals will know it by that name more readily than by the English one.

## Getting to Kvareli and the shore

Kvareli is in northeastern Kakheti, roughly two and a half to three hours by road from Tbilisi. There are two main routes:

- **Via the Gombori Pass**: shorter, winding, wooded, and dropping down toward Telavi.
- **Via Sagarejo and the valley road**: longer, but easier driving.

Without a car, take a marshrutka (shared minibus) from Tbilisi's Ortachala bus station toward Kvareli. Check departure times at the station, because schedules change. From Telavi you can easily make a day of it with a hired driver.

The lake is only a few kilometres from Kvareli's centre. A local taxi is the simplest way to get there. Agree the fare before you get in, and if you're heading somewhere awkward afterwards, ask the driver to wait or come back for you.

## Walking the lakeside without the rush

Getting the visit right is mostly about timing and manners. Here's what helps:

- **Come early.** You get still water and clearer views of the peaks before the day's haze builds over the valley. Summer afternoons in the Alazani lowlands get properly hot.
- **Carry lari in small notes.** Taxis and small kiosks in Kvareli expect cash. Don't assume card payment outside the larger hotels and wineries.
- **Learn two words.** *Gamarjoba* (hello) and *madloba* (thank you) go a long way in a small Kakhetian town.
- **Ask before photographing people**, especially families picnicking on the shore. A gesture toward your camera is enough.
- **Take your rubbish away.** Facilities beside the water are basic, and the shore stays pleasant only if visitors pack out what they bring.

If you've arranged a car for the day, tell the driver how long you plan to stay. Drivers in Kakheti are used to waiting, but they like to know when you'll be back.

## When the valley is at its best

Spring and autumn are the strongest seasons. In April and May the slopes behind the lake turn bright green, and snow often still sits on the higher peaks. Late September and October bring *rtveli*, the grape harvest. Kvareli smells of crushed fruit and the wineries are at their busiest.

Midsummer works too, as long as you come early and expect heat. Winter is quieter and colder, and the mountains can disappear into cloud for days at a time.

## Kindzmarauli country around the lake

Kvareli is the home of Kindzmarauli, the semi-sweet red made from Saperavi grapes. It's one of Georgia's best-known protected wine appellations, and the lake sits among the vineyards that produce it.

Near the lake, a winery has cut wine-storage tunnels deep into the mountainside, and visitors can tour and taste inside. Several other cellars around town offer tastings of wine made in qvevri, the large clay vessels buried in the ground that Georgian winemaking is known for. Check tasting times directly with each winery.

## Pairing it with Gremi and Nekresi

The lake fits easily into a loop of the eastern Alazani Valley. Two stops are worth building around it:

- **Gremi Citadel**: a 16th-century royal complex of the Kakhetian kings, with a brick church and tower on a hill above the main road. It's roughly 15–20 minutes from Kvareli.
- **Nekresi Monastery**: high on a forested slope above the valley, with one of the oldest church sites in Georgia. Allow time for the climb up from the parking area. Women should bring a scarf and a long skirt or wrap for the church interiors.

Start with the lake early, visit Gremi and Nekresi around midday, and finish at a Kvareli cellar. That makes a full and well-paced day in Kakheti.
