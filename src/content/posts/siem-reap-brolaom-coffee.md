## A roaster on the other bank

Siem Reap's café crowd mostly stays on one side of the river. Sister Srey, the long-running café on the west bank, faces the water across from the Old Market quarter. Brolaom Coffee sits directly opposite it on the far bank. That makes it a short walk from the town's busiest few blocks, yet on a side most visitors only pass through by tuk-tuk.

Brolaom calls itself a specialty coffee roaster. For a reader, that means the coffee is the point here, not something on the side of a full brunch menu. It's one of the newer names in town, and word about it has spread among both residents and visitors.

## Finding it from the Old Market

Navigation is simple once you use the river as your guide. Start at the Old Market (Psar Chas), walk the short stretch to the riverbank road, and look for Sister Srey facing the water. Brolaom is straight across from it.

- **On foot:** the pedestrian bridges in the centre take you over in a couple of minutes.
- **By tuk-tuk:** ask for "across from Sister Srey". Every driver in the centre knows that landmark.
- **By app:** PassApp and Grab both run in Siem Reap. Drop the pin on the map before you book, because a new place may not show up under its name yet.

Pub Street is only a few blocks from the Old Market. So even if you're staying in the backpacker grid, the walk to the river is short.

## Why coffee people are making the trip

Cambodia's everyday coffee is strong, dark and usually poured over ice with sweetened condensed milk, and you'll find it at street stalls all over town. A specialty roaster is a different thing: the beans are chosen and roasted with care so that their origin comes through in the cup.

We haven't been given Brolaom's menu, so we won't guess at its drinks or its prices. Ask at the counter which beans are on that day, and how they suggest drinking them. A roaster's staff are usually glad to talk you through it, and that conversation is half of what a place like this offers.

## Ordering and paying in Siem Reap

There are a few local conventions worth knowing before you walk in.

- **Currency:** the US dollar and the Cambodian riel are both used in Siem Reap. Change usually comes back as a mix of the two, with riel standing in for anything under a dollar.
- **Cards and QR:** some cafés take cards or Cambodia's KHQR mobile payments and some don't. Carry small dollar notes until you've checked.
- **Tipping:** it isn't expected, but leaving a little in a tip jar or rounding up is appreciated.
- **Ice:** in established cafés, ice comes from commercial suppliers and is generally fine to drink.
- **Greeting:** a "chum reap suor" (hello) and "awkun" (thank you) get a warm reaction.

Hours at newly opened places often change during their first months. Check Brolaom's social media or map listing on the day you plan to go rather than relying on an old screenshot.

## Fitting it into a temple day

Most visitors spend their mornings at the Angkor park, north of town. Many begin with sunrise at Angkor Wat and are back in town by late morning, hot and running low on energy. A riverside coffee is a good reset before the afternoon, and Brolaom's central spot means it barely costs you any travel time.

Midday in Siem Reap is fierce for most of the year. From March to May the heat stays high well into the afternoon. In those months, a coffee break in the early afternoon is when walking the town makes the least sense anyway.

## Making a loop of the riverbank

The paths along the Siem Reap River are shaded in places and link several central landmarks. After your coffee, follow the water north towards the Royal Gardens and the Preah Ang Chek Preah Ang Chorm shrine, where locals bring offerings. Or cross back south of the bridges to the Old Market, which is good for kramas (checked cotton scarves) and spices.

You can see Sister Srey across the water the whole time. Coffee on one bank and lunch on the other is an easy way to spend a slow half-day in the centre without getting in a tuk-tuk.
