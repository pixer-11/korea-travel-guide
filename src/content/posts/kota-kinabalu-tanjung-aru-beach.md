## Why go

Tanjung Aru is the beach Kota Kinabalu shows up for at the end of the day. It isn't a secluded cove — it's a working, well-loved city beach, roughly 4,000 reviews shy of being one of the most talked-about spots in Sabah, and on any clear evening you'll see why. The sand runs in a long, gently curving strip split into "First Beach" and "Second Beach," backed by a line of casuarina and coconut trees that filter the late light into long shadows across the shore. As the sun drops toward the horizon, the water turns the color of weak tea, then copper, then a flat pewter grey just before dark, and the silhouettes of the offshore islands — Gaya, Sapi, Manukan — sit out on the water like cutouts.

## Getting there

Tanjung Aru sits a few kilometers southwest of downtown Kota Kinabalu, next to Kota Kinabalu International Airport, so it's an easy stop on the way to or from a flight. A Grab from the city center runs about 15-20 minutes depending on traffic; taxis wait outside the Tanjung Aru Beach Resort and Shangri-La Tanjung Aru grounds for the return trip. Local buses running the Tanjung Aru–Kota Kinabalu route stop within walking distance, though timings are irregular enough that most visitors just use ride-hailing apps. If you're driving, there's parking near the food stall area and along the road fronting First Beach — it fills up fast in the last hour before sunset.

## What to see and do

The beach itself does the heavy lifting: a long walk along the packed sand, a swim in water calmer than most of Sabah's mainland coast, and people-watching as families set up mats and kids fly kites in the onshore breeze. First Beach, closer to the resorts, is the classic sunset-watching stretch and where most of the food stalls cluster. Second Beach, a short walk or drive further along, is quieter and popular with joggers and locals doing evening exercise laps. The row of stalls sells grilled corn, satay, fresh coconut water hacked open on the spot, and instant local favorites like cheesy popcorn and fried snacks — it's as much a night-market snack crawl as it is a beach visit. If you want a formal sundowner, the resort bars at Shangri-La Tanjung Aru and the Tanjung Aru Beach Resort both face the water and serve cocktails right at the sand's edge, for a price well above what you'd pay at the stalls.

## When to go

Sunset is the entire point of Tanjung Aru, and Kota Kinabalu's sun typically drops between roughly 6pm and 6:20pm year-round, since it sits so close to the equator — there's little seasonal swing in timing. Aim to arrive by 5pm to find a decent patch of sand and watch the light change slowly rather than rushing in for the final ten minutes. Weekday evenings are calmer than weekends, when local families turn out in force, especially around public holidays and school breaks. Because this is one of the city's most visited beaches, don't expect an empty shoreline at any hour — the draw is the atmosphere and the view, not solitude.

## How to visit like a local

Skip the resort bars for your first drink and head straight for the stalls — that's where the regulars go, paying cash for grilled skewers and coconuts rather than card. Bring a mat or rent one of the plastic stools set out near the stalls instead of assuming loungers are free and available. The biggest tourist mistake here is treating Tanjung Aru as a swim-first beach: the water is fine for wading and a splash, but the real ritual is claiming your spot on the sand an hour before sunset, ordering food, and staying put as the sky changes color — nobody rushes this. If you want a slightly less crowded angle on the same sunset, walk five or ten minutes toward Second Beach, where the crowd thins noticeably even at peak hours. Watch your belongings amid the stall crowds after dark, and don't expect much beyond basic changing facilities — this is a come-as-you-are beach, not a resort private strip, even though two resorts sit right on it.
