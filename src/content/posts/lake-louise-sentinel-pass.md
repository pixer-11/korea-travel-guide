## The climb above Moraine Lake

The trail starts at the Moraine Lake shoreline. Within minutes it leaves the turquoise water and the crowds behind, switchbacking up through spruce and subalpine fir. That forest stretch gains most of the height. It is steady, shaded and unrelenting.

Above the trees, Larch Valley opens out. It is a broad meadow ringed by Mount Temple, Pinnacle Mountain and Eiffel Peak, with the two small Minnestimma Lakes sitting on the valley floor. The final push is a series of tight scree switchbacks to the pass itself. Expect loose rock underfoot and almost no shelter from the wind.

## What you see from the top

The pass sits at about 2,611 m. It is often described as the highest point reached by a maintained trail in the Canadian Rockies. Facing south, you look back over Larch Valley to the Valley of the Ten Peaks. On the far side, the ground drops steeply into Paradise Valley, past the eroded rock spires below Pinnacle Mountain.

- **Distance:** roughly 11.6 km return from Moraine Lake
- **Elevation gain:** around 725 m
- **Time:** 5–7 hours with stops
- **Difficulty:** strenuous, with steep scree near the top

## Season and snow

Moraine Lake Road usually opens from early June to mid-October, so that window is your season. Snow often lingers on the upper switchbacks into July. Early-summer hikers can meet icy, slippery slopes there.

Late September is larch season. The needles in Larch Valley turn gold before they drop, and that is when the lower trail fills up. The pass itself stays quieter, because many people turn around at the meadow. Mid-July to early September gives the driest footing. Afternoon thunderstorms are a real risk above the treeline, so aim to reach the top by midday.

## Getting up there the Parks Canada way

Getting to the trailhead takes some planning:

- **Transport:** private vehicles are not allowed on Moraine Lake Road. Reserve a Parks Canada shuttle from the Lake Louise Park and Ride, or take Roam Transit or a licensed commercial operator. Morning seats sell out early.
- **Park pass:** you need a valid Banff National Park pass. Current fees are posted on the Parks Canada website.
- **Group rule:** when grizzlies are active in the area, Parks Canada often requires hikers to travel in tight groups of four or more. This is common in late summer and fall. People team up at the trailhead to meet it, so ask around if you arrive short.
- **Bear spray:** carry it on your hip, not in your pack, and talk loudly in the meadows.
- **Layers:** the pass is far colder and windier than the lakeshore. Pack a shell, gloves and more water than you think you'll need, because there is none to buy past the lake.

The trail is open 24 hours, but the shuttle schedule decides your real start and finish times. Check the last return departure before you set off. Trail warnings and closures appear on the Parks Canada Banff trail report, so read it the night before.
