Four days of show-jumping at a club that has hosted this competition since before most of Barcelona's tourist landmarks existed: that's the pitch for CSIO Barcelona. The 2026 edition, the 114th, runs October 1 to 4 at the Real Club de Polo de Barcelona, and it carries genuine sporting weight because it hosts the final of the Longines League of Nations, the team competition that decides which nations' show-jumping squads finish the season on top.

This isn't a festival that popped up to catch tourist traffic. CSIO Barcelona predates the vast majority of what gets marketed as "iconic" in this city, and the format reflects that institutional weight: national teams, Nations Cup-style team rounds, and individual grand prix classes spread across the four days rather than crammed into one evening show.

## What actually happens across the four days

Expect a structure typical of a CSIO five-star meeting: qualifying and team classes build through Thursday and Friday, with the biggest individual and team finals landing on the weekend. The Longines League of Nations final is the marquee fixture and draws the strongest field of the week.

- Individual grand prix and speed classes
- The Longines League of Nations final (weekend)
- Junior and accompanying equestrian classes, in past editions

## Real Club de Polo de Barcelona: the venue itself

The club sits on land near the Diagonal Mar area, closer to the coast than the Gothic core, with polo fields and equestrian rings that have hosted international competition since the early 20th century. It's a working sports club, not a stadium built for one-off events, which shapes the whole atmosphere.

Grandstand seating faces the main arena, and the grounds usually include hospitality areas, sponsor pavilions and space to watch horses warm up before they enter the ring. Bring layers: October evenings in Barcelona can turn cool once the sun drops, especially if you're seated rather than moving around.

## Getting to Real Club de Polo

The club is on the Diagonal Mar side of the city, away from the metro-dense center, so plan your route rather than assuming a station is at the door.

- By taxi or rideshare: the simplest option from most central hotels, roughly 15-20 minutes depending on traffic
- By metro: nearest stations are on Line 4 (Maresme Fòrum) or Line 2, followed by a walk or short bus/taxi hop
- By car: the club has grounds large enough for spectator parking during major events, though space fills as the day progresses

## Why the League of Nations final matters here

For a rider or a spectator who follows the sport, this is the detail that separates CSIO Barcelona from a generic showjumping weekend. The Longines League of Nations is a season-long team series across multiple countries, and Barcelona hosting the final means the strongest national squads arrive with the season's outcome on the line.

That raises the standard of horse and rider on show compared with a mid-season leg, and it tends to pull a more international crowd of spectators, sponsors and press. If you only attend one day, the day carrying the League of Nations final is the one to prioritise, though confirm which date that falls on for the 2026 edition through the official schedule.

## Reading the ring like a regular

Show-jumping crowds at CSIO Barcelona include a mix of club members, families and international visitors following specific teams, so the etiquette is closer to a golf gallery than a football stadium.

- Applause is expected after a clean round, not during a horse's approach to a jump
- Silence or quiet is the norm as horses enter the ring and line up to start
- Trade and sponsor pavilions around the main ring are worth a walk between classes, since they often include displays and refreshment stands not visible from the grandstand
- Standing at the rail near the warm-up area lets you see horses and riders preparing, which is often as informative as watching the round itself

Locals and regular attendees tend to arrive well before the marquee classes to secure grandstand seats with a clear sightline to the final fences, since sightlines vary more than at a purpose-built stadium.
