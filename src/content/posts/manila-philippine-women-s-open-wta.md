## The Philippines' own WTA week

This is the country's first WTA Tour event. The inaugural edition ran in January 2026 and gave Filipino fans professional women's tennis on home soil for the first time.

That first draw had a strongly regional flavour, with players from Hong Kong, China, Taiwan and Thailand. The second edition takes place in January 2027 in Manila. Dates, ticket links and the order of play come from the tournament organiser, and no lineup details are given here.

## Rizal Memorial and getting to Malate

The 2026 matches were played at Rizal Memorial Tennis Center. It sits inside the Rizal Memorial Sports Complex on Pablo Ocampo Street in Malate, the city's oldest major sports ground.

The tennis courts share the grounds with Rizal Memorial Coliseum, the old baseball stadium and Ninoy Aquino Stadium. Across Taft Avenue is the De La Salle University campus. From the complex, Manila Bay and the Roxas Boulevard seafront are a short ride west.

Getting there:

- **LRT Line 1:** Vito Cruz station on Taft Avenue, a few minutes' walk east to the complex gates.
- **Grab or taxi:** easiest from Makati or BGC, though Taft Avenue traffic slows to a crawl at rush hour.
- **On foot from Malate:** hotels around Remedios Circle and Adriatico Street are within walking or short-ride distance.

## January courtside: heat, shade and long sessions

January falls in Manila's dry season. Rain is unlikely, but by midday the outdoor courts still get hot, with little shade over the seating.

Tennis sessions run long because matches have no fixed end time. Fans usually bring:

- a hat and sunscreen for day sessions
- a refillable bottle, since the heat builds by early afternoon
- a light layer for evening matches, when the air off the bay cools a little

Spectators often pair a session with dinner in Malate. San Andres Street and the Remedios Circle area have plenty of options for eating after the evening match.

## Courtside manners in Malate

Tennis has its own crowd rules, and ushers enforce them. Spectators stay seated and silent during points. People move to and from their seats only at changeovers, which come after odd-numbered games, or at set breaks.

Phones stay on silent and flash photography is off-limits during play. Filipino crowds cheer loudly between points, especially for regional and home players, and a burst of "Let's go!" after a long rally is part of the atmosphere.

Around the complex, GCash and Maya are widely accepted alongside cash. Small food stalls nearby tend to prefer cash. Grab is the usual ride home, and pickups queue along Pablo Ocampo Street when a night session ends.
