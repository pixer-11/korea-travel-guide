## A new tenant in a familiar shoplot

Some Kuala Lumpur addresses carry a memory. For years, this shoplot was Triptyk. Now it is Curiosité, an all-day dining café that moved in without stripping the building back to bare plaster. Much of the heritage character stays, so you eat inside an old structure rather than a fit-out that could be anywhere.

That matters in KL. Old shoplots across the city get demolished or gutted with some regularity. A café that keeps the bones of one is making a choice. If you knew Triptyk, you will recognise the room. If you didn't, you get the fabric of an older Kuala Lumpur with a current menu on top.

## The bowl that explains the place

One dish tells you what kind of kitchen this is. Asam pedas pan mee joins two staples that rarely meet on the same plate.

- **Asam pedas** is a sour, spicy stew built on tamarind and chilli, usually cooked with fish. It is strongly associated with Malay kitchens, especially Melaka and Johor.
- **Pan mee** is a Hakka flour noodle, either hand-torn or cut. It is one of Kuala Lumpur's everyday lunches, normally served in a clear anchovy broth.

Putting the tamarind sourness of one into the comfort food of the other is the Curiosité idea in one bowl. Nothing on the verified record gives us the rest of the menu. Expect fusion cooking in that spirit, and read the board or menu on the day rather than arriving with a fixed order in mind.

## Reading a heritage shoplot

Know the building type and you will notice more. KL shoplots were built for trade on the ground floor and living space above. That gave the city its long, narrow, deep plots and its covered five-foot ways along the front.

Look up and around once you are seated. Original details survive in different ways in different buildings: window shapes, ceiling height, old wall surfaces. Curiosité's own appeal rests on how much of its building has been kept. Give the room a few minutes before your phone comes out.

## Finding Curiosité

The café is new, so map listings can lag behind. Some apps may still show the old Triptyk name at this shoplot. Search for "Curiosité" first. If that comes up empty, try the previous tenant's name and check that the pin matches before you set off.

Most visitors to the city get around by Grab, Malaysia's main ride-hailing app. It is the simplest way to reach a specific shoplot door. The LRT and MRT lines cover much of central KL, but the walk from a station to a narrow-fronted building is easier once you have checked the street view.

## Ordering and paying without fuss

A few local habits make the visit smoother:

- **Confirm before you go.** Hours and booking policy for a newly opened café can change week to week. Check Curiosité's own social channels or call ahead.
- **Pay by card or QR.** KL cafés widely accept cards, and DuitNow QR or e-wallets like Touch 'n Go are normal. A little cash still helps as a backup.
- **Tipping is optional.** Malaysia has no strong tipping custom. Sit-down places often add a service charge to the bill, so read it before adding anything extra.
- **Ask about the spice.** Asam pedas is meant to bite. If you are unsure about chilli, ask before ordering rather than sending a bowl back.

"All-day dining" means you don't have to aim for a strict lunch hour. A mid-afternoon visit, after the city's lunch break, is often a calmer time at KL cafés in general. Treat that as a common pattern, not a promise for this room.

## Who should make the trip

Curiosité suits a few kinds of traveller. If you already know Malay and Chinese-Malaysian staples, the fusion dishes are an interesting variation. If you are new to both, a bowl like asam pedas pan mee introduces two local flavours at once.

It also suits anyone who wants to see what Kuala Lumpur does with its older buildings. Many visitors spend their time in glass towers and malls. An hour here, inside a shoplot with its history still showing, makes a useful contrast.
