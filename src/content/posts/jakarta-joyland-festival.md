## Two days of music, jokes and film

Joyland isn't only about the music. It's an outdoor festival that puts music, stand-up comedy and film on the same grounds, so the day doesn't have to be a run of headline sets. Between bands you can sit in on a comedy set or catch a screening.

The 2026 edition runs over the weekend of November 28-29, at the Gelora Bung Karno sports complex, which everyone in Jakarta calls GBK. Lineup, stage layout and ticket tiers all sit on the festival's official site and ticketing pages. This guide covers the parts those pages leave out: the venue, the journey there and the weather.

## The GBK Complex as a festival ground

GBK is a huge government sports campus in Senayan, South-Central Jakarta. It was built for the 1962 Asian Games and rebuilt for the 2018 Asian Games. The main stadium sits in the middle. Around it are smaller venues, practice fields, tree-lined jogging paths and wide paved plazas.

A festival here sits inside that campus, not on a blank field. In practice that means:

- **Lots of walking.** Getting from the nearest gate to the festival entrance can take a while, because GBK's perimeter is long.
- **Mature trees** along the internal roads, which give some shade before the gates open.
- **Hard paving around the grounds**, so a heavy shower drains faster than it would on open grass.

## Getting to Senayan without the gridlock

Senayan sits at the junction of Jalan Sudirman and Jalan Gatot Subroto, two of Jakarta's most jammed roads. On a festival weekend the cars around GBK barely move. Most regulars skip the car and take the train.

- **MRT North-South Line:** Istora Mandiri station sits on Jalan Sudirman along GBK's eastern side. Senayan station is one stop north, close to FX Sudirman and Plaza Senayan.
- **TransJakarta Corridor 1** (Blok M–Kota) runs along Sudirman and stops beside the complex.
- **KRL Commuter Line:** Palmerah station, on the Rangkasbitung line, is just off GBK's western edge. It's handy if you're coming from Tangerang or the southwest.

Gojek and Grab both work, but the pick-up points around GBK get crowded once the last set ends. Many people walk a few minutes away from the gates, towards Senayan City or the Sudirman side, and request their ride from there.

## Laughing between sets: comedy and film at Joyland

This is what sets Joyland apart from Jakarta's other big music weekends. Indonesia has a busy stand-up scene, and much of it performs in Bahasa Indonesia. Comedy sets here are usually local acts talking to a local crowd, so international visitors may find the jokes harder to follow than the music.

The film side brings screenings onto the festival grounds. Short films and Indonesian independent cinema suit an audience that drifts between stages. It's also a chance to sit down for a while after hours on your feet.

Many festival-goers plan their day around these breaks. Watching a comedy set or a screening in mid-afternoon saves energy for the evening headliners.

## Dressing for late November rain

Late November falls in Jakarta's wet season, which runs roughly from November to March. Afternoon and evening downpours are common at this time of year. The days are hot and humid before the rain arrives, and they stay warm afterwards.

What experienced Jakarta festival-goers usually bring:

- **A light poncho** rather than an umbrella, since umbrellas block the view in a standing crowd.
- **Shoes you don't mind getting soaked,** because puddles collect even on paved ground.
- **A dry bag or zip pouch** for your phone, which you'll need for paying and getting home.
- **Sunscreen and a hat** for the hours before the clouds come in.

Rain rarely clears an Indonesian festival crowd. People pull on ponchos and stay at the stage.

## Paying, queuing and moving around the grounds

Jakarta's big festivals are mostly cashless. Food and drink stalls generally take QRIS, Indonesia's national QR payment standard. GoPay, OVO, Dana and local bank apps can all scan it. Some events also run their own top-up wristband or card, and the festival's own information explains which system the year uses.

If you're visiting from abroad without an Indonesian e-wallet, a contactless card works at many stalls but not all. Some cash in rupiah covers the gaps.

How the crowd behaves:

- **Entry lines** move through bag checks and ticket scans. Attendees usually keep their e-ticket QR code saved offline, because mobile signal drops in big crowds.
- **Food queues** get longest around dusk, when the day's crowd is at its biggest and everyone gets hungry at once.
- **Etiquette** is relaxed and friendly. People ask before squeezing past, and a "permisi" (excuse me) works far better than pushing.
- **Prayer facilities** are normal at large Indonesian events, and you may see people step away around maghrib, the sunset prayer.

## After the last set in Senayan

GBK has a cluster of malls around it, so there's always somewhere to eat, cool off or wait out the traffic. FX Sudirman faces the complex across Jalan Sudirman. Plaza Senayan and Senayan City sit at the northwest corner on Jalan Asia Afrika.

Visitors usually stay around Senayan, Sudirman or SCBD, south of GBK, which has a dense cluster of business hotels and restaurants. From any of these areas, the MRT or a short ride gets you to the gates. That keeps the trip back after the headliners short.
