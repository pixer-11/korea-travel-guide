## Stone faces in a mountain meadow

They are faces, and they are enormous. Rough-hewn stone heads rise out of the grass in Sno, each one several times the height of anyone standing beside it. The Greater Caucasus ridges climb behind them on every side.

This is the long-running project of Merab Phiranishvili, a sculptor from the area. He has carved figures from Georgian literature and history into massive blocks of stone. Shota Rustaveli, the medieval author of *The Knight in the Panther's Skin*, is among them, alongside writers such as Ilia Chavchavadze and Vazha-Pshavela.

Some heads look finished. Others still carry tool marks or are only roughed out. That unfinished quality is part of the appeal: you are looking at a work in progress, not a museum display.

## Finding Sno from Stepantsminda

Sno sits just south of Stepantsminda, off the Georgian Military Road. The turn for the Juta valley runs through the village, so the sculptures sit on the most common side trip in the Kazbegi area.

Your options:

- **Taxi from Stepantsminda:** drivers wait near the main square and know the place. Agree the fare before you get in.
- **Combined with Juta:** most Juta taxis will stop at the heads if you ask when you book.
- **On foot:** walking out from Stepantsminda is possible along the road. Allow time and watch for traffic on the narrow stretches.
- **Self-drive:** pull off carefully. Parking is informal, so don't block the village road.

Getting to Stepantsminda itself usually means a marshrutka or shared taxi from Didube station in Tbilisi. The trip runs over the Jvari Pass.

## What you're actually walking among

Expect a grassy, uneven field, not a landscaped park. The heads stand at different angles and heights, and you'll find yourself stepping between them through tall grass or mud depending on the season.

Signage is minimal. It helps to know a few names before you arrive so you can match faces to writers. Rustaveli's medieval epic and Chavchavadze's role in Georgia's nineteenth-century national revival are good starting points.

Phiranishvili's home and workspace are on site, so this is a working artist's ground. Sno also has its own old stone tower and church a short walk away, which are worth a look while you're in the village.

## Manners on a sculptor's doorstep

You are a guest on someone's working ground. Behave the way you would in a stranger's garden.

- **Don't climb the heads.** They're tempting for photos, but the stone is a working surface and some pieces are unfinished.
- **Greet anyone you see.** A simple "gamarjoba" (hello) goes a long way in a small Georgian village.
- **Ask before photographing people,** including the sculptor if he's working.
- **Carry small lari notes.** Village shops and taxis in Kazbegi tend to prefer cash.
- **Check before you go.** No official opening hours are published, so ask your guesthouse or driver whether visitors are welcome that day.

Pairing the stop with Juta works best when you go early. The Juta taxis leave Stepantsminda throughout the morning, and stopping on the way out means fewer cars at the field.

## When the valley is kindest

Late spring through early autumn is the easiest window. The meadow is green, the road to Juta is reliable, and the peaks behind the heads are usually clear in the mornings.

Winter changes things. Snow can cover the lower sculptures, and the Jvari Pass between Tbilisi and Stepantsminda sometimes closes after heavy snowfall. If you're coming in the cold months, check road conditions before you leave Tbilisi.

Light matters here too. Low sun picks out the chisel marks across the faces, so earlier or later in the day gives you the strongest texture in photos.

## Building a Kazbegi day around it

The heads fill a short gap in a full day. On their own they won't hold you for long, but they make a rewarding pause between bigger sights.

A practical loop:

- **Morning:** Gergeti Trinity Church above Stepantsminda, with Mount Kazbek behind it on clear days.
- **Midday:** drive south to Sno for the sculptures and the village tower.
- **Afternoon:** continue up to Juta for the walk toward the Chaukhi massif.

That route keeps backtracking to a minimum. It also means you see one of the region's quieter attractions without having to plan a separate trip.
