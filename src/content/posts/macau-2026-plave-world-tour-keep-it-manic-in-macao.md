## Two nights of avatars in Cotai

PLAVE is a five-member boy group: Yejun, Noah, Bamby, Eunho and Hamin. On stage they appear as 3D animated characters. Real performers sing and move in motion-capture suits, and their avatars play out on wall-sized LED screens in real time. That means live vocals, live banter and choreography that reacts to the room.

The KEEP IT MANIC tour was set to stop in Macau for two shows, on Saturday, October 31 and Sunday, November 1, 2026. The first night fell on Halloween. Cotai's resorts usually put up seasonal decorations that weekend, so the corridors around the arena look more festive than usual.

## The Londoner and how to reach it

The Londoner Arena sits inside The Londoner Macao on the Cotai Strip. The resort's facade copies Westminster, with a clock tower modelled on Big Ben over the main entrance. Indoor walkways link it to The Venetian Macao and The Parisian Macao, so you can move between all three without stepping outside into the humidity.

Most visitors come in through one of three entry points:

- **From Hong Kong by ferry:** Cotai Water Jet runs to the Taipa Ferry Terminal, which is a short ride from Cotai.
- **From Hong Kong by road:** cross-border buses run over the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge to the Macau port.
- **From mainland China:** the Border Gate crossing from Zhuhai.

Sands resorts run free shuttle buses from these terminals to their Cotai properties, and they are the usual way fans reach the arena. Taxis queue outside every terminal. The Taipa line of the Macau Light Rapid Transit also serves the Cotai area.

## Show night in Cotai

Fans call themselves PLLI, and the official lightstick is a common sight at their shows. Many fans also carry printed slogans and member-coloured goods. Merchandise lines at K-pop arena shows tend to form hours before doors open, which is why many attendees head to the merch booth first and eat afterwards.

A few local norms are worth knowing:

- **Paying:** resort shops and restaurants take cards. Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted next to the Macau pataca, and taxis prefer cash.
- **Language:** Cantonese and Portuguese are official, Mandarin is common and English works inside the resorts.
- **Photography:** each show had its own camera rules, set out in the ticket terms. Phone recording rules are usually enforced at the seats.

When an arena show ends, thousands of people leave at once and the shuttle and taxi stands back up quickly. Many fans stay in the connected resorts for a late dinner and let the crowd clear first.

## Staying and filling the weekend

Sleeping in Cotai saves the late-night trip across to the peninsula. The Londoner, The Venetian, The Parisian, Conrad and Sheraton are all connected under one roof. Fans on a tighter budget often stay in Taipa Village or on the Macau peninsula. Both are about fifteen minutes away by taxi.

Cotai and the nearby districts have plenty to fill a day:

- **Taipa Village:** Rua do Cunha's snack stalls serve pork chop buns and almond cookies.
- **Historic Centre of Macao:** the UNESCO-listed Senado Square and the Ruins of St. Paul's.
- **The Venetian:** indoor canals with gondolas, a ten-minute walk through the linked malls.

With two shows back to back, fans travelling from abroad often make a stop like this a three-night trip and attend both.
