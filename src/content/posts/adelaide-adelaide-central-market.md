## A city block of produce under one roof

Adelaide has been shopping here since 1869. The market fills the block behind Gouger Street, a covered hall of aisles where more than 80 vendors sell fruit, vegetables, meat, cheese, bread and much more. You walk in off the street and the city's noise drops away. What replaces it is the sound of stallholders calling prices and paper bags being filled.

It is one of the most-reviewed places in South Australia and consistently well rated. Don't expect a quiet secret. Saturday mornings are packed with Adelaide households doing the weekly shop, and visitors grazing their way along the aisles.

The pleasure is in the variety. The editorial summary puts it plainly: organic fruit, premium meats and a gourmet streak. Plan to eat as you go.

## Reading the trading week

The schedule catches visitors out. At the moment Google lists these hours:

- **Monday:** closed
- **Tuesday:** 7am–5:30pm
- **Wednesday and Thursday:** 9am–5:30pm
- **Friday:** 7am–9pm
- **Saturday:** 7am–3pm
- **Sunday:** closed

The trap is the weekend. If you fly in on a Saturday afternoon, you have until 3pm and then nothing until Tuesday. Public holidays can change things too, so check the market's official site before you plan a trip around it.

Wednesday and Thursday open two hours later than Tuesday. If you're an early riser, Tuesday, Friday or Saturday is your day.

## Friday until nine

Friday is the market's long day. It opens at 7am and keeps trading until 9pm, the only evening it stays open. That makes it the easiest slot if you're sightseeing all day and only free after dark.

An evening visit lets you do the whole of Gouger Street in one go. Shop the stalls first. Then walk out the Gouger Street side, where the street is lined with restaurants and has been one of Adelaide's main eating strips for decades.

Stocking up for a weekend picnic or a self-catering apartment? Friday evening covers you before the Saturday 3pm close and the two dark days that follow.

## What to put in the bag

South Australia's food regions are all within driving distance of the city, and the stalls show it. You won't find every item at every stall. Here's what to look for:

- **Fruit and vegetables:** seasonal and often organic, the core of the market since the beginning
- **Meat and smallgoods:** the "premium meats" that are part of its reputation
- **Cheese and deli counters:** for picnic supplies and things that travel well
- **Bread and pastries:** good for eating as you walk
- **Local labels:** produce and products from regions like the Barossa, the Adelaide Hills, McLaren Vale and Kangaroo Island

Tasting is part of the trip. Several vendors serve food to eat on the spot, so you can make a meal of small bites rather than one sit-down lunch.

## Working the aisles on market day

The rhythm here is simple. These habits help:

- **Bring your own bags.** South Australia banned lightweight single-use plastic shopping bags back in 2009. A tote or folding bag makes a full shop much easier.
- **Pay by card or phone.** Tap-to-pay is normal across Australia. Carrying some cash for smaller stalls does no harm.
- **Don't tip.** It isn't expected at market counters in Australia. Prices are as marked, and you'll find them on stall signage.
- **Walk a full lap before buying.** Several stalls often sell the same fruit or cut of meat, and quality and price differ from one to the next.
- **Ask what's in season.** Stallholders are usually glad to tell you what's best that week and how to cook it.

On timing, think about how the day works. Locals doing a serious shop tend to arrive soon after opening, when the displays are full. Late Saturday, as the 3pm close gets near, some produce stalls clear stock rather than hold it over the long break to Tuesday. Bargain hunters drift in then, but the choice is thinner.

## Getting to Gouger Street

The market is in the south-west quarter of the CBD, so most city hotels are within walking distance. Gouger Street is the main frontage and Grote Street runs along the other side.

By tram, ride the Glenelg line to the Victoria Square stop. From there it's a short walk west to the market. Adelaide's city centre is flat and laid out in a grid, so the walk is easy even with full bags.

Driving is fine, but on Saturday mornings the nearby streets fill quickly. If you're coming for a big shop, it helps to have a car or a hotel close by. Carrying a week's groceries on foot gets old fast.

## Around the block

The market's neighbours make it worth staying longer. Adelaide's Chinatown sits right beside it, centred on the Moonta Street pedestrian mall, with Asian grocers, bakeries and restaurants. Pair it with the market for a full morning of food shopping.

Victoria Square, the green space in the middle of the city grid, is a few minutes east. It works well for a picnic with whatever you just bought. Gouger Street's restaurants are a natural dinner stop after a Friday evening visit.

Give the market itself an hour if you're browsing, or two if you're eating your way through it and shopping properly. Adding Chinatown easily turns it into half a day.
