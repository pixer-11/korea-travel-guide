## A department store that sells culture instead of clothes

The name tells you how to read the place. A *Kaufhaus* is a department store, and this one stocks culture: books, records, films, gifts and stationery, stacked over several floors on Friedrichstraße. It works the way a department store does. You take the escalators, drift from one department to the next and lose track of time.

The editorial line calls it a massive bookshop, which is accurate but undersells the music and the events. It is also one of the most-reviewed shops in Berlin, and its reviews are consistently glowing. Don't expect a quiet secret. Expect a big, well-run cultural store that Berliners and visitors both pile into, especially at weekends.

## The green wall in the atrium

One thing sets it apart from every other big bookshop. A tall vertical garden by the French botanist Patrick Blanc climbs one wall of the central atrium, a sheet of living plants rising beside the shelves. It's free to look at, and it makes the middle of the store feel more like a courtyard than a sales floor.

Stand at the bottom, then ride the escalators up and look again from each level. The plants change texture as you climb. It's the one view in the building people stop to photograph, so give it a minute before heading into the stacks.

## Midnight on Friedrichstraße

The hours are this building's signature quirk. At the moment it opens 9am to midnight Monday to Friday, and 9am to 11:30pm on Saturday. Sunday is shorter, 1pm to 8pm, which matters in a country where most shops stay shut all Sunday.

That makes it a rare late-evening option in central Berlin. You can see a show nearby, have dinner, then browse fiction at 10:30pm. Check the current schedule before a late trip, because shop hours can change around public holidays.

## Floors worth planning around

The store is big enough that a loose plan helps. Some starting points:

- **English-language books**: a dedicated department, useful if your German stops at "Danke".
- **Music**: Dussmann is especially known for classical recordings and sheet music, alongside other genres on CD and vinyl.
- **Film**: DVDs and Blu-rays, including German cinema.
- **Gifts and stationery**: notebooks, cards and design objects, good for presents that fit in a suitcase.
- **The café**: a place to sit with whatever you've just bought.

Allow at least an hour if you like books. Two hours goes quickly if you're also digging through the music department. The store also runs readings and other events, so check its own events listing to see what's on during your dates.

## Browsing without the weekend crush

Timing is the biggest decision you'll make here. BestTime's forecast puts the quietest window between 9am and 10am on weekdays. The aisles are easiest to move through then, and the staff have time for questions.

Try not to arrive between 11am and 9pm at weekends. That's when it's usually busiest, with queues at the tills and people blocking the shelves you want. Sunday's opening hours sit entirely inside that window, so a Sunday visit means accepting company.

A few habits help:

- **Pay how you like, but carry a backup.** Large stores like this take cards, though Germany still leans on cash more than many countries do.
- **Bring a tote.** German shops usually charge for bags, and books get heavy fast.
- **Ask staff in English.** Booksellers in central Berlin routinely switch languages, and they know their sections well.
- **Go up first.** Start on the top floor and work down, so the café and the exit come last.
- **Watch your volume.** This is a bookshop, and people do stand and read here.

## Getting to Friedrichstraße 90

The store sits on Friedrichstraße in Mitte, between the Spree and Unter den Linden. The nearest stop is **S+U Friedrichstraße**, a major hub served by the U6 and several S-Bahn lines, including the S3, S5, S7 and S9. Leave the station heading south along Friedrichstraße and you'll reach number 90 in a few minutes.

From the south, the U6 also stops at **Unter den Linden**, which puts you one block away. It's an easy, flat walk from most hotels in Mitte.

## Around the block

Dussmann is easy to fit into a day in central Berlin, because so much is within walking distance:

- **Tränenpalast**: the former border-crossing hall beside Friedrichstraße station, now a free exhibition about divided Berlin.
- **Admiralspalast**: the historic theatre just north of the station.
- **Berliner Ensemble**: Brecht's old theatre, across the Weidendammer Brücke over the Spree.
- **Unter den Linden**: the grand boulevard, a block south, leading west to the **Brandenburg Gate** in roughly 15 minutes on foot.
- **Museum Island**: about a 10-minute walk east.

The late weekday hours make a good evening plan. Spend the afternoon at the museums, eat nearby, then finish at Dussmann after the Museum Island galleries have closed for the night.
