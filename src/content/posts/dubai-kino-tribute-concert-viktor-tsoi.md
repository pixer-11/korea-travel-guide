## A voice from 1990, a band on stage tonight

Viktor Tsoi died in 1990, and his voice still leads this show. KINO's concert is built around his original recorded vocals, and the band plays live underneath them. On January 6, 2027, that format comes to Coca-Cola Arena in Dubai.

That makes the night stranger and more moving than an ordinary tribute act. Nobody impersonates Tsoi. The baritone you hear is his, with that flat, unhurried delivery, and the guitars and drums around it are happening in the room.

## Why a Leningrad band fills a Dubai arena

KINO formed in Leningrad in the early 1980s. By the end of that decade it was the biggest rock band in the Soviet Union. Songs like "Khochu peremen!" ("I Want Changes") became anthems of the perestroika years. "Gruppa krovi" ("Blood Type") and "Zvezda po imeni Solntse" ("A Star Called the Sun") are still sung word for word by people born long after 1990.

Tsoi died in a car crash in Latvia in August 1990, aged 28. In Moscow, the graffiti-covered Tsoi Wall on Arbat Street has been a fan shrine ever since. Dubai has a large Russian-speaking community, and the audience for a show like this crosses generations. Parents who owned the cassettes often come with children who found the songs online.

## Coca-Cola Arena at City Walk

The venue is an indoor, air-conditioned arena. It is attached to City Walk, the low-rise outdoor shopping and dining district in Al Wasl, between Al Safa Road and Al Wasl Road.

That location matters for your evening. You walk out of the arena into pedestrian streets lined with restaurants and cafés, not into a car park in the desert. Downtown Dubai and the Burj Khalifa are a short drive east.

## Getting to City Walk

No Dubai Metro station sits at the arena's door. These are the usual ways in:

- **Taxi or ride-hail:** the simplest option. Careem and Uber both operate in Dubai, and RTA taxis queue at City Walk.
- **Metro plus taxi:** ride the Red Line to Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall or another nearby Red Line stop, then take a short taxi.
- **Driving:** City Walk has multi-storey parking, and it fills on big concert nights.

After the encore, everyone leaves at once. Concertgoers often walk a few blocks into City Walk before calling a car, which avoids the pickup crush at the main exits.

## Inside the arena: how a KINO night works

This crowd knows every word. People sing along from the first chorus, often louder than the speakers in the quiet verses. Lyrics are in Russian. If you don't speak it, learning the refrain of "Gruppa krovi" gets you through the loudest moment of most KINO sets.

A few things are typical of concerts at this venue:

- **Security screening** at the entrance, with bag checks. Large bags and professional cameras are generally not allowed.
- **Digital tickets** on phones are the norm, so attendees keep their battery charged for the gate.
- **Card and phone payments** work at the food and drink counters. Dubai is a near-cashless city for this kind of spending.
- **Dress** is casual. Black band T-shirts with Tsoi's face are the unofficial uniform.

Phone torches tend to go up in the slower songs, "Kukushka" especially. Fans usually leave the big filming to the people near the stage and spend the ballads singing.

## January evenings in Dubai

January is Dubai's mildest month. Evenings are cool enough that a light layer is useful outside. It also makes the walk around City Walk before or after the show pleasant rather than punishing.

Many visitors stay in Downtown Dubai, Business Bay or Jumeirah, all a short taxi ride from the arena. Before the show, people often eat on City Walk's terraces. Afterwards, some head to Dubai Mall to see the fountain at the foot of the Burj Khalifa. The organiser and the arena publish the dates, ticket links and entry rules for this concert.
