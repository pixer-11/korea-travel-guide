## Bibs, registration and race weekend

A marathon has no tickets in the concert sense. "Tickets" here means a race entry, and the entry comes with a bib. Entries for 2026 were sold through the race's official site, with categories and fees for each format, including any relay or shorter-format options for the edition.

Big French road races usually need a few documents to issue a bib:

- A valid medical certificate covering competitive running, or a French athletics federation licence
- Photo ID matching the registration name
- The registration confirmation, on paper or on a phone

Runners usually collect bibs at a race expo in the days before the start.

## Getting between Cannes and Nice

The coastal railway does most of the work on race weekend. Regional TER trains run along the shore between Cannes station and Nice-Ville, stopping at Antibes and other coastal towns on the way. Many runners stay in Nice and take the train to Cannes on race morning. Others sleep in Cannes and ride back to Nice once they have finished.

Nice Côte d'Azur Airport sits on the western edge of Nice, right beside the Promenade des Anglais. Tram Line 2 links the airport terminals with the city centre and the port. For anyone flying in, that makes the airport an easy first stop.

Coastal roads close on a marathon Sunday. Driving between the two cities that morning is slow, which is why most spectators use the train instead.

## Cheering on a French race morning

Spectators shout "Allez !" all day. Fans add the runner's first name when it is printed on the bib, or the name of their club. It costs nothing, and runners deep into the second half really do notice it.

A few habits make race morning smoother:

- **One or two spots.** The trains run alongside the course, so supporters often watch in one coastal town and then ride ahead to the Nice finish.
- **Card or phone payment.** Contactless payment is normal in Riviera cafés and bakeries. A few coins still help at small kiosks.
- **A quiet Sunday.** In France many shops close on Sundays. Boulangeries usually open in the morning, so coffee and a croissant at the roadside are easy to find.
- **A meeting point.** Finish areas fill up and phone signal near a big crowd can be patchy. Families usually choose a landmark near the Nice finish ahead of time.

## After the finish in Nice

Nice gives tired legs plenty of flat ground to recover on. The Promenade des Anglais runs for kilometres along the Baie des Anges. Its blue chairs face the water and the pebble beach sits below.

Just inland, the narrow lanes of Vieux Nice (the old town) lead to the Cours Saleya market. Socca, the chickpea pancake, is sold hot from the griddle there. Castle Hill (Colline du Château) rises at the eastern end of the bay. Its stairs give a view back over the coast the course follows, or a good reason to wait a day before climbing.

Most runners stay at least the Sunday night. Rooms near Nice-Ville station or close to the seafront are popular, since the finish, the train and dinner are all within walking distance.
