## Oud at the centre: what Cairo Steps sounds like

Basem Darwisch is an Egyptian oud player and composer who has spent much of his career in Germany. Cairo Steps is the ensemble he built around that double life. It is music rooted in Arabic modes, played by a group that draws on jazz and Western concert traditions.

In practice that means the oud carries the melodies. Around it you can usually expect:

- Egyptian hand percussion setting the rhythmic cycles
- Jazz-style improvisation passed between players
- Long modal introductions before a piece settles into its groove
- A mix of Egyptian and European musicians on stage

Audiences at Arabic music concerts in Cairo tend to be vocal. Applause after a well-turned improvised phrase is normal, not rude. A murmur of approval when the oud lands a phrase is part of the sound of the room.

## A Japanese gift on the Nile

The building has its own story. The original Khedivial Opera House in downtown Cairo burned down in 1971. Its replacement on Gezira opened in 1988, funded largely as a gift from Japan and designed in a modern Islamic style.

Look for the white domes and the arched arcades as you cross the forecourt. The same compound holds the **Museum of Modern Egyptian Art**, plus gardens and cafés where concertgoers gather during intervals. **Cairo Tower**, the lattice-sided concrete tower from 1961, stands a short walk north on the same island.

## Reaching Gezira Island

The metro is the least stressful way in. **Opera station** on **Cairo Metro Line 2** sits right beside the Opera House grounds, one stop across the river from Sadat (Tahrir Square). Sadat is the interchange with Line 1, which makes connections from most of central Cairo simple.

On foot, the **Qasr al-Nil Bridge** links Tahrir Square to the island, guarded by its pair of bronze lions. The crossing takes roughly fifteen minutes. Evening traffic on the bridge is notoriously slow, and ride-hailing drivers often drop passengers at the Opera gates after a crawl across it.

From Zamalek, the leafy northern half of Gezira, a taxi or a longer walk south along the island reaches the grounds.
