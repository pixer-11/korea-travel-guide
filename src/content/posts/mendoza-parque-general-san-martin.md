## Through the English gates

The park starts at a set of ornate wrought-iron gates. These are the Portones del Parque at the end of Av. Emilio Civit, imported from England in the early 1900s, and the Fuente de los Continentes fountain sits just inside them. Beyond, plane trees and long, straight promenades lined with statues run west towards the foothills.

French-Argentine landscape architect Charles Thays laid the park out from 1896. It is enormous, roughly 300 hectares on the city's western edge. Like Mendoza's streets, it is kept green by irrigation channels in a desert climate. Don't plan to see it all on foot in one go.

## Lake, roses and the hill

The main sights form a loose line from the gates to the foothills:

- **The lake:** a long artificial basin with a rowing club and a waterside promenade, the park's busiest stretch.
- **The Rosedal:** the rose garden beside the lake, with formal beds and pergolas. It is best from late spring into summer.
- **Teatro Griego Frank Romero Day:** the open-air amphitheatre carved into the hillside. It hosts the main show of the Fiesta Nacional de la Vendimia, the grape-harvest festival, in early March.
- **Cerro de la Gloria:** the hill topped by the bronze Monumento al Ejército de los Andes, which celebrates San Martín's 1817 crossing of the Andes. The friezes show the campaign, and the summit looks out over the city.
- **Ecoparque:** the former zoo, now run as a wildlife rescue and conservation centre.

The climb up Cerro de la Gloria is the most tiring part. It is a winding road with little shade, so carry water in summer.

## Getting there and timing

From Plaza Independencia, walk west along Av. Sarmiento and continue onto Av. Emilio Civit. The street is shady and full of grand old houses, and the walk takes about 25 minutes. Taxis and ride-hailing apps can drop you at the gates or closer to the lake.

Allow two hours for the gates, lake and Rosedal. Allow half a day if you add the hill. Summer afternoons are hot under a strong sun. October–November and March–April are milder, and in April the leaves turn gold.

Google currently lists the park as open 24 hours. Park services and the Ecoparque keep their own schedules, so check those before you go. The park is a daytime and early-evening place: once it gets dark, the paths and the hill feel empty.

## Doing the park the Mendocino way

Mendoza still slows down in the early afternoon. Families, runners and groups with a mate gourd and thermos fill the lake promenade in the late afternoon and on weekends. This is one of the city's most visited spaces, so if you want the Rosedal quiet, come earlier in the day.

- Bring your own snacks and a thermos. Picnicking on the grass is normal.
- Watch your step at the *acequias*, the open irrigation ditches along the paths and avenues. They are deep enough to twist an ankle.
- Bike rental lets you cover the full loop and still reach the base of the hill. Ask in the centre or near the gates.
- Hikers and cyclists share the Cerro road with cars, so keep to the edge.

On Vendimia weekend in early March, the area around the amphitheatre fills up and nearby roads close. Book show tickets through the official festival channels well ahead.
