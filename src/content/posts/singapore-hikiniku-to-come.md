## Minced meat and rice, nothing else

The name says it all. *Hikiniku to kome* (挽肉と米) is Japanese for "minced meat and rice," and that is the whole idea. Chef Shohei Yamamoto built the Tokyo original around one dish, and the Singapore branch keeps that one-item menu.

That is unusual in a city where most mall restaurants hand you a laminated book of options. Here you don't pick a dish. You turn up for the dish, and so does everyone else in the line.

## What arrives on the plate

The star is *hambāgu*, the Japanese hamburg steak. It sits somewhere between a burger patty without the bun and a home-style meat loaf, and it is a fixture of Japanese family cooking. Hikiniku To Come treats it as something worth queuing for.

Three things set this version apart, and all of them are in the name and the brief:

- **The beef:** 100% Japanese beef, not a blend padded with breadcrumb or pork.
- **The shaping:** patties are formed by hand rather than pressed by machine.
- **The fire:** they are grilled over binchotan, the dense Japanese white charcoal that burns hot and steady with very little smoke.

Binchotan is the reason yakitori counters across Japan swear by it. It sears the outside fast and leaves the inside juicy, with a clean charcoal edge where a gas grill would just give you heat. With a patty this loose and fatty, that sear is what holds it together.

The Tokyo shop built its following on that combination of beef, charcoal and plain rice. Don't arrive expecting sides or a dessert list. Arrive hungry for one thing done carefully.

## Finding it at VivoCity

VivoCity is Singapore's biggest mall, on the waterfront at HarbourFront facing Sentosa. The easiest way in is the MRT. HarbourFront station sits on both the North East Line (purple) and the Circle Line (orange), and it connects straight into the mall's basement, so you never have to step outside.

Coming from the city, the North East Line runs direct from Chinatown, Outram Park and Dhoby Ghaut. Buses and taxis all pull up at the mall's main drop-off along Telok Blangah Road. The mall directory boards and the VivoCity website will show the restaurant's unit. Check them before you go, because the mall is large enough to get lost in.

## Getting a seat without wasting the afternoon

A launch like this draws a crowd. Since opening, Hikiniku To Come has had serious hype and long queues, from locals and visitors alike. Plan around the wait rather than hoping to skip it.

- **Check the booking system first.** Hot openings in Singapore often run digital queues or timed reservations rather than a simple line. Look at the brand's official social channels before you set out.
- **Go off-peak by design.** Mall dining in Singapore peaks at weekday lunch, when nearby office workers come in, and again across weekend dinner. Arriving just as service starts, or in the mid-afternoon lull, usually means a shorter line.
- **Come in a small group.** Pairs and solo diners tend to get seated faster than big tables at busy restaurants.
- **Paying and tipping:** cards and contactless payment are standard at VivoCity. Tipping isn't expected in Singapore, and restaurant bills often already include a service charge.

When you sit down, eat the beef while it is hot. A patty straight off the charcoal is the whole point, and it doesn't wait well while you take photos.

## Making a trip of HarbourFront

You don't need to come all this way just for lunch. VivoCity is also the gateway to Sentosa. The Sentosa Express monorail leaves from the mall's upper level, and the Sentosa Boardwalk starts next door if you would rather walk across.

Eat first and then cross the water for Resorts World Sentosa or the beaches. Or go the other way: the cable car station at HarbourFront Tower runs up to Mount Faber, with views back over the port. The rooftop of VivoCity also has an open-air deck facing the harbour, a good place to walk off a plate of beef and rice.

Leave an hour or two for the meal, queue included. A full day pairs easily with Sentosa.

## Before you set out

This is a new opening, and details like hours, reservations and queue rules tend to change in the first months. Check the restaurant's official channels or the VivoCity directory on the day you plan to go. Prices are on the menu at the restaurant and on its official pages.
