## One night, one country, one region

Amy Lee brings Evanescence to Kuala Lumpur for a single night. The date is November 28, 2026. The band has listed it as its only Southeast Asia show, so fans from Singapore, Jakarta, Bangkok and Manila will be travelling to the same field you are.

That matters for planning. Flights into Kuala Lumpur International Airport that weekend will carry a share of black-clad passengers. Rooms along the train lines that serve Bukit Jalil will fill sooner than an ordinary late-November weekend would suggest.

The draw needs little explanation. *Fallen* came out in 2003 and turned "Bring Me to Life" and "My Immortal" into songs a whole generation can sing word for word. The band formed in Little Rock, Arkansas, and Lee's voice has carried it through every change of line-up since.

## The National Hockey Stadium, Bukit Jalil

The venue sits inside the National Sports Complex in Bukit Jalil, in the southern part of Kuala Lumpur. Locals know it as Stadium Hoki Nasional. It was built for field hockey, and its artificial turf is open to the sky.

Its neighbours are much larger. The National Stadium, Bukit Jalil, the country's biggest football ground, rises next door. Axiata Arena, the indoor venue that hosts many touring acts, is part of the same complex. On a big concert night, expect signage and stewards steering crowds between the buildings.

Know which gate you're heading to before you leave the train. The complex is spread out, and walking to the wrong side of the main stadium can take a long time.

## Getting to Bukit Jalil by rail

The train is the simplest option. Take the LRT Sri Petaling Line to **Bukit Jalil** station, which sits right beside the sports complex. From there it is a walk across the grounds to the hockey stadium.

Useful connections into the Sri Petaling Line:

- **Masjid Jamek**: interchange with the Ampang Line and the Kelana Jaya Line, handy from KLCC or KL Sentral
- **Plaza Rakyat**: a short walk from Petaling Street in Chinatown
- **Hang Tuah**: interchange with the KL Monorail, useful from Bukit Bintang

Driving is possible, but traffic around the complex slows to a crawl on event nights. Parking also fills up early. Travellers coming from the airport should take the KLIA Ekspres to KL Sentral and continue by rail.

## Monsoon season on an open field

This is the planning detail people miss. November is one of Kuala Lumpur's wettest months, and the hockey stadium is an outdoor venue. Heavy rain often arrives in the late afternoon or evening, which is exactly when you'll be queueing.

Pack for it:

- A lightweight poncho; many concert venues ban umbrellas, so check the venue rules
- A zip bag to keep your phone and ticket dry
- Shoes you don't mind soaking, since artificial turf and walkways hold water after a downpour
- A spare T-shirt for the train home

The heat remains when the rain stops. Drink water through the afternoon, not just at the gate.

## Tickets and the official word

The announced facts are the band, the date, the city and the venue. For seating zones, door times, support acts, age limits and the list of permitted items, go to the official source. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site and through the promoter's authorised ticketing partner.

Buy only through those authorised channels. A one-off show that draws fans from across the region attracts resellers, and fake transfers are a common way people end up at the gate without a valid ticket. If your ticket is in an app, download it before you set off. Mobile signal drops when thousands of people are packed onto one field.

## Moving with the Bukit Jalil crowd

Most of the crowd will pay for the train in the same way. Get a **Touch 'n Go** card, sold at many station counters and convenience stores, and top it up before the day. Rapid KL gates also take contactless bank cards. Either way, you avoid the ticket-machine queue that builds after the show.

What experienced Klang Valley concertgoers do:

- **Eat beforehand.** The Pavilion Bukit Jalil mall is a short ride away and has a large food court and restaurants. Eating there means you aren't stuck with stadium snacks.
- **Arrive early.** With assigned zones and security checks, getting there well before the doors open is the calm way in.
- **Skip the Grab rush.** Ride-hailing prices surge and pickups jam the moment the encore ends. Walk to the LRT, or walk a few streets away from the complex before booking.
- **Carry ID.** Malaysian venues often check identification against the ticket name, so bring your passport or a photocopy.
- **Expect bag checks.** Bring a small, light bag so the queue keeps moving.

Malaysia is a largely cash-light country now. DuitNow QR and card payments are common, but have a little cash for small stalls just in case.

## Making a weekend of it

Since this is a destination gig for much of the audience, spend a couple of days in the city. Staying near a Sri Petaling Line station is the most practical choice: Chinatown around Plaza Rakyat, or Bukit Bintang via the Hang Tuah interchange. Both give you a direct ride home without a taxi queue.

The day before, the Petronas Twin Towers and KLCC Park are an easy trip. Petaling Street and Central Market cover an afternoon in Chinatown. On the morning after, the Batu Caves are a KTM Komuter ride north of KL Sentral, and the steps are cooler before mid-morning. Leave the late-night plans for the show itself. The last trains run on a fixed schedule, so check the final departures before you go in.
