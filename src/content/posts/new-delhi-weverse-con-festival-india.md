## Two days of K-pop in Delhi

This is a first for India. HYBE, the Seoul company behind the Weverse fan platform, brings its flagship Weverse Con Festival to New Delhi on March 27 and 28, 2027. Earlier editions were held in South Korea, so this is the brand's first full outing on Indian soil.

The named acts are:

- **TXT** (TOMORROW X TOGETHER)
- **ENHYPEN**
- **LE SSERAFIM**
- **Hwasa**
- other artists alongside them

The format is a festival rather than a single concert. Several groups share the stage across the weekend, so fans of one act end up watching sets from the others. The organiser publishes the dates, the venue and the ticket links on its official Weverse Con pages.

## Tickets, venue and the fan platform

Weverse is where this event lives. HYBE runs its fan memberships, notices and much of its ticketing through the Weverse app. Fans of TXT, ENHYPEN and LE SSERAFIM usually already follow those artist communities there, and that is where the festival's notices appear.

Two-day events of this type normally sell tickets for each day separately. Each day's bill can differ, so fans with one favourite group usually choose the day that group plays. No prices appear in this guide. The official ticketing page shows the current categories and costs.

## Getting to New Delhi and around it

Most international fans fly into Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL). The Delhi Metro's Airport Express (Orange Line) runs from the airport to New Delhi station in central Delhi, avoiding the road traffic on that stretch. Fans from elsewhere in India often arrive by train at New Delhi or Hazrat Nizamuddin stations.

Late March in Delhi sits between the cool winter and the hot season. Days are usually warm and dry, and evenings are cooler. Light cotton clothing and a water bottle suit a full day spent in queues and on a standing floor.

Popular bases for visitors include:

- **Connaught Place**: central, with Blue and Yellow Line metro interchange at Rajiv Chowk
- **Aerocity**: hotel cluster beside the airport, on the Airport Express line
- **South Delhi** (Saket, Hauz Khas): quieter, Yellow and Magenta Line access

## How Delhi's K-pop crowd does a festival day

India's K-pop fandoms are large, organised and young. Many fans travel in groups from other cities and coordinate through fan accounts and the Weverse communities. Matching outfits, printed banners and official lightsticks are the norm, and fans usually sync their lightsticks over Bluetooth to the show once inside.

Getting around follows a few habits:

- **Metro first**: Delhi Metro stations screen every passenger at the entrance with bag scanners, so crowds after a show move slowly through the gates.
- **Women's coach**: the first coach of every Delhi Metro train is reserved for women, a useful option late at night.
- **Ride-hailing**: Uber, Ola and Rapido operate across Delhi; surge pricing after big events is common, so groups usually walk a few streets away from the venue to find a car at a calmer pickup point.
- **Paying**: UPI (QR code) payments are everywhere, from food stalls to auto-rickshaws. International visitors without an Indian bank account usually rely on cards and some cash.

Fans typically arrive well before doors to queue for merchandise, which sells out first at most K-pop events. Bag rules at Indian venues tend to be strict. Power banks, food and large banners are the items fans most often see turned back at the gate.
