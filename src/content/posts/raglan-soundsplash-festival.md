## Three days at the end of the road

Raglan sits where State Highway 23 runs out. Past the town there's only the harbour, the black-sand coast and the long bulk of Mt Karioi. Soundsplash fills this small surf town for one weekend in high summer. This edition runs from Friday, January 22 to Sunday, January 24, 2027.

It's a multi-day music and arts festival, not a single concert. That means camping and repeat trips to the stage. It also means three days of sun, salt and sand in your tent. Earlier editions built their reputation on reggae, roots, dub, hip hop and bass music. The organiser's official site carries the lineup for each year.

## The festival site and the town around it

Over the years, Soundsplash has mostly been held at Wainui Reserve. This is open farmland and park on Wainui Road, a few minutes' drive south-west of the township. It sits on the hills above Ngarunui Beach, so between sets you look out over the Tasman Sea. The organiser's official site lists the venue for each edition.

The township is the other half of the weekend. Bow Street is the main street, lined with old shop verandahs, and it ends at the harbour edge. The Māori name for the harbour and district is Whāingaroa, and you'll see it on signs all over town. Festival-goers drift into town for coffee, groceries and a swim, then head back out for the evening.

## Getting to Raglan

Most people drive. The town is about 45 minutes west of Hamilton on State Highway 23, a winding road over the hills of the Waikato. From Auckland it's roughly two and a half hours: south on State Highway 1 to Hamilton, then west.

- **By car:** SH23 is the only main road in, so traffic bunches up on Friday afternoon of festival weekend.
- **By bus:** a regional bus service links Hamilton and Raglan. Hamilton is also on the intercity coach routes.
- **By air:** Hamilton Airport is the closest. Auckland Airport has far more international flights.
- **Lifts:** groups usually share the drive from Auckland or Hamilton, and that saves on parking space at the site too.

## Tickets, camping and where people stay

The organiser publishes ticket options and passes on its official site, and that's the only reliable place to find them. The festival doesn't list prices anywhere else, and resale listings are a gamble.

Camping is part of the Soundsplash weekend for most people. Past editions offered on-site camping next to the stage area, and plenty of attendees treat the tent as the whole trip. If you'd like a bed, Raglan has holiday parks, baches (Kiwi holiday homes) and backpacker hostels. A summer weekend fills a town this size quickly, so visitors who leave it late often stay in Hamilton and drive in each day.

## Salt water between sets

This is the part of the weekend that sets Soundsplash apart from inland festivals. The ocean is minutes away. In the morning, while the stages are quiet, the campsite empties towards the beach.

- **Ngarunui Beach** is the closest. It's the main swimming beach, with surf lifeguards on patrol in summer. You swim between the red-and-yellow flags.
- **Manu Bay**, further along Wainui Road, has a long left-hand point break that surfers travel across the world to ride. It suits watching more than swimming.
- **The harbour** in town is calmer. On warm days, teenagers jump off the footbridge into the channel at high tide.

Black sand gets hot by midday. New Zealand's summer UV is also stronger than most visitors expect, so the people who spend the day at the beach and the night at the stage tend to carry hats, shirts and sunscreen.

## Festival manners in Whāingaroa

Raglan takes its rubbish seriously. The town is home to Xtreme Zero Waste, a community recycling enterprise, and the festival leans the same way. Regulars sort their waste at the stations and carry a refillable bottle. They also leave their campsite bare on Sunday.

A few more customs worth knowing:

- **Paying:** New Zealand runs on contactless cards and EFTPOS. Cash still works at small stalls, but you rarely need it.
- **Tipping:** it isn't expected at food trucks, cafés or bars. Nobody will look twice if you don't add anything.
- **In the water:** surfers take turns at Manu Bay. The person closest to the peak has the wave, and dropping in on them is the quickest way to make enemies.
- **Te reo Māori:** "kia ora" covers hello and thanks. It's used everywhere, by everyone.
- **The road:** Wainui Road is narrow and hilly. People walking between town and the coast keep well to the side, especially after dark.

## Staying on after Sunday

Many attendees add a day to recover in daylight. Wairēinga, or Bridal Veil Falls, is about 20 minutes' drive from town. A short bush walk and boardwalk lead to a lookout above a waterfall that drops more than 50 metres into a pool.

Mt Karioi has a steep, rewarding track to the summit lookout, best left for legs that haven't danced for three days. Back in town, the wharf has fish and chip shops and boats heading out onto the harbour. Hamilton Gardens, back toward the main highway, is an easy stop on the drive home.
