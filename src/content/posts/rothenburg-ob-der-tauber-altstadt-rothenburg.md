## Inside the ring of walls

Rothenburg's old town is a loop of stone. A largely intact medieval wall circles it, with a covered timber walkway (the Wehrgang) running along its inner face. You can climb up at several towers and walk long stretches looking down onto red tile roofs and backyard gardens.

Inside, cobbled lanes run between half-timbered houses with steep gables. Most streets lead toward the Marktplatz. The streets themselves never close. Only the museums, towers and churches keep hours, and those change with the season.

## The corners worth your time

Plan on a full day, or one night, to see the old town properly. These are the anchors:

- **Plönlein**: the narrow yellow half-timbered house where the lane forks, with the Siebers Tower above and the Kobolzell Gate below. It's the town's most photographed corner.
- **Marktplatz and Rathaus**: the town hall is Gothic on one side and Renaissance on the other. Its tower climb gives the widest view over the roofs.
- **Ratstrinkstube clock**: on certain hours, figures in the gable window act out the Meistertrunk. That's the legend of a mayor who drained a huge tankard of wine in 1631 to stop General Tilly sacking the town.
- **St. Jakob**: the Gothic church holds Tilman Riemenschneider's carved Holy Blood Altar.
- **Burggarten**: the former castle site at the western tip, with views down into the Tauber valley.
- **Medieval Crime Museum**: shame masks, punishment tools and legal history, laid out over several floors.

In winter the Reiterlesmarkt Christmas market fills the Marktplatz. Bakeries all over town sell Schneeballen, strips of fried pastry wrapped into a ball and dusted with sugar or chocolate.

## Arriving by rail or road

Trains reach Rothenburg on a short branch line from Steinach. From the station it's roughly a ten-minute walk west to the Röder Gate, the easiest way into the walls.

Drivers come off the A7 autobahn. Traffic inside the walls is restricted, so use the numbered car parks outside the ring and walk in through the nearest gate. Coach tours following the Romantic Road also stop here, usually as a midday visit.

## Doing Rothenburg after the coaches leave

The old town's rhythm follows the day-trippers. Coach groups arrive late morning and leave by late afternoon, so the town's character changes once they've gone. If you stay inside the walls overnight, you get the Plönlein and the wall walk mostly to yourself at dawn.

A few habits help:

- **Night Watchman tour**: the evening walk with a costumed guide starts from the Marktplatz and runs in English. It's the classic after-dark activity. Check the current schedule at the tourist office on the square.
- **Paying**: small bakeries and wine taverns may still prefer cash, though cards are now widely accepted. In restaurants, tell the server the total you want to pay, including a rounded-up tip, as you hand over payment. Don't leave money on the table.
- **Ordering wine**: this is Franconia, so ask for local Silvaner, often served in a stemmed glass or by the quarter-litre.
- **Wall etiquette**: the Wehrgang is narrow in places. Step into a tower niche to let people pass, and keep voices down near houses that back onto it.

Museum and tower hours shift through the year, so check the official Rothenburg tourism site before you plan your day around them.
