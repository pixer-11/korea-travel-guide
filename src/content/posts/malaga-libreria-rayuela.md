## A bookshop named after a game of hopscotch

*Rayuela* is Spanish for hopscotch. It is also the title of Julio Cortázar's 1963 novel, the one you can read in more than one order by jumping between chapters. Any Spanish-speaking reader will recognise the name on the sign, and that tells you what kind of place you've reached before you walk in.

The shop sits at Calle Cárcer 1, on one of the narrow streets of Málaga's old centre. Most visitors walk this district between the cathedral and the Picasso Museum and never step off the main shopping streets. That is the main reason Rayuela stays under the radar despite being so well reviewed. It is a small independent bookseller, not a chain, and it is easy to walk straight past.

## What you'll find on the shelves

This is a working bookshop in a Spanish city, so expect most of the stock to be in Spanish. That makes it useful if you read the language, or if you are learning it. It is also a good place to pick up a book that says "Málaga" more honestly than a fridge magnet does.

Good things to ask about:

- **Andalusian and Málaga authors.** The city is the birthplace of Picasso and has its own poetry tradition. Ask what the staff would hand a visitor.
- **Cortázar himself.** In a shop named after his novel, ask whether *Rayuela* is in stock.
- **Children's books in Spanish.** Picture books are an easy gift even if the person receiving it doesn't read Spanish.
- **Anything in English or other languages.** Don't assume there is a section. Ask, because stock in small shops changes.

We can't promise specific titles, sections or events, so treat the staff as your catalogue. In an independent shop, a bookseller's recommendation is usually the best thing on offer.

## Fitting it around the clock

The hours are the thing most likely to catch you out. At the moment, Rayuela opens 10am–8pm Monday to Friday and runs straight through. There's no afternoon shutdown, so it works well during the hot middle of the day when many people go indoors anyway.

Saturday is short: currently 10am–2pm only. If Saturday is your one free day, come in the morning, because by mid-afternoon the door will be locked. Sunday it is closed. These times can change, so check Google or the shop's own channels before you make a special trip.

## Getting to Calle Cárcer

Calle Cárcer is in the Distrito Centro, inside the compact old town where walking is the realistic way to get around. Many of these streets are pedestrianised or too tight for comfortable driving. Parking in the centre means an underground car park and a walk.

From the Alameda Principal, the wide avenue along the old town's southern edge, head north into the grid of lanes. From the cathedral or Plaza de la Constitución, it's a short stroll through the centre. Type the full address, C. Cárcer 1, 29008 Málaga, into your map app. The lanes bend enough that the address works better than a general "head that way".

## Browsing politely in a small Spanish bookshop

Small shops in Spain have their own manners, and following them changes how you're received.

- **Greet when you enter.** A quick "*Hola, buenas*" on the way in is normal in Spain, and so is "*Gracias, adiós*" on the way out. Walking in silently reads as cold.
- **Ask in simple Spanish.** "*¿Tiene algo de autores malagueños?*" (Do you have anything by Málaga authors?) gets a conversation going. If your Spanish runs out, switching to English is fine.
- **Ask for gift wrap.** Spanish bookshops often wrap gifts at the till. Say "*¿Me lo envuelve para regalo?*" when you pay.
- **Pay however suits you, but check.** Cards are widely used in Spain. In a small independent shop, it's still sensible to ask before you queue with a pile of books.
- **Mind the space.** In a narrow shop, keep big bags off the shelves and step aside for customers who are browsing.

If you're in town on 23 April, Spain's Día del Libro (Book Day), bookshops across the country make a point of the date. It's a good day to buy one here.

## Pairing it with the rest of the old town

Rayuela works best as a stop on a walk through the centre, not as a trip of its own. Twenty minutes is enough for a quick look. If someone starts recommending books, give it closer to 45.

Málaga's main old-town sights are all within walking distance:

- **Málaga Cathedral**, with its unfinished second tower
- **The Picasso Museum** on Calle San Agustín
- **The Alcazaba and the Roman Theatre** at the foot of Gibralfaro hill
- **Plaza de la Merced**, the square where Picasso was born

Buy something in the morning and read the first chapter over coffee in one of the old-town squares. If what you bought is *Rayuela*, you can choose which chapter that is.
