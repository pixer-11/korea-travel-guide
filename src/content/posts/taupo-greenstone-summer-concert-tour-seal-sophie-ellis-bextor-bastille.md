## Sunday Night on the Big Lake

This is a summer show in the open air. On January 31, 2027, Seal brings the Greenstone Summer Concert Tour to Taupo Amphitheatre. Sophie Ellis-Bextor and Bastille share the bill. The venue sits in central Taupō, a short walk from the shore of Lake Taupō, the largest lake in New Zealand.

Late January is high summer here. The evenings are long, and sunset comes well after 8pm. That means a good part of the night plays out in daylight, with the lake a couple of streets away. As the sun drops, the temperature usually falls quickly, which is something people from the coast often forget.

## Three Acts, Three Eras of Pop

The line-up covers three decades of radio. Each act brings songs most of the crowd will know before the first chorus.

- **Seal** headlines. He is the voice behind "Kiss from a Rose", "Crazy" and "Killer", and his soul-pop ballads tend to turn a field of strangers into a singalong.
- **Sophie Ellis-Bextor** brings disco-pop. "Murder on the Dancefloor" is the one people wait for, and it found a whole new audience in recent years.
- **Bastille** are the London indie-pop band behind "Pompeii", whose wordless "eh-eh-oh" chant suits an outdoor crowd.

The facts here give no running order beyond Seal headlining, and no set lengths or stage times. Treat any times you see quoted elsewhere with caution unless they come from the promoter.

## Two Lakes, Two Nights: The Wānaka–Taupō Split

The tour plays two lake towns on back-to-back nights. On Saturday January 30 it plays Three Parks Outdoor Arena in Wānaka, in the South Island. Twenty-four hours later it plays Taupō, in the middle of the North Island.

That gives you a choice. South Islanders and Queenstown-based visitors usually go to the Wānaka date. For Auckland, Hamilton, Tauranga, Rotorua, Napier and Wellington, Taupō is the natural pick. It sits on State Highway 1, roughly central to all of them.

The calendar helps too. Auckland Anniversary Day in 2027 falls on Monday February 1. For anyone from the Auckland region, the Taupō show lands on the Sunday of a long weekend. That makes the drive home the next day a lot less painful, though it also means a busy weekend for beds in town.

## Reaching Taupo Amphitheatre

Most concert-goers arrive by car. The usual drive times are:

- **Auckland:** about 3.5 hours south down SH1, through Hamilton and Tokoroa.
- **Wellington:** around 4.5–5 hours north, over the Desert Road past Tongariro National Park.
- **Rotorua:** about an hour south on SH5.
- **Napier and Hastings:** roughly two hours west along SH5.

Taupō Airport sits a few minutes south of town and takes small domestic flights. Most international visitors fly into Auckland and drive or take an InterCity coach south. Once you are in town, the amphitheatre is within walking distance of most central motels and the lakefront strip along Lake Terrace. Plenty of people leave the car where they are staying and walk.

## How the Night Works on the Grass

Outdoor gigs in New Zealand have a few habits worth knowing. Here is how a summer show in Taupō usually plays out.

**Paying.** Card and contactless are standard at food and bar stalls across New Zealand, and many outdoor events are cashless. Tipping isn't expected anywhere in the country, including at a gig bar.

**What comes through the gate.** Rules on chairs, rugs, bags and food are set by the event's conditions of entry, which go out with the ticketing details. People at New Zealand outdoor concerts commonly bring a rug or low beach chair where allowed. They also tend to pack a warm layer for after dark.

**The sun.** The New Zealand summer sun burns quickly, even when the air feels mild. Regulars bring hats and sunscreen, because the early sets play under open sky.

**Timing.** Most people with seats or a favourite patch of grass come through the gates well before the headliner. That tends to make the entry queues longest in the early evening. Leaving at the end is slower than arriving, as the whole crowd heads into a compact town centre at once.

**Getting home.** Taupō has nothing like big-city late-night transport. Concert-goers usually walk back to central accommodation or arrange their ride before the night starts. Taxis are limited when thousands of people leave together.

## Turning It into a Lake Weekend

Few people drive three or four hours for one evening and leave. The concert day has plenty around it.

- **Huka Falls**, about 10 minutes north of town. Here the Waikato River squeezes through a narrow rock chute and comes out an almost unreal blue. The walking track from Spa Thermal Park follows the river there.
- **Spa Thermal Park hot stream**, where a warm stream meets the cold Waikato. It is a free and popular spot to sit in the water on a hot afternoon.
- **Craters of the Moon**, a geothermal walk of steaming vents just north of town.
- **The lakefront**, with swimming beaches along the shore and, on clear days, Ruapehu, Ngauruhoe and Tongariro visible across the water to the south.
- **Tongariro Alpine Crossing**, about an hour's drive south, for anyone staying on after the long weekend with the fitness for a full-day alpine hike.

Late January is peak season, so the town fills up. Visitors who can't find a bed in central Taupō often stay at Kinloch, Acacia Bay or further around the lake at Turangi.
