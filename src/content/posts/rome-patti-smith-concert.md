## Horses, in Renzo Piano's halls

Patti Smith plays Rome on December 6, 2026. The venue is the Auditorium Parco della Musica, Renzo Piano's complex of three large halls. From outside they look like giant beetle shells clad in grey lead. The halls curve around an open-air amphitheatre, the cavea. In December, though, shows move indoors, under cherry-wood ceilings built for orchestral acoustics.

That acoustic matters for this artist. Smith's voice swings between spoken word and a full-throated howl. Halls designed for the Santa Cecilia orchestra carry both registers without blur.

## What she brings to the stage

Smith has spent five decades merging rock and poetry. Her 1975 debut *Horses* opens with her rewrite of "Gloria", still a regular closer. Other songs fans tend to wait for include:

- "Because the Night", co-written with Bruce Springsteen
- "People Have the Power", usually sung as a crowd chant
- "Dancing Barefoot" and "Pissing in a River"

She is also a National Book Award winner for her memoir *Just Kids*. Her concerts often fold readings and poems between songs. The set list and band for this date are not part of the facts here, so expect her catalogue rather than a guaranteed track.

## Reaching Viale Pietro de Coubertin

The Auditorium sits at Viale Pietro de Coubertin 30. That is about 3km north of Piazza del Popolo, in a district built around sport and modern architecture.

- **Metro + tram:** Line A to Flaminio, then Tram 2 north to the Apollodoro stop, a short walk from the entrance.
- **Bus:** route 910 runs from Termini station toward the Auditorium area.
- **Taxi:** the Auditorium has a taxi rank, though queues form after big shows.

Nearby are Pier Luigi Nervi's Palazzetto dello Sport, directly across the road, and Zaha Hadid's MAXXI museum, about ten minutes' walk away. During construction, workers uncovered the foundations of a Roman villa. Its remains are displayed in a small archaeological area within the complex.
