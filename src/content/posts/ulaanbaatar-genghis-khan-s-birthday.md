## A holiday set by the lunar calendar

The date moves every year. Mongolia marks Chinggis Khaan's birthday on the first day of the first winter month of the lunar calendar, which puts it in October or November. In 2026 it falls on November 10.

It has been a public holiday since 2012 and is also known as Mongolian Pride Day. In Mongolian you will hear it called *Chinggis Khaany törsön ödör*. Government offices, banks and schools close, so the city feels more like a long Sunday than a working Tuesday.

## Sükhbaatar Square and the Government Palace

The official ceremonies take place on Sükhbaatar Square, the wide paved plaza at the centre of Ulaanbaatar. On its north side stands the Government Palace. A huge seated bronze of Chinggis Khaan sits in its colonnaded front, with statues of Ögedei and Kublai beside him. The tributes are laid at the foot of that figure.

Within a short walk of the square:

- **Chinggis Khaan National Museum**, the large museum that opened in 2022 on the old Natural History Museum site
- **National Museum of Mongolia**, just west of the square
- **Choijin Lama Temple Museum**, a short walk south

Many families also drive about 54 km east to the stainless-steel **Chinggis Khaan Equestrian Statue** at Tsonjin Boldog. Visitors climb up through the horse's neck to a viewing platform on its head.

## What the day looks like

There is no lineup, and no part of the day is ticketed. The state marks the birthday with a formal ceremony on the square. Officials attend, the national flag is raised, and honour guards stand in the red, blue and gold uniforms of the Mongol imperial era.

Mongolians mark the birthday in the street too. Many people wear the *deel*, the traditional long robe, and photograph themselves in front of the palace. Shops and restaurants mostly stay open, while state institutions close. Opening times for the museums on a holiday are posted on each museum's own site.

Mid-November in Ulaanbaatar is properly cold. Daytime temperatures usually stay below freezing, and the square is open and windswept. Locals who stand through the ceremony wear down coats, felt or fur hats and thick boots.

## Standing with the crowd on Chinggis Day

The ceremony is a national observance, not a festival. People behave on the square much as they would at a memorial.

- **Photos** are normal and welcome. Tourists usually keep a respectful distance from the honour guard and the officials' line.
- **The name**: Mongolians say *Chinggis Khaan*, not "Genghis". Using it goes down well.
- **Getting there**: the square is a walk from most central hotels on Peace Avenue. Taxis are slow near the centre when roads around the palace close for the ceremony.
- **Payment**: cards work in most central cafés and museums. Many locals pay by bank-app QR codes.
- **Tsonjin Boldog**: drivers usually leave in the morning so they are back before the early winter dark, which falls around 5pm.

People often warm up afterwards over *buuz* (steamed mutton dumplings) and milk tea, *süütei tsai*, in the cafés around the square.
