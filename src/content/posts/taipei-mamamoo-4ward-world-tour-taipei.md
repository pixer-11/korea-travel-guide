## Two nights, one finale

The tour ends in Taipei. MAMAMOO close their 4WARD World Tour with two shows at Taipei Arena on November 28 and 29, 2026. That makes this weekend the last stop of the run, and finales tend to bring extra emotion from the stage and from the crowd.

Both nights are sold out. MAMAMOO formed in 2014 under RBW, and Solar, Moonbyul, Wheein and Hwasa built a name on live vocals rather than playback. Taiwan's MooMoo fanbase has followed them for years, so the room will know every word.

## Getting to Taipei Arena

Getting there is easy. Taipei Arena, known locally as 臺北小巨蛋 (Taipei Xiaojudan, "the Little Dome"), stands on Nanjing East Road Section 4. **Taipei Arena Station (G16)** on the green Songshan–Xindian Line brings you up beside the building, and Exit 2 is the usual way out for the arena.

Other routes in:

- **Nanjing Fuxing (G15/BR11):** one stop west, where the green line meets the brown Wenhu Line.
- **Nanjing Sanmin (G17):** one stop east, an easy walk along Nanjing East Road.
- **Taipei Songshan Airport:** a few stops north on the Wenhu Line. Fans flying in from Seoul Gimpo or Tokyo Haneda often land here, close to the arena.

An EasyCard handles every MRT ride, and most convenience stores in the city sell and top them up.

## Tickets for a sold-out finale

There is no general sale left to wait for. Both shows are sold out, so the only way in now is a ticket transferred by its holder through the official ticketing channel's own rules.

Taiwan treats ticket scalping as an offence, and fake e-tickets for K-pop shows turn up in social media resale posts. Fans without a ticket usually come for the atmosphere outside. They queue for merch, swap fan-made freebies and gather on the arena plaza in the hours leading up to doors.

## How the night works for MooMoos

K-pop fan habits apply here, along with some Taipei touches.

- **Lightsticks:** the official MAMAMOO lightstick, nicknamed the Moobong, fills the bowl. Venues like this one often sync them to the show wirelessly once it starts.
- **Fanchants:** Korean fanchants are shouted between lines, and Taiwanese fans learn them phonetically. Practising them in the MRT queue is normal.
- **Freebies:** fan clubs often hand out slogans, banners or photo cards near the entrances. Taking one and thanking the person is the usual etiquette.
- **Merch:** tour merch lines at K-pop arena shows are often the longest queue of the day. Fans usually spend hours in line at the stands on show days.

Taipei crowds are orderly. People line up in neat single files at merch stands and station gates, and walking around a queue gets noticed fast. The MRT has a strict no-eating, no-drinking rule inside the paid area, so fans finish their bubble tea outside the gates.

Late November in Taipei is cool and often damp. The northeast monsoon brings drizzle, so a light jacket and a compact umbrella are what most people carry in the plaza queue.

## Around Nanjing East Road

The arena sits in a busy grid of offices and malls, and that makes eating easy. The streets around Nanjing East Road Sections 3–5 are packed with cafés, noodle shops and convenience stores. The underground passages near the stations are where many fans eat dinner on show days.

For a proper night-market meal, **Liaoning Street Night Market** lies a short walk from Nanjing Fuxing. It is a compact strip known for seafood stir-fries and stalls that stay open late. Bigger **Raohe Street Night Market** is further east near Songshan Station on the same green line, so it is an easy ride after the show.

## Leaving after the encore

Thousands of people leave at once. Taipei Arena Station gets very crowded the moment the house lights come up, and staff use barriers to meter the flow into the platforms.

Many fans skip that crush. They walk ten minutes along Nanjing East Road to Nanjing Fuxing or Nanjing Sanmin and board there. The MRT runs until around midnight, and taxis line up along Dunhua North Road for anyone heading somewhere the metro does not reach.
