## Two days, four distances

Jakarta Running Festival packs four race distances into one weekend. The dates are Saturday, October 24 and Sunday, October 25, 2026. It is one of Indonesia's leading road races run to international standard, so you get measured courses and a proper field.

The distances:

- 5K
- 10K
- Half marathon
- Full marathon, on Sunday, October 25

The official source sets which shorter races fall on which day. Check it before you plan a weekend around a single distance.

## The 5-hour cut-off and the heat

The marathon cut-off is five hours, and organisers enforce it. It exists because Jakarta is hot and humid all year, and October falls at the turn toward the wet season. Treat it as a hard limit, not a target.

If your recent marathons have taken longer than about four and a half hours, choose the half instead. The humidity adds time even for runners who train in warm climates. Racers flying in from cooler countries should arrive several days early so the body has time to adjust to the sweat rate.

On the course, take water at every station, even in the opening kilometres. Salt tablets or an electrolyte drink you already trust will serve you better than anything new on race morning.

Getting around central Jakarta:

- **MRT Jakarta**: the North–South line runs under Jalan Sudirman and Jalan Thamrin, the city's main business spine.
- **TransJakarta**: dedicated bus lanes cover most of the city and connect with the MRT.
- **Gojek and Grab**: motorbike taxis get through traffic faster than cars, which helps before dawn.

Jakarta traffic is heavy at almost any hour. Plan your route to the start the night before, and choose a hotel near an MRT station if you can.

## Race-morning habits in Jakarta

Running in Jakarta follows the clock of the sun. Locals run before dawn, and Sunday mornings on Sudirman–Thamrin are a weekly Car Free Day when the avenue is full of runners and cyclists. The marathon falls on a Sunday, so expect the same early-rising crowd energy.

Practical behaviour that helps:

- **Pay cashless.** QRIS codes are everywhere, and Gojek and Grab take in-app payment. Keep some rupiah in small notes for street vendors.
- **Book your ride early.** Ride-hailing demand jumps near big race starts; booking a few minutes earlier than you think you need is the usual workaround.
- **Eat breakfast you know.** Nasi uduk and bubur ayam (rice porridge with chicken) sell from carts at dawn, but they are better as post-race food than as fuel.
- **Mind the drink stations.** Step to the side before you slow down, and drop cups where volunteers are collecting them.
- **Learn two words.** "Terima kasih" (thank you) goes a long way with the volunteers who stand out in the humidity for hours.

After you finish, get into air-conditioning and drink before you go looking for a celebration meal.
