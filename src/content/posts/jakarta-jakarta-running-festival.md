## The 5-hour cut-off and the heat

The marathon cut-off was five hours, and organisers said they would enforce it. The limit exists because Jakarta is hot and humid all year, and October falls at the turn toward the wet season. It was a hard limit, not a target.

Runners whose recent marathons have taken longer than about four and a half hours are usually better suited to the half. The humidity adds time even for runners who train in warm climates. Racers from cooler countries generally need several days for the body to adjust to the sweat rate.

In heat like this, experienced runners take water at every station, even in the opening kilometres. Salt tablets or a familiar electrolyte drink serve better than anything new on race morning.

## Venue, tickets and getting around

Getting around central Jakarta:

- **MRT Jakarta**: the North–South line runs under Jalan Sudirman and Jalan Thamrin, the city's main business spine.
- **TransJakarta**: dedicated bus lanes cover most of the city and connect with the MRT.
- **Gojek and Grab**: motorbike taxis get through traffic faster than cars, which helps before dawn.

Jakarta traffic is heavy at almost any hour. A hotel near an MRT station makes a pre-dawn trip to a race start much simpler.

## Race-morning habits in Jakarta

Running in Jakarta follows the clock of the sun. Locals run before dawn, and Sunday mornings on Sudirman–Thamrin are a weekly Car Free Day when the avenue is full of runners and cyclists. The marathon was scheduled for a Sunday, the same morning as Car Free Day.

Local habits that help:

- **Cashless payment.** QRIS codes are everywhere, and Gojek and Grab take in-app payment. Small rupiah notes still help with street vendors.
- **Early rides.** Ride-hailing demand jumps near big race starts; requesting a car a few minutes earlier than seems necessary is the usual workaround.
- **Familiar breakfast.** Nasi uduk and bubur ayam (rice porridge with chicken) sell from carts at dawn, but they are better as post-race food than as fuel.
- **Drink-station manners.** Runners step to the side before slowing down and drop cups where volunteers are collecting them.
- **Two useful words.** "Terima kasih" (thank you) goes a long way with the volunteers who stand out in the humidity for hours.

After a race in this heat, air-conditioning and fluids come before any celebration meal.
