## Why this palace stands out

Deoksugung is the only one of Seoul's five grand palaces with a stone Western-style building inside its walls, Seokjojeon, built in 1910 under Japanese colonial-era influence. That mix of hanok and neoclassical stone sets it apart from Gyeongbokgung or Changdeokgung. It became the residence of Emperor Gojong after 1897 and stayed the seat of the short-lived Korean Empire.

Entry sits directly opposite Seoul City Hall on Sejong-daero, one of the most recognizable intersections in the city. Seoul City Hall Station (Line 1 or 2, Exit 2) drops you almost at the gate.

## What to actually see

The grounds are compact compared to Gyeongbokgung, so two hours covers it comfortably.

- **Daehanmun Gate**: the main entrance, site of the guard ceremony
- **Junghwajeon Hall**: the throne hall, restored after a 1904 fire
- **Seokjojeon**: the stone Western-style hall, now a museum wing
- **Deoksugung Stone Wall Road**: a tree-lined path popular for a slow walk after the palace itself

The Changing of the Royal Guard ceremony runs several times a day at Daehanmun Gate; it's the palace's signature draw and photographed more than anything else on site. Check the posted schedule board near the gate on arrival, since exact ceremony times shift seasonally.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

Deoksugung is closed every Monday, unlike some palaces that rotate a different closing day, so don't show up expecting a quiet Monday morning. It runs 9am to 9pm the rest of the week, giving you a rare evening slot most palaces don't offer.

Weekends get heaviest between 11am and 8pm, when tour groups and the guard ceremony crowd overlap at the gate. If you want breathing room, arrive right at 9am or come after 7pm to catch the illuminated grounds with far fewer people.

This is a very popular, well-reviewed stop rather than a quiet find, so plan for company at the main ceremony times regardless of day.

## Watching the ceremony without the scrum

The guard-changing draws a dense semicircle of phones within minutes of each showing, so claim a spot at the barrier along Daehanmun Gate at least ten minutes early.

- Stand on the left side facing the gate for a cleaner sightline to the guards' entrance
- Keep the stone wall path for after the ceremony, once the gate crowd disperses
- Entry tickets are bought at the small booth beside the gate, cash and card both accepted; there's no need to pre-book online

Combine the visit with Seoul Museum of Art, which sits inside the same palace grounds, or cross Sejong-daero to Cheonggyecheon Stream for a cooler walk once the palace closes.
