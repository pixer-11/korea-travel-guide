## Five days on the Han River

The festivities take place on the riverfront. The programme runs from December 30, 2026 to January 3, 2027, centred on Bach Dang pedestrian street, the promenade along the Han River's west bank. The other anchor is Dragon Bridge, the steel dragon that carries traffic across to the beach side of the city.

The countdown events take place on December 31. The Da Nang Concert follows on January 1, 2027, which is a public holiday in Vietnam.

In Vietnamese the occasion is Tết Dương lịch, the Gregorian New Year. It is a lighter holiday than Lunar New Year (Tết), which falls weeks later and is when many family businesses close for days.

## Countdown night and the concert

On December 31 the crowd spreads along Bach Dang and onto the bridge approaches. The dragon's body is lined with LED lights that cycle through colours after dark. Its head faces east, toward the sea.

The Han River Bridge, a short walk north along Bach Dang, gives you a second view back across the water toward the dragon. Han Market sits one block inland from the promenade, and the cafés and street-food stalls in those blocks serve the crowd.

## Reaching Bach Dang and Dragon Bridge

The venue is close to almost everything in Da Nang. Here is what shapes the trip:

- **Airport:** Da Nang International Airport is about 3km southwest of the river, so it's a short Grab ride outside road closures.
- **Where people stay:** On the west bank, hotels near Bach Dang are within walking distance. Across Dragon Bridge, the hotels along My Khe Beach are a short ride away.
- **Closures:** Big event nights usually bring traffic restrictions around the bridge approaches, so ride-hail drop-offs tend to end a few blocks back.
- **Weather:** December falls at the tail of central Vietnam's wet season. Showers are common and evenings are mild rather than hot, so a light rain layer earns its place.

## Holding a spot on the Han River promenade

Most of the crowd is local families, and the night has its own routines:

- **Spots:** Revellers typically take their places along the river railing and the bridge approaches well before midnight. Families often bring low plastic stools or mats.
- **Motorbike parking:** Attended lots (*gửi xe*) hand out a paper ticket, and the attendant expects it back when you collect the bike.
- **Paying:** Street vendors and small cafés take cash in dong. Grab is the usual way to get a ride, and hotels and larger restaurants take cards.
- **Getting home:** Ride-hail demand spikes after midnight. Many people walk inland to Tran Phu, the street running parallel to Bach Dang, before calling a car.

The days after the concert are good for daylight trips. Son Tra Peninsula, with the tall white Lady Buddha statue at Linh Ung Pagoda, is a short drive northeast. My Khe Beach fills with local swimmers in the early morning.
