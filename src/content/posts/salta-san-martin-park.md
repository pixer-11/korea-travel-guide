## Palms, pedal boats and a hill above you

The lake is the centre of everything here. Tall palms ring the water, and rented pedal boats move slowly across it. The green slope of Cerro San Bernardo rises behind the park, close enough to fill most of your photos.

At one end, José de San Martín stands on his plinth. He's the independence general whose name is on squares across Argentina. The paths are wide and paved, with benches set out under the trees, so you can do a full lap of the water at an easy pace.

This is one of the most-reviewed outdoor spaces in Salta. Expect families, school groups and couples, not solitude.

## What fills an hour or two

Most visitors combine a few of these:

- **Pedal boats on the lake.** Rent them at the waterside stand. Prices are posted there.
- **The San Bernardo cable car (Teleférico).** Its lower station sits on the park's edge. It carries you up to the lookout over the city's grid of streets.
- **Museo de Ciencias Naturales.** Salta's natural sciences museum is inside the park grounds, which helps on a hot afternoon. Check its hours separately.
- **A lap of the lake.** Walk it to get your bearings before heading up the hill.

If you take the cable car up, budget an extra hour. Plenty of people walk down the hillside steps instead of riding back.

## Getting there and timing

The address is Av. San Martín 27, roughly a 15-minute walk southeast from Plaza 9 de Julio and the cathedral. Head along the pedestrian streets toward the hill and you can't miss it. Taxis and app rides all know it as "Parque San Martín".

At the moment the park opens 7am–10pm every day. Park hours can shift through the year, so check before planning a late visit. The boat stand, the cable car and the museum each keep their own schedules.

Salta sits at more than 1,100 metres. The midday sun is strong even in winter, so the morning shade under the palms is worth planning around.

## Doing it the Salteño way

Use the Spanish names: *Parque San Martín*, *botes a pedal*, *el teleférico*. Not everyone at the kiosks speaks English. Carry small peso notes for the boats and snack stands, because card readers aren't guaranteed at outdoor stalls.

You'll see groups on the grass passing a gourd of mate. You're not expected to join in, but if you're offered a sip, hand it back to whoever is serving. Don't wipe the metal straw first. Saying "gracias" means you're finished, so only say it when you are.

Many Salta shops close for the long midday break. The park stays open, which makes it a good place to spend those hours. Around late afternoon, families arrive once the heat eases, and the boat queue grows with them. Arriving in the morning usually means a shorter wait for a boat and an emptier path around the water.

Keep an eye on your bag near the cable car entrance and the boat stand, where the crowds bunch up most.
