## Why go

There's no other weekend in Austin quite like this one. For three days the city's usual slow drawl gives way to the shriek of twenty V6 hybrid engines, a full-blown music festival bolted onto a race weekend, and a crowd that mixes Texas oil money, European F1 diehards, and curious first-timers. For 2026, the music side featured Maroon 5 and Post Malone as headliners.

Circuit of the Americas has hosted the US Grand Prix since 2012, and it's built specifically for this. It has 20 corners, a dramatic uphill run to Turn 1, and a sequence modeled loosely on Silverstone's Maggotts-Becketts. Sightlines from the grandstands let you follow a whole lap rather than a blur.

## Getting there

COTA sits in southeast Travis County, about 15-20 minutes from downtown Austin depending on traffic, which on race weekend means considerably longer.

- **Fly into** Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (AUS). It's the closest airport and usually adds extra flights for race week
- **Get to the circuit** via shuttle services from downtown and park-and-ride lots, which typically operate for each event
- **Rideshare** works, but surge pricing and long pickup queues are common after the race and concerts end

Driving yourself is possible, but parking fills early and exit traffic afterward can take well over an hour.

## The race weekend itself

Friday usually brings practice sessions and a lower-key crowd, making it the easiest day to explore the paddock area and grandstands without shoulder-to-shoulder crowds. Saturday is qualifying, with intensity building as the grid order gets set. Sunday is race day, the big one, when the amphitheater-style main grandstand and the Turn 1 hill fill early.

For 2026, Maroon 5 and Post Malone were announced as headliners for evening sets, planned to turn the infield into a festival ground once the cars went quiet. The schedule placed practice, qualifying and concerts across Friday and Saturday, with the Grand Prix itself as Sunday's centerpiece.

## Where to sit and what to bring

Ticket tiers generally range from general admission (GA) infield access to reserved grandstand seats at specific corners, plus Paddock Club hospitality for those wanting climate control and better food.

- **Turn 1** grandstands: dramatic elevation change, great for overtakes into the first corner
- **Main straight/start-finish**: best for watching the start and catching the podium ceremony
- **GA infield**: cheaper and mobile, but with no guaranteed sightline. A small stool helps

October in Austin is still often hot, with daytime highs into the 80s°F, so sun protection matters more than many visitors assume. Trackside is loud, so earplugs are standard kit. A portable charger is useful too, since cell networks strain under the crowd.

## How to visit like a local

Austinites who go regularly treat Friday practice as the smart day: smaller crowds, easier parking, and a chance to walk the grounds before the Sunday crush.

Most payment at COTA concessions and merchandise stands is card or mobile-pay rather than cash, so an ATM is rarely needed. Shuttle queues move faster from pickup points slightly away from the main gate than from the crowd right outside it.

The classic first-timer mistake is underestimating exit time. Leaving before the podium ceremony or the final concert set to "beat traffic" is common, but locals who've done it a few times say the shuttle and rideshare lines are just as long whether you leave at lap 50 or after the finish.
