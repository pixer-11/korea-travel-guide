## A desert rock band in a tiered theatre

Queens of the Stone Age don't usually get rooms like this. The California band, led by Josh Homme since the late 1990s, normally plays sweaty halls and festival fields. In Singapore the You'll See World Tour lands at The Star Theatre on 2 December 2026. That puts their heavy, hypnotic riffs inside a steep, curved auditorium built for musicals and award shows.

That contrast is the reason to go. The Star Theatre's seating rises in tiers that wrap around the stage, so even the upper levels feel close to the band. You hear a loud, tight band that leans on rhythm, the sound behind "No One Knows", "Go With the Flow" and "Little Sister", in a room built for clear sound.

## The venue: The Star Theatre, Buona Vista

The Star Theatre is part of The Star Performing Arts Centre at 1 Vista Exchange Green. The building is a glass and steel block with a shopping mall, The Star Vista, on its lower floors. The theatre sits on top of the mall, and escalators carry you up from the shops to the auditorium foyers.

The surrounding area is one-north, Singapore's business and research district. It is full of office towers and campus buildings, and it goes quiet in the evenings, apart from the mall and the theatre crowd. Holland Village, with its bars and late-night food, is one MRT stop or a short taxi ride away.

## Getting to Buona Vista

The MRT is how most concertgoers travel here. Buona Vista station serves two lines:

- **East-West Line (green):** direct from Raffles Place, City Hall, Orchard-adjacent Tiong Bahru and Jurong East
- **Circle Line (orange):** links to Holland Village, Botanic Gardens and the Dhoby Ghaut interchange
- **one-north station (Circle Line):** also within walking distance, on the other side of the complex

From Buona Vista the theatre is a few minutes on foot, and signs for The Star Vista guide you along the route. Contactless bank cards and EZ-Link cards both work at MRT gates. Visitors usually tap the same card they use for shopping.

Taxis and ride-hailing cars collect passengers outside The Star Vista. After a full-house show, the pick-up area gets crowded and fills up quickly, so many people walk to the MRT instead.

## Seats, sound and what the night looks like

Expect a big rock show in a seated house. The Star Theatre is laid out with stalls at the front and two raised circles behind and above. The rake is steep, so sightlines from the circles are clear rather than distant.

With a band this loud, many fans stand once the first song starts, seats or not. Earplugs are common at Queens shows, and a pair takes up no room in a pocket. Opening acts, set times and ticket categories belong to the tour promoter. Those details appear on the ticket and the promoter's ticket listing.

December is part of Singapore's northeast monsoon season. Heavy afternoon and evening showers are normal, but the theatre and mall are indoors. A small umbrella covers the walk from the station.

## Doing the night the Singapore way

Concert nights at The Star follow a set routine. Ticket holders typically eat at The Star Vista first. Its food court and casual restaurants are filled with office workers and gig crowds before the doors open. Fans who want more choice usually go to Holland Village for dinner or drinks before the show, then take one stop on the Circle Line.

Some local norms are worth knowing:

- **Bag checks are standard** at the theatre entrance, and large bags slow you down.
- **Professional cameras with detachable lenses** are normally not permitted at Singapore concerts; phones are fine.
- **Card and mobile payment** work almost everywhere in the mall, including the food court.
- **Drinks inside the auditorium** follow venue rules, and the foyer bars are where most people buy them.
- **Merch stands** sit in the foyers, and their queues are longest during the half hour before the band comes on.

Singaporean audiences are attentive and generally polite, though a Queens crowd is louder than most. When the house lights come up, people go straight to the MRT. Service finishes around midnight, so anyone staying in the east of the island usually leaves without lingering.

## Where to stay and what's nearby

Most visitors stay central and ride the East-West Line out. Hotels around Tanjong Pagar, Outram and the Raffles Place area are a direct trip with no change of line.

With a free afternoon, the Circle Line also reaches the Singapore Botanic Gardens, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, three stops from Buona Vista. The old Rochester Park colonial bungalows, now restaurants, are next to the station. Dempsey Hill, further east, is the place for a long lunch among former army barracks.
