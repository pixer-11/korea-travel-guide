## A late kitchen on an old square

Hausvogteiplatz is a small, angular square tucked behind Gendarmenmarkt. BLESS Restaurant sits at number 10, among the solid stone facades of Berlin-Mitte's historic centre. Most kitchens around here wind down well before the night does. This one keeps serving until midnight every day of the week.

That late close is the first practical thing to know. It makes BLESS a reliable option after a concert at the Konzerthaus, a long day at Museum Island or a late flight into the city. It's an Asian restaurant, consistently well rated across a large and steady volume of reviews. The price level is on the pricier side, so it's a sit-down dinner rather than a quick bite.

## Getting to Hausvogteiplatz

The U-Bahn drops you almost at the door. Take the **U2** to **Hausvogteiplatz** station, and you come up on the square itself. Other options within a short walk:

- **Stadtmitte** (U2 and U6), a few minutes west along Mohrenstraße and Taubenstraße
- **Spittelmarkt** (U2), one stop east
- **Französische Straße** and **Unter den Linden** on the U6, for coming down from the north side of Mitte

On foot, Gendarmenmarkt is roughly five minutes away. The Auswärtiges Amt (Federal Foreign Office) building and Werderscher Markt are just to the north. If you're walking from Museum Island, allow about fifteen minutes heading south over the Spree.

## Dinner on Berlin's old fashion square

The square has a past worth knowing before you sit down. From the mid-1800s until the 1930s, Hausvogteiplatz was the centre of Berlin's garment trade, the *Konfektion*. Fashion houses and workshops filled the buildings around it, and many were Jewish-owned. Nazi persecution destroyed that industry.

Look at the U-Bahn exit when you arrive. A memorial stands there: a set of mirrored steps engraved with the names of the fashion houses that once operated here. The steps are easy to miss in the dark, so look for them on the way in rather than after dinner. They take a minute to read, and they give the square a weight that most restaurant streets don't have.

## The 3pm lull and the weekend crush

The crowd data here is clear. On weekdays, BLESS is quietest between **3pm and 5pm**. The kitchen opens at 11:30am Monday to Friday, so a late lunch in that window is the easiest table you'll get all week.

Weekends run on a different clock. The doors don't open until **2pm on Saturday and Sunday**, so don't plan a weekend lunch any earlier. Across the weekend, 2pm to 11pm counts as the calmer stretch overall. Inside that stretch, though, **7pm to 10pm** is the busiest it gets.

Arrive in that peak without a booking and you risk a wait or being turned away. The fix is simple: eat early, from 2pm into the late afternoon, or come late. A table after 10pm still leaves you plenty of time before the midnight close.

## Reading the menu and the bill

This is a mid-to-upscale meal by Berlin standards. Expect a proper evening out, not a noodle-bar price. We don't list prices or particular dishes here because they change. The current menu and prices are posted by the restaurant itself, so check them before you go if budget matters.

A few habits make the ordering easier:

- **Ask what's built for sharing.** Many Asian kitchens send plates to the middle of the table, and staff will tell you how much to order for your group.
- **Flag spice and allergies early.** German menus often mark allergens, but say it out loud anyway.
- **Water isn't automatic.** In Germany you order it, and it usually comes as bottled still (*still*) or sparkling (*mit Kohlensäure*).

## Booking, paying and tipping in Mitte

A reservation is worth making for any weekend evening. The square draws office crowds and visitors from Gendarmenmarkt, and the 7pm to 10pm weekend peak fills up. On a weekday afternoon you can usually just walk in.

Paying in Germany has its own routine. You don't leave money on the table and walk out. Ask for the bill (*Die Rechnung, bitte*), and the server comes to you with a card reader or a wallet. Cards are widely accepted in central Mitte. Still, Germany is more cash-minded than most of Europe, so carry some euros in case.

Tip at the moment you pay, not afterwards. Tell the server the total you want to pay, including the tip, or say *Stimmt so* ("keep the change") if you're paying cash. Around 5 to 10 percent is normal for good table service. Germans often split bills individually, so asking for *getrennt* (separate) is entirely ordinary.

## Before and after: Gendarmenmarkt to Friedrichstraße

BLESS fits neatly into an evening in this part of Mitte. Before dinner, walk to **Gendarmenmarkt** and look at the Konzerthaus framed by the French and German Cathedrals. It's one of the most handsome squares in the city. In December it also hosts one of Berlin's best-known Christmas markets.

To the west, **Friedrichstraße** runs north toward Unter den Linden. You'll pass Galeries Lafayette and the Quartier 206 shopping arcade. **Checkpoint Charlie** is about fifteen minutes' walk south along the same street.

After a late dinner, the walk north to the **Spree** and the illuminated Berliner Dom takes around fifteen minutes. With a midnight close, you can eat late and still take that walk.
