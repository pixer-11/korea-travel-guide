## Drums on the fourth Sunday

The 2027 main event falls on January 24. Dinagyang always lands on the fourth Sunday of January. It is a Catholic feast for the Santo Niño and a street dancing contest at once, so a Mass and a drumline can share the same morning in downtown Iloilo City.

The festival goes back to 1968, when a replica of the Santo Niño came to Iloilo from Cebu. The dances draw on the older Ati-Atihan tradition. Performers in dark body paint, feathered headdresses and woven costumes act out stories of the Ati, the island's earliest people, and of the Child Jesus.

## What the day looks like

Groups called tribes compete, each one made up of dozens of dancers with a drum corps behind them. They move along a set route through the old downtown and stop at judging areas to perform a full choreographed routine. Between stops the drumming keeps going, and the crowd answers with the festival chant, "Hala Bira!"

The religious side happens mostly in the days before Sunday:

- Masses and novenas at San Jose Placer Church on Plaza Libertad, home of the venerated Santo Niño image
- A fluvial procession carrying the image on the Iloilo River
- Street-side food stalls and night events across the city centre

## Seats, sidewalks and the heat

Most people watch for free from the sidewalk along the route. The covered and uncovered stands at the judging areas are ticketed. They give you the full routines, which tribes perform at the judging points and not between them. The festival organiser handles seat sales and publishes the schedule and route on its official channels.

Locals who watch from the street usually claim a spot hours before the first tribe arrives, since the curbs fill up fast. They bring the following:

- An umbrella or hat, as there is little shade along the route
- Water, because the parade runs through the hottest part of the day
- Clothes they don't mind getting stained, since dancers' body paint rubs off in the crush
- A small bag carried in front, as the crowds are packed tight

The procession and the Masses are acts of devotion. People stay quiet during the prayers and keep the aisles clear for devotees carrying Santo Niño images. Inside San Jose Placer Church, shoulders are usually covered.

## Reaching Iloilo for Dinagyang

Iloilo International Airport is in Cabatuan, roughly 19 km north of the city. Domestic flights from Manila and Cebu land there, and vans and taxis run into downtown. Fast-craft ferries cross the Guimaras Strait from Bacolod, which makes it easy to pair the trip with Negros.

Hotels in the city centre and the Smallville and Iloilo Business Park area fill up for festival weekend, and visitors often stay several days for the lead-up events. Once the streets close on parade day, walking beats any vehicle. On the evenings around the festival, Ilonggos usually head to the stalls for batchoy, the noodle soup the city is known for, or for grilled seafood.
