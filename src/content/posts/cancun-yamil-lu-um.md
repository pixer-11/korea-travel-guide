## A temple between the towers

Yamil Lu'um sits on a low hill above the Caribbean. On either side of it, resort towers line Cancún's Hotel Zone, and the ruin is far smaller than they are. The stone remains date to around the 13th century. Coastal Maya built them here when this sandbar was a trading and navigation route, long before it held hotels.

The name is Yucatec Maya and is usually translated as "hilly land". It fits: the knoll is one of the higher points along this flat strip of coast. Two small structures survive. They are generally known as the Temple of the Scorpion and the Temple of the Handprint.

## Finding the knoll at Km 12.5

The address is Blvd. Kukulcán Km 12.5. Every Hotel Zone address is a kilometre marker along this one road.

- **By bus:** the R-1 and R-2 buses run the length of Blvd. Kukulcán. Ask the driver for Km 12.5.
- **On foot:** most visitors reach the site along the beach. The temples sit on the dune line, so look up at the rise between the hotels.
- **By taxi:** say "Kilómetro doce y medio, Yamil Lu'um." Many drivers know it better by the km marker than by the name.

Resort properties border the site on both sides. That's why the beach is the usual approach, not the road frontage. Google lists it as open 24 hours, but it's an unlit outdoor ruin. Go in daylight to see the stonework and stay on safe footing.

## Small, unhurried and quiet

Don't expect Chichén Itzá. This is a pair of compact limestone structures on a sandy rise, and most people spend 20 to 30 minutes here. What you get is space. Big tour groups skip it, so you often have the stones and the view out over the turquoise water to yourself.

Morning light falls on the seaward side, and the sand is cooler underfoot. By midday the dune has almost no shade. Bring water and a hat. You won't find a kiosk on the knoll.

## Treading lightly on a protected site

Like other archaeological sites in Mexico, the temples are protected federal heritage. Behave as you would at any of the country's ruins:

- **Stay off the structures.** Climbing, sitting on walls or leaning on carved surfaces damages soft limestone that has stood since the 1200s.
- **Keep to the existing paths** across the dune. The vegetation holds the sand that holds the ruins.
- **Take every bit of litter back** down the beach with you.
- **Be discreet with drones and tripods.** Hotel guests are a few metres away, and drone use at Mexican archaeological sites is restricted.

To make a Maya half-day of it, carry on south along the boulevard:

- **Museo Maya de Cancún** at Km 16.5 holds artefacts from across Quintana Roo.
- **San Miguelito** shares the museum grounds and has small ruins under a jungle canopy.
- **El Rey** at Km 18 is the Hotel Zone's largest archaeological zone. Its iguanas sun themselves on the platforms.

Check current admission and hours for those three on the official INAH site before you go.
