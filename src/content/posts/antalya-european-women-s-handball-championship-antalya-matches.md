## Handball on the Turkish Riviera in December

Antalya in December gets few beach crowds. The resort hotels along Lara and Konyaaltı are half asleep, the harbour in Kaleiçi is quiet, and the snow on the Taurus Mountains sits behind the city. This year the indoor sport comes to town at the same time.

The 2026 European Women's Handball Championship runs from December 3 to 20. Several countries share the hosting, and Antalya stages group-stage matches. That puts the city at the start of the tournament, where every team still has something to play for.

## What the group stage looks like from the stands

Handball is fast, physical and high-scoring. A match lasts 60 minutes in two halves of 30, and teams often score more than 25 goals each. Possession flips in seconds, so the noise in the hall rarely drops.

In the group stage, each team in a group plays its rivals once. The strongest sides move on to the next round, which is held elsewhere. The Antalya matches decide who survives the opening phase. Close games here can be tighter than the later rounds.

What to watch for from your seat:

- **The pivot**, wrestling with defenders on the six-metre line
- **Seven-metre throws**, the handball penalty, taken in near silence before the roar
- **Goalkeepers**, who often swing the result on their own
- **Timeouts**, when the arena DJ fills every gap with music

## Tickets, schedule and the arena

The European Handball Federation (EHF) organises the championship. Its official EHF EURO site is where the full match schedule, the Antalya venue and the ticket links live. Group-stage days at EHF EUROs usually stack two or more matches on the same court. Ticketing has often been sold by session, so one ticket covers several games.

Prices are not listed here. The ticketing page linked from the EHF site shows the current categories. Most fans pick seats along the long sides of the court, where the speed of the counter-attacks is easiest to follow.

## Reaching Antalya and moving around

Antalya Airport (AYT) sits east of the city centre. In winter it still gets plenty of flights from Istanbul and Ankara, plus some direct European routes. The AntRay tram line runs from the airport area into town. Taxis and the BiTaksi app cover the rest.

Inside the city, trams and buses run on the rechargeable Antalyakart card. Visiting fans tend to stay in one of two places:

- **Kaleiçi**, the walled old town, for walkable evenings
- **Konyaaltı or Lara**, where the big hotels sell off-season rooms

December is the wettest stretch of Antalya's year. A light rain jacket earns its space in the bag more than sunscreen does.

## Sitting in a Turkish handball crowd

Turkish indoor crowds are loud and organised. Drums, chants and coordinated clapping run through every game, whether the home side is on court or not. Neutral fans tend to adopt one team for the day and join in.

A few habits attendees usually follow:

- **Security checks** at the doors are thorough, so fans typically get to the arena well before throw-off and leave large bags at the hotel
- **Bring a flag** if you have one; visiting supporters from across Europe follow their teams to the host cities
- **Card payment** works almost everywhere in Antalya, including most arena kiosks, though small cash in Turkish lira helps with simit sellers and dolmuş minibuses
- **A thank-you in Turkish**, "teşekkürler", goes down well with stewards and staff

Between matches on multi-game days, many fans step outside for a tea, served in tulip-shaped glasses, rather than queueing at the indoor counters.

## Filling the days between matches

The schedule leaves free mornings, and Antalya fills them easily. Hadrian's Gate, the triple Roman arch built for the emperor's visit in 130 AD, marks the entrance to Kaleiçi. Inside, Ottoman houses with wooden bay windows line the lanes down to the old harbour.

Nearby options for a half day:

- **Antalya Archaeological Museum**, with statues from Perge
- **Düden Waterfalls**, where the river drops off the cliffs straight into the sea
- **Perge and Aspendos**, the ancient city and the well-preserved Roman theatre, east of the centre
- **Konyaaltı's beach path**, a long, flat walk with the mountains ahead

Matches end into the evening, and Kaleiçi's meyhanes and kebab grills stay open after the final whistle. That makes the old town the usual place for fans to go over the day's results.
