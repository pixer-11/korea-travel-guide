## The last tournament of the Challenge Tour season

This is the week that settles the Challenge Tour season. The Rolex Grand Final closes the 2026 Challenge Tour, the development circuit that sits one level below the DP World Tour. It is held in Spain on November 1, 2026.

The field is small and selective. Only players who finished high on the season rankings get in. The leading names on the final standings earn cards for the DP World Tour the following season. Because of that, a two-foot putt on the closing holes can change a player's career, and you can watch it from a few metres away.

## Date, venue and where the details live

The 2026 edition is held on November 1, 2026. That date falls on a Sunday. On a four-round professional tour schedule, Sunday is usually the final round, when the trophy and the season-ending cards are decided.

The DP World Tour runs the Challenge Tour. Its official website carries the tournament schedule, the course, tee times and spectator information for the Grand Final. Earlier editions have often been played on the Balearic island of Mallorca, at Club de Golf Alcanada near Port d'Alcúdia. The course for any given year is the one the DP World Tour lists for that season.

## What you actually see on the course

A Challenge Tour final has no stadium and no seats in rows. You walk the course with the players, separated from the fairways by rope lines on stakes. Marshals hold up paddles when a player is about to swing.

The crowds are much smaller than at a DP World Tour event, and that is the appeal. Spectators can usually stand close to the tee boxes and greens. You hear the strike of the ball and the caddie's yardage talk.

Typical things to watch for:

- **The leaderboards** near the main greens, which show both the tournament standings and the season ranking picture
- **The practice range** in the morning, where players warm up before their tee times
- **The 18th green**, where the trophy presentation and season awards happen after the final group finishes

## On the ropes: how spectators behave here

Golf has its own spectator etiquette, and marshals enforce it politely but firmly. Regular fans follow a fairly fixed routine.

- **Phones on silent.** Photos and video during a swing are the fastest way to get a stern look from a player, a caddie or a marshal. Fans usually take pictures between shots, once the ball is in the air or the player is walking.
- **Stand still when the paddle goes up.** The marshal's raised "Quiet" sign means stop moving and stop talking. Lower your voice when the paddle comes down too.
- **Cross only at crossing points.** Fairways have marked gaps in the ropes where marshals let spectators through between groups.
- **Pick a strategy.** Some fans follow one group for all 18 holes. Others settle at a green near the finish and let the whole field come past them. On a Sunday final, the second approach usually gives the best view of the decisive putts.

Applause is the main reaction, plus a cheer for a holed putt. Shouting during a backswing is not part of the culture here. The players notice it.

## November 1 in Spain: the holiday factor

The date matters for practical reasons. November 1 is *Día de Todos los Santos*, All Saints' Day, a national public holiday in Spain. Families traditionally visit cemeteries and lay flowers, and many shops, banks and offices close for the day.

What that means for a visitor:

- Supermarkets and small shops in towns are often shut, so travelling fans usually buy supplies the day before
- Public buses and trains run, but often on reduced Sunday or holiday timetables
- Restaurants in resort and tourist areas generally stay open, though family-run places may close

Early November is shoulder season along Spain's Mediterranean coast and on the Balearic Islands. Days are usually mild and bright with a cool edge in the morning. Some seasonal beach hotels shut for the winter at the end of October, so choosing where to stay depends on whether a resort runs past the summer season.

## Getting there and moving around

Your route depends on where the DP World Tour stages the 2026 final. For a Mallorca edition, visitors fly into Palma de Mallorca Airport (PMI), which has direct flights from across Europe. From Palma, the north-east coast around Alcúdia is about an hour by road.

Golf courses rarely sit next to a train station. Most spectators drive or take a taxi. At tour events, parking and any shuttle arrangements are part of the spectator information the DP World Tour provides for each tournament.

For a full day on the course, fans usually bring:

- A light waterproof layer and sun protection, since you are outdoors for hours
- Shoes you can walk several kilometres of grass and slopes in
- A small bag, because larger bags are often restricted at tour events

A final round takes most of the day from the first tee time to the last putt. Fans who want the season-deciding moments tend to stay until the final groups finish on 18.
