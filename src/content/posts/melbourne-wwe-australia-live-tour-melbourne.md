## A Wednesday night at Melbourne Park

The ring sits where the tennis court usually is. Every January, Rod Laver Arena hosts the Australian Open finals. On December 9, 2026, it hosts WWE's Australia Live Tour, with the company's international stars wrestling under the arena's retractable roof.

The date falls on a Wednesday in early Melbourne summer. Evenings stay light late at that time of year, so many people walk in from the city while the sky over the Yarra is still bright. WWE publishes the dates, venue and ticket links for the tour on its official site.

## Inside Rod Laver Arena

A WWE live show turns a round tennis bowl into a theatre. Seats rise steeply on every side of the floor, which gives even the upper tiers a clear line down to the ropes. Most WWE events put the entrance stage at one end, so wrestlers walk a ramp to the ring before every match.

Floor seats put you closest to the action, but you are at the same level as the people around you. A low or middle row in the lower bowl usually gives a better view across the whole ring. The upper level shows you the whole spectacle at once: the lighting rig, the pyro and fifteen thousand-odd people chanting together.

The tour's match card and wrestler line-up are WWE's to set. This guide doesn't list names, because touring cards often change.

## Getting to the Melbourne Park precinct

Rod Laver Arena belongs to the sports precinct on the eastern edge of the CBD, beside the Yarra River. The Melbourne Cricket Ground and AAMI Park are its neighbours. Public transport is the easiest way in, and these are the routes most fans use:

- **Richmond Station:** the busiest option. It's on most suburban train lines, and a footbridge and paths lead from there into Melbourne Park.
- **Jolimont Station:** one stop from Flinders Street on the Mernda and Hurstbridge lines, on the other side of Yarra Park.
- **Tram route 70:** runs along Flinders Street and Batman Avenue and stops right beside the arena.
- **On foot from Flinders Street Station:** follow the river path through Birrarung Marr. It takes about 15–20 minutes.

Trains and trams run on a myki card. After the show, the crowd floods all of these exits together. Plenty of people walk back to Flinders Street rather than queue at the nearest platform.

## Signs, chants and the merch line

WWE crowds join in, and Melbourne's crowd is no different. Fans often bring handmade signs and know the chants. Many wear the shirt of their favourite wrestler. Pick a side and join in: cheer the heroes and boo the villains. Live, nobody sits on their hands.

A few habits make the night easier:

- **Merchandise:** official stands sell tour shirts and title replica gear on the concourse. The lines are longest just before the first bell and at intermission.
- **Paying:** bars and food outlets around Melbourne Park mostly run on card and phone payments. Tipping isn't expected anywhere in Australia.
- **Signs:** the arena sets its own rules for sign size and for what you can bring in. Those rules sit on the Rod Laver Arena event page.
- **Kids:** WWE live events draw lots of families. Ear protection is common among younger fans, because the pyro and entrance music are very loud indoors.

## Before the bell and after it

Most fans eat in town before the show. Swan Street in Richmond, south of the station, is a long row of pubs, Vietnamese restaurants and burger bars. It's a natural stop for anyone arriving by train. Flinders Lane and its laneways suit people walking in from the city side.

On a warm evening, Birrarung Marr and the river path between Federation Square and the arena are an easy way to fill the time before doors open. After the show, the bars on Swan Street and around Flinders Street stay busy with people still in their wrestling shirts.

Out-of-town visitors usually stay in the CBD or Southbank. That puts the arena one tram ride or a riverside walk away.

## Melbourne's place in WWE's Australian run

Melbourne has history with the company. In October 2018, WWE staged Super Show-Down at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, a few minutes' walk from Rod Laver Arena. It was one of the biggest wrestling crowds Australia had seen. In 2024, Perth hosted Elimination Chamber at Optus Stadium.

A live tour stop works differently from those stadium shows. Indoors, in a bowl built for tennis, you sit closer to the ring and the noise has nowhere to go. That closeness is why local fans keep filling arena dates between the big events.
