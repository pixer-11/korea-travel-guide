## Bronze horse, clipped borders

You'll see the statue first. A gilded-looking bronze Joan of Arc rides a rearing horse on a tall plinth, sword raised over a sunken rectangle of lawn. Flower borders frame the lawn in strict lines. The garden was laid out in 1938 around this equestrian monument. It mixes the symmetry of a classical French garden with the looser, packed beds of an English flower garden.

The result is compact and fits into one view. Gravel and paved paths run along the edges. Benches face inward, and the beds are planted in tiers, so colour shifts through the season as one display finishes and the next opens. The heaviest bloom runs from late spring into early autumn. In winter the beds sleep under snow and the statue stands alone.

## Finding it from the old city

The garden sits between Avenue Wilfrid-Laurier and the Plains of Abraham, in the Montcalm side of the Grande Allée district. From Old Quebec, the route is simple:

- Leave the walls through Porte Saint-Louis
- Walk west past the Parliament Building on Grande Allée
- Turn left toward the Plains after a few blocks; the garden lies just inside the park edge

The walk takes about 15 minutes on flat pavement. City buses run along Grande Allée if you'd rather ride. The garden belongs to the National Battlefields Park, so the Plains, the Martello towers and the river-side lookouts are all a short walk away.

## Timing a quiet visit

This is one of the quieter corners of Quebec City. Most visitors head straight for the Château Frontenac and the Petit-Champlain streets, so you can often have a bench here to yourself. Weekends change that. Try not to arrive between 11am and 3pm on Saturday or Sunday, when strollers and photo groups fill the paths.

On weekends the calm windows are 9am–11am, 5pm–6pm and 7pm–11pm. On weekdays the forecast puts the quietest hour at 10pm–11pm. Google currently lists the garden as open 24 hours. Park hours and lighting can still change with the season, so check the National Battlefields Commission site before a late visit.

## Garden manners on the Plains

The beds are what you've come to see, so treat them like a museum piece:

- Stay on the paths. The borders are tightly planted and easy to trample.
- Don't picnic or throw a frisbee here. Spread a blanket on the open lawns of the Plains next door instead.
- Keep dogs leashed and away from the flowerbeds.
- Use the garden's French name, Jardin Jeanne-d'Arc, when you ask for directions. It's what signs and locals use.

For photos, the classic shot puts Joan in the centre with the long lawn in front of her. It works best early on a weekend morning, before the paths fill. Then walk on toward the Plains for river views. You can also go back along Grande Allée, where the terraces start filling in the late afternoon.
