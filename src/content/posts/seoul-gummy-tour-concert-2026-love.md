## Two nights, one voice

Gummy plays Jangchung Arena on December 12 and 13, 2026. Her Love tour stops in Seoul for those two nights, and the hall is a good size for the kind of singer she is.

She debuted in 2003. Her reputation rests on the voice: controlled, husky in the low register, and able to hold a long ballad note without strain. Much of the Korean public knows her from drama soundtracks. "You Are My Everything" from *Descendants of the Sun* and "Remember Me" from *Hotel del Luna* both reached far past her R&B fanbase.

A tour called *Love* suggests a set built around those slow, swelling numbers, but the setlist has not been made public. Expect a vocal show first and a spectacle second. That is the reason people come.

## Inside Jangchung Arena

Jangchung Arena opened in 1963 as Korea's first indoor sports arena. For decades it was the home of Korean basketball, volleyball and wrestling, and it has since been renovated and reused as a concert venue. The stands rise steeply around a central floor, so even the upper seats feel close to the stage.

For a singer like Gummy, that closeness is the point. In a stadium a ballad turns into a sound reaching you from far away. Here you can see the breath before a high note.

- **Floor seats:** closest to the stage, but sightlines depend on the people in front of you
- **Lower stands:** a raised, clear view, and many fans' first choice for a vocal concert
- **Upper stands:** further back but steep, so the stage stays in view

## Getting to Jangchung-dong

The arena is in Jangchung-dong, Jung-gu, on the eastern foot of Namsan. The simplest route is the subway.

- **Dongguk University Station (Line 3):** the closest stop. Exit 5 puts you on the road toward the arena, a few minutes' walk away.
- **Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station (Lines 2, 4, 5):** a longer walk, but useful if you are staying around Dongdaemun.
- **Taxi:** Kakao T is the usual way to call one, though the roads around the arena clog after a show.

A T-money card covers the subway and buses and tops up at any convenience store. After the final song, the crowd mostly heads to Line 3. Fans who are staying in Myeong-dong or Euljiro often walk a stop or two before getting on.

## Jokbal before the encore

Jangchung-dong is the neighbourhood Koreans name when they talk about *jokbal*, braised pig's trotters. A row of restaurants on the main road near the arena has served the dish for decades. Several hang signs claiming to be the original.

Jokbal comes sliced on a platter, glossy and dark from its soy-and-spice braise. You wrap it in lettuce with *saeujeot* (salted shrimp), garlic and chilli. Most places sell it in sizes meant for sharing, so it suits a group better than a solo fan with an hour to spare.

A few other things nearby:

- **Taegeukdang:** a long-running bakery near Dongguk University Station, known for its old-style buns and monaka ice cream
- **Namsan:** the trails and the road up to N Seoul Tower start just west of the arena
- **Dongdaemun:** a short ride north for late-night shopping after the show

## Concert manners at a Korean ballad show

Korean concert audiences are attentive. During ballads the hall usually goes almost silent. People clap and cheer between songs rather than over them, and talking through a quiet number draws looks.

Filming and photography rules are set by each production. Many Korean concerts ban recording during the show, and staff do walk the aisles. Fans tend to save their phones for the moments the artist invites, which is often the encore.

Merchandise (called *MD* in Korean fan slang) sells at booths outside the hall. Fans who want light sticks, towels or tour goods usually queue well before doors open, because popular items sell out. Cards and mobile payment work almost everywhere in Seoul, and the booths are no exception.

Ticket holders are often asked for photo ID at entry, and the name has to match the booking. A passport covers foreign visitors.

## December on the east side of Namsan

Mid-December in Seoul is properly cold. Night temperatures below freezing are normal, and the wind off Namsan cuts along the road to the arena. Fans waiting outside for merch or the doors usually wear long padded coats, the knee-length kind you see all over the city in winter.

Inside the hall it is warm, so layers you can take off are better than one heavy jumper. Most venues of this size have a cloakroom, but it is not guaranteed. Expect to keep your coat on your lap.

The show dates also fall in Seoul's year-end season. Streets in Myeong-dong and around City Hall put up their seasonal lights, and they are a short subway ride away if you want a walk after the show.
