## Racing past sixth-century mosaics

Few marathons pass this many UNESCO sites. Ravenna's early Christian monuments hold some of the finest Byzantine mosaics anywhere. The race is built around that heritage, which is why it carries the name "Città d'Arte", City of Art.

The 2026 weekend was set for November 7-8. The programme offered three levels of effort:

- **Marathon:** the full 42.195 km
- **Half-marathon:** 21.097 km
- **Shorter races:** non-competitive distances suited to families, walkers and runners who wanted an easy outing

Ravenna sits on the flat plain of Emilia-Romagna, so its streets involve little climbing. November in the Po Valley is usually cool, and morning fog is common. Both suit fast running more than sightseeing from the roadside.

## Reaching Ravenna for race weekend

Most international runners fly into Bologna Guglielmo Marconi Airport. From there, the Marconi Express shuttle runs to Bologna Centrale. Regional trains to Ravenna station take a little over an hour.

Ravenna station sits on the eastern edge of the historic centre. Piazza del Popolo, the main square, is roughly a 10-minute walk away. The old town is compact and largely pedestrianised, so most of it is easy to reach on foot.

Rimini, with its own airport and long seafront hotel strip, is another base. Trains connect it to Ravenna along the coast. On race days, street closures in the centre typically change how people cross town.

## Entries, paperwork and the start line

Race entries were the "tickets" here, with prices and deadlines set by the organisers. Italian competitive races generally require a medical certificate for competitive sport. Runners without an Italian athletics federation membership usually need a RUNCARD as well.

The non-competitive shorter races usually have lighter paperwork, which makes them the simplest option for companions. The historic centre has limited hotel stock, and race weekends tend to fill it fast.

## Cheering, cycling and recovering like Ravennati

Spectators along Italian courses shout "Dai!" and "Forza!" Runners who shout them back get a grin. Ravenna is a cycling town, so locals cross the centre by bike even on race weekends, and spectators usually keep to the footpaths. A hired bike on the shake-out day is the quickest way to fit in.

The monuments suit a recovery day, when legs need flat, slow walking anyway:

- **Basilica of San Vitale:** the Justinian and Theodora mosaic panels
- **Mausoleum of Galla Placidia:** a deep blue, star-covered ceiling in a tiny brick building beside San Vitale
- **Basilica of Sant'Apollinare Nuovo:** processions of saints and virgins along both nave walls
- **Neonian and Arian Baptisteries:** two domed baptism scenes, one of each tradition
- **Dante's Tomb:** the poet's resting place, in the quiet streets of the Zona del Silenzio

A combined ticket covers several mosaic sites. Its current price is listed at the ticket offices and on the official monuments site. The classic refuel is a piadina romagnola. It's the regional flatbread, filled with squacquerone cheese and rocket or with cured meat, and sold from street kiosks across the city. A bowl of cappelletti in broth, a Romagna staple in the cold months, rounds it off.
