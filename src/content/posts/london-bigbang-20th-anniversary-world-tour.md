## Twenty years, one night in N17

The gap is the story here. BIGBANG's 20th anniversary world tour is their first tour since 2017. On 26 September 2026 it reaches London, at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.

The group debuted under YG Entertainment in 2006. Since T.O.P left in 2023, the active line-up has been G-Dragon, Taeyang and Daesung. For many fans in the UK this is the first time they can see them in their own country rather than in Seoul or on a European arena date.

The back catalogue built up over those twenty years is why a crowd this size turns out. "Haru Haru", "Lies", "Fantastic Baby" and "Bang Bang Bang" are the songs most people outside K-pop circles recognise. No setlist is given here, so treat those as the group's best-known songs rather than a promise of the night's running order.

## The stadium on the High Road

Tottenham Hotspur Stadium opened in 2019 on Tottenham High Road, on the site of the old White Hart Lane ground. It holds around 62,850 for football. Concert capacity depends on how the stage and floor are laid out.

It is a bowl of steel and glass with a steep single-tier South Stand. That stand is the largest single-tier stand in the UK, and from it the stage looks close even from the back rows. The football pitch slides away in sections, which is why the stadium regularly hosts major tours and NFL games.

Inside the South Stand you'll find:

- **The Goal Line Bar**, a counter that runs most of the stand's length and is billed as the longest bar in Europe
- **A Beavertown microbrewery** built into the concourse
- **Wide concourses** with food counters, so the walk between your seat and a drink is short

## Getting to Tottenham by rail and Tube

The stadium sits in north London's N17 postcode, a long way from the West End. Most people come by rail, because event-day roads around the High Road close and parking nearby is very limited.

Your main options:

- **White Hart Lane** (London Overground, Weaver line from Liverpool Street): the closest station, a short walk from the stadium's west side.
- **Northumberland Park** (Greater Anglia trains from Liverpool Street and Stratford): a walk of roughly 15 minutes from the east side.
- **Tottenham Hale** (Victoria line, plus Greater Anglia and the Stansted Express): a walk of 20 to 25 minutes up to the High Road, and the station most Tube users pick.
- **Seven Sisters** (Victoria line and Weaver line): a longer walk of around 25 to 30 minutes, but the platforms are usually less packed.

Tottenham Hale's Stansted Express link is handy for fans flying in from Europe. You can go from the airport to the stadium area with one change of platform rather than a trip through central London.

## The yellow crown sea

This is the image people remember from a BIGBANG show. The fandom is called VIP, and the official lightstick is a yellow crown on a stick. In a stadium bowl, tens of thousands of them light the stands in gold before the group walks on.

Many fans bring older versions of the crown alongside newer ones. Those who had seen the group before 2017 often carry the stick from that era. Banners and handmade slogans appear too, but the stadium's bag rules restrict what you can carry in. Big flags and poles usually stay at home.

The fanchant is the other ritual. VIPs shout the members' names in a fixed order at set points in the songs. The fandom shares chant guides online, and newcomers tend to learn the "Fantastic Baby" chant first.

## Getting through the gates at a cashless ground

Tottenham Hotspur Stadium is fully cashless. Food, drink and merchandise inside are paid for by card or phone, and notes are not accepted at the counters. Contactless is the quickest option, and it is how almost everyone pays.

Tickets for big concerts here are usually mobile tickets on the ticket holder's phone. Each gate is assigned to a particular entrance, so the wristband or seat block shown on the ticket decides which side of the stadium you walk round to. The stadium enforces a strict small-bag policy, and its official site sets out the current size limit and banned items.

Some habits of regular attendees:

- They charge their phones fully, because the ticket, payment and the trip home all depend on one battery.
- Fans who want tour merchandise typically queue at the stands outside the stadium in the afternoon. Popular sizes sell out as the evening goes on.
- Floor-standing ticket holders usually arrive well before doors to get a place near the front barrier.

## After the encore: the walk out of Tottenham

Leaving takes far longer than arriving. Tens of thousands of people empty onto the High Road at once. Stations nearby, especially White Hart Lane and Tottenham Hale, often run queuing pens after big events, with stewards letting people onto the platforms in batches.

Fans who know the area often walk south past Bruce Grove to Seven Sisters, about half an hour on foot. The trains there are usually easier to board. Others stay put at a pub or late food counter on the High Road for an hour and catch a train once the crowd has gone.

Night Tube runs on the Victoria line on Friday and Saturday nights. 26 September 2026 is a Saturday, so the line through Tottenham Hale and Seven Sisters keeps running after a late finish. Night buses along the High Road towards central London are the backup.

## Where visiting fans base themselves

Tottenham itself has few hotels. Most out-of-town fans stay somewhere with a direct line to the stadium.

Popular choices:

- **Around Liverpool Street and Shoreditch**: one Weaver line train to White Hart Lane, plus plenty of late food in east London.
- **King's Cross and Euston**: a straight run up the Victoria line to Tottenham Hale or Seven Sisters, and handy for Eurostar arrivals at St Pancras.
- **Stratford**: Greater Anglia trains to Northumberland Park, plus the big hotel cluster around the Olympic Park.

For Korean food before or after the show, many fans head to New Malden in south-west London. It is home to Europe's largest Korean community. The trip takes over an hour from Tottenham, so most people go there the day after rather than on concert day.
