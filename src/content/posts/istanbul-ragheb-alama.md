## One night, one arena

The date announced is November 15, 2026. Ragheb Alama is a Lebanese singer whose career goes back to the 1980s. Fans across the Arab world call him "the Superstar". A younger audience knows him as a long-running judge on the TV talent show *Arab Idol*.

His Istanbul show is at Volkswagen Arena. This is a purpose-built indoor concert hall inside the Uniq Istanbul complex, a cultural and events campus in Maslak. It's an indoor seated-and-standing room, so the November weather won't affect the show itself.

Expect a crowd from three groups: Istanbul's large Arabic-speaking community, visitors from the Gulf and Levant who are in town for the weekend, and Turkish fans of Arabic pop.

## Getting to Maslak

Maslak is Istanbul's business district on the European side, north of Levent. The arena is not in the old city.

- **Metro:** the M2 line (Yenikapı–Hacıosman) runs up the spine of the European side through Taksim, Şişli and Levent. Fans usually get off at İTÜ–Ayazağa and walk to Uniq Istanbul.
- **Payment:** you pay for the metro with an Istanbulkart or a contactless bank card at the turnstile.
- **From Sultanahmet:** take the T1 tram to Kabataş, then the F1 funicular up to Taksim, then the M2 north.
- **Taxi:** after the show, the roads around Maslak clog quickly. Many concertgoers walk back to the metro instead of waiting for a cab at the gate.

## How the crowd behaves at an Arabic pop concert

The audience joins in from the first chorus. People sing every word of the old hits, like "Alby Ashe'ha", and phone torches come up for the ballads.

On the floor, groups of friends and families tend to stay together and dance in place. The dress code is "going out", not "gig": jackets, heels and done-up hair are normal.

A few things that help on the night:

- **Bags:** Istanbul venues run bag and body security checks at the entrance. Fans with small bags get through the line fastest.
- **Language:** announcements may be in Turkish. Staff at Maslak venues generally handle basic English.
- **Getting into the hall:** the queue at the doors builds through the evening, so fans with floor tickets usually turn up well before the start time printed on their ticket.
- **Tickets:** the ticket itself shows the start time and seating section. Door staff scan it from a phone.

## Before and after the show

Maslak is mostly office towers, so the area is quiet once the working day ends. Most visitors stay in Şişli, Nişantaşı or around Taksim. All three are a few M2 stops south, with plenty of hotels and late restaurants.

For dinner before the show, İstinye Park and Vadi İstanbul are shopping-and-dining complexes a short taxi ride from the arena. Back in Nişantaşı or around Taksim, kitchens serve late into the night, which suits a crowd leaving a concert.

Visitors with a spare day often head north to the Bosphorus villages of Sarıyer, Emirgan and Rumeli Hisarı. Their waterfront fish restaurants and Ottoman fortress make a contrast with an evening of Lebanese pop in a glass-and-steel arena.
