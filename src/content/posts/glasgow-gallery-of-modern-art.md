## A tobacco lord's house turned into an art gallery

Start outside, because the building comes first. A row of Corinthian columns fronts Royal Exchange Square. Behind them, the Duke of Wellington sits on horseback, and his head usually wears a traffic cone. That cone has become an unofficial city emblem. The council has stopped trying to keep it off.

The core of the building is a 1778 mansion built for William Cunninghame, one of the tobacco lords. In the 1820s David Hamilton remodelled it as the Royal Exchange, where merchants traded. It has been the city's contemporary art gallery since 1996. Many visitors find that history as interesting as the art.

## What's inside

Through the portico you reach the old exchange hall. It is a long, columned room with a coffered ceiling, and the installations inside it look small by comparison. The galleries are spread over several floors. They show changing selections from the city's modern collection alongside temporary exhibitions. The programme also includes artists' talks, which are listed on the Glasgow Museums website.

- **Ground floor:** the main hall, where the largest pieces usually go.
- **Upper floors:** smaller rooms for temporary shows. They change often, so check what's on before you go.
- **Basement:** the Library at GoMA, a public lending library with tables where you can sit down.

Plan on an hour to 90 minutes. The gallery doesn't sell itself on any single famous painting, so come for the rotation. If a show in the listings interests you, that's a good reason to visit.

## Timing a visit around the crowds

Opening hours currently vary by day. Monday to Thursday and Saturday it opens at 10am. Friday and Sunday it opens at 11am. Every day it closes at 5pm. Museum hours can change around holidays, so check the official site that week.

According to typical visitor patterns, weekdays are quietest from 10am–11am and from 4pm–5pm. The early slot only works Monday to Thursday, because Friday opening is 11am. Try not to arrive on a weekend between 12pm and 4pm. That is when the main hall gets crowded with shoppers ducking in off Buchanan Street.

## Walking in from Buchanan Street

Getting here takes no planning. You walk in from the street.

- **Subway:** Buchanan Street station is a few minutes' walk. Head down Buchanan Street and turn into the lane to Royal Exchange Square.
- **Rail:** Glasgow Queen Street station is about five minutes away on foot, past George Square.
- **Entry:** Glasgow Museums keeps general admission free. If a temporary exhibition charges, the price is shown at the desk and on the official site.
- **Bags:** large bags may be checked on entry. Bring the minimum.
- **Photos:** many works can be photographed, but follow any signs in a room. Lenders sometimes restrict photography for loaned pieces.

Do the Wellington photo before you go in, then leave the cone where it is. Climbing the statue is illegal and the plinth is high. Royal Exchange Square has restaurants under its arcades if you want lunch nearby. For a longer art day, the Merchant City galleries are a short walk east. The Lighthouse, the Mackintosh-designed building on Mitchell Lane, is just across Buchanan Street.
