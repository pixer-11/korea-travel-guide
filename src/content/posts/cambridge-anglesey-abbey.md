## A priory turned collector's house

The name is older than the house. Anglesey Abbey grew from a medieval Augustinian priory, and around 1600 it was rebuilt as a gabled stone country house. Lord Fairhaven bought it in the 1920s, filled it with art and left it to the National Trust when he died in 1966.

Inside, the rooms keep the dense look of a private collection. You'll find these:

- Landscape paintings, including two works by Claude Lorrain
- Clocks and carved furniture in nearly every room
- Panelled libraries and sitting rooms arranged as Fairhaven left them

## Gardens by the season

The 98 acres are the real draw. They were laid out for set pieces rather than borders, with long avenues lined by Fairhaven's statuary. Every season has a different reason to come:

- **Winter (December–February):** the Winter Garden, a winding path of coloured stems, scented shrubs and a grove of white-barked Himalayan birches
- **February:** snowdrops in large drifts, which bring the year's heaviest weekend crowds
- **Spring:** the formal Hyacinth Garden
- **Late summer:** the Dahlia Garden at its fullest

On the northern edge stands Lode Mill, a restored watermill on the River Lode. When it is running you can watch flour being ground.

## Timing the crowds

This is a very popular property, so when you arrive shapes the visit. On weekdays the grounds are calmest from 10am to 11am and again from 4pm to 5pm. On Saturdays and Sundays, try not to arrive between 11am and 4pm, the busiest stretch, when the car park, café and paths fill up.

The only quiet weekend window is 4pm–5pm, which suits a short garden walk more than a full visit. At the moment the site opens 9:30am–5pm every day of the week. Opening times change through the year, and the house and mill often keep shorter hours than the grounds, so check the National Trust website for this month's times. Give yourself three to four hours to see the house, the mill and a full lap of the gardens.

## Getting in, the National Trust way

Entry works through the visitor centre by the car park, which is reached from Quy Road (postcode CB25 9EJ). National Trust members scan their card there. Everyone else buys a ticket at the desk or online, and current prices are posted on the National Trust website.

Some habits help:

- **Pre-book in snowdrop season.** On busy February weekends, timed slots or full car parks are common.
- **Ask about house entry when you arrive.** House access is sometimes timed, so the visitor centre can tell you the next slot and save you walking out to the front door only to wait.
- **Stay on the paths.** The garden lawns and statuary avenues are kept pristine, and staff will remind you.
- **Pay by card.** Card and contactless are standard at the café, shop and plant sales.

From Cambridge, the drive takes about 20 minutes via Quy and the B1102. Cyclists can follow quiet lanes out through Stow cum Quy. Buses to Lode run from central Cambridge, but timetables change, so check the current route before relying on one.
