## Four nights of Philly harmony

Four New Zealand dates. That is the shape of the Boyz II Men New Zealand Tour, which takes place in December 2026, with Auckland as the country's biggest stop.

The group formed in Philadelphia in the mid-1980s and signed to Motown. Their 1991 debut, *Cooleyhighharmony*, made four-part a cappella swells a chart sound. Since Michael McCary's departure in the early 2000s, they have toured as a trio: Nathan Morris, Wanya Morris and Shawn Stockman.

## The songs people come for

The catalogue is the draw. Boyz II Men hold some of the longest runs at No. 1 in Billboard Hot 100 history, and their shows lean on that run of early-'90s ballads.

- "End of the Road", from the *Boomerang* soundtrack
- "I'll Make Love to You"
- "On Bended Knee"
- "One Sweet Day", their duet with Mariah Carey
- "Motownphilly", the uptempo debut single
- "It's So Hard to Say Goodbye to Yesterday"

## Auckland in December

December is early summer in Auckland. Sunset falls around 8:30pm, so on concert nights it is often still light as people head in to the venue. Weather turns quickly on the isthmus, and a light layer usually covers both a warm afternoon and a breezy walk back after the show.

The month is also peak season. Christmas parties, cruise ships at the downtown wharves and the start of the summer school holidays all compete for hotel rooms. Visitors from elsewhere in New Zealand usually stay in the CBD, Parnell or Ponsonby. All three are close to the city's main transport hub at Britomart.

Auckland Transport runs the city's trains, buses and ferries. Most locals pay with an AT HOP card. Late-night rideshare queues after big shows are common, so many concertgoers walk a few blocks away from the venue before calling a car.

## Concert-night habits in Auckland

A few New Zealand norms catch overseas visitors out.

- **Tipping:** not expected at venue bars or food counters. Service staff are paid a wage, and nobody waits for a tip.
- **Paying:** venue bars are mostly card and EFTPOS. Contactless tapping is the everyday habit.
- **Drinking age:** 18. Bar staff ask for photo ID from anyone who looks young, and overseas visitors usually carry a passport or driver licence.
- **Alcohol rules:** New Zealand host-responsibility law means staff will refuse service to anyone visibly intoxicated, and venues enforce it.
- **Seating:** where a show is reserved-seated, ushers expect ticket holders to stay in their numbered seats instead of drifting towards the stage.

Fans typically eat in the city before the show, because queues at venue food counters build at interval. For drinks afterwards, the Viaduct Harbour and Britomart's laneway bars sit within walking distance of downtown.
