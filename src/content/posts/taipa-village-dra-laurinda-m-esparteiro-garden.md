## A pocket of green off the village lanes

This is a neighbourhood garden, not a showpiece. It sits within Taipa Village, the old core of Taipa island, where narrow lanes, low shophouse rows and Portuguese street names survive between the casino towers of Cotai and the high-rises of central Taipa.

Most visitors come to the village for Rua do Cunha's snack shops and the pastel houses on Avenida da Praia. Few go looking for this garden, and that's the reason to come. It's a quieter find, a place to stop between sights rather than a sight in itself.

It's named for Dra. Laurinda M. Esparteiro, and like most public spaces in Macau, its name appears on signs in both Chinese and Portuguese.

## Finding it

The address is only a plus code, so use your phone.

- **Navigation:** type 5H35+CJF into Google Maps. The street-level pin is more reliable than asking for directions by name, since few people will recognise it.
- **From Macau peninsula:** buses cross the bridges to Taipa Village, and the Taipa light rail line serves the wider district. Check the current route before you set out.
- **From Cotai:** the village is a short taxi ride or a longer walk from the big resorts. Plenty of visitors walk it in the cooler months.
- **On foot:** once you're in the village, the lanes are compact. Walk the garden between other stops and don't treat it as a destination of its own.

## What it's for

It's useful to plan around the garden rather than expect much of it. It's open 24 hours, seven days a week, with no fixed opening time to work around. That makes it an easy place for an early start before the shops open, or a late stop after dinner in the village.

Good pairings within the village:

- **Rua do Cunha:** the pedestrian lane of almond-cookie and pork-jerky shops.
- **Taipa Houses–Museum:** five restored Macanese houses along Avenida da Praia.
- **Our Lady of Carmel Church:** the pale chapel on the rise above the houses.
- **Largo do Camões and the old market square:** small plazas where village life spills outdoors.

Plan on 15 to 30 minutes here. Spend longer only if you've brought something to eat or read.

## Garden manners in Macau

Macau's public gardens have their own unwritten rules, and the locals follow them closely.

- **No smoking.** Macau's tobacco law bans smoking in public parks and gardens, and inspectors issue fines.
- **Early mornings belong to residents.** Across Macau, older locals use gardens at dawn for tai chi, stretching and walking. Keep to the edges and don't film people up close without asking.
- **Carry out your snacks.** If you've bought egg tarts or jerky on Rua do Cunha, take the wrappers back to a bin on the street.
- **Keep your voice down at night.** The garden stays open after dark, but it's a residential area. Late visits should be low-key.
- **Read the bilingual signs.** Rules about dogs, cycling and lawns are posted in Chinese and Portuguese. Follow them even where nobody's watching.

The village fills up with day-trippers, and Rua do Cunha gets the worst of it. Use the garden as your breather: walk the lane first, then double back here to sit before heading on to the Houses–Museum.
