## A shopfront on the Minster's doorstep

High Petergate is a short, narrow street. It runs from Bootham Bar, one of the medieval gates in York's city walls, to the area around York Minster's west end. Its line goes back far: Petergate follows a main road through the old Roman fortress. Today it is lined with small shopfronts, and the Minster's towers rise above the rooftops at the end.

The Little Apple Bookshop sits at number 13. It is easy to walk past if you're looking up at the cathedral. That's a big part of its appeal. Most visitors here are heading straight to the Minster's doors, while this independent shop stays well under the radar.

## What's on the shelves

This is a general bookshop, not a specialist one. It covers a range of genres for adults, keeps a children's selection, and sells gifts alongside the books. That mix makes it useful in a few ways:

- **A souvenir that isn't a fridge magnet.** A book bought in York, ideally one about York, packs flat and lasts.
- **Something for the kids.** The children's section makes this an easy stop on a family day around the Minster.
- **A present to take home.** The gift side of the shop helps if you're shopping for someone who doesn't read.

Reviewers consistently rate it highly, and they keep coming back to the same word: charming. Expect a compact independent rather than a chain megastore. You browse here; you don't trawl.

## Fitting it into a Minster morning

The shop works best as part of a walk, not as a destination of its own. You might spend 15–30 minutes inside, longer if you're choosing for several people. Everything below is within a few minutes on foot:

- **York Minster:** the west front is at the end of the street.
- **Bootham Bar:** the gate at the other end of High Petergate, with steps up onto the city walls.
- **Dean's Park:** the green space on the Minster's north side, good for reading what you've just bought.
- **Stonegate:** branches off near the Minster end and leads on toward the Shambles.
- **Exhibition Square and York Art Gallery:** just outside Bootham Bar.

A simple loop goes like this. Start at the Minster, walk up High Petergate to the bookshop, go out through Bootham Bar to Exhibition Square, then climb onto the walls if your legs are up for it.

## Browsing a small shop on a busy street

Small independents work a little differently from big stores, and the visit goes better if you adjust.

- **Mind your bag.** Aisles in compact shops are tight. Carry a backpack in your hand or wear it on your front so you don't sweep a display.
- **Ask the person at the till.** In independent bookshops the staff often chose much of the stock themselves. Name a book you loved and ask what's like it.
- **Ask about orders.** If a title isn't on the shelf, ask whether they can get it in for you.
- **Pay as you would anywhere in the UK.** Contactless card and phone payments are the norm in British shops. Carry a little cash anyway, just in case.

Timing is mostly common sense. The streets around the Minster get busier as the day goes on and tour groups arrive. Coming soon after opening usually means fewer people browsing next to you.

## Hours and getting there

At the moment the shop opens 9am–5:30pm Monday to Saturday and 10am–5pm on Sunday. Sunday starts an hour later and ends half an hour earlier, which matters if you're planning a late-afternoon browse. Hours can change around holidays, so check before you go.

From York railway station, walking takes roughly 10–15 minutes. Cross the river on Lendal Bridge, follow Museum Street toward the Minster, then turn onto High Petergate. Central York is best done on foot. Many streets inside the walls are pedestrian footstreets for much of the day, and parking near the Minster is limited, so drivers should use a car park or Park & Ride outside the centre.

## Why it earns a detour

York has plenty of grand sights and some heavily photographed streets. A quiet independent bookshop a few doors from the Minster offers something different: a few unhurried minutes and something to take home that was chosen, not grabbed. Pop in on your way to the cathedral or on your way back out through Bootham Bar.
