## One night, one headliner

MIX Festival 2026 was planned as a one-night event on Friday, November 6, 2026, with Kelis at the top of the bill. That gave the night a clear centre.

Kelis broke through in 1999 with "Caught Out There" from her debut album *Kaleidoscope*, which The Neptunes produced. "Milkshake" (2003) made her a global name. Later records such as *Food* (2014) moved her towards soul and funk. Many fans know her for the early hits, but her catalogue covers more ground than that.

The announced bill centred on her. Her mix of early R&B hits and later soul and funk records gave the evening its shape.

## Inside Zorlu PSM

Zorlu PSM is the performing arts centre built into Zorlu Center. The complex mixes shopping, offices and homes, and it sits on the ridge above the Bosphorus near the 15 July Martyrs Bridge. The concert halls are underground, beneath the mall levels. You reach them by escalators and corridors rather than from the street.

That layout shapes an evening there:

- **Security screening** happens at the mall doors, as at most large Istanbul malls. Bags go through an X-ray.
- **Food and drink** before a show is easy to find. Zorlu Center's restaurants and cafés are a few floors above the halls.
- **Weather** is not part of a gig here. November in Istanbul is often cool and wet, but everything happens indoors.

## Getting to Zorlu PSM

The metro is the simplest way in. Take the **M2** (Yenikapı–Hacıosman) to **Gayrettepe**. From there Zorlu Center is a short walk along Büyükdere Caddesi, a busy road.

Other routes:

- **Metrobüs:** Zincirlikuyu stop, a few minutes' walk from the complex.
- **From the Asian side:** the Marmaray to Yenikapı, then the M2 north. The Kabataş ferry plus the F1 funicular to Taksim also links up with the M2.
- **Taxi or ride-hail:** possible, but Friday-evening traffic on Büyükdere Caddesi and around the bridge approach is heavy.

Visitors from abroad usually stay in Beşiktaş, Nişantaşı or around Taksim. All three are a few M2 stops or a short taxi ride away.

## How a Friday at Zorlu goes

Friday rush hour shapes evenings at Zorlu. Many people pay for transit with an **Istanbulkart**, a reloadable card sold and topped up at machines in metro stations. One card can pay for several people on the same ride, which helps a group heading home together after the encore.

Cards and contactless payment work across Zorlu Center's restaurants and bars, so cash is rarely needed inside. Turkish concert crowds sing along loudly to the hits they know. Fans often eat in the mall first, because the halls downstairs have nowhere to sit down for a meal.

After shows, crowds head for Gayrettepe all at once. Some people let the first wave pass over a drink upstairs. Others walk down towards Beşiktaş, where the bars along the waterfront stay open late.
