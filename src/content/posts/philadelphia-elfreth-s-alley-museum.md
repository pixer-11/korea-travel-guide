## A street people still live on

The alley is barely wider than a delivery van. It runs one short block between North Front Street and North 2nd Street in Old City. Brick row houses crowd both sides, with shutters, painted doors and window boxes. The street under your feet is cobblestone, and the sidewalks are brick.

Most of these buildings date from the 18th and early 19th centuries. Artisans and tradespeople built and rented them near the Delaware River wharves. The lane takes its name from Jeremiah Elfreth, an 18th-century blacksmith who owned property here. Today it is a National Historic Landmark. It is also a real address: almost every door opens onto somebody's living room.

## Inside the museum house

The museum sits at number 126, halfway down the block. It is the one door on the alley you are actually invited through. Inside, the rooms are small and the staircases steep, which tells you how tightly working families lived here. The exhibits focus on the ordinary people who rented these houses over three centuries rather than on famous names.

Staff and volunteers run guided tours that cover the alley's history and its residents. Check the museum's official website for current tour times and admission before you go. Allow around 30 to 45 minutes for the house and a slow walk up and down the lane. Add more time if you join a full tour.

## Getting to Old City

The simplest route is SEPTA's Market-Frankford Line to **2nd Street station**. From Market Street, walk north on 2nd Street for about three blocks. The alley entrance is on your right, just past Arch Street. Old City's grid is compact, so from Independence Hall it is roughly a 10 to 15 minute walk east.

Driving is harder. Street parking in Old City is metered and scarce on weekends. Public garages near Front and Market are easier. Interstate 95 runs just east of Front Street, so expect some traffic noise at the river end of the alley.

## Walking the alley without intruding

Residents here have spent decades sharing their front steps with visitors. A few habits keep that arrangement working:

- **Stay off the stoops.** The marble and brick steps are private property, even though they sit flush with the sidewalk.
- **Keep doorways clear for photos.** Shoot from the middle of the lane, and don't frame a stranger's front window at close range.
- **Keep your voice down.** Sound carries between the facing walls, and people are working from home behind them.
- **Leave the side courts alone.** Tiny gated passages such as Bladen's Court belong to the residents.
- **Use the museum as your way in.** Questions about who lived where are best asked at number 126. Knocking on doors is not the way.

Time your visit for the museum's opening days. Monday to Thursday the museum is closed, and you'll only see the outside of the houses. The street is well known and popular with photographers. Arriving right at noon on a Friday gives you the house before weekend sightseers build up through the afternoon.

## When to plan around

The museum keeps short hours: **Friday, Saturday and Sunday, 12:00 to 4:00 PM**. It is closed Monday through Thursday. A weekday walk-through still works if you only want the facade and the cobbles.

The residents' association also runs occasional open-house events. These include **Fete Days** in early summer and **Deck the Alley** in December, when some private homes open to ticket holders. Dates change each year, so check the museum's calendar. Spring and autumn are the best seasons for a comfortable walk. Summer afternoons can be humid between the brick walls.

## Pairing it with the rest of Old City

The alley is short, so most people fold it into a longer walk. The oldest blocks of the city sit close together:

- **Betsy Ross House** on Arch Street, about a block south.
- **Christ Church** on 2nd Street, where Benjamin Franklin worshipped. Its burial ground, with Franklin's grave, is a few blocks west at 5th and Arch.
- **Arch Street Meeting House**, a Quaker meeting house on Arch at 4th.
- **Benjamin Franklin Bridge**, whose blue span towers over the north end of Old City.
- **Independence National Historical Park**, home of Independence Hall and the Liberty Bell, a short walk southwest.

Around 2nd and 3rd Streets you'll find cafés and galleries for lunch before the museum opens at noon. One route works well: walk from the Betsy Ross House to the alley, then on to Christ Church. It covers three centuries of Philadelphia in under a mile.
