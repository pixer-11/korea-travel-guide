## A ruin rebuilt around its scars

Look closely at the walls. Friedrich August Stüler designed the Neues Museum in the 1840s, and it opened in the 1850s. Bombing in the Second World War gutted it, and it stood as a shell for decades in East Berlin.

David Chipperfield's restoration, which reopened in 2009, kept the damage on show. In places you'll see shrapnel-pocked columns, faded murals and patches of bare brick. Next to them are clean new concrete walls and plain new rooms. The grand central staircase climbs through a stripped brick hall that looks half ruin, half cathedral.

Allow two to three hours. Spend half an hour of that just on the building itself.

## Nefertiti under the north dome

Most visitors make straight for the bust of Nefertiti. It's a painted limestone portrait of the Egyptian queen, made around 1345 BC in the workshop of the sculptor Thutmose. A German expedition dug it up at Amarna in 1912.

She stands alone in a domed room on the upper floor, lit softly in a glass case. Photography is not allowed in this room, and guards enforce it. Put your phone away and take your time with the colours. They are startlingly intact, and one eye was never inlaid.

The rest of the Egyptian collection fills the rooms around her. Highlights include:

- Portrait heads from Amarna, including the quieter studies left in Thutmose's workshop
- Papyri and coffins from the Egyptian Museum and Papyrus Collection
- Reliefs set into the restored courtyard galleries

## Gold, Troy and the long prehistory

The second collection here is the Museum of Prehistory and Early History, and it rewards the extra hour. The Berlin Gold Hat is a tall, cone-shaped Bronze Age ceremonial hat covered in stamped bands of gold. It is one of the strangest and most beautiful objects on the island.

Look for these as well:

- Heinrich Schliemann's finds from Troy and the story of what was lost after 1945
- The Neanderthal skull from Le Moustier in France
- Stone Age tools and grave goods arranged as a walk through time

## Reaching Bodestraße

The museum sits on the north half of Museum Island, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Spree. The closest stop is Museumsinsel on the U5, a short walk south across the Lustgarten.

You can also take the S-Bahn to Hackescher Markt (S3, S5, S7, S9). From there, cross the river by the Friedrichsbrücke. That takes about ten minutes, past the Alte Nationalgalerie's colonnades.

You'll usually go in through the James-Simon-Galerie. This white-columned visitor centre, opened in 2019, is where the ticket desks, cloakroom and lockers are. A lower-level passage takes you from there into the Neues Museum.

## Timed slots and the weekend crush

Book ahead. Tickets come with an entry time and are sold on the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin site, smb.museum, which also lists the current prices. Desk tickets are possible, but on busy days the next free slot may be hours away.

Weekends are the problem. From 11am to 6pm on Saturdays and Sundays the galleries are at their fullest, and the Nefertiti room fills up. If you can only come at the weekend, book the 10am slot and go to her first. You'll have the domed room before the midday groups arrive.

A few habits help:

- Leave big bags and coats in the James-Simon-Galerie cloakroom or lockers. Large bags aren't allowed in the galleries.
- Doors open at 10am and close at 6pm, Tuesday to Sunday. Every museum on the island is shut on Mondays, so don't plan a Monday visit.
- If you're seeing more than one island museum, look at the Museum Island day ticket on the official site.
- Speak quietly in the dome room. Guards will remind you if you don't.

## Making a day of Museum Island

The Neues Museum takes up a morning. The island can easily take up the rest of your day.

The Altes Museum next door holds Greek and Roman antiquities behind Schinkel's long Ionic colonnade. The Alte Nationalgalerie, with its 19th-century painting, sits in the colonnaded garden right behind you.

At the island's northern tip, the Bode-Museum shows sculpture and coins. The Berliner Dom's green dome rises over the Lustgarten, where people sit on the lawn between visits. For lunch, cross back to the cafés around Hackescher Markt. You'll find more choice there than on the island itself.
