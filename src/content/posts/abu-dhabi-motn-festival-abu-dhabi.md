## Four weeks of winter evenings

MOTN stands for Mother of the Nation. The title honours Sheikha Fatima bint Mubarak, widow of the UAE's founding president, Sheikh Zayed, who is known across the Emirates as Umm Al Emarat. That name tells you who the festival is for: families, several generations at a time, out together on a winter night.

The 2026 edition is announced for December 10, 2026 to January 5, 2027. That is close to four weeks, running through the UAE National Day afterglow, the school winter break and New Year. It is a long run, so you can choose your night instead of fitting in around a single weekend.

## What the name means on the ground

This is a festival built around family, not a concert. Past editions have mixed several kinds of zones in one walkable site:

- fairground rides and play areas for children of different ages
- food stalls and pop-up outlets from local and regional brands
- stage shows, roaming performers and live music
- craft workshops and hands-on activities
- Emirati heritage touches, such as traditional crafts, coffee and dates

No lineup is part of the facts we were given, so we won't guess at performers or headline acts. The organiser publishes the programme, zones and schedule on its official site. Think of the festival as a night out you wander through, more than a set list you plan your evening around.

## The Corniche question: where it happens

Recent MOTN editions have taken place along the Abu Dhabi Corniche. That is the long, landscaped waterfront on the northwest edge of the main island, facing the Arabian Gulf. The promenade is paved and lit, with palm-lined lawns on one side, the beach on the other and the city's towers behind.

Every edition sets its own venue. The organiser, the Department of Culture and Tourism Abu Dhabi (DCT Abu Dhabi), publishes the location for each year's run alongside the dates and ticket links. If the festival is on the Corniche, it shares the waterfront with some useful neighbours:

- **Corniche Beach**, the city's main public beach, with lifeguarded sections
- **Marina Mall** and the Breakwater, at the western end of the Corniche
- **Heritage Village** on the Breakwater, with replica traditional buildings
- **Qasr Al Watan** and **Emirates Palace Mandarin Oriental**, a short drive west
- **Qasr Al Hosn**, the city's oldest stone building, a few blocks inland

## Getting there without a metro

There is no metro in Abu Dhabi, so most festival-goers come by taxi or ride-hail. Inside the city, the official cream-and-gold taxis, Uber and Careem all operate, and a ride from most central districts is short. City buses also run along the Corniche Road corridor.

Coming from Dubai, the drive along the E11 takes about 90 minutes without heavy traffic. Zayed International Airport is about 30 to 40 minutes from the Corniche by car. On busy festival nights, waterfront parking fills up, so a lot of visitors don't drive the last stretch. They take a taxi instead, or park further inland and walk in.

## Moving through a family festival after dark

The crowd sets the tone here: families, strollers, grandparents and groups of teenagers. A few habits make the night easier.

- **Payment:** cards and phone payments such as Apple Pay are used across the UAE, festival stalls included. A small amount of dirhams is handy for the occasional cash-only vendor.
- **Dress:** this is a public family event in a conservative country. Shoulders and knees are usually covered, and swimwear stays on the beach.
- **Timing:** the UAE weekend is Saturday and Sunday. Weekends and the school holiday stretch around Christmas and New Year are the obvious peak nights. Weeknights earlier in the run usually feel more relaxed.
- **Photos:** people photograph the rides and stages all the time. Asking first before photographing other families, especially women and children, is standard courtesy here.

During the Gulf winter, evenings are mild, often in the low twenties Celsius, and it gets cooler near the water after dark. Locals often bring a light layer. Most families come after sunset, when the lights and stages switch on.

## How long people stay

Three to four hours covers an evening for most groups: a couple of rides, a meal from the stalls, a show and a slow loop of the site. Families with young children often stay for a shorter visit, while groups with teenagers tend to stay until closing.

The festival runs for four weeks, so plenty of Abu Dhabi residents go more than once. They might come once for the rides and again for a particular performance or workshop. Visitors who are only in the city for a few days usually fit it into one evening, after a daytime visit to Qasr Al Watan or the Louvre Abu Dhabi on Saadiyat Island.

## Tickets and official information

Ticketing, prices and gate times are set separately for each edition, and none of that was in our facts, so we won't print a figure. DCT Abu Dhabi publishes the dates, venue, opening times and ticket links for MOTN on its official festival channels. That is the reliable source for this year's run.

Past editions have used timed or zoned entry for some attractions, such as particular rides or ticketed shows. It is common for families to buy entry online and keep the ticket on a phone for scanning at the gate.
