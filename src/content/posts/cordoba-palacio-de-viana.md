## Twelve Courtyards Behind One Door

From Plaza de Don Gome the palace shows you very little. You see a long whitewashed wall and a stone doorway. Inside, the house opens into a run of courtyards, one after another, each with its own plants, paving and fountain.

Córdoba's houses face inward, built around open patios that keep them cool. Viana takes that habit further than any other house in the city. It has twelve courtyards and a formal garden, and over the centuries the house grew around them.

## What You Walk Through

The patios are the main draw. You'll find orange trees in square beds, columns, pebble-mosaic floors and potted geraniums climbing whitewashed walls. Each patio is laid out differently, so moving from one to the next is a big part of the visit.

Look out for these:

- **Patio de Recibo**, the entrance courtyard, where the visit starts
- **Patio de los Naranjos**, planted with orange trees
- **Patio de las Rejas**, named for its iron window grilles facing the street
- **Patio de la Madama**, with its fountain and statue
- **The garden**, more formal than the patios, with clipped hedges and gravel paths

Indoors, the palace holds an art and antiques collection gathered by the families who lived here. It includes tapestries, paintings, ceramics, furniture and Córdoba's embossed leatherwork, known as guadamecí. The rooms are shown as a furnished noble home, not as a gallery with bare walls.

## Choosing Your Ticket, Pacing Your Visit

There are usually two ways to visit. One ticket covers the courtyards and garden only. The other adds a guided tour of the house interior. Tour times and current prices are posted at the ticket desk and on the official website. If you want the interior, check them before you go.

Some practical points:

- **Tour times are fixed.** If you buy the interior ticket, ask for your slot first, then fill the wait with the patios.
- **Walk the patios slowly.** People here treat them as gardens, not corridors. Step aside for anyone framing a photo.
- **Don't touch the planters.** The flowers on the walls are tended with real care, so photograph them and leave them alone.
- **Give it 1.5–2 hours** with the tour, or about an hour for the patios alone.

## Beating the Crowds

It's busy here, especially at weekends. The calmest time is 10am–11am on weekdays, right at opening. You'll have the courtyards nearly to yourself before the groups arrive.

At weekends, try not to come between 11am and 3pm, or between 5pm and 6pm. That's when the patios fill and photos get harder. On Saturday the quieter window is 3pm–4pm. That doesn't work on Sunday, because the palace currently closes at 3pm.

At the moment it opens 10am–7pm from Tuesday to Saturday and 10am–3pm on Sunday, and it's closed on Mondays. Like many Andalusian sights, it changes its hours through the year, especially in the hottest summer months. The official site has this month's times.

## Seasons and the May Patios

Spring is when the courtyards look their best. In May, Córdoba holds its Festival de los Patios, when private homes open their flower-filled courtyards to visitors. Seeing Viana the same week shows you the city's patio culture at its fullest, though the city is also at its busiest then.

Córdoba summers are among the hottest in Spain. From June to September, go at opening. The shaded patios help, but the walk across town at midday is tough. Autumn and early spring are calmer and still green.

## Getting There and Nearby

The palace is in the Santa Marina neighbourhood, north of the old Jewish quarter and the Mezquita-Catedral. The walk from the Mezquita takes about 20 minutes through narrow lanes. You can also take a taxi to Plaza de Don Gome.

Places worth adding nearby:

- **Plaza de Capuchinos**, with the Cristo de los Faroles, a short walk away
- **Iglesia de Santa Marina**, one of Córdoba's medieval churches
- **Torre de la Malmuerta**, a defensive tower on the old wall line
- **The San Agustín and Santa Marina lanes**, where many homes take part in the May patio festival

A good half day: Viana at opening, then the walk south through Capuchinos to the centre for lunch.
