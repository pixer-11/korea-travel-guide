## Cross the inlet first

The SeaBus is the best way in. You board at Waterfront Station downtown and cross Burrard Inlet past the container cranes and floatplanes. When the doors open on the North Shore, the market is right in front of you, directly across from the terminal.

That's what makes Lonsdale Quay different from most city markets. You don't search for it. You arrive by water, walk a few steps and you're inside.

## What the quay actually is

It's a large waterfront building on two main levels, with a boutique hotel attached. The ground floor is food: produce stalls, bakeries, prepared-food counters and cafes. The upper level holds independent shops and small retailers. It's busy and well known, a regular stop for commuters and visitors, so don't expect it to be a secret.

Outside, a boardwalk runs along the water. From there the downtown Vancouver skyline fills the view to the south, and on a clear day the North Shore mountains stand behind you to the north.

## Eating your way along the ground floor

The ground floor has the most to see, so start there. Counters run in rows, and you order and pay at each one, the way you would at any food hall. Stalls change over the years, so skip the list of names and look for these:

- **Produce and fresh food stalls** for fruit to eat on the ferry
- **Bakery counters** for something warm with your coffee
- **Prepared-food vendors** for a takeaway lunch
- **Cafes** for an espresso while you watch the SeaBus come in

Take your food outside. Eating on the boardwalk, facing the skyline across the water, is the main reason people make the trip over.

## Upstairs, slower browsing

The upper level is calmer than the food floor. Shops here lean towards independent retailers rather than chains, which suits gift shopping or a slow wander. Leave this floor for after lunch, once the ground-floor queues have built up and you want a break from them.

The hotel is part of the same complex. Staying here puts you on the SeaBus doorstep, handy if you plan early starts up the North Shore mountains.

## Beating the lunch wave at the quay

Timing makes the biggest difference. The quietest window is 9am–11am on both weekdays and weekends. That's when you can walk between counters without bumping trays and get a boardwalk seat without hovering.

Try not to arrive on a weekend between 12pm and 7pm. That's the busiest stretch, when lines form at the popular food counters and seating outside fills up. If a weekend is your only option, catch a ferry that gets you in just after the 9am opening.

A few habits that help here:

- **Pay by card.** Cards and tap payments are accepted almost everywhere in Canada, including at market counters.
- **Tipping at counters is optional.** The card machine may prompt you for a tip. For counter service it's your choice; for table service, 15–20% is the norm in Canada.
- **Tap on for the SeaBus.** You can use a Compass Card or tap a contactless bank card at the fare gates. The current fare is posted on the TransLink website and at the terminal.
- **Keep track of closing time.** The market currently closes at 7pm, so a late-evening crossing means the shutters may be down when you arrive.

## The SeaBus as part of the outing

Treat the crossing as part of the visit, not just the way there. The trip takes roughly a quarter of an hour. The boats have big windows on all sides, so you get harbour views without paying for a cruise.

To get to the ferry, Waterfront Station is where the Expo Line, Canada Line, West Coast Express and SeaBus all meet. Follow the SeaBus signs through the station to the terminal. Sailings run often during the day, and if you just miss one, the wait for the next is short.

If you'd rather drive, the market's address is 123 Carrie Cates Ct, North Vancouver. Expect weekend parking near the waterfront to be competitive. With downtown so close by ferry, though, most visitors don't need a car.

## Beyond the market doors

The quay also makes a good starting point. Head east along the waterfront and you reach the Shipyards District, a redeveloped stretch of the old shipbuilding waterfront. It has a long pier, open plazas and the Polygon Gallery, a photography and contemporary art space with a striking saw-tooth roof.

Lonsdale Avenue climbs uphill from the market into Lower Lonsdale, with cafes and restaurants along the way. It's steep but walkable, and good for a second coffee.

For the mountains, the bus exchange beside the quay is your connection. Route 236 runs from Lonsdale Quay to Capilano Suspension Bridge Park and on to the base of Grouse Mountain. A good plan is coffee and pastries at the market at 9am, a morning on the mountain, and a late lunch back on the boardwalk once the weekday rush has passed.

Plan on an hour to an hour and a half at the market itself, plus the crossing each way. Add the Shipyards and Lower Lonsdale and it easily fills a relaxed half day.
