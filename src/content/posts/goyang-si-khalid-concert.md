## What's happening and where

Khalid is scheduled to play Goyang-si on December 5, 2026. The venue is Kintex, the country's largest convention and exhibition center, sitting in the Ilsan district of Goyang-si, just north of Seoul across the Han River.

Kintex isn't a traditional concert hall. It's built for trade shows and expos, with exhibition halls large enough to convert into arena-style concert setups for major touring acts. That means huge floor capacity, but also long walks from entrance to seat and less of the intimate feel of a dedicated music venue.

## Getting to Kintex

The simplest route is the subway. Kintex Station sits on the Ilsan Line (formerly Line 3's extension), and the walk from the station to the exhibition halls takes you through a covered connective corridor, useful in December when temperatures in the Seoul metro area sit well below freezing.

If you're coming from central Seoul, budget close to an hour door to door depending on your starting station and transfers. Taxis and rideshares are an option too, but Goyang-si traffic around Kintex backs up badly before and after any large event.

- Nearest station: Kintex Station (Ilsan Line)
- Approx. travel time from central Seoul: 45-60 minutes by subway
- Alternative: taxi/rideshare, but expect congestion pre- and post-show

## Tickets and timing

Do not treat any resale listing or third-party aggregator as the final word on price, seating chart, or door times for this show. Kintex events of this scale typically post gates and set times only shortly before the show, and exact stage configuration inside the hall varies by act.

Confirm timing and tickets on the official site, and cross-check with Kintex's own event calendar, since a single date at a multi-hall complex like this can occasionally shift halls or timing.

## Finding your way around Kintex once you're inside

Kintex is split into two main exhibition centers connected by a walkway, so check your ticket for the specific hall number before you arrive. Signage throughout the complex is bilingual in Korean and English, and staff at entry points can point you toward the correct hall if you're unsure.

Cloakrooms and food stalls tend to cluster near the main atrium rather than inside the halls themselves, so it's worth locating one on your way in if you plan to grab something during a break.

## What locals do around show nights at Kintex

Regular attendees of Kintex events know to arrive well before the posted door time, since security screening for a full-capacity show can create a long line even when the hall itself isn't full yet. Bag checks are standard for events of this size, so pack light.

- Arrive with buffer time before doors: security lines move slowly at full capacity
- Keep bags small: large bags often mean an extra check or a bag-drop detour
- Eat before you go or plan for limited on-site food options: nearby Ilsan has considerably more restaurant choice than the immediate Kintex grounds
- Payment inside Kintex is overwhelmingly card and mobile pay; carrying cash isn't necessary for concessions

## Making a night (or day) of it in Ilsan

Goyang-si's Ilsan district, where Kintex sits, has hotels within easy reach of the venue, which is worth considering given how late a concert crowd typically clears out and how tight last-train timing can get on a December night. Lake Park (Ilsan Lake Park), one of the largest man-made lake parks in Asia, is a short trip from Kintex and makes for an easy daytime add-on if you're building a full day around the show rather than just the concert itself.

If you're coming from outside Korea, note that Incheon International Airport is a manageable distance from Goyang-si, closer in fact than it is to many parts of central Seoul, which makes Kintex events surprisingly workable as a same-day arrival if your flight timing allows it.
