## A centre court beside the runways

The setting is compact. The Dubai Tennis Stadium sits inside the Aviation Club in Al Garhoud, with Dubai International Airport right next door. It is a small, steep bowl compared with the Grand Slam arenas, so even the upper rows put you close enough to see a player's footwork.

That closeness is the reason to come. Top-ten players warm up, practise and play within a short walk of each other. Between matches the crowd spills out into the club grounds rather than a car park.

## Two weeks, two tours

The 2027 edition runs from February 14 to 27. It combines two separate tournaments:

- **WTA 1000:** the women's event, one tier below the Grand Slams on the WTA calendar.
- **ATP 500:** the men's event, a mid-level stop on the ATP Tour.

By tradition the women's tournament fills the first week and the men's the second. The early rounds of each week bring the busiest schedules, with several courts in use at once. The finals weekends are the reverse: a single court and one main match.

The tournament has a long history. The men's event began in 1993 and the women's joined in 2001, and both helped put Dubai on the regular tour calendar. Players come straight from the Middle East swing and the Australian summer, so the fields are usually strong.

## Reaching Garhoud

Getting there is simple because the venue is so central. The closest Dubai Metro station is **GGICO** on the Red Line, and the club is a short taxi ride from there. Many fans skip the Metro and take a taxi or ride-hailing car straight to the Aviation Club gate. Downtown and Deira are both a short drive away.

Visitors often stay close to the venue:

- **Al Garhoud and Deira:** walking or short-taxi distance, and generally cheaper than the beach districts.
- **Dubai Creek and Festival City:** just across the water, with large hotels.
- **Downtown:** further out, but well linked by the Red Line.

Late-night taxis queue outside the club after evening sessions. On finals nights fans often walk to the main road to find one quicker.

## Courtside manners at the Aviation Club

Tennis has stricter crowd rules than football, and the Dubai stewards enforce them. These are the ones that catch first-timers out:

- **Changeovers only.** You leave and return to your seat only when the players change ends. Ushers hold people at the entrances during games.
- **Silence during points.** Fans cheer between rallies, never during them. No flash photography.
- **Dress code.** Smart-casual is normal, and shoulders and knees covered is a sensible default in the UAE. Evening sessions usually call for a light layer.
- **Payment.** Cards and phone payments are standard in Dubai, so food stalls and bars rarely need cash.

Alcohol is served at licensed outlets inside the Aviation Club, which is unusual for a Dubai sports venue. Public drunkenness is still a serious offence in the UAE. Regulars keep it to a drink or two and take a taxi home.

## Day sessions, night sessions and the Irish Village

The day changes character as it goes on. Daytime sessions in the early rounds feel relaxed. Outside courts are easy to drift between, and practice courts let you watch big names hit at close range. Many fans choose an early-round day for exactly this reason: the most tennis for one visit.

Evening sessions are the showpiece. February is one of Dubai's mildest months, with warm afternoons and cooler nights, which suits outdoor tennis under lights. The bigger names often draw the late slots, and the stadium is usually at its fullest after dark.

The **Irish Village**, the long-running pub in the Aviation Club grounds, is the tournament's unofficial lounge. Fans gather there before and after matches. Big screens show play from the courts next door.

## Fitting it into a Dubai trip

A single session takes about three to four hours, and an early-round day pass can easily fill an afternoon and evening. Garhoud is close to Dubai Creek, so it is easy to pair a morning in **Al Fahidi** or an abra crossing to the **Deira souks** with an evening at the tennis.

Tickets, session schedules and the daily order of play are all on the tournament's official site, run by the organiser, Dubai Duty Free. Families often pick weekend day sessions. Serious fans tend to go for the quarter-final evenings, when the draw has narrowed but the stadium still hosts more than one big match.
