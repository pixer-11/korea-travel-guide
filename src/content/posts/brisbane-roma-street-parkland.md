## A garden built on railway yards

Brisbane's CBD ends, and the planting begins. Roma Street Parkland opened in 2001 on land that used to be railway goods yards. Today it is one of the largest subtropical gardens in any city centre in the world.

The address is 1 Parkland Blvd. The park climbs gently from the Roma Street side up towards Spring Hill, so most walks take you uphill or downhill. Expect stone paths, timber boardwalks and steps cut into the slope. Office towers stay visible above the treeline the whole way round.

It is one of the most-reviewed outdoor spaces in Brisbane, and it gets busy. Don't come expecting a secret spot. Come for very well kept display gardens you can reach in five minutes from a train platform.

## What you'll walk through

Each section of the park has its own character. These are the ones worth planning around:

- **The Spectacle Garden**: the horticultural showpiece, with tightly planted beds that change through the year. Bring a camera.
- **Fern Gully**: a shaded, cooler walk under tree ferns, the best part of the park on a hot afternoon.
- **The Lake**: the central waterway, with paths and shaded edges where people sit and watch the water.
- **Celebration Lawn and the amphitheatre**: open grass and terraced seating used for events. On other days they are good for spreading out a picnic rug.
- **Playground and barbecue areas**: popular with families, especially on weekends.

You'll likely see water dragons sunning themselves on rocks and paths. Brush turkeys scratch through the garden beds too. Both are common across Brisbane's green spaces and quite used to people.

## Getting there from the city

Roma Street Station sits right beside the park. It's served by Queensland Rail City network trains, and the Airtrain from Brisbane Airport stops here too. Roma Street busway station is in the same transport precinct.

On foot, it takes about 10–15 minutes to walk from Queen Street Mall up to the park's lower entrances. From Spring Hill, you can drop in at the top of the slope and walk down through the gardens to the city. That's the easier direction for tired legs.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

The busiest window is on weekends, from 9am to 9pm. That covers pretty much all of Saturday and Sunday, so if you can, choose a weekday. Weekends are when the barbecues fill up, birthday groups claim the lawns and the playground gets loud.

Google lists the park as open 24 hours, every day of the week. Even so, the planting is the reason to come, and it's best seen in daylight. Brisbane summers are hot and humid. In the warmer months, the morning or late afternoon light is more comfortable for the uphill sections. Allow 1–2 hours for a proper loop, or longer if you're bringing lunch.

## Using the park the Brisbane way

People in Brisbane treat this place like a back garden in the city. A few habits will help you fit in:

- **Barbecues are first come, first served.** Public electric barbecues in Brisbane parks are shared. Clean the plate when you're done, because the next group is usually waiting.
- **Bring your own picnic.** Grab supplies in the CBD before you walk up. Shade under the trees on the lawns goes early on weekends.
- **Don't feed the wildlife.** Water dragons and brush turkeys will come close. Feeding them is discouraged and leads to pushy birds at the next picnic.
- **Stay on the paths in display beds.** The Spectacle Garden is carefully tended, and photos from the path work fine.
- **Check the events calendar.** The amphitheatre and lawns host events at different times of year. If one is on, parts of the park may be busier or fenced off. The Brisbane City Council parks pages list what's coming up.

Sun protection matters more than you'd think here. Large parts of the upper lawns are open to the sky, and the Queensland sun is strong even on mild days. Carry water and a hat.

## Pairing it with the rest of the city

The park makes an easy first or last stop on a CBD day. Walk down to Queen Street Mall for shopping and food. If you came in from the airport on the Airtrain, you could also spend a slow hour here before checking in.

From the upper edge, the streets of Spring Hill lead uphill past old Queenslander houses and terrace streets. Head south and the river and South Bank are within reach by foot or train. Roma Street makes a natural link between the city's green edge and its waterfront.
