## A Suzhou garden behind a city wall

Step through the gate and the traffic noise fades. Lou Lim Ioc Garden is a classical Chinese garden squeezed into a city block. Paths, pavilions and a pond sit inside a high wall painted in the pale colours of old Macau.

The garden belonged to the Lou family, wealthy merchants in late nineteenth-century Macau. Its layout follows the gardens of Suzhou: water at the centre, rockwork that hides one view and opens another, and paths that never run straight for long.

It's compact. You can walk the whole circuit in minutes, but the design is meant to slow you down, and it works.

## Where it really is

First, get your bearings. Some listings put the garden under Hac Sa, the black-sand beach area on Coloane. The address, Estrada de Adolfo Loureiro, is on the Macau peninsula, a short walk from Tap Seac Square. Point a taxi driver or map app at the street address rather than "Hac Sa", or you'll end up at the wrong end of the territory.

From Senado Square, walking north through the old streets takes roughly 20 minutes. Plenty of city buses stop along the surrounding avenues. Check a live route planner for the right line from your hotel.

## The nine-turn bridge over the lotus pond

The pond is the garden's centrepiece. A zigzag bridge crosses it, and its sharp turns are a classical touch: tradition holds that evil spirits can only travel in straight lines. For you, each turn opens a new angle on the water, the rockery and the pavilions.

The pond fills with lotus in summer. Late spring into July is when the leaves crowd the surface and the flowers rise above them. In winter it's flatter and plainer, though the bridge and rockwork are just as good.

What to look for from the bridge:

- Fish gathering under the zigzags, especially near the turns
- The artificial rock mountain, with its grottoes and stepped paths
- The main hall's reflection across the water

## Spring Grass Hall and the rockery

The largest building surprises people. The main hall, Chun Cou Tong (Spring Grass Hall), mixes Chinese and Portuguese styles. A colonnaded European-style front faces a garden laid out on Chinese principles, and that blend is very Macau.

Opposite stands the rockery, a man-made hill of eroded stone. Narrow paths climb through it, sometimes past openings framing a single view of the pond. The steps are uneven and tight, so take it slowly with small children or bags.

Smaller pavilions sit around the edges. They're shaded places to stop and look rather than exhibits, and that's the point. The garden rewards sitting more than ticking things off.

## Six in the morning to midnight

The long hours are unusual. The gates open at 6am and close at midnight every day of the week, so you can come at almost any hour. That gives you two quite different gardens.

Early morning is exercise time in Macau's parks. You may see people practising tai chi, stretching or walking circuits before work. Mid-morning to afternoon brings more visitors, plus the shade and the heat of the day.

After dark the garden is lit and much quieter. An evening walk after dinner near Tap Seac is easy to fit in, as long as you're out by midnight.

## Moving through the garden quietly

This is a park people use every day, not a stage. Behave as you would in someone's courtyard.

- **Keep your voice down.** The garden is small and sound carries across the water.
- **Give exercisers room.** If a tai chi group has taken a terrace in the morning, walk around rather than through.
- **Don't feed the fish** unless a sign says you can, and stay off the rocks around the pond's edge.
- **Shoot photos from the paths.** The bridge is narrow, so step to a corner to let people pass rather than stopping mid-span.
- **Sit and stay.** Benches and pavilion seats are there to be used. People here linger, and nobody will hurry you.

It's a public garden, so there's no ticket desk at the gate. Bring water in summer, because the paths are compact but humid. Little is sold inside, so buy snacks before you arrive.

## Walking on from the gate

The garden fits easily into a half-day walk through the north of the peninsula. Nearby stops include:

- **Tap Seac Square**, a few minutes away, ringed by pastel colonial buildings
- **Dr. Sun Yat-sen Memorial House**, a short walk east, a Moorish-style house that's now a museum
- **Lin Fong Temple and the Lin Zexu Museum**, a bit further north, telling the story of the commissioner behind the opium crackdown of 1839
- **Guia Hill**, uphill to the east, with its lighthouse, chapel and fort

Start at the garden early, before the heat, then work toward Tap Seac for lunch. Or reverse it and end your day on the zigzag bridge after the lights come on.
