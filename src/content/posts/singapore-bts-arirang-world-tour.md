Six nights in a row is a long run, even for a stadium. BTS brings the ARIRANG tour to Singapore's National Stadium from 17 to 22 December 2026, and that makes it the last big K-pop event of the year in Singapore. The tour takes its name from *Arirang*, the best-known Korean folk song. For a week in December, Kallang belongs to ARMY.

This guide covers what is known: the dates, the venue, how to get there and how a stadium night in Singapore works. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site before you book flights or hotels.

## Six nights at the National Stadium

The announced run is 17, 18, 19, 20, 21 and 22 December 2026. That is unusual for Singapore. Most international acts here play one to three nights, so six dates suggests the promoter expects heavy demand from around the region.

Look at the full span before you decide which night to go. Weeknight shows in the middle of the run usually draw fewer people flying in for the weekend. The 19th and 20th fall on a Saturday and Sunday, and Kallang's hotels and MRT trains fill up accordingly. Daily show times are posted with official ticketing, so check them there rather than guessing.

## Getting tickets without getting burned

Only buy through the channels BTS's official accounts and the Singapore promoter name. BTS posts tour news on Weverse and on its official social accounts. The promoter's announcement lists the authorised ticketing partner, the presale rules and the seating map.

Things to check before you pay:

- **The seller.** If BTS or the promoter didn't name it, don't use it.
- **Name and ID rules.** Big K-pop tours often tie tickets to the buyer's identity, and resold tickets can be refused at the gate.
- **The seat map.** The National Stadium is a deep bowl, so look at where the stage sits before you choose a block.
- **The current price list.** It is published with the official ticketing page. Rely on that, not on figures quoted by fans online.

Ticket scams spike whenever a stadium K-pop show is announced in Singapore, and the police regularly warn about them. If a stranger on social media offers a seat and wants a bank transfer, assume it is a scam.

## The Kallang Bowl and Its Roof

The National Stadium opened in 2014 and holds around 55,000 people. It sits at the Sports Hub precinct on the Kallang Basin, close to the city centre. Its signature feature is a retractable dome roof, one of the largest of its kind in the world.

That roof matters in December. The northeast monsoon brings regular heavy afternoon and evening downpours to Singapore at this time of year. With the roof closed, the show carries on and the seating bowl stays dry. The stadium also has a cooling system built in at seat level, so the inside isn't as hot as the humidity outside.

The walk from the MRT to the gates is a different matter. Some of the route around the Sports Hub is sheltered and some isn't. Pack a compact umbrella or a light poncho, and check the venue's prohibited-items list first, because large umbrellas are often banned inside stadiums.

## Reaching Kallang by MRT

The train is the easiest way in, and on concert nights it is the only sensible one. You have three stations to choose from:

- **Stadium (CC6, Circle Line):** the closest stop, right at the stadium. It is also the most crowded one after the show.
- **Kallang (EW10, East-West Line):** about 10 to 15 minutes on foot, partly along the river. Useful if you are staying in the east or heading towards Changi Airport.
- **Mountbatten (CC7, Circle Line):** one stop further east. A short walk, and a handy way to skip the queues at Stadium.

You can pay for the MRT by tapping a contactless bank card or a phone wallet at the gates, so visitors don't need to buy a separate travel card. Taxis and ride-hailing cars are scarce around the stadium for a while after a show, and surge pricing kicks in. Walking to Kallang or Mountbatten usually gets you home faster.

## Concert nights the Singapore way

Stadium nights in Singapore are orderly. Big crowds take MRT staff directions seriously, and that is what keeps exits moving. Some habits that help:

- **Pay by card or phone.** Food outlets, merch counters and transport around the Sports Hub are almost all cashless. Tipping isn't expected anywhere.
- **Queue for merch early, or on a non-show day if one is offered.** Official tour merch lines at big K-pop shows can take hours, so check the official merch announcement for where and when the booth opens.
- **Bring an official ARMY Bomb.** The official lightstick usually syncs with the show's lighting through the official app. Unofficial lightsticks won't join in, and if they are bulky they may be stopped at security.
- **Keep fan freebies small and tidy.** ARMY often hand out photocards and slogans near the venue. That is fine, but leaving rubbish in a public space can get you fined in Singapore, so bin everything.
- **Hydrate outside, not just inside.** The walk and the queue happen in tropical heat, even after dark.

Eating and drinking aren't allowed on trains or in MRT stations. That includes the bubble tea you were finishing on the platform, and the fines are real.

## Before the show, after the encore

Kallang Wave Mall is built into the Sports Hub, right beside the stadium. It is the obvious place for a meal, air-conditioning and a toilet before the gates open. On a concert day it gets packed, so eat early or eat elsewhere.

For something more local, Old Airport Road Food Centre is one of Singapore's best-known hawker centres. It is a short ride east of Kallang, and a plate there is a far better pre-show meal than stadium snacks. If you are staying in the city, the Marina Bay waterfront is close by. After the show, the Circle Line runs back towards Promenade and the Bayfront area.

## Planning a December trip around it

The run takes up most of the week before Christmas, when Singapore's school holidays are in full swing and Orchard Road has its Christmas lights up. Flights and hotels get expensive and book out fast. Hotels near the East-West or Circle lines make the trip home from the stadium much easier.

Plan for rain every afternoon, keep your ticket confirmation on your phone and give yourself extra time at security. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site, since that is the only place show-day details are settled.
