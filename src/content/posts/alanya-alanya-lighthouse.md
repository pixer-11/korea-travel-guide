## Where the peninsula runs out

The road ends here, and so does the land. The lighthouse is a small, pale tower on low rocks at the western foot of Alanya's castle headland. Below it the Mediterranean is clear enough to see the stones on the bottom.

Behind you, the castle walls climb the ridge. Ahead there is only sea, with the open water stretching off toward the southwest. That contrast is why the place is consistently well rated: you get a lot of scenery for very little effort.

It's well-known and well visited, so don't come expecting solitude. Come for the walk, the rocks and the sea.

## Walking out along the water

The approach is the best part. Most visitors start near **Damlataş Cave**, at the southern end of **Cleopatra Beach**, and follow the coast road around the base of the headland toward the tip.

The route is flat and paved, with the sea on one side and limestone cliffs on the other. Along the way you'll find:

- Places to stop and look back along the length of Cleopatra Beach
- Rocky edges where people sit with their feet near the water
- Views up to the castle fortifications on the ridge above

It's an easy stroll from the centre. Taxis and local dolmuş minibuses also serve the Damlataş area if you'd rather save your legs for the climb to the castle later.

## What you'll find at the tip

Don't expect a grand monument. The tower is compact, and the attraction is the setting around it: wave-worn rock, the cliff face behind and an uninterrupted horizon.

The rocks are sharp and uneven, and they get slippery near the waterline. Sandals with grip are better than flip-flops if you plan to scramble down for photos. There's little shade at the point, so carry water from town.

Google lists the site as a museum-type venue with set hours. Whether anything inside is open on a given day is worth confirming locally, rather than counting on it.

## Timing your visit

At the moment Alanya Lighthouse is listed as open 9am–5pm every day of the week. Hours at sites like this can shift with the season, so check locally or on Google before you set out.

Morning is the smart choice in summer. The walk out is fully exposed, and the rock holds heat by the afternoon. Arriving soon after 9am usually beats the people who come after a beach morning.

Spring and autumn are the most comfortable months for the walk. The sea is still warm and the light is softer on the cliffs.

## Making the walk part of a longer day

The lighthouse fits neatly into a loop around the peninsula. Nearby options include:

- **Damlataş Cave**: a dripstone cave right at the start of the walk, known for its humid air
- **Cleopatra Beach**: the long sandy stretch running west from the headland
- **Alanya Castle**: the fortress on the ridge above, reached by road or by the cable car from the Damlataş side
- **Kızılkule (Red Tower)**: the octagonal Seljuk tower by the harbour on the peninsula's east side

You can also see the lighthouse from the water. The boat trips that leave from Alanya harbour usually circle the headland and pass the sea caves on its cliffs, including the ones known as Pirates' Cave and Lovers' Cave.

## Getting the rocks to yourself

Treat the tip the way Alanya residents treat their shoreline: somewhere to sit, not just a photo stop. People settle on the flatter rocks with tea or water and watch the boats round the headland.

A few habits make the visit smoother:

- **Carry small Turkish lira notes** for the kiosks and minibuses around Damlataş, where card readers aren't guaranteed.
- **Say "merhaba"** (hello) and "teşekkürler" (thank you) at kiosks; it's noticed and appreciated.
- **Move along after your photo** at the narrowest viewpoints, because space at the point is tight when tour groups arrive.
- **Ask for "Deniz Feneri"** (the lighthouse) if you're giving directions to a taxi driver.

If you'd rather see it from the sea, book a boat at the harbour and choose a morning departure. The water around the headland is calmer then, and the cliffs are better lit for photos.
