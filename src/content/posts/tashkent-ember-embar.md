## Twenty-two floors above Tashkent

Tashkent is a flat, wide city. Very little rises above the plane trees and the Soviet-era apartment blocks, so a 22nd-floor terrace gives you a view that much of the capital never sees.

That's what Ember & Embar sells. It sits at the top of the new InterContinental Tashkent and combines a dining room with a rooftop bar. In a short time it has become one of the city's most stylish and most discussed places to spend an evening.

## Two names, one floor

The name joins two words, and the venue does two jobs. One is a sit-down dinner. The other is a bar where you can stand at the edge of the city with a drink and stay as long as you like.

You can treat it either way:

- **Dinner first:** book a table, eat, then move on to drinks once the light has gone.
- **Drinks only:** go up for a glass at golden hour and have dinner somewhere at street level.
- **Late night:** come after dinner elsewhere, when the city below is lit up and the evening has started.

We haven't been given the menu, so we won't guess at dishes. Check the hotel's current menu before you go if what's on the plate matters to you.

## Reading the skyline

From this height, the landmark to look for is the Tashkent Tower. At 375 metres it is the tallest structure in Central Asia, and its lit-up mast makes it easy to find after dark.

On clear days, look northeast for the faint line of the Western Tian Shan foothills, where locals go skiing and hiking around Chimgan. Summer haze often hides them. Spring and autumn give you the best chance of seeing that far.

Below you, the city spreads out in its grid of wide Soviet-planned avenues. Gold domes and blue tilework show here and there between the newer glass towers.

## When the terrace earns its height

The season decides a lot here. Tashkent summers are fierce, and afternoon temperatures in July and August often pass 35°C. On a rooftop that means the evening is the only comfortable time, and the hour after sunset is better still.

Late April to early June and September to October are the easiest months. The evenings are warm without being punishing, and the air tends to be clearer. In winter it's cold up here, so ask whether there's an indoor or sheltered section before you commit to the terrace.

## Getting a good table at the InterContinental

It's a hotel venue, so it works like one. Some local habits will make the evening go more smoothly.

- **Reserve, and name your seat.** It's new and in demand, so book ahead and ask for a terrace or window table. Confirm the opening hours at the same time, because we can't publish them.
- **Dress up a little.** Tashkent goes out smartly dressed, and hotel rooftops expect smart-casual. Shorts and flip-flops risk being turned away at the lift.
- **Get there by Yandex Go.** It's the taxi app everyone in the city uses. Fares are shown upfront and you can pay by card in the app, so there's no haggling outside the hotel.
- **Read the bill before you tip.** Many Tashkent restaurants add a service charge automatically. If one is already on the bill, you don't need to add a big tip.
- **Have a card ready.** International hotels generally take Visa and Mastercard, though many smaller places in town only take local Uzcard or Humo cards, or cash in som. It's worth carrying both for the rest of the night.

Arriving before sunset usually gets you better seats than turning up once the terrace has filled for the view. That's reasoning, not measurement, but it holds at most rooftop bars.

## Making a night of it

Ember & Embar works best as the high point of an evening, not the whole of it. Spend the afternoon at ground level first.

- **Chorsu Bazaar:** go earlier in the day. The huge blue-domed hall is packed with dried fruit, spices and fresh bread.
- **Tashkent Metro:** the stations are famous for their decoration, with chandeliers, marble and Soviet-era mosaics. Kosmonavtlar station, with its space theme, is the one people photograph most.
- **Amir Timur Square:** the equestrian statue and the Hotel Uzbekistan, with its lattice front, are worth a slow walk in the late afternoon.

Then take a taxi to the InterContinental and finish the day 22 floors up. Allow two to three hours for dinner and drinks, or about an hour if you're only coming for a sunset drink.
