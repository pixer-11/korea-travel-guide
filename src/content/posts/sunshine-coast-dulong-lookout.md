## A ridge-top window facing the sea

The road thins out, and then the whole eastern view opens up. Dulong Lookout sits on the slopes of the Blackall Range at Kureelpa. Below it, the hinterland falls away in folds of pasture and patches of rainforest toward the Maroochy plain.

On a clear day your eye travels from the green ridges in front of you to the flat coastal strip. Mount Coolum's rounded hump stands out against it, and the high-rises of Maroochydore and the Pacific sit behind. The lookout is a small clearing on a country road, nothing like a built viewing platform. That's a big part of its appeal.

## Why it stays quiet

This lookout gets far less traffic than the hinterland's headline stops. Most visitors driving the Blackall Range head straight for Montville's main street or the waterfalls at Kondalilla and Mapleton Falls. Dulong is a detour they never take.

That leaves a spot where you can usually park, step out and have the view mostly to yourself. Reviewers consistently rate it well for exactly that reason: it's an unhurried look at the coast without a crowd at the railing.

## Driving up Dulong Road

You need a car for this one. Dulong Road climbs off the Nambour–Mapleton Road, and the drive up from Nambour takes roughly 15 minutes through steep, winding country. Coming the other way, Mapleton village is a few minutes beyond the lookout.

A few things to know about the road:

- **It's narrow and twisting.** Drive it slowly, especially in wet weather, and watch for cyclists and farm vehicles.
- **Parking is limited.** Pull fully off the road surface so you're not blocking traffic on a bend.
- **Mobile coverage can drop.** Load your map before you leave Nambour.

Google lists it as open 24 hours. In practice it's an open roadside area with no gate, but there's no lighting either, so after dark you're on your own.

## Chasing the light

The view faces east, and that decides the best time to come. At sunrise the light comes up over the ocean and slowly works its way across the ridges toward you. The hollows below often hold low mist on cooler mornings.

By mid-afternoon the sun is behind you. That lights the plain evenly and suits photos of the coastline. Haze builds on humid summer days and softens the far horizon, so the drier, clearer months of winter usually give the sharpest long-distance view.

After a run of rain the paddocks turn an intense green. You'll also want to check the forecast, though, because cloud can sit right on the range while the coast stays sunny.

## Manners on a rural ridge

The lookout sits among farms and homes, which shapes how to behave here. Visitors are passing through someone's neighbourhood, and they're welcome as long as they act like it.

- **Keep noise down,** particularly if you've come for sunrise. Car doors and music carry a long way across a quiet valley.
- **Take every scrap of rubbish with you.** Don't count on there being a bin.
- **Stay on the cleared area.** Don't climb fences into the paddocks for a better angle.
- **Bring your own coffee.** There's no kiosk at the lookout. Pick one up in Nambour or Mapleton on the way.

The best approach is to treat Dulong as a pause in a longer drive, not a destination with facilities. Fifteen to thirty minutes is plenty unless you've packed a thermos and want to watch the light change.

## Folding it into a hinterland loop

Dulong slots neatly into a half-day circuit of the Blackall Range. Pair it with stops a short drive along the ridge:

- **Mapleton Falls National Park:** a lookout over the falls dropping into the Pencil Creek gorge, close to Mapleton village.
- **Kondalilla National Park:** rainforest walks and swimming holes near Montville.
- **Montville:** the main street of galleries, cafés and craft shops along the range.
- **Nambour:** the town at the foot of the climb, with shops and fuel before you head up.

A good order is Nambour, then up Dulong Road to the lookout, then on to Mapleton Falls. From there, follow the ridge south to Montville and Kondalilla. That way the coastal view comes first, while the morning light is still on it.
