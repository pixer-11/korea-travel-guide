## Juniors on the world circuit

Picture rows of blue tables under hall lights, each one with a different age group playing on it. WTT Youth Contender Vadodara is part of World Table Tennis's youth series. It runs singles events for each age bracket, from the under-11s up to the under-19s, with boys' and girls' draws in each.

The 2027 edition takes place in early January. Vadodara hosts this stop every year, and players travel in from across Asia and further afield.

Several matches run at once, so you can move between courts. A 12-year-old's rally plays out next to an under-19 final-round match. The difference in pace from one table to the next is half the appeal.

## Dates, tickets and the venue

The organiser, World Table Tennis, runs an event page for each Youth Contender. That page carries the draws, schedules and results. Youth events at this level tend to be low-key affairs, with family members, coaches and local club players making up most of the crowd.

Vadodara sits on the main Mumbai–Delhi rail line. Vadodara Junction railway station is the arrival point for most domestic visitors. Vadodara Airport, at Harni on the city's north-east edge, has direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru. Ahmedabad's international airport is roughly two hours away by road.

Visitors usually get around the city by auto-rickshaw or ride-hailing apps such as Uber and Ola.

## Watching from the stands

Table tennis has its own crowd etiquette, and junior players notice when people break it.

- **Move between points, not during rallies.** Spectators wait for the point to end before finding a seat or walking behind a court.
- **No flash.** Flash photography distracts players tracking a fast ball, and officials at WTT events discourage it.
- **Applaud the point, not the error.** Clapping when a player misses is considered poor form.
- **Pay digitally.** Most vendors and auto drivers in Vadodara accept UPI payments, scanned from a QR code at the counter.

Parents and coaches usually sit close to their players' courts. Neutral spectators tend to drift towards whichever table has the longest rallies.

## Vadodara around the matches

Early January is cool, dry winter in Gujarat. Mornings are crisp and afternoons are mild, which makes it comfortable to walk between sights.

Uttarayan, Gujarat's kite festival, falls on 14 January. In the weeks before it, markets in the old city fill with paper kites and spools of manja string.

- **Laxmi Vilas Palace:** the Gaekwad royal family's 1890 Indo-Saracenic residence, one of the largest private homes ever built.
- **Sayaji Baug:** the city's large central park on the Vishwamitri River, with a museum and picture gallery inside.
- **Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park:** a UNESCO World Heritage Site of mosques and fort walls, about an hour's drive north-east.
- **Food:** sev usal is Vadodara's signature snack, a spicy pea curry under crunchy sev. Gujarati thalis are easy to find all over the city.

A full day of youth matches can easily be paired with a morning at the palace or an evening in the old city. That's how many visiting families spend the gaps between their players' rounds.
