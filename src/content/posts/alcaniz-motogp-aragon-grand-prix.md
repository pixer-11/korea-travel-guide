## Why go

MotorLand Aragón is one of the more technical, fan-friendly tracks on the MotoGP calendar — a 5.3 km circuit with a long back straight, tight infield chicanes, and grandstands that give a genuine sightline advantage over some of the sport's bigger, more sprawling venues. The Aragon Grand Prix, held August 28–30, 2026, is a full three-day weekend covering free practice, qualifying, and races across MotoGP, Moto2, and Moto3, plus supporting series that typically fill out the schedule.

For visitors, it's also a chance to see a corner of Spain — the Bajo Aragón region of Teruel province — that most tourists skip entirely in favor of Madrid or Barcelona.

## Getting there

Alcañiz sits roughly 100 km southeast of Zaragoza and around 150 km northwest of Tarragona, making it a realistic day-trip base from either city if you don't want to stay overnight. MotorLand Aragón itself is about 6 km from the town center, off the A-222. Zaragoza's airport is the nearest with regular domestic and some European connections; from there it's about a 1.5-hour drive.

There's no train station directly serving the circuit, so most fans arrive by car, rental, or organized coach transfer — during race weekend, shuttle buses typically run from Alcañiz and sometimes from Zaragoza, though exact routes and pickup points change year to year, so check the official event site closer to the date. If you're driving, expect signposted parking areas around the circuit that fill up fast on Sunday morning.

## What to see and do trackside

Beyond the racing, MotorLand Aragón has a permanent driving experience center and a resort area with hotels and go-karting that stay open outside race weekends. During the Grand Prix itself, expect a fan village with merchandise stalls, food vendors, and often paddock-adjacent activities like rider autograph sessions — MotoGP typically publishes a detailed timetable for these closer to the event.

The main grandstands (like those overlooking the final corners and main straight) tend to offer the best value for atmosphere versus price; general admission areas on the hillside give a cheaper but still solid view of multiple corners since the terrain is naturally banked in places.

## When to go

The date is fixed for August 28–30, 2026, sitting in the heart of Aragón's hot late-summer stretch — expect daytime temperatures that can climb into the mid-30s Celsius, so plan for shade, sun protection, and hydration regardless of what advice you've heard about "just bringing water." Practice sessions usually run Friday, qualifying Saturday, and the main races Sunday afternoon, with MotoGP itself as the headline slot.

Always confirm the exact daily schedule on MotoGP's official site or the MotorLand Aragón site, since session times are usually only finalized a few weeks out.

## Where to stay and eat

Alcañiz's old town, built around its 12th-century castle-parador and the Plaza de España, is walkable and has a small but genuine selection of family-run hotels and guesthouses — book these well ahead of race weekend, as the town's capacity is limited relative to the crowd MotoGP draws. For food, look for regional Aragonese staples like ternasco (roast lamb) and migas, plus the jamón and olive oil the Bajo Aragón region is known for.

Zaragoza, being larger, offers more hotel inventory if Alcañiz fills up, at the cost of a longer daily commute to the circuit.

## How to visit like a local

Arrive at the circuit early on race day — ideally before the first scheduled session — since the single main access road from Alcañiz becomes congested in the hour before MotoGP's Sunday race, and locals who attend regularly plan around this by leaving well ahead of the advertised start. Cash and card are both widely accepted at circuit vendors, but smaller stalls and food trucks may prefer card only or have minimum spends, so carry some euros as backup.

Ticket holders typically enter through gates tied to their grandstand or general admission zone — check your ticket type before you go so you're not walking the perimeter looking for the right entrance. The most common visitor mistake is treating this as a single-day event: the Friday practice and Saturday qualifying sessions are usually far less crowded and give a much closer, more relaxed look at the riders and paddock area than race-day Sunday. If you want a quieter experience with easier parking, prioritize Friday.
