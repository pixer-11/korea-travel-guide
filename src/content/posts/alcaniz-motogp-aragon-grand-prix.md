## Why go

MotorLand Aragón is one of the more technical, fan-friendly tracks on the MotoGP calendar — a 5.3 km circuit with a long back straight, tight infield chicanes, and grandstands that give a genuine sightline advantage over some of the sport's bigger, more sprawling venues. The Aragon Grand Prix was set for August 28–30, 2026, a full three-day weekend of free practice, qualifying, and races across MotoGP, Moto2, and Moto3, plus the supporting series that typically fill out the schedule.

For visitors, it was also a chance to see a corner of Spain — the Bajo Aragón region of Teruel province — that most tourists skip entirely in favor of Madrid or Barcelona.

## Getting there

Alcañiz sits roughly 100 km southeast of Zaragoza and around 150 km northwest of Tarragona, which makes it a realistic day-trip base from either city. MotorLand Aragón itself is about 6 km from the town center, off the A-222. Zaragoza's airport is the nearest with regular domestic and some European connections; from there it's about a 1.5-hour drive.

No train station serves the circuit directly, so most fans arrive by car, rental, or organized coach transfer. During race weekend, shuttle buses typically run from Alcañiz and sometimes from Zaragoza, though exact routes and pickup points change year to year. Signposted parking areas ring the circuit and fill up fast on Sunday morning.

## What to see and do trackside

Beyond the racing, MotorLand Aragón has a permanent driving experience center and a resort area with hotels and go-karting that stay open outside race weekends. The Grand Prix programme included a fan village with merchandise stalls, food vendors, and paddock-adjacent activities such as rider autograph sessions.

The main grandstands, like those overlooking the final corners and main straight, tend to offer the best value for atmosphere versus price. General admission areas on the hillside give a cheaper but still solid view of multiple corners, since the terrain is naturally banked in places.

## When to go

The August 28–30 dates sat in the heart of Aragón's hot late-summer stretch — daytime temperatures in the region can climb into the mid-30s Celsius at that time of year. Practice sessions run Friday, qualifying Saturday, and the main races Sunday afternoon, with MotoGP in the headline slot.

## Where to stay and eat

Alcañiz's old town, built around its 12th-century castle-parador and the Plaza de España, is walkable and has a small but genuine selection of family-run hotels and guesthouses. For food, regional Aragonese staples like ternasco (roast lamb) and migas are the thing to look for, along with the jamón and olive oil the Bajo Aragón region is known for.

Zaragoza, being larger, has far more hotel inventory when rooms in Alcañiz run short, at the cost of a longer daily commute to the circuit.

## How to visit like a local

Regulars get to the circuit early on race day, before the first scheduled session. The single main access road from Alcañiz clogs up in the hour before MotoGP's Sunday race, and the people who come every year leave town well ahead of the advertised start. Cash and card are both widely accepted at circuit vendors, though smaller stalls and food trucks sometimes take card only or set minimum spends.

Ticket holders enter through gates tied to their grandstand or general admission zone. A common visitor mistake is treating a Grand Prix as a single-day event: Friday practice and Saturday qualifying are usually far less crowded and give a closer, more relaxed look at the riders and the paddock area than race-day Sunday.
