## Why go

MotorLand Aragón is one of the more technical, fan-friendly tracks on the MotoGP calendar — a 5.3 km circuit with a long back straight, tight infield chicanes, and grandstands that give a genuine sightline advantage over some of the sport's bigger, more sprawling venues. The Aragon Grand Prix, held August 28–30, 2026, was a full three-day weekend covering free practice, qualifying, and races across MotoGP, Moto2, and Moto3, plus supporting series that typically fill out the schedule.

For visitors, it was also a chance to see a corner of Spain — the Bajo Aragón region of Teruel province — that most tourists skip entirely in favor of Madrid or Barcelona.

## Getting there

Alcañiz sits roughly 100 km southeast of Zaragoza and around 150 km northwest of Tarragona, making it a realistic day-trip base from either city for those who didn't want to stay overnight. MotorLand Aragón itself is about 6 km from the town center, off the A-222. Zaragoza's airport is the nearest with regular domestic and some European connections; from there it's about a 1.5-hour drive.

There's no train station directly serving the circuit, so most fans arrived by car, rental, or organized coach transfer — during race weekend, shuttle buses typically run from Alcañiz and sometimes from Zaragoza, though exact routes and pickup points change year to year. Those driving could expect signposted parking areas around the circuit that fill up fast on Sunday morning.

## What to see and do trackside

Beyond the racing, MotorLand Aragón has a permanent driving experience center and a resort area with hotels and go-karting that stay open outside race weekends. During the Grand Prix itself, there was a fan village with merchandise stalls, food vendors, and often paddock-adjacent activities like rider autograph sessions.

The main grandstands (like those overlooking the final corners and main straight) tended to offer the best value for atmosphere versus price; general admission areas on the hillside gave a cheaper but still solid view of multiple corners since the terrain is naturally banked in places.

## When to go

The date was fixed for August 28–30, 2026, sitting in the heart of Aragón's hot late-summer stretch — daytime temperatures in the region can climb into the mid-30s Celsius at that time of year. Practice sessions usually ran Friday, qualifying Saturday, and the main races Sunday afternoon, with MotoGP itself as the headline slot.

## Where to stay and eat

Alcañiz's old town, built around its 12th-century castle-parador and the Plaza de España, is walkable and has a small but genuine selection of family-run hotels and guesthouses. For food, look for regional Aragonese staples like ternasco (roast lamb) and migas, plus the jamón and olive oil the Bajo Aragón region is known for.

Zaragoza, being larger, offered more hotel inventory for those unable to find rooms in Alcañiz, at the cost of a longer daily commute to the circuit.

## How to visit like a local

Arriving at the circuit early on race day — ideally before the first scheduled session — was advisable since the single main access road from Alcañiz became congested in the hour before MotoGP's Sunday race, and locals who attend regularly planned around this by leaving well ahead of the advertised start. Cash and card were both widely accepted at circuit vendors, though smaller stalls and food trucks sometimes preferred card only or had minimum spends.

Ticket holders typically entered through gates tied to their grandstand or general admission zone. A common visitor mistake was treating this as a single-day event: the Friday practice and Saturday qualifying sessions were usually far less crowded and gave a much closer, more relaxed look at the riders and paddock area than race-day Sunday.
