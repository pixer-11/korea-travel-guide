## What Cracco Roma actually is

Carlo Cracco built his reputation in Milan; Rome had never had a Cracco address until February 2026. The restaurant sits inside the Corinthia Rome, a hotel newly carved out of a 1914 Banca d'Italia building. That pedigree matters: you're eating inside a former central bank, not a purpose-built dining room.

The kitchen earned Cracco his first Michelin star this year, a fast validation for a restaurant barely out of its opening weeks. Italian and international media covered the launch as a genuine event, not routine restaurant news.

## The room and the details worth noticing

Banca d'Italia buildings were built to project permanence: expect high ceilings, heavy stonework, and the bones of an institutional interior repurposed for hospitality. That contrast between vault-like architecture and a modern tasting-menu restaurant is part of the draw.

Look closely at the table settings. The tableware is bespoke Ginori 1735, commissioned specifically for this restaurant, with patterns drawing on Roman mosaic motifs. It's a detail easy to miss if you're focused on the plate rather than what's underneath it.

## Booking and getting there

The restaurant is inside the Corinthia Rome hotel, close to the Via Veneto district, one of Rome's traditional luxury hotel and embassy quarters. Given the Michelin star and the opening buzz, tables are in demand.

Practical notes:

- Reserve through the Corinthia Rome directly rather than assuming walk-in availability.
- Confirm current opening days and service hours before booking; a brand-new Michelin-starred kitchen can adjust its schedule in its first year.
- Ask about tasting-menu versus à la carte options when you call, since formats at newly-starred restaurants often shift early on.
- Dress the part: this sits inside a five-star hotel in a formal former bank building, not a casual trattoria.

## Making the most of a visit here

Treat this as an occasion meal, not a quick stop between sights. Tasting menus at this level typically run long, so block out an unhurried evening rather than squeezing it before a show or late train.

Ask your server about the Ginori tableware and the building's Banca d'Italia history if nobody offers it upfront; staff at newly-opened flagship restaurants are usually well briefed on both. It's a natural way into the story of the room.

Given the newness of the star and the press attention, expect the restaurant to keep evolving its menu and pacing through its first year. Call or email ahead of any visit to confirm hours, dress code, and whether a fixed tasting menu is mandatory, since none of this had fully settled at launch.
