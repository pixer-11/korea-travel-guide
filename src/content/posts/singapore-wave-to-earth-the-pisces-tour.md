## One night in Kallang

The date is November 20, 2026. South Korean indie-pop trio wave to earth bring *the pisces tour* to the Singapore Indoor Stadium. It's one of the biggest rooms in the city, and a long way from the small club shows that first built their following.

The band's sound is soft and unhurried: clean electric guitar, a relaxed rhythm section and a hushed vocal with jazz and bossa nova in its bones. Songs like "seasons" and "bad" spread through streaming playlists and short-form video, which is how much of their young Southeast Asian audience found them.

## The room itself

The Singapore Indoor Stadium opened in 1989. Japanese architect Kenzo Tange designed it, and the giant pitched roof makes it easy to spot from the Kallang River. Inside, tiered seating wraps around a flat arena floor, and the room holds roughly 12,000 people at full concert capacity.

A few things about the room shape the night:

- **Seated tiers** curve around three sides, so side-block seats look across at the stage rather than straight on.
- **The floor**, when sold as standing or seated, puts you closest to the band. The upper tiers give you the full lighting rig.
- **The air-conditioning** runs cold. Regulars bring a light layer even though it's warm outside.

## Getting to the Sports Hub

The arena sits inside the Singapore Sports Hub, next to the National Stadium. Public transport is the easy way in.

- **Stadium MRT (CC6, Circle Line):** the nearest station. Covered walkways lead from the exit to the Sports Hub, which matters in November.
- **Mountbatten MRT (CC7):** one stop east, a slightly longer walk.
- **Kallang MRT (EW10, East-West Line):** around 15 minutes on foot over the Kallang River. It's useful if you're coming from the CBD or Changi side.

MRT fare gates take contactless Visa and Mastercard cards and phone wallets, so visitors rarely need a separate transport card.

## How a Singapore Indoor Stadium night runs

Singapore concert crowds are orderly, and the venue runs on that. Fans typically arrive an hour or more before doors. Merchandise lines form well before the hall opens, and tour shirts and tote bags tend to be the first things to sell out.

Bag checks are standard at Sports Hub events. The venue's conditions of entry usually exclude outside food and drink, along with professional cameras with detachable lenses. Phones are fine. Most attendees travel light to get through security faster.

Inside, people at wave to earth shows mostly sway and sing along rather than push forward. That suits songs built on quiet dynamics. The phone-light moment during the slower tracks has become something of a ritual on their tours.

Afterwards, Stadium MRT fills fast. Many concertgoers walk ten minutes to Mountbatten or Kallang station, where the queues move quicker. Others wait out the rush over food nearby. Ride-hailing apps such as Grab surge after big shows, so the wait for a car can be long.

## Eating before and after

Kallang Wave Mall is built into the Sports Hub, steps from the arena. Its food court and casual restaurants are where most people eat before the show.

For something more local, Old Airport Road Food Centre is a short taxi ride east. It's one of the city's best-known hawker centres, good for char kway teow, satay and lor mee. Hawker stalls mostly take cash or local QR payments, and diners "chope" (reserve) a table with a tissue packet before queuing.

## Weather and the walk

November brings the start of the northeast monsoon. Heavy late-afternoon and evening thunderstorms are common, and the air stays humid long after the rain stops.

The covered links between Stadium MRT, Kallang Wave Mall and the arena keep you mostly dry. The open riverside walk toward Kallang MRT does not. A compact umbrella is the one thing seasoned Singapore concertgoers carry without fail.
