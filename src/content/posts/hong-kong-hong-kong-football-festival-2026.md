## Why this event matters

Pre-season friendlies in Asia aren't new; the venue set this one apart. The Hong Kong Football Festival 2026 (July 31–August 5) was billed as one of the first major sporting spectacles for the new Kai Tak Stadium, built on the site of the old Kai Tak Airport in Kowloon East. The announced lineup put four of European football's biggest names — Manchester City, Inter Milan, Chelsea FC and Juventus — into two exhibition matches across the week-long window.

For Hong Kong, the festival was as much about showcasing its new 50,000-seat national stadium to the world as about the football itself: a first look at a genuinely new piece of civic infrastructure rather than just another summer friendly.

## Getting there and getting around

Kai Tak Stadium sits within the larger Kai Tak Sports Park development in Kowloon East, on the former runway site jutting into Kowloon Bay. The area is served by Kai Tak MTR station on the Tuen Ma line, which was purpose-built with the sports park in mind. Signage above ground points toward the sports park rather than the residential side of the development.

Coming from Hong Kong Island or Tsim Sha Tsui, match-day crowds funnelling through a single new station can back up quickly, and additional shuttle bus services are typically laid on for events of this scale. Taxis and ride-hailing face the same road congestion around Kowloon Bay and Prince Edward Road East, which makes the MTR the more predictable option on match days.

## What to expect at the festival

With two matches across a five-day window, the festival format paired marquee games with surrounding activities — fan zones, autograph or meet-and-greet sessions, and retail pop-ups are typical for events that bring top European clubs to Asian tour stops. Given the mix of Manchester City, Chelsea, Inter Milan and Juventus, the two fixtures were cross-club pairings rather than a round-robin.

New stadiums often have first-year teething issues with entry gates and bag checks, and Kai Tak's scale means longer walks from concourse to seat than older Hong Kong venues like Mong Kok Stadium.

## When to go and what to pack

Late July and early August are peak subtropical summer in Hong Kong: high heat, humidity, and a real chance of afternoon thunderstorms or a tropical cyclone warning, which can affect outdoor events. Kai Tak Stadium has a retractable roof and cooling systems designed partly to address this, but transport to and from the venue is outdoors, so light, breathable clothing, a collapsible umbrella, and a refillable water bottle are standard kit for the season.

This is also a period when Hong Kong sees strong inbound tourism for summer sales and festivals, and hotel rates in Kowloon and on Hong Kong Island tend to rise.

## Nearby and practical tips

Kai Tak Sports Park was designed as a destination in itself, with dining and retail areas intended to stay open beyond match times, and it sits a short MTR ride from established Kowloon neighborhoods like Kowloon Bay, To Kwa Wan, and the shopping and food streets of Mong Kok and Yau Ma Tei further west. The Kai Tak Sports Park website covers events at the venue, including ticketing and set times.
