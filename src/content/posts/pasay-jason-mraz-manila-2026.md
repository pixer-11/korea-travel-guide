## A Tuesday night on Manila Bay

Jason Mraz was set to play SM Mall of Asia Arena on October 27, 2026. That date was a Tuesday, a working weeknight in Metro Manila. The arena stands at the north end of the SM Mall of Asia complex in Pasay, on reclaimed land facing Manila Bay.

He has two Grammy wins, both from 2010: "Make It Mine" and "Lucky", his duet with Colbie Caillat. "I'm Yours" and "I Won't Give Up" are his best-known songs. Filipino audiences have followed him for years, and Manila has been a regular stop for him.

## Inside the MOA Arena

The arena opened in 2012 and holds up to 20,000 people at full capacity. It is a bowl, so even the high seats look straight down at the stage. Seating for concerts here usually comes in tiers:

- **Patron:** floor and closest lower-level seats
- **Lower Box:** the first ring of tiered seating
- **Upper Box:** the higher ring
- **General Admission:** unreserved seats at the very top

Ticket sales and seat maps for MOA Arena shows are normally handled by SM Tickets, which has outlets inside SM malls. Security screens bags at the entrances. Outside food and drink are normally not allowed in.

## Getting to Pasay and back out

The complex is not on a rail line. Most people taking the train ride the MRT-3 to Taft Avenue or the LRT-1 to EDSA, which are linked stations. From there, buses and jeepneys signed "MOA" cover the short trip west along EDSA.

- **Grab:** the most common choice, though surge pricing after a show is normal
- **Driving:** the complex has several multi-level car parks, and they empty slowly once the encore ends
- **From the airport:** NAIA is a short drive south, so some fans fly in on the day of a show

Weekday evening traffic on EDSA and Roxas Boulevard is heavy, and the concert's Tuesday date put it right after the evening rush. October is still rainy season in Luzon, and late-season typhoons sometimes disrupt travel.

## How MOA concert nights usually go

Fans typically come hours before doors and spend the time in the mall. The food courts and restaurants fill with people in concert shirts. Many walk out to the seaside promenade behind the mall to watch the sun go down over Manila Bay, next to the MOA Eye Ferris wheel.

- **Paying:** cards and GCash work almost everywhere in the mall, and cash covers the jeepneys
- **Merch:** stands usually go up near the entrances, and the queues are shortest before doors
- **After the show:** regulars rarely join the first crush for rides; they eat in the mall or walk the bay until traffic eases
- **Staying nearby:** Conrad Manila sits inside the complex, and the Entertainment City resorts (City of Dreams, Okada Manila) are a few minutes south

Locals call the place simply "MOA" (pronounced "moh-ah"). Drivers and jeepney barkers use that name.
