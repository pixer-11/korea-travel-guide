Vicky Lau built her reputation on a simple idea executed with total discipline: take a Chinese ingredient or memory and plate it with the precision of French fine dining. TATE, her flagship, now has a permanent address in Sheung Wan after operating out of earlier spaces, and it carries two Michelin stars into the new room.

## What makes TATE different

This isn't a menu that splits the difference between two cuisines. Dishes tend to start from a specific Chinese flavor memory, a preserved vegetable, a broth, a spice used in home cooking, then get rebuilt using French plating and sauce technique.

Lau has talked about her menus as chapters, almost like a book you move through course by course. That framing matters here: come expecting a tasting menu experience rather than an à la carte meal you can rush.

## The Sheung Wan setting

Sheung Wan sits just west of Central, one of the older parts of Hong Kong Island, known for dried seafood shops, antique dealers and a slower pace than the glass towers nearby. TATE's move into a permanent home in this district puts it a short walk from that older, mercantile Hong Kong rather than the finance-district glitz of Central proper.

That contrast is worth noticing on the walk in. You pass shopfronts selling dried scallops and abalone, then arrive at a dining room built for a very different, very controlled kind of eating.

## The wine cellar

One detail worth planning around: TATE holds a cellar of around 1,200 French wine labels. If pairing is something you care about, this is a restaurant where the sommelier's list deserves as much attention as the kitchen's chapters.

Ask, when booking or on arrival, whether a pairing flight is offered alongside the tasting menu. For a wine-focused visit, it's worth requesting a table with enough time that you're not rushing between courses and pours.

## Booking a table

Two-Michelin-star dining in Hong Kong runs on reservations, and a chef of Lau's profile in a newly permanent space is likely to book out fast.

- Reserve online or by phone well before your travel dates, ideally as soon as your Hong Kong trip is confirmed
- Have a backup date in mind since single-seating tasting menus limit nightly covers
- Confirm the current address, opening days and service times directly with TATE before you go, since fine-dining restaurants in Hong Kong frequently adjust hours around holidays and private events
- Ask about dietary accommodations when booking; tasting menus are harder to modify last-minute than an à la carte meal

## Dressing and pacing the evening

TATE sits in the upper tier of Hong Kong fine dining, so smart-casual at minimum is the safe assumption, though you should verify any specific dress code when you reserve. Tasting menus at this level typically run long, often two to three hours once wine pairings are added, so avoid booking anything tight afterward.

Treat the meal as the evening's main event rather than a stop before or after something else. If you're pairing wines from that 1,200-bottle list, budget extra time and pace yourself between courses rather than rushing to keep to a schedule.

## Getting there and making a night of it

Sheung Wan is served by its own MTR station on the Island Line, making it an easy taxi or short walk from most Central and Sai Ying Pun hotels. Arrive a few minutes early rather than late, since fine-dining rooms at this level rarely hold a table for long once the reservation window passes.

Afterward, the surrounding streets give you an easy way to extend the night without another heavy meal: dried seafood shops give way after dark to small bars and cafes threaded through Sheung Wan and neighboring Sai Ying Pun. A short walk lets the meal settle before you head back to Central or across to the harbour.
