## A stadium night on the north coast

ENHYPEN plays Jakarta International Stadium on January 23, 2027. JIS stands in Papanggo, Tanjung Priok, a few kilometres inland from the port and close to the Ancol waterfront. This is a football stadium, so the show fills a big bowl of stands, with a stage at one end and a floor section on the pitch.

Picture the stands going dark and then refilling with points of light. Most of the crowd carries ENHYPEN's official lightstick, and the organisers usually sync the sticks to the show, so whole blocks of seats change colour together. ENGENE, the group's fandom, are known for loud fanchants, and a full stadium chanting them is the reason many people buy a ticket.

## Getting to Jakarta International Stadium

North Jakarta traffic is the main thing to work around. The streets feeding JIS from the toll roads and from Jalan R.E. Martadinata back up badly on big event nights. Most attendees allow far more travel time than the distance would suggest.

The usual ways in:

- **Ride-hailing (Gojek, Grab):** the most common choice. Motorbike taxis (GoRide, GrabBike) get through jams that leave cars stuck.
- **Transjakarta:** the city bus network has routes serving the stadium area. They are cheap, but the buses fill up after the show.
- **KRL Commuter Line:** the Tanjung Priok line passes through the area. Fans who use it usually finish the trip by ride-hailing or on foot.
- **Private car:** parking near JIS is limited, so most fans avoid driving.

The ride home is the harder part. Pickup points get mobbed at the end of the night. Many fans walk a few streets away from the gates before they open the app.

## Tickets and the wristband system

The organiser publishes ticket categories, the seat map and the ticket terms on its official channels and through its ticketing partner. This guide carries no prices, and none were supplied, so the seat map and terms are the place to read them.

Indonesian promoters commonly make fans swap an e-ticket for a physical wristband before the show. That usually means showing photo ID that matches the name on the booking. Resold tickets and name mismatches can therefore get you turned away at the gate, a costly mistake that hits visitors from abroad more than anyone.

## How fan nights at JIS work

Indonesian K-pop crowds are organised and generous. Small things matter here.

- **Freebies:** fans hand out photocards, stickers, banners and snacks near the venue. Taking one politely and saying *terima kasih* (thank you) is normal. Many people bring a few freebies of their own to swap.
- **Fan projects:** fan groups often hand out slogans or paper banners, with a set song cue for holding them up. Joining in is expected.
- **Payment:** QRIS codes and e-wallets such as GoPay, OVO and DANA are everywhere, food vendors included. A little cash still helps with street sellers outside the gates.
- **Bag rules:** stadium security searches bags. Food, drinks and professional cameras are usually restricted, and the organiser's entry rules list the details.
- **Merch:** official merchandise lines tend to form well before doors. Fans often buy in pairs, one person queuing while the other holds a place elsewhere.

Fans typically arrive hours before doors open. They queue for merch, trade freebies and meet friends from group chats.

## January weather in Jakarta

January is the peak of Jakarta's rainy season. Afternoon and evening downpours are common, and heavy rain can flood low-lying North Jakarta streets, which slows traffic even more.

Fans usually carry a compact poncho rather than an umbrella, since umbrellas are awkward in a packed crowd and often not allowed inside. The heat and humidity stay high even after rain. Water, a small towel and a power bank for ride-hailing apps are standard in most concert bags.

## Staying and eating nearby

Visitors from outside Jakarta tend to split between two choices. Some stay in Ancol or Kelapa Gading, close to the stadium. Others stay in central areas such as Thamrin and Sudirman, which have more hotels and better transit, but a longer ride home.

Kelapa Gading is a big mall-and-restaurant district in North Jakarta, a practical spot for a meal before heading to JIS. Ancol, with Taman Impian Jaya Ancol and its seafront restaurants, works well for turning a concert trip into a short stay. For a daytime outing, Kota Tua, the old Dutch quarter around Fatahillah Square, sits a short ride west.
