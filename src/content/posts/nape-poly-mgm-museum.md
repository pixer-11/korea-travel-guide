## Inside MGM Macau

The museum's address is MGM Macau, level L2, on Avenida Dr. Sun Yat Sen in NAPE, the reclaimed waterfront district of the Macau peninsula. Put MGM Macau into the taxi app. The name tells you who runs it: China Poly Group, the state-owned company behind Beijing's Poly Art Museum, together with MGM. You get museum-grade Chinese art exhibitions inside a casino resort.

## Inside the Gallery

You get there through the resort, then up to L2. Past the gaming floor and the shopping arcades, the noise drops to a gallery hush. The museum shows **rotating exhibitions**, not a fixed permanent collection, so what you'll see depends on when you go.

- **Check the current show** on MGM Macau's official site before you go.
- **Allow 45 to 90 minutes.** It's a focused space, not a day-long institution.
- **Read the wall text.** Exhibition panels are usually in Chinese and English.

It doesn't get many visitors compared with the big casino attractions around it, so you can usually stand in front of a piece as long as you like.

## Getting to NAPE

The museum keeps resort hours, which is handy for fitting it into a peninsula day:

- **Monday to Friday:** 11am–7pm
- **Saturday and Sunday:** 10am–7pm

From Senado Square it's roughly a 15 to 20 minute walk south-east past the Grand Lisboa, whose golden lotus tower is easy to steer by. Taxis drop off at the MGM Macau main entrance. Coming in by ferry? The Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal is a short taxi ride away. Resort shuttle buses often run from the terminals too, so ask at the shuttle stands about the current routes.

Wynn Macau is next door, and the Macau Tower and the waterfront promenade toward Nam Van Lake are walkable from here. That makes a natural afternoon loop.

## Making the Most of a Casino-Floor Museum

The resort setting comes with a few practical habits:

- **Follow the signs for L2** and ask the staff if you lose your way. In any of the large Macau resorts, getting lost between the gaming floor and the shops is normal.
- **Ask about tickets at the museum desk or on the official site.** Whether you pay depends on the exhibition, so check the posted details. Don't assume either way.
- **Ask before you take photos.** Loaned pieces in rotating shows sometimes come with photo restrictions, and each gallery posts its own rules.
- **Pay as Macau does:** patacas and Hong Kong dollars are both widely accepted around the resort, and so are major cards.
- **Try a weekend morning.** Saturday and Sunday opening at 10am gives you the galleries before the resort's afternoon crowd builds up.

Combine the museum with the rest of the peninsula: it sits between the historic centre and the waterfront.
