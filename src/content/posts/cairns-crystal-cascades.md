## Granite, green water and a paved path

Redlynch Intake Road runs out at a car park, and the forest takes over from there. A paved, mostly flat path follows Freshwater Creek upstream for a little over a kilometre. Along the way, grey granite boulders split the water into a series of small falls and deep pools.

The water is clear enough to see the stones on the bottom. Rainforest closes over the creek, so most pools sit in partial shade. The path ends at the largest pool, where a wider fall pours in.

Allow two to three hours. That covers the walk in, a swim in one or two pools and a picnic back near the car park.

## Getting to Redlynch

Crystal Cascades is in the Redlynch Valley, roughly 14 km west of the Cairns city centre. The drive takes around 20 minutes:

- Head out through Redlynch.
- Follow Redlynch Intake Road to the end.
- Park at the day-use area, where there are toilets and picnic tables.

No public transport runs to the car park itself, so drive, cycle or take a taxi. Serious walkers can keep going on the historic Douglas Track. It climbs steeply from near the cascades towards Lake Morris and Copperlode Dam, so bring plenty of water.

## Swimming without getting hurt

Crystal Cascades has a long injury record, and the signs at the entrance explain why. The granite is polished smooth and turns slick when wet. Hidden rocks sit under water that looks deep.

- Stay out of the roped-off and signposted sections.
- Don't jump or dive from the boulders. This causes most of the serious injuries.
- Stay out of the water after heavy rain. Freshwater Creek rises quickly and flash floods happen in the wet season, roughly November to April.
- Wear reef shoes or old sneakers for climbing in and out.

The dry months, around May to October, bring lower, clearer water and cooler air. The wet season brings fuller falls but more closures and stronger currents.

## Beating the weekend crowd

It's a well-known local swimming spot, and weekends show it. Try not to arrive between 9am and 6pm on a Saturday or Sunday. The car park fills and the pools near the start of the path get crowded.

The only quiet weekend window is 8pm to 10pm. That's after dark in a rainforest gorge, so it suits a short torchlit walk, not a swim. For swimming, a weekday is the better choice, or arrive early before the weekend rush.

How Cairns uses the place:

- Many locals walk to the top pool first, then work back down, so the busier lower pools come last.
- Food and eskies go on the picnic tables near the car park, not on the rocks.
- Everything you bring comes back out with you. There are no bins along the path.
- There's no entry gate and no fee is collected on the path. Check the Cairns Regional Council website for current closures before you go, especially after rain.

For many visitors this is the answer to stinger season. From about November to May, box jellyfish make the coast risky without a stinger suit, and a freshwater creek 20 minutes from town avoids the problem.
