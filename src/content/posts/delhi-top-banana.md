## One floor above Ikk Panjab

Look for Ikk Panjab first, because Top Banana sits directly above it. The restaurant below is where you get your bearings. The bar upstairs is the reason you came.

The room is kept dim and the music comes off records. It holds 38 seats, which is about the size of a large dinner party. That number tells you most of what you need to know. Nobody comes here to vanish into a crowd; you come for a seat, a drink and a conversation you can actually hear.

This is a Bright Hospitality project, the restaurant group run by Rajan Sethi. Top Banana opened recently and is still picking up attention, so expect people who have read about it and want a look.

## What 38 seats does to a night out

A small room changes how the evening works. There isn't much standing space to fall back on when tables fill. If you turn up with six friends on a weekend and no booking, you're gambling.

It suits some nights better than others:

- **Two to four people:** the easiest fit, and the best way to get the bartenders' attention.
- **A first date or a catch-up:** low light and records keep the volume conversational.
- **A big birthday group:** ask the bar ahead of time whether they can seat you at all.

Because seats are limited, people tend to stay a while. Plan on a couple of rounds, not a quick drink between other stops.

## Clear, sour, stacked: reading the drinks list

The drinks here are built around three techniques. Knowing what each one means makes ordering easier. We weren't given the current menu, so treat this as a decoder, not a list of what's on tonight.

**Fermentation** is where sugar is broken down over days or weeks, usually turning fruit, grain or tea sour, funky or lightly fizzy. In a cocktail it can stand in for citrus, adding acidity with more depth than a squeeze of lime.

**Clarification** strips the cloudiness out of an ingredient. Bartenders often do it by filtering, sometimes through milk, which catches the solids. The result looks like water or pale tea but tastes of everything that went in. Clarified drinks tend to feel smoother and rounder in the mouth.

**Layered builds** are about structure. Flavours are stacked in stages, either in the glass or in how the drink is put together. The point is a drink that changes from first sip to last.

If a description confuses you, ask. In a room this small the bartenders are close by, and explaining their prep work is part of the job.

## The vinyl side of things

"Vinyl-led" means the records set the mood, not a streamed playlist. Records come in sides, so the music tends to arrive in album-length runs rather than shuffled singles. That gives the evening a steadier, more unhurried pace.

It also means the volume is set for a bar, not a club. If you want to dance, GK II's bigger venues are a better bet for the end of your night. Come here for the opening chapter, or for a whole evening of talking.

## Getting to GK II

Greater Kailash II is a residential and market neighbourhood in South Delhi. It's known for its restaurant and bar strips. Most visitors arrive by app cab or auto-rickshaw. At the end of the night that's also the simplest way home.

By metro, the nearest options are:

- **Greater Kailash station** on the Magenta Line.
- **Kailash Colony station** on the Violet Line, a little further out.

From either one you'll want a short auto or cab ride for the last stretch. Enter "Ikk Panjab, GK II" in your app, since the restaurant downstairs is the easier landmark to pin. On weekend nights GK II's market lanes clog with parked cars. Being dropped at the door saves you a slow walk past the traffic.

## Booking, paying and the age rule

Book before you go, and check the hours when you do. Top Banana is new, so opening times and booking policy can still shift. A 38-seat room fills up faster than the big GK II bars. Try the bar's own social channels or the usual Delhi reservation apps.

Bring photo ID. Delhi's legal drinking age is 25, higher than most of India and most other countries. Bars here are entitled to check. Visitors in their early twenties should know this before planning a night around cocktails.

On paying and tipping:

- **Cards and UPI** are normal at Delhi bars, so you don't need a wad of cash.
- **Check the bill for a service charge.** Under Indian consumer guidelines it can't be compulsory, so you're free to ask for it to be removed.
- **Tipping** on top is a gesture, not an obligation. If the bartender talked you through the menu, a little extra is appreciated.

Keep an eye on Delhi's **dry days**. Licensed bars stop serving alcohol on national holidays such as Republic Day (26 January), Independence Day (15 August) and Gandhi Jayanti (2 October). Election days and some festivals can be added to the list too. Check the calendar before you pick a date.

## Making a night of it in GK II

Dinner downstairs is the obvious way to pair it. Ikk Panjab serves Punjabi food, which means heavy, rich dishes. A clarified or fermented drink upstairs afterwards is a good contrast. Some people prefer the reverse: a drink first, then dinner.

GK II and neighbouring Greater Kailash I have enough restaurants and bars to fill an evening without crossing the city. Hauz Khas is a short ride west if you want somewhere louder later on. Top Banana is the quiet, considered stop in that mix. Go early in the night, sit at the bar if you can, and let the record finish before you order the next round.
