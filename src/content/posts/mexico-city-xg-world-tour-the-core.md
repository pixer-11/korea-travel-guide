## A Sunday night in Azcapotzalco

The date is Sunday, November 22, 2026. That night, Japanese girl group XG closes the North American leg of XG World Tour: The Core at Arena CDMX. The building is a huge indoor arena on the industrial northern edge of Mexico City, a long way from the tourist postcard.

XG is a seven-member act: Jurin, Chisa, Hinata, Juria, Maya, Harvey and Cocona. They perform mostly in English, mixing hip-hop and R&B with tight group choreography. Their fans call themselves ALPHAZ. A tour's last North American show is the one fans tend to travel for.

## Arena CDMX is not Arena México

Visitors mix these two up all the time. Arena México is the old lucha libre hall in the Doctores neighbourhood, south of the centre. Arena CDMX is a different building, on Avenida de las Granjas in Azcapotzalco, roughly 10 kilometres north.

Taxi and rideshare drivers usually know both. Typing "Arena México" into an app sends you to the wrong side of the city on a Sunday night, though. Type "Arena Ciudad de México" or "Arena CDMX" in full.

The arena opened in 2012 and holds around 22,000 people. That makes it one of the largest indoor venues in Mexico. Seating rises steeply on three sides of the floor, so even the upper tiers look straight down at the stage. Concessions and merchandise stands ring the concourse.

## Getting there on Line 6

The Metro is the most direct route. Line 6, the red line, stops at Ferrería/Arena Ciudad de México. The station was renamed for the venue, and the arena is a short walk from the exit.

Line 6 is a short east-west line. It connects with other routes at these interchanges:

- **El Rosario:** Line 7
- **Instituto del Petróleo:** Line 5
- **Deportivo 18 de Marzo:** Line 3
- **Martín Carrera:** Line 4

Visitors coming from Roma, Condesa or the Centro Histórico usually ride Line 3 north to Deportivo 18 de Marzo and switch to Line 6 there. The Metro uses a rechargeable transit card, the Tarjeta de Movilidad Integrada. Most visitors load it at a station machine on their first ride.

Rideshare apps such as Uber and DiDi are widely used across Mexico City. After a full arena empties onto Avenida de las Granjas, pickup traffic around the building backs up. Many concertgoers walk a few blocks away from the venue before calling a car, or take the Metro out first.

## How the line outside Arena CDMX works

Fan culture for Asian pop acts in Mexico City is large and organised. At sold-out shows of this kind, merchandise lines often form hours before doors. That is because popular sizes and lightsticks can sell out at the stands.

Fans with standing-floor tickets typically gather well before doors for a spot near the barrier. Seated ticket holders tend to arrive closer to showtime, since their places are assigned.

A few habits help on the day:

- **Fan projects:** ALPHAZ groups often hand out banners or slogan cards in the line. Those cards are usually meant to be raised at an agreed moment in the show.
- **Freebies:** fans exchanging small handmade gifts is normal at these shows, and a "gracias" goes a long way.
- **Paying:** cards work at most official arena stands. Street vendors outside, selling snacks and unofficial merch, usually want pesos in cash.
- **Spanish basics:** staff say "boleto" for ticket, "fila" for the line and "entrada" for the entrance.

Arena security inspects bags at the doors. Large bags and professional camera gear are commonly refused at big Mexico City venues. Fans who travel light usually get through faster.

## Where visitors stay and eat

Azcapotzalco is mostly residential and industrial. Few visitors sleep near the arena. Most stay in Roma Norte, Condesa, Juárez or the Centro Histórico and travel north for the show.

Polanco and the Centro Histórico are the better-placed of those areas for the trip home. Both sit closer to Line 6 connections than the southern neighbourhoods do.

Food around the arena runs to taco stands and street carts, which crowd the pavements before most large events. Inside the building, concession stands sell the usual arena snacks and drinks. For a proper sit-down meal, visitors usually eat in their own neighbourhood before heading north. Taquerías serving tacos al pastor, spit-roasted pork shaved onto small corn tortillas, stay open late across Roma and the Centro.

## Altitude and a November night

Mexico City sits about 2,240 metres above sea level. Visitors flying in from the coast sometimes notice it on the stairs between arena tiers, or after an hour of jumping on the floor. Water helps, and so does going easy on alcohol the first night.

Late November falls in the dry season. Days are usually clear and mild, and nights are noticeably cool. Fans who queue outside for hours tend to bring a layer for the wait, then tie it round the waist once the arena fills.

The day after, visitors with a spare morning often head to Chapultepec Park and the Museo Nacional de Antropología. It is a slower way to end a trip that started with a Sunday night in Azcapotzalco.
