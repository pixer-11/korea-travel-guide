## A former One Direction voice under the Big Dome

Louis Tomlinson plays Manila on October 17, 2026. The venue is Smart Araneta Coliseum in Quezon City, a round, domed arena that has been Metro Manila's default stage for big indoor nights since it opened in 1960.

He sang in One Direction and has since built a solo catalogue. His albums *Walls* and *Faith in the Future* gave his live shows a guitar-heavy, Britpop-leaning sound. Expect a crowd that knows every word, including the 1D-era fans who have followed him since the beginning.

Buy only from the seller the announcement names. Resale screenshots in fan group chats are a common way to lose money on big Manila shows. A ticket from an unofficial reseller may not scan at the gate.

## Getting to Cubao without the EDSA crawl

The Coliseum sits at the center of Araneta City, the mall-and-transit district where EDSA meets Aurora Boulevard. Two rail lines stop right beside it:

- **MRT-3:** Araneta Center–Cubao station, on EDSA
- **LRT-2:** Araneta Center–Cubao station, on Aurora Boulevard

From either station, the walk passes through or alongside Gateway Mall, which connects toward the Coliseum. On concert evenings, the rail lines usually beat a car by a wide margin. The roads around Cubao jam up when an arena crowd arrives at the same time as the evening commute.

Grab and taxis are fine for getting there. Leaving is harder, because thousands of people try to book a ride at once. If you are staying in Makati or BGC, take the MRT-3 south along EDSA. If you are staying near Katipunan or Marikina, take the LRT-2 east.

## Inside the Coliseum: picking your section

The arena is a bowl, so most seats look down toward the stage at a fairly steep angle. Seating usually runs in tiers from the floor and Patron levels upward through Lower Box and Upper Box to General Admission. General Admission is the high ring under the roof.

Check the seat plan before you choose. Floor setups change from show to show:

- **Floor or Patron:** closest to Louis, usually the first to sell out
- **Lower Box:** a raised, side-on view that many regulars rate as the best value for sightlines
- **Upper Box and General Admission:** far from the stage, but loud and full of energy, with the whole bowl in view

Check the official seat map for this show rather than assuming last year's layout.

## The Thrilla in Manila dome

This building has a famous back story. In 1975, Muhammad Ali fought Joe Frazier here in the bout known as the Thrilla in Manila, one of the most famous fights in boxing. The Coliseum was renamed for that fight for a time. Ali Mall, the shopping centre just next door, is still named after him.

Since then the dome has hosted Philippine basketball finals, beauty pageants and decades of international tours. Arriving early gives you time to look at that history. Walk past Ali Mall and look up at the dome's curve from the plaza, then go inside.

## Lining up and paying at Araneta City

Manila concert crowds arrive early and queue patiently. Expect fans to be gathered outside the entrances well before the gates open. Merchandise and fan-made freebies usually change hands in the plaza at this point. For seated tickets, the gate time on your ticket matters more than how early you arrive.

Practical habits that make the night smoother:

- **Pack light.** Bags are checked at the entrance. Large bags, professional cameras and outside food are commonly refused, so read the venue's list of allowed items on the official announcement.
- **Have your ticket ready.** Open the QR code or have the printed ticket in hand before you reach the front of the line. Mobile data gets patchy when thousands of phones are in one plaza.
- **Carry two ways to pay.** GCash and Maya are everywhere in Araneta City, and so are cards. Small vendors outside sometimes want cash.
- **Plan for rain.** October falls in the wet season, and showers are common. A compact umbrella or poncho makes the outdoor queue much easier.

When the show ends, don't rush the exits. Many locals wait in the mall or eat a late snack while the first wave fills the rail stations. They head home once the platforms clear.

## Eating before and after in Cubao

You won't go hungry here. Gateway Mall and Gateway Mall 2 have food courts and plenty of Filipino and international chains within a few minutes' walk of the Coliseum. Farmers Plaza and Ali Mall add cheaper, older-school options. Long-running Filipino fast food sits beside sit-down restaurants.

For something with more character, walk to Cubao Expo, on the edge of Araneta City. It is a low-rise courtyard that used to be a shoe market and is now lined with small bars, vintage shops and independent restaurants. It suits a post-concert drink while the stations empty out.

If you need a bed close by, Novotel Manila Araneta City is inside the complex, so you can walk back to your hotel. That beats queuing for a ride at midnight.
