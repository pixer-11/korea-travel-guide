## A former One Direction voice under the Big Dome

Louis Tomlinson was set to play Manila on October 17, 2026. The venue is Smart Araneta Coliseum in Quezon City, a round, domed arena that has been Metro Manila's default stage for big indoor nights since it opened in 1960.

He sang in One Direction and has since built a solo catalogue. His albums *Walls* and *Faith in the Future* gave his live shows a guitar-heavy, Britpop-leaning sound. His Manila following includes the 1D-era fans who have followed him since the beginning.

## Getting to Cubao without the EDSA crawl

The Coliseum sits at the center of Araneta City, the mall-and-transit district where EDSA meets Aurora Boulevard. Two rail lines stop right beside it:

- **MRT-3:** Araneta Center–Cubao station, on EDSA
- **LRT-2:** Araneta Center–Cubao station, on Aurora Boulevard

From either station, the walk passes through or alongside Gateway Mall, which connects toward the Coliseum. On concert evenings, the rail lines usually beat a car by a wide margin. The roads around Cubao jam up when an arena crowd arrives at the same time as the evening commute.

Grab and taxis work well for getting there. Leaving is harder, because thousands of people try to book a ride at once. For Makati or BGC, the MRT-3 runs south along EDSA. For Katipunan or Marikina, the LRT-2 runs east.

## Inside the Coliseum: picking your section

The arena is a bowl, so most seats look down toward the stage at a fairly steep angle. Seating usually runs in tiers from the floor and Patron levels upward through Lower Box and Upper Box to General Admission. General Admission is the high ring under the roof.

Floor setups change from show to show:

- **Floor or Patron:** closest to the stage, usually the first to sell out
- **Lower Box:** a raised, side-on view that many regulars rate as the best value for sightlines
- **Upper Box and General Admission:** far from the stage, but loud and full of energy, with the whole bowl in view

No two tours use quite the same layout.

## The Thrilla in Manila dome

This building has a famous back story. In 1975, Muhammad Ali fought Joe Frazier here in the bout known as the Thrilla in Manila, one of the most famous fights in boxing. The Coliseum was renamed for that fight for a time. Ali Mall, the shopping centre just next door, is still named after him.

Since then the dome has hosted Philippine basketball finals, beauty pageants and decades of international tours. The plaza beside Ali Mall gives the best view of the dome's curve.

## Lining up and paying at Araneta City

Manila concert crowds typically arrive early and queue patiently, gathering outside the entrances well before the gates open. Merchandise and fan-made freebies usually change hands in the plaza at this point. For seated tickets, the gate time on the ticket matters more than how early people arrive.

How entry and payment usually work here:

- **Bags.** Bags are checked at the entrance. Large bags, professional cameras and outside food are commonly refused.
- **Tickets.** Mobile data gets patchy when thousands of phones are in one plaza, so regulars have the QR code open or a printed ticket in hand before reaching the front of the line.
- **Payment.** GCash and Maya are everywhere in Araneta City, and so are cards. Small vendors outside sometimes want cash.
- **Rain.** October falls in the wet season, and showers are common. A compact umbrella or poncho makes the outdoor queue much easier.

After big shows, many locals wait in the mall or eat a late snack while the first wave fills the rail stations. They head home once the platforms clear.

## Eating before and after in Cubao

Food is easy to find here. Gateway Mall and Gateway Mall 2 have food courts and plenty of Filipino and international chains within a few minutes' walk of the Coliseum. Farmers Plaza and Ali Mall add cheaper, older-school options. Long-running Filipino fast food sits beside sit-down restaurants.

For something with more character, Cubao Expo sits on the edge of Araneta City. It is a low-rise courtyard that used to be a shoe market and is now lined with small bars, vintage shops and independent restaurants. It suits a post-concert drink while the stations empty out.

Novotel Manila Araneta City is inside the complex, within walking distance of the Coliseum. That beats queuing for a ride at midnight.
