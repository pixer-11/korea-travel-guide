## A red car in a shopping street

The bottom station is easy to walk straight past. It sits down a narrow lane off Lambton Quay, Wellington's main office and shopping strip, between shopfronts and suit-wearing foot traffic. Then the lane opens onto a platform, a bright red car and a track that tilts steeply up the hill.

This has been running since 1902. It is the only cable car still operating in New Zealand, and it has never been purely a tourist ride. Students, commuters and Kelburn residents use it as their way home, which is why it starts early in the morning.

## Five minutes, four stops

The whole trip takes about five minutes. The line is a funicular: two cars are fixed to the same cable, so as yours climbs, the other comes down to meet it at the passing loop in the middle.

There are stops along the way, and you will see locals get on and off at them:

- **Clifton Terrace**, just above the city
- **Talavera**, the passing point
- **Salamanca**, the stop for Victoria University's Kelburn campus
- **Kelburn**, the top terminal, the lookout and the museum

The track runs through short tunnels and over bridges. The seats are stepped to match the slope, so sit facing downhill if you want the city opening up behind you as you climb. On the way back down, face forward.

## What Kelburn gives you

You step out at Kelburn onto a terrace about 120 metres above where you started. From the railing you look down over the Wellington CBD to the harbour. Oriental Bay curves off to the left and Mount Victoria rises beyond it.

It is an exposed spot in a famously windy city. Bring a layer even if it was calm on Lambton Quay. The wind hits the hilltop much harder than the sheltered streets below.

Allow 20 minutes at the lookout. It is the best free panorama of the city you can reach without a car or a long climb.

## The old winding house

The Cable Car Museum sits right beside the top terminal, in the brick building that once held the winding machinery. Inside you can see how the line worked before it was rebuilt in 1979. Original cars from the early system are kept there, painted in their old colours.

It is a compact museum and takes 20–30 minutes. It is the place to understand why a city this hilly needed a rail line straight up a slope in the first place. The museum keeps its own hours, so check the official site before you plan around it.

## Walking down through the Botanic Garden

Most first-timers buy a return ticket. A better plan is to ride up and walk down. The Wellington Botanic Garden starts right at the Kelburn terminal, and paths zigzag down through native bush, conifer groves and lawns to the Thorndon side of the city.

On the way down, look for these:

- **Space Place at Carter Observatory**, a short walk from the top terminal
- **The Lady Norwood Rose Garden**, near the bottom of the garden
- **Bolton Street Cemetery**, one of the city's oldest burial grounds, on a footpath route back toward town

At an easy pace the walk takes 30–45 minutes. You come out near Parliament and the Beehive, a short flat walk from Lambton Quay. It is downhill all the way, which is the reason to do it in this direction.

## Riding it without the queue

The ride is very popular with visitors, and on fine weekends the line at the bottom station can stretch down Cable Car Lane. The fix is timing:

- **Weekdays:** quietest from 8am–9am and 7pm–9pm. From Monday to Thursday the car currently stops at 8pm, so the evening window is really 7pm–8pm. Friday runs until 9pm.
- **Weekends:** quietest from 9am–11am and 5pm–9pm. Avoid 11am–5pm, when the queues form. On Sundays the car currently finishes at 7pm, so the late window is 5pm–7pm.

Buy your ticket at the Lambton Quay terminal before you join the boarding line. Check the official Wellington Cable Car site for current fares and payment options; regular riders tap on with a transport card. If you plan to walk down, ask for a single.

On board, hold the rail or sit down. The car starts and stops on a real gradient. During the weekday morning and late-afternoon commute, step aside for students heading to the Salamanca stop rather than crowding the doors with a camera.

## Timing the ride around the day

Opening times are currently 7:30am–8pm Monday to Thursday, 7:30am–9pm on Friday, 8:30am–9pm on Saturday and 8:30am–7pm on Sunday. These are this week's hours and can change, so confirm on the official site before a late ride.

An early weekday ride lets you look down on the city waking up before the 9am office rush. The quiet evening windows catch the CBD lights coming on below the lookout. How dark it is when you get there depends on the season.

Give the whole outing two to three hours if you add the museum and the garden walk. If you only want the ride and the view, 45 minutes is enough.
