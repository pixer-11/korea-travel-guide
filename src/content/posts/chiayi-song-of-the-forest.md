## Timber laid where trains once ran

Look up and you see sleepers. Hundreds of old wooden railroad ties are stacked and angled into a great open dome, the kind of timber that once carried logging trains down from Alishan. Light comes through the gaps between the beams, so the shadows on the ground move as the sun does.

Its Mandarin name is 森林之歌 (Sēnlín zhī gē), "Song of the Forest". The name and the material both point to Chiayi's past as the lowland railhead of the Alishan Forest Railway. The city grew rich on cypress and cedar hauled from the mountains.

The artwork sits in a landscaped green space on Wenhua Road. Grass, paths and low planting surround it, so you can walk all the way round and step right under the canopy.

## What you actually do here

This is a short, visual stop. Most people stay 20–40 minutes.

- **Walk underneath.** The view straight up through the beams is the shot most visitors come for.
- **Circle the outside.** From a distance the dome reads as one shape. Up close you see that it is made of separate weathered ties, and many still carry bolt holes and wear marks.
- **Watch the light.** The gaps between the timbers throw striped shadows, and these shift through the day.
- **Sit on the lawn.** The surrounding green space makes this a natural pause between sights in the northern half of the city.

There are no exhibits, no ticket booth and no indoor space. Read it as open-air sculpture in a pocket park, not a museum.

## Dodging the weekend camera crowd

It is one of Chiayi City's most photographed spots, and on weekends it fills up. BestTime's forecast of typical foot traffic puts the busiest weekend windows at **8am–12pm and 4pm–8pm**. Try not to arrive in either one, because that is when you will be waiting for a clear frame under the dome.

The forecast gives no quietest hours, but the gap between those two peaks is the obvious weekend choice. A weekday visit is the simpler answer if your schedule allows.

Google currently lists the park as open 24 hours. Outdoor public spaces can still change access for events or maintenance, so check locally if you plan an unusual hour.

## Photo manners under the beams

Taiwanese visitors take photos seriously here, and there is an unspoken rhythm to it.

- **Take turns.** People often wait for a clear shot of the dome. Step out of frame once you have yours, rather than lingering in the centre.
- **Don't climb.** The ties are an artwork, not a jungle gym. Stay on the ground.
- **Keep tripods compact.** On a busy weekend a wide tripod blocks the path under the canopy.
- **Use the name.** If you take a taxi, show the driver 森林之歌. It gets you there faster than an English name.
- **Carry your rubbish out.** Public bins can be sparse in Taiwanese parks, and locals often take waste home.

## Getting there from Chiayi Station

The park is on Wenhua Road in the East District, in the northern part of central Chiayi City. From Chiayi TRA Station it is a short taxi ride, or you can walk if you don't mind city streets.

Chiayi City runs the YouBike public bike system, and that suits the flat grid of streets well. Pick up a bike near the station and you can link this stop with others in the same part of town.

If you arrive on the High Speed Rail, remember that Chiayi HSR Station is out in Taibao. Take the shuttle bus or a taxi into the city first, then continue to Wenhua Road.

## Pairing it with the rest of Chiayi

Song of the Forest works best as one stop on a half-day loop about the timber railway. In the same northern part of the city you will find **Hinoki Village**, a cluster of restored Japanese-era wooden houses built for forestry staff. **Beimen Station**, the old wooden station on the Alishan Forest Railway, is nearby too.

Wenhua Road itself runs south into the city centre and its well-known night market. That is the place to try **Chiayi turkey rice** (火雞肉飯), the shredded-turkey-over-rice dish the city is known for across Taiwan.

**Chiayi Park**, with its Shinto shrine remains and the Sun Shooting Tower, is a short ride east. It makes a good second stop for a longer stretch of green.
