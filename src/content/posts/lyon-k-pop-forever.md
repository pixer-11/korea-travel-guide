## Lightsticks on the tram to Décines

The night starts on the tram. On arena dates the cars heading east from Vaulx-en-Velin La Soie fill with fans in group hoodies, holding lightsticks that glow before anyone has reached a seat.

K-Pop Forever is a touring tribute show that celebrates K-pop hits. It is not a concert by one original band. It comes to the LDLC Arena in Lyon-Décines on **December 27, 2026**. That Sunday falls in the quiet week between Christmas and New Year, when much of Lyon is still on holiday.

## What the evening is

This is a tribute production, and that shapes the night. You come for the songs and the choreography of the genre, performed live in a large indoor arena. The appeal is hearing a whole run of hits in one evening rather than one group's set list.

The show's material does not name its performers or the songs on the set list, so this guide doesn't either. The arena is a modern indoor hall with a standing floor in front of the stage and banked seating rising around it. It is built for amplified shows like this one.

## Tickets and the French seating terms

French ticket pages use their own words, and knowing them helps you pick the right zone:

- **Fosse**: the standing floor in front of the stage, unreserved
- **Gradins**: the tiered seating around the hall, usually with numbered seats
- **Placement libre**: you stand or sit wherever you like within your zone
- **Placement numéroté**: your seat is assigned on the ticket

Big arena shows in the Lyon area are sold through the venue's own channels and the main French ticketing networks. Fans who want a spot near the stage usually go for the fosse. Families with younger children tend to prefer the gradins, where everyone has a guaranteed view and somewhere to sit.

## Getting out to the LDLC Arena

The arena is not in central Lyon. It sits in the OL Vallée complex in Décines-Charpieu, right next to Groupama Stadium, home of Olympique Lyonnais.

The standard route on public transport:

- **Metro Line A** to its eastern terminus, **Vaulx-en-Velin La Soie**
- **Tram T7** from La Soie to **Décines OL Vallée**, a short walk from the arena doors
- **Tram T3** from Part-Dieu Villette heads out in the same direction, and the TCL network often adds services on big event nights

Driving is possible, but traffic around the complex slows after a show lets out. Most attendees staying in the city ride the tram both ways. Allow roughly 40 minutes to an hour from Bellecour or Part-Dieu, depending on connections.

## How the crowd does arena night

K-pop audiences have their own habits, and a tribute show draws the same fans. Lightsticks from different fandoms sit side by side in the same rows. Fans shout the familiar chant lines in Korean during the choruses. Joining in is welcome, even if you only know the hooks.

A few local practicalities shape the evening:

- **Security**: French arenas run bag checks at the entrance. Small bags get through fastest, and large backpacks slow the line.
- **Paying**: bars and merch stands inside the arena take cards and contactless phones as standard.
- **Language**: staff announcements are in French. *Sortie* means exit and *vestiaire* means cloakroom.
- **The ride home**: crowds head for the tram as soon as the lights come up. People who stay ten minutes for a drink usually find the platforms calmer.

Late December in Lyon is cold and often damp. Most people arrive in coats, so the cloakroom queue is worth thinking about, or you can carry a lighter layer.

## A Lyon weekend around the show

A Sunday-night show leaves the daytime free in a city that rewards walking. In late December the streets are quieter than they were for the Fête des Lumières earlier in the month.

Ways to spend the hours before the tram east:

- **Vieux Lyon**: Renaissance lanes on the west bank of the Saône, with the *traboules*, the covered passages that run through the buildings
- **Fourvière**: take the funicular from Vieux Lyon up to the basilica and the Roman theatres, which have a wide view over the rooftops
- **Les Halles de Lyon Paul Bocuse**: the covered food market near Part-Dieu, handy because it sits on the way to the T3
- **A bouchon**: Lyon's traditional bistros, serving *quenelles*, *saucisson brioché* and *tarte aux pralines*

Part-Dieu and the Presqu'île are the most practical bases. Both are a quick metro ride from La Soie, so getting home after the encore is simple.
