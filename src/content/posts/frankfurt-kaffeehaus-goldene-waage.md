## A merchant's house, rebuilt beam by beam

Look up before you go in. The Goldene Waage ("Golden Scales") has a stone arcade at street level and carved, dark-timbered upper storeys that jut out over the lane. It was first built in 1618–1619 for Abraham van Hamel, a Dutch spice and confectionery merchant. That fits a house that now serves coffee and cake.

Bombing in 1944 destroyed the original. The house you see was reconstructed during the DomRömer project, which reopened this quarter of narrow lanes in 2018. Markt is the old coronation route between the Cathedral and the Römer. Of all the buildings along it, this corner house gets photographed the most.

## Finding Markt 5

Markt 5 sits right opposite the tower of the Kaiserdom St. Bartholomäus.

- **U-Bahn:** Dom/Römer station (U4, U5). Take the exit toward the Cathedral and the Markt, and the house is a couple of minutes' walk away.
- **On foot from the Römerberg:** walk east along Markt, past the Hühnermarkt square and its Stoltze fountain.
- **From the river:** the Eiserner Steg footbridge is about five minutes south.

Give it 45 minutes to an hour for coffee and cake. Add another hour if you also walk the Neue Altstadt lanes, the Römerberg and the Schirn Kunsthalle next door.

## Timing your table

The doors open at 11am every day. Closing is 7pm, except Saturday, when it is 7:30pm. That makes this a late-morning or afternoon stop, not a breakfast one.

On weekends the quiet window is short: **11am to 12pm**. From 12pm to 7pm it stays busy all afternoon. Cathedral visitors and Old Town walkers fill the tables, and you may have to wait for a seat. We have no weekday crowd figures. Even so, a weekday morning soon after 11am is the safest bet for a calm table.

The house is popular and Google reviewers rate it consistently well. Don't expect a quiet local secret. Expect a well-run café in one of Frankfurt's most-visited streets.

## Coffee and cake, the Frankfurt way

German café habits are worth knowing before you sit down.

- **Kaffee und Kuchen:** the mid-afternoon coffee-and-cake ritual is the reason places like this exist. That is also the busiest time, so a late-morning slice beats the crowds.
- **Seating:** at the door, check whether staff are seating guests or whether you can take a free table. When it's busy, don't hover by occupied tables.
- **The bill:** catch your server's eye and say "Zahlen, bitte." Each person can pay for their own order; say "getrennt" (separately) or "zusammen" (together).
- **Tipping:** round up, or add about a tenth. Tell the server the total you want to pay as you hand over cash or card, or say "stimmt so" to keep the change.
- **Payment:** check whether cards are accepted before you order. It still varies between German cafés.
- **Prices:** these are on the menu at your table. Expect mid-range prices for the Old Town, not a bargain.

Afterwards, cross Markt and climb the Cathedral tower for a view down onto the rooftops you just walked between.
