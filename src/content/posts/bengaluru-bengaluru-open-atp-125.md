## Six days of Challenger tennis in January

The 2027 edition runs from January 5 to 10. That puts it in the first full week of the tennis season, while the tour's top tier plays warm-ups for the Australian Open. At Challenger 125 level, the second-highest rung of the men's professional ladder, the draw mixes ranked players chasing points with Indian hopefuls on home courts.

Indian players have won here before. Sumit Nagal took the singles title in 2017, and Indian doubles pairs regularly go deep in the draw. Early-week days usually mean several courts running at once. The finals weekend narrows everything down to the centre court.

## KSLTA Stadium, Cubbon Park

The tournament has been held at the KSLTA Stadium, home of the Karnataka State Lawn Tennis Association. The stadium sits inside Cubbon Park, the large green park at the centre of the city's old cantonment. The courts are outdoor hardcourts. The main show court has banked concrete stands, and the outside courts have smaller seating areas where you sit almost at the baseline.

January is Bengaluru's dry season. Mornings are cool and the afternoons are sunny and mild, so the open stands get direct sun by midday. Spectators usually come with a cap and sunscreen.

- **Metro:** Cubbon Park station (Purple Line) is the closest, and Vidhana Soudha station on the same line also works.
- **Next door:** M. Chinnaswamy Stadium, the cricket ground, is just across the road.
- **Within walking distance:** Vidhana Soudha, the State Central Library's red Gothic building, and the shopping and food streets of MG Road and Church Street.

## Getting through the gate and onto the courts

KSLTA runs the event, and ticket and entry information comes from KSLTA. The association has its own clubhouse on the stadium grounds. Most fans come by metro or by app cab, using Uber, Ola or Namma Yatri. Auto-rickshaws work too, and drivers know the venue as "KSLTA" more readily than by the tournament name. On park-closure mornings, cars can't use the roads inside Cubbon Park, which makes the metro the easier option.

Courtside etiquette follows the tour standard, and umpires do enforce it:

- Stay seated during points and move only at changeovers or between games.
- Flash photography is out, and phones stay silent in the stands.
- On the outside courts, people tend to watch from the end where they came in rather than walk behind the players.

Food stalls on site and in the surrounding streets mostly take UPI. Visitors without an Indian bank account usually carry some cash or pay by card where it is accepted.

## Building a day around the matches

The early rounds suit court-hopping. You can watch a set on one court, catch a tiebreak on another and see a doubles match without a reserved seat. Weekend crowds fill the main court, and that's where the semi-finals and the January 10 final are played.

Between sessions, many spectators walk out into Cubbon Park's bamboo groves and avenues of old rain trees, or head to Church Street for filter coffee and dosa. A full day at the venue fits easily alongside an evening on MG Road. The park and the stadium are close enough together that the two blend into one outing.
