## Two nights of the Redline

NCT 127 plays Hong Kong on October 10 and 11, 2026. That is a Saturday and a Sunday, so the crowd includes fans from Macau, Shenzhen and Guangzhou who come in for the weekend. Neo City: The Redline is the group's 5th world tour. It continues the Neo City series that ran through The Origin, The Link and The Unity.

The sound is the 127 sound: heavy bass, hip-hop structure and the noisy, maximal title tracks the unit built its name on. The fandom is NCTzen, and the stands stay loud the whole night.

## Venue, tickets and getting across the city

SM Entertainment, NCT 127's agency, sends out its tour schedules, venue names and official ticketing partners through its NCT channels. Seat maps and entry rules sit with the ticketing partner for each city.

Hong Kong's large K-pop shows usually go to one of three places:

- **AsiaWorld-Expo** on Lantau, beside the airport, which has its own Airport Express stop
- **Kai Tak Sports Park** in Kowloon City, near Kai Tak MTR station on the Tuen Ma Line
- **Hong Kong Coliseum** in Hung Hom, a short walk from Hung Hom MTR station

Each one gives you a different night. At AsiaWorld-Expo, people leaving after the show queue for the Airport Express or the shuttle buses to Tung Chung, and that takes a while. The Kowloon venues send everyone back into the MTR at once.

## Hong Kong in October

October falls where the humid summer gives way to the drier, clearer months. Typhoon season can still reach into early October, though. When the Observatory raises the No. 8 signal, public transport and large events in Hong Kong are routinely disrupted.

Fans from overseas usually stay in Tsim Sha Tsui, Mong Kok or Causeway Bay. The MTR and the Airport Express connect all three to every major venue. An Octopus card covers the MTR, buses, ferries and most convenience-store snacks.

## How NCTzens run concert weekend in Hong Kong

The fan activity starts days before the first show. Fans usually host cupsleeve events in cafés around Mong Kok and Causeway Bay. These are member-themed cafés where any drink comes with printed sleeves, photocards and stickers. Fan-run freebies get handed out outside the venue on show days too, so people carry a tote bag for the photocards and fans they collect.

Inside, these are the things regulars come prepared for:

- **The official lightstick**, synced to your seat through the app so the stadium-wide colour effects work
- **Fanchants**, which NCTzens learn from the official chant guides for the title tracks and shout in Korean
- **No professional cameras**, which Hong Kong venues routinely refuse at the gate; phones are the norm
- **Merch lines**, which form on the venue's merchandise booth hours before doors

After the encore, many fans skip the first crush at the station. They eat late at a cha chaan teng or a dai pai dong instead, then take the MTR home once the platforms have cleared.
