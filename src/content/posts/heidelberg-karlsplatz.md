## Where Hauptstraße finally opens up

For more than a kilometre, Hauptstraße squeezes you between shopfronts. Then, past Kornmarkt, the buildings pull back. You're standing on Karlsplatz, a broad rectangle of old-town paving, and Heidelberg Castle fills the hillside right above you.

Most visitors look at the castle from the river or the Old Bridge. From here you look up at it, close and steep, with its red sandstone walls rising out of the trees. It's the same castle on every postcard, but from right underneath.

## What surrounds the square

Karlsplatz is framed by buildings, not shops. That's why it feels calmer than the rest of the street. Look for these:

- **Palais Boisserée** on the south side. This baroque town palace once held the Boisserée brothers' collection of medieval paintings and now belongs to Heidelberg University.
- **The Grand Ducal Palace** on the east side. It's home to the Heidelberg Academy of Sciences and Humanities.
- **The castle** above, its terraces and ruined towers clearly visible over the rooftops.
- **Restaurant frontages** along the edges, where tables tend to move outdoors in warmer months.

The square sits at Hauptstraße 214, at the point where the main pedestrian street runs out of its busiest stretch. Keep walking east and the old town thins out towards the Karlstor, the triumphal-arch gate at the edge of the Altstadt.

## Getting to the castle end of the old town

On foot from Bismarckplatz, follow Hauptstraße all the way east. It takes about 25 minutes at a stroll, and you pass Universitätsplatz and Marktplatz on the way. From Marktplatz and the Church of the Holy Spirit, it's about five minutes more.

If your legs need a break, buses stop at Rathaus/Bergbahn, a short walk away. From there you can reach the square or the funicular without climbing.

To go up to the castle, you have two options:

- **Kurzer Buckel**: a steep stepped lane rising from the Karlsplatz side. It's short, tough and quiet.
- **Bergbahn funicular**: from Kornmarkt station, a two-minute walk west. Tickets and times are posted at the station.

## Using the square well

Treat Karlsplatz as a pause, not a destination. Give it 15 to 30 minutes. Most of that is sitting, looking up and getting your bearings before or after the castle.

The square is open around the clock. Early in the day, before tour groups move up Hauptstraße, you'll usually have the view mostly to yourself. Evening is when the castle is lit from below. Because the square is wide, it stays usable even when Marktplatz is packed.

If you're doing the full old-town loop, a smooth order goes like this:

- Castle in the morning, by funicular from Kornmarkt
- Down the Kurzer Buckel steps to Karlsplatz
- West along Hauptstraße to Marktplatz
- Down Steingasse to the Old Bridge

## Winter on Karlsplatz

In winter the square changes character. During Heidelberg's Christmas season, Karlsplatz has traditionally hosted an ice rink, with skaters circling under the floodlit castle. It's one of the most photographed winter scenes in town.

The Christmas market stalls spread across several old-town squares, including Kornmarkt next door. That makes Karlsplatz an easy final stop on a market walk. Check the city's official event pages for each year's dates before planning around the rink.

## Making the most of the castle view

The best photo isn't from the middle of the square. Walk to the western edge, near the corner with Hauptstraße, and frame the castle above the rooftops. Then cross towards Palais Boisserée for a tighter shot of the ruins.

Etiquette here is relaxed, but a few habits help:

- Card payment is common in Heidelberg, but some small old-town spots still prefer cash. Carry a little if you plan to sit down for a drink.
- At café tables, a server comes to you. Round up the bill or add about ten percent when you pay, and say the total out loud as you hand over the money.
- The Kurzer Buckel steps are uneven and get slick when wet. Go up them rather than down if your knees complain.
- Residents live above many of these buildings. Keep noise down if you're passing through late at night.

Compared with the crowds on Marktplatz, Karlsplatz is the old town's quieter corner. You get a castle view as good as any, and space to enjoy it.
