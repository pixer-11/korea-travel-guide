The first thing you'd have noticed, days before a single engine fired, was the scaffolding. Grandstands climbed over the Padang and along Raffles Boulevard, temporary bridges arced across roads walked the week before, and the glass towers of the CBD suddenly had a racetrack running underneath them. Singapore's Grand Prix weekend turned the financial district into a 5-kilometre street circuit, and for three nights in October the skyline itself became part of the spectacle, floodlights bouncing off the Marina Bay Sands facade, the Singapore Flyer lit up behind the final corners.

## Why this year was different

The 2026 race, held October 9-11 at the Marina Bay Street Circuit, marked the first time Singapore hosted an F1 Sprint weekend, meaning an extra qualifying and Sprint race were added to Saturday, on top of the usual Friday practice and Sunday Grand Prix. That compressed the on-track action into a busier, more front-loaded schedule than in past years, so a single day's ticket for Saturday carried more racing than it used to.

The off-track side had also expanded: organisers built out the post-session concert program as a draw in its own right alongside the race.

## Getting there and getting around

The circuit wrapped around the Marina Bay, City Hall, and Esplanade districts, built for a city that already runs on its MRT. Promenade and Bayfront stations (Circle Line and Downtown Line respectively) sit closest to the paddock and Padang grandstands, while City Hall and Raffles Place stations (both interchange stations on multiple lines) are a walkable 10-15 minutes from the outer sections.

Trains were scheduled to run later than usual on race nights. Roads inside the circuit footprint were set to close progressively in the days before the race, affecting hotels inside the loop and rerouting some usual entrances.

## What the weekend actually looked like

Racing under floodlights is the whole point here, Singapore was F1's first night race, and the heat that builds up during the day radiates back off the tarmac after dark, so it's warm and humid trackside even at 10pm. Grandstands along the Marina Bay straight and around the Anderson Bridge area give you both track action and skyline backdrop in the same sightline, which is why they were consistently among the first to sell out.

Beyond the track, the concert stages and fan zones around the Padang were planned to keep running well after the chequered flag, so a single ticket was intended to buy a full night rather than just a race.

## When to go and how long to stay

Most visitors build a four-to-five day trip around the Friday-Sunday sessions, arriving a day early to deal with jet lag and secure hotel logistics before roads start closing, and staying a day after for the flight-home buffer. October sits in Singapore's wetter season, so a light rain layer was a sensible pack, as the race itself was scheduled to run regardless of drizzle.

## How to visit like a local

Regulars buy single-day Padang or Bay Grandstand tickets rather than the pricier three-day packages if they only care about the Sunday race, and they book accommodation in Bugis, Chinatown, or Tanjong Pagar rather than paying inflated Marina Bay hotel rates during race week. Payment at food and drink stalls inside the circuit was set up to be overwhelmingly cashless, with tap-to-pay cards and mobile wallets as the norm.

The mistake most first-timers make is underestimating the walk between MRT exits and the actual grandstand gate, which can be 15-20 minutes through security screening lines; locals arrive a full session earlier than they think they need to. Because this was a high-demand, heavily attended event, the advised crowd-avoidance move was timing an MRT ride 30-45 minutes before or after the main session breaks, rather than moving with the bulk of the crowd.
