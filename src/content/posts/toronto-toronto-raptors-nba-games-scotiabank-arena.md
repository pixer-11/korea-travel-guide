## Inside the Raptors' home bowl

The Toronto Raptors play their winter 2026-27 home games at Scotiabank Arena. The building sits at the foot of Bay Street, where the Financial District meets the Gardiner Expressway and the railway lands. It is the only NBA arena outside the United States. On game nights that gives the evening a distinct flavour: the crowd waves "We The North" towels, and two anthems are often sung before tip-off.

The arena is shared with the NHL's Maple Leafs, so the floor converts between ice and hardwood across the season. Seating rises in two main tiers: the lower 100 level close to the court and the steep 300 level above. The upper rows look straight down on the court and up at the centre-hung scoreboard. The franchise joined the NBA in 1995 and won its first championship in 2019.

## Dates and tickets for 2026-27

The NBA regular season runs from autumn into spring. The Raptors' home games are spread across that stretch, mostly on weekday and weekend evenings. The team publishes the full home schedule and ticket links on its official site, which is also where seating maps and arena policies live.

Prices vary by opponent, night and seat, and no figure appears here. Marquee opponents and weekend dates typically draw the fullest houses. Midweek games against less fashionable teams tend to have the widest choice of seats.

## Getting to Bay Street

The arena is plugged into Toronto's main transit hub.

- **Subway:** TTC Line 1 (Yonge-University) to Union Station, then a short indoor walk south.
- **Regional trains:** GO Transit lines all terminate at Union Station.
- **From the airport:** the UP Express runs from Pearson to Union Station.
- **On foot:** the PATH underground network links Union Station and the Financial District towers to the arena. In January that keeps you out of the wind off Lake Ontario.

Driving downtown on game nights means heavy traffic on Lake Shore Boulevard and the Gardiner. Most visitors leave the car at the hotel.

## Game night the Toronto way

Fans usually gather in Maple Leaf Square on the arena's west side before heading in. Bars and restaurants surround it, and during playoff runs it becomes the outdoor viewing zone known as Jurassic Park. Many attendees eat in the Union Station food hall or along Front Street rather than queue on the concourse.

Card and tap payment are the norm at arena counters and nearby bars. In bars and restaurants, tipping on top of the bill is standard practice in Canada. Concourse lines peak at halftime, so regulars tend to make their food run during a timeout in the second quarter.

When the opposing team is American, both anthems are sung, and the crowd stands for both. Fans typically arrive well before tip-off to watch warm-ups from the lower bowl.

After the final buzzer, the PATH and Union Station absorb most of the crowd within minutes. Fans heading north often walk a few blocks to St Andrew or King station to skip the Union crush. Nearby, the CN Tower, Ripley's Aquarium and the Hockey Hall of Fame (at Yonge and Front) are all within a ten-minute walk. That makes it easy to build a game into a downtown day.
