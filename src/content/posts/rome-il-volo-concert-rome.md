Three voices and one ribbed concrete dome. On December 17, 2026, Il Volo plays the Palazzo dello Sport in Rome, a hometown-country show for the trio that took Italian operatic pop around the world. The arena sits on a rise in EUR, the planned district south of the historic centre, and its round roof is visible from across the neighbourhood's wide, straight avenues.

## The trio on the bill

Il Volo is Piero Barone, Ignazio Boschetto and Gianluca Ginoble. They met as teenagers on the Italian TV talent show *Ti lascio una canzone* in 2009. Their sound is classical crossover: tenor and baritone lines stacked over orchestral pop.

In 2015 they won the Sanremo Festival with "Grande amore", then finished third at Eurovision with the same song. Later projects included a tribute to the Three Tenors and an album of Ennio Morricone's film themes. A Rome crowd usually knows every word of the Italian standards and sings them back.

## Inside the Palazzo dello Sport

This is a piece of architecture in its own right. Pier Luigi Nervi and Marcello Piacentini designed it for the 1960 Rome Olympics. The interior ceiling is a lattice of prefabricated concrete ribs, radiating from the centre like the underside of a giant shell.

Romans have long called it the PalaEUR, and you will hear that name from taxi drivers. The seating rises in a full bowl, so the stage end changes the feel of the room. Seats along the long sides sit closer to the performers than the far curve does.

For a show built on unamplified-sounding vocal power, the bowl suits the music. Even the upper tiers look down onto the stage instead of across a flat floor.

## Reaching EUR on Metro Line B

The metro is the simplest route from central Rome. Line B runs from Termini south through Colosseo and Piramide to the EUR stations.

- **EUR Palasport**: the closest stop, a short walk uphill to the arena.
- **EUR Fermi**: one stop further, beside the artificial lake, a slightly longer walk.
- **EUR Magliana**: also on the Roma–Lido train line, useful if you are staying toward Ostia.

From Termini the ride takes roughly 20 minutes. Concertgoers on show nights usually fill the southbound carriages, so the crowd itself shows you where to get off.

## Getting home after the encore

December 17, 2026 is a Thursday. Rome's metro stops earlier on weeknights than on Fridays and Saturdays, and a long concert can end close to the last trains. For exactly this reason, many attendees already have their ride home sorted when the lights go down.

Local habits worth knowing:

- **Taxis**: Rome's licensed cabs are white with a roof sign. Romans book them through apps such as itTaxi or FreeNow rather than waving on the street.
- **Taxi ranks**: queues form fast after the show, so people often walk a few blocks from the arena and book from there.
- **Night buses**: Rome's "N" lines run after the metro closes; route maps are posted on stop poles.
- **Payment**: cards and contactless are widely accepted at the arena bars and in cabs, though small cafés sometimes prefer cash for a coffee.

Coffee works the Roman way here too. Standing at the bar and drinking an espresso fast is normal, and it usually costs less than sitting at a table.

## An evening in EUR before the show

EUR is unlike the rest of Rome. It was planned in the 1930s for a world's fair that never happened, so the streets are broad and the buildings are white travertine blocks.

A few things lie within walking distance of the arena:

- **Laghetto dell'EUR**: the artificial lake below the Palazzo, lined with a cherry-tree promenade.
- **Palazzo della Civiltà Italiana**: the "Square Colosseum", stacked with rows of arches, north of the lake.
- **Basilica dei Santi Pietro e Paolo**: the district's large domed church on a hill.

Romans eat late, and a pre-concert meal often becomes an *aperitivo* instead. A spritz arrives with small snacks at bars along Viale Europa. December evenings in Rome are cool rather than cold, so a jacket for the walk from the metro is enough.
