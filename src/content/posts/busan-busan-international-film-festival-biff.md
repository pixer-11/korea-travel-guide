## What the 2026 edition looks like

BIFF's 31st year was set for October 6-15, 2026, a ten-day festival that anchors Busan's autumn calendar. The announced slate featured films from dozens of countries, spanning new Asian cinema, world premieres and restored classics. Organisers named Michelle Yeoh Asian Filmmaker of the Year, an honour that usually brings a retrospective screening and a public conversation. BIFF has always used the festival to spotlight Asian cinema first, Hollywood a distant second, so the crowd typically skews toward serious festival-goers and industry buyers over casual tourists.

## Finding the Busan Cinema Center

Almost everything funnels through the Busan Cinema Center in Centum City, Haeundae-gu. The building itself is worth the trip regardless of programming: a vast cantilevered roof designed by Austrian firm Coop Himmelb(l)au, with an open-air theater underneath used for the opening and closing ceremonies. Busan Metro Line 2 stops at Centum City Station, and exit signs point toward the Busan Cinema Center. It's a short walk from the station, next to the BEXCO convention center and Shinsegae Centum City department store.

BIFF screenings typically also spread to satellite cinemas nearby, including CGV and Lotte Cinema branches in Centum City and Haeundae. Each ticket names the exact theater, not just "BIFF."

## Getting tickets without guessing

Ticketing for BIFF typically opens online before the festival, and popular gala and red-carpet screenings usually sell out within minutes. The standard rules:

- Tickets were sold per screening, not as a festival pass, through the official BIFF website and app.
- A festival badge or online account registration is usually required before purchase.
- Events tied to the year's honoree, in 2026 Michelle Yeoh, typically draw the most demand, along with the most popular titles.
- Same-day or standby tickets sometimes appear at the venue box office for less in-demand slots.

Sale dates, prices and screening venues shift from year to year.

## Watching a screening like a festival regular

Festival regulars usually arrive 20-30 minutes before a screening; BIFF enforces seating by ticket time, and latecomers are often held until a break in the film. Q&A sessions with directors or cast frequently follow gala screenings, so much of the audience stays put when the credits roll. Most signage and subtitles appear in Korean and English, and staff at the Busan Cinema Center's info desk switch easily between both.

For meals between screenings, the surrounding Centum City strip has fast, affordable options built for exactly this rhythm: quick Korean and Japanese chains rather than long sit-down restaurants. Moving between venues means a walk or a short taxi ride, since satellite cinemas aren't always adjacent to the Cinema Center itself.
