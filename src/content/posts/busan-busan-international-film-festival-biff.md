## What the 2026 edition looks like

BIFF's 31st year runs October 6-15, 2026, a ten-day run that anchors Busan's autumn calendar. Expect 316 films from 59 countries, spanning new Asian cinema, world premieres and restored classics. This year's festival honors Michelle Yeoh as Asian Filmmaker of the Year, a slot that usually brings a retrospective screening and a public conversation. BIFF has always used the festival to spotlight Asian cinema first, Hollywood a distant second, so the crowd skews toward serious festival-goers and industry buyers over casual tourists.

## Finding the Busan Cinema Center

Almost everything funnels through the Busan Cinema Center in Centum City, Haeundae-gu. The building itself is worth the trip regardless of programming: a vast cantilevered roof designed by Austrian firm Coop Himmelb(l)au, with an open-air theater underneath used for the opening and closing ceremonies. Take Busan Metro Line 2 to Centum City Station and follow exit signs toward the Busan Cinema Center; it's a short walk from the station, next to the BEXCO convention center and Shinsegae Centum City, the world's largest department store.

Screenings during the festival also spread to satellite cinemas nearby, including CGV and Lotte Cinema branches in Centum City and Haeundae, so check your ticket for the exact theater, not just "BIFF."

## Getting tickets without guessing

Ticketing for BIFF opens online before the festival and typically sells out popular gala and red-carpet screenings within minutes. Standard rules to expect, though you should confirm each on the official site:

- Tickets are sold per screening, not as a festival pass, through the official BIFF website and app.
- A festival badge or online account registration is usually required before you can buy.
- Popular titles and any Michelle Yeoh-related events will move fastest; have your preferred screening times ready before the sale window opens.
- Same-day or standby tickets sometimes appear at the venue box office for less in-demand slots.

Confirm timing and tickets on the official BIFF site, since sale dates, prices and screening venues shift year to year.

## Watching a screening like a festival regular

Arrive at least 20-30 minutes before any screening; BIFF enforces seating by ticket time, and latecomers are often held until a break in the film. Q&A sessions with directors or cast frequently follow gala screenings, so don't sprint for the exit the moment credits roll. Most signage and subtitles run in Korean and English, and staff at the Busan Cinema Center's info desk switch easily between both.

For meals between screenings, the surrounding Centum City strip has fast, affordable options built for exactly this rhythm: quick Korean and Japanese chains rather than long sit-down restaurants. If you're catching multiple films across venues, budget the walk or a short taxi ride, since satellite cinemas aren't always adjacent to the Cinema Center itself.
