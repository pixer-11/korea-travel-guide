## Sixty-nine floors in under a minute

Expect your ears to pop on the way up. The Sky Garden lift climbs about 273 metres to the 69th floor in roughly 40 seconds. When the tower opened in 1993, that made it the fastest passenger elevator in the world. Taipei 101 took the record in 2004, but the climb still gets a reaction from first-timers.

The tower is 296 metres tall. It was Japan's tallest building for two decades until Osaka's Abeno Harukas passed it in 2014. The lower floors hold Landmark Plaza, a multi-level shopping mall, with offices and a hotel stacked above. Most visitors come for one floor only, the 69th.

## What the Sky Garden shows you

The deck runs right around the building. Floor-to-ceiling glass faces every direction, so you work your way round the loop one side at a time. Each side has its own view:

- **East, over the bay:** the white cables of Yokohama Bay Bridge, Osanbashi Pier and the Cosmo World Ferris wheel just below
- **Toward the old port:** the brick Red Brick Warehouse buildings and the Yamashita Park waterfront
- **North:** the long sprawl into Tokyo, with Tokyo Skytree visible when the air is clear
- **West:** Mount Fuji, when haze and cloud allow

Fuji is the view people hope for and often miss. Your best odds come in the dry, cold months from roughly November to February, when the air over the Kanto plain clears. In summer humidity, the mountain often disappears completely.

## The stone dock at the tower's feet

Before you go up, look down. At the base of the tower sits Dockyard Garden, a sunken plaza built inside the former No. 2 Dock of the Yokohama Dock Company. This granite dry dock dates from the late 1890s, when ships were floated in and drained for repair.

The stepped stone walls are original. The dock is designated an Important Cultural Property of Japan. Today it serves as an open-air event space, and the contrast is the point: Meiji-era masonry with glass and steel climbing straight up beside it. It costs you nothing to walk down the steps, and most people heading for the lifts walk straight past.

## Getting there from Sakuragichō

Two stations serve the tower:

- **Sakuragichō Station:** on the JR Negishi Line and the Yokohama Municipal Subway Blue Line. Take the exit toward Minato Mirai, then follow the elevated walkway with moving sidewalks straight to Landmark Plaza.
- **Minatomirai Station:** on the Minatomirai Line, which runs through from the Tokyu Toyoko Line from Shibuya. Follow signs through the Queen's Square complex, which connects to the tower indoors.

From central Tokyo, the Toyoko Line through-service to Minatomirai is the simplest ride, with no change needed. The walkway from Sakuragichō frames the tower head-on, which makes it the more scenic way in.

## Timing the light

Google lists the hours as 11am to 8pm every day of the week, with no closing day. The observation deck can run on its own schedule, so check the official Sky Garden page before a late visit.

The window worth planning around is sunset. In winter, the sun goes down around 4:30pm, so you can watch daylight, dusk and the city lights coming on within one visit. In summer, sunset comes closer to 7pm, which still fits before the listed 8pm close. Give the deck 45 minutes to an hour. Add another hour if you plan to eat or shop in Landmark Plaza.

On grey or rainy days, the top floors can sit inside the cloud. Look up at the tower from the station before you buy a ticket. If the upper floors look smudged, the view will be too.

## Tickets, queues and manners on the 69th floor

This is one of Yokohama's most visited buildings, so plan around the crowds rather than hoping for a quiet deck. A few habits make the visit easier:

- **Find the entrance:** tickets and the dedicated lift are inside Landmark Plaza, signposted in English as "Sky Garden". Current admission prices are posted at the counter and on the official site.
- **Pay however suits you:** cards and IC transit cards are widely accepted in Japan. Tipping is not part of the culture anywhere here, including the café counter upstairs.
- **Line up in the lift queue:** staff group visitors into each car. Stand where they point and face the door; nobody pushes.
- **Shoot through glass properly:** at night, reflections ruin photos. Press your lens close to the glass and keep tripods packed unless staff allow them.
- **Avoid peak days:** weekends and Japanese public holidays fill the deck at sunset. A weekday visit in the first hours after opening is usually calmer than any evening.

Keep your voice low at the window. Couples come up at dusk, and the deck tends to stay quiet even when it is full.

## Walking on from the tower

The tower is the western anchor of Minato Mirai, and everything you saw from the top is walkable from the base. Head toward the water and you reach Nippon Maru Memorial Park, where a fully rigged training ship sits in a dry dock of its own. It makes a good pairing with Dockyard Garden.

Keep going along the waterfront to the Cosmo World Ferris wheel and the Red Brick Warehouse buildings, roughly a 15-minute walk. Beyond them lies Osanbashi Pier with its rolling timber-decked roof. From there, Chinatown and Yamashita Park are a short stroll further. Together they make an easy afternoon circuit that ends back at Minato Mirai's lights.
