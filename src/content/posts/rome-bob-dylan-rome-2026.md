The venue is the Palazzo dello Sport, the round, ribbed-concrete arena that Pier Luigi Nervi and Marcello Piacentini designed for the 1960 Rome Olympics. Romans still call it the PalaEur. From the inside, the shallow dome reads as a pattern of concrete ribs radiating from the centre. It is a big indoor arena, and the seats rake steeply toward the stage.

## Reaching EUR from the centre

EUR sits south of the historic centre. Its wide, axial streets and white travertine buildings were laid out for the 1942 exposition that never happened. The arena stands on a rise at the southern end of the district.

- **Metro:** Line B to **EUR Palasport**, then a short walk uphill. **EUR Fermi**, one stop on, also works.
- **From Termini:** Line B runs direct, with no change of trains.
- **From Trastevere or the Vatican:** a bus or tram goes to Piramide, where you switch to Line B.
- **Taxi:** the ranks fill quickly after shows, and hailing one is easier a few blocks away from the arena.

On a weeknight, the trip back is the tight part of an evening at the arena. Line B's last departures are posted on the ATAC site, and the metro does not run all night.

## Doing the night the Roman way

Italian concert crowds tend to arrive late and fill arenas in the final half hour, so entrances usually bunch up just before a show starts. Early arrivals face shorter lines at security and the bag check.

- **Food:** EUR's bars do *aperitivo* from early evening. A spritz or a glass of wine comes with small plates, which makes an easy dinner before a weeknight show.
- **Pay:** most Roman bars take contactless cards. A few coins help at kiosks and the metro machines.
- **Tipping:** it isn't expected. Rounding up at the bar is plenty.
- **Bags:** arenas restrict large bags, and EUR has no convenient luggage storage nearby.
- **Language:** *biglietto* means ticket, *ingresso* is entrance, *uscita* is exit. *Posto a sedere* is a seat; *parterre* or *prato* is the standing floor.

With an afternoon free, EUR is worth walking. The Palazzo della Civiltà Italiana, the arched white cube Romans call the Square Colosseum, is a short walk from the arena. So is the lake park below it.
