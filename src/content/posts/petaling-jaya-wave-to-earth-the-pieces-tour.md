## A quiet band in a loud arena

wave to earth make small songs. The South Korean trio's sound sits somewhere between bossa nova, soft jazz chords and lo-fi indie rock. The vocals are hushed enough that you lean in to catch them.

On 22 November 2026 those songs fill Idea Live Arena in Petaling Jaya, as part of **the pieces tour**. It is a return visit, so plenty of the crowd will have seen the band in Malaysia before. Expect people who know every word of "seasons" and "bad".

## Getting to Petaling Jaya

Petaling Jaya is a separate city in Selangor, right on Kuala Lumpur's western edge. From central KL it is a short drive when the roads are clear. The Federal Highway and the LDP are the main routes in, and both jam at weekend peak times.

Most concertgoers come one of these ways:

- **Grab:** this is how most people travel in the Klang Valley. Fares surge sharply in the hour after a big show ends.
- **Rail:** the LRT Kelana Jaya Line and the MRT Kajang Line both run through Petaling Jaya. The last leg to the venue is usually a short Grab ride.
- **Driving:** parking near large PJ venues fills up fast on show nights. Car-sharing groups are common in fan chats.

## How a Klang Valley gig night works

Malaysian concert crowds are polite and organised. They also queue seriously. At K-indie shows, the merchandise line usually forms well before doors open, and the tour shirts and posters in popular sizes tend to go first. Fans who care about merch tend to get to the venue a few hours before the show.

Some local habits worth knowing:

- **Paying:** cards and e-wallets such as Touch 'n Go eWallet and DuitNow QR work almost everywhere. Merch stalls usually take them too.
- **Tipping:** it isn't expected at food stalls or in Grab cars.
- **Singing along:** quiet songs often get a quiet crowd. People hum the verses and save the full volume for the choruses.
- **Phones:** fans film a few songs, then put the phone away. Holding a screen over the person behind you for the whole set gets you looks.
- **Getting home:** many people wait out the first surge of Grab demand with a late meal nearby rather than standing at the curb.

## Weather, food and the night after

Late November is one of the wettest stretches of the year in the Klang Valley. Heavy thunderstorms often roll in during the afternoon and evening. Regular concertgoers bring a compact umbrella and allow extra travel time, because rain slows every road in PJ.

For food, the neighbourhoods are the draw. SS2 is known for its night-time mamak restaurants and hawker spots. Damansara Utama has the cafés and bars. Mamak places running into the early hours are normal across PJ, so a teh tarik and roti canai after the encore is easy to find.

If you're staying on, Kuala Lumpur's city centre is close: KLCC, Petaling Street and the Bukit Bintang strip are all a short trip away. That makes the gig an easy anchor for a long weekend.
