## Two days of national-circuit snooker

The French Snooker Masters (TN2) runs over a single weekend. Play takes place on Saturday 12 and Sunday 13 December 2026 in Maromme, and the event is part of the French national snooker circuit.

Within French snooker, "TN" is shorthand for a national tournament, a ranking stop on the domestic calendar. This is not a televised arena event with a stage and a big crowd. It is competitive amateur and semi-pro snooker played at close range, and spectators often stand only a few metres from the table.

Expect full-size tables lit by the usual low lamps over green baize. A referee in gloves re-spots the colours, and the scoreboards tick up frame by frame. Over two days, the early rounds whittle the draw down to the matches that decide the title.

## Reaching Maromme from Rouen

Maromme is a small commune in Seine-Maritime, about 5 km northwest of central Rouen in the Cailly valley. Most visitors coming from further away base themselves in Rouen and make the short trip out.

- **By train from Paris:** services from Paris Saint-Lazare reach Rouen-Rive-Droite in roughly 1h15–1h30.
- **Local rail:** Maromme has its own TER Normandie stop on the Rouen–Le Havre line, a few minutes from Rouen.
- **Bus:** Rouen's Astuce network connects the city centre with Maromme and the neighbouring Cailly valley towns.
- **Car:** the drive from central Rouen is short, and the town sits close to the main roads heading west towards Le Havre.

Mid-December in Normandy usually means short daylight, damp air and temperatures in single digits. Spectators tend to come with layers, because a long session in a hall feels very different from the walk back to the station after dark.

## Watching without breaking a frame

Snooker etiquette is strict, and amateur players notice every lapse. Spectators at national-circuit events follow the same habits as at professional ones:

- **Silence on the shot.** Talk stops as soon as a player settles over the cue ball.
- **Move between frames.** People leave or swap seats at the end of a frame, not halfway through a break.
- **Stay out of the eyeline.** Nobody walks behind the pocket a player is aiming at.
- **Phones on silent, no flash.** A camera flash in the low light round a table is as distracting as a ringtone.
- **Applause for good play.** A century or a hard clearance gets a round of clapping once the ball has stopped.

Signs and announcements are mostly in French. Knowing *la frame* (a frame), *la manche* and *le break* makes the scoreboard easy to follow.

## Rounding out the weekend

The tournament falls in the run-up to Christmas, so an evening in Rouen fits easily around the matches. Rouen's Christmas market usually fills the square in front of Notre-Dame cathedral at this time of year, a short walk from the Gros-Horloge, the old clock arching over the medieval street of the same name.

The Place du Vieux-Marché, where Joan of Arc was executed in 1431, is a few streets further on and lined with brasseries. Traders take payment by card almost everywhere in Rouen, though small market stalls sometimes prefer cash.
