## A waterfall that runs sideways

Bow Falls is wider than it is tall. The Bow River comes out of town heading east, hits a band of tilted limestone and drops in a broad, churning step of roughly ten metres. Then it runs on toward the mouth of the Spray River.

There's no plunge pool or misty cliff here. What you get is a wall of white water stretched across the valley, with glacial turquoise above and below it. Mount Rundle's slabs rise on one side, and the stone towers of the Fairmont Banff Springs show above the trees on the other.

The setting explains the shape. This is a big river valley that past glaciers ground out. The falls mark the spot where the river cuts through harder rock on its way down it.

## Walking out from Banff Avenue

You don't need a car. The easiest way in is from the Bow River Bridge at the south end of Banff Avenue, then along the river on foot.

- **The riverside route:** from the bridge, follow the trail downstream along the south bank. It's mostly flat and runs under spruce, with the river beside you the whole way.
- **Distance:** about 1–1.5 km each way from the bridge, depending on where you start downtown.
- **Time:** allow 1–2 hours return, including stops at the viewing area.
- **Terrain:** packed trail with a few short climbs. It's easy going in summer and can be icy in the shoulder seasons and in winter.

At the end you come out at the viewing area beside the Bow Falls parking lot. Interpretive signs there cover the geology. A low barrier keeps you back from the rock shelf.

## Driving and parking at the falls

Driving in is fine if you're short on time. Head toward the Banff Springs hotel and follow Bow Falls Avenue to the small lot at the bottom, beside the river.

The lot is small and fills quickly in summer. If it's full, it often makes more sense to park in town and walk the trail than to circle.

Bow Falls is inside Banff National Park, so you need a valid Parks Canada pass to stop here. Current pass options and prices are on the Parks Canada website and at the park gates.

## Surprise Corner, the view from across the river

The best overview of Bow Falls isn't at the falls. It's from Surprise Corner, a pull-off on the north side of the river up Buffalo Street, near the start of Tunnel Mountain Drive.

From there you look down the valley at the falls spread across the river, with the Banff Springs hotel sitting above them on its wooded bench. This is the postcard view you've probably seen, and it's worth getting both angles: close-up at the falls, then the wide shot from across the water.

Walkers can link the two. The Hoodoo trail and the riverside paths in this corner of town make an easy loop on foot.

## Hollywood on the riverbank

The falls have a small film history. In the 1950s, Hollywood used this stretch of the Bow River for *River of No Return*, the Western starring Marilyn Monroe and Robert Mitchum. Rafting scenes were shot in the rough water around the falls.

Nothing on site really shows it now, apart from the water itself. It helps explain why a fairly modest drop became such a well-known Banff landmark.

## When the river is loudest

The look of the falls changes with the season.

- **Late May to July:** snowmelt from the Rockies swells the Bow, and the falls are at their widest and loudest. This is the most dramatic time to go.
- **August to September:** lower, clearer water and the strongest turquoise colour. Larch and aspen colour arrives in the valley around mid to late September.
- **Winter:** parts of the falls freeze into ice shelves while open water keeps moving underneath. The trail needs grippy footwear or ice cleats.

Within the day, early morning usually means fewer people at the small viewing area. Arriving before the tour vans start their circuit of the Banff Springs and Surprise Corner is a reasonable bet. Late evening light on Rundle works well for photos from Surprise Corner.

## Reading the rocks and the river

This is a natural feature, not a ticketed site. The etiquette is about the river and the wildlife.

- **Stay behind the barrier.** The rock shelf looks walkable, but the current below the falls is strong, cold and fast. People have been swept away along this river.
- **Give elk room.** Elk often graze the open ground near the Banff Springs and along the river. Parks Canada asks visitors to keep at least 30 metres from elk, and more from bears. Never feed or approach them.
- **Bring your own water and snacks.** There's nothing to buy at the falls. The cafés are back on Banff Avenue or up at the hotel.
- **Pack out everything.** The bins at the parking lot are wildlife-proof, so latch them properly.
- **Keep dogs leashed.** That's the rule in the national park, and it matters here because of the elk.

If you'd like more time on foot, the Spray River trails start close by on the hotel side. You can keep walking along that valley well beyond the falls.
