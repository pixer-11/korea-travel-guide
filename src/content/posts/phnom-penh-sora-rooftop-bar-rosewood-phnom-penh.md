Phnom Penh doesn't have many true skyscrapers, which is exactly why Sora feels so disproportionate to the city beneath it. The bar occupies the upper floors of Rosewood Phnom Penh, inside the Vattanac Capital Tower, long the tallest building in the country. Step out of the lift and the room opens onto a wraparound deck with a view that runs from the Royal Palace rooftops to the confluence of the Mekong, Tonlé Sap and Bassac rivers.

That confluence is the whole point of coming here. Phnom Penh is one of the few capitals built exactly where two great rivers meet, and Sora is one of the only places to actually see the geography of that from above. At night the rivers turn to black glass threaded with boat lights, while the low-rise city glows orange and gold in every direction.

## Why it made Asia's 50 Best list

Sora's inclusion on the extended Asia's 50 Best Bars 2026 list is a genuine landmark for Cambodia's drinking scene. It's the only Phnom Penh bar on that roster, which puts it in company usually dominated by Bangkok, Singapore and Hong Kong venues. The recognition reflects a cocktail program built around Cambodian and regional ingredients rather than a generic international rooftop formula, paired with a design language that leans into local materials and craftsmanship instead of importing a template from elsewhere.

## Getting there and settling in

Rosewood Phnom Penh sits in the Vattanac Capital Tower on Monivong Boulevard, in the city's central business district, an easy tuk-tuk or taxi ride from riverside landmarks like the Royal Palace and the National Museum. Dedicated lifts run guests up to the hotel's sky lobby before a second lift continues to the rooftop level.

- Arrive by taxi or ride-hailing app rather than trying to navigate parking yourself; central Phnom Penh traffic can be slow at peak hours.
- Dress code leans smart-casual to elevated; this is a five-star hotel rooftop, not a beach bar.
- Confirm current opening hours and reservation policy directly with the hotel before you go, since these details can shift.

## Timing your visit around the light

Sunset is the obvious draw, and for good reason: the sky over the river confluence turns through pink, orange and deep blue in a fairly short window. Arriving 30 to 45 minutes before sunset gives you time to get settled and order before the crowd thickens for the main event.

If you'd rather skip the busiest stretch, consider a later slot after the sunset rush has cleared, when the deck often settles into a calmer, more conversational mood. Either way, the city view doesn't disappear once the sun goes down; it just changes character; the illuminated palace roofs and river traffic keep the scene interesting well into the evening.

## Booking a table without the scramble

Given the recent global recognition, Sora is no longer a hotel-guest secret, and word travels fast among Phnom Penh's small but attentive dining and drinking scene. Reserve a table in advance, particularly for window or riverside-facing seats around sunset.

- Contact the Rosewood Phnom Penh directly, either through the hotel's booking channels or by phone, rather than walking in and hoping.
- Ask specifically for river-view seating when you book; the layout means not every table gets the full panorama.
- If you're flexible, a weeknight visit is generally easier to secure on short notice than a weekend slot.

## What to expect once you're up there

Expect a polished, hotel-rooftop atmosphere rather than a rowdy nightlife scene: attentive service, considered cocktails, and a room designed to let the view do most of the talking. Because specific menu items and prices change with the season and the bar's own creative direction, it's worth asking your server what's currently being poured rather than expecting a fixed list. Budget for a mid-range to higher-end rooftop bar experience, in line with what you'd expect from a Rosewood property, and treat the visit as an occasion rather than a quick drink on the way somewhere else.
