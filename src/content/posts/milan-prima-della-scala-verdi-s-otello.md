## Saint Ambrose's night, Verdi's opera

Milan opens its opera year on its patron saint's feast. On December 7, 2026, Teatro alla Scala starts its 2026/2027 season with Verdi's *Otello*. The evening is also the city's most formal night out. It marks Myung-Whun Chung's first opening night as Music Director.

The choice of opera carries history. *Otello* had its world premiere in this house in 1887. Arrigo Boito's libretto cuts Shakespeare down to four taut acts: a storm, a jealousy and a murder. It now returns under the same horseshoe of red-and-gold boxes where it was first heard.

## Reaching Piazza della Scala

Giuseppe Piermarini's neoclassical theatre opened in 1778. Its pale facade on Piazza della Scala looks restrained from outside. The gilding, the red velvet and the tiers of boxes rising to the galleries are all inside. Across the square, a statue of Leonardo da Vinci faces the theatre, with Palazzo Marino, Milan's city hall, behind it.

Getting there:
- **Metro:** Duomo (M1 red and M3 yellow). Walk north through Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, which opens straight onto Piazza della Scala.
- **Montenapoleone (M3):** a short walk east of the theatre along Via Manzoni.
- **Tram:** lines run down Via Manzoni, beside the theatre.

On Prima night, barriers usually go up across the piazza. Crowds line them to watch the guests arrive, and photographers work the steps.

## Black tie and the loggione

The Prima is the hardest night of the season to get into and the most expensive. Teatro alla Scala sells its own tickets on its official website and at its box office, where current prices are posted. A large part of the house goes to subscribers, sponsors and official guests.

Here is how the night usually works:
- **Dress:** evening dress is standard. Men wear dinner jackets and women wear long gowns. A suit looks underdressed on this one night.
- **The loggione:** the top galleries hold La Scala's most demanding listeners, the *loggionisti*. They cheer loudly and boo without hesitation. Their verdict at the curtain calls makes the next morning's papers.
- **Applause:** clapping after the big set pieces is normal. Verdi's late style runs scene into scene, though, and regulars keep quiet until a natural break.
- **Intervals:** this is when people are seen as much as when they listen. The foyers and the theatre's museum rooms fill with guests in evening dress.

In past years the piazza has also drawn demonstrators on December 7. They use the cameras to make their point, and that has become part of the ritual too.

## Hearing it without a seat

Most of Milan follows the Prima from outside the theatre. Rai has traditionally broadcast it live on television and on Rai Radio 3. The Comune di Milano's *Prima Diffusa* programme has screened it in squares, cinemas and community spaces across the city. In recent seasons La Scala has also held an under-30 preview performance a few days before opening night.

December 7 is a full city holiday. The Oh Bej! Oh Bej! fair fills the streets around Castello Sforzesco with stalls selling roasted chestnuts and Christmas goods. The Basilica di Sant'Ambrogio honours the saint the day is named for. December 8, the Immacolata, follows straight after. Many Milanese take the two days together as a long weekend, so central hotels fill quickly.
