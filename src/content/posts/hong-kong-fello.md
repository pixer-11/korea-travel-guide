## Why Fello stands out

Wyndham Street has no shortage of bars, but Fello arrived with a pedigree that made people pay attention. It's the work of Shelley Tai, the bartender behind Mius, currently ranked No. 36 on Asia's 50 Best Bars for 2026. That track record shows in how deliberate the drinks feel here — nothing thrown together, everything considered.

The space unfolds over two floors, which immediately changes how you experience it. Downstairs tends to carry the energy of a proper social bar: conversation, clinking glassware, bartenders moving with purpose behind the counter. Head upstairs and the mood shifts — a little calmer, a little more room to actually taste what's in your glass.

## The drinks worth ordering

Two names come up again and again on Fello's menu: the Gyokuro Melon Punch and the Pineapple Basil Smash. The first leans on gyokuro, a shaded Japanese green tea prized for its deep umami sweetness, paired against ripe melon — a combination that reads more like a dessert course than a punch.

The Pineapple Basil Smash takes a more familiar tiki-adjacent shape but sharpens it with fresh basil, cutting the tropical sweetness with something herbal and green. Both drinks say a lot about Fello's approach: craft cocktail technique, but built for a relaxed night out rather than a reverent tasting menu.

Expect the wider list to rotate and expand, so treat any specific drink name as a starting point for conversation with the bartenders rather than a fixed order.

## Finding it in Central

Wyndham Street sits in the heart of Central, one of the streets that — along with neighbouring Elgin and Staunton — forms the backbone of Hong Kong's Soho bar scene. It's a short, steep walk up from Central MTR station, or you can ride the Mid-Levels Escalator partway and cut across on foot.

The street is walkable and dense with bars, so it's worth building Fello into a wider crawl rather than treating it as a single destination. Arrive on foot — parking in this pocket of Central is limited and not worth the hassle for an evening out.

## When to go

Central's Soho strip runs busiest from around 9pm on Thursday through Saturday, when after-work drinks tip over into full nights out. If you want to actually hear your bartender describe the Gyokuro Melon Punch's tea, aim earlier.

A weeknight visit, or an early arrival — right around opening — gives you the calmer version of Fello, with staff able to talk you through the menu properly. As the night goes on and the ground floor fills, expect a louder, more convivial atmosphere rather than a quiet one.

## How to visit like a local

Book a table if you can, especially for a weekend evening — a bar built by a bartender with this level of recognition tends to fill fast once word spreads. If you turn up without a reservation, go early and be ready to wait or perch at the bar.

- **Pay by card or mobile** — Hong Kong's bar scene is overwhelmingly cashless now, though it's sensible to carry a little cash as backup.
- **Tipping** is not obligatory in Hong Kong; a service charge is often already added to the bill, so check before adding more.
- **Pick a floor deliberately** — downstairs for energy and people-watching, upstairs if you want to talk and taste properly.
- **Ask the bartender** what's new — menus at bars like this evolve, and staff often have off-menu suggestions worth hearing.

The mistake visitors make on Wyndham Street generally is bar-hopping too fast and never actually sitting still anywhere. Fello rewards slowing down — order one considered drink, let the bartender talk you through it, and stay a while.

Before you go, confirm Fello's current opening hours and whether reservations are being taken, since details for recently opened bars can shift quickly in the first months.
