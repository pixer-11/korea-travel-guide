## Getting across Quezon City

Traffic is the real headliner on a Metro Manila Saturday. EDSA, the main ring road, runs along the edge of Quezon City and clogs in the late afternoon. Most out-of-town fans plan their whole evening around it.

Fans typically get there in one of these ways:

- **Grab** is the default ride-hailing app in the Philippines. It takes cards and GCash, and drivers work from a pinned address.
- **MRT-3** runs along EDSA through Quezon City, with stations at Quezon Avenue, GMA-Kamuning and Araneta Center-Cubao. Fans usually take the train for the longest stretch and a short Grab for the rest.
- **Taxis** are common, but meters and card payment vary from car to car.

Getting home is the harder part. When a few thousand people order rides from one block, surge pricing and long waits follow. Many fans walk a few streets away and order their car from there, or eat nearby and leave after the crush.

## Doing it the Filipino K-pop way

Manila's fandoms run concert day like an event in its own right. Here is how the day usually unfolds.

- **Freebies come before the show.** Fan groups hand out photocards, stickers, fans and banners outside the venue. Some give them free; others ask fans to follow an account or show a ticket.
- **The official lightstick matters.** Many K-pop shows sync lightsticks to the stage over Bluetooth. Fans usually pair theirs at their seat when staff or the screens prompt them.
- **Fanchants are part of the show.** Chant guides for izna's title tracks circulate among fans. In Manila, a crowd that shouts along makes up a big part of the night.
- **Payment is mostly cashless.** GCash and Maya are everywhere, but small food stalls nearby still prefer cash.
- **Queue numbers are respected.** For standing sections, fans who leave the line hand back their spot. Cutting in draws loud, public objections.

Large bags, professional cameras and outside food are commonly restricted at Metro Manila concert venues. For this show, the rules printed on the ticket were the ones set for the night.

## Making a weekend of it

Quezon City is a decent base for a K-pop weekend. Araneta City in Cubao combines malls with the Smart Araneta Coliseum, and the restaurant strips along Tomas Morato and Maginhawa Street draw diners from across the metro. UP Diliman's wide, tree-lined campus is a calm morning walk the next day.

Visitors flying in usually land at Ninoy Aquino International Airport in Pasay. The ride north to Quezon City can take well over an hour in traffic. Fans coming from abroad often stay in Quezon City itself, which keeps the post-concert trip short.
