## A first for Europe, in a pagoda

Paris gets VERIVERY's first European tour show. The seven-member group from Jellyfish Entertainment takes the GIVE ME FIVE, KEEP THAT VIBE tour to the Bataclan on December 11, 2026. Fans there see a group that debuted in Seoul in January 2019 with *VERI-US* and is only now crossing to this continent.

The room suits a first visit. The Bataclan is a mid-sized hall, not an arena, so the stage stays within shouting distance even from the back of the floor. The night is built for fanchants and lightsticks, and in a space this compact the sound carries.

## The Bataclan on Boulevard Voltaire

From the pavement, the building looks like nobody else's concert hall. The façade dates from 1864–65 and was designed by Charles Duval in a 19th-century take on Chinese style, with a pagoda-shaped roofline painted in bright colours. The name comes from *Ba-ta-clan*, an Offenbach operetta of the same era.

Inside, you get one main floor facing a raised stage, with a balcony wrapping around above it. For pop shows the floor is usually standing. The balcony gives a clear overhead view for anyone who would rather not spend the night in the crush.

The venue also carries the memory of the 13 November 2015 attack. A memorial plaque sits on the façade, and many visitors pause there quietly on their way in.

## Reaching the 11th arrondissement

The Bataclan sits on Boulevard Voltaire, between Place de la République and the Oberkampf bar strip. The metro is the simplest way in.

- **Oberkampf** (Lines 5 and 9): the nearest station, a minute or two on foot.
- **Saint-Ambroise** (Line 9): one stop further along, an easy walk back up the boulevard.
- **Filles du Calvaire** (Line 8): a short walk west, past the round Cirque d'Hiver.
- **République** (Lines 3, 5, 8, 9 and 11): the main hub, about ten minutes on foot down Boulevard Voltaire.

The metro keeps running after most concerts end, and Line 9 runs direct to the Grands Boulevards hotel area. Late on a December weeknight, taxis and ride-hailing cars line up along the boulevard after the show.

## How the queue works at the Bataclan

Paris K-pop crowds take the line seriously. On show days, fans usually start gathering along the Boulevard Voltaire pavement hours before doors, especially those with standing tickets who want the barrier. Staff organise the queue by ticket type, and Bataclan security does bag checks at the entrance.

What attendees usually do here:

- **Carry a small bag.** French venues are strict at security, and big bags slow you down.
- **Dress in layers.** December evenings in Paris are cold and often wet outside, while a packed standing floor gets hot.
- **Learn the fanchants.** Fans typically practise VERIVERY's official chants beforehand, and the room shouts them together.
- **Bring the lightstick.** Many fans carry the group's official lightstick, and some fan groups hand out printed slogans or banner cards in the queue.
- **Speak a little French.** A *bonsoir* to security and staff goes a long way, and most of them also speak English.

The merchandise stand usually draws its own line, separate from the entry queue. Many fans head there first.

## Before and after on Rue Oberkampf

The streets around the venue are among the best in Paris for a night out. Rue Oberkampf climbs uphill from the metro, lined with bars, wine bars and late kitchens. Rue Jean-Pierre Timbaud runs parallel, with more of the same plus cheap North African and Middle Eastern places for a quick meal.

For a sit-down dinner, Rue de la Folie-Méricourt and the side streets toward Canal Saint-Martin have small bistros. The canal itself is about a fifteen-minute walk north, with iron footbridges and quieter bars along the water.

Most fans visiting from outside Paris stay around République, Le Marais or the canal. All three are within walking distance or one metro ride of the venue.

## Tickets and the tour

The tour organiser publishes the Paris date, door details and official ticket links on its own channels. VERIVERY's official social accounts share the same information for the wider tour. Official resale routes are the safe option if a show sells through; tickets passed hand to hand outside a K-pop concert are a known risk.

The title GIVE ME FIVE, KEEP THAT VIBE reads as a nod to the group's long run since 2019. For European fans who have only seen VERIVERY on a screen, December 11 at the Bataclan is the first chance to hear them live without flying to Seoul.
