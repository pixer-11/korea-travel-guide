## Why go

You round a corner off an ordinary residential street in Nantun District and the pavement itself turns into a canvas — cartoon soldiers, owls with human faces, tigers, and planes in cobalt blue, mustard yellow, and fire-engine red climb up the low concrete walls of a row of squat single-story houses. This is Rainbow Village, and the story behind it is as disarming as the paint job: in the early 2010s this was one of hundreds of "military dependents' villages" built for Nationalist soldiers and their families after 1949, and it was slated for demolition once residents moved out. One holdout, a veteran in his nineties named Huang Yung-fu, started painting the walls of his own house out of boredom. Local university students noticed, campaigned to save it, and the city eventually preserved roughly a dozen houses as a public art site instead of bulldozing them.

## Getting there

Rainbow Village sits at 春安路56巷25號 in Nantun District, tucked behind Ling Tung University. There's no MRT here — Taichung's metro network doesn't reach this pocket of the city — so the practical route is a city bus toward Ling Tung University/Dadu, with several routes (including the 25, 75, and 51) stopping within a short walk of the entrance; check current routing on the Taichung bus app or with your hotel since numbers occasionally shift. A taxi or ride-share from central Taichung (near the train station) runs about 15–20 minutes depending on traffic. If you're already at Feng Chia University or the night market area, factor in another 20-25 minutes by road. There's no admission fee, and the site is open-air, so you can walk the lanes at any hour, though the small gift kiosk and murals are obviously best appreciated in daylight.

## What to see

The whole site is really just two or three narrow lanes between low houses, but every surface is worked: painted doors, window frames turned into eyes, hand-painted signage in Huang's loose, childlike style. Look for his self-portraits — a stooped figure in a straw hat recurs throughout, since he often depicted himself among the animals and soldiers. A few rooms have been left as viewing points where you can see photos of the village before restoration, giving you a sense of how close this whole block came to being flattened for a parking lot or apartment block. Because the space is compact, most visitors move through slowly, phones out, and the crowd density in the narrow lanes can feel tighter than the footprint suggests — this is very much a well-visited, camera-ready stop on the Taichung circuit now, not a quiet find.

## When to go

Given how popular this spot has become, timing matters more than at a lot of Taichung attractions. Weekday mornings, shortly after opening, are noticeably calmer than afternoons and weekends, when tour groups and school trips cluster in the lanes. Overcast days are actually a plus here — Taichung's midday sun bounces hard off the light-colored walls, and the murals photograph better in the flatter light of early morning or late afternoon. Plan on 45 minutes to an hour: enough to walk both lanes, read the informational panels, and take photos without feeling rushed, though photography enthusiasts often linger longer for the light.

## How to visit like a local

Don't expect a ticket booth or turnstile — you simply walk in off the street, and there's no set opening/closing ceremony, though official hours are posted and worth confirming before you go since they can shift seasonally. Cash is still king at the small snack and souvenir stands near the entrance; card acceptance is inconsistent at this kind of small, family-run kiosk. The single biggest mistake visitors make is treating it as a five-minute drive-by photo op squeezed between the airport and downtown — because it's compact, arriving at the same time as a tour bus means you'll be shoulder-to-shoulder in a two-meter-wide lane. Arrive right after opening or in the last couple of hours before closing instead. There's minimal shade, so a hat matters more than you'd think in summer. And keep your voice down near the original preserved rooms — this remains, technically, someone's former home, and the site's caretakers appreciate visitors treating it as a memorial to a vanishing kind of community, not just a backdrop.

## Nearby and combining your visit

Ling Tung University's night market sits close by if you want food after, and it's an easy taxi ride to Fengjia Night Market or the Taichung Metropolitan Opera House if you're building a half-day loop through Nantun and Xitun districts. Given the short visit length, most travelers pair Rainbow Village with one or two other stops rather than making it a standalone destination.
