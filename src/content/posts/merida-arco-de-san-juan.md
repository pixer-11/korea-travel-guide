## An arch you can drive through

Traffic still runs under Arco de San Juan. Cars and motorbikes squeeze through the single opening on Calle 64, the same gap that once marked where colonial Mérida ended and the road out began. Look up as you walk past and you'll see thick masonry and worn stucco, rising above the low one-storey facades on both sides.

Mérida once had several of these gateway arches, built in the late 17th century. Three survive: the Arco de Dragones, the Arco del Puente and this one. Of the three, San Juan sits right beside a neighbourhood square, so it's the easiest to enjoy slowly instead of just driving past.

## Finding it on the numbered grid

The old centre is a grid of numbered streets, which makes the arch easy to find. Even-numbered streets run north–south and odd-numbered ones run east–west. The arch is on Calle 64, near where it meets Calle 69, in Centro.

- **From the Plaza Grande:** walk south on Calle 62 or Calle 64 for about 10 minutes. The street numbers go up as you head south.
- **From the CAME bus terminal (ADO):** the station is a few blocks west, around Calle 70. That makes the arch a sensible first stop if you arrive by long-distance bus.
- **By taxi or ride-hail:** ask for "el Arco de San Juan, Calle 64". Drivers know it, and the Parque de San Juan right next to it is an easy drop-off point.

## What's around the arch

The arch frames one end of Parque de San Juan, a shaded square with benches and a central fountain. The Iglesia de San Juan Bautista faces the square, and the church and arch together give the area its name. Plan on 20–30 minutes for the arch and the park. Allow longer if you want to sit and watch the neighbourhood go by.

This part of Centro sees far fewer visitors than the streets around the cathedral. The houses here are lived in, many of them painted in the flat ochres, pinks and blues typical of Mérida's old town. Doorways open straight onto narrow pavements. If you want a quieter side of the historic centre, the walk between the Plaza Grande and the arch shows you a lot of it.

## Beating the Yucatán heat

The heat decides your timing more than crowds do. Mérida is hot all year, and April and May are the hardest months, with very little shade on the pavements. In the hours just after sunrise the stone is still cool and the streets are calm. After sunset is the other good window.

Google currently lists the arch as open 24 hours, which makes sense: it's a public street structure, not a ticketed site. The church next to it has its own schedule, so check before you go if you want to look inside.

## Around the arch after dark

At street level you share the arch with traffic, so stick to the pavements. Don't step into the opening for a photo while cars are coming through. The best shot is usually from inside Parque de San Juan, looking back at the arch with the street running through it.

A few habits that fit right in here:

- **Greet people.** A "buenos días" or "buenas tardes" to anyone on a bench or in a shop doorway is normal in Mérida's neighbourhoods.
- **Carry small pesos.** Street vendors and corner shops in residential parts of Centro often prefer cash. In the evening, carts selling marquesitas (crisp rolled crêpes, often filled with Edam cheese) are a common sight in the city's parks.
- **Do as the neighbours do.** In the cooler evening hours, families sit out in the squares. Pick a bench, stay a while and leave the park as you found it.
- **Mind the church.** If a service is on at San Juan Bautista, keep your voice down near the doors and cover your shoulders if you go in.

## Linking it into a Centro walk

The arch works best as one stop on a loop of the old city. From here, walk north along Calle 64 or 62 to the Plaza Grande and the Catedral de San Ildefonso. Then head up Calle 60 to the Parque Santa Lucía and the Parque de Santa Ana, and on towards Paseo de Montejo. Or link the three surviving arches into a half-day walk focused on the colonial city's old edges.

If you're coming to or from the CAME terminal, the arch is a short stop on the way, even with a backpack on.
