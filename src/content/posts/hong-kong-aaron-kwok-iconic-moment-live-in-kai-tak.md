## A countdown with a Heavenly King

Few Cantopop names carry Aaron Kwok's weight. He is one of the Four Heavenly Kings, alongside Jacky Cheung, Andy Lau and Leon Lai, the group that dominated Hong Kong pop through the 1990s. Of the four, Kwok was the dancer, and his stage shows are built around choreography, costume changes and big set-piece entrances.

His Live in Kai Tak concert puts that show on the turn of the year. It spans the night of December 31, 2026 into January 1, 2027, so the countdown to midnight is part of the concert itself. Expect a crowd that knows every word of hits like "對你愛不完" (Love You Endlessly) and the dance-floor favourite "Para Para Sakura".

## Kai Tak: the old runway

The venue name points to Kai Tak, the site of Hong Kong's old international airport. Planes stopped landing here in 1998, and the long reclaimed strip in East Kowloon has since filled with housing, parks and the cruise terminal at the runway's tip. Kai Tak Sports Park sits on this land, holding the main stadium, a smaller indoor arena and open public space.

The organiser publishes the exact venue, seat map and ticket links on its official site. That is the place for door times and entry rules; this page names none, because none were given to us.

## Getting there and away

The MTR is how most concertgoers reach Kai Tak. Useful stops:

- **Kai Tak Station** (Tuen Ma Line): the closest rail link to the sports park, with a walkway towards the venues.
- **Sung Wong Toi Station** (Tuen Ma Line): one stop along, near the southern end of the district.
- **Kowloon Tong** and **Diamond Hill**: interchanges for the Kwun Tong and East Rail lines, a short ride from Kai Tak.

On New Year's Eve the MTR has traditionally run overnight on many lines. Taxis are hard to find after a big Kowloon show, so most fans head straight for the trains. Kowloon City, a short walk inland, is known for its Thai restaurants and Kowloon Walled City Park, and many attendees eat there before the show.

## Countdown night in a Cantopop crowd

Hong Kong concert crowds are loud but orderly. Fans usually hold their glow sticks up through the ballads and sing the choruses in Cantonese, and on the faster numbers the whole floor joins in with Kwok's dance moves. Learning the "Para Para Sakura" arm routine is a long-standing fan habit.

Some practical habits:

- **Octopus card:** locals tap in on the MTR with an Octopus card or a phone wallet. A preloaded card avoids the queues at ticket machines after midnight.
- **Merchandise:** official lightsticks and tour goods sell at stalls near the venue. Queues usually build in the hours before the show.
- **Photos:** policies on phones and cameras differ by promoter. The house rules printed with the tickets cover this.
- **Midnight:** New Year's Eve in Hong Kong traditionally centres on Victoria Harbour. Across the city, crowds and road closures make the late-night trip home slower than on a normal concert night.

Most attendees plan the night as concert, countdown and then a slow ride home. They treat the walk back to the MTR as part of the celebration.
