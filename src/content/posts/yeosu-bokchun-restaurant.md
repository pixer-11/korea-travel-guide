## Why go

Jeollanam-do is widely regarded as South Korea's most food-obsessed province, and Yeosu — a port city on the southern coast — inherits that reputation with seafood-heavy tables loaded with banchan (side dishes). Bokchun Restaurant, tucked into the Gyodong neighborhood in Yeosu's older downtown core, has built a solid local following over the years, reflected in its 4.2 rating across 941 reviews. It's the kind of place where the cooking style leans traditional rather than trendy: think stews, grilled or braised fish, and a generous, ever-refilled spread of shared side dishes rather than a single showpiece plate.

## Getting there

The restaurant sits at 5-8 Gyodongnam 1-gil, in the Gyodong-dong area of central Yeosu — walkable from the old Yeosu city center rather than the newer Yeosu Expo Station side of town. If you're coming from Yeosu Expo Station (the KTX/train terminus near Yeosu Ocean Park and the Expo grounds), plan on a short taxi ride (roughly 10–15 minutes) or a bus into the older downtown, since this neighborhood predates the Expo-era development. Gyodong is a historic residential and market district, so expect narrow streets, small parking, and a more local, unpolished streetscape than the waterfront tourist strip.

## What to see / eat

Expect the meal to center on a main fish or seafood-based dish — commonly a stew (jjigae or maeuntang-style) built around whatever is fresh that day — accompanied by a wide array of banchan typical of the region: seasoned greens, pickled vegetables, dried and fresh seafood preparations, and rice. Jeollanam-do restaurants are famous nationally for over-delivering on side dishes, and Bokchun's reputation and price level (mid-range, priced as a "2" on a typical 1–4 scale) suggest a table-filling, home-style experience rather than an à la carte one. Because portions and banchan are often meant for sharing, this is a better fit for two or more diners than a solo quick bite.

Afterward, walk off the meal in the surrounding Gyodong/Jongpo area, where Yeosu's older market streets and residential lanes give a more authentic sense of the city than the reconstructed Expo waterfront. Yeosu's famous night view — the illuminated Dolsan Bridge and Odongdo Island — is a short taxi ride away and pairs naturally with an earlier dinner here.

## When to go

Lunch on a weekday is the easiest time to get a table without a wait, and it lets you see the banchan freshly set rather than picked over from an earlier dinner service. Yeosu draws heavy domestic tourist traffic in summer (July–August) for its coastal scenery and night views, and around the Yeosu International Fireworks Festival dates, so a restaurant like this in an older neighborhood can still see spillover crowds during those peaks. Spring and autumn are calmer, with milder weather for the walk to and from the restaurant.

## How to visit like a local

Order is typically taken by the table rather than at a counter, and payment is almost always settled at the end of the meal by card or cash at a small front register — tipping is not expected or practiced in Korean restaurants, so don't leave extra. If you're unsure what to order, saying "오늘 뭐가 좋아요?" (oneul mwoga joayo — "what's good today?") to staff is a normal way locals defer to the day's fresh catch rather than picking blind from a menu. The most common visitor mistake here is under-ordering: banchan is refillable and included, so it's fine to ask for "더 주세요" (deo juseyo — "more, please") on a side dish you like rather than assuming portions are fixed. Because this is a residential neighborhood restaurant rather than a tourist-zone one, don't expect an English menu or staff — having the address or restaurant name saved in Korean on your phone to show a taxi driver makes arrival far smoother. Come hungry and ideally with a companion or two, since the banchan-heavy format rewards sharing over solo dining.
