## Rakabganj, not the Taj Ganj strip

Most Agra shopping happens in the lanes by the Taj. AGRA BAZAAR sits somewhere else: 8/63 Sewala Jat, Dhawlikar, in Rakabganj, a residential and commercial district in central Agra with the 282003 postcode. You get there by city traffic, auto-rickshaws and ordinary shopfronts. Nothing here is built for tour groups.

That matters for how you plan. You are not wandering into it between monuments. You have to go on purpose, with the address saved on your phone.

Few reviewers have written about it, and it doesn't turn up on the usual souvenir circuit. The reviews it does have are solidly positive. Treat it as a modest, practical stop and you won't be disappointed.

- Shopkeepers are used to being asked to show several options before you decide.
- Staff in central Agra usually speak some English. A few words of Hindi, such as *namaste* and *kitna hai?* ("how much is it?"), go a long way.

## The Saturday shutter

This is the one detail that trips people up. Most Indian markets close one weekday. AGRA BAZAAR closes on **Saturday** instead, and it opens on Sunday.

The weekly schedule:

- **Sunday to Friday:** 10:00am to 8:30pm
- **Saturday:** closed

Plenty of visitors plan their "free shopping day" for the weekend. If your Agra stop falls on a Saturday, this one is off the list, so swap it with a Friday or Sunday. It's also worth knowing that the Taj Mahal itself is closed on Fridays. That makes Friday a natural day for errands like this, while the rest of Agra's visitors head to Agra Fort or Fatehpur Sikri.

The day is long, so there is no need to rush in at opening. A late-afternoon visit leaves your mornings free for monuments, which is when the Taj is at its best. Just make sure you arrive with time to spare before 8:30pm.

## Getting across town

Rakabganj is a short drive from Agra Fort, and the drive from the Taj Mahal's gates is a bit longer. Nothing about the route is complicated, but the address is. "Sewala Jat, Dhawlikar" is not a name every driver will know straight away.

Some ways to make it simple:

- **App cabs and autos:** Uber and Ola both work in Agra. Drop a pin on the exact address rather than typing "Agra Bazaar". That name gets confused with the city's many other *bazaars*, including Sadar Bazaar and Kinari Bazaar.
- **Street auto-rickshaws:** agree the fare before you get in, and show the driver the written address.
- **From the railway:** Agra Cantt is the main long-distance station, and Agra Fort station is the older one near the fort. Both put you within a short auto ride of Rakabganj.

## Buying without a middleman

This is the section that saves you money. Agra's shopping trade has a long-standing commission system. Some drivers, guides and "helpful" strangers steer visitors to particular shops and take a cut, and that cut ends up in your price.

Here's how to avoid it:

- **Arrive under your own steam.** Book the ride yourself to the exact address, and decline any detour offered along the way.
- **Don't mention who sent you.** If a driver wants to come inside with you, it's fine to say no.
- **Pay directly.** UPI (India's QR-code payment system) is used almost everywhere. Foreign cards are less reliable at small outlets, so carry some rupees as backup.
- **Ask for a bill** for anything substantial, particularly if you plan to ship it home.
- **Take your time.** You don't have to buy on the first visit, and a polite "I'll think about it" is normal here.

Haggling in Agra is conversational, not combative. Start by asking the price, show you're comparing, and settle somewhere you're both comfortable. If a price is marked as fixed, respect it.
