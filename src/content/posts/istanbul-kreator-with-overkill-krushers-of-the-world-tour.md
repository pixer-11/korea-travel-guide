## One night, two thrash lineages

This is a single Istanbul date. Kreator headline JJ Arena on Sunday, November 15, 2026, with Overkill as special guests. There is no second night and no other Turkish stop on the facts we hold.

The pairing works because the two bands grew up in parallel. Kreator came out of Essen in Germany's Ruhr valley in the early 1980s. Overkill formed in New Jersey around the same time. Both have kept releasing records and touring for more than four decades.

## What each band brings

Kreator are the loudest name in Teutonic thrash, the German scene they shared with Sodom, Destruction and Tankard. Mille Petrozza has fronted the band from the start. Fans tend to wait for songs from these records:

- *Pleasure to Kill* (1986), still one of the fastest records of its era
- *Extreme Aggression* (1989)
- *Coma of Souls* (1990)
- *Violent Revolution* (2001), the album that brought them back to straight thrash

Overkill are East Coast thrash: punk-fast, groove-heavy and built around Bobby "Blitz" Ellsworth's high, sneering vocals and D.D. Verni's bass. *Feel the Fire* (1985), *The Years of Decay* (1989) and *Horrorscope* (1991) are the records their set usually leans on.

## Getting to JJ Arena

The show is held at JJ Arena in Istanbul. Istanbul is split by the Bosphorus, and crossing it at the wrong hour can take far longer than any map suggests. Fans coming from the other continent usually leave a generous margin.

For public transport, the city runs on the Istanbulkart. One card covers metro, Marmaray, tram, bus and ferry, and several people can tap through on it. Many visitors also pay contactless directly at the gates on newer lines. For taxis, locals mostly use the BiTaksi app rather than flagging cars outside, which avoids arguments over the meter.

## How Istanbul metal crowds do a show

Istanbul has a large, loyal metal audience that has turned out for Kreator before. Sets are sung along to in full. The circle pit opens early and stays open. A few habits make the night smoother:

- **Tickets live on your phone.** Most Turkish ticketing is e-ticket based, and the name on the ticket can be matched against ID at the door. Foreign visitors usually carry a passport.
- **Bags stay small.** Arena security in Turkey routinely searches bags. Fans tend to leave large backpacks, chains and spiked jewellery at the hotel.
- **Merch goes fast.** Tour shirts in common sizes are usually the first thing to sell out, so regulars head to the stand before the support set.
- **Cards work.** Card and contactless payment is standard at Istanbul venues and bars. Turkish lira cash is useful for street food on the way home.
- **Sunday night logistics.** November 15 falls on a Sunday and the next morning is a working day. Many attendees line up a BiTaksi or a ride home with friends, because the late-night rush thins out public transport options.

Kadıköy and Beşiktaş are where metal fans usually end up for a drink before or after, both packed with rock bars. A thrash shirt is the closest thing to a dress code.
