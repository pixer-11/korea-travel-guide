## Inside the 1898 hall

The Ferry Building's nave-like interior runs the length of two football fields, lit by a barrel-vaulted skylight over the central corridor. It survived the 1906 and 1989 earthquakes and served up to 50,000 commuters a day before the Bay Bridge opened in 1936. A 2003 restoration stripped out a mid-century office conversion and exposed the original steel trusses and skylight. The 245-foot clock tower facing Market Street was modeled on Seville's Giralda bell tower.

## What's actually inside

Vendors line both sides of the central nave, with a mix of grab-and-go counters and sit-down spots.

- Cheese, charcuterie, and specialty grocers for picnic supplies
- Coffee roasters and a chocolate shop for a walking snack
- Prepared-food counters serving oysters, dim sum, and sandwiches
- A wine and spirits shop with Bay Area labels
- Bay-facing tables outside for eating with a view of the water

Open daily 6am to 10pm, though individual vendors keep their own shorter hours inside that window; grab-and-go stalls tend to open earliest, sit-down restaurants later.

## Beating the Saturday crush

This is one of San Francisco's most-visited food halls, not a quiet find, so plan around the rush rather than expecting to avoid it. Crowds build steadily from mid-morning and peak on weekends between 9am and 7pm, especially when the farmers market is running outside. Both weekdays and weekends stay comparatively calmer from 7am onward before that late-morning surge hits. Arrive at opening or after the dinner shift starts if you want room to browse without a shoulder in your back.

Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays bring the Ferry Plaza Farmers Market to the building's bay side, adding fruit, flower, and produce stands to the open-air plaza. Saturday's market is the largest and draws its own separate crowd on top of the indoor hall's regular traffic.

## Getting there and moving through it

The building sits at the foot of Market Street on the Embarcadero, facing the Bay Bridge. It's a straightforward walk from the Embarcadero BART/Muni Metro station, and the F-line historic streetcar stops directly out front. Ferries from Sausalito, Tiburon, Oakland, and Alameda dock at the piers just behind the building, making it a natural first or last stop on a bay crossing.

Inside, the traffic pattern is simple: one central corridor with vendors opening off either side, so a full loop end to end takes maybe twenty minutes without stopping. Most people linger closer to an hour once they start sampling. Skip the sit-down restaurants if you're short on time and stick to the counter vendors, which move fast even when the hall is busy. Bikes are common outside; there's a bike valet near the plaza during market days for anyone riding the Embarcadero path.
