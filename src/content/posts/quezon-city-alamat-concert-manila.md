## Eight voices, several languages

ALAMAT plays the New Frontier Theater on November 8, 2026. The group is a multi-regional P-pop boy band that debuted in 2021 under Viva Records. Their whole idea is linguistic: songs move between Tagalog and regional languages such as Ilocano, Kapampangan and Bisaya, often inside a single chorus.

That makes a Manila show a sing-along in more than one tongue. Fans, known as Magiliw, usually know the regional lines as well as the Tagalog ones. Tracks like "kbye" and "Maharani" are the ones the room tends to carry.

## The room at Araneta City

The New Frontier Theater is a proscenium theatre, not an arena. It sits inside Araneta City in Cubao, right beside the Smart Araneta Coliseum. Seating rises in tiers from orchestra to balcony, so sightlines stay close even from the back rows.

For a group built on choreography, that scale matters. You see footwork and facial expressions without relying on screens. Ticket tiers, seat maps and the ticketing link sit with the organiser and ALAMAT's official social accounts.

## Reaching Cubao by rail

Rail beats driving on a Quezon City Saturday night. Cubao is one of the few places where two Metro Manila rail lines meet:

- **MRT-3:** Araneta Center–Cubao station, linked by footbridge to Gateway Mall.
- **LRT-2:** Araneta Center–Cubao station, also feeding into Gateway Mall.
- **On foot:** from Gateway Mall, the theatre is a short walk through Araneta City's covered walkways.

Ride-hailing drop-offs around the Coliseum clog when events overlap, and EDSA traffic is notoriously slow. Attendees who drive usually use the Araneta City mall car parks. Gateway Mall, Ali Mall and Farmers Plaza cover pre-show dinner, from fast-food chains to sit-down restaurants.

## How Magiliw do a concert night

P-pop crowds run on ritual. Fans typically gather outside the theatre well before doors to trade freebies: printed photocards, stickers and fan-made banners handed out for free. Merch lines form at the official booth, and many sellers and fan traders take GCash alongside cash.

Inside, expect organised fanchants called out between lines. Fan clubs often circulate the chant guides on social media in the days before a show. Lightsticks and phone torches go up for ballads. The crowd stays standing for the high-energy numbers even in a seated theatre.

A few habits regulars follow:

- They keep bags small, since venue security checks bags at the entrance.
- They stay in their own seat section; ushers enforce ticketed zones.
- They respect the venue's photo and video rules, which vary by show.

After the encore, the crowd flows back toward Gateway Mall and the stations. The last trains on MRT-3 and LRT-2 run relatively early in the evening by big-city standards, so fans from outside Quezon City often plan around a car ride home or a hotel in Cubao.

If you have a free afternoon, Cubao Expo is a short ride away. It's a horseshoe of small bars, vintage shops and cafés in a former shoe market.
