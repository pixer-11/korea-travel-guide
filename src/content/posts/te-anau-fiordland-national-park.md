## Where the road runs out

Te Anau is where the farmland stops. The town's main street faces Lake Te Anau, the largest lake in the South Island. Across the water, forested ranges rise straight out of the lake and keep going west for more than 12,000 square kilometres.

That is Fiordland, New Zealand's largest national park, set up in 1952. It forms part of the Te Wāhipounamu World Heritage Area. Glaciers carved the fiords, lakes and hanging valleys you're about to drive through, and they're far bigger than photos suggest.

## The Milford Road, one pull-in at a time

State Highway 94 runs about 120 km from Te Anau to Milford Sound. Driving it straight through takes roughly two hours. Nobody should drive it straight through. The lay-bys are the point, and each one shows you a different kind of landscape:

- **Eglinton Valley:** wide tussock flats with beech forest along both edges.
- **Mirror Lakes:** a short boardwalk where, on a calm morning, the Earl Mountains show upside down in still water.
- **Homer Tunnel:** a single-lane bore through granite, run on traffic lights, with keas often working the queue.
- **The Chasm:** a short forest loop to where the Cleddau River has scoured holes through the rock.

You won't find fuel past Te Anau and there's almost no mobile signal on the way, so fill the tank and download your maps in town. In winter, carry chains. The road can close for avalanche risk.

## Doubtful Sound, the longer way in

Milford gets the crowds. Doubtful Sound gets the silence. To reach it you leave from Manapouri, about 20 minutes south of Te Anau. A boat takes you across Lake Manapouri, then a coach climbs over Wilmot Pass and drops you to the fiord.

It's a full day, and every part of it can only be done on a tour. The sound is longer and more branched than Milford, with fewer boats sharing it. If you can only spare one fiord and you value quiet over a famous view, this is the one.

## Walking: from an hour to four days

Fiordland has three of New Zealand's Great Walks. The **Kepler Track** loops out of Te Anau itself, so you can walk its lakeshore start from the edge of town without booking anything. The **Milford Track** starts at Glade Wharf, at the north end of Lake Te Anau, and takes four days. The **Routeburn** crosses the park's northern edge.

Huts on all three Great Walks are booked through the Department of Conservation. In peak season they fill months ahead. If you haven't booked, do short walks off the Milford Road, like Key Summit, or walk the Kepler's first lakeside stretch to Brod Bay and back.

## Moving through Fiordland without getting caught out

Rain is part of the deal. This is one of the wettest places in the country. The good news is that heavy rain turns every cliff above Milford Sound into a waterfall, so don't cancel a cruise because of a grey forecast. Pack a proper waterproof jacket, not a showerproof one.

Then there are the sandflies. They gather at lakeshores and car parks, and insect repellent is what people here actually carry. Keep your car windows shut at stops or you'll drive the next hour with passengers.

Stop in at the Fiordland National Park Visitor Centre on the Te Anau lakefront before you head out. Rangers there have the current track conditions, the road status and hut availability. Opening times change with the season, so check before you go. On the tracks, stay on the formed path and carry out all your rubbish.

## Planning your days from Te Anau

Spend your evenings in town. The Te Anau Glowworm Caves sit across the lake and are reached by boat. Punanga Manu o Te Anau, the bird sanctuary along the lakeshore, is one of the few places you can see takahē, a flightless bird once thought extinct.

On weekends, the park is calmest between 8am–9am, 3pm–4pm and 6pm–9pm. Avoid 7am–8am, 10am–11am and 12pm–1pm, when traffic and arrivals peak. The 6pm–9pm window is a good time for lakeshore walks on long summer evenings.

A sensible plan:

- **Day one:** the Milford Road and a cruise on the sound.
- **Day two:** Doubtful Sound, or a Kepler day walk followed by the glowworm caves.
- **Day three, if you have it:** go back for whatever the weather took from you.
