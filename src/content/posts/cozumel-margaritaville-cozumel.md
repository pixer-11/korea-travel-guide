## A themed bar on the cruise-pier road

This is a chain restaurant, and it knows it. Margaritaville takes its name from Jimmy Buffett's 1977 song, and the brand sells that song's idea of a holiday: a frozen drink, a cheeseburger, no schedule. The Cozumel branch sits on the coast road just south of town, in the stretch where the big ships tie up.

The address puts it inside the SSA México terminal area at km 4.5 of the Carretera a Chankanaab, also called the Costera Sur. That location explains who comes. Most diners walk in straight off a ship, eat, and walk back. They aren't trying to find a restaurant in the town.

## Finding km 4.5 on the Costera Sur

Cozumel's west coast has one main road. Heading south from the San Miguel waterfront, it becomes the Carretera Costera Sur, and the kilometre markers count up from town. Km 4.5 puts you roughly 4.5 km south of the centre, a short taxi ride.

- **From a ship at the SSA pier:** you're already in the terminal zone. Follow the walkway signage inland from the dock.
- **From downtown San Miguel:** take a taxi south along the coast. Cozumel taxis have no meters and charge fixed zone fares. Agree the fare before you get in, or check the rate board at a taxi stand.
- **By rented scooter or car:** stay on the coast road heading south and watch the km posts.

## What arrives at the table

Google lists it as an American restaurant, so think burgers and bar food more than Yucatecan cooking. With the Margaritaville name, the margarita is the drink most people order. The full menu and current prices are on the menu at the table, so check them there instead of trusting an old list online.

Google puts it in its lowest price band. For a branded venue right beside a cruise terminal, that's a fair reason to sit down without worrying about the bill. Reviewers have rated it consistently well across a couple of thousand reviews. That kind of record usually means the place delivers what it promises: easy food, cold drinks and no surprises.

If you want cochinita pibil or a marquesita, go to San Miguel's streets in the evening. This place is for an easy, predictable meal between the gangway and the beach.

## Friday's extra two hours

The opening hours follow the ship schedule. Margaritaville opens at 9am every day, about when the first passengers come ashore. Six days a week it closes at 6pm, around when the all-aboard calls start.

**Friday is the exception: it stays open until 8pm.** That's the only evening slot all week. If you're staying on the island, not just docked for the day, Friday is when you can catch the sunset over the channel and still have a drink in hand.

Don't plan a dinner here on any other day. By 6pm Monday to Thursday, and on weekends, the doors are shut.

## Beating the gangway rush

The foot-traffic data gives a clear answer. **On weekdays the quietest window is 4pm to 7pm.** From Monday to Thursday that means 4pm until the 6pm close. On Friday you get the whole stretch, with an hour to spare before the 8pm close.

Weekends are calmer still. **On Saturday and Sunday it's quiet right through from 9am to 6pm**, and there's no recorded weekend peak.

A late lunch on a weekday works well. By 4pm many cruise passengers are heading back, and you'll have more space at the tables.

## Paying and tipping at a cruise-terminal table

The habits here match the terminal more than the town. A few things help:

- **Currency:** this close to the pier, US dollars and Mexican pesos usually both work, and cards are standard. Paying in pesos usually avoids a poor rate when staff convert your dollars at the table.
- **Card machines:** staff usually bring the terminal to your table. If it asks whether to charge in pesos or your home currency, choose pesos to skip the card-side conversion markup.
- **Tipping:** in Mexico a 10 to 15 percent tip is normal at table-service restaurants, and tourist-zone venues expect it. Check the bill first, because some places near the piers add a service charge already.
- **Ordering:** English is the working language here, so you won't need Spanish. A "gracias" when the drinks arrive is still polite.
- **Ship time:** watch the clock, not the bar. Your ship sails on its own schedule, and the restaurant won't remind you.

Nobody would call it a local secret. It's a well-known branded stop that most visitors find on their own. The most useful thing you can do here is time your visit around the ships, which the section above covers.

## Down the coast road after lunch

The Costera Sur runs south from here past snorkelling and beach spots. That makes Margaritaville a sensible first or last stop on a day spent along the coast.

- **Chankanaab National Park:** the road is named for it. The park lies further south on the same highway and is known for its lagoon, snorkelling and botanical garden. A taxi ride from km 4.5 is short.
- **San Miguel waterfront:** head back north for the malecón, the main square and Mexican food in the evening once Margaritaville has closed.
- **The west-coast reefs:** Cozumel is one of the Caribbean's best-known diving islands, and many dive and snorkel boats run along this stretch of coast.

Allow about an hour for a drink and a meal. If you pair it with an afternoon at Chankanaab, it fits easily into a single cruise day.
