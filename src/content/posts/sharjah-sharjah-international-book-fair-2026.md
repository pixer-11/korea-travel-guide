## What the fair actually is

This was the 45th Sharjah International Book Fair, set for November 4-15, 2026. The venue was Expo Centre Sharjah, the emirate's main exhibition ground on Airport Road, a few minutes from Sharjah International Airport.

Oman held Guest of Honour status this edition. Omani publishers, cultural programming and pavilion space dedicated to Omani literature were planned alongside the fair's usual mix of Arabic and international publishing houses. Sharjah's fair is one of the largest in the Arabic-speaking world. It typically spans multiple halls of the Expo Centre, with publisher stalls, author talks and children's programming running in parallel.

## Getting to Expo Centre Sharjah

Expo Centre Sharjah sits on Airport Road (Al Taawun/Airport Road corridor), making it an easy taxi or rideshare trip from both Sharjah and Dubai. It's roughly a 25-40 minute drive from central Dubai depending on traffic, and closer still from Sharjah's own residential districts.

- Parking is available on-site at the Expo Centre grounds.
- Public buses serve the Expo Centre area from Sharjah's central bus station.
- Sharjah International Airport is the closest airport, only a short drive from the venue.

With a two-week format, weekday mornings tend to be calmer than weekend afternoons and evenings, when families and school groups add to the numbers.

## Tickets and what to expect inside

Hall opening hours, any entry pricing and the day-by-day author and events schedule were set by the Sharjah Book Authority, which organises the fair.

Inside, the fair typically splits into publisher halls (stalls selling Arabic, English and other-language titles), a stage and talks programme with author panels and signings, and dedicated children's and young-readers zones. Sharjah's fair is known for its scale of discounted books, so many visitors come with a shopping list and a spare bag.

Serious browsing usually takes at least half a day. A full day suits visitors who also catch talks or bring children to the children's programming.

## Making the most of a visit

Weekday mornings are usually the quietest time to browse the stalls. The halls fill up noticeably after school hours and on weekend afternoons.

- A tote or foldable bag is useful, since book-buying here often means multiple heavy purchases.
- Payment methods vary by stall, and each publisher sets what it accepts.
- Author talks and signings can draw queues, especially for well-known names.
- Children's zones get busy on weekends, so weekday mornings tend to be quieter for families.

Sharjah promotes itself as a UNESCO World Book Capital designee (2019) and invests heavily in reading culture citywide. The fair feels more like a civic event than a purely commercial expo, with free public programming alongside the sales floor.
