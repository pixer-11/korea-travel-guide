## Christmas Eve, Hangul Banners and a Hall the Size of an Airfield

KINTEX is a flat field of glass and steel on Goyang's western edge. Its exhibition halls normally hold car shows and trade fairs. For SBS Gayo Daejeon 2026, on December 24–25, part of that space becomes a broadcast stage for SBS's year-end K-pop show.

Gayo Daejeon is a fixture of the Korean holiday season. Families watch it on television the way other countries watch a Christmas special. Inside the hall, you get the version the cameras only half capture: lightsticks in a dozen fandom colours and staging built for one night of TV.

## What the Night Looks Like

The show runs as a long broadcast, not a standard concert. Expect a stack of acts with short sets, collaboration stages and special performances put together for the year-end slot. The verified facts describe "top K-pop acts". SBS announces the specific lineup through its own channels, and this guide doesn't guess at it.

Because it is live television, the pacing belongs to the broadcast:

- Stage changes and camera resets between performances
- Hosts' segments aimed at the home audience
- A running time far longer than a single-artist concert

KINTEX halls have flat concrete floors and very high ceilings. Sightlines depend on how the production builds the stage and seating, not on fixed arena tiers.

## Reaching KINTEX in Ilsan

KINTEX sits in Ilsanseo-gu, Goyang, roughly an hour from central Seoul by subway. The usual rail route is Seoul Subway Line 3 (the orange line) to its western end at Daehwa Station. From there, attendees either walk the wide boulevards toward the exhibition centre or take a short taxi ride.

From Gimpo Airport, a taxi across the Han River via the Ilsan bridges is the simplest option. From Incheon Airport, airport limousine buses serve Ilsan. A T-money card covers subway and city buses and tops up at any convenience store.

Late December in Goyang is properly cold, and nights often drop below freezing. Wind blows across the open plazas between halls. Fans who queue outside usually come in long padded coats, gloves and hand warmers.

## Getting Through a KINTEX Show Night

K-pop broadcast shows have their own way of doing things, and Gayo Daejeon follows them.

- **Tickets and ID.** Mobile tickets are standard for major K-pop events in Korea, and organisers routinely match names to ID at entry. International fans usually carry their passport rather than a photocopy.
- **Fan sharing (나눔, nanum).** Outside K-pop shows, fans hand out free photocards, stickers and snacks themselves. It's friendly and informal. A quiet "감사합니다" (gamsahamnida, thank you) is the expected response.
- **Lightsticks.** Each fandom brings its official lightstick. Some productions sync them to the stage show, so fans keep them charged and switched on.
- **Filming.** Broadcast productions often restrict cameras inside, and staff enforce the rules. Most fans follow the venue's posted policy rather than risk being stopped mid-show.
- **Leaving.** When thousands of people exit at once, the taxi ranks fill quickly. Many fans walk toward Daehwa or Juyeop stations, or book a car through the Kakao T app, which most Korea visitors install.

## Staying and Eating Around Ilsan

Visitors usually stay in Ilsan rather than travelling back to Seoul late at night. The streets around Jeongbalsan and Juyeop stations have business hotels, and so does the KINTEX area. Restaurants, cafés and late-opening bars cluster in the La Festa and Western Dom pedestrian shopping streets, close to Jeongbalsan Station on Line 3.

Ilsan Lake Park is a short ride away, with a wide loop path around an artificial lake. Hyundai Motor Studio Goyang stands beside KINTEX, and the Hyundai department store next to it has a food hall and a warm place to wait.

## Before and After the Broadcast

Gayo Daejeon falls on Christmas, a public holiday in Korea and a big date-night occasion. Seoul's restaurants and illuminated streets are busy across both days. Fans who come in from abroad often spend the rest of their trip in Seoul, then come out to Goyang for the show.

SBS broadcasts Gayo Daejeon on its television network. Fans who can't get into the hall usually watch on SBS or its official online streams, which is how most of Korea sees it.
