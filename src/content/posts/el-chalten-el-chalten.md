## Where the road ends at Fitz Roy

Route 23 stops here. El Chaltén sits at the end of the road where the Río de las Vueltas and Río Fitz Roy meet. Behind it, the granite spires of Cerro Fitz Roy and Cerro Torre rise over the northern sector of Los Glaciares National Park.

The town was founded in 1985, which makes it one of Argentina's youngest. Argentina later named it the country's National Trekking Capital. The name comes from the Tehuelche word for Fitz Roy, usually translated as "smoking mountain" because cloud so often streams off the summit.

Most of town lies along Avenida San Martín. Hostels, gear-rental shops, bakeries and microbreweries line it, and the trails start from the ends of the street. You don't need a car once you arrive.

## Getting there from El Calafate

The nearest airport is El Calafate (FTE). From there it is roughly 220 km by road, north on Ruta 40 and then west on Ruta 23. Several bus companies run the trip daily in summer and take about three hours. Book a seat ahead in January and February.

Just before town, the road crosses the Río Fitz Roy and passes the national park visitor centre. Stop there for:

- current trail conditions and closures
- wind and weather forecasts for the high sections
- campsite rules and the park's leave-no-trace briefing

## Picking your trail

Trails leave straight from the village, and most visitors stay two to four nights to fit in the main routes.

- **Laguna de los Tres:** the classic Fitz Roy viewpoint, a full day of 8–10 hours return. The final hour is a steep, loose climb to the glacial lake at the base of the spires.
- **Laguna Torre:** a gentler valley walk of about 6–7 hours return to a lake below Cerro Torre, often with icebergs floating in it.
- **Mirador de los Cóndores and Mirador de las Águilas:** short climbs from the south end of town, good on arrival day or at sunset.
- **Loma del Pliegue Tumbado:** a long ridge climb with a side-on view of both massifs.

Summer daylight runs late into the evening, so a mid-morning start still works for the long routes. Leaving before dawn for Laguna de los Tres gets you the sunrise glow on Fitz Roy, if the clouds allow.

## Trail manners and town logistics

Weather decides everything here. Wind on the exposed ridges can knock a hiker off balance, and the peaks may hide for days. Locals and guides plan around the forecast and keep a spare day so the Fitz Roy hike can wait for a clear morning.

How the town works:

- **Cash:** there are few ATMs and they can run dry in peak season. Bring Argentine pesos from El Calafate, though many businesses take cards.
- **Water:** hikers commonly fill bottles from the streams higher up. Never wash or relieve yourself near them.
- **Camping:** pitch only at the designated park campsites. Fires are banned throughout the park.
- **Trail etiquette:** stay on the marked path, pack out all rubbish, and give way to people coming down the steep sections.

The common mistake is booking just one night and expecting to see Fitz Roy. Book three if you can.
