Stand at the edge of the grass and the whole of central Perth sits in front of you, low and wide across the water. The towers of the CBD line the far bank. Between you and them runs the Swan River, which the Whadjuk Noongar people call Derbarl Yerrigan. Mindeerup puts you on the South Perth side of that view, and it's the side most visitors never cross over to.

## What Mindeerup is

It's a named foreshore attraction, not a building. Google lists Mindeerup as a tourist attraction on South Perth Esplanade, and it's open at every hour of every day. There's no gate, no ticket window and no closing time to plan around.

The name fits the pattern of Noongar place names, where the "-up" ending, as in Joondalup or Nannup, usually marks a place. Any interpretive signs on site will tell you what the name means and why it was chosen. Read those rather than trusting a guidebook's paraphrase.

It's a small, quiet listing, visited far less than Kings Park or Elizabeth Quay. The few people who do review it rate it very highly. That gap is the reason to come.

## The skyline from the south bank

Most photos of Perth are taken from the city looking out. From here you get the reverse: the city itself as the subject. Because the Esplanade faces roughly north, the towers catch the sun through the afternoon while you stand with it behind or beside you.

Dusk is the moment to aim for. The sun drops in the west toward the Narrows Bridge and Kings Park. Then the office windows across the river light up floor by floor and reflect off the water. Stay after dark and the skyline turns into a line of lights over black water.

What to look for along this stretch:

- Black swans, the river's namesake, often feeding close to the bank
- Pelicans working the shallows
- Transperth ferries crossing between Mends Street and Elizabeth Quay
- Kings Park's escarpment rising to the west, above the Narrows Bridge

## Getting to South Perth Esplanade

The ferry is the best way in. Transperth ferries run from Elizabeth Quay Jetty in the city to Mends Street Jetty in South Perth. It's a short crossing, and you get a second view of the skyline on the way. Mends Street Jetty sits on the Esplanade, so you're walking the foreshore the moment you step off.

Other ways in:

- **On foot or by bike:** the shared path over the Narrows Bridge links the city side to the South Perth foreshore, and the riverside paths run the length of the Esplanade.
- **By bus:** Transperth routes run along Mill Point Road, a short walk inland from the water.
- **By car:** there's street and foreshore parking along the Esplanade. Check the signs for time limits and payment rules, which the City of South Perth sets and can change.

The fares for Transperth ferries and buses are posted on the Transperth website and at the jetty.

## Walking the foreshore around it

Treat Mindeerup as one stop on a longer riverside walk. The Esplanade is flat, paved and shaded in patches. It suits an easy hour or two.

Head west and you reach Mill Point, where the whitewashed Old Mill stands near the Narrows Bridge approach. It's one of the oldest surviving colonial buildings in the Perth area. Head east and the grass opens into Sir James Mitchell Park, a long, wide green strip along the river with more skyline views.

Inland from Mends Street Jetty, Mends Street has cafés and places to eat. Perth Zoo is a short walk further up on Labouchere Road, if you want to make a full day of the south bank.

## Sitting on the grass after dark

Being open 24 hours matters here. Plenty of Perth's lookouts are best in daylight. This one arguably improves after sunset, when the city across the water is at its brightest.

The foreshore is a public park, so treat it like one at night. Stick to the lit paths, keep your phone charged for the ride home, and check the last ferry time before you settle in. If you miss the last boat, buses on Mill Point Road or a rideshare will get you back to the city.

## Picnics, cyclists and river etiquette

Evenings on the South Perth foreshore follow the Perth routine. People bring a picnic rug and takeaway, sit on the grass facing the city and stay until the light goes. Do the same. A rug, something warm for after sunset in the cooler months, and water are all you really need.

A few habits that keep you in step:

- **Keep left on the shared paths.** Cyclists move quickly along the Esplanade and expect walkers to stay to one side. Many ring a bell before passing.
- **Pay by card.** Transperth takes SmartRider cards and contactless bank cards on its services. The cafés on Mends Street take tap-and-go as standard.
- **Don't feed the birds.** The swans and pelicans are wildlife on a protected river. Bread does them harm.
- **Check the rules on the signs.** Drinking alcohol and lighting barbecues in public foreshore areas depend on local council by-laws. Read the posted signs before you open a bottle.
- **Respect the name.** Mindeerup is a Noongar-linked place on Whadjuk country. Take a minute with any cultural signage before you get the camera out.

## How long to give it

The view on its own takes twenty minutes. The way to do it properly takes longer: ferry across in the late afternoon, walk the Esplanade to Mill Point and back, then sit through sunset. That's two to three hours, finishing with a ride back to Elizabeth Quay under the lit-up towers you've been looking at all evening.

Opening hours won't limit you. The ferry timetable and the daylight will. Check both before you set out.
