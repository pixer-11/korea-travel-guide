## Inside the walls

High stone walls close around you here. Adelaide Gaol took its first prisoners in 1841, only five years after the colony was founded. It kept operating until 1988, which makes it one of the longest continuously running prisons in Australia.

Most of the city drives past it on the way to Port Road. That leaves the cell blocks and exercise yards surprisingly quiet for a heritage site this close to the CBD.

The appeal is how little the place has been softened. You walk the same corridors, look into the cramped cells and stand in the yards where inmates spent their days. The museum displays fill in 147 years of South Australian crime and punishment.

## The parts that stay with you

Some corners are quietly confronting. Executions were carried out at the gaol, and those prisoners were buried inside its walls. Look for the small initials and dates marking the graves; they are easy to walk past if you aren't watching for them.

Things to seek out:

- **The cell blocks.** Compare the earliest wings with the later additions to see how ideas about imprisonment changed.
- **The exercise yards.** They show the scale of daily life under guard.
- **The burial markers.** These are the gaol's bluntest reminder of capital punishment in the state.
- **Museum displays.** Escapes, notorious inmates and prison routines are covered here.

## Getting to Gaol Road

The gaol sits in the Park Lands between the city and Thebarton, close to Bonython Park and the River Torrens. From North Terrace it's roughly a 30-minute walk west along the riverbank path. The route is flat, shaded in stretches and passes the Adelaide Oval precinct.

You can also take the tram out along Port Road towards the Entertainment Centre and walk the last stretch. If you're driving, the gaol is just off Port Road.

Afterwards, Bonython Park has open lawns beside the river for a picnic, and Thebarton's pubs are a short walk west.

## Timing your visit and the tours

At the moment the gaol opens 10am–4pm, seven days a week. Museum hours shift through the year and on public holidays, so check the official site before you go. Last entry falls before closing, which means a 3:30pm arrival is too late to see it properly.

Plan on 90 minutes to two hours for a self-guided visit. Get there in the morning or by early afternoon. A first-thing arrival usually beats any school or tour groups that come later in the day.

Guided history tours add stories and context the signs can't. Ghost tours run separately in the evenings and must be booked ahead, as dates are limited. Check the official site for the current schedule and to buy tickets; prices are posted there too.

A few practical notes:

- Much of the site is outdoors, with exposed yards, so bring a hat in summer and a layer in winter.
- Some cells are small and dim; if you're claustrophobic, the yards and museum rooms are more open.
- Photography is generally welcome. The burial area is a grave site, so keep your voice down there.
