## A market hiding behind a doorway

From High Street, the entrance looks like a gap between grand Georgian frontages. Step through and the noise of the street drops away. You are in a covered hall of honey-coloured Bath stone, lined with small stalls under a roof that lets in daylight.

A market has traded on this spot for centuries. The building you walk through today is 19th century, with a domed central space that the stall rows lead into. Plenty of visitors walk straight past it on the way to the Abbey, which is exactly why it rewards a detour.

## What fills the stalls

The mix here is practical and small-scale rather than polished. You will find:

- **Deli counters** for picnic supplies: cheese, cured meats and things to eat on a bench by the river
- **Confectionery**, the kind of sweet stall where jars and bags do the selling
- **Secondhand and new books**, good for slow browsing
- **Pet supplies**, a reminder that this is a working market used by residents, not a showpiece
- **A café**, where you can sit with a cup of tea under the stone arches

Stall line-ups change over time, so treat this as the shape of the place rather than a fixed directory. Half the pleasure is the browsing itself. The aisles are narrow, the traders are close, and it takes only a few minutes to walk the full loop.

Look up as you go. The stonework and the light from above are as much a reason to come as anything on sale.

## Finding it in the city centre

The market sits on High Street, postcode BA2 4AW, right beside the Guildhall itself. That puts it in the tight cluster of Bath's best-known sights. Everything here is close enough to walk between in minutes.

- **Bath Abbey and the Roman Baths**: a short walk south along High Street
- **Pulteney Bridge**: just north, across the road and down towards the river
- **Victoria Art Gallery**: next door, on the corner by the bridge
- **Bath Spa railway station**: roughly a 10–15 minute walk south through the centre

There is a second way in from Grand Parade, the riverside side. Using both entrances turns the market into a handy shortcut between High Street and the views over the weir.

## Browsing the stalls the Bath way

This is a place for lingering, not ticking off. A few habits make it easier.

- **Pay as each stall prefers.** Most traders take cards, but small independent stalls sometimes prefer cash for small buys, so carry a little.
- **Ask before you handle.** Books and food are the traders' stock, and a quick question usually gets you a recommendation as well.
- **Don't tip at the counters.** Buying is the norm. At the café, leaving small change is a kindness, not an expectation.
- **Keep the aisles clear.** They are narrow, so step into a stall's space to look rather than stopping mid-walkway with a bag.

Timing is a matter of reasoning rather than measurement. Bath fills with day-trippers from late morning, so arriving soon after the 9am opening usually means calmer aisles. Saturdays tend to draw shoppers into the centre, which makes weekdays the easier choice.

## Planning around the hours

At the moment the market opens Monday to Saturday from 9am to 5pm. It is currently closed on Sundays, a costly surprise if you have built a weekend trip around it. Visit on Saturday instead, or plan a Sunday around the Abbey and the riverside.

Some individual stalls may keep shorter hours than the building, especially late in the afternoon. If there is one trader you want, go earlier in the day. Check the market's official listings before a special trip, as hours can change around public holidays.

## Putting it into a day

The market works best as a pause between bigger sights. A sensible loop starts at Bath Abbey and walks up High Street into the market. From there, leave through the Grand Parade side for Pulteney Bridge and the river.

Buy picnic food at the deli stalls, then carry it to Parade Gardens or along the riverbank. If the weather turns, the café gives you a dry seat under stone. Either way, it is a quieter half hour in a city centre that rarely offers one.
