## A village at the top of the switchbacks

Taiping is a reward for a climb. The village sits on a ridge above Meishan Township, and the road up winds through tea terraces and bamboo. When the hairpins stop, a small mountain settlement opens out on the slope with views back over the lowlands.

The old street is short and walkable. Most people come for three things together: the colonial-era shopfronts, the snacks sold along them, and the suspension bridge just outside the village. This is a well-known day trip from Chiayi, not a secret. Plan around the crowds rather than hoping to dodge them.

## Walking 太平老街

The street is known locally as 太平老街 (Tàipíng Lǎojiē). Its character comes from buildings put up during the Japanese colonial period. You'll see low facades, timber and plaster details, and ground floors opening straight onto the lane. Today most of them sell food, tea and souvenirs.

The street itself has no gate and no ticket. You can walk it any time, and Google currently lists it as open 24 hours. That only applies to the public lane, though. Each stall keeps its own hours, so arriving after dark mostly means shuttered fronts and a quiet mountain road.

Walk to one end and back slowly. Look up above the shop signs at the older upper storeys, which are easy to miss when you're watching the food. Then step off onto the side paths for views down the valley.

## The 36 Bends up from Meishan

The road is what makes this trip different. The drive from Meishan to Taiping is famous in Taiwan as the 36 Bends (三十六彎), a run of tight hairpins up a steep mountainside. Many visitors remember the drive as clearly as the street.

A few things help:

- **Go up in daylight.** The views over the plains are the point, and the curves are easier to read.
- **Use a low gear coming down** rather than riding the brakes the whole way.
- **Watch for scooters and cyclists** on the inside of the bends, especially at weekends.
- **If you get carsick, take something first.** The corners come one after another.

Without a car, look up Chiayi County bus routes or tourist shuttle services toward Meishan and Taiping before you go. Services into the hills are limited, so check the current timetable rather than turning up at the stop and hoping.

## Out onto the Taiping Suspension Bridge

The bridge is the big draw. The Taiping Suspension Bridge, known as 太平雲梯 (the "Taiping Cloud Ladder"), crosses a deep valley near the village. Its walkway hangs high above forested slopes. On clear days you can see across the Chiayi plain, and when the mountain mist rolls in, the far end can vanish into it.

The bridge runs separately from the old street. It has its own operating hours, its own entry arrangements and its own closures for weather and maintenance. Check the official Chiayi County tourism information for this month's times and entry details before you set your day around it.

The deck moves a little underfoot, as suspension bridges do. Anyone uneasy with heights should try the first section before committing to the full crossing.

## Snacks, tea and mountain produce

You eat as you walk here. Stalls along the lane sell grilled snacks, sweets and drinks to carry. Meishan Township is tea country, and the hills around Taiping grow high-mountain tea, so tea shops and packaged leaf are a constant along the street.

Some easy ways to graze:

- **Start with something hot** from a grill stall. The ridge is usually cooler than Chiayi city.
- **Taste before you buy tea.** Many sellers will pour a sample, and the leaf varies a lot in style.
- **Pick up local produce** like fruit or dried goods if you see it. Small mountain villages often sell what's grown on the slopes around them.

Stock and menus change with the season and the vendor, so take what looks freshest rather than hunting for one particular item.

## Paying, parking and patience on the ridge

A few habits make the day easier.

**Carry cash.** Small stalls on Taiwan's old streets often take cash only, so don't count on paying everything by card or app. Tipping isn't expected at food stalls.

**Hold on to your rubbish.** Public bins are scarce in Taiwan. People usually carry wrappers and cups until they find a vendor's bin or get back to the car.

**Arrive early for parking.** Space is tight on a mountain ridge. Popular sites in Taiwan fill up through the late morning on weekends and public holidays, so an early start usually means an easier spot and a calmer lane.

**Order simply.** Pointing and holding up fingers works at most stalls. Saying 這個 (zhège, "this one") and 謝謝 (xièxie, "thank you") goes a long way.

**Pack a layer.** Even in warm months, the air at Taiping can feel noticeably fresher than the plains. Mist and light rain can roll in quickly.

## Building a Meishan day around it

The village works best as part of a hill day. Walk the old street, cross the bridge, then drive back down through Meishan Township. Meishan Park and the town centre at the foot of the bends make an easy stop on the way to Chiayi city.

Allow two to three hours at Taiping itself, plus driving time each way. If you have more than a day in the area, the Alishan National Scenic Area lies deeper into the same mountains. Taiping makes a good shorter outing when Alishan's longer trip won't fit.
