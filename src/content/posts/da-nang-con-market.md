## What Con Market actually is

Con Market, known locally as Chợ Cồn, is Da Nang's largest traditional market, a multi-storey concrete building at 90 Hùng Vương in Hải Châu district. Thousands of individual vendor stalls pack the ground floor and upper levels: fresh produce, raw meat and seafood, dry goods, fabric, shoes, kitchenware, souvenirs.

It isn't a curated food hall or a tourist-built night market. It's the place Da Nang residents have shopped for decades, which means narrow aisles, stacked crates, and vendors calling out prices rather than posted menus. Expect it to feel dense and a little chaotic on first entry.

## Finding your way through the floors

The building is organized loosely by category, though the boundaries blur fast once you're inside.

- Ground floor: fresh produce, meat, seafood, cooked food stalls
- Ground floor rear: dried goods, spices, coffee beans
- Upper floor: clothing, fabric, shoes, souvenirs, housewares

There's no signage system for tourists, so the fastest way to orient yourself is to pick one edge of the building and walk its perimeter first before diving into the interior aisles.

## The breakfast noodle stalls

The ground-floor food section is where Con Market earns its reputation beyond shopping. Rows of small stalls serve bowls of mì Quảng, bún mắm, and bánh canh cooked to order over portable burners, eaten standing or perched on plastic stools at shared metal tables. This is Da Nang's everyday breakfast circuit, not a display for visitors, so stalls open early and start winding down their broth by mid-morning. If you want a specific bowl of mì Quảng rather than whatever's still simmering, arrive well before 9am.

## Bargaining on the clothing floor

Upstairs, prices on clothing, fabric, and souvenirs are rarely fixed. Vendors expect a counter-offer, and the opening price quoted to a foreign visitor is usually higher than what a local pays. A polite, unhurried back-and-forth is normal and not considered rude; walking away is a legitimate negotiating move and often brings the price down before you've left the stall. Cash, preferably Vietnamese dong in small denominations, is the default currency here; card readers are essentially nonexistent among individual stall vendors.

## Beating the crowds, not the market

Con Market is a very popular stop on Da Nang itineraries, not a quiet backstreet find, so the aisles fill with both shoppers and tour groups through the middle of the day. Weekday mornings right at the 7am opening are calmer, before both local shoppers and visiting groups build up. Late afternoon, roughly the last hour or two before the 7pm close, is another workable window, when some stalls are already packing up but the aisles have thinned out. Weekends are the busiest stretch overall, so if your schedule allows it, save Con Market for a weekday.

## What the stall system expects of you

Nobody hands you a shopping basket or a receipt here. Each stall is its own small business, so you pay that vendor directly and in cash before moving to the next one.

- Bring small dong notes; large bills slow down every transaction
- A short phrase of Vietnamese, even just "cảm ơn" (thank you), goes further than English
- Point at what you want if language stalls; vendors are used to this
- Don't photograph a stallholder's face or goods up close without asking first

Wear shoes you don't mind getting slightly wet or muddy near the seafood and meat sections; the floors there are hosed down periodically through the day.

## Getting there and making it a loop

Con Market sits on Hùng Vương street in Hải Châu, close enough to the Han River and Da Nang's central hotel strip to reach on foot from many downtown stays, or by a short taxi or Grab ride from further out. It pairs naturally with a walk along the Han River or a visit to the Da Nang Cathedral (Con Ga Church), both a short distance away, so it works well as a mid-morning stop before the afternoon heat sets in.

Plan on 45 minutes for a quick pass through the food and produce sections, or up to two hours if you're also working through the clothing and housewares floors.
