## Scala, the building on the slope above King's Cross

The building began as a picture house in the 1920s. You can still tell. The façade on the corner of Pentonville Road is pale and solid in the way of interwar cinemas. Inside, the levels stack up the way a cinema's tiers do, not the way a purpose-built rock club spreads out.

In the 1980s the Scala was London's best-known repertory cinema, famous for all-night film marathons. It reopened as a live music venue in 1999. Since then it has been a regular stop for international acts playing their first London shows.

Here is the layout as most gig-goers find it:

- **Main floor:** standing, facing a raised stage, where the most committed fans gather.
- **Upper levels:** balconies looking down on the stage, the best view for anyone who wants sightlines rather than the crush.
- **Bars:** on more than one level, so drinks queues are spread out.
