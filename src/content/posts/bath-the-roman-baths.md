## Steam over green water

Hot water still rises here, as it has for two thousand years. The Great Bath is a rectangular open-air pool of opaque green spring water, lined with lead the Romans laid. Stone columns and Victorian statues of Roman emperors and governors line the terrace above it.

From that terrace you look down on the pool before the route drops to the original Roman pavement at water level. The spring feeding it comes out of the ground at around 46°C. That heat is why the surface steams on cool mornings.

## What the route covers

The visit is one-way, underground and indoors for most of its length. Key stops:

- **The Sacred Spring**, where the hot water bubbles up in an orange-stained reservoir
- **The Temple of Sulis Minerva**, reconstructed from its surviving carved pediment, including the Gorgon's head
- **The gilt bronze head of Minerva**, the museum's best-known single object
- **The curse tablets**, small lead sheets on which Romans scratched pleas to the goddess against thieves
- **The East and West Baths**, with the exposed hypocaust pillars that held up heated floors

An audio guide is included, with a separate children's track. At the end you can taste the spa water, which is warm and has a distinctly mineral taste. The Georgian Pump Room restaurant sits above the baths and connects to the exit.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

This is one of the most visited sites in England, and weekends show it. On weekdays it stays comparatively calm across the full opening hours, 9am to 6pm. On weekends only 9am to 10am is quiet.

Try not to arrive between 11am and 5pm on a Saturday or Sunday. That is when the terrace fills and the narrow galleries around the temple slow to a shuffle. The site opens 9am to 6pm every day of the week. Last entry comes before closing, so check the official website for the current cut-off.

Two hours covers the route at a steady pace with the audio guide. Budget more if you listen to every track.

## Getting through without the shuffle

- **Book a timed ticket online.** Entry is by time slot, and walk-up slots on busy days can sell out. Prices are listed on the official Roman Baths website.
- **Don't touch the Great Bath water.** It is untreated and unsafe to drink or bathe in. The tasting fountain at the end is the only water meant for visitors.
- **Want to actually swim?** Head to Thermae Bath Spa, a few minutes' walk away on Hot Bath Street, which draws on the same thermal source.
- **Pair it with the neighbours.** Bath Abbey faces the entrance across Abbey Churchyard, and Pulteney Bridge is under ten minutes' walk north-east.
- **Getting there:** Bath Spa railway station is roughly a ten-minute walk south along Manvers Street and Stall Street. The city centre is compact, and driving in is slow, so park-and-ride or the train makes more sense.
- **Accessibility:** much of the route has lifts and ramps, but some areas at the Roman level are uneven. Check the official access page before you go.
