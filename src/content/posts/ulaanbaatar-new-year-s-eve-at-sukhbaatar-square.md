## A stage, a statue and a midnight sky

Ulaanbaatar's main square turns into a free open-air concert for one night. A stage goes up on the granite paving and Mongolian pop and rock acts play to a standing crowd. The night builds to a midnight fireworks display over the Chinggis Khaan monument.

That monument is a huge seated bronze Chinggis set into the colonnade of the Government Palace, on the square's north side. When the fireworks go up behind it, the whole crowd is facing the same way. The equestrian statue of Damdin Sükhbaatar stands in the middle of the square, and it makes a useful landmark when you lose your group.

## Dates, tickets and the venue

The celebration runs from the evening of December 31, 2026 into January 1, 2027. Entry is free and there's no ticketing: you walk in from the surrounding streets.

Sukhbaatar Square sits at the centre of the city, just north of Peace Avenue (Enkh Taivan Avenue). The buildings around it double as orientation points:

- **North:** the Government Palace and the Chinggis Khaan monument
- **East:** the salmon-pink State Opera and Ballet Theatre, and the Cultural Palace
- **Southwest:** the Central Post Office and the glass sail of the Blue Sky Tower
- **Northwest:** the National Museum of Mongolia, a short walk away

Ulaanbaatar has no metro. Cars on Peace Avenue tend to jam on big nights, so visitors staying anywhere central usually walk to the square. Hotels on Peace Avenue and in the blocks around the State Department Store are within walking distance.

## Dressing for a Mongolian midnight

Late-December nights in Ulaanbaatar regularly fall well below −20°C, and you'll be standing on stone for hours. Phones lose charge quickly in that cold, and fingers stop working on touchscreens.

People who stay through to the fireworks usually wear:

- a thermal base layer under a down jacket that covers the hips
- insulated boots with thick soles, because the paving draws heat out of your feet
- a hat that covers the ears, plus a scarf or neck gaiter pulled up over the nose
- mittens over thin liner gloves
- a power bank kept in an inside pocket

Many people move between the square and the warm lobbies and cafés along Peace Avenue during the concert. They tend to come back out for the countdown.

## Saying Shine Jil like Ulaanbaatar does

Mongolians call this holiday **Shine Jil** (New Year). It is separate from Tsagaan Sar, the lunar new year, which comes later in winter. For many families, 31 December means a big meal and gifts at home. Plenty of people eat first and come to the square later in the evening, so the crowd usually gets bigger as midnight gets closer.

The greeting you'll hear is *Shine oni mend hürgeye*, which means "New Year greetings". Saying it back, even badly, goes down well.

Mongolian New Year has its own symbols. You'll see Övlön Övgön, the Mongolian Grandfather Frost in a blue or white deel, and decorated fir trees around the square and the Government Palace. People take photos in front of both, and you'll usually see a short queue at each.

Card payment is normal in central Ulaanbaatar, and mobile banking apps are used everywhere. Street vendors and small kiosks near the square may still want cash in tögrög. After midnight, people at the back of the crowd usually head off first along Peace Avenue. Ride-hailing and taxis are hard to find for the first hour, so most central guests walk back to their hotels.
