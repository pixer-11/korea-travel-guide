## Six members, one tiered hall

iKON were scheduled to play Tokyo Garden Theater in November 2026. The group debuted under YG Entertainment in 2015 and moved to 143 Entertainment in 2023. The current six-member line-up is Jay, Song, Bobby, DK, Ju-ne and Chan.

Japan has been a core market for iKON since their early arena years. Tokyo audiences know the catalogue well, from "Rhythm Ta" to "Love Scenario".

The venue opened in 2020 and holds roughly 8,000. It is a theatre, not an arena. Seats rise in steep balconies wrapped around the stage, so even the upper tiers look down onto the performers rather than across a long floor.

## Reaching Ariake

Tokyo Garden Theater is in Ariake, Koto ward, on the reclaimed waterfront next to Odaiba. Two stations serve it:

- **Ariake-tennis-no-mori Station** (Yurikamome line): the closest, linked to the complex by an elevated walkway.
- **Kokusai-tenjijo Station** (Rinkai Line): a slightly longer walk, with through trains to Osaki and the JR Saikyo Line.
- **Shimbashi Station**: where most fans board the Yurikamome. It is the driverless line that crosses Rainbow Bridge.

After concerts here, Yurikamome platforms fill up quickly. Many attendees walk to Kokusai-tenjijo instead, where Rinkai trains run more often and carry more people.

## Tickets, entry and the venue's own rules

Japanese K-pop ticketing usually works through lotteries rather than first-come sales. Fan club members typically get the first draw, followed by general lotteries on the major Japanese platforms. Tickets for this tour were sold through the tour's Japanese ticketing channels.

Entry tends to work like this in Tokyo:

- Tickets are usually digital, held in a smartphone app and tied to the buyer's name.
- Staff often run photo ID checks at the gate, and resale outside official trade systems is commonly void.
- Seat numbers sometimes appear on the ticket only on the day.

Photography and filming during shows are normally prohibited at Japanese concerts, and staff enforce it. Large banners that block sightlines are generally banned as well.

## How Tokyo iKONICs spend show day

Merchandise lines at Ariake shows often form hours before doors. Japanese fans typically queue in the marked lanes staff set up outside and keep strictly to their place. Official light sticks (Konbat) are the standard accessory, and many fans carry a spare battery.

Inside, Japanese K-pop crowds sing fan chants loudly but stay in their assigned seats. Leaving by block when staff direct it (規制退場, kisei taijō) is normal practice, so the exit is slow but orderly.

The Ariake Garden mall in the same complex has food courts, cafés and a supermarket, which is where most fans eat before shows. Hotel Villa Fontaine Grand Tokyo-Ariake connects directly to the theatre and is the obvious overnight stay. Tokyo Big Sight and Ariake Arena are a short walk away. Odaiba's waterfront and shopping malls are two or three Yurikamome stops north.

Japanese cash is still handy at some merch stalls, though IC cards such as Suica cover trains and most mall purchases.
