## Race morning on the Queen's Necklace

The 22nd Tata Mumbai Marathon runs on January 17, 2027. It is Asia's largest marathon and the continent's biggest mass-participation sporting event. For one Sunday morning, the city's arterial roads belong to runners, not taxis.

The races start in the dark because Mumbai's humidity builds quickly after sunrise. The full marathon heads north from the CSMT area, along the curve of Marine Drive, past Haji Ali and across the Bandra-Worli Sea Link. Then it turns back toward the finish at Azad Maidan. The sea link closes to traffic for the race, so the only way across it that morning is on foot, wearing a bib.

## Entries, categories and the official site

Organiser Procam International publishes the dates, race categories and registration links on the official Tata Mumbai Marathon site. Entries are the event's version of tickets. Categories in recent editions have included:

- Full marathon (42.195 km)
- Half marathon (21.097 km)
- Dream Run, a shorter, festive fun run
- Senior Citizens' Run
- Champions with Disability run

The race carries a World Athletics label. That status brings elite East African and Indian fields to the front of the start, with tens of thousands of amateurs filling in behind them. Entry fees appear on the official site; this guide names no figure.

## Where to stand and how to get there

Spectators typically line Marine Drive, where the promenade wall gives a long, unobstructed view of the field against the Arabian Sea. Pedder Road and the stretch near Haji Ali draw crowds too. The finish zone around Azad Maidan gets the densest crowds as runners come in.

Road closures cover much of South Mumbai on race morning, so most people travel by the suburban railway:

- **CSMT** (Central and Harbour lines) for the start and finish area
- **Churchgate** and **Marine Lines** (Western line) for Marine Drive
- **Mahalaxmi** (Western line) for the Haji Ali section

Visitors usually stay in Colaba, Fort or Churchgate, all within walking distance of the course. Hotels near the course fill quickly that weekend.

## Cheering the Mumbai way

Spectating here is participatory. Residents along the route usually hand out water, orange slices, biscuits and bananas. Housing societies and colleges set up drum groups and dance troupes at the roadside, and the noise picks up noticeably along Marine Drive.

The marathon is also one of India's largest charity fundraising events. Many runners wear the T-shirts of NGOs they are raising money for, and costumed charity groups fill the Dream Run. Shouting a runner's name off their bib is normal and welcome.

A few habits keep the morning smooth:

- Stay behind the barricades; marshals are strict, especially on the Sea Link approaches.
- Carry small cash or UPI on your phone for chai and breakfast stalls near the stations.
- Expect the trains to fill with runners in foil blankets after the finish, and plan a slow ride out.

After the race, the Kala Ghoda art district and the Fort area's Irani cafés are a short walk from Azad Maidan. Both are popular places for a late breakfast.
