## Sixteen winners, one table's worth of nerves

Nobody gets into this event just by being ranked high. The Champion of Champions field is 16 players who won significant titles over the past year. That makes it one of the few tournaments where every match is between people who have recently lifted a trophy.

The 2026 edition was set for 9 to 15 November at Leicester Arena: seven days of snooker in a Midlands city that is easy to reach by train. The arena puts spectators closer to the baize than a TV picture ever does. From the seats you can see a player chalk the cue, study the angle from both ends of the table and settle into the stance.

## How the group days work

The format is what sets this tournament apart. For years the event has split its 16 players into four groups of four. Each group plays out across its own day, and only one player comes out of it.

A group day usually has two opening matches. Their winners then meet in a group final, and the last player standing goes through to the semi-finals. In practice:

- **Early in the week**, each ticket covers a self-contained story with a winner at the end of it
- **Group finals** are often the tensest matches of the week, because a whole day's work rides on them
- **The closing weekend** brings the semi-finals and the final, played over longer distances

Fans who can only manage one day often pick a group day for the variety. Fans who want the trophy moment go for the final.

## Inside Leicester Arena

The arena turns into a snooker hall for the tournament week. Tiered seating banks around the playing area, with low light on the crowd and a bright pool of light on the table. A referee in white gloves re-spots the colours, and a scoreboard tracks every frame.

Snooker crowds sit very quietly. During a shot you can hear the click of ball on ball and the soft thud of a pot into a pocket. The applause comes between shots and swells after a big break, so seat position matters. Higher seats show the whole table like a diagram, and lower ones put you level with the players.

## Watching without getting the evil eye

The etiquette here is stricter than at almost any other sport with paying spectators. People take it seriously, and stewards enforce it.

- **Spectators stay still while a player is on a shot.** People leave their seats and come back only between frames, when ushers usually open the doors.
- **Phones go fully silent.** A ringtone in a silent arena gets heard by the player, the referee and the TV microphones.
- **No flash photography.** It distracts players and is not allowed during play.
- **Good pots and big breaks get applause** once the ball has dropped, never while it is still rolling.
- **Voices stay low** when the table is live. Comments wait for the mid-session interval.

Regulars usually take their seats before a session begins rather than mid-frame, because getting in during play can mean a long wait at the door. Sessions also mean sitting still for hours, so regulars tend to dress in layers.

## Getting to Leicester in November

Leicester railway station is on the Midland Main Line. East Midlands Railway runs direct trains from London St Pancras that take about an hour, and there are direct trains from Sheffield and Nottingham too. Most visitors coming from London do the trip as a day return, or stay one night for an evening session.

From the station, fans tend to take a taxi or the local buses to the arena rather than walk with bags. Mid-November in the English Midlands is cold, often wet, and dark by late afternoon. Evening sessions finish long after nightfall.

## Filling the gaps between sessions

Leicester has more to do than most visitors expect, and much of it sits within walking distance of the centre.

- **King Richard III Visitor Centre** tells the story of the king's remains, found under a city car park in 2012. The excavation site itself is on view.
- **Leicester Cathedral**, just across the way, holds his reinterred tomb.
- **National Space Centre**, north of the centre, is easy to spot by its tall rocket tower.
- **Leicester Market** is one of the largest covered outdoor markets in the country, and close to the shops at Highcross.
