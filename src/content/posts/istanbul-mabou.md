Five tables. That number shapes everything about an evening at Mabou, and it is the main thing to plan around. A room this size seats only a handful of parties a night. Everyone eats from one tasting menu set by the chef, not from a long list of choices.

Mabou sits in Beyoğlu, the hilly district above the Golden Horn that holds İstiklal Avenue, Galata Tower and some of Istanbul's most ambitious restaurants. A German chef runs it and pairs Turkish ingredients with French technique. That mix has made it one of the city's most talked-about tables, and one of the hardest to get.

## Five tables, one menu

A tasting menu takes the decisions away from you. You don't pick dishes. You sit down, and the kitchen sends a sequence of courses it has thought through from start to finish. At a place with five tables, the chef is cooking for a room small enough to keep track of every plate.

This guide won't list dishes. The menu isn't published here, and tasting menus usually change with what the market has that week. What stays the same is the format: several small courses in a fixed order, served at a pace that fills an evening, not a quick meal.

## Turkish produce, French grammar

The idea is easy to sum up. The ingredients come from Turkey, a country with one of the deepest larders in the Mediterranean world. The methods come from the French kitchen, with its sauces, reductions and exact timing.

For a visitor, that makes Mabou a different kind of meal from the meyhane or kebab house down the street. You will taste flavours you may know from the bazaars, handled by a cook trained in another tradition. It works best as a contrast. Eat the classics elsewhere in the city during your stay, then come here to see the same produce done another way.

## Getting a seat at one of five tables

Booking is the hard part, so plan it before you plan anything else. A few things help:

- **Reserve as early as your dates allow.** With five tables, a single good review can fill weeks of bookings.
- **Mention dietary needs when you book, not at the table.** A fixed menu means the kitchen has to prepare substitutions ahead of time.
- **Confirm the address and hours directly.** Mabou opened recently. Details like service nights can change, and this guide does not list them.
- **Arrive on time.** In a small room working through one menu, a late table holds up the whole sequence of courses.
- **If plans change, cancel early.** Someone on a waiting list can use that table, and small kitchens buy produce based on covers.

In Turkish restaurants, a tip of around 10 percent is the usual custom when service isn't already on the bill. Check the receipt for a service line before you add anything. Cards are widely accepted across Beyoğlu. Still, ask about payment when you confirm your booking if it matters to you.

## Reaching Beyoğlu

Beyoğlu is easy to reach. The M2 metro stops at both Şişhane and Taksim, at the two ends of the neighbourhood. If you're coming from the old city, take the T1 tram to Karaköy. From there, ride the Tünel, the short underground funicular that has run up the hill since 1875, to the southern end of İstiklal Avenue.

The streets here are steep, cobbled in places, and often unsigned. Get the exact address with your confirmation and drop a pin before you leave. Taxis can struggle in the narrow lanes after dark. Walking the last few minutes from Şişhane or the top of the Tünel is usually quicker than sitting in traffic.

## An evening built around the booking

The dinner is the main event, but Beyoğlu gives you plenty to fill the hours around it.

- **Before dinner:** Galata Tower stands on the slope just below. Climb it for the view across the Golden Horn to the minarets of the old city.
- **Earlier in the day:** The Pera Museum, near Şişhane, holds Orientalist painting, including Osman Hamdi Bey's *The Tortoise Trainer*.
- **A walk to work up an appetite:** Walk İstiklal Avenue from Tünel toward Taksim. You'll pass the historic red tram and the arcaded passages that branch off the street.
- **After dinner:** Walk downhill to Karaköy and the Galata Bridge, where the Bosphorus lights reflect off the water.

Leave plenty of time for the meal itself. Tasting menus are slow on purpose, and you shouldn't book a show or a late ferry straight after.

## Who should go, and who might skip it

Mabou suits travellers who plan their trips around food. It's also good for couples marking an occasion, and for repeat visitors to Istanbul who have done the lokanta lunches and fish sandwiches and want to see where the city's cooking is heading. A room with five tables is quiet and personal in a way large dining rooms can't be.

It's a poor fit if you want a menu you can choose from, a quick bite before a night out, or something easy to arrange on the day. It also takes commitment. You book well ahead, you give your evening to the kitchen, and you let a chef from outside Turkey show you Turkish ingredients in a new way.
