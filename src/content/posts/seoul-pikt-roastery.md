## A roaster on Mangwon's side streets

Mangwon rewards slow walking. The district sits in Mapo-gu, one subway stop west of Hapjeong. Its low-rise residential lanes hold more small, independent cafés than most Seoul neighbourhoods its size.

PIKT Roastery is one of them. It roasts its own coffee and keeps the room minimalist. In 2026 it landed on a "Korea Coffee Hot Place" list, which is what pulled it out of the neighbourhood and onto out-of-towners' maps.

It is a recent opening and still trending. Treat the details below as a starting point, and confirm opening hours and any reservation policy on the café's Naver Map listing or social channels before you go.

## What to put on the tray

Two items made PIKT's name, and most first-timers order both:

- **Black Latte**: the house signature drink. The name doesn't tell you much about the recipe, so ask at the counter how it's built and whether it comes hot or iced.
- **Matcha Basque cheesecake**: Basque cheesecake is the crustless style baked hot enough to darken the top, with a soft, almost custardy centre. This one is made with matcha, so expect green tea bitterness against the cream cheese.

Since the shop roasts its own beans, a plain espresso or filter is the cleanest way to taste the roasting itself. What's on offer changes, so look at the board rather than going in with a fixed order.

## Finding the door

Take Seoul Subway **Line 6 to Mangwon Station**. Exit 2 points you toward Mangwon Market, the neighbourhood's main landmark, and the café streets spread out from there. Hapjeong Station, on Lines 2 and 6, is a short walk away if you're coming from Hongdae.

The summary of PIKT that reached us calls it "tucked away", and that's the right expectation. Don't navigate with Google Maps. Its walking directions in Korea are patchy at best.

Use **Naver Map** or **KakaoMap**, searching the café's name in English or Korean. Both give turn-by-turn walking routes through the side lanes. A T-money card covers the subway ride.

## Ordering and sitting, Seoul café style

Seoul's café culture has its own etiquette, and PIKT works within it.

- **No tipping.** Korea doesn't do it, and leaving cash on the table usually just confuses staff.
- **Pay by card.** Foreign credit cards and contactless payment are standard in Seoul cafés.
- **One person, one drink.** If you plan to sit, each person in your group is expected to order something. Sharing one latte between three at a table is considered bad form.
- **Say whether you're staying.** Staff often ask "for here or to go?" The answer matters, since disposable cups are restricted for dine-in in many places.
- **Return your tray.** In many Seoul cafés you carry your cups and plates back to the counter when you leave.

Small, list-featured cafés in Mapo tend to fill up on weekend afternoons, when people from all over the city do café hops. Arriving early in the day, or on a weekday, is a reasonable bet if you want a seat.

## Building an afternoon around it

PIKT is one stop on a Mangwon loop, not the whole trip. The neighbourhood's other draws are within walking distance:

- **Mangwon Market**: a covered traditional market known for cheap street snacks, including fried chicken and croquettes. Eat here first, then save the cheesecake for dessert.
- **Mangwon Hangang Park**: the riverside park on the Han, roughly fifteen minutes' walk south. A coffee to go fits a sunset walk along the water.
- **Hapjeong and Hongdae**: a stop or two east for record shops, bars and the busier university-area streets.

Allow about an hour at the café itself. Give the whole Mangwon walk half a day.

## Before you set out

Because PIKT is newly trending, the details change fast. Hours, holiday closures, seasonal drinks and whether a slice of that cheesecake is left by late afternoon can all shift.

Check the Naver listing or the café's social accounts on the day. Korean cafés often post closures and sold-out notices there first. If either signature item is the reason you're crossing the city, go earlier rather than later.
