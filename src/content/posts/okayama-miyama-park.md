## A pond, a playground and paths under trees

Miyama Park (深山公園, Miyama Kōen) sits on the wooded slopes above Tamano. This is the port city at the southern tip of Okayama Prefecture. Below it, ferries leave Uno Port for Naoshima.

The park is a local mainstay rather than a showpiece. Its focal point is the duck pond, ringed by walking paths. Families head straight for the playground equipment. Everyone else follows the tree-lined trails, which make a good loop if you have an hour or two.

The planting changes with the seasons:

- **Spring:** cherry blossom brings picnic tarps out under the trees.
- **Summer:** the tree canopy gives you shade on the paths.
- **Autumn:** maple colour around the water draws photographers.

At the park's edge is Miyama English Garden, which keeps its own hours. Check its official site before you plan around it.

## Getting up to Tai

The address is 2-chōme-4490 Tai, Tamano. The JR Uno Line runs from Okayama Station down to Uno Station in Tamano, and the trip takes roughly an hour. The park is inland and uphill from the line, so from the station it's easiest to take a taxi or drive.

Most local visitors come by car. On busy weekends the car parks fill along with the paths. Make it a half-day by pairing the park with Uno Port's waterfront, or catch a ferry to Naoshima afterwards.

## Timing around the weekend crowd

The grounds are currently listed as open 24 hours. Measured foot traffic shows weekends stay busy from 8am to 5pm. On a Saturday or Sunday, that covers every daylight hour you'd want for walking.

If you can, go on a weekday instead. The quietest measured windows come late:

- **Weekdays:** 10pm–11pm
- **Weekends:** 9pm–11pm

After dark the paths are a different place. Bring a torch, and don't expect a sightseeing night walk. For the pond and the trees, a weekday morning is the practical choice. If a weekend is your only option, the busy period ends at 5pm, so a late-afternoon visit is better than arriving at 8am with every family in Tamano.

## Picnic manners at Miyama Kōen

The etiquette is the same as in any Japanese municipal park, and people here follow it.

- **Carry your rubbish out.** Bins are scarce in Japanese parks, so bring a bag for your trash.
- **Tarps at hanami.** People spread blue sheets under the cherry trees and take their shoes off before stepping onto them. Don't walk across someone else's sheet.
- **The ducks.** Read the signs by the pond before you feed anything. Rules on feeding wildlife differ from park to park.
- **Keep the volume down** near the playground and the paths. Speakers playing music get noticed.
- **Bring supplies.** There are few shops on the hillside, so buy drinks and snacks at a konbini in town on the way up.

To ask a taxi driver for the park, say "Miyama Kōen made" ("to Miyama Park").
