## Thrash on a Monday in December

Slayer plays Movistar Arena on December 14, 2026. That date is a Monday, and in Buenos Aires it falls in early summer, so the city is warm late into the evening.

This is the band behind *Reign in Blood* (1986), *South of Heaven* and *Seasons in the Abyss*. "Raining Blood" and "Angel of Death" are the songs that made their name. Slayer formed in Huntington Park, California, in 1981 and ended its touring years with a farewell world tour that closed in 2019. A Buenos Aires arena date now is a return few fans expected.

There is a local angle too. Singer and bassist Tom Araya was born in Viña del Mar, Chile, so for South American crowds a Slayer show has always felt partly like a homecoming.

## Inside Movistar Arena on Humboldt

Movistar Arena is a purpose-built indoor arena on Calle Humboldt in Villa Crespo. It sits next to the ground of Club Atlético Atlanta, the neighbourhood's football club. It is a covered, climate-controlled hall, unlike the open-air football stadiums Buenos Aires uses for its biggest rock shows.

For a thrash show that matters. The sound stays inside the bowl instead of drifting away over a pitch. The tiered seating curves close around the floor, so even the upper rows look straight down into the pit.

The ticket categories you'll usually see are:

- **Campo**: the standing floor in front of the stage, where the pit forms
- **Plateas**: numbered seats in the tiers around the floor
- Accessible spaces, sold through the venue's own channels

## Getting to Villa Crespo

Most people get there on the Subte. Line B runs under Avenida Corrientes, and **Dorrego** and **Malabia** stations are both a walk of a few blocks from the arena. Leaving the train, you head along the residential streets towards Humboldt. On show nights you just follow the crowd in black T-shirts.

Taxis and ride-hailing apps such as Uber and Cabify also work, but the streets around the arena are narrow and clog up as the show ends. Attendees going home by car usually walk a few blocks towards Avenida Corrientes or Avenida Córdoba before calling one. The Subte stops running at night, so a late finish can mean a bus, a taxi or a walk into Palermo.

## Getting through the doors on Humboldt

Movistar Arena lists its events, ticket links and entry details on its official website. Tickets in Argentina are usually digital and tied to the buyer's ID. Fans typically carry the same passport or DNI used at purchase, along with the ticket on a charged phone.

- Bring little: large bags and anything that looks like a weapon get stopped at the search

Tipping isn't part of arena bars. Taxis are usually rounded up to the nearest convenient note.

## The pogo and the chant

Argentine metal crowds have a reputation, and a Slayer pit in Buenos Aires earns it. The local word for moshing is *pogo*, and the floor at the front of the Campo turns into one from the first song.

Between songs the crowd chants. You hear "olé, olé, olé" rolling around the tiers, sometimes with the band's name in it, as at football matches and every big rock show in the city. Joining in is expected.

Pit etiquette here matches other thrash crowds: pick fallen people up straight away and lift crowd-surfers forward. Fans who don't want to pogo stand towards the back of the Campo or sit in the plateas, where the view is better anyway.

## Summer heat in the Campo

December evenings in Buenos Aires are warm and often humid, and a packed standing floor gets much hotter. Regulars on the floor wear light layers, not leather jackets, and keep a jacket for the walk home if anything.

Water and soft drinks are sold inside. Those who stay in the pit for the whole set usually step out between songs to drink something rather than wait until the encore.

## Before and after in Villa Crespo

The arena is in a neighbourhood people actually live in, which makes it an easy place to spend the hours before a show. Villa Crespo borders Palermo Soho, and its streets have bars, cafés and *parrillas* (grill restaurants) where a steak or a *choripán* is the default order.

Along Avenida Corrientes near Dorrego and Malabia there are pizzerias in the Buenos Aires style: thick, cheesy and sold by the slice at the counter. On event nights, grill stalls outside venues are a familiar sight across the city.

Visitors from abroad often stay in Palermo, one neighbourhood over. From there it's a walk or a short ride to the arena and back, which helps after midnight when the Subte has stopped.
