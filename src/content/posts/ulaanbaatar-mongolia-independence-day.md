## A winter date with 1911

December 29 marks the day in 1911 when Mongolia declared independence from the collapsing Qing dynasty. The Bogd Khan, the Buddhist leader in Urga (today's Ulaanbaatar), was proclaimed head of the new state. The 2026 observance falls on Tuesday, December 29.

The day carries state and public commemorations in the capital. It is a national holiday, so government offices and banks close. It also sits two days before New Year, which Mongolians celebrate on a large scale, so the city is already in holiday mode.

## Sükhbaatar Square and the Government Palace

There is no single ticketed venue. The civic centre of Ulaanbaatar is Sükhbaatar Square, also called Chinggis Square, the broad paved plaza off Peace Avenue (Enkhtaivan Avenue). Its north side is the Government Palace, with a seated bronze Chinggis Khaan in its colonnaded front.

Several sites put the date in context, all within walking distance or a short taxi ride:

- **National Museum of Mongolia**, just west of the square, covers the 1911 period and the twentieth century.
- **Bogd Khan Palace Museum**, south of the centre toward Zaisan, is the winter palace of the man proclaimed ruler in 1911.
- **Gandantegchinlen Monastery**, west of the centre, is the city's main working monastery.

Museums keep their own holiday schedules, which change through the year; each posts its hours on its official site.

## Dressing for the coldest capital

Ulaanbaatar is the coldest national capital on earth, and late December is deep winter. Daytime temperatures commonly sit well below minus 20°C. Winter smog from coal and ger-district stoves often hangs over the valley on still days.

People standing outdoors for any length of time typically wear:

- a down parka that covers the hips
- insulated boots with thick soles, since paving gets icy
- a hat that covers the ears, plus a face covering
- mittens over thin gloves, so a phone stays usable
- an N95-type mask on smoggy days

Phone batteries drain fast in this cold. Visitors usually keep a spare in an inside pocket.

## Moving through the city on a holiday

Most visitors fly into Chinggis Khaan International Airport, roughly 50 km south of the city, and take a taxi or hotel transfer to the centre. Hotels on and around Peace Avenue put the square within a short walk.

Traffic in Ulaanbaatar is heavy, and holiday-week shopping for New Year adds to it. Locals often walk central distances instead of sitting in a car. Ride-hailing apps and registered taxis both operate. Cards are widely accepted in central shops, hotels and restaurants. Tögrög cash is handy for small kiosks and markets.

Mongolians treat the national flag and state symbols with real respect. Visitors usually keep a quiet distance from official ceremonies and avoid climbing on monuments for photos. Photographing soldiers or officials up close without a nod of permission goes down badly. In Mongolian the occasion is often shortened to *Tusgaar Togtnoliin Ödör* (Independence Day). Greeting someone with *Sain baina uu* goes a long way.

Warming up between sites is part of the day. Busy cafés and *guanz* canteens near the square serve hot milk tea (*süütei tsai*) and *buuz*, the steamed mutton dumplings that fill Mongolian holiday tables.
