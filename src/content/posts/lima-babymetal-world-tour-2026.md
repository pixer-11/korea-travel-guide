## Three fingers up for the fox god

Pinch your middle and ring fingers against your thumb. Leave the index and little finger standing. That is the kitsune sign, the fox-god salute BABYMETAL fans raise at every show. On December 7, 2026 it goes up at Costa 21 in Lima, when the group's World Tour 2026 Latin America leg reaches Peru.

BABYMETAL formed in Tokyo in 2010. The idea was simple and loud: J-pop idol vocals and synchronised choreography on top of full-weight heavy metal from a live backing band. The current front line is SU-METAL, MOAMETAL and MOMOMETAL. Songs like "Gimme Chocolate!!", "Megitsune" and "Road of Resistance" made them one of Japan's most successful exports to metal festivals worldwide.

## Costa 21, between cliff and ocean

The venue sits at sea level. Costa 21 is an open-air event ground on the Costa Verde, the coastal highway that runs along the foot of Lima's cliffs in San Miguel district. On one side is the Pacific; on the other, the eroded brown bluffs that hold up the city.

Big concerts here are typically laid out as zoned standing areas, with your ticket tier deciding how close you get to the stage. Early December is the start of Lima's summer. The city almost never sees rain, but the breeze off the ocean takes the warmth out of the evening air. Fans usually bring a light layer.

## Getting to San Miguel on a Monday night

No rail line reaches the Costa Verde, so nearly everyone arrives by car. Taxis and the ride-hailing apps Uber, Cabify and inDrive all operate in Lima. Most visitors use one of those instead of hailing a cab on the street.

Visitors to Lima usually stay in Miraflores or Barranco. Both are coastal districts linked to San Miguel by the same Costa Verde road, and the drive runs along the water the whole way. When the show ends, the pickup area by the venue jams up fast. Many fans walk a few minutes along the road before they request a ride, which gets them out of the knot of cars.

## Through the Costa 21 gates the Lima way

Peruvian crowds are loud and organised. They chant between songs, and they know the words even when the words are Japanese. Circle pits and walls of death have been part of BABYMETAL shows for years. The barrier area is where they happen, so the zones further back suit anyone who wants to watch rather than mosh.

What attendees typically carry:

- **ID:** a passport for visitors, a DNI for Peruvians
- **A small bag:** large bags slow down security lines at outdoor venues
- **A layer:** for the sea breeze late in the evening
- **Soles in cash and a card:** food and merch stands vary in what they take; locals often pay by Yape or Plin, mobile apps that need a Peruvian bank account

A few useful words: *entrada* (ticket), *puerta* (gate), *zona* (section), *salida* (exit). Staff at the gates speak Spanish first. A phone with a translation app helps with anything beyond those basics.

## Before and after: eating around the show

Plan your ceviche for lunch. In Lima it is a midday dish, and cevicherías usually open around lunchtime and close in the afternoon. Visiting fans often eat one in Miraflores before heading along the coast.

After the show, the late-night food is grilled. Anticuchos, skewers of marinated beef heart, are the classic Lima street snack. If you want something more ordinary, the Plaza San Miguel mall is a short drive inland on Avenida La Marina. In sit-down restaurants, a tip of around 10% is customary when service isn't already added.
