## A walled garden on the ring

Bethmann Park is small, and it's fenced. It covers 3 hectares on Friedberger Landstraße, at the northeast corner of the green belt that rings Frankfurt's old centre. The name comes from the Bethmann banking dynasty, who once owned the grounds. Now it's a public park, and it rates among the city's best-liked green spaces.

The perimeter railing changes how the place feels. Once you're through a gate, the traffic on the Anlagenring fades behind hedges and trees. Inside are lawns, flowerbeds and gravel paths you can loop in a few minutes.

## The Chinese garden

You come for the Chinese garden. It's a separately enclosed section built around a pond. A pagoda-style pavilion sits right at the water's edge, and bridges cross to the far banks. Rockwork, curved roofs and planting frame the views across the water, the way a classical Chinese garden is meant to.

Give it time. Rushing through takes ten minutes. Sitting by the pond and letting the pavilion's reflection settle on the water takes longer, and that's the point of the place.

Most visitors spend somewhere between 30 minutes and an hour in the whole park. That makes it a good stop to pair with something else rather than a half-day outing.

## Getting there and what's nearby

The park is easy to reach on foot from several directions:

- **Merianplatz (U4):** the closest U-Bahn station, a short walk down Berger Straße.
- **Konstablerwache (S-Bahn and U-Bahn hub):** about ten minutes' walk north through the city.
- **The Anlagenring paths:** walk the green belt from Eschenheimer Tor and you arrive at the park's corner.

Berger Straße starts right outside. It's one of Frankfurt's best-known streets for cafés, bakeries and independent shops, and it runs northeast into Bornheim. The Zeil and Konstablerwache's Thursday and Saturday farmers' market are close enough to fit into the same walk.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

This is a popular park, so treat it like one. On weekdays the quietest window is 7am–8am. That's when you're most likely to have the pond and pavilion to yourself, or close to it.

Weekends follow a clear pattern. From 12pm to 8pm the paths fill and the benches by the water get taken. Our weekend data marks 7am–11pm as the quiet span overall, so the morning before noon is your best chance for a calm visit.

How to behave inside:

- **Keep your voice down in the Chinese garden.** People go there to sit and look, and sound carries across the water.
- **Stay on the paths around the pond.** The rockwork and planted banks aren't for climbing.
- **Check the posted opening times at the gate.** The park is locked at night, and the hours change with the season, so don't count on getting in late.
- **Use the lawns in the main park for picnics,** not the Chinese section's narrow walkways.

In wet weather, the pavilion's covered edge is the obvious place to wait out a shower. Expect others to have the same idea.
