## What Tembusu is

Tembusu is a Straits dining house set inside the Ginger Garden, a themed section of the Singapore Botanic Gardens known for its collection of ginger family plants. The kitchen reworks Malay and Peranakan staples with produce grown in the gardens themselves. Nasi Lemak and Squid Ink Claypot Rice both appear on the menu, signalling a Southeast Asian repertoire rather than a generic hotel-garden cafe. The name comes from the tembusu tree, a heritage species found throughout the Botanic Gardens and printed on the Singapore five-dollar note.

## Why it stands out here

Most dining inside the Botanic Gardens skews toward casual snack kiosks near the main gates. Tembusu instead sits in the quieter Ginger Garden precinct, putting a proper sit-down restaurant among planted foliage rather than at a park entrance. Pairing Peranakan cooking with ingredients sourced from the surrounding beds is unusual for a public garden venue in Singapore. As a recently opened spot, it's still building its reputation, which means shorter waits than the gardens' longer-established food stops, at least for now.

## Getting there and the garden around it

The Singapore Botanic Gardens is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with several gates; the Tanglin and Bukit Timah entrances sit closest to the Ginger Garden. Nearest MRT access is Botanic Gardens station on the Circle and Downtown lines, an easy walk to the Bukit Timah Gate. Combine a visit with the nearby National Orchid Garden, a short stroll away within the same grounds.

Because Tembusu is new, confirm current opening hours and whether reservations are required before setting out, either through the restaurant's own listing or the Botanic Gardens' official site.

## Ordering and garden etiquette

Treat a meal at Tembusu as a destination in itself, not a snack stop between garden paths.

- Book ahead where possible; new restaurants inside the gardens tend to fill quickly once word spreads.
- Arrive with time to walk the Ginger Garden beforehand or after; the beds are planted for slow viewing, not a quick pass-through.
- Expect Peranakan sharing-style dishes; claypot rice in particular is meant for the table, not solo eating.
- Keep to marked paths in the Ginger Garden itself; it's a curated botanical collection, not open lawn.
- Bring cash or card flexibility until you've confirmed the restaurant's payment setup, as newly opened venues sometimes lag on card terminal rollout.
