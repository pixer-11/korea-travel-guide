Look up, not around. On Tai Nan Street the shop signs at street level belong to leather suppliers, hardware sellers and cafés. The bookshop you came for is three storeys above them, behind a building lobby that looks like any other in Kowloon. That's the whole point of Book Punch, and of the Hong Kong habit it belongs to.

The address to hold onto is **3/F, Tai Nan Commercial Building, 169-171 Tai Nan Street**. Once you're through the entrance, you're in the tradition the city calls *lau seung syu dim*: the upstairs bookshop.

## Why Hong Kong's bookshops climb stairs

The reason is rent. Ground-floor rents in Mong Kok rank among the highest anywhere, so for decades small booksellers have moved into upper floors of commercial and residential blocks. Their customers follow them up.

The result is a particular kind of visit. There's no window display to browse from the pavement and no passing trade. Everyone in the room meant to be there. That changes the mood. People come to read spines, not to kill time.

Book Punch is small and well reviewed, with a few hundred reviews rather than thousands. It still sits well off the tourist circuit, even though it's only a few blocks from the crowds on Nathan Road.

## Reading the name: 一拳書館

The Chinese name is worth understanding before you climb. **一拳** means "one punch" or "one fist", and **書館** is an old-fashioned word for a book hall or reading house. Put together, it's a deliberately punchy name for a bookshop.

It also helps in practical ways:

- Show **一拳書館** to a taxi driver along with the building name, **大南商業大廈** (Tai Nan Commercial Building).
- Look for the Chinese characters on the floor directory in the lobby. Upstairs shops often list themselves only in Chinese.
- If you're asking a shopkeeper downstairs, say the street, Tai Nan Street (大南街), and point up.

## Finding Tai Nan Street from the MTR

**Prince Edward station** is the usual approach. It's an interchange for the Tsuen Wan and Kwun Tong lines. From there, walk west across the grid of streets away from Nathan Road and you reach Tai Nan Street. It's a walk of roughly 10–15 minutes.

**Olympic station** on the Tung Chung line is a similar distance from the south-west, and it's the better choice if you're coming from Central or the airport line.

Numbers 169-171 sit toward the Tong Mi end of Tai Nan Street, where Mong Kok runs into Tai Kok Tsui. Follow the street numbers carefully. Tai Nan Street is long, and its other end reaches deep into Sham Shui Po.

## Browsing upstairs without being awkward

A small upstairs bookshop behaves more like someone's front room than a chain store. A few habits make the visit go smoothly:

- **Keep your voice down.** The rooms are small, and other people are reading.
- **Ask before photographing.** Shelves and stock belong to an independent seller, and a quick gesture at your phone is polite.
- **Put books back where you found them.** Small shops often shelve by their own logic, not by the alphabet.
- **Carry a bag that won't sweep the shelves.** Aisles in upper-floor units tend to be tight.

On payment, Hong Kong runs on the Octopus card, cash and, more and more, mobile wallets. What a small independent shop takes is its own decision, so carry some cash as a fallback and ask at the counter. Tipping isn't expected in Hong Kong shops.

The hours of upstairs bookshops can be irregular, and no schedule is listed here. Check the shop's own social pages or Google listing the same day, because the climb up three floors feels long when the door at the top is shut.

## Pairing it with the street below

Tai Nan Street pays back the trip on its own. The Sham Shui Po end is known for leather craft suppliers selling hides, buckles and hardware. Over the past decade small cafés and design studios have moved in between them, so walking the street from one end to the other is a good way to spend an afternoon.

Nearby, a short walk or one MTR stop away:

- **Yuen Po Street Bird Garden** and the **Flower Market Road** stalls, both east of Prince Edward station.
- **Ki Lung Street** and **Nam Cheong Street** in Sham Shui Po, for fabric, ribbons and buttons.
- **Apliu Street** in Sham Shui Po, for its electronics and second-hand gadget market.
- **Ladies' Market** on Tung Choi Street, if you want the loud, touristy side of Mong Kok afterwards.

## How long to give it

Allow 30–60 minutes inside, longer if you read Chinese and the shelves pull you in. The shop is small, so it suits a slow browse rather than a big shopping trip.

Treat it as one stop in a half-day walk. Start at the Flower Market, cut west to Book Punch, then follow Tai Nan Street north into Sham Shui Po for food and the fabric streets. That route takes you from Mong Kok's crowds to one of its quietest rooms and back out into the street-level city.
