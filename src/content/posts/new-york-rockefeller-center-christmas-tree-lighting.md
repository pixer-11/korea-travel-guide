## The tree above the rink

A Norway spruce, usually, and a very large one. It stands at the top of the sunken plaza, above the ice rink and the gilded Prometheus statue. Behind it rises 30 Rockefeller Plaza, the Art Deco tower that houses NBC's studios. Tens of thousands of LED bulbs are strung across the branches, and a crystal star sits at the tip.

The tradition goes back to 1931. That year, construction workers building the complex put up a modest tree on the site. The first official lighting followed in 1933, and the ceremony has since become one of the most-watched holiday broadcasts in the United States.

## December 2, 2026: what the night involves

The 2026 ceremony takes place on Wednesday, December 2. It is held on the plaza between Fifth and Sixth Avenues, from West 48th to West 51st Street.

Most of the evening is a televised concert. NBC's "Christmas in Rockefeller Center" special mixes live music with the countdown to the moment the tree lights up. Performers sing on staging around the plaza. The tree stays dark until the switch is thrown near the end of the broadcast.

Rockefeller Center's official website is where information about the ceremony and the tree's season lives. The broadcast lineup and running times are not part of the facts here, so this guide leaves them out.

## Tickets, pens and standing room

There is no general public ticket. The areas closest to the stage and rink are held for invited guests and broadcast production. Everyone else watches from the street.

How the crowd physically works:
- NYPD officers fill metal-barricaded pens on Fifth Avenue, Sixth Avenue and the side streets, one pen at a time
- Once a pen is full, the next arrivals are directed further out
- Leaving a pen, even briefly, usually means losing the spot
- Security screening is common, and large bags slow entry or get turned away

The view from most pens is partial: a slice of tree between buildings and heads. Many New Yorkers skip the ceremony itself. They come in the days afterwards, when the tree is lit every evening and the plaza is far easier to walk through.

## Holding a spot on Sixth Avenue

The nearest subway stop is 47–50 Sts–Rockefeller Center on the B, D, F and M lines, which opens straight onto Sixth Avenue. On ceremony night some exits and street crossings are usually restricted. Riders often get off a stop or two away (5 Av/53 St, or 49 St on the N, Q, R and W) and walk in.

What regular attendees typically do:
- Show up in the afternoon, hours before the broadcast, and stay put
- Eat beforehand, because food and restrooms sit outside the pens
- Dress in layers for long hours on cold pavement
- Bring a phone charger, since there is a lot of waiting

The easier version is watching the NBC broadcast at a bar or hotel nearby, then walking over late in the evening.

Afterwards the neighbourhood stays busy. St. Patrick's Cathedral is directly across Fifth Avenue. Saks Fifth Avenue's holiday window displays face the Channel Gardens, and Radio City Music Hall is a block west on Sixth Avenue. For a view down onto the tree, the Top of the Rock observation deck sits in the same complex.
