## A pop star made of light

Hatsune Miku plays The O2 in London on November 12, 2026. She is a Vocaloid: singing-synthesiser software released by Crypton Future Media in 2007. Her voice was built from recordings of voice actor Saki Fujita, and she is drawn as a 16-year-old with long teal twin tails.

Fans have written many thousands of songs for her. That catalogue, mostly fan-made, is what she sings on stage.

Miku concerts usually work like this. Her animated figure is projected onto a transparent screen at the front of the stage, and a live band plays behind it. The O2 arena sits under the white tented dome held up by yellow masts, and holds around 20,000 people.

## Getting to Greenwich Peninsula

Four routes serve the arena:

- **Jubilee line** to North Greenwich station, which opens almost onto the venue's front plaza.
- **Uber Boat by Thames Clippers** river buses, which stop at the pier beside The O2.
- **IFS Cloud Cable Car**, which crosses the Thames from Royal Docks to the peninsula.
- **Buses**, which terminate at North Greenwich bus station beside the tube.

When an arena show ends, the whole crowd heads for the Jubilee line at the same moment. Staff often hold people back at the station entrance to control numbers. Many attendees take the boat or wait out the rush in the restaurants inside the dome. Greenwich, with the Cutty Sark and the Royal Observatory, is one stop upriver by boat.

## Penlights, colours and crowd manners

Miku audiences join in in a particular way. Most fans carry penlights, battery glow sticks that switch colour, and change them to match whoever is singing. Teal is Miku's colour. Cosplay is everywhere, from twin-tail wigs to the leek she is often drawn holding.

Here is what regulars usually do:

- Keep penlights at chest height. In Japanese concert etiquette, waving them above your head is rude because it blocks the view behind you.
- Learn the call-and-response parts of the best-known songs. Many lyrics are in Japanese, so fans often learn them from fan videos.
- Bring spare batteries. A penlight that dies mid-set is a common complaint.

The cheering follows Japanese idol-fan habits more than Western arena habits. Expect coordinated chants and colour changes in sync, not a mosh pit.

## Tickets, seats and the view

The concert date is November 12, 2026, at The O2. Tickets for arena events are sold through The O2's official site and its authorised ticket partners. The venue's own event pages carry the seating plan for each show.

Where you sit matters more here than at a normal gig. The projection screen is flat and faces forward, so seats far round to the side of the lower tier can see Miku at a sharp angle and a thinner image. Seats facing the stage head-on, even high up, get the full effect.

Floor tickets put you closest to the band. Tiered seats give the clearer view of the projection and of the whole arena glowing with penlights.
