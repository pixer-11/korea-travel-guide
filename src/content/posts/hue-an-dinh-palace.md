## A Prince's House Gone European

The gate faces a canal, not a moat. An Dinh Palace stands on Phan Đình Phùng, along the An Cựu waterway south of the Perfume River. Compared with the Citadel's walled courts, the grounds feel domestic: a garden, a gatehouse and a pale, many-windowed main building with European columns and stucco.

Khải Định began it in the early 1900s while he was still a prince. He enlarged it after taking the throne. His son Bảo Đại, the last emperor, also lived here, so the rooms tell the dynasty's final chapter. That chapter is about Western furniture and painted walls, not throne halls.

## What's Inside the Rooms

Look up once you step inside. The main hall is known for its wall and ceiling murals, painted landscapes framed by ornate plasterwork, restored after decades of neglect. Displays across the ground floor include:

- Period rooms laid out with the royal family's style of furnishing
- Fine art and decorative pieces from the late Nguyễn court
- Photographs of Khải Định and Bảo Đại's era
- Mosaic and relief decoration that blends Vietnamese motifs with European forms

If you've seen Khải Định's tomb, you'll recognise his taste here: East and West fused, with more ornament than restraint. Allow 45 minutes to an hour. That covers the building, the garden and the gatehouse without rushing.

## Timing the Gate on Phan Đình Phùng

Weekend mornings are the pinch point. Our measured crowd data puts the weekend peak at 8am–11am. Arriving then means sharing the mural hall with groups. On a Saturday or Sunday, come after 11am, or go straight at the 7am opening before the rush builds.

Hours are 7am to 5pm daily, with no closing day. Aim to arrive by about 4pm so the last hour isn't a scramble. Some practical habits:

- **Tickets:** buy at the window by the entrance, where the current price is posted. Carrying Vietnamese đồng is the simplest way to pay.
- **Dress:** this was a royal residence, so shoulders-covered clothing fits the setting.
- **Photos:** get the facade shot from the garden path. The whole front only fits in frame from a few steps back.
- **Weather:** Hue's heavy rain season runs roughly September to December. The palace is mostly indoors, which makes it a good wet-day choice.

## Getting There and Pairing It

It's on the south bank, so there's no river to cross if you're staying in the hotel area around Lê Lợi and Phạm Ngũ Lão. A Grab car or motorbike from there takes a few minutes. From the Citadel, cross the Trường Tiền Bridge and head south-east toward the An Cựu canal.

Good pairings:

- **The Imperial City:** the official, ceremonial side of the dynasty, north across the river. See it first for context.
- **Khải Định's tomb:** south of the city in the hills, built by the same emperor in the same hybrid style.
- **Đông Ba Market:** by the river on the north bank, for lunch after a morning visit.

The palace is well known and regularly toured, so don't expect to have it to yourself. Timing, not secrecy, gets you a quiet room.
