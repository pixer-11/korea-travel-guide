## Three million bulbs along the river

The Parkway becomes a light route. The Niagara Falls Winter Festival of Lights is billed as Canada's largest free light festival. This edition runs from November 14, 2026 to January 10, 2027, with more than three million lights strung along the Niagara Parkway on the Canadian side of the gorge.

The setting is the main draw. Displays sit between the river and the hotel strip, so the coloured falls illumination, mist and lit trees all share one view. Most visitors work the light route and the falls viewing wall into the same evening.

## Where the displays sit

The festival covers a stretch of riverside, so it isn't a single venue with a gate. Its main spine is the Niagara Parkway, running past these places:

- **Queen Victoria Park**, the lawns and gardens facing the American and Horseshoe Falls
- **Table Rock Centre**, at the brink of the Horseshoe Falls
- **Dufferin Islands**, the wooded islets south of the falls, which earlier editions lit as a loop
- **Clifton Hill**, the arcade-and-attraction street running uphill from the river near Rainbow Bridge

Drivers from Toronto usually take the QEW, about 90 minutes in normal traffic. GO Transit and VIA Rail both serve Niagara Falls station, a taxi ride from the river. Parking near the falls is in paid lots along the Parkway and around Clifton Hill.

## Walking the lights after dark

Most people walk. They go from Clifton Hill down to Queen Victoria Park, then follow the railing south towards Table Rock, which takes about an hour at an easy pace. Families with small children often drive the Dufferin Islands section slowly with the headlights dimmed. Others park once and do the whole route on foot.

Dress for the gorge, not the town. The river throws spray, and in a cold snap that spray freezes onto railings, paths and trees near Table Rock. Regular visitors wear boots with grip and pack gloves that still work a phone screen.

The winter fireworks series is launched over the falls. People who come for it usually claim a spot along the Queen Victoria Park wall, where the view is open across to the American side. The organiser posts the fireworks nights and times on its official festival site.

## Doing the festival the way regulars do

Weekends in December draw the biggest crowds, especially the Saturdays before Christmas. Many repeat visitors pick a weeknight in late November or the quieter stretch after New Year, up to the closing date of January 10, 2027.

A few habits are common here:

- **Pay by card.** Parking machines, Clifton Hill restaurants and Table Rock Centre all take cards. Canada uses tap payments widely.
- **Tip 15–20%** at sit-down restaurants, the Ontario norm.
- **Warm up indoors.** Table Rock Centre has shops and food counters right at the brink, so people use it to break up the walk.
- **Stay overnight.** Hotels along Fallsview Boulevard look down on the Parkway, and many of their rooms overlook the falls illumination and the fireworks launch area.

Visitors from the US cross at Rainbow Bridge with a passport or other accepted border document. Its Canadian end is a short walk from Clifton Hill and the start of the lights.
