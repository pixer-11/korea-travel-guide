## An island lit from the water

Nodeul Island is a narrow strip of land under Hangang Bridge. For ten nights it becomes a laser stage. The Seoul city government describes the Seoul Light Hangang Bitseom Festival as the nation's largest waterfront laser art festival. The setting is the reason for it: beams go out over open river, with the Yeouido towers and the 63 Building lit up to the west.

The 2026 edition runs October 2 to 11 and is free. "Bitseom" means "island of light". Two public holidays fall inside the run, National Foundation Day on Saturday, October 3, and Hangeul Day on Friday, October 9. Those nights draw the city's day-off crowds on top of the usual weekend visitors.

## Reaching Nodeul Island

The island has no subway station of its own. You walk on from the bridge sidewalks.

- **From the south:** Nodeul Station (Line 9). Exit 2 puts you at the foot of Hangang Bridge, a few minutes' walk from the island.
- **From the north:** Ichon Station (Line 4 and Gyeongui-Jungang Line). The walk south across the bridge deck takes about 15 minutes and looks straight down the river.
- **By bus:** many city routes cross Hangang Bridge and stop at the Nodeulseom stops in the middle.

Noryangjin Station (Lines 1 and 9) is one stop from Nodeul. Plenty of visitors have dinner at Noryangjin Fish Market first, then walk over once it is dark.

## What the evening is like

Nodeul Island has a tiered lawn and terraced concrete decks that step down towards the water. That makes it a natural amphitheatre for watching the river. Laser work needs darkness, so the island fills after sunset. Early October sunsets in Seoul come at around 6pm.

Give it an hour to walk the island and take in the projections, and longer if you add the bridge. Evenings on the river in early October are usually cool, and wind comes off the water across the open decks. Most people bring a layer heavier than the daytime weather seems to call for.

## Doing it the Hangang way

Seoul treats its riverbanks as a picnic ground, and festival nights are no exception.

- **Mats and snacks:** locals spread a folding mat on the lawn and eat convenience-store food bought from a CU or GS25 near Nodeul Station.
- **Paying:** a T-money card covers the subway and buses and also works at most convenience stores. Cards are accepted almost everywhere.
- **Rubbish:** bins on the riverbank fill up quickly, so Koreans usually carry their trash out with them.
- **Timing:** weekends and the two holidays are when the island and the bridge sidewalks get most crowded. Weeknights give the lawn more room.

When the island hits capacity, the Hangang Bridge sidewalks become an overflow viewing deck. Photographers often prefer them anyway, because the full spread of beams over the water fits in one frame.
