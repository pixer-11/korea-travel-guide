## One night of Hong Kong rock in Cotai

The date is Saturday, November 21, 2026. Supper Moment, the Hong Kong rock band, brings a Macau-only edition of its "Thank You World" tour to The Venetian Arena. This is the Macau stop of the tour, and it is a single night.

Supper Moment have been part of Hong Kong's guitar-band scene since the late 2000s. They write and sing in Cantonese, with big choruses built for a room to sing back. "無盡" (Endless) is the song most casual listeners know, and it has long been a crowd singalong.

## Inside The Venetian Arena

This is an indoor arena with tiered seating. It is built into The Venetian Macao, the large Venice-themed resort on Cotai, and it opened with the resort in 2007. It usually holds around 15,000 people, and it is one of Macau's main rooms for touring Cantopop and Asian pop acts.

Because the arena is part of the resort, you reach it through covered, air-conditioned corridors, not across an open plaza. That counts for something in November, when Macau evenings are mild and dry but crowds still bunch up at the entrances.

Seating sections and floor plans follow the usual arena layout:

- **Floor:** the area nearest the stage, set up as seated or standing depending on the show
- **Lower bowl:** raked seats close to the stage, with good sightlines to the side screens
- **Upper tiers:** further back, and the best view of a full arena singing along

Venetian Arena shows are usually ticketed through Cotai Ticketing, the resort group's own platform. Seat maps and prices for this show sit with the official ticket seller, not here.

## Getting to Cotai

Getting to the arena is simple once you reach Macau. The Venetian is on the Cotai Strip in Taipa, between the old Taipa village and Coloane. Visitors usually arrive one of three ways.

- **Ferry from Hong Kong:** TurboJET and Cotai Water Jet sail from Hong Kong to Macau. Cotai Water Jet lands at Taipa Ferry Terminal, a short drive from the resort.
- **Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge:** shuttle buses run from the Hong Kong port across the bridge to the Macau port, where resort shuttles and taxis are waiting.
- **From mainland China:** most people walk through the Border Gate (Portas do Cerco) or Hengqin crossing, then take a shuttle or taxi to Cotai.

The big resorts run free shuttle buses from the ferry terminals and border crossings, and The Venetian is one of the main stops. The Macau Light Rapid Transit's Taipa Line also serves Cotai, and taxis queue at the resort entrances.

## The last-crossing question

Many people in this crowd will be heading back to Hong Kong that same night. When the encore ends, thousands of fans want to leave at once, and the queues for shuttles and taxis show it.

Hong Kong fans usually sort out the trip home before the show. The bridge shuttle buses run through the night, so they are the usual backup when the last ferry has gone. Ferry timetables are shorter late at night, and the operators post them on their own sites.

Plenty of fans skip the problem and stay over. Cotai has the largest cluster of hotel rooms in Macau, all within walking distance of the arena. On a Saturday with a sold-out show nearby, those rooms are in demand.

Remember that Macau has its own immigration control, separate from Hong Kong and the mainland. Even fans who live an hour away cross a border both ways, so they carry their passport or travel permit.

## Singing along, Macau-style

A Supper Moment crowd joins in loudly, in Cantonese. If you don't speak the language, learning the chorus of "無盡" before the night is the simplest way to feel part of the room. Phones held up as lights during the slower songs are normal here.

Some practical habits for the night:

- **Money:** Macau's currency is the pataca. Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted on Cotai, but change often comes back in patacas. Cards and mobile wallets work at resort shops and food outlets.
- **Bags:** arenas in Macau usually screen bags at the door. A small bag gets you through faster.
- **Merchandise:** tour merch stands at arena shows usually have queues before doors and again at the interval, so fans tend to buy as soon as they get in.
- **Cameras:** whether professional cameras are allowed is decided by the promoter, not the venue, and the ticket terms say which.

On the casino floors, photography is restricted and under-21s are not allowed. The walk to the arena can take you near gaming areas, so follow the signs and stick to the shopping corridors.

## Before the doors, after the encore

Most people make a whole evening of the trip. The Grand Canal Shoppes run through the same building, with indoor canals, gondolas and a painted sky ceiling, so there is plenty to fill an hour.

For something less themed, Taipa Village is a short taxi ride north. Rua do Cunha, its pedestrian lane, is lined with bakeries selling almond cookies and pork-chop buns, the Macanese snacks people carry home in boxes. Just past the village, the Taipa Houses-Museum is a row of pastel-green colonial villas looking over what used to be the waterfront.

Late at night after the show, the food courts and restaurants inside the Cotai resorts are the easiest places to eat. Fans waiting for a later shuttle often end up there, still humming the last song.
