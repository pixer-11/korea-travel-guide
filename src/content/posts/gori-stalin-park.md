## An Avenue That Still Carries His Name

Stalin Avenue runs straight through Gori. The park sits partway along it, a strip of lawn, paths and clipped trees that work as a forecourt to a grand stone museum. Nothing about the layout is accidental. It was planned as a place of pilgrimage, and it still walks like one.

You move from the street to a small pavilion, then towards the museum's arched facade. Off to one side stands a dark green railway carriage. Those three objects are the whole story here, and the park is the stage that holds them together.

It's well known and on most day-trip itineraries out of Tbilisi. Don't expect solitude, especially when tour minibuses pull up together.

## The Birth House Under Its Marble Canopy

The first stop is the strangest. Stalin was born in 1878 in a modest two-room house, the kind a shoemaker's family rented in a provincial town. In the 1930s the authorities wrapped it in a classical pavilion of columns and a heavy roof, so the humble cottage now sits under a monument.

Most of the surrounding neighbourhood was cleared to give it space. That is why the house stands alone on the lawn instead of on a street of similar homes.

Walk all the way around it. The contrast between the small brick-and-timber dwelling and the grand canopy above it says more about the Soviet cult of personality than any caption inside.

## Stalin's Green Railway Carriage

Near the museum building sits Stalin's personal railway carriage, painted a deep green. He disliked flying and travelled long distances by rail. This carriage is widely reported to be the one he used, including on the journey to the 1945 Yalta Conference.

Inside are a compact conference room, sleeping quarters, a bathroom and a small kitchen. Access to the interior is usually arranged through the museum, so ask at the ticket desk rather than trying the door. Even from outside, the armoured bulk of it is worth a slow look.

## The Museum Behind the Lawn

The Joseph Stalin Museum is the building the whole park points towards. It opened in the 1950s, built in the monumental style of the era, with a sweeping staircase and halls lined with photographs, paintings, gifts from foreign delegations and personal belongings.

Visitors and historians have long criticised the main exhibition for telling a largely admiring, Soviet-era version of his life. The famine and the purges get little space. Go in knowing that, and the museum becomes an exhibit in itself: a record of how a regime wanted its leader remembered.

Allow about an hour inside. Add 30–45 minutes for the birth house, the carriage and the grounds.

## Visiting Gori's Most Uncomfortable Monument

A few habits make the visit smoother and more respectful.

- **Take the guided tour.** Museum guides lead groups through the halls, and English is commonly offered. The building's labels alone will not give you the context.
- **Ask about photography.** Rules inside the museum and carriage have varied, sometimes with a separate permit. Check at the desk before you raise a camera.
- **Mind the conversation.** Opinions on Stalin in Georgia range from bitter to nostalgic, and Gori's own history makes the subject raw. Russian forces bombed and occupied the town during the 2008 war. Ask questions, but don't assume what anyone around you thinks.
- **Check the price at the ticket office.** Fees are posted at the museum entrance, and there may be separate tickets for the carriage or a tour.

On timing, arriving soon after the 10am opening usually puts you ahead of the minibus tours that leave Tbilisi in the morning. The schedule shown right now runs until 6pm on every day of the week. Times can change with the season and on holidays, so confirm on the day.

## Getting to Gori from Tbilisi

Gori sits roughly 80 km west of Tbilisi, an easy day trip.

- **Marshrutka (minibus):** these leave frequently from Didube bus station in Tbilisi (Didube metro, Line 1). The ride takes around an hour, depending on traffic.
- **Train:** regional trains run from Tbilisi Central Station to Gori. They are slower to arrange, but more comfortable.
- **Organised tour:** many Tbilisi agencies bundle Gori with Uplistsikhe and sometimes Mtskheta.

From the bus or train station in Gori, the museum is a walk or a short taxi ride along Stalin Avenue. The address to show a driver is სტალინის გამზირი (Stalin Avenue).

## Fortress, Caves and the Rest of the Day

Gori Fortress (Goris Tsikhe) crowns the hill in the centre of town. Its walls give a view over the rooftops, the Mtkvari valley and the Caucasus foothills. Below it is a memorial to the soldiers who died in the 2008 war, a quieter counterpoint to the museum.

About 10 km east lies Uplistsikhe. This rock-cut cave town was carved into the sandstone above the Mtkvari over many centuries, with halls, a pagan-era theatre and a later Christian basilica. Taxis from Gori make the trip easily. Most visitors do the park and museum in the morning and the caves in the afternoon.

Those three stops together make a full, balanced day: the Soviet myth, the scars of 2008 and a city far older than either.
