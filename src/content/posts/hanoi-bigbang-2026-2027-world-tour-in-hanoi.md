## Getting to My Dinh National Stadium

My Dinh National Stadium sits in Nam Tu Liem District, about 8km west of Hanoi's Old Quarter. It's Vietnam's national stadium, built for the 2003 SEA Games, and it's the default venue for the country's biggest concerts and football fixtures.

Getting there:

- **Taxi/ride-hail (Grab):** the simplest option from anywhere in the city; expect surge pricing right after the show ends.
- **Bus:** several city routes stop near the stadium gates, cheap but slower during event traffic.
- **Motorbike:** common for locals, but parking fills fast and exiting afterward is slow.

Budget extra time both ways. With two full stadium crowds converging on the same intersections, the roads around My Dinh choke up badly in the hour before doors and for well over an hour after the final encore.

## Tickets and seating

Stadium shows like this usually split into standing floor zones (often the priciest and most sought-after) and tiered stand seating around the pitch. Floor sections sell out first and typically require the fastest fingers when sales open.

Because ticketing platforms, price tiers and on-sale windows shift by market and can change without notice, don't rely on secondhand screenshots or resale listings as your source of truth. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site before purchasing anything, and double-check the platform is the officially designated one for Vietnam.

## How to visit like a local

Hanoi concertgoers travel light and move fast once doors open — bring only what you need and expect bag checks at the gates. Cash is still useful for street food stalls near the stadium, but most taxis and convenience stores now take cards or e-wallets like Momo.

Book your Grab ride well before the encore ends rather than after; everyone else has the same idea and surge pricing spikes hard in that first post-show window. If you're staying in the Old Quarter, factor in a 20-30 minute ride each way outside peak traffic, longer on show nights.

Vietnamese fans tend to arrive early to soak up the pre-show atmosphere and trade merchandise or light sticks outside the gates — it's part of the experience, not just a queue to endure. Follow that lead rather than rolling up at the last minute.

Street vendors set up near the stadium selling food, drinks and unofficial merch; prices aren't fixed, so a quick, friendly haggle is normal and expected. Keep small notes on hand for this rather than breaking a large bill.

## Nearby and what else to do

My Dinh sits near the Vietnam National Convention Center and within reach of West Lake (Ho Tay), a good spot to unwind before or after the show with lakeside cafés and a slower pace than the Old Quarter. If you're building a longer trip around the concert, pair it with a day exploring the Old Quarter's street food and a visit to the Temple of Literature, both a manageable taxi ride from the stadium.
