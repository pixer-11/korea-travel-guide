## A tower in a working village

Most visitors pass Gergeti Tower on their way to somewhere else. They are bound for Gergeti Trinity Church on the ridge above, and they hurry through Gergeti village without looking up. The tower is one of the stone defensive towers that families in the Khevi highlands built when raids came down the valley. It still stands among lived-in houses, hay stores and garden walls.

That is the draw. You are not standing on a viewing platform. You are in a highland village where the old fortifications stand right next to the farmyards, and Mount Kazbek's glaciated cone fills the skyline to the west. The tower is well rated by the people who do stop, and it sees a fraction of the foot traffic the church gets.

## Getting to Gergeti

Stepantsminda sits on the Georgian Military Road, about three hours north of Tbilisi by car.

- **From Tbilisi:** marshrutkas (minibuses) leave from Didube bus station. Shared taxis wait in the same lot.
- **The route:** it crosses the Jvari Pass, so expect snow closures in winter.
- **On foot from town:** cross the bridge over the Tergi at the east side of Stepantsminda. Then take the lanes uphill into Gergeti. The ground is steep but short.
- **Plus code:** MJ6G+7HC puts you on the right spot in any map app. That helps, because the village lanes are unsigned.

## Pairing it with the Trinity Church climb

The church trail runs straight up through Gergeti village, so the tower makes a natural first stop. From the village it is roughly a 1.5-hour climb to the church, which stands at about 2,170 m. Stop at the tower while your legs are fresh, or on the way down when the light is lower across the valley.

Go between late spring and early autumn if you can. Then the lanes are dry and the Jvari Pass is reliably open. In winter, snow and ice turn the village paths slick, and the road from Tbilisi can close for days.

## Walking through Gergeti politely

People live and farm here, and that sets the rules.

- **Stay on the lanes.** Ask before you step into a yard or lean over a wall for a photo. A wave and *gamarjoba* (hello) goes a long way.
- **Don't climb the stonework.** Old towers like this one are fragile, and loose stones are a real hazard.
- **Give way to animals.** Cows and dogs use the same paths. Village dogs usually lose interest if you keep walking calmly.
- **Carry Georgian lari in cash.** Guesthouses and small shops in Gergeti rarely take cards. Card machines and ATMs are back in Stepantsminda's centre.
- **Dress for the church if you're carrying on.** Gergeti Trinity expects covered shoulders and knees, and women cover their hair. Wraps are usually available at the door.

Nobody publishes opening hours for the tower. Ask your guesthouse whether it is open to visitors this season before you plan around going inside. It is an outdoor village monument, so arriving early mostly means you have the lanes to yourself before the church hikers come up.
