## Inside the market hall

This is a working market, not a showpiece. Mercado Juárez sits on Calle Juárez in Monterrey's Centro, where the street is lined with discount shops, shoe stores and street vendors. It's one of the city's most-reviewed markets, and plenty of people come here to shop for the week, not to sightsee.

The stalls are packed close and grouped by trade. As you walk the aisles, expect:

- **Produce and dried goods:** chiles, beans and spices sold by weight
- **Yerberías:** medicinal herbs, teas and folk remedies, with candles and religious items nearby
- **Piñatas and party supplies:** hanging overhead in bright stacks
- **Kitchenware and crafts:** clay pots, molcajetes and woven goods

## Eating at the fondas

Come hungry. The fondas are small food counters with stools, and they serve full meals that cost far less than restaurants in San Pedro or Barrio Antiguo.

Nuevo León's signature breakfast is machacado con huevo: dried shredded beef scrambled with egg, served with flour tortillas. Look for it on the hand-written boards, along with gorditas, tacos and caldos. Coffee usually comes in a mug or a styrofoam cup. Cooks do the frying right behind the counter, so the menu is whatever they're making that day.

At the moment the market opens 7:30am–7:30pm. Breakfast is the time to come for the fondas, when the food is freshest and the shoppers are out. Late in the afternoon some stalls start packing up before the official close, so don't save your meal for the last hour.

## Paying and ordering at the stalls

Cash in pesos is the norm. Small vendors rarely take cards, and a big note can be awkward early in the morning, so break bills at an OXXO before you come in.

- **Ordering:** point and ask "¿Cuánto cuesta?" or "¿Me da uno de estos?" Most stall-holders speak little English, but they're used to pointing.
- **Prices:** food and produce prices are fixed. Light haggling is sometimes accepted on crafts or larger buys, never on a plate of food.
- **Tipping:** leave some coins at a sit-down fonda. It isn't expected for counter purchases.
- **Seating:** stools are shared and turn over quickly. Eat, pay and give up your seat to whoever is waiting.
- **Photos:** ask before you photograph a vendor or their stall, especially at the herb and religious stands.

Calle Juárez is crowded, so keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket. Don't carry big bags through the narrow aisles.

## Getting to Calle Juárez

The market is in the Centro grid, near Calle Vicente Guerrero in the Ruperto Martínez area. Metrorrey Line 2 runs along Avenida Cuauhtémoc a few blocks west. From Alameda station it's a short walk east through the shopping streets, past the Alameda Mariano Escobedo park.

Taxis and ride-hailing apps can drop you right at the entrance. Weekday traffic around Centro is slow, though, and street parking is hard to find, so the metro is usually easier.

Most people spend an hour here, or two with a sit-down meal. Afterwards you can walk south through Centro toward the Macroplaza and Barrio Antiguo, which makes a full day of central Monterrey on foot.
