Karaköy sits right where the Golden Horn meets the Bosphorus, a former port district now packed with galleries, design shops and a new wave of hotel rooftops competing for the same water view. Lina Anatolian Kitchen, on top of The Maestro Hotel, is one of the newer entries in that competition, and in 2026 it's become one of the names people actually mention first.

## What makes it stand out

This isn't another rooftop doing the standard Istanbul mezze platter and a view. Lina's whole premise is Anatolian cooking, the regional dishes of central and eastern Turkey, reworked for a rooftop dinner-party setting rather than a village sofra.

That distinction matters in a city where "Anatolian" often just means a generic kebab menu. The kitchen here is clearly aiming at something more composed and plated, without losing the reference point.

## The setting

You're on a hotel rooftop, so the draw is as much architectural as culinary: open sky, low skyline in front of you, and the water doing the work no interior design ever could. Karaköy's rooftops face across to the Old City on one side and up the Bosphorus on the other, so where exactly you're seated changes what you're looking at.

Go at golden hour if you can arrange it. The light off the water in the last hour before sunset is the reason this stretch of rooftops exists at all.

## Getting there

Karaköy is served by the Tünel funicular from Beyoğlu and sits a short walk from Karaköy tram stop on the T1 line, which also connects to Kabataş and Eminönü. If you're coming from Sultanahmet or the Grand Bazaar, the tram is the simplest route, no transfers.

From the tram stop, The Maestro Hotel is within easy walking distance through Karaköy's grid of narrow streets, many of them lined with coffee roasters and small design studios worth a slow wander on the way.

## Reservations and timing

Because it's a hotel rooftop with a view and a trending reputation, table space is limited by physical footprint, not just demand. That combination is exactly what fills up fast in Istanbul's current rooftop scene.

- Book ahead rather than walking in, particularly for evening and sunset slots
- Confirm the restaurant's current opening hours directly with the hotel before setting out, since these can shift seasonally
- Ask about outdoor versus covered seating if weather or wind off the water is a concern
- If a view table matters to you, say so when you book rather than hoping on arrival

## Eating and drinking like it's not your first rooftop

Istanbul rooftop culture runs on reservations and pacing, not walk-in spontaneity, and Lina fits that pattern. Expect a dinner service built around courses rather than quick turnover, so plan the evening around the meal instead of squeezing it between other stops.

Tipping in Istanbul restaurants generally follows a modest percentage left in cash or added at your discretion if paying by card; ask your server what's customary if it isn't already on the bill. Reservations are typically handled by phone or through the hotel directly rather than third-party apps, so start there.

If you're unsure about dietary details or specific dishes on the night, ask the server rather than guessing from a name alone. Anatolian regional cooking varies enormously from province to province, and a modern kitchen reinterpreting it may be drawing on several traditions in one dish.

## Making it part of a Karaköy evening

Pair a dinner here with a pre-meal walk along Kemeraltı Caddesi or a coffee stop at one of Karaköy's specialty roasters before you head up. The neighbourhood rewards arriving early and wandering rather than showing up exactly at your reservation time.

Afterwards, the Galata Bridge and the ferry terminals at Karaköy and Eminönü are both close enough to walk to if you want to close the night with a view of the water from below instead of above it.
