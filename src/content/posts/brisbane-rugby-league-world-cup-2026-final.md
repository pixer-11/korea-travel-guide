## Two finals, one day at Lang Park

Both world titles get decided on the same afternoon. On 15 November 2026, Suncorp Stadium hosts the men's and women's finals of the 2026 Rugby League World Cup. That day closes a month-long tournament played across Australia, New Zealand and Papua New Guinea.

The ground is the right place for it. Brisbane locals still call it Lang Park, and it holds about 52,500 people. It is the Queensland home of State of Origin. The stands rise steeply and close to the touchline, so even the upper tiers look almost straight down onto the in-goal areas.

Confirm kick-off times, the match order and ticket details on the official tournament site.

## Tickets and seating

Buy from the official source only. Resale sites and social media offers are where fake finals tickets turn up. Prices are listed on the official ticketing page, not here.

Where you sit changes the day:

- **Lower bowl, sideline:** closest to the collisions. Shade depends on which side you pick.
- **Upper tiers:** the best view of set plays and kicking duels, and cheaper.
- **Behind the posts:** this is usually where the loudest travelling supporters gather.

November in Brisbane is late spring heading into summer. Afternoon sun on the exposed sections is strong, so pack sunscreen and a hat if your seat is uncovered.

## Getting to Suncorp Stadium

The stadium sits in Milton, on the western edge of the CBD. Driving is the wrong choice because there is no public parking at the venue. Event-day road closures also make nearby streets slow.

- **Train:** Milton station sits just across from the stadium on the Ipswich/Rosewood and Springfield lines. Roma Street station is about a 15-minute walk away along Caxton Street, on most suburban lines.
- **Bus:** event shuttles usually run from the CBD and from suburban hubs.
- **Ticket travel:** Suncorp event tickets have traditionally included Translink public transport on match day. Check the conditions printed on your ticket.

Allow at least an hour after full-time to leave. Platforms at Milton fill first, so walking to Roma Street is often quicker.

## Matchday on Caxton Street

Brisbane's pre-match ritual happens on one street. Caxton Street runs from Petrie Terrace downhill to the stadium. It is lined with pubs, the Caxton Hotel among them, and they fill with jerseys hours before kick-off. Fans have a drink there, then walk the last few hundred metres with the crowd.

How the day runs inside:

- **Payment:** the stadium is cashless, so carry a card or phone wallet for food and drink.
- **Bags:** venue bag rules apply. Check the stadium's website before you bring anything bigger than a small bag.
- **Anthems and war dances:** the Pacific nations perform the Sipi Tau, the Haka and the Cibi before kick-off. The stadium goes quiet for them, and that is expected of you too.
- **Staying for both:** many fans hold their seats between the two finals instead of going back out. Re-entry rules vary, so ask at the gate before you leave.

If you have one free morning, Brisbane's South Bank parklands and the Queensland Museum are a short ride across the river from Roma Street. They are easy to see before an afternoon at the stadium.
