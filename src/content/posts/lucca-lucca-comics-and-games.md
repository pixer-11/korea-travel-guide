## A convention with ramparts

Most conventions happen in a hall. This one fills a medieval city.

The 2026 edition was set for **October 28 to November 1, 2026**, across the old centre of Lucca. Lucca Comics and Games is one of the largest comics, games and pop-culture gatherings in the world. During the festival, exhibitor pavilions usually go up in the squares, and Via Fillungo turns into a slow river of capes, armour and foam swords.

The setting is the whole point. Lucca's brick Renaissance walls run in an unbroken loop around the centre, and the festival sits inside that ring. Visitors walk between game publishers and comic artists along streets laid out in Roman times, past Romanesque church fronts.

## How the pavilions and piazzas work

The festival has two layers.

- **Ticketed areas:** the large temporary pavilions and exhibition spaces in the squares and on the walls. This is where publishers, game studios, artists' tables and signings are.
- **Open streets:** the lanes, piazzas and ramparts between them. Anyone can walk these, and this is where most of the cosplay happens.

Plenty of people come only for the open-air side. They stroll, photograph costumes and eat. The stands, demos and dealers' areas required a ticket for the day, with ticket types, prices and day-specific rules set by the organisers.

## Getting to Lucca

The train is the easiest way in. **Lucca railway station** stands just outside the southern walls. From there it is a short walk through Porta San Pietro into the centre.

- **From Florence:** regional trains run from Firenze Santa Maria Novella. The ride is a little over an hour.
- **From Pisa:** trains from Pisa Centrale take around half an hour. Pisa's Galileo Galilei airport is the nearest international airport.
- **By car:** driving is awkward. The historic centre is a restricted traffic zone, and parking outside the walls fills up during the festival.

Regional trains are usually packed on festival days, especially in the morning and late afternoon. Many people in costume travel this way, so a carriage full of elves is normal.

## Walking the walls between halls

The walls are wide, tree-lined and flat on top. That makes them Lucca's best shortcut during the festival.

The full circuit is roughly four kilometres. Ramps and steps lead up at almost any of the bastions and back down near most piazzas. This route is quicker than Via Fillungo, which becomes a shuffle at peak times. The ramparts also give open views over terracotta roofs, and cosplayers have the space to pose properly.

A few landmarks help with orientation in the centre:

- **Piazza dell'Anfiteatro:** an oval square whose houses were built into the ring of a Roman amphitheatre.
- **Torre Guinigi:** the brick tower with holm oaks growing from its top. It is visible over the rooftops.
- **San Michele in Foro:** the church with the tall, layered white façade, standing on the old Roman forum.
- **Duomo di San Martino:** Lucca's cathedral, towards the southeast of the centre.

## Moving through the crowds without losing your day

The old centre is compact, and its streets are narrow, so the festival rewards a little focus.

- **Most visitors pick two or three stands.** Trying to cover everything usually means queueing all day.
- **Mornings suit the pavilions.** The costume crowd on the walls and in the streets tends to peak in the afternoon.
- **Props go through security.** Bag and prop checks are common at large conventions, and replica weapons are usually subject to specific rules.
- **Fixed meeting points help.** Phone signal can struggle in very dense crowds, and a bastion or a church door makes an easy backup.
- **Cosplayers appreciate being asked.** A quick "*posso fare una foto?*" ("can I take a photo?") goes a long way. Most will happily pose, and stepping aside afterwards keeps the lane clear.

Paying by card is widespread in Lucca. Small food stalls and bars sometimes prefer coins for a quick coffee. In Italy you usually pay at the till first, then show your receipt at the counter.

## Eating in Lucca on a festival day

Restaurants inside the walls fill up quickly at lunchtime on festival days, and tables go first to those who reserve or eat early.

Lucca has its own dishes, and they are worth tracking down:

- **Tordelli lucchesi:** large meat-filled pasta in a meat ragù, the city's signature first course.
- **Buccellato:** a sweet, ring-shaped bread with raisins and aniseed, sold by Lucca's bakeries.
- **Torta coi becchi:** a pastry tart with a scalloped edge, filled with a sweet vegetable or chocolate filling.

For a fast bite between pavilions, the bakeries and focaccia counters on the side streets off Via Fillungo are much quicker than a sit-down trattoria. Most Italians drink their espresso standing at the bar, which takes only a few minutes.

## Where to stay when Lucca is full

Rooms inside the walls go very quickly for the festival dates. Towns along the rail line make good alternatives.

- **Pisa:** close by train and well stocked with hotels. A good base for anyone flying into Galileo Galilei.
- **Viareggio:** a seaside town on the coast line with direct trains to Lucca. It is quieter in the autumn off-season.
- **Florence:** further away, but practical as part of a longer Tuscany trip.

One day covers the open streets. Two to three days suit the pavilions, signings and gaming areas without rushing.
