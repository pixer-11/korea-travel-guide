## What you're walking into

San Juan isn't Manila's usual bar-crawl territory, which is part of why Malagihay works. It sits on the second floor of a coffee shop, no rooftop views or neon, just a stairwell that leads to a room built to feel like someone's living room rather than a venue.

Ian Libang runs the place, and the layout shows it: low seating, close tables, a bar you can actually talk across. It reads less like a launch-week spot chasing attention and more like a room somebody built for people to stay in.

## The drinks are the argument

The idea here is straightforward but rarely executed well: take cocktail structures everyone already knows and rebuild them with Filipino ingredients. Sioktong, the fermented rice spirit more commonly poured at home or at a wet market stall than behind a bar, shows up alongside familiar spirit bases.

That's the whole pitch, and it's a good one. Instead of importing a template from Singapore or Tokyo, Malagihay is asking what a classic sour or a stirred drink looks like when a Filipino spirit does the heavy lifting.

Don't expect a printed menu of prices to reference in advance. Confirm current offerings and any reservation policy directly with the bar before you go, since a young, trending spot like this can shift its hours or booking system month to month.

## Finding the entrance

There's no dedicated bar frontage at street level, so the coffee shop below is your landmark, not the club itself.

- Look for the ground-floor coffee shop in San Juan; the club is directly above it
- The entrance to the bar is a stairwell, easy to miss if you're expecting a marquee
- Google Maps will get you to the block; the last twenty meters are on you

Because there's no separate street sign, it's worth checking online or calling ahead to confirm you have the right building before you're standing on the sidewalk squinting at doorways.

## Reading the room

This is a small, second-floor space designed for conversation, not a dance floor or a photo backdrop. Treat it like a living room because that's the intent: quieter voices, no rushing the bartender, no expecting a club-sized crowd to lose yourself in.

Given the size, a full house here means something different than a full house at a Poblacion megabar. If you're coming with a group larger than four or five, it's worth asking ahead whether the room can actually hold you, rather than finding out at the door.

## Ordering like you mean it

The bar's whole identity is built around Filipino spirits reworked into familiar formats, so lead with curiosity rather than defaulting to a gin and tonic. Ask what's built around sioktong that night; a bar this focused on a specific ingredient usually has a few standing options even without a fixed printed list.

Tipping and payment norms in Manila's craft bar scene generally follow standard Philippine practice: card is accepted at most serious cocktail bars now, but it doesn't hurt to carry cash for a tip if service impresses you. Confirm payment methods when you book or ask when you arrive.

## Why it's worth the trip to San Juan

Malagihay isn't chasing the BGC or Poblacion crowd, and that's precisely why it currently reads as one of the quieter, more distinctive rooms in Manila's cocktail scene rather than another stop on a bar tour. A neighborhood location above a coffee shop keeps it low-key by design, not by accident.

If you're the kind of traveler who wants a Manila night that isn't a rooftop with a skyline view, this is the alternative: a small room, a bartender with a clear point of view, and a spirit most visiting drinkers have never had put in front of them in a cocktail glass.
