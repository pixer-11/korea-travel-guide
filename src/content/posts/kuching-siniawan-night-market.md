## One street, three nights a week

Siniawan is a single-street town. Two facing rows of old wooden shophouses line Jalan Siniawan, the market's official address. It is a historic Hakka Chinese settlement from Sarawak's gold-mining era. On weekend evenings the street shuts to traffic, and folding tables and hawker carts fill the space between the shopfronts.

Red lanterns hang over the road. After dark they are the main light, glowing against timber facades and shutters, many of which are closed for the rest of the week.

The market keeps short hours. It opens Friday and Sunday from 5:00 to 9:30 PM, and Saturday from 5:00 to 10:00 PM. From Monday to Thursday it is closed and the town is quiet. This is a well-known Kuching outing, not a secret, so expect families and day-trippers from the city.

## What to eat under the lanterns

Stalls change between weekends, but the food is Sarawak hawker fare. Graze your way along the street instead of committing to one sit-down meal. Look for:

- **Kolo mee**: springy noodles tossed dry and topped with minced pork and char siu, Kuching's everyday breakfast dish, served here at night
- **Sarawak laksa**: a sambal-and-coconut broth with prawns, omelette strips and lime
- **Satay** from charcoal grills, served with peanut sauce
- **Kueh and local snacks**, sold by the piece from trays
- Sugarcane juice and iced drinks for the humid evening

Some shophouses open as casual eateries with their own tables. They are a better choice if you want a proper seat and a plate of zi char.

## The drive out from Kuching

No train or rapid bus runs here. Most visitors drive or take a Grab from Kuching, about 25 km along the Kuching–Bau road. The trip takes about 30 minutes, longer if you leave at the Friday rush hour.

Rides back can be scarce late in the evening. Many people ask their driver to wait or agree a return time in advance. If you drive, park on the side roads before the closed-off section and walk in.

Siniawan pairs well with an afternoon around Bau. **Fairy Cave** and **Wind Cave** are both a short drive away, and so is **Tasik Biru**, the old mining lake. See the caves in daylight, then arrive at the market for its 5pm opening.

## Working the stalls like a regular

The routine is simple. You order and pay at each stall, then carry your food to any free seat at the shared tables. Strangers sit together, and nobody minds. Some sit-down eateries bring the dish to your table and take payment afterwards. Watch the table next to you and follow what they do.

- **Pay in cash, small notes.** Some stalls display a DuitNow QR code, but plenty take cash only, and card machines are rare.
- **Tipping is not expected** at hawker stalls.
- **Pork is common** in this Chinese town, so ask before ordering if that matters to you. Halal stalls usually mark themselves clearly.
- **Arrive early for a table.** Families come for dinner, so seats around the busiest stalls fill up as the evening goes on.
- **Bring a small pack of tissues.** At hawker tables, napkins are often your own job.

Stalls start packing up before the official close, especially on Sunday. If you arrive after 9pm, many carts will be closing.
