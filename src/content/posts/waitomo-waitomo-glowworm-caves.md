## A ceiling that switches on in the dark

The last part of the tour happens in silence. You step down into a flat-bottomed boat and the guide pulls it along overhead ropes, with no engine and no paddle splash. The lights go out, and the rock above the Waitomo River turns into a field of small blue-green points.

Those points are the larvae of *Arachnocampa luminosa*, a fungus gnat found only in New Zealand. Each one hangs sticky silk threads to catch insects drawn to its light. Your eyes need a minute to adjust. After that, the Glowworm Grotto looks less like a cave and more like a night sky someone has lowered within reach.

## How the walk unfolds

The tour runs about 45 minutes and follows a fixed route. You start on foot along pathways and stairs through dry limestone chambers. Stalactites and stalagmites come first, then the Cathedral, a tall chamber known for its acoustics. Opera singer Kiri Te Kanawa famously sang there, and guides often ask a group to test the echo.

The boat comes last. Floors can be damp and the air is cool all year, so a light layer earns its place in your bag. The route suits most visitors, but it does include steps and low-lit stretches.

The caves have a documented history. Local Māori chief Tane Tinorau and English surveyor Fred Mace explored them by raft in 1887, and tours began soon after. In 1989 the land was returned to Tinorau's descendants. Many of the guides today are his descendants, and they share in the cave's revenue.

## Waitomo village and the road in

Waitomo is a small cluster of cafés, lodges and tour offices in the King Country, in the Waikato region of the North Island. From Auckland, you drive south on State Highway 1 and then State Highway 3. Turn off at the Waitomo junction near Otorohanga onto State Highway 37. Hamilton is about an hour away, and Rotorua and Hobbiton are both within day-trip range.

The visitor centre is hard to miss. Its curving timber-lattice roof, opened in 2010, sits over the cave entrance and the river exit. Tickets, a café and the gift shop are all under that roof, and so is the jetty where the boat comes out into daylight.

## Getting a quieter boat

This is one of the most-visited attractions in the North Island. Treat the visit as a booking job, not a turn-up-and-hope trip.

- **Book a timed slot online.** Tours leave regularly, but popular times in the summer holidays and on weekends can sell out.
- **Think about the coach schedule.** Day tours from Auckland usually reach Waitomo around the middle of the day. The first tours after opening and the late-afternoon slots tend to avoid them.
- **Leave the camera in your pocket.** Photography is not allowed inside the cave. Light and noise disturb the glowworms, and the boat ride depends on everyone staying quiet.
- **Listen to the guide's stories.** Guides mix geology with family history and the cave's Māori names. Asking about Tane Tinorau is welcome.

At the moment the site opens 9am–6:30pm every day this week, but cave attractions adjust hours with the season and around holidays. Check the official Waitomo site for this month's tour times before you set off.

## Ruakuri, Aranui and the wetter options

The same operator runs two neighbouring caves, which makes combination tickets easy to arrange.

- **Ruakuri Cave:** entered by a spiral ramp, with longer walkways and glowworms along the way. A good choice if you want more time underground.
- **Aranui Cave:** dry and glowworm-free, known for delicate formations. It suits people who want a quieter, slower look at the limestone.

If you want to get wet, Waitomo is also where black-water rafting started. You float through underground streams on an inner tube, in a wetsuit and helmet. These trips take a few hours and are run by separate operators in the village.

Outside the caves, the Ruakuri Walkway is a short forest loop past natural tunnels and limestone bridges. Some people come back after dark with a torch to spot glowworms on the banks. It costs nothing and fills the gap well if your cave slot is a couple of hours away.

## How long to give it

The main cave tour takes under an hour. Allow about 90 minutes with check-in and the visitor centre. Adding Ruakuri or Aranui turns it into a half day. Black-water rafting or an overnight stay in the village fills a full day without rushing back up State Highway 3.
