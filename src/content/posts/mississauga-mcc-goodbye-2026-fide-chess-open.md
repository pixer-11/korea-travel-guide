Clocks on every table, scoresheets or none, and the soft clack of a hand slapping a button after each move. That is a rapid open. The MCC Goodbye 2026 FIDE Chess Open was set to bring that sound to Mississauga, Ontario, from November 11 to 25, 2026.

The name said what it was. The tournament was billed as a farewell to the 2026 chess year, rated by FIDE, the international chess federation, and open to competitive players across the rating range.

## What "FIDE rapid" means at this board

Rapid sits between blitz and classical. Under FIDE's Laws of Chess, a rapid game gives each player more than 10 minutes but less than 60, with any increment counted over 60 moves. Games end in under two hours, and most finish well inside that.

FIDE keeps a separate rapid rating list from its standard and blitz lists. Results from this open were set to feed the rapid list only. A strong showing in a rapid event moves a player's rapid number, not the classical rating most players quote.

That separation matters for who turns up. Rapid opens tend to draw:

- Club players chasing a first FIDE rapid rating
- Juniors building rated games between school terms
- Experienced players who prefer a shorter, sharper game

The calendar had a quirk. November 11, 2026 and November 25, 2026 both fall on a Wednesday. The tournament was scheduled to open and close on the same weekday, two weeks apart.

Mid-week dates shape the field. Players with day jobs and school timetables make up much of it at events like this, fitting rounds around work and classes.

Mid-November is also when southern Ontario turns cold and dark. Sunset comes before 5pm after the clocks go back in early November. Anyone leaving a playing hall after an evening round walks out into full night and, often, a raw wind off Lake Ontario.

## Opening on Remembrance Day

The first date carried extra weight in Canada. November 11 is Remembrance Day, marked across the country with poppies pinned to coats and two minutes of silence at 11am.

In Ontario it is not a statutory holiday. Shops, transit and offices run as normal, so getting around on November 11 works the same as on any Wednesday. Visitors from abroad see a lot of red poppies on lapels in the days leading up to it, and on the day itself.

## Entering the tournament

There were no spectator tickets in the concert sense. "Tickets" here meant a player's entry, made through registration with the organiser.

The FIDE side has its own requirement. To be rated, a player needs a FIDE ID, issued through their national federation. In Canada that federation is the Chess Federation of Canada (CFC). Players from other countries hold their ID through their own federation, and their results still count.

Players usually bring a few things to a rapid open:

- Their FIDE ID number and federation details
- A pen, in case the event asks for scoresheets
- A water bottle and a snack for gaps between rounds
- A warm layer, since hall temperatures vary

## Manners at a rapid board

Rapid etiquette follows the FIDE Laws, and arbiters enforce them. A few habits keep players out of trouble.

Touch-move applies. Touch a piece and you must move it; say "j'adoube" or "I adjust" before straightening one. Press the clock with the same hand that moved the piece. That rule catches newcomers more than any other.

Phones are the costly mistake. FIDE rules let arbiters forfeit a player whose phone makes a sound in the playing area, and many events ban phones from the hall outright. Regulars switch theirs fully off and leave them in a bag.

Players usually shake hands before and after each game. After a loss, the custom is to stop the clock and offer a hand rather than leave a flagged position on the board. Post-mortems happen outside the playing hall, never beside a game still in progress.

## Getting to Mississauga

Mississauga sits directly west of Toronto in Peel Region. Toronto Pearson International Airport is actually inside Mississauga's city limits, which makes the trip simple for anyone flying in.

Getting around works like this:

- **MiWay** is the city's bus network, with routes reaching most neighbourhoods
- **GO Transit** trains link Mississauga to Union Station in downtown Toronto on the Lakeshore West and Milton lines
- **UP Express** runs between Pearson and Union Station for travellers staying downtown
- **Driving** is common across the city, which is laid out on wide suburban arterial roads

Visiting players usually match a venue to a MiWay route or a GO station when choosing where to stay.

## Between rounds in the city

Most visiting players gravitate toward City Centre. Square One Shopping Centre has the largest food court in the area, and Mississauga Celebration Square sits beside it with City Hall's clock tower above.

For something quieter, Port Credit lies on the lakefront at the mouth of the Credit River. Its main street, Lakeshore Road, has cafés and restaurants and a lighthouse-shaped landmark by the water. It has its own GO station on the Lakeshore West line, so it is easy to reach without a car.

Toronto is close enough for a rest day. A GO train covers the ride downtown, and the city's museums and neighbourhoods are an easy reach between rounds.
