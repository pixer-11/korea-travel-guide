## A secret in name only

The name means "secret vessel". The winery is anything but secret. Vasija Secreta sits right on Ruta 40 at Km 4343, the first bodega you pass as the highway enters Cafayate from the north. It is one of the most-reviewed wineries in the Calchaquí Valleys and among the oldest in town, so expect company.

Its age is the reason to come. Cafayate's wine story began in bodegas like this one, long before boutique labels arrived. Old winemaking equipment is displayed on site. Vines run right up to the buildings, with the bare ridges of the valley behind them.

## Getting to Km 4343

Getting there from the plaza is quick:

- **Bike:** rental shops around the plaza make this an easy flat ride, and you can carry on to other bodegas.
- **Taxi or remis:** a few minutes from the centre. Ask the driver to come back for you, or have your hotel book the return.
- **Walking:** possible along the highway, but the shoulder is exposed and there is little shade in the afternoon.

Coming from Salta city, the drive south on RN68 through the Quebrada de las Conchas takes roughly three and a half to four hours. Most people stop at the rock formations on the way in. If that's your plan, reach Cafayate the evening before so the tasting doesn't get squeezed against closing time.

## Tasting without the tour-group crush

At the moment the winery opens 9:00 AM–5:30 PM every day, Sunday included. That matters in a town where many shops shut for the afternoon siesta. Hours can change, so check before you set off.

- **Ask for a *degustación*.** That's the tasting. Start with Torrontés, the aromatic white the high-altitude Cafayate valley is known for, then move to the reds.
- **Go early.** Arriving soon after 9:00 AM usually beats the minibus day trips from Salta, which reach Cafayate later in the day.
- **Spanish helps.** Guided visits in English may not run at every slot, so ask at the counter which languages are on that day.
- **Pay attention at checkout.** Bottles are sold on site. Ask whether cash or card is preferred, since Argentine businesses sometimes price the two differently. Current tasting prices are posted at the winery.
- **Don't drive after tasting.** Salta province enforces zero tolerance for alcohol behind the wheel. A taxi or bike is the sensible choice.

Leave a tip for the person pouring if the tasting was generous. Rounding up or leaving around ten percent is the norm in Argentina.

## Making a half-day of the northern bodegas

Plan on 45 minutes to an hour here. Then use the location: you're already on Ruta 40 with the town ahead of you, so other wineries are close.

- **Bodega El Esteco:** a short way along the highway, with its colonial-style estate at Patios de Cafayate.
- **Museo de la Vid y el Vino:** in town, a good primer if you want context on valley winemaking before or after the tasting.
- **The plaza:** a lunch stop between tastings. Try empanadas salteñas with a glass of the Torrontés you just tasted.

Autumn harvest, roughly March into April, puts working activity among the vines. Cafayate's dry climate and sun keep the valley a year-round stop. A hat and water make sense in any month at this altitude.
