## A Gold Label race on a winter Sunday

The 26th Dubai Marathon takes place on Sunday, January 17, 2027. World Athletics gives it Gold Label status, one of the top tiers of its road race labels. That means an elite field is on the same tarmac as the mass race.

The start is on Umm Suqeim Road. The road runs inland from the Jumeirah coast toward Sheikh Zayed Road and Mall of the Emirates. Expect flat, wide asphalt with almost no gradient. That flatness is why Dubai has a reputation for fast times. Haile Gebrselassie won here three years running, from 2008 to 2010, and Ethiopian runners have dominated the podium for years.

## Weather, timing and the course

January is one of the coolest months in Dubai. It is still warm by European standards, and the sun climbs fast after dawn. Desert road races here traditionally start early in the morning so the field finishes before the heat builds.

- **Surface:** smooth city roads, near sea level, very little elevation change
- **Scenery:** low villas, palm-lined boulevards and the coastal skyline of Umm Suqeim, the district around the Burj Al Arab
- **Shade:** very little; the course is mostly open sky
- **Weekend:** Sunday is part of the UAE's Saturday–Sunday weekend, so commuter traffic is light

The organiser publishes the full route, distances and start times on the official race site.

## Getting to Umm Suqeim Road

Road closures shape race morning. Runners usually travel by Dubai Metro or ride-hailing apps rather than drive. Closed streets around the start make parking slow.

- **Metro:** the Red Line runs along Sheikh Zayed Road; Mall of the Emirates station sits near where Umm Suqeim Road meets it
- **Taxis and Careem:** drivers usually drop runners at the edge of the closure zone, and runners walk the rest
- **Staying nearby:** visiting runners often book hotels in Umm Suqeim, Al Barsha or along Jumeirah Beach Road, within walking or short-ride distance of the start

## How race morning works in Dubai

Spectating costs nothing and needs no ticket. Supporters line the roadside in the residential stretches, where the pavements are wide and quiet. Families from the surrounding villas often come out to watch.

- **Paying:** the Metro runs on the Nol card, which you top up at station machines; cafés and kiosks almost all take cards and phone payments
- **Dress:** running kit is normal on the course; off the course, malls and the Metro expect shoulders and knees covered
- **Hydration:** runners from cooler climates usually drink more than they would at home, even in January
- **After the finish:** many runners walk down to Jumeirah Public Beach or Kite Beach in Umm Suqeim to wade into the Gulf
- **Recovery meal:** Mall of the Emirates is close, with plenty of places to eat and its indoor ski slope

Registration, ticketing and bib collection are all handled through the official race site.
