## Stalls from midnight

Carbon Market runs on farmers' hours. Most days it opens at midnight, while trucks from the Cebu highlands are still unloading. The stalls run along covered aisles and spill onto the pavement around M. C. Briones Street. You'll find:

- Fresh produce: mangoes, bananas, highland vegetables, bundles of herbs
- Seafood on ice, sold by weight
- Dry goods: rice by the sack, dried fish, spices, sugar
- Household basics like baskets, plastic tubs and kitchen tools

It takes its name from the coal ("carbon") once stored here for the old Cebu railway. It is one of the most-reviewed places in the city, so expect company.

## Getting downtown

The address is 59 M. C. Briones St, in the old port district of Cebu City. Grab and metered taxis are the easiest way in. Ask to be dropped at Carbon Market, since every driver knows it.

Jeepneys marked "Carbon" end their routes here. They're cheap and they run constantly, but they fill up fast. The market is on foot from the rest of the old downtown:

- Colon Street, often called the oldest street in the Philippines
- Basilica Minore del Santo Niño and Magellan's Cross
- Fort San Pedro, towards the waterfront

Plan an hour at the market, then walk the heritage circuit. Downtown traffic jams quickly after mid-morning.

## Picking your hour

The hours move around a lot during the week, so check before you go:

- Monday to Wednesday and Sunday: 12:00am to 7:00pm
- Thursday: 5:00am to 6:30pm
- Friday: 12:30am to 10:30am only
- Saturday: 12:00am to 3:30pm

At weekends, try not to come between 3pm and 7pm. That's the busiest stretch, and the aisles turn into slow shuffles. Because Saturday closes at 3:30pm, the afternoon crush mostly falls on Sunday. On weekdays, turn up early in the morning, soon after the overnight deliveries. The produce is at its freshest and the heat hasn't built up yet.

## Haggling in Cebuano

Pay cash in pesos, and bring small notes and coins. Stallholders often can't break big notes early in the day. Few stalls take cards. Prices are rarely written down, so you'll need to ask. A few words of Cebuano help:

- "Pila?" means how much?
- "Palihug" means please
- "Salamat" means thank you
- "Tawad" means a discount. Ask with a smile, not a hard bargain

Buy fruit by the kilo, and let the vendor pick unless you ask to choose yourself. Wear shoes that can handle wet floors, because the fish section is always hosed down. The lanes get tight, so keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket or a zipped bag. If you want to photograph a vendor, ask first. Most say yes, but they're working.

Bring a reusable bag, or you'll leave with a fistful of thin plastic ones. For a treat on the spot, many fruit stalls will slice a ripe mango for you to eat standing up.
