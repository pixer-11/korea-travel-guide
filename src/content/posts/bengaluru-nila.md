Cambridge Layout is not where you'd expect Bengaluru's most-talked-about new opening of 2026. It's a quiet, tree-lined residential pocket off Indiranagar's edges, mostly known for its bungalows and small parks, not tasting menus. That's exactly where Nila has set up, inside one of those renovated houses, and the contrast is part of the draw.

## What Nila actually is

This is a solo project from chef Rahul Sharma, built around a single idea: a 12-course progressive tasting menu that draws entirely on Nagaland's food traditions. That's a cuisine rarely given this kind of treatment on an Indian tasting-menu circuit dominated by European and pan-Asian formats.

Expect dishes like black rice momos and Naga-smoked pork to appear on the lineup, built from ingredients and techniques (fermentation, smoking, bamboo shoot, dry chillies) that define Naga cooking. The format is progressive, meaning courses are designed to build on each other rather than stand alone, so plan to spend a full evening rather than ducking in for a quick bite.

## Why it's the reservation everyone wants

Because it runs a single tasting format rather than an à la carte menu, tables turn slower, which tightens availability further.

## Finding the bungalow

Cambridge Layout sits between Indiranagar and Domlur, a short stretch from the busier commercial roads of 100 Feet Road and CMH Road. The area is mostly residential, so Nila's renovated house doesn't announce itself with the kind of signage you'd see on a main street.

Because it's tucked into a layout of similar bungalows, it's worth confirming the exact address and cross-street before you head out, and giving yourself a few extra minutes to locate the building on foot once you're in the neighbourhood. Auto-rickshaw or cab drop-off at the layout entrance is usually easier than trying to be dropped directly outside.

## Eating through a Naga tasting menu

Naga food isn't built around individual showpiece dishes the way a lot of fine dining is. It's built on layers: smoke, fermentation, heat from dry chillies, and the bamboo and leafy greens that don't usually make it onto Bengaluru menus at all. Going in with that in mind changes how you eat.

Pace yourself rather than filling up on the earlier courses. Twelve courses is a real commitment, and the kitchen is sequencing flavours deliberately, building from lighter, fresher notes toward the smokier, heavier dishes like the Naga-smoked pork. If a course arrives that's unfamiliar, ask; the staff at a concept this specific are used to explaining ingredients most Bengaluru diners haven't encountered before.
