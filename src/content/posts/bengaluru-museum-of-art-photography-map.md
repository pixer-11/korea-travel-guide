## Why go

MAP is one of the few Indian museums built from scratch for a permanent collection rather than retrofitted into a colonial building. The result feels different the moment you walk in: daylight-filled atrium, clean concrete lines, art hung at a pace that lets you actually look.

The collection spans over 18,000 works — textiles, photography, folk and tribal art, modern Indian painting, colonial-era prints. You'll find M.F. Husain hanging near century-old temple photographs, and a research library that takes photography as seriously as painting.

## Getting there

MAP sits on Kasturba Road in Ashok Nagar, right across from Cubbon Park's southern edge. The nearest metro stop is Vidhana Soudha or MG Road on the Purple Line, both about a 10–15 minute walk or a short auto ride away.

If you're coming from MG Road or Church Street, it's an easy 20-minute walk through the park itself — a pleasant approach on a cool morning. Autos and cabs know the museum by name now; just say "MAP, Kasturba Road."

## What to see

Start on the upper floors and work down — the layout rewards it. Highlights worth budgeting time for:

- The photography galleries, with vintage studio portraits and colonial-era Bengaluru images
- A rotating textiles gallery, often the most tactile and least crowded room
- The modern and contemporary Indian art floor, anchoring names like Husain and Souza
- A dedicated kids' gallery with hands-on, sensory exhibits — genuinely good for families

Give yourself two to three hours minimum. There's a rooftop café for a break midway, and the ground-floor shop is worth ten minutes for its art books and prints.

## When to go

MAP is closed every Monday, so don't build a Monday visit around it. Tuesday through Friday it runs 10am–6:30pm; Saturday and Sunday it stays open later, until 7:30pm.

Weekends get busy from noon through closing, so if you want space to actually stand in front of a painting, aim for the 10am–11am opening window on Saturday or Sunday. Weekday mornings are generally calmer too, though no hard data confirms the quietest weekday hour — arriving soon after opening is a safe bet either way.

## How to visit like a local

MAP is well-known in Bengaluru now, not a hidden find, so plan around the crowds rather than expecting to have it to yourself.

- **Book ahead**: tickets are usually sold online through MAP's own site as well as at the door; booking in advance skips the counter queue, especially on weekends.
- **Payment**: cards and UPI are standard; cash is accepted but rarely necessary.
- **Timing**: come right at 10am on a weekend, or any weekday afternoon before the after-work crowd. Avoid arriving between noon and closing on Saturday or Sunday, when tour groups and families cluster.
- **Etiquette**: photography is allowed in most galleries but flash and tripods usually aren't — check gallery signage rather than assuming.
- **Local mistake to avoid**: treating it as a quick one-hour stop. The museum is deceptively large across five levels, and rushing the textiles or photography floors is the most common regret visitors mention.

Pair the visit with a stroll through Cubbon Park right next door, or walk over to the Karnataka Chitrakala Parishath if you want a second, more traditional art stop the same day.
