## A synthwave show built like a metal gig

Carpenter Brut plays Istanbul on Saturday, November 28, 2026. Behind the name is Franck Hueso, a producer from Poitiers in France. He took the neon, VHS-horror strand of 1980s synth music and drove it straight into heavy metal.

Most first-timers know him from one of three places:

- **"Turbo Killer"**, the track with the lurid, much-shared music video
- **The *Hotline Miami* soundtracks**, which carried his tracks to gamers worldwide
- **The albums**: the *Trilogy* compilation, *Leather Teeth* (2018) and *Leather Terror* (2022)

He has toured as a live trio, with guitar and drums playing alongside the synths, in front of a wall of video. The crowd mixes metalheads, gamers and electronic fans. It behaves like a rock audience, so expect a pit near the stage.

## Getting there and back on a Saturday night

Late November in Istanbul is cool and often wet. Concertgoers usually wear a layer they can leave at the cloakroom.

The Istanbulkart, the city's reloadable transit card, works on everything you will need:

- **M2 metro**: runs north from Taksim through Şişli and Levent to Maslak
- **Marmaray**: the rail tunnel under the Bosphorus linking Europe and Asia
- **Ferries**: cross between Kadıköy, Karaköy, Eminönü and Beşiktaş

After the encore, a few thousand people leave the hall at once. Locals tend to book a cab on the BiTaksi app or walk to a main road before hailing one. Visitors staying near Taksim, Karaköy or Kadıköy can usually walk or take a short ride from most central venues.

## Doors, bag searches and the pit

Every Istanbul venue screens people at the entrance. Security searches bags and often puts them through an X-ray, so most fans carry very little. Large bags, professional cameras and drinks from outside are routinely turned away at the door.

Tickets are scanned from a phone, and many attendees keep a printed or offline copy in case the venue's signal is weak. Turkish venues usually take cards at the bar. Staff speak enough English to manage the cloakroom and drinks.

Fans who want the barrier tend to queue well before doors. People who would rather not be in the pit usually stand towards the sound desk. The visuals read best from there, and the low end hits hard anywhere in the room.
