## Why Root House by Gēn stands out

George Town's dining scene has no shortage of hawker legends, but Root House by Gēn represents a different lane entirely: a considered, design-led restaurant built inside a genuine piece of the city's built heritage. The 1926 Heritage Hotel that houses it is one of George Town's old shophouse-era buildings, restored rather than gutted, so the restaurant sits within original bones — high ceilings, period detailing, and the kind of patina that can't be manufactured. Onto that backdrop, the team behind the acclaimed Gēn restaurant has layered a refined take on Malaysian Chinese cuisine, the cooking style that grew out of generations of Chinese immigrant communities adapting recipes to local Penang ingredients and Nyonya influences. The result is a spot that reads as both a heritage experience and a serious modern kitchen, which is a rarer combination in George Town than the sheer number of "heritage cafés" would suggest.

## Where it is and how to get there

Root House by Gēn is located inside the 1926 Heritage Hotel in George Town, Penang's UNESCO-listed historic core — the grid of streets bounded roughly by Love Lane, Chulia Street, and the waterfront that holds most of the city's best-known heritage buildings, clan jetties, and street art. Because George Town's old town is compact and largely walkable, the most practical way to reach it is on foot or by short trishaw or e-hailing ride from wherever you're staying within the heritage zone; if you're coming from further out (the airport, Batu Ferringhi, or the mainland via the Penang Bridge), a taxi or e-hailing app is the simplest option, since public bus routes into the old town can be slow and indirect for visitors unfamiliar with the network.

## What to expect at the table

Exact dishes and prices aren't published widely yet, given how recently this concept launched, so treat any menu details you see online as provisional until you check directly with the restaurant. What's consistent across coverage of the opening is the positioning: Malaysian Chinese cuisine reworked with modern technique and presentation, served in a setting designed to feel considered rather than casual. Expect a slower pace than a hawker stall or kopitiam — this is a sit-down restaurant meant for a proper meal, not a quick bite between sightseeing stops. Given the pedigree of the team behind Gēn, portions and plating are likely to lean toward a tasting or shared-plates format rather than the single-bowl, single-dish tradition of most Penang street food.

## Pairing it with the neighbourhood

Because it sits inside George Town's historic core, Root House by Gēn works well as an anchor for an evening that also takes in the area's other landmarks. Armenian Street, the Cheong Fatt Tze Mansion ("Blue Mansion"), and the George Town street art trail are all within the same walkable district, so consider spending the afternoon exploring on foot and finishing with dinner here once the day's heat has broken. The building itself — a restored 1926 property — is worth a few extra minutes before or after your meal simply to look at the architecture and restoration work, which is as much a part of the draw as the food.

## When to go

Evenings are the natural time for a restaurant positioned around a considered dining experience, and George Town's old town is genuinely more pleasant after dark once the daytime heat and tour-bus crowds thin out. As a newly opened, trending concept, tables are likely to be in demand, particularly on weekends, so this isn't the kind of place to turn up to without a plan.

## How to visit like a local

Book ahead rather than walking in — as a recently opened, widely talked-about concept, Root House by Gēn is unlikely to have the kind of easy walk-in availability that older neighbourhood restaurants do, and reservations are the standard way Penang diners secure a table at buzzy new openings. Aim for a weekday evening if you want a calmer room; weekend dinner service at any new George Town opening tends to draw the heaviest local and visitor interest. Card and mobile payments (DuitNow, e-wallets) are standard across Penang's restaurant scene, so don't assume you need cash on hand. Dress is likely smart-casual given the heritage-hotel setting — shorts and flip-flops that work fine at a hawker stall will feel out of place here. The most common mistake visitors make with new heritage-building restaurants is treating them as a five-minute photo stop; budget real time for the meal itself, and confirm opening hours and reservation policy directly with the restaurant before you go, since both can shift in the months after a launch.
