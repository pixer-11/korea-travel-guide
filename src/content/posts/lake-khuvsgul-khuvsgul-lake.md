## A sea in the mountains

Mongolians call it *Dalai Eej*, "Mother Sea." The lake sits at roughly 1,645m, runs about 136km north toward the Russian border, and drops to more than 260m deep. It holds most of Mongolia's fresh water. Locals often call it Baikal's younger sister, and the geology backs them up: the two lie on the same rift system.

The water is clear enough to see stones well below the surface, and it stays cold even in July. Larch and pine forest comes right down to shingle beaches. In early summer the meadows between the trees fill with wildflowers. Few international visitors make the trip this far north, so long stretches of shore stay empty.

## Reaching Khatgal

Most trips start with a domestic flight or a long road journey from Ulaanbaatar to Mörön. From there it is about 100km north to Khatgal by jeep or shared minivan. Khatgal is the practical base, with guesthouses, small shops and the park checkpoint.

Khövsgöl Lake National Park covers the lake and its shores. You pay a park entry fee at the checkpoint, and the current amount is posted there. Most ger camps line the west shore north of Khatgal around Jankhai, reached by rough tracks. The east shore is wilder and much harder to drive.

- **How long:** plan 3–5 days. The journey alone takes a full day each way.
- **Summer (June–August):** open water, camps operating, horse and kayak trips.
- **Winter:** the lake freezes thick enough for vehicles to cross. Khatgal holds an ice festival in late winter.
- **Shoulder months:** many camps close and nights drop well below freezing.

## On the shore and in the saddle

Horse treks are the classic way to move along the shore. Guides in Khatgal arrange anything from a half-day ride to multi-day loops. Kayaks are another option, but keep close to land: the water is cold enough to be dangerous, and winds rise quickly.

Anglers come for the Khövsgöl grayling, a fish found only in this lake. Fishing is regulated, so ask your camp about permits before you cast. West of the lake, in the remote taiga, live the Dukha (Tsaatan) reindeer herders. Reaching their camps takes days on horseback with a guide. Reindeer you see near the lakeshore tracks have usually been brought down for visitors.

## Ger manners and ovoo stones

A few habits will smooth every stay.

- **Ovoo cairns:** you will pass stone piles hung with blue *khadag* silk on passes and headlands. Walk around them clockwise three times and add a stone.
- **Inside a ger:** step over the threshold, never on it. Move clockwise, and don't point your feet at the stove.
- **Offered tea or airag:** take the bowl with your right hand, supported by your left. Taste it even if you don't finish it.
- **Payment:** carry Mongolian tögrög in cash. Card machines are rare beyond Mörön and mobile signal fades along the shore.
- **Booking:** reserve your camp ahead in July. That's when Naadam holidays bring domestic travellers north.

Pack layers whatever the season, because a summer morning on the shore can feel like early spring.
