## Glass, iron and a rainforest under a dome

Three glasshouses mark out the garden's history. The Palm House dates from 1877. It was prefabricated in Germany and shipped out in pieces, and today it holds dry-climate plants from Madagascar under white-painted iron and old glass.

A short walk further on, the Bicentennial Conservatory opened in 1989. Its low, curved glass shell covers a tropical rainforest with raised walkways, and misting systems keep the air humid inside. The Amazon Waterlily Pavilion is the smallest of the three. Its pond holds *Victoria* waterlilies, whose leaves can grow wider than a café table.

## Beds, lawns and one very old pine

The garden spreads over 125 acres, and it has been growing on this site since 1855. Outside the glasshouses, look out for:

- **The Wollemi pine**, a conifer known only from fossils until 1994, when a small stand was found in a canyon near Sydney
- **The Santos Museum of Economic Botany**, an 1881 building of timber cases full of seeds, fibres and papier-mâché fungi, and the last museum of its kind in the world
- **The International Rose Garden**, where cultivars are trialled in labelled beds
- **The Main Lake and First Creek**, lined with Moreton Bay figs whose roots buckle the paths
- **Statuary** along the older avenues, set among the formal beds

Dotted between these are cafés. Most visitors loop the eastern half first and finish on the lawns.

## Getting in off North Terrace

Walk east along North Terrace past the Art Gallery and the University of Adelaide. The main gate is at the far end, near the corner of Botanic Road. The city tram now runs along North Terrace to a Botanic Garden stop, and the free City Connector bus loops nearby. From the Adelaide Railway Station, the walk takes about 20 minutes.

Some tips for the visit itself:

- Entry to the grounds is free. Check the official site for anything ticketed or any special event.
- People bring picnics and sit on the lawns. Pack your rubbish out, because bins fill up on warm weekends.
- In the Conservatory, stay on the walkways and leave the plants alone. The misting keeps the floor damp.
- The garden is exposed on hot summer afternoons, so come in the morning or after the heat breaks.
- The garden team runs guided walks from the Schomburgk Pavilion near the Hackney Road side. Times are listed online.

Opening and closing times follow daylight and shift across the year. Look them up on the day rather than assuming the gates stay open until dark.

## What sits right next door

The garden sits at the start of Adelaide's parkland belt, so you can walk straight on to the next stop:

- **Adelaide Zoo**, reached along Plane Tree Drive on the northern edge, where the giant pandas lived until 2024
- **National Wine Centre of Australia**, at the eastern corner on Hackney Road, with tastings of South Australian wine regions
- **Art Gallery of South Australia** and the **South Australian Museum**, a few minutes west on North Terrace
- **Botanic Park**, the open lawn to the north that hosts WOMADelaide each March

If you're pairing the garden with the zoo or the gallery, plan on a full half day and start with the glasshouses.
