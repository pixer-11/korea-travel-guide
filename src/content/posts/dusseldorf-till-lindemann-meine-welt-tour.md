## One night, one arena, one very loud frontman

Rammstein's singer goes it alone here. On November 21, 2026, Till Lindemann brings his "Meine Welt" tour to the PSD Bank Dome in Düsseldorf. The title means "My World," which fits a solo career built around his own voice and his own taste for provocation.

That solo work goes back almost a decade. He first stepped out with the Lindemann project alongside Swedish producer Peter Tägtgren, releasing *Skills in Pills* (2015) and *F & M* (2019). The solo album *Zunge* followed in 2023. Expect a show in that vein: harder, stranger and more personal than a stadium Rammstein night, and in a room a fraction of the size.

## The PSD Bank Dome in Rath

The venue is an ice hockey arena first. It opened in 2006 as the ISS Dome and is home to the Düsseldorfer EG hockey club. For concerts the ice is covered and the floor becomes a standing area. Tiered seating wraps around it on all sides, so even the upper rows look down on the stage rather than across at it.

It sits in Rath, a residential and light-industrial district in the north of the city, a short way from Düsseldorf Airport. You won't find much around it besides car parks and the arena itself. Food, drink and nightlife are back in town, so most people plan their evening around the trip out and the trip back.

## Getting out to Rath and back

Public transport is how most concertgoers make the trip. The S-Bahn line S6 stops at Düsseldorf-Rath, and from there it is a walk through the district to the arena. Rheinbahn, the city's transport operator, runs trams through the area, and its journey planner covers the route from the Hauptbahnhof.

- **From the centre:** S-Bahn or tram from Düsseldorf Hauptbahnhof to Rath.
- **From the airport:** a short hop, which makes it convenient for anyone flying in for the night.
- **By car:** the arena has its own parking, though exit queues after a full house are the usual price of driving.

After the encore, everyone leaves at once. Stations near the arena fill fast, and fans who aren't in a hurry often wait out the first wave on the walk back.

## Doing the night the Düsseldorf way

The usual move is to eat and drink in the Altstadt, Düsseldorf's old town by the Rhine, then head out to Rath. The local beer is **Altbier**, a dark top-fermented ale served in small, straight glasses. In the traditional brewery pubs it is brought to you, not ordered at a bar.

A few things locals know:

- **The Köbes:** the blue-aproned waiter in a brewery pub keeps replacing your empty glass until you stop him.
- **How to stop:** lay your beer mat on top of the glass. Your tally goes on the mat in pencil marks.
- **Paying:** many old-town pubs still prefer cash, and rounding up the bill is the normal way to tip.
- **Small talk:** Düsseldorf and Cologne have a long-running rivalry. Asking for Kölsch here gets you a look.

Lindemann's audiences take the show seriously. Expect plenty of black clothing, plenty of fans singing the German lyrics word for word, and a crowd that mostly stays put until the last song.

## Making a weekend of it

November 21, 2026 falls on a Saturday, so plenty of fans turn the concert into a weekend away. Most stay in the centre, around the Hauptbahnhof or the Altstadt, rather than near the arena. The S-Bahn and trams make the trip back easy.

The rest of the weekend fills itself. Walk the Rheinuferpromenade along the river, look at Frank Gehry's crooked buildings at the MedienHafen, or browse the Königsallee, the city's canal-lined shopping street.
