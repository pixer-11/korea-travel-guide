## Rainforest at the end of a suburban street

Edge Hill is mostly a quiet residential suburb. Then Collins Avenue runs into a wall of palms, gingers and buttress-rooted trees, and the gardens begin. Mount Whitfield rises steeply behind them, covered in forest, and planes on approach to Cairns Airport pass low overhead.

This is one of the most-reviewed attractions in the Cairns area, and it rates consistently well. People come for the tropical plant collection. The gardens date back to an 1880s recreation reserve, and today the plantings are dense and layered, so the paths feel shaded and enclosed even at midday.

## Flecker Garden and the mirrored visitor centre

Start with the building that seems to vanish into the trees. The visitor centre is clad in mirrored panels, so the walls reflect the surrounding foliage and you can easily walk past it. Pick up a map there, and ask about the guided walks and audio tours.

Behind it is Flecker Garden, the oldest and most formal part of the gardens. Paths loop between beds grouped by theme, and these are the plants worth looking for:

- Heliconias and gingers, in bright red, orange and pink
- Palms and cycads, some with trunks taller than the canopy around them
- Bromeliads and ferns growing in the shade beneath them
- Plants with Aboriginal uses, labelled with how they were used for food and materials

The labels are the real draw here. Read them slowly and Flecker alone can fill an hour.

## Over the road: Centenary Lakes and the Gondwana trail

The gardens carry on across Collins Avenue. A raised boardwalk leads through lowland rainforest and paperbark swamp to Centenary Lakes, where there are two lakes: one freshwater and one saltwater. The saltwater lake connects to the tidal creek system, and the warning signs about estuarine crocodiles are meant seriously. Keep to the paths and away from the water's edge.

The Gondwana Evolutionary Trail runs through this part of the gardens. It walks you through plant evolution in order, starting with ancient ferns and cycads and finishing with flowering plants. It's a flat, easy stroll and a good way to see how old Queensland's forests really are. The lakes also attract plenty of birds, so binoculars are worth bringing.

## The Red Arrow before the heat

The best workout in Cairns starts at the edge of the gardens. The Red Arrow track climbs into Mount Whitfield Conservation Park and makes a loop of about 1.3km. Most of it is steep stairs and switchbacks under rainforest. A lookout partway up gives you a view across the city towards Trinity Inlet.

It's a short walk but a sweaty one in tropical humidity. Carry water, wear shoes with grip, and allow 45 minutes to an hour. Fit walkers can take the much longer Blue Arrow circuit further into the conservation park. Many locals treat the Red Arrow as their regular early workout, so if you climb straight after the 7:30am opening you'll be sharing the steps with them.

## Getting to Edge Hill

From the Cairns Esplanade, the gardens are a short drive or taxi ride north, roughly ten minutes. There's free parking around Collins Avenue and near the Tanks, but it fills up on busy weekend mornings.

Sunbus local buses run between the city centre and Edge Hill, so check the current timetable before you set off. A bike is another option: the route is mostly flat until you reach the foot of Mount Whitfield. From the airport, the gardens are close enough to visit on your first or last day in town.

## Beating the weekend morning crush

Timing makes a bigger difference here than anything else. Weekends are busiest from 8am to 2pm, when parking and the main paths around Flecker Garden fill with families and walking groups. If you can only come on a Saturday or Sunday, arrive after 2pm. That still leaves more than three hours before the 5:30pm close, and the light under the canopy softens later in the day.

How to fit in on the paths:

- Keep left on the boardwalk and the Red Arrow stairs. People coming down move fast.
- Use repellent. Mosquitoes and midges thrive near the swamp and lakes, especially in the wet season.
- Leave every flower and seed pod where it is. This is a living collection, and staff do notice.
- Ask at the visitor centre about guided walks, since their schedules change. If you'd rather go at your own pace, the audio tour is the alternative.

Seasons matter too. The wet season, roughly December to March, brings heavy afternoon downpours, the strongest growth and the most flowers. From May to October the air is drier and cooler, and the Red Arrow climb is much easier.

## Next door at the Tanks

Finish your visit at the Tanks Arts Centre, a short walk from Flecker Garden. It's housed in former World War II naval fuel storage tanks, which are huge round concrete shells now used as galleries and performance spaces. Inside, the curved walls give exhibitions and gigs an unusual echo.

Look up what's on before you go, because programming varies through the year. Even when nothing is showing, the tanks are worth seeing from outside, half swallowed by figs and vines. Put the gardens, the lakes, the Red Arrow and the Tanks together and you can easily fill a morning or a long afternoon in Edge Hill.
