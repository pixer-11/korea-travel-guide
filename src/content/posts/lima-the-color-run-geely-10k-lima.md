## A 10K in white, finished in colour

The Color Run started in the United States in 2011. It sold itself as a fun run, not a race. Lima's edition doubles the usual 5K format to 10 kilometres, and the Chinese carmaker Geely is the title sponsor.

The idea is simple. You set off in a white shirt. At colour stations along the route, volunteers throw powdered paint at you, and by the finish you are pink, blue, yellow and green from head to shoe.

Nobody is chasing a podium. Families, office groups and friends in matching tutus walk and jog the same course. The finish area works as a party with music and mass colour throws.

## Estadio Monumental U and the route out east

The run starts at the Estadio Monumental, home of Universitario de Deportes ("la U") and the largest stadium in Peru. It sits in the Ate district, just off Avenida Javier Prado Este. The grounds around the stadium are huge, with plenty of room for start corrals and a finish festival.

Getting there from the coast takes some planning:

- **From Miraflores or Barranco:** Javier Prado runs east to the stadium. A taxi or ride-hail takes far less time on a Sunday morning than on a weekday.
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber, Cabify and InDrive all work across Lima. Most runners use them because finishing covered in powder makes buses awkward.
- **Driving:** the stadium is built for football crowds, but race-day road closures around the complex are standard for events this size.

November is late spring in Lima. Mornings are often grey, and the sun tends to break through later. That suits a 10K: it's mild, not humid, and rarely wet.

## Kits, powder and the morning itself

Peruvian races hand out a registration kit, the *entrega de kits*. Most runners collect it in the days before the run, not at the start line. The Color Run kit traditionally includes the white race shirt and a bib. The organiser publishes the pickup details alongside the ticket links.

What runners typically bring or wear:

- **The white kit shirt**, plus old shorts and shoes you don't mind staining
- **Sunglasses or a buff**: the powder is cornstarch-based but still gets in eyes and mouths
- **A zip-lock bag for your phone**; fine powder gets into ports and cases
- **A towel or old sheet for the ride home**; drivers appreciate it

A 10K at a relaxed pace takes most participants 60–90 minutes, often longer with photo stops at every colour station. With the finish party, people usually spend most of the morning there.

## Paying, talking and getting home in Lima

Lima pays by phone. Peruvians use **Yape** and **Plin** for small purchases at food and drink stalls. Cards work at bigger vendors, and soles in coins cover the rest. Foreign visitors without a Peruvian bank account rely on cards and cash.

A little Spanish helps at the venue. Your T-shirt is a *polo*, your bib is the *número* or *dorsal*, and the start line is the *largada*. Volunteers shouting "¡color!" mean a powder station is coming.

The exit is the one bottleneck. Thousands of people leave the same complex at once, and ride-hail prices and waits rise around the stadium. Many runners walk a few blocks along Javier Prado before ordering a car. Others stay for the closing colour throw and leave once the crowd thins.

Afterwards, many visitors head back west to Miraflores for lunch. The ceviche spots there are busiest around Sunday midday, the traditional time for ceviche in Lima.
