## One Night in Bukit Jalil

Seven members, one date, one arena. YG Entertainment's BABYMONSTER was set to bring the CHOOM World Tour to Unifi Arena in Kuala Lumpur on November 14, 2026. *Choom* (춤) is simply the Korean word for dance, which says what the group puts front and centre.

The lineup announced was the group itself: Ruka, Pharita, Asa, Ahyeon, Rami, Rora and Chiquita. Anyone who knows them from "Sheesh", "Batter Up" or "Drip" knows the sound: hard-hitting choreography, rap verses and big vocal hooks.

It was the only Malaysian date on the tour as announced.

## The Arena You Might Know by Another Name

Unifi Arena is the indoor hall inside the Bukit Jalil National Sports Complex, south of the city centre. Many Malaysians still call it by its former name, Axiata Arena, and both names come up with drivers and in fan chats. It sits right beside Bukit Jalil National Stadium, the huge open-air bowl next door.

Inside, it's an enclosed, air-conditioned arena with tiered seating wrapping around the floor. That cooling is a big deal in a city where November evenings stay warm and humid. Entrances are spread around the complex, which is large, and walking round the wrong side of the stadium takes longer than most people think.

## Getting to Bukit Jalil

Rail beats the road on a concert night. These are the main options:

- **LRT Sri Petaling Line to Bukit Jalil station.** This is the closest station to the sports complex, and the arena is a walk from there.
- **From KL Sentral or Masjid Jamek,** change onto the Sri Petaling Line. Masjid Jamek is the interchange where it meets the Kelana Jaya Line.
- **Grab** is the ride-hailing app everyone uses here. Traffic on the roads around Bukit Jalil slows as doors open and again when a show ends.
- **Driving** is possible, but parking at the complex fills up fast on big event nights.

From Bukit Bintang or KLCC, the trip is on the LRT. It's simple but not short.

## The Bukit Jalil Exit Shuffle

When a show ends, thousands of people head for one small LRT station. The platform at Bukit Jalil was never built for a full arena emptying at once. Staff usually meter the crowd onto the trains, which means a slow, patient queue in the open air.

There are three usual ways out:

- **Staying put for a while.** The first wave goes, and the walk to the station is easier once the crowd thins.
- **Walking off the rush.** Pavilion Bukit Jalil, the shopping mall near the complex, is a good place to wait for traffic to clear.
- **Grab, with a surge.** Wait times and fares climb right after a show, and pickup points get crowded.

Rapid KL publishes last-train times for the line, and trains don't run all night.

## Tickets Without the Heartbreak

Scams follow sold-out K-pop shows. Tickets were sold through the ticketing partner and promoter named in the official tour announcement from BABYMONSTER and YG Entertainment. Screenshots on social media are a common way fakes get passed around.

A few points about K-pop ticketing in general:

- **Resale offers in fan groups** are where most people lose money. A buyer can't tell a genuine e-ticket from a reused QR code.
- **The name on the ticket** often has to match the holder's ID at entry, depending on the terms of the order.
- **Seating at the arena** splits into the floor area and the tiered seats, and the two feel completely different.

## Arena Etiquette, Malaysian Style

Malaysian K-pop crowds are loud, organised and generous. Before big shows, fans often hand out free stuff near the venue: photocards, stickers, snacks and fan-made banners. Taking one is fine. A thank-you and a smile is the usual reply.

How fans blend in on a show night:

- **The official lightstick** is the big one. MONSTIEZ (the fandom name) fill arenas with it, and the official app can sync it to the show.
- **Fanchants** are shouted at fixed points in the songs, and following along is half the fun.
- **Card and e-wallet** work almost everywhere. Touch 'n Go eWallet is used all over KL, though small stalls outside often take cash.
- **Bag and camera rules** at arenas like this are strict. Banned items get taken at the gate and nobody gives them back.
- **The city's dress norms** are relaxed, and a light layer makes the LRT and the air-conditioned arena more comfortable.

## Making a Weekend of It

November 14, 2026 is a Saturday, which made the show a natural weekend trip. November falls in KL's wetter season, and sudden afternoon thunderstorms are common. A compact umbrella or rain jacket earns its place on the walk from the station.

For a Sunday, the Petronas Twin Towers and KLCC Park are an easy trip back up the line. Bukit Bintang has Jalan Alor's street food after dark, and Batu Caves works as a morning trip on the KTM Komuter from KL Sentral. Merch lines at arena shows tend to be long, and the widest choice usually goes in the first hours of sales.
