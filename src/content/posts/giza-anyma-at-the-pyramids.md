## A techno show in front of a 4,500-year-old tomb

The setting is the whole point. Anyma performs at the Great Pyramid of Giza on March 19, 2027. The Great Pyramid is the largest of the three pyramids on the plateau, built for the pharaoh Khufu, and it is the last survivor of the ancient Seven Wonders.

The plateau is a limestone shelf of sand and rubble on the edge of the Western Desert. The city ends abruptly at its foot. The apartment blocks and rooftops of Nazlet el-Samman press right up against the ticket gates, so the show sits between a sprawling modern city and open desert.

## Who Anyma is, and why this backdrop fits

Anyma is the solo project of Matteo Milleri, one half of the Italian duo Tale Of Us and a central figure on the Afterlife label. His shows are built around towering visuals: chrome figures, giant digital faces and slow cinematic builds timed to melodic techno.

That style suits a backdrop made of 2.3 million stone blocks. The pyramids also have a long concert history. The Grateful Dead played at the foot of the Sphinx in 1978, and Jean-Michel Jarre staged his millennium show on the plateau in 1999. This date continues that line, with electronic music on the same monumental scale.

## Reaching the plateau from Cairo

Giza is part of Greater Cairo, and getting there is mostly a question of traffic. Downtown Cairo is roughly 15–20 km away. The drive can take anywhere from half an hour to well over an hour, depending on the Ring Road and Pyramids Road.

- **Ride-hailing:** Uber and Careem both work across Cairo and Giza. Most visitors use them for the trip out.
- **Metro:** Giza station on Line 2 is a long way from the pyramids. It is no use on foot, so you would still need a taxi from there.
- **From the airport:** Cairo International Airport is on the far eastern side of the city. A transfer straight to Giza crosses the whole metropolis.
- **Pre-arranged drivers:** these are common among hotel guests. A driver also solves the return trip, when hundreds of people want a car at the same moment.

## Where people stay for a pyramid show

Staying close cuts out the cross-city drive. Marriott Mena House sits at the foot of the plateau, in a former royal hunting lodge. Along the streets of Nazlet el-Samman, small guesthouses advertise rooftop terraces facing the pyramids.

The Grand Egyptian Museum stands on the plateau's edge. It makes a natural daytime pairing for anyone in town for the concert, since its galleries hold Tutankhamun's treasures. Visitors who prefer cafés and nightlife usually base themselves in Zamalek or Downtown Cairo and accept the longer ride.

## Getting through the night on the sand

Most of the night is about the ground and the temperature. Desert nights in March turn cool after a warm day, so concertgoers usually bring a layer for after sunset. The surface is sand and loose grit, which is hard going in sandals and open shoes.

At large plateau events, fans typically reach the area well before the music starts. The approach roads are narrow and traffic slows at the security checkpoints. Bag searches are standard at Egyptian monument sites.

## Moving around Giza the way Cairenes do

Most of the daily friction in Giza is about money and approaches. Small payments run on cash in Egyptian pounds: drivers, snack stalls and tips, known locally as *baksheesh*. Small notes make every one of those easier. Cards work in hotels and larger restaurants, and the ride-hailing apps take cards.

The approaches to the pyramids are known for persistent touts offering camel rides, horse rides and "shortcuts." A firm, friendly *la shukran* ("no, thank you") while you keep walking is the usual response. Agree on a taxi fare before getting in, or use an app so the price is fixed.

The organiser publishes the date, venue details and ticket links on its official site. That is the reference for entry rules and what is allowed through the gates.
