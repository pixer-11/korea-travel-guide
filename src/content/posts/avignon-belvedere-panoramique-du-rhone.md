The Rhône is wide at Avignon, and it runs faster than most visitors expect. From the Belvédère Panoramique du Rhône you look across that water at a second town and a broken bridge. The walled city sits behind you.

Its listed address is just an "Unnamed Road" in 84000 Avignon. That tells you something: this is a viewing point, not a building. It's well rated by the people who find it, and there aren't many of them yet.

## The view, piece by piece

The river is what fills the frame. On the Avignon bank it curves past the old city, and the Île de la Barthelasse lies flat and green in the middle of it. Barthelasse is one of the largest river islands in France, mostly farmland and orchards.

These are the landmarks to look for:

- **Pont Saint-Bénézet**, the Pont d'Avignon from the song, with its four surviving arches ending in mid-river.
- **Villeneuve-lès-Avignon** on the far bank, where the Tour Philippe le Bel guarded the bridge's western end.
- **Fort Saint-André**, whose twin towers sit on the hill above Villeneuve.
- **The Mont Ventoux ridge** far to the east on clear days, if your angle faces that way.

The pull of this spot is that you see the river frontier the popes faced. In the 14th century the Rhône was the border between the papal enclave and the Kingdom of France. The fortifications on each bank were built to watch each other.

## Finding a road with no name

Because the listing has no street address, drop a pin from the map listing on your phone before you set out. Don't try to work it out on arrival. Avignon's walled centre is small, but its lanes twist, and the ramparts cut you off from the riverbank except at the gates.

If you're coming by train, Avignon Centre station sits just outside the walls by the Porte de la République. From there, rue de la République runs straight up to Place de l'Horloge and the Palais des Papes. From Avignon TGV, a short shuttle train runs to Avignon Centre.

Most of the old town is easiest on foot. Driving inside the walls is restricted and slow. If you come by car, park outside the ramparts and walk in.

## Working with the hours

At the moment the listing shows 7:30am–6pm, the same every day of the week. Treat that as this season's schedule, not a fixed rule. Viewpoints and gardens in Avignon change their hours through the year, so check the current times before you plan an evening visit.

Within those hours, the light does most of the work. Early in the morning the sun comes from behind the city, which lights up Villeneuve and the bridge arches from the front. By late afternoon you're looking more into the light and the river turns silver.

Twenty minutes is enough for photos. Stay forty if you want to pick out each tower and watch the barges and river shuttles.

## Making the most of a quiet lookout

This place has no ticket desk, café or guide. Its quiet is the attraction, and it's easy to keep it that way.

- **Bring water and anything else you need.** You won't find a kiosk at a spot like this, so buy supplies in town first.
- **Keep your voice down.** Sound carries over the water.
- **Bring a zoom lens or binoculars.** The details on Fort Saint-André and the Tour Philippe le Bel are worth seeing up close.
- **Brace against the mistral.** This cold north wind funnels down the Rhône valley, sometimes for days. Hold on to hats and light tripods, and pack a layer even in spring.
- **Take your rubbish with you.** Small viewpoints rarely have bins, and they stay pleasant only while visitors do this.

French greetings still apply on the walk there. Say a "bonjour" when you pass someone on a narrow path; it's expected.

## Building a half day around it

The lookout works best as one stop on a loop rather than a destination in itself. Pair it with:

- **The Palais des Papes**, the huge Gothic palace that dominates the town above Place du Palais.
- **The Rocher des Doms**, the garden on the rock beside the cathedral, with its own terraces over the river.
- **Pont Saint-Bénézet itself**, which you can walk out onto after seeing it from a distance.
- **Les Halles d'Avignon** on Place Pie, the covered food market, for lunch or picnic supplies.

To see the view from the other direction, cross the river to Villeneuve-lès-Avignon. From Fort Saint-André the whole skyline of Avignon is laid out across the water. The river shuttle to Barthelasse, which runs in the warmer months, is another way to get out onto the water.

## Who it suits

Come here if the busy squares around the palace have worn you out and you want twenty quiet minutes facing the river. It suits photographers, people who like to see a city's geography laid out, and anyone curious why Avignon was built where it is.

Skip it if you only have a few hours in town. The Rocher des Doms gives a similar view right next to the main sights.
