## A building that argues with you

This museum doesn't try to look calm. Architects Ashton Raggatt McDougall designed it, and it opened in 2001 on the Acton Peninsula. You get clashing colours, sharp angles, and a giant orange loop that curls out of the roof and points toward the lake. It's one of the most-reviewed attractions in the capital, and people keep arguing about the building as much as the collection.

Inside, the galleries tell Australia's story from deep Indigenous time through colonisation and on to modern suburbia. The outdoor Garden of Australian Dreams sits between the wings. It's a courtyard printed with a huge, jumbled map of the continent that you can walk across.

## What to seek out

Give yourself two to three hours. The permanent galleries are large, so choose before you wander. Worth prioritising:

- **First Australians gallery:** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander objects, art and stories, the core of the museum's 50,000-year span.
- **Phar Lap's heart:** the enormous heart of the champion racehorse, kept here while his hide is in Melbourne.
- **Holden Prototype No. 1:** the car behind Australia's home-built motoring story.
- **Garden of Australian Dreams:** best seen from the upper walkways, where the map pattern reads clearly.

General admission to the permanent galleries is free. Special exhibitions are ticketed, and the current details are posted on the museum's official website.

## Timing the crowds on Acton Peninsula

The hours are simple: 9am to 5pm, seven days a week. The crowds follow a sharper pattern.

- **Weekdays:** quiet the whole day, 9am–5pm. Any weekday slot works.
- **Weekends:** only the first hour, 9am–10am, is calm.
- **Avoid:** weekends from 11am to 5pm. That's when families and school-holiday groups fill the galleries and the café.

On a weekend, go straight to the busiest galleries at opening. Leave the garden and the lakeside for later, because outdoor space absorbs a crowd far better than a gallery does.

## Getting there and making a day of it

The address is Lawson Crescent, Acton, about 2km west of the City (Civic) centre. You can walk it through the Australian National University campus and along the lakeshore in around half an hour. There's on-site parking, but check the museum website for current arrangements before you drive.

A few habits help the visit go smoothly:

- Pick up a floor map at the entrance. The layout loops, and gallery order isn't obvious.
- Photography for personal use is generally allowed in the permanent galleries. Watch for signs in loan exhibitions and around some Indigenous material, where restrictions apply out of cultural respect.
- The café looks out over Lake Burley Griffin. Eat early on weekends, before the late-morning rush reaches it.

Nearby, the National Film and Sound Archive is also in Acton, a short walk back toward the city. Across the water are the National Library and the Parliamentary Triangle, so you can cover two institutions in a day. The peninsula's lakeside paths are the best place to see the building's loop and angles in full.
