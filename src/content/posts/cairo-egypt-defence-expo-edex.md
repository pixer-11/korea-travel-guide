## A defence fair on the desert edge

EDEX is a trade show, not a public day out. Egypt's biennial international defence and security exhibition fills the halls of the Egypt International Exhibition Centre (EIEC) in New Cairo. Exhibitors and official delegations come from around the world, and the 2026 edition is scheduled for December.

Inside, expect national pavilions, manufacturers' stands and static displays of vehicles and equipment. Most of the business happens in meeting rooms and at stand tables. The first edition was held in 2018, and the show has since grown into one of the main defence gatherings in the Middle East and Africa.

## Reaching the EIEC in New Cairo

The venue sits on El Moshir Tantawy Axis in New Cairo, east of the old city. Cairo Metro does not reach it, so nearly everyone arrives by road.

- **From Cairo International Airport:** usually the quickest leg, a direct drive south along the ring-road network.
- **From Downtown or Zamalek:** expect around an hour or more, as the Ring Road and Salah Salem traffic decide the timing.
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber and Careem both operate widely across Cairo and are how most independent visitors get to the gate.
- **Delegations:** official groups typically travel by organised shuttle or embassy transport.

Most visitors base themselves in New Cairo and the Fifth Settlement, or near the airport in Heliopolis. Both cut the daily commute sharply compared with staying by the Nile.

## Registration and badges

There is no general-admission ticket to buy at the gate. Access runs through registration for industry professionals, government and military delegations, and accredited media. The organiser publishes the dates, visitor categories and registration links on the official EDEX website, and no price is given here.

Badge collection involves identity checks. International attendees usually carry the passport they registered with, and security screening at the entrance is thorough. Large bags slow the process, so regulars tend to carry a slim briefcase or nothing at all.

## Working the halls the Cairo way

Business dress is the norm: suits for most men, smart formal wear for women. December in Cairo brings mild, sunny days and noticeably cool evenings, so a jacket earns its place after dark.

A few habits that make the days smoother:

- **Photography:** many stands restrict it, and defence exhibitors take this seriously. Visitors ask before raising a phone.
- **Greetings:** a handshake and an exchanged business card open most conversations; "Salam alaikum" is warmly received.
- **Cash:** Egyptian pounds in small notes cover tips for drivers, porters and café staff, even where cards are accepted.
- **Coffee:** meetings often begin with tea or Turkish coffee, and declining outright can come across as abrupt.
- **Pacing:** opening-day ceremonies and VIP tours can close off sections of the halls for a time, which is why regulars schedule key meetings for later in the run.

Outside show hours, New Cairo's malls and restaurant strips around the Fifth Settlement handle most business dinners. The Pyramids of Giza and the Grand Egyptian Museum sit on the far side of the city, so delegates usually set aside a separate half-day for them rather than squeezing them in after the halls close.
