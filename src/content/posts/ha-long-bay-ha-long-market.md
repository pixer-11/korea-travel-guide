## What's actually there

Ha Long Market sits in Vạn Xuân, in the Hồng Gai ward of Quảng Ninh province, a few minutes from the bay's main harbour front. It's a real supply market: fishmongers, crab and squid sellers, produce stalls, flower vendors, all under one roof or along covered rows. This isn't a curated night market for photos. It's where restaurants and households buy their fish.

The seafood section is the reason to come. Tanks and buckets hold live crab, shrimp, and squid, often still moving, priced by weight and sold by vendors who expect you to look before you buy. Produce and flower stalls fill out the rest, giving the place its color more than its scale.

## When to go

The market runs 6:00 AM to 6:30 PM every day of the week, no closing day. Weekends are the crunch point: foot traffic builds steadily from 9am and stays heavy until closing at 6pm. If you want to move through aisles without shuffling behind tour groups, arrive close to opening, before 9am, especially on a Saturday or Sunday.

Weekday mornings are calmer by default, since the weekend rush is specifically called out and weekdays aren't. Early is also when the seafood is freshest, since stock turns over as boats and suppliers deliver through the morning.

## Walking the market like a buyer, not a tourist

Vendors here sell to locals and restaurant buyers, so the rhythm is transactional, not performative.

- Cash is the norm; small bills help, since change for large notes can be slow at a stall selling live crab out of a bucket.
- Prices aren't usually posted for seafood; ask before you commit to a look, and expect the vendor to weigh in front of you.
- If you're not buying, a nod and moving on is fine. Lingering to photograph someone's stall without buying can draw a flat stare, not hostility, just business as usual.
- Wear shoes you don't mind getting wet. Floors near the fish section stay damp from ice and rinse water all day.
- The flower and produce rows near the edges are a calmer lap if the seafood aisles feel too tight or too pungent.

## Fitting it into a bay itinerary

Ha Long Market isn't a standalone half-day trip. Pair it with a walk along the Hồng Gai waterfront or a stop before heading out to the bay's boat piers, since it sits inland from the main tourist harbour rather than on it. It's rated well by a large volume of reviewers, a sign it's firmly on the well-known side of things rather than an undiscovered stop, so treat the early-morning window as your main tool for a better visit rather than expecting to have it to yourself.

If you want food rather than groceries, the market itself is about buying raw seafood, not eating on the spot; plan to cook it back at accommodation with a kitchen, or ask a nearby restaurant if they'll prepare what you've bought.
