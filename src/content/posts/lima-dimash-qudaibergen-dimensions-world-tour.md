## A first night in Peru

Peru has never had a Dimash concert before. That changes on December 1, 2026, when the Dimensions World Tour reaches Costa 21 in Lima.

For a lot of South American fans this is the nearest the tour gets. Dimash built his name across Asia and Central Asia, and that following is enormous. Latin American fans, who mostly found him through clips online, have waited a long time to hear that voice in a field instead of through headphones.

## The voice people cross borders for

Dimash Qudaibergen is a Kazakh singer known for a vocal range that runs from deep baritone to whistle-register notes. Many people outside Kazakhstan first saw him on the Chinese competition show *Singer* in 2017. His version of "SOS d'un terrien en détresse" there spread far beyond China.

His repertoire mixes classical technique, pop and Kazakh folk. A setlist for any given night is not part of what is known here, so expect nothing in particular. His fans call themselves "Dears", and the community is famously international:

- Fans travel from neighbouring countries for a single date.
- National flags are a common sight in the crowd at his shows.
- Many fans learn lyrics in Kazakh, Russian or Chinese phonetically.

## Costa 21, on the edge of the Pacific

Costa 21 is not an arena. It is a large open-air esplanade on the Costa Verde in the San Miguel district. The cliffs of Lima's coast rise behind it and the Circuito de Playas highway runs alongside.

That setting shapes the night. You stand or sit on flat ground under open sky, with the ocean close by. December is early summer in Lima and the grey winter haze is usually lifting. Evenings by the water still pick up a sea breeze, so concertgoers usually bring a light layer.

Seating layout, zones and ticket categories are specific to this production. The organiser's ticketing page is where those details live. Fans of the tour follow announcements through Dimash's official site and social channels.

## Getting to San Miguel and back

Most visitors base themselves in Miraflores or Barranco. Those two coastal districts have the densest choice of hotels and restaurants. Both sit south along the same Costa Verde shoreline, so the trip to Costa 21 is a straight run up the coast road. San Miguel itself has plenty of hotels near the Plaza San Miguel shopping centre.

Jorge Chávez International Airport is in Callao, next door to San Miguel. That makes Costa 21 one of the easier big Lima venues to reach for fans flying in the same day. Parque de las Leyendas, the zoo and archaeological park, is also in San Miguel if you have a spare afternoon.

## Leaving the Costa Verde without the scrum

At the end of a big show the coast road is the bottleneck. This is how Lima concertgoers usually handle it:

- **Ride-hailing apps.** Uber, Cabify and InDrive are all in common use in Lima. Many people walk a few blocks inland before requesting a car, because pickups right at the exit get jammed.
- **Agreeing on a meeting point.** Phone signal can strain when thousands of people leave at once. Groups often fix a landmark beforehand.
- **Paying.** The local currency is the Peruvian sol. Cards are widely accepted in Miraflores and Barranco. Street vendors outside venues tend to prefer cash or Yape, the mobile payment app most Peruvians use.
- **Street taxis.** Locals rarely flag random taxis late at night. Apps or official taxi ranks are the norm.

Spanish is the working language at the gates and at food stands. A few phrases help: *entrada* (ticket), *puerta* (gate), *salida* (exit).

## Making a weekend of it

December 1, 2026 falls on a Tuesday. Fans travelling in often turn the date into a few days in Lima, and the city makes that easy.

- **Eat:** ceviche at lunch, which is when Lima eats it, and lomo saltado or anticuchos in the evening.
- **Walk:** the Malecón clifftop path in Miraflores, which ends at Larcomar above the ocean.
- **Look:** Barranco's Puente de los Suspiros and its painted streets, or the colonial centre around Plaza Mayor.
