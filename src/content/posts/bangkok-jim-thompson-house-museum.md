## Teak walls beside a working canal

You reach it through a narrow lane. Soi Kasem San 2 runs off Rama I Road, and at the end of it is a garden that blocks out the traffic. Inside are raised teak houses with steep red-tiled roofs. They stand on pillars over planting thick enough that you can hardly see the city beyond.

Jim Thompson was an American who settled in Bangkok after the Second World War. He built an international market for hand-woven Thai silk. In 1959 he had several old teak houses taken apart and rebuilt here, some brought from Ayutthaya. He vanished on a walk in Malaysia's Cameron Highlands in 1967 and was never found. The case remains one of Southeast Asia's best-known mysteries.

## What the tour shows you

The houses can only be entered on a guided tour. Guides lead small groups in English and several other languages, and the tour runs about 45 minutes to an hour. Expect to see:

- **The joined houses**: Thompson linked separate buildings into one home, turning the wall panels inward so the carved sides face the rooms.
- **Raised thresholds**: you step over them between rooms. They were traditionally built to keep out spirits and floodwater.
- **His collection**: Buddha images, Chinese porcelain, Burmese carvings and old Thai paintings, kept where he placed them.
- **The canal view**: the main house faces Khlong Saen Saeb. Across the water is Ban Khrua, the Muslim weaving community whose silk Thompson first sold.

The garden and courtyard are free to walk at your own pace once the tour ends. Allow 90 minutes in total, including time in the shop.

## Reaching Soi Kasem San 2

The BTS Skytrain gets you closest.

- **BTS:** National Stadium station (Silom Line), Exit 1. Walk along Rama I Road and turn down Soi Kasem San 2. It takes about five minutes.
- **Canal boat:** the Khlong Saen Saeb express boat stops at Hua Chang pier, a short walk across the bridge. It's a quick way in from the old town side.
- **Combine it:** the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre and MBK Center are just across the junction from National Stadium station. Siam Square is one stop east.

The museum is currently open 10am–5pm every day this week. Museum hours can change with holidays and over the year, so check the official site before going late in the afternoon.

## Shoes off, cameras down

The houses run on house rules, and guides enforce them.

- **Footwear:** you take your shoes off before climbing into the houses. The teak floors are smooth, so socks are fine but can slip.
- **Photography:** it is not allowed inside the houses. You can take photos freely in the garden and from the terraces.
- **Joining a tour:** tours leave in turns and group sizes are limited. At the desk you're given a tour time in your language, then you wait in the garden until it's called.
- **Timing:** this is one of Bangkok's most visited small museums. It is usually quietest from 10am to 11am, so arrive at the 10am opening and you will likely get a smaller group.
- **Dress:** no strict code applies, but you climb stairs and step over thresholds. Clothes you can move in work better than a tight skirt.

Admission is posted at the ticket desk and on the official website. Leave large bags in the lockers provided, because the rooms are tight and full of fragile objects.
