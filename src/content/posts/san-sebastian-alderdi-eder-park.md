## What you're looking at

Alderdi Eder is the green wedge between the Ayuntamiento building (San Sebastián's former casino, now city hall) and the curve of Playa de la Concha. Palms and tamarind trees line gravel paths, with flowerbeds cut into geometric patterns that change with the season. A small carousel runs near the seafront edge, a fixture here for generations. The whole park covers only a few thousand square metres, so it reads more as an elegant threshold than a destination park.

## Getting there and getting around it

It's a flat, five-minute walk from Parte Vieja (the old town) or from the Boulevard, San Sebastián's main pedestrian spine. The nearest bus stops serving the centre (Alameda del Boulevard) sit right at the park's edge. There's no gate and no ticket: it's open around the clock, every day.

- Enter from the Boulevard side for the formal gardens and fountain
- Enter from the beach side to walk straight onto La Concha's sand
- The carousel operates on its own daytime schedule, separate from park hours

Because it's essentially a corridor between the old town, the Ayuntamiento, and the beach, most visitors pass through rather than linger, which keeps foot traffic constantly moving even when the park itself feels calm.

## When to come

This is one of San Sebastián's most-visited green spaces, rated highly by tens of thousands of reviewers, and it shows: weekends from 11am to 11pm bring dense pedestrian traffic, especially with beach crowds spilling in. If you want the gardens and palms without the crush, arrive between 7am and 8am on a weekday, or 7am to 9am on weekends. Early light also suits photos of the Ayuntamiento's facade without people crossing the frame.

Midday in high summer means shade is scarce; the tamarind trees give some cover, but the open lawns don't. Late afternoon into evening is when the carousel draws families, so expect more noise and slower walking pace near the seafront path.

## Making the most of a short stop

Treat Alderdi Eder as a five-to-thirty-minute add-on, not a standalone outing. There's no entry fee and nothing to book, so the only real decision is timing.

- Pair it with a Concha beach walk: exit park side, follow the promenade west
- Combine with the Ayuntamiento's exterior and the nearby funicular to Monte Igueldo, a short walk further along the bay
- Skip bringing food to eat on the lawns; it's a passage space, not a picnic park, and benches fill fast on weekends
- If travelling with young kids, time your visit around the carousel's daytime hours rather than early morning, since the ride itself won't be running yet at 7am

Because it's unticketed and open-air, there's no real etiquette minefield here: locals and visitors alike just walk through, sit briefly on a bench, or let kids ride the carousel before continuing to the beach or old town.
