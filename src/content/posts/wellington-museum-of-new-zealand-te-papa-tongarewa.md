## Six floors on the harbour edge

Te Papa is a big angular block that sits right where Wellington's waterfront promenade meets the harbour. The name translates roughly as "container of treasures". It opened in 1998 as the country's national museum, covering Māori and colonial history, art and natural history in a single building.

It is one of the most-reviewed attractions in the country and very well rated. Expect school groups, families and cruise passengers, not a quiet hall. The building rests on base isolators, rubber-and-lead bearings that let it ride out earthquakes. Few places explain the city's shaky ground better.

## What to see first

Head up rather than wandering the ground floor. The essentials:

- **Te Taiao Nature**: the colossal squid, the only intact specimen on display anywhere, plus a house that shakes to simulate an earthquake.
- **Rongomaraeroa marae**: a working marae with a carved meeting house, Te Hono ki Hawaiki, built for the museum.
- **Toi Art**: New Zealand's national art collection, spread over two upper levels.
- **Gallipoli: The Scale of Our War**: Weta Workshop's oversized, hyper-real soldiers. It has run for years, but confirm it is still open before you plan around it.

You need three to four hours to see all of it without rushing. With two hours, pick Te Taiao and the marae.

## Moving through the marae and galleries

Te Papa treats the marae as a living space, not a display. Before you step into the meeting house your shoes come off, and visitors keep their voices down inside. Ask before photographing taonga (treasured objects) in areas where signs request it. Staff wearing name badges, called hosts, will explain protocol if you ask.

Daily guided tours and family activities start from the ground floor information desk. Signing up there is the quickest way to make sense of the Māori collections. Payment in the shop and cafés is contactless card as standard. Tipping is not expected in New Zealand. Go at the 10am opening if you can: you get about an hour before school groups fill the nature galleries.

Opening hours are currently 10am–6pm every day of this week. Museum hours shift around holidays and special events, so check the official Te Papa site before you go. Entry charges and paid exhibitions are listed on the same site.

## Getting there and what's next door

From Wellington Railway Station, walk south along the waterfront past Queens Wharf. It is flat the whole way and takes about 15 minutes. Buses along Courtenay Place stop a short block away, and the Airport Express bus runs into the city centre. Driving is a poor option: there is an underground car park on Cable Street, but it fills up.

Afterwards you have several options within walking distance:

- **Oriental Bay**: the sandy city beach is about 10 minutes east along the promenade.
- **Cuba Street**: cafés and secondhand shops are about 10 minutes inland through Te Aro.
- **Wellington Museum**: on Queens Wharf, for the city's maritime story.
- **Wellington Cable Car**: leaves from Lambton Quay up to the Botanic Garden, roughly 20 minutes away on foot.
