## A YG group, a baseball dome

PULSE ON brings TREASURE to a dome stage in Osaka. The YG Entertainment group plays Kyocera Dome Osaka in late 2026. Tens of thousands of seats rise from the field to the roof in one continuous bowl, and every one of them faces the same stage.

TREASURE debuted in 2020. Japan has been central to the group's touring from early on, and its Japanese fandom is large and organised. A dome booking puts this Osaka stop among the biggest rooms the group plays anywhere. The organiser publishes the exact dates and ticket links on its official site.

## Inside Kyocera Dome Osaka

From the outside the dome looks like a stack of silver discs. It sits beside the Kizu and Shirinashi rivers on the western side of central Osaka. It opened in 1997 as Osaka Dome. It is home ground for the Orix Buffaloes baseball team, and it is one of Japan's main venues for big touring concerts.

For a concert the stage usually stands at one end of the field. Floor seats, called *arena* seats, are laid out on the turf. Above them the stands climb in tiers, and the upper levels are steep.

From the upper levels the members are small figures and the screens do most of the work. That's also where the light-stick waves look best, rolling around the whole bowl. Because the roof is closed, the show runs the same whatever the weather.

## Three stations, three crowds

Kyocera Dome is unusual because three railway lines stop beside it. Most of the crowd splits between them.

- **Dome-mae Chiyozaki** (Osaka Metro Nagahori Tsurumi-ryokuchi Line): the closest station, a few minutes' walk from the dome. It's the obvious choice from Shinsaibashi.
- **Dome-mae** (Hanshin Namba Line): handy from Osaka Namba and the Kobe side, with through trains toward Kobe-Sannomiya.
- **Taisho** (JR Osaka Loop Line, plus the same Metro line): a slightly longer walk across the river bridge. It's the easiest route from Umeda and Osaka Station.

The Loop Line and the Metro link Taisho to almost every hotel district. That's why many visitors coming from Umeda or Tennoji use it, even though the walk is longer.

## Getting through the gate

Big K-pop shows in Japan usually run on the Japanese system. Tickets are sold by lottery, mostly through the group's official fan club and partner ticketing platforms, rather than first come, first served.

Tickets are commonly digital, held in a smartphone app. Seats are often revealed only shortly before the show. For many dome concerts in Japan the ticket is tied to a name, and staff may ask for photo ID at entry. Overseas fans usually carry their passport for that reason.

Resale outside official channels is widely banned at Japanese concerts. A ticket bought that way can be refused at the door. The organiser's official site lists the authorised ways to buy and transfer tickets.

## Lightsticks, goods lines and dome manners

A Japanese dome concert has its own unwritten rules. TREASURE's fans, known as Teume, generally keep to them.

The day starts at the official goods (merch) booths outside the dome. These often open hours before the show. Fans join the lines for towels, slogans, photo cards and the official light stick, which syncs with the show's lighting. Many buy goods online in advance and skip the line completely.

Things attendees typically do:

- **Keep phones down.** Japanese concerts generally ban photos and filming unless staff announce otherwise. Ushers do enforce it.
- **Stay in your seat block.** Standing is normal at your own seat, but moving between blocks isn't.
- **Hold signs at chest height.** Slogan towels and fan boards are held low so they don't block the view behind.
- **Use coin lockers at the stations.** The ones at Taisho and Namba fill up on show days. Fans with big bags often leave them at the hotel.
- **Pay by IC card.** Suica or ICOCA work on every train line to the dome and at most convenience stores nearby.

Fan chants are learned beforehand from official chant guides. The crowd shouts the member names together on the right beats. Arriving knowing the chants is the clearest sign of respect a new fan can show.

## When the encore ends

A sold-out dome takes a long time to empty. Staff normally let the crowd out one block at a time, with announcements in Japanese telling each section when to stand. Some fans wait at their seats for 20 to 40 minutes before their block is called.

After that the three stations each take a share of the crowd. Platforms at Dome-mae Chiyozaki get the most packed because it's closest. Some attendees walk to Taisho or Hanshin's Dome-mae instead, or head for Namba on foot and eat dinner on the way.

## Making a weekend of it in Osaka

The dome sits between Osaka's two main hotel districts, so either works.

- **Namba and Shinsaibashi:** one Metro stop on from the dome toward the centre. Here you'll find Dotonbori's canal-side neon, takoyaki stands and late-night ramen. This is the most common base for concertgoers.
- **Umeda:** the JR hub, best for anyone also travelling to Kyoto or out to Kansai Airport by train.
- **Kobe side:** the Hanshin Namba Line runs straight there, which suits fans splitting the trip between the two cities.

Shinsaibashi-suji's covered shopping street is also where the K-pop shops, photo card traders and café pop-ups cluster around Osaka's bigger tour dates. On show weekends many fans spend the hours before doors open shopping there, then take a short Metro ride to the dome.
