## A season opener on the edge of Bass Strait

Few circuits sit this close to the sea. Phillip Island Grand Prix Circuit runs along a clifftop on the island's southern shore. From most of the grass banks, the grey-blue water of Bass Strait fills the horizon behind the bikes.

The 2027 Superbike World Championship round takes place here from February 19 to 21, and it is the first round of the season. That gives the weekend a particular charge. Nobody has scored a point yet, new bikes and new rider pairings are racing for real for the first time, and the paddock carries a full winter of questions.

The round draws motorsport fans from across Australia and well beyond, including strong followings from Asia. On the banks you'll hear several languages and see a lot of team colours.

## Getting to the island

There is one road bridge onto Phillip Island. It crosses from San Remo to Newhaven, and every car, coach and motorbike heading to the circuit uses it. From Melbourne the usual route is the M1 Monash Freeway, then the South Gippsland and Bass Highways, about 140 km in all.

The circuit is on Back Beach Road, a short drive from Cowes, the island's main town. A huge share of spectators ride in on their own motorcycles, so expect the highway on race morning to be thick with bikes. Without a car, V/Line runs a train-and-coach connection from Melbourne to Cowes, and the last stretch to the track is a taxi or a long walk.

Race-day traffic backs up toward the bridge in the evening. Many fans either leave during the final support race or stay put until the queue clears.

## Lukey Heights, Siberia and the grass banks

The track is about 4.4 km long, runs anticlockwise and has a mix of very fast sweepers and hard braking points. Much of the spectating is on open grass slopes, and each corner gives you a different race to watch.

- **Gardner Straight and Doohan Corner (Turn 1):** the start, the pit lane and a downhill plunge into the first corner at full speed. This is where most overtakes are attempted on the opening lap.
- **Southern Loop and Stoner Corner:** a long, loaded left followed by a fast flick where you see riders hanging off the bike at a lean.
- **Honda Hairpin:** heavy braking and a tight turn, good for close-up action and late lunges.
- **Siberia and the Hayshed:** an exposed, windswept part of the lap that earns its name.
- **Lukey Heights and MG Hairpin:** a blind crest and a sharp descent, with one of the best panoramic views of the circuit.

Many regulars walk the perimeter over the weekend, moving corner to corner between sessions. The circuit is compact enough for that to work.

## Four seasons in one lap

The island's weather is the stuff of paddock legend. The circuit is exposed to wind straight off Bass Strait, and conditions can turn from hot sun to cold, wet gusts within the same afternoon. February is late summer in Victoria, but that offers no guarantee.

Experienced spectators pack for both extremes:

- sunscreen and a hat, since the grass banks have little shade
- a windproof, waterproof layer
- something to sit on, such as a folding chair or a blanket
- ear protection, especially for children

The wind also matters to the racing. Riders deal with gusts on the fast sections, and a cool track or a damp morning can shake up the order.

## How a WorldSBK weekend unfolds at Phillip Island

The weekend has a clear rhythm. Friday is usually practice, when the stands are quieter and the walk between corners is easiest. Saturday and Sunday carry the main business: in the championship's current format, qualifying (Superpole) sets the grid, and the points races are split across the two days. Support classes such as WorldSSP fill the gaps.

The organiser publishes the full timetable, ticket types and venue maps on its official site. Exact session times and race distances belong there, not in a guide.

Fans who come for all three days often camp. Campgrounds and caravan parks around the island and in San Remo fill with racing crowds, and many set up near the circuit so they can walk in. Day visitors from Melbourne usually aim to be over the bridge well before the first session, because the morning queue builds steadily.

At the circuit, payment is mostly by card or phone, in line with the rest of Australia. Tipping is not expected at food stalls. Most people bring water bottles and snacks for the long hours on the banks. The paddock and fan areas reward wandering, and many spectators spend part of a quieter session browsing team merchandise or the bike displays rather than watching every lap.

## Between sessions: the rest of the island

The racing finishes in the late afternoon, and the island has plenty for the evenings and the days either side.

- **Penguin Parade, Summerland Peninsula:** at dusk, little penguins come ashore and cross the beach to their burrows, watched from viewing stands. It's a short drive west of the circuit.
- **The Nobbies:** boardwalks over the cliffs at the western tip, looking out toward Seal Rocks.
- **Cape Woolamai:** a walking track around the granite headland at the island's eastern end, with a surf beach below.
- **Cowes:** the main town, with a jetty, a foreshore and most of the island's cafés and restaurants.
- **Churchill Island:** a heritage farm reached by a small bridge off the island's eastern side.

The Penguin Parade is a big draw at the best of times. Race weekend adds thousands more visitors to the island, so its sessions are busy.

## Staying for the weekend

Accommodation on the island splits between Cowes, smaller settlements like Rhyll and Ventnor, and San Remo just over the bridge. Holiday houses, motels and campgrounds make up most of what's on offer.

A race weekend fills the island, so many fans stay further back on the mainland, in towns along the Bass Coast or as far as the outer suburbs of Melbourne. They make the drive each morning. Those who stay on the island get the biggest prize of the weekend: no bridge queue on Sunday morning, and the chance to watch the first session with a coffee instead of brake lights.
