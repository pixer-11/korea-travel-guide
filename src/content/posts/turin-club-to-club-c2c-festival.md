## Four nights in a car factory

The 2026 edition of C2C was planned for an old Fiat plant. The Lingotto complex is a long concrete building on Via Nizza in south Turin. Fiat built it between 1916 and 1923 to make cars, with the assembly line running upward floor by floor. Finished cars were tested on a track on the roof.

Renzo Piano converted it into exhibition halls, a shopping arcade, a hotel and a gallery. Over four autumn nights, those halls were to hold Italy's leading festival of electronic and experimental music. The spaces are long, high-ceilinged industrial rooms rather than a field, and C2C is mostly an after-dark festival.

## The 2026 edition: dates and line-up

The 2026 festival was set for Thursday, October 29 to Sunday, November 1. The announced line-up included:

- **Robyn**, the Swedish pop artist whose dancefloor records have a big following among club audiences
- **The Avalanches**, the Australian sample collage group
- **BadBadNotGood**, the Toronto band that moves between jazz, hip-hop and soundtrack work

Plenty of other international acts were announced alongside them. C2C began in Turin in 2002, and it has always booked pop crossovers alongside harder experimental sets. A typical C2C night mixes an hour in front of a famous name with the next in a dark room with someone few people have heard of.

The festival is known for pairing big names with lesser-known experimental acts, and the 2026 bill followed that pattern.

## Halloween, Ognissanti and the long weekend

The festival weekend fell on two holidays. Saturday, October 31 was Halloween, a night when the city's bars and clubs usually run their own parties. Sunday, November 1 was Ognissanti (All Saints' Day), which is a national public holiday in Italy.

On Ognissanti, some shops and smaller businesses close and public transport runs a reduced holiday timetable. Pharmacies and supermarkets keep shorter hours or shut for the day.

Many Italians make a ponte (long weekend) of it, so trains into Turin and central hotels tend to fill up. Turin's contemporary art week, built around the Artissima fair, usually falls in early November too. Galleries are busy then and rooms are scarce.

## Getting to Lingotto

Metro Line 1 is Turin's only metro line, so it's easy to use. It runs from Fermi in the west, through Porta Susa and Porta Nuova, down Via Nizza. The complex is a short walk from **Lingotto** station.

- **From Porta Nuova (main station):** Line 1 south takes about ten minutes.
- **By train:** Torino Lingotto railway station is just behind the complex, which suits regional arrivals.
- **From the airport:** Turin-Caselle is north of the city. The usual route is into the centre first, then Line 1 south.

Late-night metro service is limited, and GTT runs fewer trains as the night goes on. After the last train, the 2km walk north up Via Nizza to Porta Nuova is flat and straight.

## The roof track and the streets around it

Lingotto is worth seeing in daylight. The rooftop test track is now La Pista 500, a planted walkway along the old banked oval. The Pinacoteca Agnelli sits on top in a glass and steel box called the "Scrigno" (treasure chest). Its permanent collection includes works by Canaletto, Matisse and Picasso.

Across the road from the metro is **Eataly Lingotto**, the first branch of the food chain. It opened in 2007 in the former Carpano vermouth factory and serves proper sit-down meals as well as counters and shops. The neighbourhood south of Porta Nuova is mostly residential, so food choices thin out as the night goes on.

## Aperitivo first, Lingotto later

Turin runs on aperitivo. Vermouth was invented here in the 1780s, and from early evening the bars around Piazza San Carlo, Via Po and San Salvario put out snacks with your drink. Locals eat dinner late, around 8:30 or 9pm, and festival crowds usually keep the same hours.

A few local habits:

- **Ordering like a Torinese.** Locals ask for "un vermouth" or a Negroni at the bar and take what comes with it. At most bars the snacks come with your drink.
- **Late sets.** At C2C the best-known names often play late in the night.
- **Paying by card.** Cards and phones work almost everywhere in central Turin. A little cash helps at the odd small bar and late-night snack counter.
- **Tipping.** Tipping isn't expected. Rounding up for good table service is enough.
- **Coats.** The Lingotto halls are large, and late-October nights in Piedmont are cold. A layer that can be checked in or tied round the waist is the norm.

On the days after, a bicerin (layered coffee, chocolate and cream) at a café in the historic centre is how Turin gets its mornings back.

## Where to stay for the festival

The choice is between being close to the venue and being close to everything else. Staying near Lingotto puts the halls a short walk away, but restaurants nearby are few.

San Salvario is closer to the centre, beside Porta Nuova and the Parco del Valentino. It's Turin's nightlife district, packed with bars, and Line 1 runs straight down to the venue. The Quadrilatero Romano and the streets around Via Garibaldi suit people who want daytime sightseeing between festival nights. On the Ognissanti holiday weekend, rooms in all these areas are in high demand.
