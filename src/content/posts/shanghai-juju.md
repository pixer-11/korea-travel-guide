## What JUJU actually is

JUJU is a Korean barbecue concept that soft-opened on September 21, 2026, inside the former Cannery space on the Bund. That address alone signals intent: Cannery was a known name in Shanghai's riverfront dining scene, and JUJU has stepped into its shoes rather than starting from a blank site elsewhere.

The format is "grill-and-ganbei," meaning the tabletop grill and the drinking games run on the same clock. Servers keep pace with the toasts as much as with the meat, so tables tend to get loud in bursts rather than staying at a steady simmer.

The headline product is UYYU wagyu, cut with heavy marbling and meant to be grilled fast at the table. Expect other grilled cuts alongside it, though the exact roster of the menu hasn't settled this early into the run.

## Why the Bund location matters

Being on the Bund puts JUJU among Shanghai's most photographed riverfront blocks, facing the Huangpu River with the Pudong skyline across the water. It's a location built for a night out that starts with dinner and ends with a walk along the promenade.

Taking over a space with a prior identity like Cannery's means the room likely still carries some of that layout and energy, useful context if you've eaten there before under the old name. For newcomers, it just means you're stepping into a space that already had a reputation for being a scene, not a quiet dining room.

- Have a backup plan for the same night in case tables are full

## Ordering and etiquette at the grill

Korean barbecue in a "grill-and-ganbei" format runs on shared responsibility at the table: someone tends the grill, and the rounds of drinks move at the same tempo as the cooking. If you're new to this style, let whoever's most experienced at your table take the first pass at the grill, then rotate in.

Ganbei, the Mandarin toast meaning "dry the glass," sets the tempo here more than a typical BBQ joint would. Pace yourself early if you're staying for multiple rounds of meat.

Given the location and the newness of the concept, expect payment to run through standard card and mobile pay channels common across Shanghai's Bund dining strip, though you should confirm this on-site rather than assume. Ask your server about the UYYU wagyu specifically if you want the cut the restaurant is currently best known for.
