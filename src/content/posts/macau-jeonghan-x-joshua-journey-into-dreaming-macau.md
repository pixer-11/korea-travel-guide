## Two nights on the Cotai Strip

SEVENTEEN's Jeonghan and Joshua play two Macau dates as a duo. JEONGHAN X JOSHUA JOURNEY INTO [DREAMING] takes place on December 11 and 12, 2026, at Galaxy Arena. The arena is inside the Galaxy Macau resort on Cotai, the reclaimed land between Taipa and Coloane where the large casino resorts stand.

This is a sub-unit concert, not a full SEVENTEEN show. You get two members on one stage, with a set built around their pairing. Fans write the tour title with its brackets, [DREAMING], and that is the spelling to use when you search for fan posts and seat views.

## Crossing into Macau

Most international fans travel in through Hong Kong. These are the usual routes:

- **Ferry** from Hong Kong to Taipa Ferry Terminal, the terminal closest to Cotai.
- **Shuttle bus over the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macau Bridge**, which stops at the Macau border port.
- **Flights** into Macau International Airport on Taipa, a short drive from the resorts.

Galaxy Macau runs free shuttle buses from the ferry terminals and the border crossings, and most visitors reach Cotai that way. Metered taxis queue at every terminal. December in Macau is usually cool and dry, so a light jacket covers the evening walk between the resorts.

## Tickets, seats and where to stay

The promoter and its ticketing partner handle sales, seat maps and entry rules. Prices are listed there and nowhere else. Earlier SEVENTEEN tours gave CARAT membership holders a presale window, so many fans hold a membership before a tour is announced.

Galaxy Arena is part of the resort complex. That means hotel rooms, restaurants and the venue are all under connected roofs. Fans usually stay in the Cotai hotels or in cheaper rooms in Taipa. On concert weekends, rooms near the arena are the first to sell out.

For a meal away from the resort crowds, Taipa Village is a short taxi ride away. Rua do Cunha there is lined with pork chop bun counters and almond cookie shops.

## How CARATs handle show night

Macau's K-pop crowds follow the same routine as Seoul's. Here is what to expect:

- **Lightsticks:** fans bring SEVENTEEN's official lightstick, nicknamed the Carat Bong, and at most shows they are synced centrally once the lights go down.
- **Merch queues:** lines at the official merch booth usually form hours before doors, and many fans shop on day one to avoid a second queue.
- **Freebies:** fans often hand out homemade photocards, stickers and keychains outside the venue. Bringing a few of your own to trade is normal.
- **ID:** K-pop shows often match the name on the ticket against photo ID at entry, so fans carry their passport.
- **Paying:** the Macanese pataca is the local currency, and Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted, though change often comes back in patacas. The resort outlets take cards.
- **Casino floors:** the gaming areas are restricted to people aged 21 and over. Younger fans walk around them, not through them, on the way to the arena.

At the end of the night, most of the crowd heads straight for the shuttle bays or the taxi rank. Fans staying in Cotai usually walk back through the indoor connections instead.
