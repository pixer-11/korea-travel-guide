## The venue: Ippodromo SNAI San Siro

You're not heading to the San Siro football stadium. That's a common mix-up. The Ippodromo SNAI San Siro is Milan's historic horse-racing track, a few minutes' walk from the stadium in the same western district of the city.

The infield opens up into a genuinely large, flat outdoor space, so sightlines depend heavily on where in it you stand. September nights in Milan can turn cool once the sun drops behind the grandstand.

- **Area**: San Siro, west Milan
- **Site type**: open-air horse-racing track, festival stage on the infield
- **Nearest landmark**: San Siro (Meazza) football stadium, walking distance away

## Getting there

Public transport is by far the easiest option here, since festival-night parking around San Siro is limited and traffic clogs up fast.

- **Metro**: Line 5 (lilac) to San Siro Ippodromo station, which sits right at the gates.
- **Alternative**: Line 1 (red) to Lotto, then a short walk or shuttle toward the racecourse.
- **Tram/bus**: extra surface routes typically run into San Siro on event nights.

Rideshares and taxis can drop passengers nearby, but the final stretch involves a walk, as roads close to vehicle traffic on event nights.

## How to visit like a local

Milanese festival-goers tend to treat I-DAYS as a weeknight-friendly outing rather than a full-day commitment. Most turn up within an hour or two of doors instead of camping out all afternoon, since the infield is large enough to give a workable view without an early stakeout.

Payment on-site leans cashless, with card and contactless standard at bars and food stalls. I-DAYS points buyers to its official channels and verified resale partners; unofficial resale around San Siro on show nights is common and risky.

A frequent pitfall for visitors is underestimating the walk from the metro and the post-show crush back onto Line 5.

The standard local kit for a genuinely outdoor night is flat, comfortable shoes for grass and gravel and a jacket for after dark, even in early September. Locals also treat the aperitivo bars around Lotto and San Siro as a natural pre-show stop, turning up fed and hydrated rather than relying only on festival food.
