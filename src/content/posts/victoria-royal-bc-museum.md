## A corner of the Inner Harbour

A carillon tower, a stand of carved cedar poles and a long modernist block share one corner of Belleville Street. That corner is the Royal BC Museum. Across the lawn stand the copper domes of the BC Parliament Buildings. Look the other way and you see the floatplane docks and the ivy-covered front of the Fairmont Empress.

It is one of the most visited attractions in British Columbia and very well reviewed. Expect company. Give it two to three hours for the galleries, plus another hour if you add an IMAX film.

## What fills the galleries

The museum's natural history collections are its core. The life-size woolly mammoth has long been the figure families head for first. Around it, walk-through dioramas recreate BC's landscapes, from coastal rainforest to shoreline.

Touring exhibitions rotate through, and dinosaurs have featured among them. Some long-running galleries have closed in recent years for redevelopment. Check the official site before you go so you know which floors are open and what's showing.

The IMAX Victoria theatre is inside the building. It runs nature and science documentaries on a giant screen. Films and exhibitions are usually ticketed separately, and the current options are listed on the museum's website.

## Thunderbird Park and the grounds

Some of the best things here sit outside the doors. Thunderbird Park, on the corner of Belleville and Douglas, holds a group of totem poles carved by Indigenous artists. Beside them stands Wawaditła, the big house built by Kwakwaka'wakw chief and carver Mungo Martin in 1953.

A few steps away is Helmcken House. Built in 1852, it is one of the oldest houses in British Columbia still standing on its original site. The Netherlands Centennial Carillon tower rises over the lawn and was a gift from Victoria's Dutch community. You can walk the grounds freely, so they fill a useful twenty minutes before your entry slot.

## Getting through the doors ahead of the crowds

Timing matters most here. It is calmest from 10am to 11am, on weekdays and on weekends alike. On weekends the crush builds from 12pm to 4pm, so a Saturday afternoon visit means sharing the mammoth with school groups and cruise passengers.

- **Arrive at opening.** The museum currently opens at 10am. Hours shift through the year and on holidays, so confirm on the official site.
- **Buy tickets online.** It saves a stop at the desk, and timed IMAX screenings can sell out on rainy days.
- **Do the busiest gallery first.** Head straight for the natural history floor and leave the quieter exhibits for later.
- **Pay by card.** It is accepted throughout, and tipping isn't part of a museum visit.
- **Last entry.** Closing is currently 5pm. Arriving after 3:30pm leaves too little time for both the galleries and a film.

## Arriving by foot, ferry or floatplane

There's no metro in Victoria, and you won't need one. From Government Street in downtown, the walk south past the Empress takes about ten minutes.

If you come from the US, the Victoria Clipper from Seattle and the Coho ferry from Port Angeles both dock on Belleville Street, steps from the museum. Harbour Air floatplanes from Vancouver land in the Inner Harbour, a short walk north along the waterfront. BC Ferries arrive at Swartz Bay, north of the city. From there, BC Transit buses run into downtown.

The surrounding streets have little parking. Downtown parkades are a better bet than circling Belleville.

## Making a half-day of the harbour

Leave the museum and the rest of the Inner Harbour is right there.

- **BC Parliament Buildings:** next door, with free guided tours when the legislature schedule allows. At night the building is outlined in lights.
- **Inner Harbour causeway:** the stone walkway below the Empress, where buskers and harbour ferries gather.
- **Beacon Hill Park:** about ten minutes' walk south along Douglas Street.
- **Fisherman's Wharf:** a waterfront walk west, with floating homes and seafood shacks.

A good order on a busy weekend: the museum at 10am, Thunderbird Park at midday while the galleries fill, then the waterfront in the afternoon.
