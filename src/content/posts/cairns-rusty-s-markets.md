## Fruit you won't find down south

Rusty's is where far north Queensland's growing season goes on sale. The market is an enclosed hall at 89 Grafton Street, a short walk inland from the Esplanade, and it operates on weekends. Produce makes up most of it. Flowers, clothing and gift stalls fill in the rest.

Come for the tropical fruit that rarely reaches southern supermarkets. Depending on the season, look for:

- Rambutan, mangosteen and lychee
- Dragon fruit, soursop and custard apple
- Black sapote, sometimes called chocolate pudding fruit
- Mangoes, which peak around the early wet season
- Local bananas, papaya and pineapple

Ask a stallholder what's ripe today. Many will cut a sample if you're unsure about something unfamiliar.

## Three days a week only

Opening hours are the biggest thing to plan around. The market currently trades:

- **Friday:** 5am–4pm
- **Saturday:** 5am–4pm
- **Sunday:** 5am–3pm, an hour shorter
- **Monday to Thursday:** closed

If you're in Cairns midweek, you'll miss it entirely. Book reef trips and the Kuranda run for those days instead. Allow 45 minutes to an hour and a half, longer if you plan to eat there.

## Getting to Grafton Street

Rusty's is in the Cairns City grid, roughly a 10-minute walk from the Esplanade Lagoon. Walk inland from the waterfront along Shields or Spence Street until you reach Grafton Street. Cairns Central shopping centre and the train station are a few blocks south, so it's easy to pair the market with a supermarket run.

Driving in is possible, but the streets around the market fill on Saturday mornings. Staying anywhere near the Esplanade? Walk.

## Working the stalls before the heat

The early start works in your favour. Arriving soon after opening usually means the widest choice of fruit and cooler air in the hall, which matters in the humid wet season from roughly December to March. By midday the best tropical fruit has often sold out.

Some practical habits:

- **Pay:** many stalls take cards, but carry some cash for smaller sellers and quicker transactions.
- **Tipping:** not expected, which is normal across Australia.
- **Bags:** bring your own. A sturdy one helps with pineapples and papaya.
- **Late deals:** produce sellers often clear stock cheaply near closing, especially on Sunday afternoons.
- **Quarantine:** fruit carried south out of the region can hit interstate biosecurity rules, so eat it in Cairns.

Rusty's is consistently well rated and well known around town, so on weekends you'll be sharing the aisles with plenty of other shoppers. Grab a fresh juice or a tropical fruit cup to drink while you walk. Then carry a bag of rambutans back to the Esplanade and eat them on the lawn by the lagoon.
