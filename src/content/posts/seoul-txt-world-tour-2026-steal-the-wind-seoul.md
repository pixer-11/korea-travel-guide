## Three nights under the Olympic roof

TOMORROW X TOGETHER brings STEAL THE WIND home in mid-November. The world tour plays KSPO Dome in Seoul on November 13, 14 and 15, 2026. After that it heads to Macau later the same month.

That makes the Seoul run the homecoming leg. For fans flying in, that matters. A Seoul show is where the group plays to the biggest crowd of its home fandom, MOA, and the venue is one TXT has filled before.

Three consecutive dates also take some pressure off. Many fans book one night for the floor and another from the stands. Others make a full long weekend of it.

## KSPO Dome, a gymnastics arena turned concert hall

The building has an Olympic past. KSPO Dome opened as the Olympic Gymnastics Arena for the 1988 Seoul Summer Olympics. Today it is one of the city's main indoor stages for K-pop, holding roughly 15,000 people in concert layout.

From outside you see a low, round dome set into the green of Olympic Park. Inside, the seating bowl rises steeply on all sides. That means even upper-tier seats look down on the stage instead of across a flat hall.

Seat sections are labelled by floor and block, and the numbering is printed on the ticket. Floor sections sit on the arena level. The tiered stands wrap around the dome, and seats behind the stage are sometimes sold depending on the stage design.

## Getting to Olympic Park

Three subway lines bring you to the park. Pick whichever matches where you are staying.

- **Olympic Park Station (Line 5):** the closest for KSPO Dome. Exit 3 lets you out near the arena side of the park.
- **Mongchontoseong Station (Line 8):** at the park's western edge, near the World Peace Gate. From here it is a longer walk through the park.
- **Hanseong Baekje Station (Line 9):** also on the west side, and handy if you are coming from Gangnam or Gimpo Airport.

Getting out after the show is the harder part. Thousands of people head for Line 5 at once. A lot of attendees walk west to the Line 8 or Line 9 stations instead, or wait inside the park until the first crowd has gone. Taxis around the gates are hard to find straight after a show.

Most visitors stay in Jamsil or Songpa. Lotte World Tower and the Jamsil hotels are a few subway stops away. Gangnam is also an easy ride on Line 9.

## The light-stick sea and the park before doors

This is what concert day looks like in Olympic Park. Hours before the show, the paths around KSPO Dome are already full of fans. The lawns and plazas fill with MOA who come for the day, not only the show.

You will usually see:

- **Official merchandise lines** at the booths near the dome. On the first day of a run they often form long before showtime.
- **Fan-made freebies:** photocards, stickers and slogan banners that fans hand out to each other by the walkways.
- **Light sticks being paired.** The official TXT light stick connects to the show's system once it is registered to your seat. During the concert the whole bowl changes colour together.
- **Photo spots** with tour posters and banners, where people take turns.

When the house lights go down, the light sticks follow the show. Colours shift together across all fifteen thousand seats, which is the moment most fans travel for.

## Getting through the gates at KSPO Dome

Korean arena concerts follow a tight routine. Knowing it makes the day go smoothly.

**Tickets and ID.** HYBE-label concerts in Korea often check photo ID against the name on the ticket. International fans usually bring their passport, since a hotel key card or phone photo will not count. Tickets bought through resale outside official channels risk being refused at the gate.

**Entry and bags.** Entry is organised by section or ticket number, and staff call groups through in order. Professional cameras and recording devices are generally banned. Big bags slow down the security check, so most people bring a small crossbody bag.

**Paying for things.** A T-money card covers the subway and works in convenience stores. Merch booths in Seoul generally take cards, and many fans also pay by phone. Korean fans rarely queue with only cash.

**Etiquette inside.** Fans stay in their own seats or blocks and keep banners at chest height so the people behind can still see. Fanchants are a big part of a TXT show. Fans often learn them from official fanchant guides so they can shout the members' names on cue.

## Around the dome: Olympic Park and Songpa

The park itself is worth a few hours. Olympic Park covers a large area of lawns, sculptures and walking paths. Two sights stand out:

- **Mongchontoseong**, the earthen walls of a Baekje-era fortress, now grassy ridges you can walk along.
- **The World Peace Gate**, the winged concrete gateway at the western entrance, built for the 1988 Games.

The **SOMA Museum of Art** and the **Hanseong Baekje Museum** are both inside or next to the park, which helps if you are waiting around before a show.

Jamsil is close by for meals and a non-concert day. **Seokchon Lake** has a loop path beside Lotte World Tower. The tower's lower floors hold the Lotte World Mall, and Seoul Sky is the observation deck at the top. The mall's food courts are where a lot of concertgoers end up eating after the show.

## Seoul in mid-November

The run falls in late autumn. Seoul in mid-November is usually cold and dry, especially after dark, and the last of the ginkgo and maple leaves often still line the park. Queueing outside for merch or entry means hours standing in the open. Most attendees dress in layers they can take off once they are inside the warm dome.

Three nights in a row also bring hotel demand to the Jamsil area. Fans who come from abroad often stay a few stops away, around Gangnam or Seongsu, and ride in on Line 2 or Line 9.

The tour's next stop after Seoul is Macau later in November. A few fans follow it there.
