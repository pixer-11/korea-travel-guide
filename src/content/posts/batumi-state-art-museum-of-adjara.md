## A quieter room behind the seafront

Batumi markets itself on glass towers, a long seaside boulevard and a dancing fountain. The State Art Museum of Adjara offers something else. It is a modest museum on Zurab Gorgiladze Street with galleries of paintings and sculpture spanning a range of eras, and it gets far fewer visitors than the city's waterfront sights.

That works in your favour. You can usually stand in front of a canvas without anyone else in the frame, read a label at your own pace and walk out an hour later without having queued for anything. On the street, look for the Georgian name over the entrance: **აჭარის ხელოვნების მუზეუმი**.

## What hangs on the walls

The museum splits into two parts. The permanent collection covers art from different periods. The temporary exhibitions change through the year, so what you see depends partly on when you come.

- **Permanent galleries:** paintings and sculpture from several eras, giving a broad overview more than a deep dive into one movement.
- **Rotating exhibitions:** the reason to come back, and worth checking before you go.
- **Scale:** modest by design. Expect a manageable set of rooms you can see properly, not an endless wing-after-wing marathon.

Don't expect a blockbuster. Reviewers consistently rate it well, and they tend to praise it as a focused, well-kept stop rather than a grand national gallery.

## Finding Gorgiladze Street

The museum sits at 8 Zurab Gorgiladze St, in the walkable central part of Batumi. Batumi has no metro, so you either walk or take a taxi.

- **On foot:** from the Old Town streets around Europe Square and the Piazza, it is an easy walk through the centre, and you can carry on toward Batumi Boulevard afterwards.
- **By taxi:** ride-hailing apps such as Bolt work well in Batumi; type in the street address, not just the museum name.
- **From the train or bus:** Batumi Central railway station is outside the compact centre, so a short taxi ride is the simplest option.

## Inside the galleries: how it works

There are no timed slots or booking systems here. You pay at the front desk and walk in.

- **Tickets:** bought at the desk on arrival; the current price is posted there, so check it at the counter.
- **Payment:** cards are widely accepted in central Batumi, but carry a few lari as a backup for small venues.
- **Photography:** rules can differ between the permanent galleries and temporary shows, so ask at the desk before raising your phone.
- **Language:** labels may be mostly in Georgian, with English in places. A translation app's camera mode helps with the longer texts.
- **Greeting:** a simple *gamarjoba* (hello) and *madloba* (thank you) to the attendants goes a long way.

Gallery attendants in Georgian museums often sit in each room. Keep your voice down and keep backpacks off your shoulders near the sculpture.

## When to go and how long to stay

Google currently lists the museum as open 10am–6pm every day, Monday included. Museum hours shift through the year and on public holidays, so confirm this month's schedule on the official site before you set out.

An hour covers most visitors; allow up to 90 minutes if a temporary exhibition catches you. Arriving at least an hour before closing means you won't be hurried through the last rooms.

Batumi is one of the wettest cities in Georgia, and showery days are common, especially outside high summer. That makes the museum a natural plan B when the beach is out of the question. On a hot July or August afternoon it doubles as an escape from the sun.

## Pairing it with the rest of Batumi

The museum fits easily into a half-day loop of the centre. Spend the morning here and then walk to the sea.

- **Europe Square and the Piazza:** the Old Town's squares, with cafés for a post-museum coffee.
- **Batumi Boulevard:** the long seafront promenade, best for an evening stroll.
- **Alphabetic Tower and the Ali and Nino statue:** at the far end of the boulevard, and liveliest after dark.
- **Batumi Botanical Garden:** a separate trip north of the city, so save it for another day.

Put together, that gives you a day with some indoor variety: an hour of painting and sculpture in a quiet museum, then Batumi's busier seafront.
