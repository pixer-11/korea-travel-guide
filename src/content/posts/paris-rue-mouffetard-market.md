## What you're walking into

Rue Mouffetard slopes downhill from Place de la Contrescarpe toward Saint-Médard church, cobblestones underfoot, shopfronts stacked close on both sides. It's not a single market square but a street of independent vendors: fishmongers with ice-packed crates out front, butchers with whole birds hanging in the window, cheese shops with wheels stacked to the ceiling. The address given, 139 Rue Mouffetard, sits toward the southern, quieter end near Saint-Médard.

Trading happens Tuesday to Sunday, 8am to 1pm, and the street is dead on Mondays. There's no evening market here; by early afternoon, shutters start coming down.

## Why it's rated the way it is

At 4.5 stars across more than 2,000 reviews, this is one of the better-reviewed market streets in the city, and it draws a mixed crowd of residents and visitors rather than one or the other. Expect produce stalls beside boulangeries, a couple of well-known cheese counters, and narrow footways that fill up fast once the morning gets going. It's a well-known stop on the Left Bank circuit, not an undiscovered one, so don't expect empty lanes.

## Getting there and timing it right

The nearest metro is Place Monge (Line 7), a short walk uphill to Place de la Contrescarpe, where the market street begins. Censier-Daubenton (also Line 7) sits closer to the southern end near Saint-Médard.

- Best window: right at 8am opening, before tour groups and lunch-hour shoppers arrive
- Avoid: late morning on Saturday, when the street narrows to a shuffle
- Sundays: same hours as weekdays, market wraps by 1pm
- Mondays: closed entirely, plan around it

Give it 45 minutes to an hour if you're browsing; longer if you're stopping to eat.

## Shopping the stalls like a regular

Vendors expect cash for small purchases, though card machines are increasingly common at bigger shopfronts; carry some coins for quick buys like fruit or a crepe. Queue discipline is informal but real: stand behind whoever's being served, and don't reach past the counter.

Point at what you want rather than handling produce yourself, especially at fruit and vegetable stalls where the vendor bags it for you. Portions are often sold by weight, so a quick "un peu plus" (a little more) or "ça suffit" (that's enough) as they weigh it works fine even with minimal French.

The street continues past the market hours as a dining strip: bakeries, cheese shops, and small bistros stay open longer, so a market-morning visit pairs naturally with an early lunch nearby. Saint-Médard church, at the southern end, is worth the extra five minutes even outside market hours, its interior far quieter than the street outside.
