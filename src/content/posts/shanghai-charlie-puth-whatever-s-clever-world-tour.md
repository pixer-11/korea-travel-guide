## Buying and Getting In with Your Passport

Ticketing in mainland China works differently from Europe or North America. For large concerts, real-name ticketing is the norm. Each ticket is tied to one buyer's identity document, and foreign visitors normally use their passport.

At the gate, staff usually check that the name on the ticket matches the ID presented. A ticket bought by a friend under their name may not get someone in. Resale tickets with another person's details can fail at exactly that point.

How it tends to work for visitors:

- **Tickets go under the visitor's own passport number,** spelled exactly as it is on the passport.
- **The physical passport is needed** on show night. A photo of it is not the same thing.
- **Security takes time.** Big Chinese venues screen bags. Power banks, umbrellas, lighters and professional cameras are common restrictions, and each venue sets its own rules.
- **Mobile wallets do the paying.** Alipay and WeChat Pay are how Shanghai pays for nearly everything, from metro rides to bottled water. Both accept a linked foreign bank card, and setup is easiest before the flight.

Chinese crowds at pop concerts often sing every chorus and wave light sticks. The heavy filming seen at Western shows happens here too, though staff may step in when a phone stays up through an entire song.

## Moving Across Shanghai on Show Night

Shanghai is huge, and the metro is the sensible way to cross it. The network is one of the largest in the world, signs and announcements are in both Chinese and English, and the trains are clean and frequent.

Riders can tap through the gates with Alipay or WeChat Pay using the Metro QR code feature. That means no queue at a ticket machine as a crowd leaves.

For any venue, two details matter most:

- **The nearest station and line.** The exit number matters too, because big stations have many.
- **The last train.** Metro service ends late in the evening and lines finish at different times. DiDi, China's main ride-hailing app, takes foreign cards and has an English interface, which makes it the usual option after a long encore.
