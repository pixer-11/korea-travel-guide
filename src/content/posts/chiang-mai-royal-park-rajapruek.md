## What's actually here

Royal Park Rajapruek was built for Thailand's 2006 royal horticultural expo and never closed after. The grounds cover roughly 80 hectares of themed gardens: a Chinese garden, a Japanese garden, a rose garden, cactus and bonsai sections, all arranged along paved paths and connected by a small tram.

The centerpiece is the Ho Kham Royal Pavilion, a golden, multi-tiered teak structure built in traditional Lanna style, honoring King Bhumibol. A separate commemorative chapel and sculpture displays round out the grounds, all set against the backdrop of Doi Suthep to the north.

Expect two to three hours if you walk it, less if you take the tram between garden zones.

## Getting to Mae Hia

The park sits south of Chiang Mai city center, near Chiang Mai University's Mae Hia campus. A taxi or Grab from the Old City runs about 20-30 minutes depending on traffic. Songthaews (red trucks) also serve the area, though a private ride is more reliable for the return trip.

There's no train or songthaew route dedicated to the park, so most visitors arrange transport both ways rather than count on flagging one down at the gate.

## When to go

The park is open every day, 8am to 6pm, with no midweek variation. Weekends stay busy the entire day, so if you want quieter paths, come on a weekday morning close to opening.

Cooler months (November through February) suit walking the outdoor gardens best; Chiang Mai's hot season (March-May) makes the shadeless stretches between garden zones uncomfortable by midday. Rainy season (June-October) keeps the gardens green but afternoon downpours are common, so mornings are safer bets.

## Getting around without wasting time

Buy your ticket at the entrance gate; check current admission and any electric-tram fares before you go, since these change periodically.

- Grab a park map at the gate; the layout is large enough to disorient first-time visitors.
- Ride the tram for the outer loop, then walk the closer-together themed gardens on foot.
- Photographers should head for the Ho Kham Pavilion first: its reflecting pool draws crowds later in the day.
- Wear closed shoes; gravel and uneven garden paths are common throughout.
- Bring water. Shade is inconsistent between garden sections, especially at midday.

Given the very-popular status and the weekend crush lasting all day, this isn't a low-key back road anymore. It draws tour groups and local families in equal number, so treat a weekend visit as a full-crowd event and plan accordingly, or shift to a weekday if your schedule allows it.
