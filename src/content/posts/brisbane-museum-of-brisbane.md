## Three floors above King George Square

You reach this museum through a sandstone civic building. Brisbane City Hall was finished in 1930. Its columned front and clock tower face King George Square, and the museum occupies the third floor, above council offices and the domed auditorium.

That setting shapes the whole visit. You come for exhibitions about the city, and you leave having climbed inside one of its best-known landmarks. Allow about 90 minutes for the galleries and a tower tour. Add more time if you join a full City Hall tour.

Hours are simple: 10am to 5pm, seven days a week, with no shorter day in the schedule.

## What fills the galleries

This is a museum about one city. The galleries rotate, so exhibitions change through the year, but the subject stays the same: Brisbane's art, design, people and history, often told through local artists and community stories.

Expect a mix of:

- **Historical exhibits** on how the river city grew
- **Contemporary artwork**, much of it by Brisbane-based artists
- **Design and social history** shows that treat ordinary objects as city history

The rooms are compact, and in some places the old building shows through. Because the program changes, check the museum's website before you go, especially if you are coming back for a second look.

## Riding the cage lift into the clock tower

The clock tower tour is the reason many visitors climb to Level 3. The museum runs it, and it starts with a trip in a historic cage lift. It is one of the oldest working lifts in the city, and you can watch the shaft pass through the mesh as you rise.

At the top, the observation level sits just behind the huge clock faces. You see the mechanism up close, then look out over the CBD, the Brisbane River and the streets around the square. For decades after 1930, this tower was the highest point in the city skyline.

Groups are small and the lift is tight. The bells up there are loud when they strike. If you have sensitive hearing, or small children with you, ask staff how the tour fits around the chimes.

## The rest of City Hall

The building is worth exploring beyond the museum floor. The Main Auditorium sits under a copper dome and is one of the most recognisable interiors in Brisbane. It houses a grand pipe organ, originally built by the English maker Henry "Father" Willis.

City Hall went through a major restoration and reopened in 2013. That is also when the museum moved into its current home. Look for original marble, timber and terrazzo in the stairwells and foyers on your way up and down.

The museum also runs guided tours of City Hall that explain its architecture and civic role. They are a good choice if the tower is booked out.

## Booking the tower before the day fills

Tower tour places are limited, so plan them before you do anything else. Here is how to get in without wasting time:

- **Book the clock tower first.** Reserve online or at the museum front desk on Level 3 as soon as you arrive. Then look around the galleries while you wait for your slot.
- **Check prices at the source.** Entry, tour tickets and any charges for special exhibitions are listed on the museum's official website and at the front desk.
- **Pay by card.** Tapping a card or phone is standard in Brisbane, and tipping staff or tour guides is not expected.
- **Keep bags small.** The cage lift and tower stairs leave little room, so large backpacks are awkward up there.
- **Ask the volunteers.** The guides and front-desk staff know the building well. They will usually tell you which rooms are open that day.

## When to go: the weekend morning window

On weekends, the calmest hour is **10am to 11am**, right after opening. Arriving then gives you the widest choice of tower times, and the galleries are at their emptiest.

As the day goes on, the square fills with people from the city centre, and the tower slots go. If you can only come on a Saturday or Sunday, aim for the 10am opening. Keep the afternoon for the shops and cafés nearby.

Remember the last entry comes before the 5pm close. Arriving at 4:30pm leaves too little time for a tower tour.

## Around King George Square

Getting here is easy from anywhere in the CBD. **King George Square busway station** sits directly under the square, and **Central Station** is a short walk north along Ann Street or Adelaide Street.

Once you are back at street level, you have plenty to do within a few minutes' walk:

- **Queen Street Mall**, one block south, for shopping and food courts
- **Brisbane Arcade**, a heritage shopping arcade running off the mall
- **Anzac Square**, a war memorial and park near Central Station
- **Roma Street Parkland**, a longer walk west, for gardens after the galleries

A good half-day plan: museum and tower in the morning, then lunch around the mall, then down to the river in the afternoon.
