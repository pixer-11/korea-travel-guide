Five December nights, one arena, and a Bologna songwriter whose choruses half of Italy can finish for him. Cesare Cremonini brings a multi-night run to the Unipol Forum in Assago between December 15 and 19, 2026. That makes it one of the larger Italian pop events of the Milan winter.

This guide covers who he is, how the arena works, and how the evening usually unfolds from the metro platform to the last encore.

## Who you are going to hear

Cremonini first broke through as the teenage frontman of Lùnapop. Their 1999 debut album produced "50 Special", the Vespa song that still gets played at Italian weddings. He went solo in the early 2000s and has spent two decades since filling arenas and stadiums.

His catalogue mixes piano ballads with big, bright pop. Songs that Italian audiences reliably know word for word include:

- "50 Special" (from the Lùnapop years)
- "Marmellata #25"
- "Poetica"
- "Logico #1"
- "Nessuno vuole essere Robin"
- "Buon viaggio (Share the Love)"

No setlist is given here, because none has been published for these nights. Expect a crowd that sings, though. Italian arena audiences treat a familiar chorus as a shared duty, not a private pleasure.

## The Unipol Forum in Assago

The Forum is an indoor arena in Assago, a municipality on Milan's south-western edge. It sits beside the Milanofiori business district, close to the Tangenziale Ovest ring road. For decades it has been Milan's main covered venue for both concerts and basketball, and it was known as the Mediolanum Forum before taking its current name.

Inside, the layout is a classic bowl. The floor in front of the stage is the **parterre**, usually standing for pop shows. Around it rise the seated tiers, called the **primo anello** and **secondo anello** (first and second ring). Upper-ring seats sit higher and farther back, but the sightlines down to the stage are clear.

A December show means a fully indoor evening. The weather outside matters only for the walk from the metro.

## Riding the M2 to Assago

The green line has a quirk that catches visitors out. M2 splits at Famagosta station: one branch runs to Abbiategrasso, the other to Assago. Only trains marked **Assago Milanofiori Forum** go to the arena.

From central Milan, the usual route looks like this:

- Board M2 at Cadorna, Garibaldi or Centrale, all on the green line.
- Watch the destination board and take a train signed for Assago.
- Get off at **Assago Milanofiori Forum**, the end of the line.
- Walk roughly 10 minutes to the arena, following the crowd and the signs.

The station before it, Assago Milanofiori Nord, serves the office district, not the Forum. Staying on to the terminus avoids a longer walk.

On concert nights the trains toward Assago fill with people in tour T-shirts well before showtime. The ride from Centrale takes around half an hour, including the change of branch.

## Getting out after the encore

The return trip is where planning pays off. When thousands leave at once, the queue for Assago Milanofiori Forum station backs up along the approach. Staff often meter entry to the platform.

What attendees usually do:

- **Stay put for a song or two.** Fans who linger in their seats during the house lights often find the station queue has already thinned.
- **Buy the return ticket in advance.** Milan's metro also takes contactless bank cards at the gates, which skips the ticket machines entirely.
- **Line up by the far doors.** The terminus has trains waiting, and the front carriages fill first.

Many fans from outside Milan drive in instead. The Forum has large car parks reached from the Tangenziale Ovest, and the exit from them after a sold-out pop show is slow. Car sharers usually expect a wait before reaching the ring road.

## Inside the bowl on a Cremonini night

Doors open well before the start, and the arena fills gradually. Parterre fans tend to arrive first so they can stand near the barrier. Ticket holders in the anelli usually drift in later, knowing their seat is waiting.

Italian concert crowds have their own habits. Chants of the artist's first name between songs are standard. Phone torches come up for the ballads. Plenty of people come as a family, with parents who bought the Lùnapop record and children who know the solo singles.

Cards are widely accepted at Italian arena bars and merch stands, though a small amount of cash helps if a terminal stalls. The merchandise counters sit on the concourse, and the queues there are shortest once the show has started.

## Where fans eat and stay

Assago itself is mostly offices and housing around the arena. Pre-show dinners are usually in Milan, around the Navigli canals or near Porta Genova, both a short hop from the M2 line. Milanofiori also has hotels and restaurants within walking distance of the Forum, which suits people driving in.

Visitors from abroad generally stay in central Milan. A base near Cadorna, Sant'Ambrogio or Centrale keeps the whole trip on one metro line.

## December in Milan around the shows

A mid-December run lands in Milan's Christmas season. The city is at its most festive by day, and that makes the concert easy to pair with sightseeing.

- **Piazza del Duomo** has its large Christmas tree, a short walk from the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II.
- **The Navigli** canals have riverside bars and are a common pre-show meeting point.
- **Sant'Ambrogio** sits on the green line, close to the basilica of the same name. Its traditional Christmas market, the Oh Bej! Oh Bej!, is usually held in early December, so it may be over by the time these concerts start.
