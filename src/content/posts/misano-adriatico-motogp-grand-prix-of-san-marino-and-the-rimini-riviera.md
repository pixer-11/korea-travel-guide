## Why this race weekend is different

Most MotoGP rounds feel like a big sporting event dropped into a random town. Misano feels like the opposite: a seaside strip that spends all year waiting for this. The Misano World Circuit Marco Simoncelli sits just inland from the beach resorts of Misano Adriatico, and for one weekend in September the whole Riviera — the bike shops, the gelaterias, the boardwalk bars — turns yellow and orange in tribute to Valentino Rossi and, quieter but ever-present, to Marco Simoncelli, the local rider the circuit is named after.

You feel that history the moment you see the grandstands: this isn't a neutral venue, it's home ground for Italian motorcycle racing.

## Getting there

Misano Adriatico sits on the Adriatic coast between Rimini and Riccione, in Emilia-Romagna. The nearest airport is Federico Fellini International Airport (Rimini), a short taxi or shuttle ride away; Bologna's airport is a workable backup if Rimini flights are booked out, with a train connection down the coast. By rail, Rimini and Riccione stations both sit on the main Adriatic line, and race weekend usually brings extra shuttle buses running from the stations straight to the circuit gates — worth checking for on the official race site rather than assuming a fixed timetable.

If you're driving, expect the roads around the circuit to clog badly on Saturday and Sunday mornings; locals and returning fans build in an extra hour without complaint.

## The race weekend itself

The Grand Prix runs across three days, September 11-13, 2026. Friday is free and timed practice, a good day to walk the paddock area and get close to the garages with a fraction of the crowd. Saturday brings final practice, qualifying, and the MotoGP sprint race — by mid-afternoon the grandstands are loud and full. Sunday is race day proper, with the Moto3, Moto2 and MotoGP races run back to back, building to the main event in the last slot of the afternoon.

Ticket categories, exact session times, and grandstand maps change year to year, so treat any schedule you see online as provisional until you've checked the official MotoGP and Misano circuit websites closer to the date.

## What to see and do beyond the circuit

The paddock and fan zones outside the main straight are worth arriving early for — team merchandise stalls, rider appearances, and a fan village atmosphere build through the morning before the track goes loud. Between sessions, it's a five-minute walk into Misano Adriatico's town center for a coffee or plate of piadina, the flatbread that's the signature street food of this whole coast.

If you have a spare half-day, Riccione's beach promenade and Rimini's old town (with its Roman arch and Malatesta Temple) are both a short bus or train ride away, and make a good decompression from two days of engine noise.

## When to go and how long to stay

Plan for the full three days if you're a serious fan — practice, qualifying and race day each have a different rhythm and the sprint format means Saturday now matters almost as much as Sunday. If you can only manage one day, Sunday delivers the most racing but also the heaviest crowds and traffic; Friday is the calm alternative if your priority is soaking in the paddock atmosphere rather than watching a full race.

September on this stretch of the Adriatic is still warm enough for the beach in the mornings before you head to the circuit.

## How to visit like a local

Regulars book their hotel in Misano, Riccione, or Cattolica months ahead — rooms within walking or short cycling distance of the circuit disappear early, and prices climb as the date nears. Most fans get to the track by bike or the shuttle buses rather than driving in, since parking near the circuit is limited and exit traffic after the main race can take well over an hour.

Cash still helps at some of the smaller food and merchandise stands inside the fan zones, though cards are widely accepted. Bring ear protection if you're near the start-finish straight — even seasoned fans wear it — and pack a cap and sunscreen, since Riviera grandstands offer little shade in September. The rookie mistake is treating this like a one-day trip to the coast: the circuit sits a little inland from the beachfront hotels, so factor in the walk or shuttle each way rather than assuming you can dash back to the sand between sessions.

Above all, confirm the final schedule, ticket tiers, and gate times on the official MotoGP and Misano World Circuit websites before you commit to travel — details firm up only a few months out.
