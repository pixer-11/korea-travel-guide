## A knock on the door, two days after Christmas

The date is a Saturday: December 26, 2026. BOYNEXTDOOR bring the second chapter of their KNOCK ON concert series to Kuala Lumpur. It comes at the very end of the year's K-pop calendar, so it is one of the last big K-pop nights of 2026 in Malaysia.

BOYNEXTDOOR are the six-member group from KOZ Entertainment, a label under HYBE. The members are Sungho, Riwoo, Jaehyun, Taesan, Leehan and Woonhak, and they debuted in 2023. Their fans call themselves ONEDOOR. The tour name follows the group's door idea, and Vol.2 follows the first KNOCK ON run.

## Unifi Arena and the Bukit Jalil sports city

You come into the arena through the National Sports Complex at Bukit Jalil. This is a wide stretch of stadiums, car parks and walkways built for the 1998 Commonwealth Games. The 87,000-capacity Bukit Jalil National Stadium is the giant bowl next door. The arena is the smaller domed building beside it, a seated indoor hall for concerts and badminton.

Many Kuala Lumpur concertgoers still know the venue by its earlier name, Axiata Arena. Older fan posts and travel threads use that name, but it is the same building. Inside, tiered seats wrap the floor on all sides. That means even the upper rows look down on the stage, not across a flat field.

## Riding the Sri Petaling Line south

Most fans arrive by train. The LRT Sri Petaling Line (the green-and-yellow line on most maps) stops at **Bukit Jalil station**, and from there you walk across the sports complex to the arena. From the central interchanges this is how it usually goes:

- **From KL Sentral:** take the LRT Kelana Jaya Line or the monorail to Hang Tuah or Masjid Jamek, then change to the Sri Petaling Line heading south.
- **From Bukit Bintang:** the monorail runs to Hang Tuah, where you change to the Sri Petaling Line.
- **By Grab:** e-hailing is the default backup in Kuala Lumpur. Drop-offs work fine, but after the show the roads around the sports complex jam up fast.

Rail fares are paid with a Touch 'n Go card or a contactless bank card at the gates, so nobody queues for paper tokens. Many visitors to Bukit Jalil stay near Pavilion Bukit Jalil or in the city centre. A city-centre hotel puts you a few stops from Bukit Bintang's food streets.

## Tickets, tiers and the seat map

The tiers and seat plan belong to the show's promoter and its ticketing partner. This guide gives no prices, because none were supplied. Any figure you see quoted elsewhere without a source is a guess.

Here is how the arena layout usually works for seated shows like this one:

- **Floor sections** sit nearest the stage, either standing or seated, depending on how the promoter sets up the room.
- **Lower tier** seats rise steeply from the floor and give the clearest straight view of the stage.
- **Upper tier** seats sit higher and further back but take in the whole stage and the lightstick ocean.

Resale is where most fans get burned at big K-pop shows in Malaysia. Fans usually stick to the official ticketing channel and treat screenshots of e-tickets sold in social media groups with suspicion.

## Lightsticks, freebies and the late-night LRT crush

Kuala Lumpur K-pop crowds have their own routines. Knowing them makes the day easier.

**Merch and freebies.** Official merchandise booths usually draw the longest lines of the day, well before doors. Around the plaza, fan clubs often hand out free photocards, stickers and slogan banners. Taking one with a thank-you is normal, and many fans bring small items of their own to swap.

**The lightstick.** BOYNEXTDOOR have an official lightstick. Fans usually pair it to the show's control system at their seat, so the arena changes colour in sync with the songs. Unofficial glow sticks still light up, but they won't follow the show's colours.

**Etiquette in the seats.** Malaysian venues usually set strict rules on professional cameras and recording, and staff do enforce them. Fan chants are learned in advance from fan-made guides. The room gets loudest on the call-and-response parts.

**Getting out.** When the show ends, the crowd heads for Bukit Jalil station all at once. Fans who don't want that crush usually eat nearby and take a later train. Others walk a little way out before calling a Grab, because surge pricing and gridlock are worst right at the gates.

## Boxing Day weekend in Kuala Lumpur

The date brings its own quirks. Christmas Day is a public holiday in Malaysia, so the concert falls at the start of a long year-end stretch. Malls run their festive decorations and sales, and many people travel around the city. Shopping centres like Pavilion Kuala Lumpur and Suria KLCC are at their most crowded in this season.

December also falls in the northeast monsoon. In Kuala Lumpur that usually means heavy afternoon or evening downpours that pass within an hour or two. Fans waiting outside the arena tend to carry a compact umbrella or a poncho. The walk from the LRT is mostly open ground.

## Around the arena before and after

You don't need to go back into the city to eat. **Pavilion Bukit Jalil**, the large mall that opened in 2024 beside the sports complex, has a food court and a long run of restaurants. Concertgoers commonly use it as a meeting point before the show.

A short ride north, **Sri Petaling** is a residential neighbourhood with many Chinese coffee shops and late-opening mamak stalls. Teh tarik and roti canai there make a classic Malaysian post-concert supper. If you have spare days around the show, Bukit Bintang, the Petronas Twin Towers and Merdeka Square are all on the same rail network that brought you to Bukit Jalil.
