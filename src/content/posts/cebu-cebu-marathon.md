## A race timed to Sinulog

Cebu City in January is already in festival mode. Streets are hung with Santo Niño banners, drums practise in barangay halls, and hotel lobbies fill with people in for the Sinulog. The Cebu Marathon runs into the middle of all this on **Sunday, January 10, 2027**, exactly one week before Sinulog's main parade day on the third Sunday of the month.

That timing shapes the trip. A runner who lands for the race can stay on for the festival, and many visitors plan their week that way. The marathon is the athletic start to the city's busiest fortnight of the year.

## Four distances, one certified course

The race offers four distances, so a whole group can enter together:

- **5K**: the entry point, popular with families and first-timers
- **10K**: for regular runners who want a fast morning out
- **21K**: the half marathon
- **42K**: the full marathon, the headline event

The course is **AIMS-certified**. That means the Association of International Marathons and Distance Races has measured it to international standard. A time you set here is a properly measured time, and it counts for qualifying where races accept AIMS-measured courses.

## Registration, route and race kit

The organiser publishes registration links, the route map, the start and finish area and the race-kit collection details on its official site. Those pages are the source for entry categories and fees too. This guide prints no prices, because any figure here would be a guess.

Entrants in big Philippine road races usually pick up their kits in the days before race day. That leaves a gap of a day or two in the city, and most out-of-town runners fill it with an easy shakeout jog and a slow walk around the old downtown.

## Running through a tropical January

January is in Cebu's drier, cooler stretch of the year. "Cooler" is relative, though: mornings are still warm and humid by northern standards. That is why road races across the Philippines usually start in darkness, so most of the field can finish before the sun gets strong.

Runners coming from winter climates often find the humidity harder than the distance. Experienced visitors arrive in Cebu a few days early, drink steadily at every water station, and run the first half more conservatively than they would at home. Light, quick-drying kit and a cap tend to matter more here than a fancy shoe.

## Race morning in Cebu City

Local habits decide how smoothly race weekend goes.

- **Getting to the start:** taxis and the Grab ride-hailing app are the usual ways to cross town before dawn. Many runners book a hotel within walking distance of the start area, because road closures slow down cars on race morning.
- **Paying:** small eateries and sari-sari stores mostly take cash. Mobile wallets like GCash are widely used. Hotels and malls take cards.
- **The crowd:** Cebuano is the street language. Spectators often shout *"Padayon!"* ("keep going"), and during Sinulog week you'll also hear *"Pit Señor!"*, the festival's devotional cheer.
- **Rooms:** Sinulog fills the city's hotels. Visitors who are staying on for the festival usually reserve their beds months ahead.

Water and post-race food tend to come in local style, so a hot bowl of rice-based breakfast is easy to find once you've crossed the line. Ask for *pochero* or *lechon*, Cebu's famous roast pig, which many locals count as a fair reward after 42 kilometres.

## After the finish line

Your legs will be sore, so the best post-race outing is a short, flat walk around the old city:

- **Basilica Minore del Santo Niño**, the spiritual centre of Sinulog and the busiest church in the city that week
- **Magellan's Cross**, in a small pavilion beside the basilica
- **Fort San Pedro**, the Spanish-era stone fort near the port
- **Colon Street**, often called the oldest street in the country

If you'd rather recover by the sea, head to Mactan Island, across the channel in Lapu-Lapu City. It has the resort beaches and **Mactan-Cebu International Airport**, where most international runners land. Some people split their stay: Cebu City for race weekend, then Mactan for a few days of swimming before Sinulog brings them back across the bridge.
