## Why Otres Beach

You come to Otres because Sihanoukville's main beaches got loud, and this one hasn't, not entirely.

The sand runs pale and fine for a couple of kilometers, with tamarisk and casuarina trees leaning in from the tree line, throwing patchy shade over hammocks and low wooden loungers. There's no wall of high-rises here — just a scatter of bamboo bars, guesthouses, and the occasional beach dog dozing in the shade. It's a working beach, with fishing boats pulled up at either end, not a manicured resort strip.

## Getting there

Otres sits about 6-7 km southeast of downtown Sihanoukville, split into two zones locals call Otres 1 and Otres 2.

A tuk-tuk from the city center or the airport takes roughly 15-20 minutes. Expect to negotiate the fare before you get in — there's no meter, and prices creep up after dark. Grab or PassApp (Cambodia's ride-hailing apps) usually give you a fairer, fixed price if you have data on your phone.

Otres 1 has the denser cluster of bars and guesthouses; Otres 2 is quieter, more spread out, and better if you just want a long walk on empty sand.

## What to do on the beach

Most of the day here is unstructured, and that's the point.

Rent a lounger at one of the beachfront bars — usually free if you order food or drinks — and just watch the fishing boats work the bay. Swimming is calm and shallow for a good stretch, decent for families. A few operators run kayak rentals and boat trips out toward Koh Ta Kiev and the other nearby islands, worth doing if you have a spare half-day.

Late afternoon is when the beach earns its reputation: the light turns the sand orange-pink, and by dusk the bars string up fairy lights and fire pits.

## Where to eat and drink

Seafood is the reason to linger past sunset.

Beachfront shacks grill whatever came in that morning — barracuda, squid, prawns — often for a fraction of what you'd pay at a sit-down restaurant in the city. Order it with a cold Angkor beer and a plate of morning glory stir-fry on the side. Otres 2 has a slightly more relaxed, less party-oriented set of bars than Otres 1, which leans younger and louder after dark.

Prices are generally budget-friendly across the strip, though a few newer beach clubs charge more for the same view.

## When to go

Cambodia's dry season, roughly November through April, is the safest bet for clear skies and calm water.

The wet season (May to October) brings heavier afternoon downpours, though mornings are often still fine and the beach empties out considerably — good if you'd rather have the sand to yourself. Early morning, before 9am, is consistently the quietest stretch of the day here, before the loungers fill and the tuk-tuks start arriving in numbers.

## How to visit like a local

Otres isn't an undiscovered spot anymore — word's gotten out, especially about Otres 2's sunset bars — so plan around the crowds rather than expecting to have it to yourself.

- Arrive before 9am or after 4pm to avoid the midday crush and harshest sun
- Carry small-denomination US dollars — Cambodia runs on USD for most tourist transactions, with riel used for change
- Negotiate your tuk-tuk fare before boarding, and agree on a pickup time if you want a return ride
- Tipping isn't obligatory but rounding up at beach bars is appreciated
- Bring cash for the beach — many smaller shacks don't take cards

The most common tourist mistake is stopping at Otres 1 and assuming that's the whole beach. Walk (or ride) further down to Otres 2 for a genuinely calmer stretch of sand, especially outside peak sunset hours.
