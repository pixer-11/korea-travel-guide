## Eleven days for the Santo Niño

Sinulog runs from January 8 to 18, 2027. It honours the Santo Niño, the image of the Child Jesus that Magellan's expedition brought to Cebu in 1521. The image is kept today in the Basilica Minore del Santo Niño on Osmeña Boulevard.

The name comes from the Cebuano word *sulog*, a water current. The dance copies it: two steps forward, one step back. It is set to drums and danced by contingents in feathered, sequinned costumes, with dancers holding up replicas of the Santo Niño. From the first day to the last you hear one chant everywhere: "Pit Señor!"

## How the schedule unfolds

The festival has two calendars running side by side, one religious and one civic.

- **Novena masses** fill the basilica's open-air pilgrim centre every day in the run-up to the feast.
- **The Walk with Jesus**, a pre-dawn foot procession through downtown streets, traditionally opens the main weekend.
- **The fluvial procession** carries the image by boat across the Mactan Channel on the Saturday before the feast.
- **The grand parade and ritual showcase** traditionally fall on the third Sunday of January, which is January 17 in 2027.

The Sunday is the longest day by far. Contingents dance the parade route for hours before the judged ritual showcase.

## Venue, tickets and getting in

Downtown is the festival's anchor. The basilica sits next to Magellan's Cross, a short walk from Colon Street and Plaza Independencia. The parade route links that core with the competition venue. In recent editions that venue has moved between the Cebu City Sports Center and the South Road Properties.

Standing along the parade route costs nothing. Grandstand seats at the competition venue are ticketed through the Sinulog Foundation, the event's organiser, which also sets the route and venue each year.

Most visitors fly into Mactan-Cebu International Airport in Lapu-Lapu City and cross one of the Mactan bridges into the city. Main roads downtown close for the processions. Attendees usually walk the last stretch. Hotels near Fuente Osmeña and the IT Park fill months ahead of the festival weekend.

## Moving through the crowds on parade day

Sinulog draws some of the largest crowds in the Philippines. Locals handle it with a few well-worn habits.

- **Dress the part.** People wear the festival shirt and get their faces painted by street artists along the route, and joining in is expected.
- **Candle dancers.** Women outside the basilica sell candles and dance a short Sinulog prayer for you. Most visitors pay them in small cash notes.
- **Carry little.** Phones go in front pockets and bags stay zipped in the packed streets around Colon Street.
- **Respect the image.** Inside the basilica the mood is devotional. People queue quietly to touch the glass case, then raise their hands and answer "Pit Señor!"
- **Eat on foot.** Lechon, Cebu's spit-roasted pig, and puso, rice cooked in woven coconut leaves, come from stalls and carinderias along the route.

During the dance, you will see hands lifted toward each passing Santo Niño. Spectators join the wave at the same moment the dancers do.
