Juhu has plenty of beachfront hotels with pools, but nothing quite like this. Bastian Hospitality's Beach Club is being talked about as Mumbai's first proper beach club, a category the city has never really had despite its long coastline.

## What makes it different from a hotel pool bar

Most Mumbai poolside venues sit behind five-star hotel gates, built for guests first and everyone else second. This one is designed as a destination in its own right, with a central pool as the organizing feature rather than an afterthought bolted onto a restaurant.

The design, from Minal Chopra, borrows visual cues from Ibiza and Mykonos: think whitewashed surfaces, loungers arranged for lingering rather than just marking time between drinks, and a layout built for daylight hours as much as evening ones. It's the kind of space Mumbai has imported in concept from European beach towns but rarely executed at this scale.

## The team behind it

Ranjit Bindra, the restaurateur behind the wider Bastian Hospitality group, has partnered with actress Shilpa Shetty on this project. Bastian's name already carries weight in Mumbai's dining scene for its seafood-forward, Asian-inflected menus, and that pedigree shows up here too.

Expect Bastian-style dishes rather than a generic club menu: hamachi ceviche and Arabian seabass are among the dishes associated with the kitchen's approach, leaning toward fresh fish prepared with a modern, pan-Asian touch. This isn't a place doing basic nachos-and-fries poolside fare; the food is positioned as a real part of the draw, not filler between cocktails.

## Where it sits in Juhu

Juhu is Mumbai's best-known beach neighborhood, long associated with Bollywood residences, seafront promenades, and a mix of old-money bungalows and newer commercial development. A beach club opening here plays directly into that identity, giving the area a genuinely new kind of venue rather than another restaurant or bar.

The neighborhood is well used to hosting nightlife and celebrity-adjacent crowds, so a high-profile opening like this fits the existing rhythm of the area even as it raises the bar for design and ambition.

## Why it's become one of the city's buzziest openings

Steep cover charges are not unusual for Mumbai's high-end nightlife, but a beach club format is new enough here that curiosity alone is driving traffic. Combine that with Shetty's public profile and Bastian's existing fan base, and you get a venue that's been generating conversation well beyond typical restaurant openings.

That buzz cuts both ways for visitors. It means the space is genuinely worth seeing if you want a sense of where Mumbai's trend-driven crowd is currently spending its evenings, but it also means demand is likely to outstrip easy walk-in access, especially on weekends.

## Getting in without wasting a trip

Because this is a newly opened, high-demand venue, treat a visit here more like planning a night out at a exclusive club than dropping by a neighborhood café.

- Call ahead or book through whatever reservation channel the venue currently uses; don't assume walk-ins are guaranteed entry.
- Confirm the cover charge and any spending minimum before you go, since these details shift as venues settle into their pricing.
- Ask about dress code expectations; design-forward beach clubs in this vein generally lean smart-casual to dressy rather than beachwear.
- Arrive earlier in the evening if you want a calmer read on the pool and design before the space fills up.
- Double-check current opening hours directly with the venue, since newly launched spots often adjust their schedule in the first months.

## Setting expectations before you book

This is a destination built on atmosphere and novelty as much as on food, so go in expecting a scene: good lighting, a design worth photographing, and a crowd that's there partly to be seen. If you want a quiet, low-key evening by the water, this isn't currently that kind of place. If you want to see what a genuine beach club looks like in a city that's never really had one, it's worth the effort to book a table.
