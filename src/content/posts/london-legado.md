## A chef's name on a Shoreditch door

The name comes first. In Spanish, *legado* means "legacy", an unusually personal choice for a chef who has spent two decades shaping how London eats Spanish food.

Nieves Barragán Mohacho grew up in Bilbao. She ran the kitchen at Barrafina when it won a Michelin star, then opened Sabor off Regent Street, which earned its own. Legado is her move east to Shoreditch, and the city's food press has followed her there.

## From Heddon Street to the East End

Sabor sits on Heddon Street, a short pedestrian lane in Mayfair. Shoreditch is a different London: railway arches, converted warehouses, street art on the side walls, and late bars along Curtain Road and Great Eastern Street.

That shift is part of the story. A chef known for a West End room is now cooking in a neighbourhood that runs on younger crowds and later nights.

What she has built inside is hers to describe, not ours to guess. Before you go, look at Legado's own website and social accounts. They will show the room, the current menu and how seating works far more reliably than any secondhand account.

## What a Barragán kitchen is known for

Her reputation is a matter of public record. At Barrafina and Sabor, she became known for:

- Spanish regional cooking, with a strong pull towards the north of the country
- Small plates meant for sharing rather than one main course each
- Produce-led dishes where the ingredient does most of the talking
- Rooms where watching the cooks is part of the evening

Legado's menu has not been published here on purpose, because it is new and likely to change. Read the current menu online the day you book. Expect the sharing format to shape how you order. If you are a table of two, three or four dishes in a first round usually leaves room to add more.

## Booking a table at a new opening

Newly opened London restaurants with a chef this well known fill up fast. Here is how getting in usually works:

- **Book through the official channel.** That means the restaurant's own website or the booking platform it links to. Avoid resale listings.
- **Watch for release dates.** Many London restaurants open bookings weeks ahead at a set time. Their newsletter or Instagram usually announces it.
- **Join the waitlist.** Cancellations happen, especially the day before and on the day.
- **Ask about walk-ins.** Some places keep seats free for people who turn up. Check whether Legado does before you plan on it.
- **Be flexible on timing.** An early or late sitting midweek is easier to get than prime time on a Friday.

Hours and booking rules at a new restaurant often change in the first months. Confirm both on the official site shortly before you go, not when you first read about it.

## Paying, tipping and table manners

London restaurants run almost entirely on card and contactless payments, and phone wallets work nearly everywhere. Many restaurants now refuse cash, so a card is the safer bet.

Most London restaurants add a discretionary service charge to the bill. Check the bill before you tip. If service is already included, you don't need to leave more unless you want to.

Sharing plates come with a simple etiquette. Order for the table, not for yourself. Let the staff pace the dishes, since they usually arrive as they are ready rather than in courses. If you have allergies, say so when you book and again when you sit down. A kitchen built around sharing needs to know early.

## Getting to Shoreditch

Shoreditch is well served, though the nearest stop depends on Legado's exact address, which you should check when you book. These are the main options:

- **Shoreditch High Street** (London Overground) is the stop for the neighbourhood's central streets.
- **Old Street** (Northern line, Bank branch) covers the western edge, around the Silicon Roundabout.
- **Liverpool Street** (Central, Circle, Hammersmith & City, Metropolitan and Elizabeth lines) is a ten-to-fifteen-minute walk north to most of Shoreditch.

Night buses run along Shoreditch High Street and Old Street. The Overground and the Elizabeth line both make it easy to get back to central London after a late dinner.

## Building an evening around it

Shoreditch is best on foot, before or after dinner. A few well-known anchors nearby:

- **Boxpark Shoreditch**, the shipping-container food and retail park beside Shoreditch High Street station
- **Brick Lane**, a short walk east, with its curry houses, vintage shops and the Truman Brewery
- **Old Spitalfields Market**, south towards Liverpool Street, under its Victorian roof
- **Columbia Road Flower Market**, on Sunday mornings only, if you are staying the weekend
- **Redchurch Street**, lined with independent shops and galleries

For a drink before dinner, the streets around Curtain Road and Rivington Street are full of bars. Plan two to three hours for the meal itself. A sharing-plate dinner at a chef-led restaurant is meant to take its time. Booking the restaurant first and fitting the walk around it beats hoping for a table on the night.
