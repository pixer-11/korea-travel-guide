## What Prés de L'AS actually is

This is not a copy of L'AS, it's a deliberate loosening of it. The original L'AS built its name on precise, French-technique tasting menus with a Japanese sense of restraint. Prés de L'AS takes that same kitchen discipline and drops the formality: fewer courses, a shorter sitting, a room built for a weeknight dinner rather than an event.

The signature holdover is the crispy foie gras sandwich, the dish that made the original restaurant's reputation. Expect it framed here as one part of a tighter, more approachable omakase course rather than a single showpiece plate.

## Why Harajuku, and why it stands out

Harajuku is saturated with fast fashion and crepe stands; a modern French kitchen with this pedigree is a genuine outlier in the neighborhood. It gives L'AS a second, lower-friction address without diluting the cooking behind it, the same reason chefs elsewhere open a "little sister" bistro near a flagship.

What sets it apart from the wave of new Tokyo French openings is lineage: this isn't a debut chef testing ideas, it's a proven kitchen scaling down deliberately.

- Relaxed omakase format, not a la carte grazing
- Shorter course count than the flagship L'AS
- Signature crispy foie gras sandwich carried over
- Casual room, easier for a solo diner or a two-top

## Booking a table without the guesswork

Treat this like any trending Tokyo restaurant riding a big name: reservations move fast once a spot is written up.

- Book through the restaurant's own site or a reservation platform well before your date, not a walk-in
- Confirm the current opening days and service times directly with the restaurant before finalizing plans, as recently opened spots often adjust hours in the first months
- Ask about a cancellation waitlist if your preferred date is full
- Have your reservation confirmation ready, in English or Japanese, for the door

Because this is a new offshoot, published details online can lag reality. Call or check the official channel the same week you plan to visit.

## Eating there without the flagship's formality

The whole point of the Harajuku outpost is that you can eat well without the ceremony of the original L'AS. Dress smart-casual rather than for a special-occasion tasting menu.

Pace yourself through the courses; a shorter omakase still moves through several plates, and the kitchen is working from the same French technique as the flagship, just edited down.

- Order still water or a glass of wine rather than assuming a wine pairing is built in, and ask staff what's offered that night
- Save room and appetite for the foie gras sandwich course rather than filling up early
- If you loved this version, the original L'AS is the next booking to chase, expect it to be considerably harder to secure

Pay by card; most contemporary Tokyo restaurants at this level accept them, but carry some cash as backup for smaller reservation deposits or incidentals.
