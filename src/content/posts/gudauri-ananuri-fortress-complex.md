Stone walls run down a grassy slope to turquoise water, and two church domes rise inside them. The Zhinvali Reservoir laps almost at the foot of the fortress. Cars on the Georgian Military Road slow on the bend right above it, and most of them pull in.

Ananuri is the first big stop north of Tbilisi on the road to Gudauri and Kazbegi. It ranks among the most-reviewed historic sites in Georgia. You won't have it to yourself, but you can choose a time when it feels close.

## A ducal stronghold on the Aragvi

The complex was the seat of the Eristavis, the Dukes of Aragvi. They ruled this valley from the 13th century, and most of what stands today dates from the 16th and 17th centuries. The editorial summary calls it a castle that saw many battles, and that undersells how violent its history was.

The fortress has two parts:

- **The upper fortress**, still mostly intact, holding both churches and the big square tower.
- **The lower fortress**, now largely ruined and partly lost below the waterline.

The reservoir itself is modern. The Zhinvali dam was finished in the 1980s and flooded the old village around the walls. That is why the water now sits so close to the fortifications.

## The carved wall of the Church of the Assumption

Go round to the outside walls of the larger church before you go in. The Church of the Assumption was built in 1689, and its façades carry some of the finest stone carving of its period in Georgia. The south side is the one to stand in front of.

Look for a large carved cross growing out of grapevines, the Georgian symbol tied to Saint Nino. There are figures and animals around it, and on the north side the stone is cut in a pattern like rope. Inside, the frescoes are darkened by candle smoke and partly lost.

Next to it stands the smaller, older Church of the Saviour, and around them both are the bell tower and the walls. Give the carvings at least twenty minutes. Most visitors walk past them on the way to the view.

## Climbing Sheupovari, the tower that didn't surrender

The square tower in the upper fortress is called Sheupovari, roughly "the indomitable". It's said to be where the last defenders held out. In 1739 the rival Duke of Ksani took Ananuri, set it on fire and killed the Aragvi Eristavi family. Their line ended a few decades later.

When it's open, you climb the inside on narrow, steep wooden steps and ladders. They aren't easy in a long skirt or with a backpack. At the top, small openings look straight down the reservoir and up the valley towards the mountains. You can also walk the wall walk along parts of the battlements for the classic downhill view of the domes and water.

## Timing your stop around the tour buses

The crowds follow the day-trip schedule from Tbilisi. On weekends, **10am–3pm is the busiest window**. Minibuses arrive one after another and the tower stairs back up. Skip that window if you can.

The quiet windows we have:

- **Weekdays:** 3pm–8pm
- **Weekends:** 6pm–8pm

At the moment it opens 9am–8pm every day of the week. Historic sites in Georgia can change their hours with the season or for church holidays, so check before planning a late visit. If you're staying in Gudauri, a late-afternoon weekday stop on the drive back south fits the quiet window well. Low sun also lights the south façade.

## Headscarves, candles and the church door

Both churches are active Orthodox churches, and the dress code is applied. Women cover their hair and wear a skirt. Men wear long trousers and go in bareheaded. Wraps and scarves are usually available near the church door for anyone caught out, but carrying your own is quicker.

A few habits that will make your visit easier:

- Keep your voice down inside. Don't stand between worshippers and the iconostasis.
- Ask before photographing inside, and never use flash during a service.
- Thin beeswax candles are sold inside. People light one and set it in the sand trays in front of the icons.
- Stalls by the car park sell churchkhela (walnuts dipped in thickened grape juice) and souvenirs. Carry some small Georgian lari notes, as card machines are rare at roadside stalls.

## Getting there from Gudauri or Tbilisi

Ananuri sits right on the Georgian Military Road (the E117), about an hour's drive south of Gudauri, with the Jinvali and Pasanauri stretch in between. Coming from Tbilisi, it's about 72 km, or roughly an hour and a half by car. There's a roadside car park just below the walls.

Without a car, marshrutkas (shared minibuses) to Stepantsminda leave from Didube bus station in Tbilisi and pass the fortress. Ask the driver to drop you at Ananuri. Flagging down onward minibuses at the roadside is possible but unpredictable. That's why most people come by taxi, with a private driver or on a Kazbegi day tour that stops here.

## Pairing Ananuri with the rest of the road

Most people spend 45–90 minutes here, enough for both churches, the tower and a walk along the water. Spend longer and you'll be eating into time for the stops further north.

On the same road:

- **Pasanauri**, famous for khinkali, the large Georgian soup dumplings.
- **Gudauri** and the Russia–Georgia Friendship Monument with its tiled panorama above the Devil's Valley.
- **Stepantsminda** and the Gergeti Trinity Church below Mount Kazbek, further north.

On the way down from Gudauri, Ananuri is the natural last stop before Tbilisi.
