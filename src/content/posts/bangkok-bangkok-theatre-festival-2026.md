## Sixteen days of stage work in Pathum Wan

The 24th Bangkok Theatre Festival runs from November 14 to 29, 2026. Its programme covers theatre, contemporary performance and movement works, so a single evening can hold a scripted drama and a wordless dance piece.

Venues include the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre and TK Park. Both are in central Bangkok's shopping district, a short walk or one Skytrain stop apart. Mid-November is also the start of Bangkok's cooler, drier season, which makes walking between venues after dark easier than it is in the monsoon months.

## Reaching BACC and TK Park

BACC is the white, curved building on the Pathum Wan intersection. A skywalk links it to **National Stadium BTS station (Silom Line)**, so you can step off the train and into the lobby without crossing Rama I Road. Inside, a spiral ramp climbs through the atrium past galleries, and the lower floors hold small shops and cafés.

TK Park, the Thailand Knowledge Park learning centre, is on an upper floor of **CentralWorld**. The nearest stations are:

- **Chit Lom BTS (Sukhumvit Line)**, linked to the mall by skywalk
- **Siam BTS**, the interchange for both lines, about a 10-minute walk along the elevated walkway

Rama I and Ratchadamri roads jam badly in the evening. Festivalgoers moving between shows usually take the BTS rather than a taxi.

## How seats and audiences work at the festival

A lot of the programme plays in small studio-style rooms. Seat counts are low, and popular performances often fill up. Regulars tend to choose their shows early in the run, while the festival's buzz builds in the second week.

- **Language:** much of the work is in Thai. Some productions add English surtitles, and movement and physical theatre pieces need no translation. Each show's listing says which language it uses.
- **Paying:** Thai audiences mostly pay by PromptPay QR transfer or card. Small independent companies often sell seats through their own social media pages.
- **Etiquette:** phones go fully dark in small rooms, where any screen glow lights up the space. Latecomers in black-box venues are often held at the door until a suitable break.
- **Talkbacks:** some performances end with a post-show discussion, and Thai audiences typically stay for it.

Ticket prices vary by production and appear on each show's listing.

## Around the venues between shows

Back-to-back evenings are easy here because so much is close by. From BACC, **MBK Center** sits directly across the intersection, and its food court is quick and cheap. **Siam Discovery** and **Siam Paragon** are one stop east along Rama I.

**Jim Thompson House**, the teak-house museum by the Saen Saep canal, is a short walk from National Stadium station. It fills a daytime gap before an evening performance well.

Around CentralWorld, the Ratchaprasong intersection has the **Erawan Shrine**, which smells of incense and marigold garlands at any hour. The mall's upper-floor restaurants stay busy until late. Visitors following the full festival usually stay near Siam or Chit Lom, because both venues are then one stop or a short walk from their hotel.
