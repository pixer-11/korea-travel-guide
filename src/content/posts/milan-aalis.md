## A French bakery on a Milanese side street

AALIS opened on September 26, 2026. It is still finding its regulars. It sits on Via Nirone, a quiet street in the western part of the centro storico. This is the Magenta side of town, where Milan does its weekday business in tailored coats and its weekends at long tables.

The idea is simple. It is a neighbourhood café crossed with a contemporary bakery, and the French influence sets it apart from the city's standard Italian bar. You come for pastry in the morning and stay into brunch, or you drift back for something sweet mid-afternoon.

## From first pastry to afternoon snack

The bakery is built to cover the whole day rather than one meal. That matters in Milan. Most bars here serve a quick cornetto at the counter and little else until aperitivo.

Expect three distinct moods:

- **Morning:** pastries and coffee, the Milanese *colazione* hour, when people stop on their way to work.
- **Late morning:** brunch, which in this city fills up most on Saturdays and Sundays.
- **Afternoon:** snacks and a lull before the evening crowd heads out for aperitivo.

The menu was not public when this guide was written, so treat the counter as the real menu. Look at what is laid out before you decide. A new bakery's range often shifts in its first months, so a repeat visit may look different.

## Reaching Via Nirone

The street is easy to reach on foot from two metro stations. **Sant'Ambrogio** on the green M2 line puts you beside the basilica of the same name. **Cadorna**, served by both M1 (red) and M2, is also within a short walk and is where the Malpensa Express arrives.

Tram 16 runs along Corso Magenta, the area's main street, if you are coming from the Duomo side. From the Duomo itself it is a comfortable walk west of about 20 minutes. The route takes you past palazzi and courtyard doors rather than shopfronts.

## Ordering the Milanese way

A few local habits will make the visit smoother. Greet the staff with a *buongiorno* before noon and *buonasera* after it. In Italy that hello counts as part of the order.

- **Coffee rules:** cappuccino is a morning drink in Italy. After lunch, locals switch to an espresso (*un caffè*) or a *macchiato*.
- **How to pay:** card and contactless payment are standard in Milan. Some bars still have you pay at the till first and show the receipt at the counter, so watch what the person ahead of you does.
- **Tipping:** you are not expected to tip. Rounding up or leaving small change is welcome but optional.
- **Counter or table:** a quick pastry standing up is the classic Milanese breakfast. Sit down if you want brunch or plan to stay a while.

Because AALIS is new and getting attention, check its hours and reservation policy before you go, especially for weekend brunch. Arriving early in the morning usually means you get the full pastry selection before anything sells out.

## A morning around Corso Magenta

The bakery's location makes it easy to fit into a day of sightseeing. Several of Milan's major sights are a few minutes away.

- **Santa Maria delle Grazie:** home of Leonardo da Vinci's *Last Supper*, on Corso Magenta. Entry is by timed ticket, often booked weeks ahead, so a pastry stop works well before your slot.
- **Basilica di Sant'Ambrogio:** one of the city's oldest churches, with a brick atrium that predates the Duomo by centuries.
- **Museo Nazionale della Scienza e della Tecnologia:** on Via San Vittore, with galleries devoted to Leonardo's machines.
- **Civico Museo Archeologico:** on Corso Magenta, built into the former Monastero Maggiore, next to the frescoed church of San Maurizio.

Allow 30 to 45 minutes for a coffee and pastry, and an hour or more for brunch. Then walk it off on the cloister-and-courtyard streets that make this corner of Milan feel calmer than the Duomo crowds a short way east.
