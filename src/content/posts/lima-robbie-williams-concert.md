## A Saturday Night at San Marcos

Robbie Williams plays Lima on December 12, 2026. That date falls on a Saturday, which matters in a city where weeknight traffic can swallow an evening whole. Estadio San Marcos is the venue.

The stadium sits inside the campus of Universidad Nacional Mayor de San Marcos, founded in 1551 and usually described as the oldest university in the Americas. So the gig is a British pop showman working a crowd in a university stadium. That gives the night a different feel from an arena show in a shopping-mall district.

## The Showman Lima Is Getting

He has had a long time to perfect the act. Williams left Take That in 1995 and started his solo career the following year. By the end of the decade he had become one of the biggest-selling British solo artists of his era.

The songs most of the stadium knows by heart:

- "Angels", the ballad that usually turns the whole crowd into one choir
- "Feel"
- "Rock DJ"
- "Let Me Entertain You", long his favourite way to kick a show into gear
- "She's the One" and "Millennium"

The 2024 biopic *Better Man*, in which he is played as a digitally rendered chimpanzee, brought his story to a younger audience. Expect that mix in the stands: fans who bought the singles on CD, plus their kids who found him through the film.

Williams talks to the crowd constantly between songs, and the banter is a big part of the show. The setlist for Lima has not been published. The hits above are simply the core of his live act over many years.

## Estadio San Marcos and Ciudad Universitaria

The stadium is in the northwest of the university's large Ciudad Universitaria campus. That puts it in Cercado de Lima, near where the district meets San Miguel. The campus is bordered by Avenida Venezuela and Avenida Universitaria, two of the main roads across this side of the city.

It is a large open-air bowl. That suits a singer who likes to see every face in the crowd and get them all singing. With no roof, the sound and the mood depend partly on the weather. In Lima in December, the weather is reliably on your side (more on that below).

Parque de las Leyendas, the zoo and archaeological park in San Miguel, is a short distance away. It is the most useful landmark if you are giving directions to a driver unfamiliar with the campus gates.

## Crossing Lima on Show Day

Most international visitors base themselves in Miraflores, Barranco or San Isidro, and none of them is next door to the stadium. The usual route runs north and west across the city. Lima's traffic is notorious, so a ride that is quick at midnight can take far longer in the late afternoon.

How attendees typically get there:

- **Ride-hail apps.** Uber, Cabify and InDrive all work in Lima, and this is how many visitors travel.
- **Registered taxis.** Hotel front desks call these routinely. Agreeing the fare before getting in is normal with street taxis.
- **Hotel or tour transfers.** These are popular with groups who don't want to bargain after midnight.

A Saturday helps. The weekday commuter rush is absent, though big events still create their own bottleneck on the avenues around the campus.

## Lima's First Summer Nights

December is when Lima's grey season lets go. From roughly June to November, the coastal fog the city calls *garúa* hangs low over everything. By December, early summer is arriving and the sky is clearer.

Lima also barely gets rain. The coastal desert climate means an outdoor stadium show is rarely at risk of a downpour. That is a real advantage over many cities on a world tour.

Evenings near the Pacific still cool down after dark, even in summer. Fans usually bring a light layer for the hours after sunset. Days are warm enough that sunscreen matters if you queue in daylight.

## How the Crowd Moves at San Marcos

Lima's big stadium concerts follow a familiar pattern. Queues build along the campus perimeter well before the gates open. Fans with general-admission standing tickets tend to come earliest, hoping for a place near the barrier.

What locals usually do:

- **Pay digitally.** Peruvians use cards and mobile wallets such as Yape and Plin, but many carry some soles in cash for street food sellers outside.
- **Keep phones in front pockets.** In big crowds this is standard advice in Lima, as it is in most large cities.
- **Plan the exit.** Ride-hail prices and wait times jump the moment tens of thousands leave together. Many fans walk a few blocks away from the gates before requesting a car.
- **Pick a meeting point.** Mobile signal often drops when a stadium is full, so groups agree on a landmark beforehand.

Peruvian crowds sing loudly and know the English lyrics. If you are travelling for the show, expect "Angels" to sound like a full choir.

## Before and After the Show

Many visitors build the concert into a longer Lima stay. The historic centre, with Plaza Mayor, the Cathedral and the Monastery of San Francisco and its catacombs, is a short ride east of the campus. It makes a natural daytime stop on concert day.

Lima is one of South America's great food capitals, and a ceviche lunch is a local Saturday habit. Cevicherías traditionally serve at midday, since the fish is bought fresh that morning. Many fans eat a long lunch, rest at the hotel, then head across town.

After the encore, Miraflores and Barranco are where the night usually continues. Barranco's bars around the Puente de los Suspiros stay lively late on weekends.
