## A bookshop on the daytime side of Hindley Street

Hindley Street has a reputation, and it isn't for books. After dark this stretch of Adelaide's West End belongs to bars, kebab shops and clubs. In daylight it's a different street: students crossing from the university campuses, office workers on lunch, shopfronts with their doors open.

Imprints Booksellers sits at number 107, among those daytime doors. It's a street-level independent bookshop. You walk in off the footpath, the traffic noise falls away, and the room is shelves and tables of books. It's consistently well rated by the people who find it, and it has far fewer reviews than the big attractions a few blocks away. Most visitors to Adelaide never walk this far down the street.

## What you're there to do

Come here to browse, not to tick something off. An independent bookshop is a set of choices someone has made about what deserves shelf space. The pleasure is in seeing what those choices are. They won't be the same as the airport chain's.

Some things worth doing while you're inside:

- **Look for local writers.** South Australian and wider Australian authors are the obvious souvenir that doesn't end up in a drawer.
- **Read the table displays.** At an independent shop, the front tables usually show what the staff are excited about right now.
- **Ask for a recommendation.** Name a book you loved and see what comes back.
- **Pick up something light for the flight.** A paperback travels better than anything from a gift shop.

The shop doesn't publish a single signature section or specialty, so don't arrive expecting one. Arrive curious and let the shelves decide.

## Hindley Street by daylight

The shop's hours shape how you'll experience the street. At the moment Imprints opens at 10am and closes at 5:30pm, Monday to Saturday. That's almost exactly the window when Hindley Street is a quiet commercial street rather than a nightlife strip.

This works in your favour. A mid-morning or early-afternoon walk down Hindley shows you the West End's daytime life: cafés, small businesses and the university crowd. It's also a reminder that Adelaide's city grid is compact. The shift from the shopping crowds of Rundle Mall to the quieter West End takes one crossing at King William Street.

If you want the nightlife side, come back later. Just don't plan an evening book browse, because the door will be shut.

## Getting to 107 Hindley St

The shop is in the city's square mile, so most visitors will walk. From the western end of Rundle Mall, cross King William Street and keep going west along Hindley; the street numbers climb as you go.

Other ways in:

- **Tram:** the city tram runs along King William Street and North Terrace. Get off on King William Street near Rundle Mall and walk west, or at a North Terrace stop and cut south.
- **Train:** Adelaide Railway Station on North Terrace is a short walk north of Hindley Street.
- **Bus:** the free City Connector buses loop through the CBD and are handy if you're coming from the East End or North Adelaide.
- **Car:** there's no dedicated parking. Use a city car park and walk, since Hindley Street itself is narrow and busy.

## Browsing etiquette at an independent counter

Small bookshops run on conversation more than self-service. A few habits make the visit easier for you and for the staff.

- **Pay by card.** Contactless card and phone payments are the everyday norm in Australia. Prices on the tag include GST, so what you see is what you pay.
- **No tipping.** It isn't expected in Australian shops.
- **Ask about ordering.** If a title isn't on the shelf, independent booksellers can usually order it in. Ask at the counter how long that takes before you leave town.
- **Leave your coffee outside.** A takeaway cup near new books makes every bookseller nervous.
- **Be quick with your backpack.** Aisles in small shops are narrow, so swing it to your front when you squeeze past someone.

Mention it if you're visiting from overseas. Staff can point you to Australian writers you won't easily find at home, and that's the most useful thing this shop can give you.

## Pairing it with the West End and North Terrace

Imprints is easy to fit into a day of Adelaide's cultural institutions, most of which line North Terrace just north of Hindley Street.

- **State Library of South Australia:** the Mortlock Wing's galleried reading room is one of the city's most photographed interiors. After a bookshop, it's the natural next stop.
- **Art Gallery of South Australia:** further east along North Terrace, next to the South Australian Museum.
- **Rundle Mall:** the main pedestrian shopping street, directly east across King William Street.
- **Adelaide Central Market:** about a 15-minute walk south, good for lunch before or after.

A sensible loop runs from the Central Market to Hindley Street, then on to the State Library. It covers food, books and architecture without needing transport.

## Timing your visit

Plan around the Sunday closure first. If you're in Adelaide for a weekend, Saturday is your only chance. On weekdays you have the full 10am–5:30pm window.

Opening hours can change, especially around public holidays, so check the shop's listing before you set out. If you're visiting in March, the Adelaide Festival and Adelaide Writers' Week bring a big literary crowd into the city. That makes it a good time to browse Australian fiction while the authors are in town.

Give yourself 30 minutes for a quick look and an hour if you like to read first pages before you buy. Bring some space in your bag. Few people leave an independent bookshop empty-handed.
