## A stone chapel built for one view

It's a small building with a lot of sky behind it. The Church of the Good Shepherd went up in 1935 as a memorial to the Mackenzie Country's pioneer runholders. Its walls are rough fieldstone gathered from the surrounding land, and it stands on a low rise right above the turquoise water.

Step inside and the plan is plain: a short aisle of wooden pews leading to a clear window behind the altar. There is no stained glass. The altar window frames Lake Tekapo and the mountains beyond it, and that framed view is what everyone came to see. The chapel still holds interdenominational services.

Outside, a few minutes' walk along the shore, stands the bronze sheepdog statue. The high-country farming families commissioned it in 1968 to honour the collies the Mackenzie runs depended on.

## Beating the coaches to Pioneer Drive

Pick the right hour and you'll have the altar window to yourself. Pick the wrong one and you'll see it over twenty heads.

- **Quietest:** 9am–10am on weekdays and weekends alike, which is the first hour the doors are open.
- **Avoid:** 11am–12pm and 3pm–5pm on weekends. That's when buses on the Christchurch–Queenstown run stop here.
- **Current hours:** 9am–5pm. The church shuts for weddings and services, so check the noticeboard at the door before you plan around it.

Fifteen to thirty minutes covers the chapel, the lakeshore and the dog statue. Add time if you want photographs from the slope with the church and the water together.

## Inside a working church

Treat it as a place of worship that happens to be famous.

- **No photos inside.** Signs at the entrance ask visitors not to photograph the interior, and volunteers enforce it. Take your pictures from the grounds.
- **Keep your voice down** and step aside from the altar if someone is sitting quietly in a pew.
- **Donations** go toward upkeep. There's a box inside, and no entry charge is posted.
- **Stay on the paths.** The ground around the church has been roped off in places to stop erosion, and the stone walls are not for climbing.
- **Leave the lupins alone.** In late spring, roughly November–December, purple and pink lupins grow along the shore. Trampling them for a photo is the classic way to annoy the locals.

## Getting there and staying for the dark

The church is about three hours' drive from Christchurch on State Highway 8, and about the same from Queenstown. Aoraki/Mount Cook Village is roughly an hour and a quarter further on. From the township you can walk to it along the lakefront in a few minutes. If you're driving, the car park on Pioneer Drive fills quickly at the weekend peaks.

Lake Tekapo sits inside the Aoraki Mackenzie International Dark Sky Reserve. That's why the church appears in so many star photographs, with the Milky Way above the roof. The interior is closed at night, but the dark sky is the other reason to stay overnight in the township.

Nearby, Mt John Observatory looks down on the lake and runs guided stargazing tours. Tekapo Springs, at the foot of the same hill, has hot pools for after a cold night under the stars.
