## Getting there and getting oriented

Khao Sam Roi Yot means "mountain of three hundred peaks," a fair description of the jagged limestone karsts that spike straight out of the marshland here. The park covers 98 square kilometers of coastline, caves and wetland about 60-70km south of Hua Hin, roughly an hour by car along Highway 4.

There's no public bus that drops you at the entrance, so most visitors hire a car, join a tour, or ride a motorbike from Hua Hin. The park has multiple access points; the visitor center near Khao Daeng and the pier at Bang Pu (for Phraya Nakhon Cave) are the two most used.

## Phraya Nakhon Cave, the reason most people come

This is the park's signature sight: a limestone cave with a collapsed roof section that lets sunlight pour onto Kuha Karuhas, a small wooden pavilion built for a royal visit in the 1890s. Photos of sunbeams hitting that pavilion roof are what put this park on Instagram.

Reaching it means a boat ride from Bang Pu pier to Laem Sala beach, then a steep, sweaty 430-meter climb over rock steps. Expect 20-30 minutes uphill each way; wear real shoes, not sandals.

The light shaft angles best from roughly 10:30 AM to noon, so aim to summit by then. Arrive at Bang Pu when the park opens at 8:00 AM to beat both the heat and the boat queue.

## Beyond the cave: beaches, birds and a coastal lookout

Sam Phraya beach, a long stretch of white sand with basic park facilities, works well for lunch or a swim after the cave hike. It's calmer than Hua Hin's town beaches and rarely crowded even on weekends.

Khao Daeng, near the southern park office, has a short trail to a viewpoint over the estuary; go late afternoon for boats returning against the karst backdrop. The freshwater marshes here are also one of Thailand's best birdwatching sites, especially November to March when migratory species pass through.

Tham Kaeo, a smaller cave with stalactites, sits inland and needs a local guide with a lantern, arranged at the park office.

## Practical notes for the day

The park runs 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM every day; plan your boat and cave visit to finish well before closing, since the trail back down gets dark fast under tree cover.

- Bring cash for boat fares and park entry, paid at the respective checkpoints
- Wear sturdy shoes for the cave climb and sun protection for the boat ride
- Pack water; there's little shade on the ascent
- Check tide and weather before booking a boat, since rough seas can cancel the Bang Pu crossing
- Combine the cave with Sam Phraya beach or Khao Daeng viewpoint to fill a full day

This is a well-known stop on the Hua Hin coast, not a secret find, so weekends and Thai holidays bring real crowds at Bang Pu pier. A weekday visit, timed for the 8:00 AM opening, gives you the cleanest run at both the boat queue and the light inside the cave.
