## Why Monza, why this race

Monza is the "Temple of Speed," and it earns the nickname the moment you hear the first cars leave the pit lane. The Autodromo Nazionale Monza sits inside a royal park north of Milan, its long straights cut through century-old trees. This is the fastest, most historic circuit on the F1 calendar, run continuously since 1922, and the Italian Grand Prix is the sport's second-oldest race after the British GP.

What makes Monza different from a modern street circuit is the noise. Cars hit their highest top speeds of the season on the straights before the Parabolica and Ascari chicanes, and the old high-speed banking from the original 1950s oval still stands, crumbling and photogenic, in the woods behind the current track. Tifosi turn out in red for Ferrari regardless of form, and the podium ceremony afterward is one of the loudest in racing.

## When it happens

The 2026 Pirelli Gran Premio d'Italia runs September 4–6, with the race itself on Sunday, September 6. Friday brings practice sessions, Saturday qualifying, and Sunday the 53-lap main event around the 5.79km circuit.

Confirm timing and tickets on the official Formula 1 and Autodromo Nazionale Monza websites, since session times and any schedule adjustments are published there.

## Getting there and around

Monza sits about 15km northeast of central Milan, and most visitors base themselves in the city and travel out for race weekend.

- Take a regional train from Milano Centrale or Milano Porta Garibaldi to Monza station, then a shuttle bus to the circuit — this is the standard race-weekend route.
- Shuttle buses run from Monza town and from Milan itself on event days, timed around session schedules.
- Driving is possible but parking fills early and roads around the park congest badly; arrive well before the first session if you go by car.
- The circuit sits inside Parco di Monza, a large public park, so expect a walk from transport drop-off points to the grandstands.

## Tickets and where to sit

Grandstand options at Monza range from the Ascari and Roggia stands overlooking the chicanes to the Parabolica grandstand at the final corner, plus general admission areas inside the park that let you roam between viewing points and catch the cars on several parts of the lap.

- General admission tickets are the most affordable way in and give access to hillside and infield viewing areas.
- Grandstand seats cost more but guarantee a fixed view of a specific corner or straight.
- The main straight and start/finish grandstands sell out fastest given the podium and pit lane views.
- Buy directly through the official Monza circuit site or authorized resellers, and check the current ticket page for exact pricing and availability.

## What to do beyond the race

Race weekend is really a three-day festival. Fridays and Saturdays are quieter and cheaper, and general admission holders can walk the park, watch support races, and get close to the fencing at braking zones like the first chicane or Lesmo curves.

The Parco di Monza itself is worth the walk — it's one of Europe's largest enclosed parks, with the Villa Reale di Monza nearby if you want a break from engine noise. Monza's old town, a short walk or bus ride from the station, has a compact historic centre with the striped Duomo di Monza and everyday trattorias serving Lombard food away from the circuit crowds.

## How to visit like a local

Regulars treat Friday and Saturday as the real value: practice and qualifying sessions cost less to attend and the crowds thin out enough to move freely around the park's general admission zones.

- Pay for shuttle buses and food stalls with contactless card or cash; both are widely accepted around the circuit.
- Arrive at least an hour before your session — security queues at the main gates back up fast on Sunday morning.
- Bring earplugs; Monza's straights make it one of the loudest circuits in F1.
- Wear Ferrari red if you want to blend in — the tifosi turn the main grandstand into a sea of it, especially on Sunday.
- The rookie mistake is underestimating the walk inside the park — distances between grandstands and gates are longer than they look on the map, so plan extra time.
