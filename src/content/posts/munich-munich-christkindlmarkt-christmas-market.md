## Under the Rathaus tower

Marienplatz is Munich's central square. The stalls sit beneath the neo-Gothic front of the Neues Rathaus, its blackened stone facade rising behind a tall decorated Christmas tree. The Glockenspiel in the town hall tower overlooks the whole market, and the crowd usually stops and looks up when its figures turn.

The wooden huts run west from the square along the Kaufingerstraße and Neuhauser Straße pedestrian zone toward Karlsplatz (Stachus). Walking it end to end, you move past roofs hung with greenery, along the busiest shopping street in the city.

## Dates, tickets and the wider market map

The 2026 edition is dated **November 20 to December 24, 2026**. Christmas Eve is the final day, so the market is over before Christmas Day itself.

You don't need a ticket. The market is open streets, and you browse and buy stall by stall. Munich runs several smaller themed markets over the same weeks. The best known is the **medieval market at Wittelsbacherplatz**, a short walk north near Odeonsplatz (U3, U4, U5, U6).

A typical loop:

- **Marienplatz**: the main tree, the Rathaus and the densest cluster of stalls
- **Kaufingerstraße to Karlsplatz**: the long tail of huts along the shopping street
- **Wittelsbacherplatz**: the medieval-themed market, an easy walk via Theatinerstraße
- **Frauenkirche**: its twin onion-domed towers are a block off the main route

Most visitors give the main market an hour or two and add the medieval market to fill an evening.

## What fills the stalls

This is a traditional market, and most of the huts sell either food or handmade decorations. The ornament stalls carry wooden figures, glass baubles, straw stars and nativity pieces. People shop here for the tree as much as they come for the drinks.

What people usually eat and drink:

- **Glühwein**: hot spiced wine, the default order
- **Feuerzangenbowle**: mulled wine with a rum-soaked sugar cone set alight above it
- **Gebrannte Mandeln**: sugar-roasted almonds in paper cones
- **Lebkuchen**: Nuremberg-style spiced gingerbread
- **Bratwurst in a roll** and **Reiberdatschi** (potato fritters with apple sauce)

## Mugs, deposits and the Glühwein queue

Hot drinks come in ceramic mugs with a deposit added, known as *Pfand*. When you hand the mug back at any stall from the same vendor, you get the deposit refunded. Plenty of visitors keep the mug as a souvenir instead. Many of the designs are printed with the year, which makes them collectable.

There is no formal queue at the drink huts. People press toward the counter, give their order in a few words ("Einen Glühwein, bitte"), and pay at once. Small stalls are often more comfortable with cash than cards, so locals tend to carry coins and small notes. Ask for *mit Schuss* if you want a shot of rum or amaretto added.

The huts have no seating. Groups gather around tall standing tables, and strangers commonly share them. Weekday afternoons are generally calmer than weekend evenings, when office workers and day-trippers fill the pedestrian zone. Many visitors who want space go to Wittelsbacherplatz or the side streets once Marienplatz is packed shoulder to shoulder.
