## Lanes cut for shade

The alleys here are barely wider than your outstretched arms. That's deliberate. Walls of coral stone and gypsum rise on both sides and keep the lanes in shadow for most of the day. Above the rooftops stand square wind towers (*barjeel*), open on four sides to catch any breeze and push it down into the rooms below.

Merchants from Bastak, in southern Iran, settled this stretch of Dubai Creek in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. They brought the wind-tower design with them, and the district takes its name from their home town. Today it's officially the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood. The restored courtyard houses hold galleries, small museums and cafés.

## Doors worth pushing

Most visitors come for the whole maze, not one building. These are the places to aim for:

- **Sheikh Mohammed Centre for Cultural Understanding**: runs heritage walks, cultural meals and open Q&A sessions on Emirati life. Book ahead.
- **XVA Gallery**: contemporary art from the region, set around a courtyard.
- **Coffee Museum**: roasting tools, pots and the Arabic coffee ritual.
- **Al Fahidi Fort**: Dubai's oldest standing building, on the district's edge. It housed the Dubai Museum, so check its current status before you plan around it.

Every March the Sikka Art and Design Festival turns the houses and courtyards into temporary exhibition spaces. Opening hours differ from house to house and none are posted here, so check each venue before you set off.

## Reaching the Creek quarter

Al Fahidi Station on the Metro Green Line is the closest stop. Walk north towards the Creek and you'll reach the quarter's edge in a few minutes. Taxis drop off along Al Fahidi Street.

From there, continue on foot:

- **Bur Dubai Souq (Textile Souq)**: its covered lanes start just west of the quarter.
- **Bur Dubai Abra Station**: take a wooden abra across the Creek to the Deira Old Souq, the Spice Souq and the Gold Souq.
- **Al Seef**: the rebuilt waterfront promenade, a short walk along the Creek to the south.

## Walking it at the right pace

The quarter rewards slow, unplanned turns, and it's still off the main tourist circuit. Walk in with no route in mind. Dead ends usually end at a courtyard door or a wind tower worth craning your neck for.

- **Timing:** from May to September the midday heat is punishing, even in shaded lanes. Go at opening time or late in the afternoon, when the Creek-side light turns the coral walls gold.
- **Dress:** cover shoulders and knees. It's a heritage area next to working mosques, including the Grand Mosque in Bur Dubai.
- **Photos:** the walls and towers are fair game. Ask before photographing people, especially women.
- **Fridays:** things slow down around midday prayers. Plan café stops and museum visits around them.
- **Paying:** cafés and galleries usually take cards. Abras are cash-only, so carry small dirhams for the crossing.
- **Ask questions:** at SMCCU, questions about religion, dress and local customs are welcome. That's the point of the place.

Finish in a courtyard café with Arabic coffee and dates. Then walk to the abra station at dusk, when the boats cross back and forth with Deira's lights coming on across the water.
