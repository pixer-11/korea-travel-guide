## Where it is

The address, Rua do Mercado de Iao Hon, puts the market in the Iao Hon neighbourhood of the northern Macau peninsula. Pair it with other sights on the northern peninsula.

In Cantonese the market is 祐漢街市, Iao Hon gaai si. Showing those characters to a taxi driver works better than the Portuguese street name.

## What's inside a Macau municipal market

A municipal market here is a government-run hall of rented stalls, and its job is feeding the neighbourhood. Expect the standard sections:

- **Wet goods:** fishmongers with tanks and ice beds, butchers working at open counters
- **Produce:** leafy Cantonese greens, root vegetables, fruit stacked by season
- **Dry goods:** dried seafood, preserved items and household staples at the edges

There are no souvenir stalls and no English menus. Iao Hon is a dense residential district of tall apartment blocks. The people shopping here are buying tonight's dinner, so the market is a direct look at how the north of the city eats.

It is also small and not much reviewed, though shoppers rate it consistently well. A walk round takes 30 to 45 minutes. Tourists rarely find it, so you will mostly have it to yourself.

## Shopping alongside the regulars

The routine is simple: point, then pay. Some practical points:

- **Payment:** carry cash. Patacas and Hong Kong dollars both circulate across Macau; small stalls may not take cards, so ask before choosing anything.
- **Prices:** look for handwritten signs on the produce. Where there is no sign, ask the stallholder before anything is weighed.
- **Language:** Cantonese is the working language. Numbers on a phone screen get past most gaps.
- **Photos:** stallholders are working. Ask with a gesture before you photograph faces, and keep out of the aisle.
- **Floors:** wet sections are hosed down, so wear shoes you don't mind splashing.

Don't handle the fish or meat yourself. The stallholder picks it out, weighs it and bags it for you.

## When to come and what's nearby

The doors open at 7am and close at 8pm every day of the week. On weekdays the market is quiet from opening to closing, so any hour suits you.

Weekends are different. Come between 7am and 9am, when stock is fresh and the aisles are clear. Avoid 10am to 12pm on Saturdays and Sundays, because that is the peak and the narrow aisles fill with shopping trolleys.

Early is still the best choice for seafood. The best fish usually goes first, whatever the day.

From Iao Hon it is a short walk to the Red Market (Mercado Almirante Lacerda), a larger and much-photographed Art Deco market. Lin Fong Temple is also close. Together they make a half-day loop of the northern peninsula, well away from the crowds around Senado Square.

Check current bus routes before you go. Routes in the north change, and a taxi is the simplest option.
