## Soft guitars in an airport hall

A wave to earth show is a quiet thing in a very large room. The South Korean trio plays hushed, jazz-tinted indie rock: clean chorused guitar, a soft and close vocal, a rhythm section that holds back. On November 18, 2026, that sound fills AsiaWorld-Expo Hall 10 in Hong Kong as part of The Pieces Tour.

The band calls this its largest tour to date. That fits a group whose songs, "seasons" and "bad" especially, travelled far beyond Korea through streaming and short-video clips. A big share of the audience found the band that way, and they tend to sing along softly rather than shout.

## Hall 10, at the end of the line

AsiaWorld-Expo sits on reclaimed land at Chek Lap Kok, right beside Hong Kong International Airport. It is the city's big exhibition and concert complex: a chain of large, column-free halls joined by long indoor corridors. Hall 10 is one of those exhibition halls, fitted out with a stage for concerts.

Expect a flat floor and a high ceiling. The industrial lighting rig hangs overhead. It feels nothing like a cosy club, so the band's gentle dynamics depend on the PA and on the crowd keeping quiet between songs. Fans who know the catalogue usually do.

Here is what the location means in practice:

- You are about 30 kilometres from Central, not downtown.
- Planes on approach are part of the view outside.
- There are few restaurants inside the complex itself, so most people eat before arriving or at the airport.

## The Airport Express run

Getting there is the part of this gig that's unique to Hong Kong. The MTR Airport Express line ends at AsiaWorld-Expo Station, one stop beyond Airport Station. You step off the train and walk straight into the exhibition complex. The ride from Hong Kong Station in Central takes roughly half an hour, with stops at Kowloon, Tsing Yi and the airport.

Other ways in:

- **Tung Chung Line** to Tung Chung, then a taxi or local bus across to the airport island. It takes longer but suits anyone staying on Lantau.
- **Taxi** from Kowloon or the Island. It's direct, but the trip is long and the fare is metered.
- **Airport buses** ("A" routes) run between the city and the airport area, and some concertgoers combine one with a short hop to the venue.

Pay with an Octopus card or a contactless bank card at the gates. Both work across the MTR, so there's no need to queue at a ticket machine.

## Tickets and the Hong Kong date

The Hong Kong stop of The Pieces Tour is November 18, 2026, at AsiaWorld-Expo Hall 10. The band and its local promoter handle sales through official channels. For a band this popular across Asia, the Hong Kong allocation is usually contested.

Unofficial resale is the main risk with fan-heavy indie tours in Hong Kong. Screenshots of e-tickets and transfers arranged over social media are where fans most often lose money. Seasoned concertgoers stick to the official platform and keep the ticket in the account it was bought on.

## Queues, merch and manners in an AsiaWorld-Expo crowd

Hong Kong gig crowds are orderly, and this one is no exception. Staff mark out queue lanes in the corridors outside the hall. Fans fall into them without much prompting, and the line moves in blocks as entry opens.

What attendees typically do:

- **Merch first.** The merch stand draws the longest line of the night. Fans usually head to it well before doors, because popular sizes go early.
- **Travel light.** Large bags slow the security check, and the hall has little room for them on the floor.
- **Film briefly.** Most of the crowd records a chorus, then lowers the phone. Holding a screen up for a whole quiet song draws looks from people behind you.
- **Keep the quiet bits quiet.** The softest moments of the set depend on it, and the crowd tends to shush talkers.
- **Speak English or Cantonese.** Venue staff handle both, and signage is bilingual.

Water and snacks are easier to sort out at the airport, one stop back, than inside the complex.

## After the encore

When the house lights come up, thousands of people head for the same small station at once. Staff run a holding queue toward the Airport Express platform and let groups through in turn. Fans who live in Kowloon or on the Island usually expect to wait a while before boarding, then ride back with the rest of the crowd.

Some people dodge the crush by staying close. Two hotels sit almost on the doorstep:

- **Hong Kong SkyCity Marriott Hotel**, next to AsiaWorld-Expo
- **Regal Airport Hotel**, linked to the airport terminal

Travellers flying out the next morning often book one of these and walk back after the show. Tung Chung, a short taxi ride away, offers more choice in hotels and serviced apartments.

## Making a Lantau day of it

The venue is far from the city centre, but the surroundings are worth a few hours. Lantau's best-known sights sit within easy reach of Tung Chung, so a concert evening fits neatly after a day out on the island.

- **Ngong Ping 360**: a cable car from Tung Chung up to the Ngong Ping plateau, with long views over the airport and the South China Sea.
- **Tian Tan Buddha and Po Lin Monastery**: the giant bronze Buddha at Ngong Ping, reached by a climb of 268 steps.
- **Tai O**: a fishing village with stilt houses over the water, reached by bus from Tung Chung or Ngong Ping.
- **Citygate Outlets**: the mall above Tung Chung Station, with plenty of places to eat before heading to the hall.

Allow a full afternoon for Ngong Ping. The weather in November is usually dry and mild, which makes this a comfortable month to be up on the plateau.
