## Getting to the show and home on the MTR

Most Hong Kong concert venues are reached by MTR, and the rules are simple:

- **Pay with an Octopus card** or a contactless bank card at the gates. Tap in and tap out. The Octopus also works in convenience stores and many food stalls near venues.
- **Don't eat or drink** in paid areas of the MTR or on trains. Station staff enforce this, and that includes the drink you're finishing after the gig.
- **Stand right, walk left** on escalators, and keep to the queue lines painted on the platform.
- **Check last-train times** for your line in the MTR app before the show. Lines stop running shortly after midnight, and a Tuesday encore can run close to that.

Taxis are colour-coded by area. Red ones cover most of the city, green ones the New Territories and blue ones Lantau. Have your hotel's address written in Chinese to show the driver. Ride-hailing apps work too, but surge pricing after a big show is common.

## December nights in the city

Early December is one of Hong Kong's most comfortable months: dry, clear and cool after dark. A light layer covers the walk home. Air-conditioning inside venues can be strong, so a hoodie earns its place.

On a Tuesday night, eat before the doors open. Harbourside neighbourhoods like Tsim Sha Tsui and Wan Chai have cha chaan teng diners serving milk tea and pork-chop buns into the evening. Christmas lights usually go up along the Victoria Harbour waterfront in late November. The Tsim Sha Tsui Promenade is an easy walk after the show if your route home passes through Kowloon.

Flying in for the gig? Book a hotel near an MTR interchange such as Admiralty, Central or Mong Kok. From there you can reach most of the city without changing trains more than once.
