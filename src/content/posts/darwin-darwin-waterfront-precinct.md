## Swimming without the stingers

Darwin's harbour is off-limits for a casual dip. Box jellyfish and saltwater crocodiles make the open sea a bad idea, and that is why the Waterfront gets so much use. The precinct gives you two safe places to get wet, sitting side by side.

- **Recreation Lagoon:** a sandy beach and enclosed swimming area, netted off from the harbour. It is open to the public, with lawn and shade along its edge.
- **Wave Lagoon:** a separate pool with machine-made waves, run as a paid, supervised facility. Session times and entry prices are posted at the gate and on the official Waterfront site.

Families take the lawns around the Recreation Lagoon early. Bring a hat. Tropical sun reflects hard off the water and the pale paving.

## Eating along the promenade

The restaurants and bars line a pedestrian boardwalk. Most of the terraces face the lagoons or the harbour. The strip runs from casual counters to sit-down dining, and many terraces use fans and misters to deal with the humidity. Sunset over the harbour is the evening draw, so terrace tables go first.

A few minutes' walk south, **Stokes Hill Wharf** has its own cluster of casual food outlets on a working pier. Right next to it is the **Royal Flying Doctor Service Tourist Facility**, which covers the 1942 Bombing of Darwin and outback aeromedical history. The **WWII Oil Storage Tunnels**, dug into the cliff below the city, are close by on Kitchener Drive.

## Getting down from the city

The precinct sits below the CBD escarpment at 19B Kitchener Dr. Most visitors walk in from Smith Street, where a lift and an elevated **Sky Bridge** carry you over the road and down to the waterfront. That saves you the steep climb, which is welcome in wet-season heat. Plan on about ten minutes on foot from Smith Street Mall.

Driving is simple, and there is parking along Kitchener Drive and around the Darwin Convention Centre, which anchors the precinct's eastern side. Half a day covers a swim, a meal and the wharf. Add the tunnels or the Flying Doctor museum and you have a full day.

## Beating the weekend rush

Weekends run busiest from 11am to 11pm, so that stretch covers lunch, the afternoon swim and dinner. If a weekend is your only option, get there before 11am and claim lagoon-side shade before the lawns fill.

- **Weekdays** are the better choice for a relaxed swim and a table on the promenade.
- **Dry season (May–October)** brings low humidity and clear sunsets. It is also peak tourist season in Darwin.
- **Wet season (November–April)** is hot and heavy with afternoon storms. Swimming areas can close during lightning, so watch for signage at the lagoon.
- **Paying:** card and tap payment are standard across the precinct's venues. Tipping is optional in Australia and nobody expects it.

Lifeguards patrol the swimming areas, so follow their flags and instructions. A Waterfront swim is still a stinger-free swim, and that makes it a rare thing in Top End salt water.
