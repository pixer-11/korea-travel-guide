## Three nights, one round room

Janice Vidal brings "Out of Frame" home to Hong Kong Coliseum for three nights. The run covers Saturday 14, Sunday 15 and Tuesday 17 November 2026. Monday the 16th is a rest night.

The tour celebrates two decades since her mid-2000s debut. That period made her one of Cantopop's most recognisable voices, with ballads like "就算世界無童話" that Hong Kong audiences tend to know word for word.

Weekend shows usually pull more out-of-town fans. The Tuesday date tends to be the commuter crowd, people heading straight from the office.

## Inside Hong Kong Coliseum

The Coliseum is the inverted concrete pyramid sitting on top of the Hung Hom transport hub. It opened in 1983 and has hosted Cantopop's big residencies ever since. It holds roughly 12,500 people, and concerts usually use a central stage.

That centre stage shapes your night in a few ways:

- Every block faces the middle, so there is no true "back row".
- Upper tiers look down steeply, which gives a clear view of stage choreography and lighting rigs.
- Singers usually turn through all four sides during a set, so even side seats get face time.

## Hung Hom by rail and on foot

Hung Hom Station is on the MTR East Rail Line and Tuen Ma Line. A covered footbridge from the station concourse leads straight to the Coliseum's entrance level, so the walk stays dry even in a November shower.

From Hong Kong Island, the East Rail Line links Admiralty to Hung Hom in one stop. From Tsim Sha Tsui and East Tsim Sha Tsui stations, people often walk the promenade-side route east instead. Hong Kong Polytechnic University sits just across the road, which helps you get your bearings.

Before the show, many attendees eat in Hung Hom's side streets or at Whampoa, one stop away on the Tuen Ma Line. Its dining streets are full of Cantonese cha chaan teng and noodle shops.

## Singing along, Coliseum style

The crowd has its own rhythm here. Hong Kong concert audiences sing the choruses of the big ballads in Cantonese, loudly, and light sticks or phone torches go up for the slow numbers. If you don't know the lyrics, swaying along is fine. Nobody expects a visitor to know every word.

- **Encores:** Cantopop shows customarily run multiple encores. Fans stay put through the chanting of the singer's name until the house lights come fully up.
- **Cameras:** phone photos are common. Professional cameras and long lenses are generally barred at Coliseum concerts.
- **Paying for travel:** most locals tap an Octopus card or a contactless bank card at MTR gates. That's quicker than queueing at ticket machines when 12,000 people leave at once.
- **Leaving:** the footbridge to Hung Hom Station fills instantly after the final bow. Many fans wait ten minutes in the concourse, or walk to Whampoa or Tsim Sha Tsui East to catch a less crowded train.

Cross-harbour buses also stop at the Hung Hom tunnel toll plaza nearby. They're a useful option for the Island once the MTR platforms back up.
