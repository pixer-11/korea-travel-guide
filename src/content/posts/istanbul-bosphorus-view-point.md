Kuzguncuk doesn't announce itself the way Karaköy or Beşiktaş does. This is a low-rise neighbourhood of wooden houses, tucked between the Bosphorus shore and a steep hillside on the Asian side of Üsküdar district, and Bosphorus View Point is a scenic overlook reached by climbing away from the water rather than standing at it.

## Finding Bicanefendi Sokak

The address, No:15 on Bicanefendi Sokak, sits above the neighbourhood's main strip. From the Kuzguncuk waterfront or İcadiye Caddesi, the street climbs steadily inland and uphill.

Expect narrow pavement, parked cars, and residential houses on either side. There's no signage pointing tourists in from the main road, which is part of why it stays quiet.

- Walk up from İcadiye Caddesi, the neighbourhood's central shopping street
- Follow Bicanefendi Sokak as it gains elevation
- Look for the point where the street opens toward the water

## What you actually see

The draw here is the angle: houses and gardens framing a slice of the Bosphorus strait, with the European shore and the strait traffic of tankers, ferries, and fishing boats visible below. It's a residential-street view, not a purpose-built platform with railings and information boards, so keep expectations calibrated to that. You're looking down through a gap in the hillside neighbourhood rather than standing on an open terrace.

The rating, 4.7 from a modest 145 reviews, tells you this is a spot appreciated by the people who make the effort to find it, not a bus-tour stop. Under-the-radar is the right description for now.

## Best time to catch the light

Late afternoon works well here because the sun drops toward the European side across the water, backlighting the ferries and giving the strait a warmer color than the flat light of midday. Sunset itself, weather depending, throws that same light straight down the water toward Üsküdar and the old city skyline in the far distance.

Morning is fine if you want the neighbourhood quiet and the light cool and clear rather than golden. Either way, this isn't a spot with posted hours or a ticket gate, so timing is about light and neighbourhood atmosphere, not admission.

## Wandering Kuzguncuk before or after

Kuzguncuk rewards a slow approach. It's known for a mix of Ottoman-era wooden houses, small synagogues, churches, and mosques standing close together, a legacy of the neighbourhood's mixed Greek, Armenian, Jewish, and Turkish community over the last century.

Walk İcadiye Caddesi for small cafes, a produce market, and antique and bric-a-brac shops before or after the climb up Bicanefendi Sokak. It's a compact enough neighbourhood to cover on foot in under an hour, on top of whatever time you spend at the viewpoint itself.

## Getting there without a car

Kuzguncuk is most easily reached by ferry or bus from central Istanbul rather than by driving; parking on these narrow residential streets is tight and mostly reserved for residents. The nearest reliable options are:

- Ferry to Üsküdar, then a bus or taxi/rideshare the short distance north to Kuzguncuk
- Metrobus or city bus services running along the Asian shore, with a stop near Kuzguncuk
- Taxi or rideshare directly to İcadiye Caddesi, then walk uphill from there

Because this is a residential street with private homes, keep noise down and stick to the public right-of-way rather than lingering directly outside someone's gate. There's no ticket booth, staff, or facilities on site, so bring water and use cafes on İcadiye Caddesi for a break.

## Pairing it with the rest of Üsküdar

Bosphorus View Point works well as one stop on a longer Asian-side afternoon. Üsküdar's waterfront, with the Maiden's Tower visible offshore, is a short onward trip south along the coast from Kuzguncuk.

Because the viewpoint has no set hours or entry process, it slots easily either at the start of a Kuzguncuk visit, before the streets fill with afternoon shoppers, or at the end, timed to the sunset light over the strait.
