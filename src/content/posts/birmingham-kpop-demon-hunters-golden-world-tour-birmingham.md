## A cartoon girl group, brought to a real stage

The film started this tour. KPop Demon Hunters is the Netflix animated feature about HUNTR/X, a K-pop trio of Rumi, Mira and Zoey who fight demons with their voices. The Golden World Tour turns that story into a live concert experience, and its Birmingham stop is at Utilita Arena Birmingham in 2026.

The name comes from "Golden", the film's breakout song. Its soundtrack also includes "How It's Done", "Soda Pop" and "Your Idol", the last two sung by the rival demon boy band, the Saja Boys. The organiser publishes the date, ticket links and the show's own description on its official site.

## The arena on the canal

Utilita Arena Birmingham is a big indoor bowl on King Edward's Road, on the western edge of the city centre. It opened in 1991 as the National Indoor Arena, and plenty of Brummies still call it the NIA. The NEC Group runs it, along with the city's other large venues.

Inside, it works like most UK arenas:

- **Standing floor** in front of the stage
- **Tiered seating** wrapping around three sides
- **Concourses** behind the seats with bars, food counters and merchandise stands

The back of the building faces the Birmingham Canal Navigations. A short footbridge links it to the bars and restaurants of Brindleyplace, so many people arrive along the water rather than from the road.

## Getting to King Edward's Road

Almost everyone walks the last stretch. From **Birmingham New Street** station, the route goes through Victoria Square and Centenary Square, then across Broad Street and down to the canal at Brindleyplace. It takes roughly 15–20 minutes at an unhurried pace.

Other options:

- **West Midlands Metro tram** to the **Library** stop in Centenary Square, then about a 10-minute walk.
- **Five Ways** station on the Cross-City line, around 15 minutes on foot.
- **Moor Street** and **Snow Hill** stations, a longer walk but useful for Chiltern and some local services.

The arena has car parks of its own. Broad Street and the roads around it slow to a crawl when a full house leaves, so drivers often take a while to get out. Trains from New Street fill up after big shows as well.

## Doors, merch lines and the walk out

The rhythm of an arena night here is predictable. Fans typically arrive an hour or so before doors. The merchandise stands usually have the longest queues just after doors open and again in the interval or straight after the encore. Standing-floor ticket holders tend to queue first, because the spot you end up in depends on when you get in.

A few things that usually smooth the night:

- **Tickets** are mostly mobile at UK arenas, so a charged phone matters more than a printout.
- **Payment** is by contactless card or phone almost everywhere in central Birmingham, the arena concourses included.
- **Bags** go through a security search at the entrance. UK arenas usually set size limits, and the venue's website gives the current policy.
- **Age rules** for under-16s are set by the venue and the ticket type, and they are printed with the ticket terms.

This film has a big following among children and teenagers, so expect plenty of families. Some fans dress as their favourite character, which suits a story about stage outfits. When the show ends, the canal-side exit towards Brindleyplace gets busy fast. Groups usually agree on a meeting point, such as the footbridge or Centenary Square, before the lights go up.

## Before and after on the waterside

The arena sits in one of the busiest eating areas of the city centre. **Brindleyplace**, just over the footbridge, has a cluster of chain and independent restaurants around its squares. **Broad Street** has the bars and late-opening places.

Short walks from the arena:

- **Gas Street Basin**, where narrowboats tie up among old canal warehouses, about 10 minutes along the towpath.
- **The Mailbox**, a former Royal Mail sorting office now full of shops and restaurants, further along the same canal.
- **Library of Birmingham**, the building wrapped in metal rings on Centenary Square, with a free roof garden.
- **Symphony Hall** and **The ICC**, directly across the canal.

## Making a day of Birmingham

Fans travelling from further away often make a weekend of it. Hotels cluster around Broad Street, Brindleyplace and the streets near New Street, all close enough to walk back after the show.

During the day, the **Jewellery Quarter** is a 20-minute walk north. You'll find working workshops and the Museum of the Jewellery Quarter there. **Birmingham Museum and Art Gallery** on Chamberlain Square is known for its Pre-Raphaelite collection. South of the centre, the **Balti Triangle** around Ladypool Road is where the city's own style of curry grew up. Eating there makes a better Birmingham dinner than a quick bite near the arena.
