## Six days of singles in November

A table tennis rally at this level lasts a few seconds. The ball crosses the net faster than most spectators can follow, and the sound carries all the way up the stands. That sound, a hollow click followed by the squeak of shoes on the court floor, is what you get at WTT Champions Germany in Frankfurt, from Tuesday, November 3 to Sunday, November 8, 2026.

Champions is one of the top tiers of the World Table Tennis circuit, below the Grand Smashes. The draws are small and filled by world ranking, so the early rounds already put elite players against each other. Nobody here is coming through a long qualifying run.

## Sessions, tables and tickets

World Table Tennis publishes the venue, the daily order of play and the ticket links on its official site, worldtabletennis.com. The facts we have do not name the arena, so this guide leaves it to the organiser.

WTT events usually follow the same pattern across the week:

- **Early days:** several matches run on separate tables at once, so you can watch one rally and then turn to another.
- **Later rounds:** play narrows to a single show court with full lighting and walk-on music.
- **Final day:** fewer matches and the most expensive seats. Sunday, November 8 is the day most people want.

Fans who mainly want to see a lot of players usually buy midweek sessions. Fans who want the tension of a title match usually go for the weekend.

## Getting into Frankfurt

Frankfurt is one of the easiest cities in Europe to reach. Frankfurt Airport (FRA) is among the continent's largest hubs. S-Bahn lines S8 and S9 run from the airport's regional station to Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof in about a quarter of an hour.

The Hauptbahnhof is also a major ICE high-speed stop. Direct trains come in from Cologne, Munich, Berlin and Amsterdam. Within the city, the RMV network covers the S-Bahn, U-Bahn, trams and buses on a single ticket system. Visitors often buy a day ticket in the DB Navigator or RMV app instead of using the station machines.

Many visitors stay near the Hauptbahnhof or around the Messe for the transport links. Others pick Sachsenhausen, across the Main, for its quieter streets.

## Courtside manners between points

Table tennis crowds have their own rhythm. Getting it wrong gets you looked at.

- **Silence during the rally.** Talking, rustling or calling out stops while the ball is in play. You make noise when the point is over, and German crowds clap hard between points.
- **Move between games.** Ushers usually hold people at the aisle until a game ends, and the players change ends. Fans going for drinks plan their trips around this.
- **No flash.** Players read the ball against the background, and a flash during a serve can stop play.
- **Paying.** Card and mobile payment are widely accepted in German arenas, but small cash still helps at some food stands and in older Frankfurt bars.

Most spectators learn the count quickly. Games go to 11 points, players switch serve every two points, and the umpire calls the score in English.

## Between sessions in Frankfurt

Frankfurt's main sights are close together, so an afternoon between sessions goes a long way.

- **Römerberg:** the old town square with the reconstructed half-timbered row and the Römer town hall. Frankfurt Cathedral is a short walk away.
- **Kleinmarkthalle:** a covered food market where office workers stand at the counters eating sausages and Hessian green sauce.
- **Museumsufer:** a line of museums on the Sachsenhausen bank. The Städel holds the city's big art collection.
- **Main Tower:** the skyline viewing platform. Its hours change with the season, so they are on the tower's official site.

At night, people head to the Sachsenhausen taverns for *Ebbelwoi*, the local apple wine. It comes in a ribbed glass called a *Gerippter*, poured from a grey stoneware jug, the *Bembel*.

## What early November is like

Frankfurt in the first week of November is cool and often grey. It gets dark in the late afternoon, so evening sessions start in the dark. Fans generally bring a warm layer for the walk from the transit stop to the arena.

The tournament falls between two busy seasons. The large autumn trade fairs are mostly over, and the Römerberg Christmas market usually opens in the second half of the month. That timing tends to make the week calmer than the city's peak periods.
