## Getting there and getting in

The MFA sits at 465 Huntington Ave, on the edge of the Fenway neighborhood, close to Boston's Longwood Medical Area. The Museum of Fine Arts stop on the E branch of the Green Line drops you at the front steps. Parking is limited and pricier than a taxi or rideshare on a weekend; the museum runs its own garage off Museum Road if you'd rather drive.

Closed Tuesdays. Monday, Wednesday, Saturday, and Sunday run 10am to 5pm; Thursday and Friday stretch to 10pm. Those two late evenings are the easiest time to see the galleries without a crowd pressing in behind you.

## What's inside and how long to spend

The collection spans ancient Egyptian funerary art through contemporary American painting, split across a neoclassical original building and a glass-walled modern wing (the Art of the Americas Wing, opened 2010). Highlights include Egyptian mummies and tomb goods, John Singer Sargent murals in the rotunda, and a strong run of Monet paintings, one of the largest Monet holdings outside France.

- Egyptian galleries: mummies, sarcophagi, tomb reliefs
- Art of the Americas Wing: Copley, Sargent, Homer
- Impressionist rooms: Monet, Degas, Renoir
- Contemporary wing: rotating installations

Budget two to three hours for a focused pass through two or three wings; a full walk of everything easily fills half a day.

## Skipping the weekend crush

The MFA is a very popular stop on any Boston itinerary, not a quiet local secret, so plan around the crowds rather than expecting to dodge them entirely. Weekends between 11am and 5pm draw the heaviest traffic, especially in the Egyptian and Impressionist galleries.

Arrive right at 10am on a Saturday or Sunday to get thirty minutes ahead of the bulk of visitors. Better still, use one of the Thursday or Friday nights until 10pm, when school groups and tour buses have gone home.

Tickets are timed-entry online in busier seasons; buying ahead saves a line at the door. Members and Massachusetts residents get separate rate structures, so check the museum's own site for current pricing before you go, since admission costs shift year to year.

## Eating, resting, and nearby stops

The museum has a sit-down restaurant and a faster café inside, both useful if you're doing a half-day visit and don't want to lose your place in line by leaving. Bags are checked at the entrance, and large backpacks aren't allowed into galleries.

Symphony Hall is a ten-minute walk north, and the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum sits directly across the street, an easy pairing if you're making a full afternoon of Boston's museum row on Huntington Ave.
