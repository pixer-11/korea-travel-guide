## A white hull at the harbour mouth

The museum stands right on the water. A pale, angular building sits at the southern tip of the Macau peninsula, where the Inner Harbour opens out. Its stepped roofline is often compared to the sails of a ship. Across the cobbled square, incense drifts from A-Ma Temple, the shrine that gave Macau its name.

Inside, the subject is narrow and deep: the fishing communities of the South China coast and the sea routes that made Macau. It has been here a long time. It also sits slightly off the main tourist circuit, so the galleries are often far calmer than the temple courtyard a minute's walk away.

## Which Fisherman's Wharf?

Clear up one thing before you set off. Map apps and listings sometimes file this museum under Fisherman's Wharf. That name belongs to the themed waterfront complex in the Outer Harbour, near the ferry terminal and the NAPE casino strip.

The museum is nowhere near it. Its address is 1 Largo do Pagode da Barra, in the Barra district on the opposite side of the peninsula. Send a taxi driver to "Fisherman's Wharf" and you will end up on the wrong coast.

Ask instead for the Maritime Museum by A-Ma Temple. The Chinese name of the temple, Ma Kok Miu, is the landmark every driver knows.

## Three floors of junks, nets and navigators

The galleries rise floor by floor, and each one has its own theme. Here is roughly what you will find going up:

- **Ground floor:** the fishing life of the South China coast. Expect dioramas of boat-dwelling families, fishing gear and the festivals tied to the sea.
- **Upper galleries:** the age of maritime trade and exploration, both Portuguese and Chinese, and Macau's place on those routes.
- **Technology and ship models:** detailed scale junks and other traditional craft, plus navigation instruments. These reward slow looking.
- **Aquarium annex:** a small section of tanks, popular with children.

Labels come in Chinese, Portuguese and English. Most visitors take 60 to 90 minutes. Ship-model enthusiasts can easily stay longer.

## The goddess across the square

The museum's position is deliberate. A-Ma is the sea goddess of southern Chinese fishermen. According to local tradition, Portuguese sailors landing here heard the place called A-Ma-Gao, the bay of A-Ma, and that became "Macau".

Many of the ground-floor displays explain what you then see in the temple: why boat people prayed to A-Ma, and how her festival fills the bay with vessels. See the museum first and the temple makes far more sense. The temple's rock-carved inscriptions and stacked pavilions stop being scenery and become a working shrine for a seafaring community.

## Reaching Barra

Barra is the most southerly point of the old peninsula, and it is easy to reach.

- **Light Rail:** Barra Station on the Macau LRT links across the Sai Van Bridge to Taipa. It puts you within a short walk of the square.
- **Bus:** many city routes terminate at or pass through the Barra / A-Ma Temple stops. Check the route board at your stop or the official transport app.
- **On foot:** from Senado Square, allow about 25 to 30 minutes. The walk takes you south through the old streets past Lilau Square and the Mandarin's House.
- **Taxi:** say "Ma Kok Miu" and you will be dropped at the square.

## Tuesdays, tickets and the temple tide

A few habits make the visit smoother.

**Mind the Tuesday closure.** The museum opens 10am to 6pm on Monday and from Wednesday through Sunday. It is shut all day Tuesday. That catches out visitors who assume, as in much of Europe, that Monday is museum day off. Here, Monday is fine.

**Check the price at the door.** Admission is set by the Macau government and can change. The current rate is posted at the ticket desk and on the official museum website.

**Sequence it against the temple.** A-Ma Temple draws tour groups through the day, and the square fills with them. Arriving at the museum close to 10am lets you see the galleries first. The temple tends to quieten towards late afternoon, so it makes a natural second stop.

**Keep it low-key inside.** These are quiet galleries, more school-trip than blockbuster. Speak softly and follow any no-photo signs on loaned items. Large bags may need to go to the counter.

**Leave time before 6pm.** The last hour slips by quickly once you reach the ship models. Treat 5pm as your latest comfortable arrival.

## Walking on from the waterfront

Barra is the start of one of Macau's best walks. Several of the old city's quieter World Heritage sites sit within 15 minutes on foot:

- **A-Ma Temple:** directly across the square, the oldest temple in the city.
- **Moorish Barracks:** a short climb up Calçada da Barra, with yellow arcades designed for Indian police troops.
- **Lilau Square:** a small, shaded plaza once famous for its spring water. It sits in the old Portuguese residential quarter.
- **Mandarin's House:** the sprawling courtyard home of the reformer Zheng Guanying, just off Lilau Square.
- **Penha Hill:** a steeper walk up to the hilltop chapel, with views over the Inner Harbour and towards the bridges.

Do them in that order and the route climbs gently away from the water. It then winds back towards Senado Square by evening. The museum makes a good first stop: it gives you the backstory for everything else on the walk.
