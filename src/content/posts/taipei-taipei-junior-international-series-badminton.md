## Courtside etiquette at a junior event

A badminton hall has its own rules, and at a junior event the players are teenagers playing for ranking points. A few habits will keep you on good terms with everyone there:

- **Stay still during rallies.** People only walk along the stands or change seats between points. Officials also keep doors shut and air flow steady, because a draught moves the shuttle.
- **No flash.** Flash photography is banned on court. Phones are fine without flash.
- **Clap after the point, not during it.** National team benches cheer loudly in chants. Neutral spectators usually just applaud the rally.
- **Leave coaching corners clear.** Coaches sit right behind the court, so the seats next to them are not spectator spots.

Taiwanese venues are tidy places. People carry their rubbish out with them because public bins are scarce. Convenience stores near any sports hall sell drinks and onigiri rice balls for long session days.

## Getting around Taipei in December

Taipei is cool and damp in December. Daytime temperatures usually sit in the mid-teens Celsius, and drizzle is common. Sports halls are often cooler inside than visitors expect, so many spectators bring a light layer.

The MRT reaches most of the city's sports venues. An EasyCard covers the metro, buses and YouBike shared bicycles, and you can also use it to pay at 7-Eleven and FamilyMart. Taxis are easy to flag down and cheap by regional standards, which helps after evening sessions.

Many visitors stay around Zhongshan or Ximending for the food and the direct MRT links. On a free day before the finals, a few things fit between sessions:

- Raohe Street Night Market
- Longshan Temple
- The ride up to Taipei 101
