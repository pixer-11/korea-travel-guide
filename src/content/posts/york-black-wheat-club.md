## Fossgate, not the Shambles

Fossgate is where York's restaurant scene actually lives. The street runs downhill from Pavement towards the bridge over the River Foss, then carries on as Walmgate. Number 52 sits among a run of independent bars, cafés and restaurants. The crowds pressing through the Shambles and Stonegate thin out quickly once you cross here.

Black Wheat Club is consistently well rated by the people who find it. It still flies under the radar compared with the city's better-known dining rooms. The name echoes *blé noir*, the Breton word for buckwheat. Check the current menu rather than assuming what that means on the plate.

## Two services, two different visits

The schedule decides your visit. Daytime and evening are separate sittings, and the evening one runs only three nights a week.

- **Monday, Wednesday, Sunday:** 9am to 3pm only
- **Tuesday:** closed all day
- **Thursday and Friday:** 9am to 3pm, then 6pm to 11pm
- **Saturday:** 9am to 4pm, then 6pm to 11pm

Saturday's daytime service runs an hour longer than the other days, so it suits a late lunch. If you want dinner, you need to be in York from Thursday to Saturday. Arriving on a Monday or Wednesday evening means a locked door.

## Getting to 52 Fossgate

York railway station is roughly a 15 to 20 minute walk. Cross Lendal Bridge or Ouse Bridge, then head east through the centre to Pavement and turn down Fossgate. From York Minster it is about ten minutes on foot via Goodramgate or Colliergate.

The city centre is compact and largely pedestrianised, so drivers do better at one of the edge-of-centre car parks or the Park & Ride. Pair the meal with places on your way:

- **Merchant Adventurers' Hall:** the medieval guildhall, entered from Fossgate itself
- **The Shambles and Shambles Market:** a five-minute walk uphill
- **Clifford's Tower:** about ten minutes south-west
- **Walmgate Bar:** the walled gate at the far end of Walmgate

## Eating on Fossgate without the guesswork

Book the evening sittings. With dinner limited to three nights, Thursday to Saturday tables go first, and weekend visitors to York compete for the same slots. For daytime, turning up at the 9am opening is the reasoning choice: you beat both the midday lunch rush and the coach groups heading for the Minster.

Card and contactless payments are standard across York's restaurants, and plenty of places are card-only. Tipping around ten percent is customary at table service. First check whether a service charge is already printed on the bill, because many UK restaurants add one.

If you want a drink before dinner, Fossgate's bars sit within a few doors. Plan around the 6pm start, as there is no service between the end of daytime and the evening sitting.

Prices are mid-range for York. The menu is the place to check current costs before you commit.
