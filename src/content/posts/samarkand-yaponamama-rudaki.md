## Sushi in a city of plov

Samarkand feeds you plov, samsa and bread from the tandoor. After a few days of that, Yaponamama Rudaki is a real change of pace. It is a Japanese fusion restaurant where the main draw is the sushi, served in a room with a lot of attention paid to the design.

The look is what people talk about. Woven rattan light fittings cover the ceiling, so the light comes through cane instead of bare bulbs. In a city better known for blue tilework, that soft, woven glow is the reason it shows up in so many photos.

## What Yaponamama Rudaki actually is

Yaponamama is a Japanese fusion brand from Uzbekistan. "Rudaki" in the name identifies this branch, and it points to Rudaki Street, one of the main roads through the modern part of Samarkand. This is the newer, Soviet-era side of the city, a long way in feel from the Timurid monuments, even if the distance is short.

Treat it as a fusion kitchen, not a traditional Japanese one. The sushi has a good reputation. The rest of the menu is best read on the day, not guessed at here. In a short time the place has become one of Samarkand's most fashionable dining rooms, so expect a dressed-up crowd more than backpackers.

## Getting to the Rudaki branch

Most visitors stay near the Registan, the Gur-e-Amir mausoleum or Siab Bazaar. From any of these, a taxi is the easy way to get there. Yandex Go is the ride-hailing app everyone in Samarkand uses, and it saves you agreeing a fare in Russian or Uzbek.

- **From the Registan:** a short taxi ride into the modern centre.
- **From Samarkand railway station:** the Afrosiyob high-speed train from Tashkent arrives here, and a taxi into town is the usual next step.

## Fitting it into a Samarkand day

A good evening plan is to see the Registan at dusk, then take a taxi to dinner. The three madrasas look their best as the light drops. A meal here makes a good contrast with the fifteenth-century tilework you've just been looking at.

Allow around an hour and a half for a proper sit-down meal. Sushi is made to order, and the room suits lingering. If you're travelling for a while in Uzbekistan, this is a good place to give your stomach a rest from all the mutton and oil.

You can also go at lunch, if the hours you check allow it. That would split a long day between Shah-i-Zinda and the Bibi-Khanym Mosque with something lighter than another lagman.
