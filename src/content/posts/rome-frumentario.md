## A slice finished in front of you

Rectangular trays, scissors and paper: that is pizza al taglio. In Rome it has long been the quick lunch you eat standing up. Frumentario belongs to the newer wave of counters that take the format seriously, the movement Gabriele Bonci's Pizzarium near Cipro helped start.

Chef Alessandro Santilli runs the place. The baking happens in an open kitchen, so you watch the dough go in and come out. Here the topping isn't set before the slice reaches the counter. Sauces and toppings go on after you order, so each slice is finished just before you eat it.

## Where Piazza dei Re di Roma sits

The square is in Appio-Latino, a residential district southeast of the historic centre. It sits just outside the Aurelian Walls, along Via Appia Nuova. The Re di Roma station on Metro Line A opens onto the piazza itself, and Frumentario is a short walk from the platform.

From Termini, the ride on Line A takes a few stops. You can fit it around a morning at the Basilica of San Giovanni in Laterano, one stop north on the same line. It's an easy walk back too, through Porta San Giovanni.

Things worth pairing nearby:
- **San Giovanni in Laterano** and the Scala Santa, Rome's cathedral complex
- **Via Appia Nuova**, the long shopping street running through the square
- **Parco della Caffarella**, a stretch of green farmland and ruins to the south, reached by bus or a longer walk

## Ordering at the counter

The trays sit behind glass. You point, and the server cuts. Show the size with your hands or say *"un pezzo così"* (a piece like this). Ask for *"un po' di più"* if you want a bigger piece. Al taglio slices are traditionally sold by weight, and the counter staff will tell you how they charge.

Useful phrases and habits:
- **"Da portare via"** means to take away. **"Qui"** means you'll eat it here.
- Order two or three narrow strips rather than one big square. That's how Romans try several toppings at once.
- Ask what's coming out of the oven next. With bake-to-order trays, a fresh one may be minutes away.
- Tipping isn't expected at a pizza al taglio counter. Just pay the till total.

Space at small Roman counters is usually tight. Plan on eating standing up or on a bench in the piazza. Romans tend to have pizza al taglio at lunchtime or as an afternoon *merenda* (snack), and go to sit-down round-pizza restaurants for dinner.

## Before you make the trip

Frumentario is new, so it may change things faster than older shops do. Hours and days off can still shift, and there's little point crossing the city to a shutter. Check its current schedule online or on its social channels before you head out. Also confirm whether it takes bookings or is walk-in only.

Allow about half an hour for the visit. That covers watching a tray come out, choosing and eating, but not a sit-down meal. If you want a full afternoon, combine it with San Giovanni and a walk along the Aurelian Walls.
