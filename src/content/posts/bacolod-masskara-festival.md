## What's happening and when

MassKara is Bacolod's "Festival of Smiles," a month-long civic celebration built around masked street dancing. The 2026 edition was set for October 1-25, with the street and arena dance competitions, the festival's real spectacle, on October 9-18. The programme put closing ceremonies on October 25.

The mask is the whole point: oversized, painted, permanently grinning, worn by dance troupes in choreographed routines down the main avenues. It began in the 1980s as a deliberate civic answer to hard economic times, a decision to smile through hardship, and that origin still shapes the festival's tone today.

## Where it happens

Bacolod is on Negros Occidental, reachable by direct flights to Bacolod-Silay International Airport from Manila and Cebu. The competitions and street parties center on downtown Bacolod, radiating from the Bacolod Public Plaza and the main thoroughfares used for the dance parade routes.

Arena-style final competitions typically move indoors to a venue with grandstand seating, separate from the free street viewing. Street segments are free to watch from the sidewalks; grandstand or arena seating for the finals usually requires a ticket.

- Bacolod-Silay International Airport (BCD) serves the city
- Downtown hotels are within walking distance of the plaza
- October is the city's highest-demand month for accommodation

## Tickets and what things cost

Street-side viewing along the parade route costs nothing. Reserved grandstand seats for the main dance competitions were sold separately, with prices varying by seating tier and proximity to the judges' area.

Exact ticket prices, on-sale dates and seating maps change from year to year, so figures from earlier editions are no guide to 2026. The official MassKara Festival channels and the Bacolod City government's tourism office handle festival ticketing and timing.

## Making the most of the streets

On dance competition days, the best free sidewalk spots along the parade route typically fill well before the troupes start moving. Vendors sell local specialties like chicken inasal and piaya along the route, and cash is the default payment at street stalls.

Speaker trucks usually travel the length of the route at high volume for hours, which can be hard on anyone sensitive to sound. Dancers rehearse for months, so routines are tight and long; each troupe's segment typically lasts well past a quick photo stop.

Grandstand seats with good sightlines tend to sell out, and walk-up availability is rarely reliable. Traffic rerouting is heavy on competition days, and drivers coming in from outside Bacolod usually park at the edge of downtown and walk the last stretch.
