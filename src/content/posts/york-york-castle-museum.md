## Two prisons on the Eye of York

The setting is part of the story. The museum fills the old Debtors' Prison and Female Prison, which face a circular lawn called the Eye of York, and Clifford's Tower stands on its mound right beside them. The highwayman Dick Turpin was held at York Castle before his execution in 1739, and the museum keeps the condemned cell linked to him.

The address is Tower St, York YO1 9RY. From York railway station it's a flat walk of 15 to 20 minutes, past Micklegate or along the river Ouse. From the Shambles, allow about five minutes heading south.

## Walking down Kirkgate

Kirkgate is the main reason people come. It's a full indoor Victorian street, first opened in 1938 and built around the collection of Dr John Kirk, a country doctor who gathered everyday objects. It has cobbles underfoot, real shopfronts and dim gaslit lighting, and costumed guides stand behind the counters.

Talk to them. They answer in character about prices, trades and daily life, and that's where much of the detail comes from. Elsewhere in the building you'll find:

- **Prison cells**, with stories of real inmates told through projections and voices
- **Period rooms** that show how York homes changed over the centuries
- **Rotating social-history galleries** covering fashion, toys and 20th-century life

Exhibitions change, so check the official York Museums Trust site to see what's on.

## Timing the crowds

Opening hours are 10am–5pm from Tuesday to Sunday. On Mondays it opens an hour later, at 11am. Weekdays are the easiest time to come: the quiet stretch runs all day, from 10am to 5pm.

Weekends are harder. Try not to arrive between 12pm and 4pm on a Saturday or Sunday, because that's when Kirkgate gets packed shoulder to shoulder and the narrow prison corridors back up. The quiet weekend window is 4pm–5pm, which only leaves one hour, and that isn't enough to see everything. If a weekend is your only option, arrive at 10am and do Kirkgate first.

## Getting in and moving through

Book online before you go. York Museums Trust sells timed and day tickets on its official website, and current prices are listed there and at the desk. If you're in York for a few days, look at the Trust's annual membership card. Clifford's Tower is next door but is run separately by English Heritage, so it needs its own ticket.

The way through tends to lead you along a set route. Do Kirkgate early, while you're still fresh, and leave the prison wing for later; its cells are tight and low-lit. The museum takes cards, and there's a café on site if you need a break.

Afterwards, you're a short walk from:

- Clifford's Tower
- Fairfax House, a Georgian townhouse on Castlegate
- Jorvik Viking Centre on Coppergate
