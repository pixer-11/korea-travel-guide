## A Korean band in a Civic District picture palace

On Saturday, November 14, 2026, LUCY bring their 'ISLAND' tour back for an encore at Capitol Theatre. Few rooms in Singapore are better suited to a band built around a violin.

LUCY are a South Korean band who came to wide notice by winning JTBC's audition show *Superband 2*. Their sound puts an electric violin up front, carrying melodies a guitar would carry in most rock bands. Bright, bouncing pop-rock suits a theatre where you can see the bow move from the balcony.

## Who LUCY are, for the newly curious

Plenty of people in the seats come along with a friend and know two songs at most. That's fine. Here is what to expect on stage:

- **The violin as lead voice.** Riffs, hooks and solos often come from the violin instead of a guitar.
- **Band-first arrangements.** LUCY write and play their own material. That is the difference between them and a dance-led idol act.
- **Bright, upbeat pop-rock.** Expect an optimistic, catchy tone. The heavy moments are rare.
- **Singalong choruses.** K-pop and K-band audiences learn the fan chants, and Singapore crowds tend to join in loudly.

"Encore" here means the tour returning for further dates after its main run, with Singapore one of those stops. The organiser sets the full schedule and setlist; this page doesn't guess at either.

## Capitol Theatre: the room itself

Capitol Theatre opened in 1930 as one of Singapore's grandest cinemas. It sits at the corner of Stamford Road and North Bridge Road, inside the Capitol Singapore complex, which also holds the Capitol Kempinski hotel and a row of shops.

After a long restoration it is a seated performance hall. The Art Deco interior has curved balconies and decorative plasterwork, and a domed ceiling looks down on the stalls. By concert standards the room is small, so even the upper seats feel close to the stage.

Being seated changes the night. This is not a standing-pit gig where you fight for the barrier. You have an assigned seat, and the sightlines from the balcony are the reason some fans prefer it to the stalls.

## Getting to Stamford Road

The theatre is one of the easiest venues in the city to reach by train.

- **City Hall MRT** (North South Line NS25 and East West Line EW13) is the nearest station. It's a few minutes' walk, crossing from the Raffles City side.
- **Bras Basah MRT** (Circle Line CC2) is a short walk north along Stamford Road.
- **Esplanade MRT** (Circle Line CC3) also works if you are coming from Marina Bay.
- **Taxis and Grab** can drop on North Bridge Road or Stamford Road. Pick-ups get slow when a full house leaves at once.

November is when Singapore's northeast monsoon begins, and heavy late-afternoon and evening showers are common. Locals carry a compact umbrella out of habit. Covered walkways and the malls around City Hall help you dodge the worst of it.

## Seats, chants and the theatre rules

Concert nights at Capitol run on assigned seating. Fans usually get there well before the doors open, so they can collect any merchandise and find their row without hurrying. Ushers seat latecomers, but sometimes only at a suitable break.

Some habits Singapore K-band crowds tend to share:

- **Learn the chants.** Fans often go through the official fan chants for the bigger songs beforehand. Joining in is welcome.
- **Stay in your seat.** Standing up through a whole song blocks the rows behind you in a raked theatre. Most people get up for the big choruses and the encore, then sit down again.
- **Photography rules belong to the promoter.** Whether phones are allowed is the organiser's call, and the rules are usually posted at the entrance and announced before the show.
- **Pay by card or phone.** Singapore is close to cashless, and contactless cards and mobile wallets work at almost every counter near the venue.

The organiser handles ticket sales, seating plans and entry conditions for this show. Tickets bought from unofficial resellers carry a real risk of being turned away at the door.

## Before and after on the Civic District block

Capitol sits among some of the city's best-known landmarks, so the night doesn't have to start or finish at the theatre doors.

- **CHIJMES**, a former convent with a Gothic chapel, has restaurants and bars around its courtyard a few minutes' walk away.
- **Raffles City**, just across the junction, has a basement food court and quick-service options for an easy dinner.
- **Funan**, a short walk west toward the river, has more casual places to eat.
- **St Andrew's Cathedral** and the **National Gallery Singapore** are close by, and both look good lit up after dark.
- **The Padang** and **Esplanade waterfront** are a pleasant stroll south once the crowd has dispersed.

For a late bite after the show, many people walk toward Bras Basah or across to Raffles City instead of crowding the hotel bars.

## Staying nearby

The Civic District puts you close to the theatre and to the airport line. Visitors coming in for the show usually stay around City Hall, Bras Basah or Marina Bay, where walking back after the encore beats any taxi queue.

Capitol Kempinski is in the same complex. Bugis, two stops up the East West Line, is popular with fans travelling on a tighter budget. Either way, the MRT network ties you into Changi Airport for the trip home.
