## A spring the city was built around

Largo do Lilau is small enough to cross in twenty steps. Pastel Portuguese-era houses frame it, and big trees shade the paving. The centrepiece is the Lilau spring, now a fountain set into a wall. For centuries it was one of the main sources of fresh water on the peninsula.

This was one of the first neighbourhoods Portuguese settlers lived in. The square sits inside the Historic Centre of Macao, inscribed by UNESCO in 2005. There's no gate or ticket booth, and the plaza is open 24 hours. Most visitors walk past on their way to A-Ma Temple, so it usually feels much quieter than Senado Square.

## The saying behind the water

Macanese Portuguese has a rhyme about this spring: "Quem bebe água do Lilau não esquece Macau." Whoever drinks Lilau water never forgets Macau. The longer version says they'll either marry here or come back. That line is why people stop at the fountain, and it is the square's whole story in one sentence.

Don't drink from the spout. Treat it as a historic fountain, not a tap, and take the rhyme as the souvenir.

## Lingering without blocking the lane

The square is a residential corner, not a stage set. People live in the surrounding houses, and the lanes that feed into it are narrow. Here's how to fit in:

- **Photograph from the edges.** Leave the doorways and the lane mouths clear, especially for scooters coming down from Rua de São Lourenço.
- **Keep your voice down after dark.** The square never closes, but evenings here are domestic, with no nightlife.
- **Sit on the benches, not the steps** of private houses.
- **Go early for empty frames.** Tour groups bound for A-Ma Temple tend to arrive later in the day, so morning light gives you the facades with fewer people in them.
- **Check hours for neighbouring sites.** The square itself is always open, but the Mandarin's House keeps its own schedule and closes on some days. Confirm on the Macao government heritage website before you plan around it.

## Walking the Barra loop

Lilau works best as one stop on a downhill walk through Barra.

- **Mandarin's House (Casa do Mandarim):** around the corner on Travessa de António da Silva. This large courtyard residence was the home of the reformist writer Zheng Guanying. Allow 45 minutes.
- **A-Ma Temple:** a few minutes' walk down toward the waterfront. It's the temple the name Macau is said to come from.
- **Moorish Barracks:** on Calçada da Barra, a colonnaded building in yellow and white, between the square and the temple.
- **St. Lawrence's Church:** uphill on Rua de São Lourenço, a good starting point if you're coming from the centre.

From Senado Square, the walk takes about 20 minutes heading south through the old lanes. If you're coming from Taipa, take the Light Rail Transit (LRT) to Barra Station, beside A-Ma Temple, and walk uphill. That route reverses the loop, so you finish at Lilau rather than start there.
