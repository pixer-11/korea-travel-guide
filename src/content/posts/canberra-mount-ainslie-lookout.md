## The line the city was drawn on

From the top, Canberra makes sense. Below the paved deck, Anzac Parade runs dead straight from the copper dome of the Australian War Memorial to Lake Burley Griffin. Behind the lake sit Old Parliament House and, last, Parliament House with its flagmast.

This is the land axis Walter Burley Griffin planned the capital around, and Mount Ainslie was one of its anchor points. Nowhere else shows the design this clearly.

Information plaques around the deck name the landmarks you are looking at. Swing west and you will find Black Mountain with its telecommunications tower. To the east, planes land and take off at Canberra Airport. The summit stands at 843 metres, high enough to see the whole Molonglo valley and the ranges past it.

## Getting to the top

You have two options, and they make for very different visits.

- **By car:** Mount Ainslie Drive turns off Fairbairn Avenue and winds up through bush to a car park at the summit. It is a short drive from the city centre, and the deck is a few steps from your car.
- **On foot:** the Kokoda Track Memorial Walk starts behind the Australian War Memorial in Campbell. It climbs roughly 2 km through dry eucalypt woodland, with plaques along the way marking the 1942 Kokoda campaign. The grade is steady and steep in places.
- **By bike:** cyclists ride the summit road too, so drivers should expect them on the bends.

## Timing the light

Sunrise and sunset are the classic times to come. Low light picks out the War Memorial dome and turns the lake to metal. Google currently lists the lookout as open 24 hours, but the road and reserve can be affected by maintenance or fire danger, so check ACT Parks before a night or early trip.

After dark, the city lights trace the avenues and the lit Parliament House sits at the end of the axis. In March, during the Canberra Balloon Spectacular, hot-air balloons drift over the parliamentary triangle at dawn. Winter mornings are sharp and often frosty at the summit.

## Sharing the summit sensibly

This is one of Canberra's most visited viewpoints, so plan around other people.

- **Parking:** the summit car park fills around sunset and on clear weekends. Arriving early for the light usually beats the rush, or walk up and skip the car park entirely.
- **Wildlife:** Mount Ainslie Nature Reserve is home to eastern grey kangaroos, which graze near the road at dawn and dusk. Drive the bends slowly and never feed them.
- **Walking up:** carry water. The Kokoda track has little shade, and in summer an early start is much more comfortable than a midday climb.
- **Respect:** the memorial walk commemorates soldiers, so keep the plaques clear for people who stop to read them.
- **Combine it:** pair the walk with a visit to the Australian War Memorial at the trailhead. Check its hours separately.

Bring a jacket at any time of year. The deck is exposed, and it is noticeably windier and colder up here than in the city below.
