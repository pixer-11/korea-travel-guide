## Three nights at Tecnópolis

Three nights, one outdoor park. Puerto Rican reggaeton and Latin trap star Anuel AA plays Tecnópolis on December 3, 5 and 6, 2026. December 4 is a rest night.

The set list draws on a catalogue built since the late 2010s. That includes *Real Hasta la Muerte* (2018), *Emmanuel* (2020) and the *Las Leyendas Nunca Mueren* albums. Crowds come for the singalong hits:

- "China"
- "Secreto"
- "Ella Quiere Beber"
- "Bebé"

The organiser publishes dates, ticket tiers and ticket links on its official site. Tickets for big Argentine shows are almost always digital, held as a QR code on a phone.

## The grounds in Villa Martelli

Tecnópolis is not an arena. It is a large science and technology exhibition park, opened for Argentina's 2011 bicentennial. It sits in Villa Martelli, in the Vicente López district, on the provincial side of Avenida General Paz.

Concerts here are staged in the open air across flat, wide grounds. That means standing on concrete and grass, under whatever the sky delivers. Early December is the start of the Buenos Aires summer. Evenings are usually warm and humid, and summer thunderstorms roll in fast.

The address is Juan Bautista de La Salle 4500. From Palermo or Belgrano it is a short drive north and west along General Paz, the ring road that marks the city limit.

## Getting there and back

Most fans travel by taxi, rideshare or city bus (colectivo). Getting home is the hard part. Tens of thousands of people leave the grounds at the same moment, and General Paz jams solid.

Rideshare pickups near the gates are slow after the encore. Many fans walk several blocks away from the venue before calling a car. Groups often agree on a meeting point beforehand, because mobile data tends to drop when a full crowd is on one cell network.

Fans travelling from abroad usually stay in Palermo or Belgrano. Both neighbourhoods are the closest central areas with plenty of hotels, and they have late-night food when the show lets out.

## Show nights in Villa Martelli

Argentine crowds are loud and physical. Fans sing every word, chant between songs and form a *pogo* (mosh pit) near the front of the general-admission floor. People who want to watch rather than jump usually stand further back or to the sides.

How fans handle the practical side:

- **Payment:** inside the grounds, food and drink stalls usually take cards and Mercado Pago QR as well as pesos. Queues get shorter for card payers.
- **Dinner:** porteños eat late, so many fans have a choripán or pizza near the venue, or eat in Belgrano after the show.
- **Bags:** small bags clear security fastest; large backpacks slow the entry lines.
- **Language:** in Spanish, a general-admission ticket is *campo*, and seated or premium areas are *platea* or *VIP*.

Fans typically arrive an hour or two before doors for floor spots. Fans holding seats tend to come later. Night buses and taxis run well after midnight, but surge pricing on rideshare apps is normal after a stadium-sized show.
