## Why Somsak stands out

Bangkok has no shortage of Isaan restaurants serving grilled meats, som tam, and sticky rice, but Somsak turns the genre into a themed spectacle. The concept comes from the team behind Fatboy Izakaya, a name already known in Bangkok's nightlife-dining scene for blending serious food with maximalist design and a party atmosphere. Somsak applies that same formula to northeastern Thai cuisine, wrapping it in a fictional narrative: a Bangkok taxi driver named Somsak who supposedly struck it rich in Scotland and brought his newfound wealth (and eccentric taste) back home. The result is a restaurant that leans into kitsch — think eclectic, story-driven decor rather than the rustic, open-air Isaan joints typical of the genre — while still keeping the food itself rooted in the fiery, funky flavors the region is known for, like larb, grilled moo yang, and tangy soups.

## Where it is and getting there

Somsak is located in Ekkamai, one of Bangkok's trendiest districts for nightlife and dining, sitting alongside Thonglor as the epicenter of the city's after-dark scene. Ekkamai is best reached via the BTS Sukhumvit Line, getting off at Ekkamai Station and taking a short taxi, motorbike taxi, or ride-share from there, since much of the neighborhood's best dining is tucked along Sukhumvit Soi 63 (Ekkamai Road) and its side sois rather than directly on the main strip. Given the area's soi (side-street) layout, it's worth using a map pin or ride-hailing app rather than trying to walk from the BTS in Bangkok's heat.

## What to expect on the plate

Isaan food is the cuisine of Thailand's northeast — bold, sour, spicy, and often grilled or fermented. At a restaurant with this concept, expect the format to hit the genre's classics: som tam (green papaya salad), larb (minced meat salad with herbs and toasted rice), grilled meats like moo yang or gai yang, and sticky rice as the staple carb alongside everything. Because specific menu items and prices for Somsak weren't independently confirmed at the time of writing, treat any dish names you see online as a guide rather than gospel, and check the current menu on arrival or through the restaurant's own channels before building expectations around particular plates.

## The atmosphere: dinner as theater

What separates Somsak from a standard Isaan restaurant is the production value. Expect a themed interior built around the "Somsak" character myth, loud music, and a room designed to photograph well — a hallmark of the Fatboy Izakaya group's other venues, which tend to double as informal nightlife stops rather than purely food-focused restaurants. This is a place to go in a group, not for a quiet one-on-one dinner. If you're after a mellow, traditional Isaan meal, look elsewhere in the city; if you want spectacle alongside your larb, this is built for that.
