## A grain elevator full of scrap-metal machines

Start with the address. Steampunk HQ is filed under Lake Tekapo, but it sits on the coast at 1 Humber Street, Oamaru. Treat it as a day trip, or as a stop on the way between the Mackenzie Basin and Dunedin.

The building is a Victorian grain elevator made of the pale Oamaru limestone used all through the surrounding streets. Outside, an old steam locomotive has been rebuilt into a spiked, riveted contraption. Inside, the museum describes itself as sci-fi exhibits built around steam power, and it is the reason Oamaru calls itself New Zealand's steampunk capital.

## The drive down from Lake Tekapo

Allow around two hours each way, and more if you stop. The usual route runs south on State Highway 8 through Twizel to Omarama. From there, State Highway 83 follows the Waitaki River to the coast. It is a scenic road with plenty of places to pull over:

- The hydro lakes of the Waitaki: Benmore, Aviemore and Waitaki
- Elephant Rocks, the limestone outcrops near Duntroon
- Kurow, a small town about halfway, useful for coffee and fuel

When you reach Oamaru, follow Thames Street towards the harbour. Humber Street is at the northern edge of the Victorian Precinct, near Harbour and Tyne Streets. Parking on the precinct streets is usually easy.

## What you walk through

The yard comes first. Before you buy a ticket, you can see the converted locomotive and the welded sculptures around it. Old machinery has been bolted into new and impossible shapes. Plenty of visitors take their photos out here and then decide whether to go inside.

Inside, the rooms are dark and crowded with props. Expect gears, gauges, mannequins in goggles, and installations made from salvaged metal, all lit for mood rather than for reading labels. The best-known exhibit is the Portal, a mirrored chamber of lights and sound that seems to stretch forever.

Kids usually head straight for the parts they can operate. Adults tend to linger over the detail on the walls. Most people take about an hour.

## Timing it around the 4pm close

The hours give you a narrow window. At the moment it opens 10am–4pm, and museum hours can shift through the year, so check the official site before you drive two hours to get here.

On weekdays the quietest stretch is 3pm–4pm. It suits the drive from Tekapo, because you can leave in the late morning and stop along the Waitaki on the way. Arrive by 3pm, though: an hour inside is about right.

At weekends, avoid 11am–3pm. That is the busiest period, and with families queuing for the Portal, it is hard to stand still in the narrow rooms. On a weekend, aim to be at the door at 10am instead.

## Tickets, coins and pulling levers

Buy your ticket at the desk inside. The current admission price is posted there and on the museum's website. Cards are accepted almost everywhere in New Zealand, and nobody expects a tip.

A few things are worth knowing before you go in:

- Keep a coin handy. Some of the machines in the yard come alive when you feed them one, with noise, light and sometimes a burst of steam.
- Touching is part of it. Buttons, levers and keyboards are there to be used, and that is the point of the place.
- Let your eyes adjust. Pause when you come in from the bright yard, because the interior is deliberately dim.
- Take photos freely. Steampunk fans dress up for this place, especially around the Steampunk NZ Festival in early June, when Oamaru fills with costumes.

## Staying on in Oamaru

The museum closes at 4pm, which leaves the evening free. Here is how to fill it:

- Walk Harbour and Tyne Streets in the Victorian Precinct. The whitestone warehouses there now hold bookbinders, galleries and secondhand shops.
- Visit the Oamaru Blue Penguin Colony on Waterfront Road. Little penguins come ashore at dusk, and viewing is ticketed.
- Stroll the Oamaru Public Gardens on the other side of the town centre.
- Drive to the Moeraki Boulders, about 40 minutes south on State Highway 1, if you are heading towards Dunedin.

If you are going back to the lakes, the drive back up the Waitaki is long after a full day, and Lake Tekapo's night sky will still be there tomorrow. Staying a night in Oamaru is the gentler option.
