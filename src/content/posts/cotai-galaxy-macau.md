## Gold domes on the Cotai strip

Look for the gold domes. Galaxy Macau's towers sit on the western side of the Cotai strip, on reclaimed land between Taipa and Coloane. Their glass is tinted gold and each tower is topped with a crown-like dome, so the complex is easy to pick out from the Venetian's campanile across the road.

The resort opened in 2011 and has kept growing in phases. It is less a single hotel than a cluster of them, linked by indoor promenades and lobbies. Under one roof you'll find Galaxy Hotel, Banyan Tree Macau, Hotel Okura Macau, JW Marriott, The Ritz-Carlton, Raffles at Galaxy Macau, Andaz Macau and Broadway Macau.

It is one of the most-reviewed resorts in Macau and consistently well rated. Nobody would call it a secret. Expect tour groups, families with pool bags, conference crowds and serious gamblers, all sharing the same air-conditioned corridors.

## The Fortune Diamond in the lobby

This lobby ritual is unique to Galaxy. The Diamond Lobby of Galaxy Hotel is built around a fountain, and at intervals a huge crystal "Fortune Diamond" rises out of the water while music plays and the jets dance around it. People drift in from the shopping arcades, form a loose ring around the pool and lift their phones.

It is free to watch and lasts only a few minutes. Showtimes run on a schedule, so ask at the concierge desk or a guest services counter when you arrive. Find the next slot and plan your wander around it rather than waiting by the fountain.

A tip on position: the rails right at the water fill first. Standing a few steps back, slightly raised on the lobby's edges, gives you the full rise of the diamond without heads in the frame.

## Up on the Grand Resort Deck

The deck is the reason families book here. The Grand Resort Deck is a rooftop-level garden and water park. Galaxy bills its Skytop Wave Pool as the world's largest, and it sits beside a stretch of white sand piled on top of the building. Around it you'll find:

- **The Skytop Wave Pool**, with machine-made waves rolling in toward the sand
- **The Skytop Adventure Rapids**, a long lazy river you float around on a tube
- **Water slides** and shallower pools for children
- **Cabanas and loungers** spread among landscaped palms

Access is generally tied to staying in one of the resort's hotels. Check your booking's inclusions before you pack a swimsuit. In Macau's humid summer the deck is the most sought-after space in the complex, so claim a lounger early in the day.

## Eating from fine dining to Broadway's food street

The food range here is unusually wide. The upmarket end sits inside the hotel towers, with formal Cantonese, Japanese and steakhouse rooms where booking ahead is normal.

The more interesting end is Broadway Macau. Its open-air food street gathers Macau and Hong Kong snack vendors in one walkable lane. It's the place for the city's street-level classics:

- **Pork chop buns**, Macau's signature fried-chop sandwich
- **Portuguese-style egg tarts**, with blistered tops
- **Almond cookies and beef jerky**, the souvenir staples of Macau's old streets
- **Wonton noodles and congee** for a cheaper sit-down meal

Prices sit across the full range, from budget-friendly snacks to on-the-pricier-side tasting menus. Check the posted menu at each counter, because they vary widely within a few metres.

## Getting to the Galaxy side of Cotai

Most visitors arrive by shuttle. The resort runs complimentary shuttle buses from Macau's main entry points, and these have historically included the ferry terminals, the Border Gate, the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge port and the airport. Routes have changed in recent years, so confirm on Galaxy's official site before you travel.

The Taipa line of the Macau Light Rapid Transit runs through Cotai. Its stops on the strip put you a short covered walk from the resort's entrances. Taxis queue at the main hotel driveways, and the doormen will write your destination in Chinese if you ask. Galaxy's Chinese name is 澳門銀河 (Ou Mun Ngan Ho in Cantonese).

Walking along Cotai's roads looks short on a map but feels long in summer heat. Use the indoor links and footbridges where you can.

## Casino floors, shuttles and patacas: how the place works

A few rules here are firm.

- **Age 21 and ID.** Macau's legal age for casino entry is 21. Guards at the doors check passports, and they do stop young-looking visitors.
- **No photos on the gaming floor.** Phones come out freely in the lobby and the Diamond show, but not at the tables.
- **Two currencies.** The Macau pataca is the local currency, and Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted across the resort. Change from a purchase often comes back in patacas, which are hard to use once you leave Macau.
- **Service charge.** Restaurant bills in Macau's resorts usually carry a service charge already. Read the bill before adding a tip.
- **Dress.** Smart-casual is fine almost everywhere. Some fine-dining rooms prefer no beachwear or flip-flops, so change after the deck.

The complex is easy to get lost in. Signage names the hotels rather than compass directions. Pick one hotel lobby as your landmark, such as Galaxy Hotel's Diamond Lobby, and navigate back to it.

## Next door and nearby

The rest of Cotai is close by. The Venetian Macao, The Parisian with its half-scale Eiffel Tower, and The Londoner sit across Estrada do Istmo. City of Dreams and its House of Dancing Water theatre are a short hop beyond. The Galaxy International Convention Center and Galaxy Arena are part of the same complex.

For contrast, head north to Taipa Village. Its narrow lanes, Rua do Cunha snack shops and pastel Taipa Houses-Museum are a quick taxi ride away. Together they make a good counterweight to an afternoon under the gold domes.

Half a day covers the lobbies, the Diamond show and a Broadway snack crawl. Staying guests with deck access can easily fill two days.
