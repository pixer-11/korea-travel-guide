## What you're looking at

This low, cream-coloured building at 351 Upper Bukit Timah Road was the Ford Motor Company's first Southeast Asian assembly plant, opened in 1941. Ten months later it hosted one of the war's pivotal moments: Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival signed the British surrender here on 15 February 1942, handing Singapore to General Yamashita Tomoyuki. The Japanese used it afterward as a repair depot. Today the National Archives of Singapore runs it as a permanent WWII exhibition called "Surviving the Japanese Occupation."

The building itself is worth studying before you even walk in: long horizontal windows, curved corners, a factory floor plan still legible under the gallery fit-out. That industrial shell is part of the story, not just a container for it.

## Inside the galleries

Exhibits move chronologically through the fall of Malaya, the surrender, and the Occupation years that followed. Expect oral history recordings, ration-era household objects, propaganda posters, and a reconstruction of the room where the surrender documents were signed. Panels lean on first-person accounts from Singaporeans who lived through 1942-45, which gives it a more personal register than a typical war-history museum.

- Budget 45-60 minutes for a full walkthrough
- Galleries are air-conditioned and mostly indoors, useful on a hot afternoon
- Signage is in English, with some sections in Chinese and Malay

## Getting there and timing it right

There's no MRT station at the door. The nearest is Beauty World (Downtown Line), about a 15-20 minute walk, or a short bus ride along Upper Bukit Timah Road. Drivers will find limited on-site parking; the roadside can fill up on weekends when hikers are also headed to nearby Bukit Timah Nature Reserve.

Hours run Tuesday to Sunday, 9am to 5:30pm; it's closed all day Monday. Because it sits next to one of Singapore's most-visited nature reserves but isn't itself a hiking stop, it draws a fraction of that foot traffic. Arriving right after opening on a weekday is your best shot at having galleries largely to yourself.

## Making the most of a short visit

Pair it with the nature reserve trailhead across the road if you want a half-day combining history and hiking; the museum's air conditioning is a good place to cool off before or after a trail. There's no café inside, so eat beforehand at the hawker options along Upper Bukit Timah Road. Photography is generally allowed in the permanent galleries but check posted signs near any temporary exhibits.

Because the site is compact, it works well as a 45-minute add-on rather than an anchor for a full day out, so slot it alongside other Bukit Timah plans rather than building a trip around it alone.
