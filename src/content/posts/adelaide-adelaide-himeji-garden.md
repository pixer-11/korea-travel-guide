## A walled pocket of Japan on South Terrace

South Terrace is loud with traffic. Step through the gate on the corner of Glen Osmond Road and a wall and planting close off most of that noise. What's left is gravel, water, stone and clipped greenery, all arranged to be looked at slowly.

The garden marks the sister-city relationship between Adelaide and Himeji, the Japanese city known for its white castle. It has two classic Japanese garden forms side by side: a lake-and-mountain garden built around water, and a dry garden where raked gravel stands in for water. Most visitors walk the whole loop in 30 to 45 minutes.

## What you'll find along the paths

The garden is compact, so everything is close together. Paths and stepping stones take you from one planned view to the next, and each view changes as you move.

Things to look for:

- **The ornamental gate** at the entrance, which frames your first view inside
- **The lake**, the centre of the lake-and-mountain section, with rocks placed to suggest a landscape much larger than the plot
- **The dry garden**, where lines raked in the gravel read as ripples around the stones
- **Stone lanterns and a water basin**, the standard fittings of a Japanese garden, set beside the paths
- **The teahouse**, a small traditional structure that works as a visual focal point

The planting changes through the year. Autumn colour and spring blossom get the most attention, but in winter the bare branches make the stonework and the shape of the layout easier to see.

## Picking your hour

Timing matters more here than at bigger gardens. The space is small and the paths are narrow, so a few groups can make it feel full.

On weekdays it's calmest between 8am and 10am. At weekends only the first hour, 8am to 9am, is reliably quiet. Try not to arrive between 10am and 5pm on a Saturday or Sunday. That stretch is the busiest, and people wait at the stepping stones for others to cross.

The gates open at 8am and close at 5:30pm every day of the week. Late afternoon on a weekday works if you can't make the morning. Leave yourself enough time before closing, because this isn't a garden to rush.

## Walking the garden the way it was designed

A Japanese garden is built to be walked slowly, and it gives more back when you do. These habits help:

- **Stay on the paths and stones.** The moss, gravel and lake edges take time to repair, and the raked patterns in the dry garden are redone by hand.
- **Don't climb the rocks or step onto the gravel** for a photo. Every stone was put there on purpose, and climbing one spoils the view for everyone else.
- **Keep your voice down.** Sound carries in an enclosed space, and many people come here to sit quietly.
- **Stop at the benches and viewpoints.** The design is built around set viewing positions. Stand still at each one for a minute and see how the lake, rocks and planting line up.
- **Share the photo spots.** The gate and the lake edge are popular with couples and photographers, especially at weekends. Take your shot and move on.

Whatever else you do, go in the early window if you want the calm the garden was made for.

## Getting there on foot or by bus

The garden sits on the city side of the Adelaide Park Lands, at the south-eastern corner of the city grid. From the southern end of Hutt Street it's a short walk. Hutt Street's cafés make a good stop for coffee before an 8am visit.

From Victoria Square, walking south and east across the grid takes around 20 minutes. Buses run along Glen Osmond Road and past the South Terrace corner. Check Adelaide Metro's journey planner for the current routes. If you're driving, street parking on South Terrace is the usual option, so read the signs for time limits.

## Pairing it with the South Parklands

The garden is small enough to combine with other things nearby. Head west along South Terrace to reach Veale Gardens, a much larger formal Park Lands garden with lawns, flowerbeds and water features. It makes a good contrast after the tight, controlled layout of Himeji.

The wider South Parklands also have open grass and walking tracks for a longer stroll. From there, Hutt Street is the easiest place to get lunch before you head back into the centre.
