## A concrete tower on dairy flats

The tower is built of grey concrete. It rises above flat Waikato pasture, and nothing else nearby comes close to its height. Josiah Clifton Firth, an Auckland businessman, ran the vast Matamata estate in the 1880s, and in 1882 he put up this tower as its office. It was an early use of concrete in New Zealand, at a time when most colonial buildings were timber.

Inside, the tower holds history exhibits on the estate and the district. Around it, the reserve's lawns hold a small group of older buildings, and you walk between them on grass. It reads like a scattered settlement more than a single museum hall.

## Firth Tower's hours and a sensible visit length

Check the day before you drive out. The current schedule is:

- **Thursday to Monday:** 10am–4pm
- **Tuesday and Wednesday:** closed

These are this week's hours. Museums change them for holidays and seasons, so confirm on the museum's official site before a special trip. Give it one to two hours: the tower takes 20–30 minutes, and the grounds take longer if you read the panels. Arriving after 3pm cuts things short because doors close at 4pm.

## Walking the reserve without rushing

This is one of the quieter stops around Matamata. Most visitors to the town head straight for the Hobbiton Movie Set, so the reserve rarely feels crowded. A few habits help:

- **Start at the tower,** where the estate story begins, then work outward through the grounds.
- **Ask about entry at the counter.** Admission details are posted on site and on the official site. Volunteer-run museums like this often welcome a donation on top of entry.
- **Talk to whoever is on duty.** Small regional museums usually run on local volunteers, and they often know the building histories better than the labels do.
- **Bring a picnic.** The open lawns suit lunch on a fine day, and Tower Road has no cafés.
- **Stay off fenced or roped areas.** Some interiors are fragile and viewed from the doorway.

## Getting to Tower Road and what's nearby

You need a car. No regular bus goes out to 266A Tower Road, Matamata 3471. From the town centre it's a short drive northeast past farmland, with parking at the reserve.

Matamata is roughly two hours' drive south of Auckland, under an hour from Hamilton and about an hour from Tauranga. That makes the tower an easy add-on to a day built around somewhere else:

- **Hobbiton Movie Set,** near Hinuera south of town: book a fixed tour time and visit the tower before or after.
- **Matamata i-SITE** on Broadway: the visitor centre is built as a hobbit-style gatehouse and stocks regional maps.
- **Wairere Falls,** in the Kaimai Range east of town: a forest track climbs to one of the North Island's tallest waterfalls.

Because the museum closes Tuesdays and Wednesdays, keep those days for the falls or the Kaimai walks. Put the tower on a Thursday-to-Monday afternoon.
