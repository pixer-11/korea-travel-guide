## Eight days at the boards

Picture rows of tables under a hotel ceiling. Each one holds a board, a clock, two score sheets and two players who hardly look up. The XXIII Torneo Internacional Gran Hotel Bali runs from November 28 to December 5, 2026, and around 150 competitors take part.

It is an international open. Titled players and club amateurs enter the same event, so a grandmaster's board can sit a few tables from a teenager's first foreign tournament. The quiet is the whole atmosphere: the tap of clocks, pens scratching moves, the scrape of a chair when someone gets up to pace.

## A tournament that comes back every year

The Roman numerals in the name matter. XXIII makes this the 23rd edition, and that kind of run means a lot of returning faces. Many entrants plan their late-autumn calendar around Benidorm.

The tournament takes its name from the Gran Hotel Bali, the tall hotel tower that stands out on Benidorm's skyline even next to the city's other high-rises. For visiting players the appeal is simple. You play serious chess somewhere you can walk to the Mediterranean between rounds.

## Chess in Benidorm's shoulder season

The timing is the quirk that makes this event. Late November and early December is well past the summer peak. The midsummer crowds are gone, and Benidorm's long-stay winter visitors from northern Europe settle in instead.

Days are usually mild enough for a walk along the seafront in a light jacket. The sea is too cool for most swimmers, though. Evenings get dark early, which suits a tournament: you finish a round, eat, then go over the game before bed.

Players and their families tend to treat the week as a working holiday. The usual pattern is:

- Mornings for preparation or a coastal walk
- The round itself, which takes up the core of the day
- Late dinners in the old town, then analysis

## Getting to Benidorm

Alicante–Elche Airport is the usual way in for international visitors, roughly 60 km south of Benidorm. Direct coaches run from the airport to Benidorm's bus station, and taxis and transfers make the trip along the coast motorway.

From central Alicante, the TRAM d'Alacant Line 1 runs from Luceros station up the coast to Benidorm. It is slow but scenic and handy for anyone staying in Alicante. Inside Benidorm, local buses and taxis cover the distances between Levante, Poniente and the hotel districts. Plenty of people simply walk.

## Sitting quietly by the boards

Watching an open is free-form, but there is etiquette. Spectators at Spanish opens usually keep well back from the tables and stay silent. They step away from the playing area before saying anything, even in a whisper.

A few conventions apply at almost every FIDE-rated open:

- **Phones:** players are generally banned from carrying them in the hall. Spectators keep theirs silent and out of sight.
- **Photos:** organisers often allow photography only in the first minutes of a round, and never with flash.
- **No hints, no faces:** reacting to a position, even with a raised eyebrow, can get a spectator removed.
- **Results:** open tournaments in Spain usually post pairings and standings on chess-results.com, which is how most friends and coaches follow along remotely.

The tournament's organisers publish the registration terms, schedule and any spectator arrangements through the event's own channels. Open events of this kind are normally Swiss-system, so the top boards in the final rounds are where the decisive games usually happen.

## Between rounds: Benidorm on foot

Benidorm is compact enough to fill a rest morning without transport. The two big beaches, Levante and Poniente, meet at the rocky headland of the old town. The whitewashed Balcón del Mediterráneo lookout sits at its tip, with views both ways along the coast.

Behind it, the narrow streets around Calle Santo Domingo are known for tapas bars. Locals and visitors stand at the counter and order a few small plates at a time.

For more air, the Serra Gelada natural park rises at the eastern end of Levante. Its cliff path climbs above the sea toward Albir. Boats also run out to the Isla de Benidorm, the small wedge-shaped island visible from every beach.

## Where players usually stay

Plenty of competitors book rooms in the host hotel itself, since it cuts the walk to the playing hall to an elevator ride. Others spread into the many mid-range hotels and apartments along Levante, which are plentiful and easy to find outside summer.

Families travelling with a young player often prefer an apartment with a kitchen. Eight days of meals out adds up, and a quiet room for preparation helps. Alicante is also an option for anyone who prefers a city base and doesn't mind the tram ride.
