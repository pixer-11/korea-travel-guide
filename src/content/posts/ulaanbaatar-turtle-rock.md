## A turtle the size of a building

Squint, and the shape is obvious. A rounded granite shell sits on the valley floor with a blunt head pushing forward, and the whole outcrop looks as if it stopped walking a few thousand years ago. Mongolians call it Melkhii Khad, which simply means Turtle Rock.

It stands in Gorkhi-Terelj National Park, where the steppe east of Ulaanbaatar turns into larch forest and weathered granite tors. Herders' gers dot the slopes around it, and horses graze right up to the road. Here you can tell that the capital, for all its traffic, sits next to open country.

## Getting out to Terelj

Turtle Rock is in Terelj, not in the city. It falls under Ulaanbaatar's administrative area, but the drive from Sükhbaatar Square is a long one and usually takes well over an hour. Traffic leaving the city is the main variable.

Most people get there in one of these ways:

- **A day tour** booked through a guesthouse or agency in central Ulaanbaatar, often combined with other Terelj stops
- **A hired car and driver**, which lets you set your own pace and stay for sunset
- **Public transport** to Terelj village in the summer months, though schedules change, so confirm locally before relying on it

The road into the park passes a checkpoint where a park entry fee is collected. The current rate is posted there. Turtle Rock sits beside the main paved road through the park, so no hiking is needed to reach the base.

## What you do when you arrive

You walk around it, then you climb a little. The granite has plenty of grip, and you can scramble partway up the flanks for a view down the valley toward the Tuul River side of the park. Going higher than you're comfortable with is not worth it: the rock is rounded and the drops are real.

At the base you'll usually find small souvenir stalls and herders offering photos with camels, horses or eagles. Prices aren't posted, so agree on one before anyone hands you an animal. Horse rides from nearby ger camps are easy to arrange if you want to see the valley from the saddle.

Allow 30–60 minutes for the rock itself. Make it half a day if you add a horse ride or the walk to the temple.

## Pairing it with the rest of the park

Turtle Rock works best as one stop on a Terelj loop, not a destination on its own. The obvious companion is **Aryabal Meditation Temple**, a Buddhist retreat set high on a hillside further into the park. You reach it by a footbridge and a long climb of stairs past prayer wheels and illustrated signboards.

Other stops to fit into the same day:

- **Gorkhi-Terelj ger camps** for lunch or an overnight stay in a felt tent
- **The Genghis Khan Equestrian Statue at Tsonjin Boldog**, the giant steel horseman east of the city, often bundled into the same tour despite lying in a different direction
- **Riverside meadows along the Terelj River**, good for a picnic in summer

## Seasons in the park

Summer is the friendly season. From June to August the valley is green, ger camps are open and the roads are at their best. July also brings Naadam, when Ulaanbaatar is busy and Terelj fills up with city families escaping for the day.

September turns the larches gold and thins the visitors. Winter is a different trip: snow on the shell, frozen rivers and temperatures well below freezing. It's beautiful, but it needs proper cold-weather gear and a driver who knows the road.

## Out in Terelj, Mongolian style

A few habits will smooth the day. Carry Mongolian tögrög in small notes, because stalls, camel owners and horse handlers out here often deal in cash. Cards are far more reliable back in the city.

You may notice an ovoo, a cairn of stones often wrapped in blue khadag scarves, on hilltops around the park. Mongolians walk around it clockwise three times and may add a stone. Join in if you like, and don't take anything from it.

Ask before photographing herders or their gers. If a family invites you inside, accept the tea or snack with your right hand, and don't step on the threshold.

Drivers are usually hired for the whole day. Agree on stops, waiting time and return time before you leave Ulaanbaatar, not at the rock.
