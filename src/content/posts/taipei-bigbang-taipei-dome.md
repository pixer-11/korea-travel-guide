## Two nights under Taipei's new roof

BIGBANG was set to play the Taipei Dome on October 10 and 11, 2026. Both dates fell in the group's anniversary year, two decades after its 2006 debut with YG Entertainment.

The venue is part of the story too. Taipei Dome opened at the end of 2023 as Taiwan's first enclosed stadium. It has a fixed roof, so rain stops nothing. At BIGBANG concerts, the fandom, known as VIPs, fills the room with the yellow glow of crown-shaped lightsticks.

One date detail stands out. October 10 is Double Ten, Taiwan's National Day and a public holiday. Hotels around Xinyi and the MRT usually carry holiday crowds that weekend.

## Getting to the Taipei Dome

The MRT is the practical route for a crowd this size.

- **Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall Station (Blue/Bannan Line, BL17):** Exit 5 has an underground connection to the dome complex. This is the main route, so it is also the most crowded.
- **Taipei City Hall Station (BL18):** one stop east, about a 15-minute walk. A calmer option after big events.
- **Zhongxiao Dunhua Station (BL16):** one stop west, a similar walk through the Dunhua shopping streets.

An EasyCard or a contactless bank card works at the gates, and both work across the MRT and city buses. Taxis and ride-hail cars face road closures and gridlock around Zhongxiao East Road after big events. A walk of a few blocks usually makes it easier to find one.

## Inside the dome

Taipei Dome seats roughly 40,000 for baseball, the sport it was built for. Concert configurations put the stage at one end and add floor seating. Upper-tier seats are steep and high, and small binoculars help near the top.

The complex has a shopping mall attached, with restaurants and convenience stores that ease long waits for merchandise. Bag rules, camera policy and lightstick rules are set per show and differ from one concert to the next.

## Concert-day manners in Taipei

Taiwan takes ticket and transit rules seriously, and concert days follow a few clear habits.

- **Authorised sellers only.** Taiwan amended its law in 2022 to penalise scalping. Resale tickets can be voided, and real-name entry is common at large shows, with ID matching the name on the ticket.
- **Orderly queues.** Lines for merch and entry are long, but people keep to them. Staff with signs mark where each line ends.
- **Early merch lines.** Lines usually form hours before doors open.
- **Slow exits.** After the encore at big dome shows, tens of thousands head for Exit 5 at once. Many people wait a few minutes or walk to Taipei City Hall Station instead.
- **Strict MRT rules.** Eating and drinking are banned past the fare gates, including water and gum, and fines are enforced. Bubble tea gets finished outside.

## Around the venue

The dome sits in one of Taipei's easiest neighbourhoods for a spare afternoon.

- **Songshan Cultural and Creative Park:** next door, in a former 1930s tobacco factory. It has design shops, exhibitions, and the Eslite Spectrum bookshop and store.
- **Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall:** a short walk south, with a hilltop-style hall and open plaza. The changing of the guard happens here.
- **Taipei 101:** about 20 minutes on foot, or two stops on the Red Line from Taipei City Hall area via Xiangshan.
- **Raohe Street Night Market:** a quick taxi or bus ride northeast, open late and good for a post-show meal of pepper buns and grilled sausages.

October weather in Taipei is usually warm and humid, and late-season typhoons are possible. Shows at the dome are indoors, but the MRT lines and merch queues are not. The Central Weather Administration issues forecasts and typhoon warnings for the city.
