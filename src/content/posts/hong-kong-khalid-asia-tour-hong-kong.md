## How Hong Kong concert nights run

Ticketing here mostly happens online. Big shows in the city usually sell through platforms such as Cityline, HK Ticketing, Ticketflap or Klook. Popular dates tend to go during the first rush, so fans often wait in the online queue the moment sales open.

Tickets are mostly e-tickets or QR codes, and staff scan them on phones at the gate. Many attendees screenshot their QR code in advance, because mobile signal inside a packed hall can drop.

Bag checks are standard at the doors. Professional cameras and large bags are often refused. Merch stands usually open before doors, and they tend to be busiest in the last hour before the show and straight after it ends.

Paying is easy:

- Contactless cards work almost everywhere.
- Octopus cards and phone wallets cover the MTR, convenience stores and many food stalls.
- Tipping is not expected at concession counters.

## Getting there and getting back

You can reach any of the likely venues without a taxi. An **Octopus card**, or a phone wallet set up for Octopus, gets you through the MTR gates in a second. It saves you queuing at the ticket machines with everyone else from the show.

Leaving is the slow part. When thousands of people come out of a hall at once, the nearest station platform backs up for a while. Locals often walk one station further, or stop for a late bowl of noodles nearby until the crowd thins.

- From **Hung Hom**, the East Rail and Tuen Ma lines go to Kowloon, the New Territories and Admiralty.
- From **AsiaWorld-Expo**, the Airport Express runs back to Kowloon and Hong Kong stations.
- From **Kai Tak**, the Tuen Ma Line connects at Diamond Hill and Ho Man Tin.

## December weather and what to wear

Early December is one of the most comfortable times of year in Hong Kong. The air is drier and the evenings are cool, without the summer humidity. Inside, venues run strong air conditioning, so most concertgoers carry a light layer.

Christmas lights go up across the city around this time. The harbourfront along Tsim Sha Tsui is a good walk if you arrive with time to spare.
