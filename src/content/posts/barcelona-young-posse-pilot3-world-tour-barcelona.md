## Sala Salamandra, explained

Salamandra is a long-running live-music club in L'Hospitalet de Llobregat. L'Hospitalet is a separate municipality, but it runs straight on from Barcelona's Sants district with no visible break. The venue is a dark, black-walled box built for standing crowds, with bars along the sides and the stage at one end.

The layout makes for a particular kind of night:

- **Standing floor.** Where you end up depends on when you joined the queue, not on a seat number.
- **Low stage, close sightlines.** Even near the back you are much nearer the performers than at an arena show.
- **Compact space.** Lightstick waves and fan chants fill the room quickly, so a modest crowd sounds loud.

## Queues, lightsticks and getting home: how the night works

K-pop club shows follow their own customs. For a standing show, fans typically start queuing outside well before doors open. The first through the door head for the barrier, and everyone else spreads back toward the bar.

What attendees usually bring and do:

- **Official lightstick**, if they own one. A room this size turns the stick waves into a real light show.
- **Fan chants** called out between lyrics. Regulars learn them from the group's fan community, and newcomers pick them up by the second chorus.
- **Merch first.** Tour stands often sell out of popular sizes, so many people buy before the show starts.
- **Contactless payment.** Cards and phones work at almost every bar and stand in Barcelona. Tipping at a venue bar is not expected.

Spanish evenings start late, and dinner in many neighbourhood restaurants doesn't get going until well into the evening. Concertgoers usually eat a late lunch, or grab bocadillos from a bakery near the metro. A "gràcies" in Catalan, or "gracias" in Spanish, goes down well at the counter.
