## A club show, not an arena

Young Posse are playing Barcelona in a room, not a stadium. The date is Thursday, October 29, 2026, and the venue is Sala Salamandra. That puts a K-pop girl group in the kind of club where the front barrier sits a few steps from the stage lip.

This is the Barcelona leg of **PILOT3: The 1st FLIGHT TO EUROPE**, the group's first tour of the continent. For European fans who have only seen them through a phone screen, it is a first chance to hear the rap-heavy, hip-hop-leaning sound live. That sound has set the group apart since their 2023 debut with "Macaroni Cheese".

## Sala Salamandra, explained

Salamandra is a long-running live-music club in L'Hospitalet de Llobregat. L'Hospitalet is a separate municipality, but it runs straight on from Barcelona's Sants district with no visible break. The venue is a dark, black-walled box built for standing crowds, with bars along the sides and the stage at one end.

The layout makes for a particular kind of night:

- **Standing floor.** Where you end up depends on when you joined the queue, not on a seat number.
- **Low stage, close sightlines.** Even near the back you are much nearer the performers than at an arena show.
- **Compact space.** Lightstick waves and fan chants fill the room quickly, so a modest crowd sounds loud.

## Getting to L'Hospitalet

The metro makes the trip easy. Line 1 (red) runs to **Avinguda Carrilet**, the stop closest to the venue. From Plaça de Catalunya the ride is direct, with no change, and takes about twenty minutes.

FGC suburban trains from Plaça Espanya also stop at L'Hospitalet–Av. Carrilet. That helps if you are staying near Montjuïc or along Gran Via. Taxis and ride-hail cars reach the area from central Barcelona in about the same time as the metro, traffic allowing.

October 29 falls on a weeknight. Barcelona's metro stops around midnight from Monday to Thursday. Fans who stay for merch or the crowd outside afterwards often go home on the NitBus night buses or by taxi instead.

## Queues, lightsticks and getting home: how the night works

K-pop club shows follow their own customs. For a standing show, fans typically start queuing outside well before doors open. The first through the door head for the barrier, and everyone else spreads back toward the bar.

What attendees usually bring and do:

- **Official lightstick**, if they own one. A room this size turns the stick waves into a real light show.
- **Fan chants** called out between lyrics. Regulars learn them from the group's fan community, and newcomers pick them up by the second chorus.
- **Merch first.** Tour stands often sell out of popular sizes, so many people buy before the show starts.
- **Contactless payment.** Cards and phones work at almost every bar and stand in Barcelona. Tipping at a venue bar is not expected.

Spanish evenings start late, and most neighbourhood restaurants don't serve dinner until 8:30pm or 9pm. Concertgoers usually eat a late lunch, or grab bocadillos from a bakery near the metro. A "gràcies" in Catalan, or "gracias" in Spanish, goes down well at the counter.

## Around the show

L'Hospitalet is mostly residential, so it makes sense to plan the rest of the day in Barcelona. Sants and Hostafrancs are just over the border. Their small bars along Carrer de Sants and Carrer de la Creu Coberta make an easy stop before the show.

Montjuïc is a short ride from Plaça Espanya, and you can spend an afternoon among its gardens, the Fundació Joan Miró and the MNAC. Visitors who are only in town for the concert often stay near Plaça Espanya or Sants station. Both are a short metro or train ride from the venue and well connected to the airport.
