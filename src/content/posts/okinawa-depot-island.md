## Pastel walls on the seawall

Depot Island looks like a beach town that someone painted with highlighters. Mint, coral, mustard and sky-blue buildings stack up in two and three storeys. Outside stairways and narrow walkways link them, and they sit right against the seawall at Mihama in Chatan.

It is the westernmost slice of American Village, the shopping district built near the US bases on the island's central west coast. The editorial summary calls it a waterfront complex with dozens of shops, restaurants, bars and a hotel, plus frequent events. That is accurate. It is a well-visited, consistently well-rated spot, and on weekends it feels like it.

## Getting to Mihama from Naha

Okinawa's main island has no train this far north, so you will come by bus or car. The address is 9-1 Mihama, Chatan.

- **By bus:** from Naha Bus Terminal, take one of the lines running north along Route 58. Get off at the Kuwae or Gunbyoin-mae stops and walk west toward the ferris wheel, about 10–15 minutes.
- **By car:** allow 30–45 minutes from Naha, longer at evening rush. American Village has large public parking lots, but they fill on weekend evenings.
- **From Kadena or Okinawa City:** it is a short taxi ride south.

The ferris wheel is your landmark. Depot Island sits between it and the sea.

## Working the alleys and stairs

Treat the place as a maze, not a mall. The ground floors hold the obvious storefronts. Upper levels hide smaller shops and cafés reached by external stairs, and many visitors never climb them.

What you will find across the blocks:

- Beachwear, surf brands and Okinawan souvenirs
- Vintage and American-style clothing shops
- Cafés and dessert stands, plus burger, taco and bar menus that reflect the base-town setting
- Balconies and terraces facing the water

Give yourself two to three hours for shopping and a meal. Add another hour if you want sunset and dinner.

## Sunset on the seawall

The complex faces west, which is the whole point. A wooden boardwalk runs along the seawall in front of the buildings. In the late afternoon people line it up, sitting on the edge with drinks while the sun drops into the East China Sea.

Sunset Beach, a small man-made beach, is a short walk north along the same shoreline. After dark the painted façades are lit and the bars on the upper decks fill up. Shops currently close at 9pm, though individual bars and restaurants keep their own hours.

## Beating the crowd at American Village

This is one of the busiest evening spots in central Okinawa, so plan around the crowd. Here is how regulars tend to handle it.

- **Come early on weekends.** Doors open at 10am, and arriving soon after usually beats the afternoon arrivals from Naha and the cruise and tour groups.
- **Claim a sunset seat early.** Terrace tables with sea views go first. Walk in at least 45 minutes before sunset, or sit on the seawall instead.
- **Ask about tax-free shopping.** Larger shops often offer it to foreign visitors. Carry your passport and ask at the counter before you pay.
- **Pay how the shop prefers.** Cards and IC cards work in many places, but small stalls may want cash. Tipping is not expected in Japan, even at the American-style bars.
- **Keep the stairs clear.** The walkways are narrow, so step aside for photos rather than blocking a landing.

Weekday afternoons are calmer, a reasonable guess given how the area fills with locals and base staff after work and on weekends. Events add to the crowds. Check the American Village notice boards or official site before you go.

## Stretching the evening nearby

Chatan rewards an evening, not a quick stop. You can walk to the Mihama ferris wheel, the larger American Village shopping streets and Sunset Beach from the boardwalk.

For more coastline, the seaside path continues toward Araha Beach to the south. That makes a good morning walk if you are staying in Chatan. Many visitors base themselves here for a night or two rather than doing the round trip from Naha each time.
