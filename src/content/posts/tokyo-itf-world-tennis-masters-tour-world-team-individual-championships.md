## Five days of Masters tennis on the bay

The ITF World Tennis Masters Tour runs November 11-15, 2026. This is the ITF's circuit for adult competitive players, once known as the Seniors tour. These World Championships are the top event on that circuit.

This edition covers the youngest Masters age groups, 30 and 35. Many of the players in these draws are former college players, ex-professionals and strong national-level competitors. The ball comes off the racket hard. Expect heavy groundstrokes and real tactics, played by people who still train seriously.

The format pairs two competitions. The **World Team Championships** put players together in national sides. The **Individual Championships** are traditional singles and doubles draws. Over the five days you can watch nations play for each other one afternoon and players go after personal titles on another.

## Ariake Tennis Forest Park, the venue

Ariake Tennis Forest Park (有明テニスの森公園, Ariake Tennis no Mori Kōen) is the biggest tennis venue in Japan. It sits on reclaimed land in Kōtō ward, on the Tokyo Bay waterfront.

Its centrepiece is **Ariake Coliseum**, the stadium with a retractable roof. It has long hosted Japan's top professional events, including the Japan Open and the Pan Pacific Open. The park was renovated for the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, and the Olympic tennis event was played here.

Around the Coliseum, rows of outdoor courts run through the park, separated by tree-lined paths. At a Masters event you can walk from match to match. On the outer courts, spectators usually stand or sit close enough to hear the players call the score.

## Getting to Ariake on the Yurikamome

The **Yurikamome** is the driverless elevated train that loops across the bay from Shimbashi. It is the easiest way in. Get off at **Ariake-tennis-no-mori Station**, the stop named for the park. The entrance is a short walk away.

Other options:

- **Rinkai Line** to **Kokusai-tenjijō Station**, then walk south-east past Tokyo Big Sight. It's longer, but handy if you're coming from Shinjuku or Shibuya, because the Rinkai connects with JR lines at Ōsaki.
- **Yurikamome from Toyosu**: change from the Tokyo Metro Yūrakuchō Line at Toyosu. Ariake-tennis-no-mori is a few stops west.
- **From central Tokyo**: the Yurikamome from Shimbashi crosses the Rainbow Bridge. It's slow, but the ride is a view in itself.

Suica and Pasmo IC cards work on all of these lines.

## Courtside manners at a Masters event

Tennis etiquette in Japan is strict, and crowds keep to it. On the outer courts nobody walks behind a baseline during a point. Spectators wait at the court gate and slip in or out on a changeover, every odd game.

Other habits worth copying:

- **Applause comes after the point**, never during the rally. Spectators clap good shots by either player, not only their own country's.
- **Phones go on silent mode** (マナーモード, *manner mode*). Calls on the outer courts carry clearly to the players.
- **Bins are scarce** in Tokyo public spaces. Most people take their bottles and wrappers home or drop them at the station.
- **There is no tipping** at food stalls, cafés or taxis around Ariake. IC cards pay at most convenience stores and vending machines.

The team days work a little differently. National squads cheer for their teammates from the sidelines, so the noise between points can be louder than in the individual draws. When the ball is in play, the courts still go quiet.

## Racing the mid-November light

Mid-November is one of Tokyo's most comfortable months for outdoor sport. Days are typically mild and dry, with cool mornings off the bay. Many players and spectators wear layers they can take off as the day warms up.

The catch is the sunset, which comes at around 4:30pm at this time of year. On the open bayside courts the light drops quickly in the late afternoon, and the wind off the water turns cold. Regulars tend to bring a jacket for the last matches of the day, even after a warm lunchtime.

The trees in the park turn colour around this time, so the outdoor courts have autumn yellows around them on the walk between matches.

## Between matches: Toyosu, Big Sight and Odaiba

Ariake has plenty around it when you want a break from the courts.

- **Ariake Garden**, the large mall near the Yurikamome line, has food halls and restaurants. It's the closest place for a proper sit-down meal.
- **Toyosu Market** is one stop east at Shijō-mae. It is Tokyo's wholesale fish market, with sushi counters inside. It's at its busiest in the morning.
- **teamLab Planets**, the walk-through digital art museum, is in Toyosu next to Shin-toyosu Station.
- **Tokyo Big Sight**, the convention centre with the upside-down pyramids, is walkable from the park. Trade shows there can fill the trains.
- **Odaiba Seaside Park** and its Rainbow Bridge views are a few Yurikamome stops west. It's a good place for the evening after play ends.

Visitors on a tennis-focused trip usually stay in Toyosu or Shiodome. Both are on direct lines to the courts and close to the rest of the city.

## Where dates, draws and results live

The International Tennis Federation organises the World Tennis Masters Tour. Its Masters Tour pages carry the tournament's draws and results, along with entry details for players. The tournament runs November 11-15, 2026 at Ariake Tennis Forest Park. Day-by-day schedules and court assignments come from the organiser, not from this guide.

The Coliseum and the outer courts can be used differently from one day to the next. Spectators following a particular player or nation usually start at the tournament desk or the posted order of play when they reach the park.
