## A Wednesday night of hurdy-gurdy and blast beats

Eluveitie plays Razzmatazz on December 2, 2026. That date falls on a Wednesday, which shapes the whole evening, from the crowd to the last metro home.

The bill has three bands from three countries. Swiss folk metal leads, Swedish industrial metal sits in the middle, and Finnish melodic death metal fills out the night. Few venues in the city suit a show like this better than Razzmatazz, the long-running club on the edge of Poblenou.

## Who's on stage

Eluveitie formed in Winterthur in 2002, founded by frontman Chrigel Glanzmann. Their sound mixes melodic death metal with instruments you rarely see next to a wall of amplifiers. Expect to see some of these on stage:

- Hurdy-gurdy
- Bagpipes
- Tin whistles and flutes
- Fiddle

Some of their lyrics are in reconstructed Gaulish, the extinct Celtic language of ancient Switzerland and France. That's why parts of the crowd sing along phonetically to words nobody speaks anymore.

**PAIN** is the industrial metal project of Peter Tägtgren, the Swedish musician and producer better known for Hypocrisy. Expect pounding electronic rhythms built for jumping. **Wolfheart**, led by Finnish guitarist Tuomas Saukkonen, plays cold, melodic death metal that fans often call "winter metal". It's a fitting opener for a December night.

## Inside Razzmatazz

Razzmatazz occupies the industrial building that once housed Zeleste, a key venue in Barcelona's live music scene from the 1980s. It now holds several separate rooms. Sala 1 (also called "Razz Club" on some bills) is the large main hall used for touring bands of this size.

The main room is a big, dark, high-ceilinged space. There's a raised balcony area and bars along the sides. The floor is standing only, so the front half usually turns into a pit when the bagpipes kick in. People who want a clear view without the shoving tend to drift towards the back or the raised sections.

## Getting to Poblenou and back

The venue sits on Carrer dels Almogàvers, with a second entrance on Carrer de Pamplona. This is a few blocks inland from the beach in the old factory district of Poblenou.

- **Marina (L1, red line):** about a five-minute walk
- **Bogatell (L4, yellow line):** a similar distance on the other side
- **Llacuna (L4):** handy if you're heading back along Rambla del Poblenou

Barcelona's metro stops running around midnight from Monday to Thursday. For a Wednesday show with three bands, that matters. Many attendees leave the encore and walk straight to Marina. Others ride the NitBus night buses, which cover the main avenues until morning, or take a taxi from Avinguda Diagonal or Carrer de Pujades.

## How a metal night at Razzmatazz works

The venue's own website handles ticket sales for its concerts, and that's where most fans buy. Tickets are usually scanned from a phone at the door. Bag checks at the entrance are normal, so people who travel light get through the line faster.

A few habits are worth knowing:

- **Paying at the bar:** card and contactless payments are standard across Barcelona, and phone payments work fine at the bar.
- **Tipping:** at a concert bar it isn't expected. Leaving the coins from your change is plenty.
- **Coats:** December evenings by the sea are cool, but the hall gets hot fast. Regulars tend to use the cloakroom rather than holding a jacket in the pit.
- **Pit etiquette:** Spanish metal crowds are loud and physical but look out for each other. If someone goes down, the people around them pick them up.

Fans who want a spot near the barrier usually queue on Almogàvers well before doors open. The street has the low warehouse fronts typical of the area, and the line forms along the pavement. Folk metal shows often draw costumes, kilts and band shirts from decades of touring, so the queue itself is half the scene.

## Before and after in Poblenou

Poblenou has a very different feel from the Gothic Quarter. The streets are wide and grid-like, and old brick chimneys stand between new tech offices. **Rambla del Poblenou** is the neighbourhood's pedestrian spine, lined with bars and restaurants. It's a ten-minute walk from the venue and a common place to eat before the show.

Barcelona dines late, and many kitchens don't get busy until after 9pm. On concert nights, many people eat before the support acts or grab something on the Rambla. **Bogatell beach** is a short walk south. **Parc del Centre del Poblenou** and the Torre Glòries tower lie a little further north, for anyone making a day of it in the district.
