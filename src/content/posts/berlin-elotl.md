## A beef roulade braised like birria

Start with the name. *Elotl* is the Nahuatl word for a tender ear of corn, the root of the Spanish *elote*. It tells you before you sit down that Mexico sets the terms here, even when the dish is German.

That's the idea behind chef Franco Yahir's Schöneberg opening. He takes dishes most Berliners grew up on and runs them through Mexican and Latin American cooking. His CV covers two very different Berlin kitchens: Bar Normal and Frea, the zero-waste restaurant in Mitte.

## Reading the menu

You'll recognise the German half of each dish name, and the other half is the twist. Two dishes show how it works:

- **Rinderroulade Birria**: Rinderroulade is thin beef rolled around a filling and slowly braised, a Sunday-lunch staple in German homes. Birria is the chile-heavy braise from Jalisco, usually served with its rich cooking broth. Here the two become one dish.
- **Quarkknödel with Chile Ancho**: Quarkknödel are soft dumplings made with quark, the fresh German curd cheese, and often served sweet. Ancho is the dried poblano, more smoky and raisin-like than hot.

The menu is new and will probably change, so treat these as examples, not a promise. If you already know the German originals, ask your server which dishes stray furthest from them. Those are the ones that show what the kitchen can do.

## Booking, paying and the bill

New Berlin restaurants with a chef people already know fill up fast, and a walk-in table isn't guaranteed. Here's how to get one and how to settle up:

- **Book ahead.** Use the restaurant's own website or social channels, and check the current hours there too. Both are still settling at a place this new.
- **Ask about cards before ordering.** Plenty of Berlin restaurants still prefer cash, or only take some cards.
- **Splitting is normal.** When the bill comes you'll be asked *"Zusammen oder getrennt?"* (together or separate). Separate checks are routine.
- **Tip as you pay.** Tell the server the total you want to pay, including roughly 5 to 10 percent. Leaving coins on the table is less common.
- **Settle up yourself.** Servers rarely bring the bill unasked, so say *"Zahlen, bitte"* when you're ready.

Weeknight bookings are usually easier to get than Friday and Saturday ones. That's normal for any newly opened Berlin restaurant, not a quirk of this one.

## Making an evening of Schöneberg

Schöneberg is a residential district in the west of the city, made up of grand old apartment blocks, corner bars and leafy squares. It feels slower than Mitte or Kreuzberg. Look up the exact address when you book, then plan your U-Bahn or S-Bahn route from there. The district has several stations across the U1, U2, U3, U4 and U7 lines.

Around the restaurant you'll find:

- **Winterfeldtplatz**, home to one of Berlin's best-known farmers' markets on Wednesday and Saturday mornings.
- **Nollendorfplatz**, the centre of the city's historic gay neighbourhood and still full of bars.
- **Akazienstraße and Goltzstraße**, both lined with cafés and small shops, good for a drink before dinner.
- **Rathaus Schöneberg**, the town hall where John F. Kennedy gave his "Ich bin ein Berliner" speech in 1963.

Give dinner two hours, and leave time afterwards for a drink on one of these streets.
