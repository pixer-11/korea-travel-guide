## A city-state on a volcanic ridge

Yagul sits on a rocky spur above the Tlacolula Valley. The Zapotec lived here for well over a thousand years, and the city peaked after Monte Albán declined. It was still occupied when the Spanish arrived.

It belongs to the UNESCO World Heritage listing "Prehistoric Caves of Yagul and Mitla in the Central Valley of Oaxaca." The caves in the surrounding hills hold some of the earliest evidence of plant domestication in North America. Even so, you can often walk Yagul's stone courts with only a handful of other visitors around.

## What you'll actually walk through

The site rises in tiers, and each one is steeper than the last.

- **The ball court:** a long I-shaped court with sloping stone walls, often cited as the second largest in Mesoamerica. It sits lower down and is the first big structure you reach.
- **Palace of the Six Patios (Palacio de los Seis Patios):** a tight labyrinth of rooms and courtyards built from fitted stone, thought to be an elite residence.
- **The tombs:** several are cut into the patio floors. Tomb 30 has carved stone heads on its facade.
- **The fortress (La Fortaleza):** a rough scramble up the ridge to the summit walls. From the top you can see the whole valley floor, and on clear days you can make out the cave-pocked cliffs of the UNESCO zone.

There is almost no shade. You need sturdy soles for the loose rock on the upper path.

## Getting there from Oaxaca

Take Highway 190 east toward Mitla. Yagul's signed turnoff is a few kilometres past Tlacolula de Matamoros.

- **By car:** it's about 40 minutes from the city. A paved road runs about 1.5 km uphill from the highway to the gate.
- **By bus or colectivo:** catch any Mitla-bound service and ask the driver for the "crucero de Yagul." From the turnoff you walk up the access road, which is exposed and climbs gently.
- **By taxi:** shared or private taxis wait in Tlacolula and can drop you right at the entrance.

## Timing your Yagul morning

Google currently lists 10am–4pm daily. Opening hours at archaeological sites change with the season and close on some holidays, so confirm on INAH's site that week.

- **Arrive close to 10am.** You'll do the fortress climb before the valley heats up, and you'll have time left for a second stop.
- **Pay at the booth.** The ticket price is posted at the entrance. Carry pesos in cash, because card payment can't be relied on at small rural sites.
- **Bring water and a hat.** The site has no café.
- **Stay off the walls.** Keep to marked paths and don't climb the ball court slopes. Custodians will call you back.
- **Combine stops.** Go on a Sunday and you can start at the Tlacolula market for barbacoa, then come up to Yagul. Or carry on east to Mitla's carved mosaic fretwork, where the site and town are both much busier.

Yagul is the quiet one on the Tlacolula road. Because it gets far fewer visitors than Mitla or Monte Albán, you can often stand in the ball court with only birds and wind for company.
