## A rock garden that drops toward the Mersey

The gardens are built into a slope. Alderman James Fletcher Moss gave the land to the people of Manchester in 1919. His old home, the Parsonage, sits right next door with its own walled garden. Paths zigzag down a south-facing hillside of rockwork, alpine planting and small ponds, and the flood meadows of the River Mersey open out at the bottom.

The gardens have a second claim to fame. Emily Williamson founded what became the RSPB in 1889 at her house here in Didsbury, and a statue of her now stands in the grounds. Lower down you'll find tennis courts and other sports facilities, and there's a café for tea. Give yourself an hour for the gardens, or two if you add the riverside.

## Getting to Stenner Lane

The address is 18 Stenner Lane, M20 2RQ, just off Wilmslow Road at the southern end of Didsbury village.

- **Tram:** take the Metrolink East Didsbury line to Didsbury Village. From there it's about a 15-minute walk south along Wilmslow Road, past St James' Church.
- **Bus:** frequent Wilmslow Road buses run from Piccadilly Gardens and the Oxford Road universities to Didsbury.
- **Car:** parking near the gate is limited, and the surrounding streets are residential. On a weekend afternoon, the tram is the less stressful choice.

## Walking the slope the way regulars do

The gardens are free and open 7am to 8:30pm daily. There's no ticket and no booking; you just walk in off Stenner Lane.

Timing matters more than anything else here. Any weekday between 7am and 8pm is calm. On Saturdays and Sundays, arrive between 7am and 9am. After 10am, families and dog walkers fill the narrow rock-garden paths until around 6pm.

- **Stay on the paths.** The alpine beds are shallow planting on stone, and one step off the path does damage.
- **Pay by card.** The café, like most in Didsbury, takes contactless. Tipping at a counter is optional.
- **Wear shoes with grip.** The stone steps get slick after rain, and the meadows below flood when the river is high.
- **Keep your voice down near the ponds.** Birdwatchers and photographers wait there for herons and wildfowl.

## Past the bottom gate

Keep going downhill and the gardens run out into Stenner Woods and the Mersey Valley. Riverside paths follow the embankment in both directions, and the flat meadows are a good place for a longer walk after the tight switchbacks of the rock garden.

Back at the top, St James' Church and the Parsonage gardens are a two-minute detour. From there, Wilmslow Road leads back into Didsbury village, which has plenty of pubs and independent cafés for lunch. If you arrive by tram, a sensible loop is village, church, gardens, river, then back.
