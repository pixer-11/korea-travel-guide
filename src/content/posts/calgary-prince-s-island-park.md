## An island you reach on foot

Downtown Calgary stops at the river. Walk north from the office towers through Eau Claire and the street grid runs out at the Bow, where a footbridge carries you over a narrow lagoon channel onto Prince's Island Park. No cars come onto the island. Once you cross, it's grass, gravel paths, cottonwoods and moving water.

The Bow wraps around the north side, wide and fast and a cold glacial green-blue. The skyline sits just behind you to the south. That contrast is the whole appeal: you're a few minutes from downtown offices and standing on a riverbank.

## From sawmill to city lawn

This ground was industrial before it was a park. The island takes its name from Peter Prince, whose Eau Claire and Bow River Lumber Company ran a sawmill here. The channel that separates the island from the shore was dug for the mill's work.

Today the old mill site is open lawn, picnic areas and looping trails. The city has turned it into downtown Calgary's main outdoor gathering spot, and it's one of the most reviewed and best-liked parks in the city. Nobody would call it a secret, so plan around the crowds.

## What you'll find on the island

The island is small enough to loop in under an hour at an easy walk. Most of its space is open lawn, with a few distinct areas:

- **The riverside path** along the north shore, where the current runs close to the edge
- **The lagoon side** facing Eau Claire, calmer water lined with trees
- **The main lawn and stage area**, where the big summer events set up
- **Picnic spots** shaded by cottonwoods
- **River Café**, the restaurant on the island, which you'll want to check ahead for its current hours and reservations

The Bow River Pathway runs past both ends of the island. Head west along the south bank and you reach the red Peace Bridge, Santiago Calatrava's helix-shaped footbridge, in a few minutes. Cross it into Sunnyside, and Kensington's cafés and shops are a short walk further on.

## Getting onto Prince's Island Park

The street address is 698 Eau Claire Ave SW, though you won't arrive at a door. Several pedestrian bridges link the island to the downtown bank, and the main crossings come off the Eau Claire side. One more bridge connects to the north shore.

From the downtown CTrain stations along 7 Avenue, you're looking at a 10–15 minute walk north through the core. Chinatown is just east of Eau Claire if you want to combine the two. Cyclists can ride in along the river pathway system from either direction. Driving means downtown paid parking, so check the posted rates at whichever lot you choose.

## Timing the lawn and the paths

The measured foot traffic is easy to read. On weekdays and weekends alike, the calmest window is 7am–9am. At that hour you mostly share the paths with runners and cyclists commuting along the river, and the light comes low across the water.

Try not to arrive on a weekend after 11am. From then until 10pm the park is at its busiest, the bridges slow down and good picnic shade is gone. Google currently lists the island as open 24 hours, but city park rules and event closures can change that, so check the City of Calgary's park page before planning a late visit.

Summer is when the island fills. It's the home ground of the Calgary Folk Music Festival in July, and Canada Day crowds pack in on July 1. Theatre Calgary's Shakespeare by the Bow also runs here in summer. During ticketed events, parts of the island are fenced off, so look at the event calendar before you go.

## Using the island the way Calgarians do

The river looks inviting on a hot afternoon. Don't get in. The Bow is cold and fast, and on summer days you'll watch rafters and floaters drifting past with life jackets on. If you're tempted, join them on a proper float trip with the right gear rather than wading in from the bank.

A few habits will make the day easier:

- **Bring your picnic with you.** Grab food in Eau Claire or downtown first, then carry it across
- **Keep dogs leashed.** Calgary's off-leash parks are signed, and this one is on-leash
- **Stay to the right on the pathway.** Cyclists move fast along the river, and they will ring a bell
- **Watch for geese.** They graze the lawns, so look before you spread a blanket
- **Pack layers.** Even in summer, evenings by the water cool off fast once the sun drops behind the towers

If you're short on time, give it an hour: cross the lagoon bridge, walk the north shore, and finish at the Peace Bridge.
