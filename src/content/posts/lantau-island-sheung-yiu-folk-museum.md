Sheung Yiu Folk Museum is in Sai Kung Country Park, in the New Territories, near the Pak Tam Chung entrance to the park.

## What you're actually looking at

The museum occupies a walled Hakka village built in the late 1800s by the Wong clan, who worked the land here and fired bricks at a small kiln nearby. The Antiquities and Monuments Office restored it in the 1980s, keeping the original granite-block walls, the gated entrance, and the internal courtyard layout intact.

Inside the low, tile-roofed buildings you'll find the rooms laid out as a working household would have used them: a kitchen with a wood-fired stove, a bedroom with simple wooden furniture, and storage areas holding actual farming implements like ploughs, sieves, and grain baskets. Everything is labelled in English and Chinese, and the whole site is compact enough to see properly in under an hour.

## Getting there

The museum sits inside Sai Kung Country Park, reached via the MacLehose Trail's Stage 2 or by road from Sai Kung town. Most visitors combine it with a walk from Pak Tam Chung, where minibuses from Sai Kung town (routes running from the Sai Kung bus terminus) drop off near the park entrance.

From Pak Tam Chung it's a flat, easy walk of about 20 minutes along a paved path to reach the museum. There's no MTR station nearby; the practical route from Hong Kong Island or Kowloon is bus or taxi to Sai Kung town, then minibus onward.

## When to go

The museum runs 10am to 6pm every day except Tuesday, when it's closed entirely. Because it's under-the-radar compared with Hong Kong's bigger museums, you're unlikely to encounter real crowds at any hour, but weekday mornings right after opening are your quietest bet.

Weekends bring more foot traffic since the surrounding country park is a popular hiking start point, especially for people heading onward to High Island Reservoir or the East Dam. If you want the museum to yourself, arrive on a weekday.

## Reading the village like a local would

There's no admission fee and no ticket booth to navigate, so the main skill here is pacing yourself. Walk the rooms in order rather than doubling back; the layout follows a logical progression from communal courtyard to private living quarters to storage and work areas.

- Look for the kiln remains near the entrance, easy to miss if you head straight for the main buildings
- Read the tool labels before guessing what each implement did; several look similar but served different farming stages
- Keep voices low inside the smaller rooms, since acoustics carry in the stone-walled spaces
- Bring water and sun protection if you're walking in from Pak Tam Chung, as the path has little shade

Photography is generally fine throughout, though flash isn't necessary given how well-lit the rooms are by their window openings.

## Pairing it with the rest of Sai Kung

Because the museum is small, almost nobody treats it as a full day's outing on its own. Most visitors fold it into a longer country park walk, stopping here as a 30-45 minute detour on the way to or from the trailheads at Pak Tam Chung.

Sai Kung town itself, a short bus ride back toward the harbour, is worth the return trip for its seafood restaurants and waterfront promenade. If you're building an itinerary, treat Sheung Yiu as the quiet, low-key middle chapter between a hike and a seafood dinner, not the headline event.

## Before you set out

Double-check opening hours before travelling, particularly if your trip falls near a public holiday, since museum schedules in Hong Kong sometimes shift around those dates. Confirm the Tuesday closure applies to the week you're visiting, and if you're combining the museum with a hike, check trail conditions too, since parts of the MacLehose Trail can close temporarily after heavy rain.
