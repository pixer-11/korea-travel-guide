## Two centuries on one strip of Fangbang Road

Shanghai Old Street is a single long lane. It follows Fangbang Middle Road through the old walled-city district of Huangpu. The frontages were rebuilt around the turn of the millennium in period styles. Walk from east to west and the architecture changes as you go.

The eastern end, closest to Yu Garden, has upturned eaves, carved timber and dark roof tiles in the Ming and Qing manner. Further west the style shifts to later Qing and Republican-era shopfronts. Here you get stone facades, tall windows and painted signboards.

It is a reconstruction, not a preserved quarter, and it doesn't pretend otherwise. What you get is a walkable run of teahouses, craft shops and snack counters. It tends to feel calmer than the packed bazaar lanes a few minutes away.

## Getting to Fangbang Middle Road

- **Metro:** Yuyuan Garden Station (Lines 10 and 14) is the closest. From there it's a short walk south to Fangbang Middle Road.
- **Taxi or DiDi:** show the driver 上海老街 or 方浜中路. The address is listed under Bao Dai Long, Sipailou, Huangpu.
- **On foot:** the Bund is about a 15 to 20 minute walk east. It's easy to combine the two in one afternoon.

Opening times vary by shop and none are published for the street as a whole. Check the individual teahouse or museum you have in mind before going.

## What lines the lane

Most frontages are small independent shops. Expect:

- Teahouses with upstairs windows over the street
- Stalls selling fans, chopsticks, seals and calligraphy brushes
- Antique and curio shops, many of them reproduction pieces
- Snack windows selling Shanghainese sweets and dumplings

The City God Temple and Yu Garden sit just off the eastern end. Old Street works well as the approach to both, or as the way out once you've done them. Give the lane itself about an hour. Allow longer if you stop for tea.

## Paying, haggling and timing on Old Street

The rules here are the Shanghai rules. Mobile payment runs almost everything: most counters show a QR code for Alipay or WeChat Pay. Both apps now let foreign visitors link an international card, so set this up before you arrive. Carry some cash for the odd stall that won't take a scan.

- **Bargain at curio and souvenir stalls.** The first figure is an opening offer, and walking away politely is normal. Teahouses and snack counters charge fixed menu prices.
- **"Antique" usually means reproduction.** Buy something because you like it, not as an investment.
- **Ask before photographing** shopkeepers or craftspeople at work.
- **Don't tip.** Tipping isn't expected in teahouses or shops.
- **Go early.** Yu Garden's tour groups spill into the eastern end as the day goes on. Starting at the quieter western end and walking toward the garden usually keeps you ahead of them.
