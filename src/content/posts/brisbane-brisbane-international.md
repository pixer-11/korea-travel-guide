## Nine days on the Tennyson hardcourts

Brisbane starts the tennis year. For nine days, from January 3 to 11, 2027, the Brisbane International fills the Queensland Tennis Centre with players working their way into form before the Australian Open in Melbourne.

The tournament carries both ATP and WTA draws, so men's and women's matches share the same grounds and the same schedule. Courts are blue outdoor hardcourts, similar to the surface used at Melbourne Park. Many players treat this week as their real rehearsal for the first Grand Slam of the season.

## The Queensland Tennis Centre and Pat Rafter Arena

The venue sits on the Brisbane River in Tennyson, a leafy suburb roughly 8km southwest of the city centre. Its centrepiece is Pat Rafter Arena, named after the Queensland-raised two-time US Open champion. The stadium holds around 5,500 people, so even the back rows sit fairly close to the baseline.

Around the main stadium you will find a group of outside show courts and practice courts. This is where the tournament feels most open. You stand at a fence a few metres from a player drilling serves, or you grab a bench seat for a first-round match between two players you may never have heard of.

- **Pat Rafter Arena:** the feature matches, with reserved seating
- **Outside courts:** earlier rounds, doubles and qualifying, usually open seating
- **Practice courts:** warm-ups and hitting sessions, often the closest look you get at top players

## Getting to Tennyson

Rail is the simplest way in. Yeerongpilly station, on the Beenleigh and Gold Coast lines, is the nearest stop. From there the walk to the gates is short. Trains from Central and Roma Street stations take around 15 minutes, and Brisbane's public transport runs on the Translink network.

Driving is possible, but on-site parking is limited during the tournament week. Most visitors coming from the CBD, South Bank or Fortitude Valley take the train. If you are staying near the river, rideshare drop-offs along Fairfield Road are another common option.

## Courtside etiquette between the changeovers

Tennis has rules for spectators, and Brisbane's ushers follow them strictly. Plan your movements around the game so you aren't stuck standing at a stairwell for a whole set.

- **Move only at changeovers.** Ushers hold you at the aisle until players sit down after odd-numbered games or between sets.
- **Silence during points.** Clapping a good rally is welcome once the point is over. A ringing phone or a shout during a serve draws looks from everyone around you.
- **Pay by card or phone.** Australian venues, this one included, run largely on tap-to-pay. Tipping at food and drink stalls is not expected.
- **No flash photography** while play is under way.

A ground pass is the flexible choice: it lets you wander between outside courts. Arena seats are the way to see the headline matches. Regulars often combine the two, spending the day session outside and saving the arena for the evening.

## Brisbane in January: sun, humidity and the afternoon storm

This section is about weather, not tennis. The tournament falls in the hottest and most humid part of Brisbane's year, and the subtropical sun is strong even under light cloud. Daytime temperatures in the low 30s are normal, and the outside courts give you little shade.

Summer also brings sudden afternoon thunderstorms to southeast Queensland. They can roll in fast and stop play on the outside courts for a while. Pat Rafter Arena has a retractable roof, so feature matches often keep going in a downpour while the outer courts are covered.

Seasoned attendees dress for both kinds of weather:

- A broad-brimmed hat and SPF 50+ sunscreen, since Australian sunscreen ratings are high for a reason
- A refillable water bottle
- A light rain layer stuffed in the bag for the late-afternoon storm

Night sessions are noticeably more comfortable. That is part of why the evening arena matches are so popular.

## A short history of the curtain-raiser

The Brisbane International started in 2009. It combined older Australian summer events into one combined tournament on the new Tennyson site. Since then it has become a regular warm-up stop for leading players. Andy Murray, Serena Williams and Roger Federer are among its past champions, and Federer won his 1,000th tour-level match here in the 2015 final.

The event's place on the calendar shapes the tennis you see. Some players come with fresh off-season fitness and play loose, aggressive tennis. Others are managing their bodies with Melbourne in mind. That's why the early rounds can throw up results that are hard to predict.

## Making a day of it by the river

Tennyson is a quiet residential suburb, and most of the eating and drinking happens inside the venue during tournament week. For dinner, visitors usually head back toward the city after the day session. They go to South Bank's riverside restaurants, West End's cafés and bars on Boundary Street, or Fortitude Valley for later nights.

Since the gap between sessions is short, people who hold tickets for both often stay on the grounds. Those who leave between sessions tend to go somewhere close to the rail line. On free mornings, the Brisbane River's CityCat ferries and the South Bank Parklands lagoon are an easy way to cool off before an afternoon of tennis.

The organiser publishes the full draw, the daily order of play and ticket options on the official Brisbane International website. The tournament also sits within the wider Australian Open Series calendar on Tennis Australia's channels.
