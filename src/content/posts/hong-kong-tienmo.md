## A late table on Staunton Street

Staunton Street cuts across the slope above Central. It sits in SoHo, the steep grid of restaurants and bars named for being "south of Hollywood Road". The Central–Mid-Levels Escalator crosses it overhead, and on most evenings people step off the walkway straight into dinner.

TIENMO is a Korean street food concept from Timo Kim. It works more like a night out than a dinner booking. You get playful Korean comfort dishes on one side and Champagne and cocktails on the other, all in a room that takes its energy from nightlife rather than fine dining.

Expect noise and a crowd that settles in for the evening. This is not the place for a quiet business dinner.

## Fried, spicy and sparkling

The pairing is the point. Korean street food grew up in pojangmacha tents and market stalls. Most of it is salty, fried or chilli-hot, and it was made to go with a drink. Champagne's acidity and bubbles cut through that kind of richness, which is why the match has caught on in bars well beyond Seoul.

TIENMO calls its cooking playful comfort food, so expect familiar Korean flavours handled with a bit of wit, not a formal tasting menu. The restaurant is new, and the menu may still be changing. Check its own social channels for current dishes rather than relying on lists elsewhere.

## Getting up the hill

SoHo is steep, so use the transport built for it.

- **MTR:** Central Station (Island Line and Tsuen Wan Line). Leave by Exit D2 for Queen's Road Central, then walk a block west to the bottom of the escalator.
- **Central–Mid-Levels Escalator:** this covered chain of moving walkways runs downhill during the morning commute and uphill for the rest of the day. By evening it carries you up to Staunton Street without the climb.
- **From Sheung Wan:** Sheung Wan Station (Island Line) is a steeper walk up via Aberdeen Street. It makes sense if you're starting at PMQ.
- **Taxi:** ask for Staunton Street, SoHo. The lanes are narrow and many are one-way, so being dropped at the nearest corner is often quicker.

## Booking, paying and ordering

New openings change things fast. Hours, booking policies and walk-in rules often shift in the first months, so confirm all three before you head up. SoHo fills on Friday and Saturday nights, and booking is the safer bet if you're going then.

- **Payment:** cards and mobile wallets are widely accepted at Central bars. An Octopus card covers the MTR and escalator-side convenience stores.
- **Service charge:** Hong Kong sit-down venues usually add a service charge to the bill. Tipping on top is optional, so check the bill before adding more.
- **Share the table:** Korean drinking food is made for groups. Order several plates and put them in the middle rather than one each.
- **Pour for others:** in Korean drinking culture you top up your companions' glasses, not your own. Nobody will insist, but it suits the food.

## Building a night around SoHo

TIENMO sits in one of the easiest parts of Hong Kong to wander on foot, and most stops are a few minutes apart.

- **Tai Kwun**, on Hollywood Road, is the former Central Police Station compound. It now holds galleries, courtyards and bars, which makes it good for an early drink.
- **PMQ**, on Aberdeen Street, is the former Police Married Quarters, now full of design studios and small shops. It's best seen before dinner.
- **Man Mo Temple**, further west on Hollywood Road, has huge incense coils hanging from its ceiling.
- **Elgin Street and Peel Street** are lined with bars if you want another round after eating.
- **Lan Kwai Fong** is downhill toward Central if the night is still going.

A loose order works well. Start at Tai Kwun, walk up to TIENMO to eat, then let the slope pull you back down toward Lan Kwai Fong.

## Who will like it

TIENMO suits groups who order loudly, couples who want something livelier than candlelight, and anyone who likes Korean flavours in a bar rather than a barbecue house. It is newly opened and getting attention, so come for the energy and expect company.

If you want a slow, quiet meal, book somewhere else in SoHo for dinner. Come here afterwards for the Champagne.
