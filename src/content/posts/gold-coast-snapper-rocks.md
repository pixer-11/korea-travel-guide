## Where the Gold Coast runs out

Snapper Rocks is where the coast stops. A low shelf of dark volcanic rock pushes out into the Pacific at the very bottom of Queensland. Behind it, the curve of Rainbow Bay runs west toward Greenmount and Coolangatta, and the Gold Coast skyline stands in a pale row on the horizon to the north.

The address is Snapper Rocks Rd, Coolangatta, and the road ends almost at the water. Over the hill is Point Danger, and past that the state line into New South Wales. People come for three things: to watch some of the best-known surfing in Australia, to wander the rocks and pools, and to look north up the whole Gold Coast.

## Watching the Superbank

Most people are watching the surf. The long right-hand wave that peels off the point is the top of the "Superbank". This sand bar formed after the Tweed River sand bypass project began pumping sand north across the border. On a good swell a wave can carry a surfer from the rocks down past Rainbow Bay and on toward Greenmount.

The break has hosted the World Surf League's Championship Tour many times. On an ordinary morning the lineup is still full, and the standard of surfing is high. The grassy slope and the rocks above the break give you a front-row view without getting wet.

## Rocks, pools and the headland walk

At low tide the rock shelf opens up. This is when the rock pools show at their best, with shallow water trapped among the ridges of rock. Wear shoes with grip, because the rock is uneven and the lower sections stay slick with weed and spray.

From the rocks, a short uphill walk takes you to Point Danger. Up there you'll find:

- The Captain Cook Memorial and its lighthouse, set right on the state border
- Lookouts facing south over Duranbah Beach and the Tweed River mouth
- A clear line of sight north along the Gold Coast
- Humpback whales passing offshore during the winter migration, roughly June to November

A curious detail: Queensland doesn't observe daylight saving and New South Wales does. So in the warmer months you can cross into a different time zone by walking to the end of the headland.

## Reading the water at Snapper

This is a working surf break, and the unwritten rules are strictly kept. If you surf, the paddle-out usually starts with a jump off the rocks into the current. Watch how others time it with the sets before you try it yourself. Priority goes to the surfer closest to the peak. Dropping in on someone here gets you noticed quickly.

The current is the thing to respect. A strong sweep pulls north along the Superbank, and it can drag weak swimmers and tired surfers a long way down the beach. If you only want a swim, walk to Rainbow Bay and stay between the red-and-yellow flags. Surf lifesavers from Rainbow Bay Surf Life Saving Club patrol there.

For watchers, arriving around sunrise is a common habit. Dawn is when many surfers paddle out before work, and the light comes in low across the water. Weekends and contest days fill the headland and the small car parks quickly, so plan to walk a few streets.

## Getting to Coolangatta's southern point

Snapper Rocks is one of the easiest big-name spots on the coast to reach from the airport. Gold Coast Airport is at Bilinga, a short drive north. By car, follow the Gold Coast Highway south into Coolangatta, then take the roads toward Rainbow Bay and Point Danger.

Parking sits close to the rocks, but there isn't much of it. When it's full, park near Rainbow Bay or Greenmount and walk along the beachfront path. Local buses run the length of the Gold Coast Highway to Coolangatta. Check the Translink journey planner for the current routes, since the G:link light rail doesn't reach this far south.

## Pairing it with the rest of the southern beaches

An hour or two covers the rocks and Point Danger. The coastline makes it easy to stay the whole day. Walk north along the Superbank and each beach has its own feel:

- **Rainbow Bay**, the patrolled swimming beach right below Snapper
- **Greenmount**, a sheltered corner with a grassy park behind the sand
- **Coolangatta**, the town beach, with cafés and shops a block back
- **Kirra**, famous for its own barrelling point break further along

Heading south instead, Duranbah Beach sits just over the border at Point Danger. Past it you reach Tweed Heads, the New South Wales twin town. Check the tide times before you go. They decide whether the rock pools show, and whether the Superbank is working.
