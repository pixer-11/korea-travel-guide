## A new table at The Orient

Sanjiva opened in September 2026. It sits on the ground floor of The Orient Jakarta, so you walk in from a hotel lobby, not off a street-food lane. That tells you what kind of evening this is: a sit-down meal of contemporary Indonesian cooking, served with the polish of a hotel dining room.

The restaurant describes its idea as storytelling. Every meal is meant to link three things: the ingredient, the kitchen that cooks it and the table where you eat it. You come here to find out where your food comes from as well as to eat it.

## Ingredient, kitchen, table

That three-part idea is the reason to choose Sanjiva over the many other places to eat Indonesian food in Jakarta. Most meals in this city are fast and wonderfully casual. Sanjiva asks you to slow down and follow each plate from start to finish.

We have not been given a menu, and the restaurant is too new for us to name its signature dishes. So go in curious. Questions are welcome in a room built around storytelling, and the staff are there to answer them. Good ones to ask:

- Which region of the archipelago a dish comes from
- Where the main ingredient is grown, caught or raised
- How the kitchen has reworked a traditional recipe
- Which sambal or condiment the kitchen recommends with each course

## What "contemporary Indonesian" usually means

Indonesia is a cuisine of thousands of islands, not one national menu. Sumatran, Javanese, Balinese, Sulawesian and Eastern Indonesian cooking each have their own spice pastes, souring agents and ways with coconut, chilli and smoke. A contemporary kitchen usually starts from those regional roots and then rethinks how a dish is plated or paced.

Expect courses that are smaller and more composed than a home-style spread, where everything lands on the table at once. Treat this as a description of the style, not a promise about Sanjiva's menu. The restaurant's own channels are where you'll see what's actually being served this season.

## Getting to The Orient Jakarta

The Orient Jakarta is in the city centre, among the office towers and hotels where most international visitors stay. The easiest way there is ride-hailing. Grab and Gojek both work across the city, and a car drops you at the hotel entrance, so you don't have to cross a big road on foot in the heat.

Type "The Orient Jakarta" as your destination, not the restaurant name. A new restaurant may not be pinned yet, but the hotel is. Once you're inside, ask the front desk for Sanjiva on the ground floor.

Jakarta traffic is the real timekeeper here. Weekday rush hours turn short distances into long crawls, and rain makes it worse. Leave much earlier than the map suggests, especially for a dinner booking.

## Booking a table at Sanjiva

Plan this meal before the day. A restaurant this new is still settling its schedule, and early hours can change from one week to the next. Check current opening times and make a reservation through The Orient Jakarta's official website or the restaurant's own channels before you set out.

When you book, mention any dietary needs. Indonesian cooking often relies on shrimp paste, peanuts, coconut and chilli, so a kitchen telling a story through its ingredients will want to know about allergies early. If you're asking about halal status or alcohol, ask the restaurant directly. Don't assume either way.

Here's how a bill usually works in Jakarta's hotel restaurants:

- Prices on the menu are often marked "++", which means a service charge and government tax are added at the end
- Cards are widely accepted in hotel dining rooms, and many places also take QRIS mobile payments
- Because service is already on the bill, extra tipping is optional; rounding up or leaving a little for excellent service is a kind gesture, not an expectation
- Smart-casual dress fits the setting; beachwear and gym kit don't

The posted menu shows the current prices and whether the "++" applies. Check it there, not online.

## Pacing the evening

Leave time for a story-led meal. A dinner that walks you from ingredient to table works best unhurried, so don't book Sanjiva as a quick stop before a late flight or a show across town.

A good plan is to make it the main event of your night. Arrive a little early, since traffic makes timing unreliable anyway. Order whatever the staff point you towards and let the courses set the pace. If you're traveling with people who are new to Indonesian food, this is a gentle introduction. The explanations come with the plates, and the heat level can usually be talked through before you order.

## Who Sanjiva suits

Sanjiva makes sense for travelers who want one considered Indonesian meal in Jakarta, alongside the warungs and food courts that make up most of the city's eating. It also works for a business dinner, a celebration, or a first night when you're staying nearby and want to stay off the roads.

It's less suited to anyone after a fast, cheap plate or late-night street food. For that, Jakarta's street stalls and malls are the better bet. Come to Sanjiva for the story behind the food, and check the restaurant's latest details before you commit your evening.
