## Getting to Nagoya

Nagoya sits on the Tokaido Shinkansen line, roughly 100 minutes from Tokyo and under an hour from Osaka on the fastest trains. Chubu Centrair International Airport, on an artificial island in Ise Bay, is the main gateway for international visitors and connects to Nagoya Station by train in about 30 minutes.

Within the city, the subway network (Higashiyama and Sakura-dori lines are the workhorses) reaches most central neighborhoods. The venue plan covered Aichi Prefecture, not just Nagoya proper, and events in satellite cities like Toyota or Okazaki add transit time.

- Shinkansen: Tokyo–Nagoya in \~100 min, Osaka–Nagoya in \~50 min
- Chubu Centrair Airport → Nagoya Station: \~30 min by Meitetsu train
- Local subway: Higashiyama Line and Sakura-dori Line cover the city core

## How to visit like a local

Nagoya locals treat big events with practical calm rather than frenzy: efficient queuing, not chaotic crowds. IC transit cards (Manaca, or nationally compatible Suica/Pasmo) work across subway, bus, and most convenience stores, which beats fumbling with cash tickets.

Meals near venues mean long lines at peak session breaks; locals sidestep this by eating slightly off the main strip, a five-minute walk from the stadium gates, where prices stay normal and seats are open. Nagoya specialties are what the city is known for here, not generic stadium concessions.

Security screening lines lengthen fast in the final 30 minutes before big sessions, so locals build in a buffer rather than turning up right at the scheduled start. Trains run precisely on schedule, but that also means missing one costs real time. Last return trains matter for evening events out in the satellite cities.

The most common visitor assumption is that all venues sit inside central Nagoya. Many didn't — events in outer Aichi cities carry an extra hour of travel.
