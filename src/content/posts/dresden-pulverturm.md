## A table beside the Frauenkirche

The address does most of the selling here. Pulverturm sits at An der Frauenkirche 12, so the sandstone dome of Dresden's most photographed church is just outside the door. Neumarkt fills the space between them, with its rebuilt baroque facades in pastel ochre and cream.

The name points back to the city's old fortifications, where a powder tower once guarded this corner of the Altstadt. Today the building's age is part of the meal. Expect an old-fashioned dining room in a centuries-old structure, set up for a long lunch or a full evening.

## What lands on the table

The kitchen cooks international dishes rather than sticking to one regional style. That makes it an easy choice for a mixed group where one person wants Saxon comfort food and another does not. The menu changes, so check the current version on the restaurant's own site before you go.

Price level sits on the higher side for central Dresden. Treat it as a proper sit-down meal, not a quick refuel between museums. Starters, mains and dessert with drinks is the natural pace, so plan around 90 minutes to two hours at the table.

Things worth deciding before you sit down:

- Lunch or dinner, since weekend afternoons are the crush hours
- A local drink, such as a Saxon wine from the Elbe valley or a regional beer
- Room for dessert if you are not planning a separate coffee-and-cake stop

## Timing around the crowds

This is one of the most-reviewed restaurants in the Altstadt, and it shows on weekends. Try not to turn up between 2pm and 10pm on Saturday or Sunday without a booking. That is the measured peak, and the whole evening service falls inside it.

The weekend doors open at 11am, so a late-morning lunch lands before the busiest window starts. Weekday service begins at noon on Monday, Tuesday and Thursday. Wednesday is the trap: the restaurant is closed all day.

Hours at a glance:

- **Monday, Tuesday, Thursday:** noon to 11pm
- **Wednesday:** closed
- **Friday and Saturday:** 11am to midnight
- **Sunday:** 11am to 11pm

Friday and Saturday run until midnight. That gives you room for a late dinner after a performance at the Semperoper, around ten minutes' walk away.

## Booking, tipping and paying the bill

Book first, then walk over. Reserving a table is the single most useful step for a place this popular, especially on Friday and Saturday nights.

Once seated, German restaurant habits apply:

- **Wait to be seated** rather than picking a free table yourself.
- **Ask for the bill** ("Die Rechnung, bitte"). Servers will not bring it unprompted, because lingering is normal.
- **Splitting is easy.** The server will ask "Zusammen oder getrennt?", meaning together or separate. Separate bills per person are routine.
- **Tip as you pay.** Rounding up or adding around 5 to 10 percent is usual. Say the total you want to pay out loud when you hand over cash or card, rather than leaving coins on the table.

If paying by card matters to you, ask when you book. Some German restaurants still lean towards cash.

## Getting to Neumarkt

Neumarkt is pedestrianised, so you arrive on foot whichever way you come. The closest tram stops are Pirnaischer Platz and Altmarkt, each a short walk away across the Altstadt. From Dresden Hauptbahnhof, the walk up Prager Straße and past Altmarkt takes roughly 20 minutes.

Parking in the old town is limited and mostly in underground garages. Public transport is the calmer option, particularly on weekend evenings.

## Building a day around it

The location makes Pulverturm easy to slot between sights. Before or after your meal, you can reach several highlights within a few minutes:

- **Frauenkirche:** step inside the rebuilt church next door, or climb the dome for views over the Elbe.
- **Fürstenzug:** the long porcelain-tile procession of Saxon rulers on Augustusstraße.
- **Brühlsche Terrasse:** the raised riverside promenade, a good after-dinner walk above the Elbe.
- **Albertinum:** modern and Romantic art at the end of the terrace.
- **Zwinger and Semperoper:** a short walk west, past the Residenzschloss.

A sensible rhythm on a weekday is a morning at the Zwinger, then lunch here from noon. Spend the afternoon on the Brühlsche Terrasse as the light drops over the river.
