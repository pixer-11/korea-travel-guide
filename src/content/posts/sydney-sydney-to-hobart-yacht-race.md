## Boxing Day on the harbour

The start draws a crowd big enough to fill both sides of the harbour. The Sydney to Hobart Yacht Race leaves Sydney Harbour on 26 December 2026. It is a Boxing Day fixture that has run since 1945, and the fleet races about 628 nautical miles down the coast and across Bass Strait to Hobart, Tasmania.

The yachts start on the harbour inside Sydney Heads, so the action happens close to shore. Spinnakers and mainsails crowd the start area, and a flotilla of spectator boats follows along behind. Within minutes the fleet rounds the turning marks and heads out between North and South Head into open ocean.

The Cruising Yacht Club of Australia organises the race and publishes the start details, the entry list and live tracking on its official site.

## Where the crowds stand

You can watch for free from the harbour foreshore. Each vantage point gives you a different view:

- **Nielsen Park, Vaucluse:** grassy slopes and a netted beach looking straight onto the start area. This is the classic family picnic spot.
- **Watsons Bay and South Head:** the walking track past Hornby Lighthouse looks down on yachts as they leave the Heads.
- **North Head, Manly:** sandstone cliffs on the opposite side, with a wide view of the fleet turning south.
- **Bradleys Head and Middle Head:** lower crowds on the northern shore, with partial views down the harbour.
- **Dover Heights and the Eastern Suburbs cliffs:** good for following the boats down the coast after they exit.

Shade on the headlands is mostly whatever the scrubby trees and your own umbrella provide. The cliff tops are exposed sandstone with sheer drops and fences in places.

## Ferries, buses and the water

Driving to the eastern headlands on race day doesn't work well. Streets around Vaucluse and Watsons Bay are narrow and residential, so most spectators take public transport.

- **Watsons Bay:** ferries leave from Circular Quay, and the trip itself passes the start zone.
- **Nielsen Park and Vaucluse:** buses run along New South Head Road and Vaucluse Road from the city.
- **Manly and North Head:** the Manly ferry from Circular Quay, then a bus or an uphill walk to the headland.

To watch from the water, charter operators and ferry companies run dedicated spectator cruises that follow the fleet. These sell their own tickets, and the operators set their own prices. Private boats are kept outside the exclusion zones around the start lines, and police and maritime patrol vessels enforce them.

## Claiming a patch of headland

Locals treat race day as a picnic day as well as a sporting event. Families usually spread blankets on Nielsen Park's slopes and the South Head track hours before the start. Many make a day of it with leftover Christmas food and a portable radio or a phone streaming the broadcast commentary.

- Bring your own water and food. Kiosks at Watsons Bay and Nielsen Park get swamped.
- A pair of binoculars makes it easier to pick out sail numbers and match them to the entry list.
- The live tracker on the organiser's site shows which yacht is which once the fleet spreads out.
- Sun protection matters. The December sun on the open headlands is fierce, and there's little natural cover.

After the start, the crowds all head for the ferry wharves and bus stops together. Many spectators stay for a swim at Nielsen Park or a late lunch at Watsons Bay and let the queues thin out first.
