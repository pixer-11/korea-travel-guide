Its Main Hall seats a few thousand and is where the city puts its biggest visiting orchestras, opera productions and marquee jazz names. ## Getting there and finding your seat

- **Bus:** Numerous city buses stop along Sejong-daero directly in front of the complex.

## Why a jazz date fills this particular hall

Sejong Cultural Centre's Main Hall wasn't designed with jazz trios in mind first; it's a multi-purpose house that is also home to the Seoul Philharmonic and hosts touring musicals. But it has become the default room for international jazz and vocal acts because Seoul has few other spaces that combine that seat count with hall acoustics rather than arena sound reinforcement. A jazz show here plays more like a recital than a rock gig: seated, attentive, with minimal crowd noise between songs.

That shapes the atmosphere inside. This isn't a standing-room club. Seating is assigned, the house stays quiet during numbers, and applause is saved for song breaks rather than kept up throughout.

## Concert-hall etiquette at Sejong

Korean concert-hall audiences for seated jazz and classical bookings tend to be formal by international standards. A few habits are standard:

- Phones go fully silent, not just on vibrate; screen light during a quiet piano passage draws visible attention in this hall.
- Latecomers are typically held at the back until a natural pause in the programme, not seated mid-song.
- Photography during the performance is generally restricted, with signage at the seats. Flash is never allowed, and photos are usually not allowed at all once the lights go down.
- Tickets are usually named and scanned at entry rather than relying on paper stubs alone, and some ticket types require the same ID used at booking.
