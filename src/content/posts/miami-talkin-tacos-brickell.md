## Why go

Brickell doesn't lack for slick restaurants with good lighting and mediocre food, so a taco counter that's consistently packed and consistently well rated stands out. Talkin' Tacos Brickell has built its name on exactly that combination: a big, loud, neon-lit room serving tacos, tequila, and mezcal. It's the kind of place where a work happy hour bleeds into a birthday party bleeds into a 1am plate of queso, and nobody at any of those tables looks out of place.

## Getting there

The address is 97 SW 8th St, sitting right where Brickell meets the edge of Downtown Miami — an easy landmark if you're coming from either the financial-district high-rises or the Miami River side. The closest transit is the Brickell Metromover station, part of Miami's free automated people-mover, about a five-to-ten-minute walk north. If you're coming from further out, the Brickell Metrorail station connects to the Metromover and gets you within the same walk.

Rideshare is simple too — 8th Street is a major east-west artery, so drop-off is straightforward, though parking on the street in Brickell at night is competitive; a paid lot nearby is the more relaxed option if you're driving.

## What to eat

The name is the menu, more or less: a long roster of tacos, from classic carne asada and al pastor to Miami-inflected riffs, meant for ordering several at once and sharing across the table. Expect a raw bar and starter section too — think ceviche and guac made table-ready — alongside a tequila and mezcal list long enough to build a whole evening around.

Portions are built for grazing rather than a single neat entrée, so the move is to order in rounds: a couple of tacos each to start, see what the table wants seconds of, then decide if queso or a dessert taco is happening. Price-wise it sits in the mid-range bracket — not a cheap taqueria, but nowhere near Brickell's steakhouse territory either.

## When to go

Because the kitchen keeps long hours, this is one of the rare Brickell spots that works equally well as a late breakfast-taco stop, a weekday lunch, an after-work bar, or a proper late-night food run after the clubs start clearing out. If you want the room close to yourself, the late-morning-to-early-afternoon window on a weekday is your best bet before the lunch rush builds.

The loudest, most packed stretch is Friday and Saturday night, roughly from happy hour through midnight, when Brickell's after-work and bar-hopping crowds converge here at once — if you'd rather talk to the person across the table than shout, aim either side of that window.

## How to visit like a local

Nobody's going to pretend this is a secret — it's one of the best-known taco rooms in Brickell, and it fills up accordingly, so the smartest move is timing rather than discovery. Walk-ins are the norm, but on weekend nights expect a wait at the door; putting your name in and grabbing a drink at the bar while you wait is standard practice here, not a workaround.

Payment is straightforward card-or-cash like any US restaurant, and tipping the usual 18–20% is expected on the pre-tax total. If you're solo or in a pair, the bar counter is often faster to seat than the dining room and gives you a front-row view of the taco assembly line. The most common visitor mistake is treating this like a quiet sit-down dinner reservation spot — it's built for noise, music, and a rotating crowd, so come ready to share plates, order in rounds, and not expect an early, quiet exit.

Late night — after 10pm on a weekday, once the office crowd has cleared out — is when the room feels most like a neighborhood bar that happens to serve excellent tacos, rather than a happy-hour scrum.
