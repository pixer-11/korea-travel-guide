The Parc de l'Orangerie is Strasbourg's oldest public garden, laid out in the late 1600s and reworked in the early 1800s under Josephine de Beauharnais, and the Temple de l'Amour is one of its quieter architectural leftovers. It's a small round pavilion, columned in the neoclassical style that was fashionable for garden follies across France in that period, set among trees rather than at any grand axis or entrance. You won't find it on the park's main sightlines; it rewards people who wander off the central paths.

## Finding it inside the park

Don't expect signage pointing you straight there.

The Temple sits within the wooded, less-manicured stretches of the Orangerie, away from the stork enclosures and the lake that draw most visitors. Enter from Avenue de l'Europe or Allée de la Robertsau and head toward the park's quieter interior paths rather than the areas closest to the restaurant and playgrounds. A paper or phone map of the park helps, since the paths curve and there's no ticket booth or staff to ask.

## Why this one is worth the detour

Strasbourg has grander monuments: the cathedral, the Palais Rohan, the half-timbered houses of Petite France. A small garden folly can't compete with any of those on scale, and it isn't trying to.

What it offers instead is stillness. Few visitors make it this far into the park, so you often get the structure and the surrounding clearing to yourself. It's the kind of stop that rewards someone who already knows and loves the Orangerie, not a first-time Strasbourg visitor ticking off landmarks.

## When to go

Because it's open 24 hours every day, there's no opening rush or closing time to plan around here. That said, the Orangerie itself is a public park, and daylight hours are the practical window: paths can be unlit and uneven after dark, and the folly has no lighting of its own.

Spring and early autumn mornings are pleasant for the walk in, with mild temperatures and fewer joggers and dog walkers on the paths near the temple. Midday in summer brings more foot traffic to the park generally, though the temple's off-path location means it rarely feels crowded even then.

## Making it part of a longer visit

Treat the Temple de l'Amour as one stop on a slower loop of the Orangerie rather than a standalone errand.

A natural route:

- Start near the park's stork enclosures and lake, the busiest section
- Walk the wooded paths toward the park's interior, watching for the columned pavilion
- Sit at the Temple de l'Amour for a few quiet minutes
- Loop back past the Pavillon Joséphine, another Napoleonic-era garden structure

This turns a five-minute photo stop into a comfortable hour or more of walking, with the temple as a calm midpoint rather than the whole point.

## Getting there and moving through quietly

The Parc de l'Orangerie sits northeast of Strasbourg's city centre, a walk or short tram ride from the cathedral area. Tram lines serving the Robertsau/Orangerie side of the park are the easiest public transport option; check current routes before you go since tram lines in Strasbourg have shifted numbering over the years.

Once inside, this is a spot to treat gently:

- Keep noise down; there's no barrier or attendant, and the appeal is the quiet
- Stick to worn paths rather than cutting across planted beds to reach it
- Visit on foot or by bike; the park's interior paths aren't set up for cars
- Skip picnicking directly at the structure itself, and use the park's benches and lawns instead

Because it's under-the-radar rather than a marked sight, part of the experience is simply the search. Don't rush it, and don't expect a plaque with much detail; the pleasure here is a quiet corner of a very old park, not an information point.
