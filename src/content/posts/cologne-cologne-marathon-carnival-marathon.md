## A race on Cologne's loudest date

The date matters more than anything else here. In Cologne, November 11 belongs to carnival. The city treats it as a public celebration even though shops and offices stay open. The Carnival Marathon was set for that day in 2026, a Wednesday. That put the race in a city full of people dressed as clowns, bees and cowboys from mid-morning on.

It was one of several city road races on the German calendar that autumn. Most of the others are held on quiet Sunday streets. This one fell on a day of brass bands, trams packed with costumes and a cathedral square full of people singing in Kölsch, the local dialect.

## Eleven past eleven on the eleventh

Cologne's carnival season, the "fünfte Jahreszeit" or fifth season, officially opens at 11:11am on 11.11. Eleven is the fool's number in Rhineland carnival, and the timing is not a joke. The main organised opening takes place on the Heumarkt in the Altstadt. It features stage acts and the season's first big "Kölle Alaaf!" from the crowd.

The other hotspot is Zülpicher Straße in the student quarter near Zülpicher Platz. It fills so densely that the city has closed it off in recent years and redirected people to overflow areas. Between the race and the hotels, the Altstadt and the "Zülpicher" are the two places where walking slows to a shuffle.

The party continues into the evening in pubs across the centre. It is not limited to the 11:11 moment.

Most visitors come in by rail. Köln Hauptbahnhof sits directly beside the Cathedral, and the station exit opens straight into the Altstadt. ICE trains connect it with Frankfurt, Düsseldorf, Brussels and Amsterdam.

- **From Cologne Bonn Airport:** S-Bahn S19 and regional trains run to Köln Hbf in roughly a quarter of an hour.
- **From across the Rhine:** Köln Messe/Deutz station is one walk over the Hohenzollern Bridge from the Cathedral.
- **Around the city:** KVB trams and U-Bahn lines cover the centre; the Deutschlandticket is valid on them.

On 11.11, regional trains from Bonn, Düsseldorf and the surrounding towns carry carnival crowds into the city all morning. Runners usually allow extra time for this. Driving into the centre is the slowest option of all on this date.

## Running and watching in November

November in the Rhineland is usually cool, grey and often damp, and the sun sets around a quarter to five. Runners' kit leans towards long sleeves, gloves and a throwaway layer for the start.

Spectators face a different problem: standing still for hours. Warm layers and a waterproof jacket matter more than anything else. Many locals simply wear a costume over a thick jumper, which keeps them warm and blends them in. Groups that split up often meet at a fixed landmark. Phone signal is unreliable in dense crowds, and the Cathedral's west front is hard to miss.

## Kölsch, costumes and the word "Alaaf"

Carnival in Cologne has its own rules. A few of them come up all the time:

- **Say "Alaaf", never "Helau."** Helau is the cheer in Düsseldorf and Mainz, and Cologne takes the rivalry seriously.
- **Kölsch comes in a Stange,** a slim 0.2-litre glass. In a traditional brewery pub, the waiter, called a Köbes, keeps replacing an empty glass until the drinker lays a beer mat on top.
- **Tabs are often marked on that beer mat.** Guests settle up at the end. Cards are widely accepted, but small pubs and street stalls may still prefer cash.
- **A "Bützchen",** a quick peck on the cheek, is a carnival custom. A polite no is fine.
- **Costumes are normal for adults.** Wearing one makes a visitor part of the day rather than an observer.

Runners usually keep the Kölsch for after the finish. Brewery pubs around the Altstadt, such as Früh am Dom near the Cathedral, are usually packed on 11.11. Runners often celebrate in their own neighbourhood instead.

## After the finish line

The Cathedral is the obvious first stop, and its twin spires stand right beside the main station. Behind it, the Hohenzollern Bridge carries thousands of love padlocks over the Rhine. On the far bank, the KölnTriangle viewing platform in Deutz looks back across the river at the whole old town.

For tired legs, the Rheinauhafen to the south is calmer. The Chocolate Museum sits there on a peninsula, a short walk along the riverside promenade. The Altstadt itself, between the Heumarkt and Alter Markt, is the place for watching carnival rather than escaping it.

On this date, the city's celebration usually lasts far longer than any race, and many visitors stay out to see it.
