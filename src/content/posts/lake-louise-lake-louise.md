## The shore and the glacier

The colour is real. Meltwater from Victoria Glacier carries fine rock flour into the lake, and the suspended silt turns the water a milky turquoise once the ice goes out, usually in late May or June.

The postcard view is from the eastern shore. Mount Victoria and its glacier fill the far end of the valley. Behind you is the long façade of the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise. The paved strip along the water is where nearly everyone stops. Walking a few minutes in either direction thins the crowd fast.

## Trails that start at the water

You can be on a trail in under five minutes from the car. The main options:

- **Lakeshore Trail:** flat, about 2 km each way along the north shore to the inflow flats beneath the glacier. Suits any fitness level.
- **Lake Agnes Tea House:** roughly 3.5 km one way and about 400 m of climbing, passing Mirror Lake. The historic teahouse serves tea and simple food. It is cash-friendly and has no road access.
- **Plain of Six Glaciers:** continues past the end of the Lakeshore Trail to a second teahouse with close views of the icefall.
- **Canoeing:** the boathouse on the shore rents canoes in summer. Rates are posted at the dock.

In winter the lake freezes into a skating rink below the Chateau, and the Ice Magic festival brings ice carvings in January. Avalanche terrain closes some trails, so check the Parks Canada trail report first.

## Reaching Lake Louise

Lake Louise is 57 km west of Banff town on the Trans-Canada Highway (Highway 1), and about two hours from Calgary. Leave the highway at Lake Louise village, then follow Lake Louise Drive up the hill for 4 km.

Without a car, Roam Transit runs a regional route from Banff in season. Moraine Lake is 14 km away, but its road has been closed to private vehicles since 2023. Pair the two lakes with Parks Canada's connector shuttle or a licensed bus operator.

## Parking, passes and the shuttle

Planning matters more here than at almost anywhere else in the Rockies.

- **Park pass:** you need a valid Parks Canada pass for every day you are in Banff National Park. Buy it online or at the Banff and Lake Louise visitor centres.
- **Parking:** the lakeshore lot charges for parking in season and often fills early on summer mornings. The pay machines and Parks Canada's site list current rates.
- **Shuttle:** Parks Canada shuttles run from the Park and Ride at the Lake Louise Ski Resort. Seats are released online ahead of the season and go quickly. Reserve your outbound time, because the return trip works first-come.
- **Timing:** the area itself is open 24 hours, so sunrise is your best chance at a parking spot and still water. Shuttle and lot seasons change every year, so confirm dates on the official site.

At the lake, stay behind the shoreline railings and off the fragile ground near the inflow. Pack out everything you bring. Bears use these valleys too, so carry bear spray on the trails. Group-travel restrictions sometimes apply on the Plain of Six Glaciers and Lake Agnes routes.
