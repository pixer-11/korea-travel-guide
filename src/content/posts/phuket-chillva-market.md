## What Chillva actually is

Chillva Market sits in Ratsada, on the edge of Phuket Town, built from repurposed shipping containers stacked and painted into stalls, bars and a stage area. It's not a temple market or a tourist-trap night bazaar; it reads more like a semi-permanent festival ground. Vendors sell grilled seafood, skewered meats, Thai desserts, fresh fruit and handmade wares side by side. With well over 10,000 reviews and a 4.4 rating, it's one of the most-reviewed night markets on the island, and it draws a real crowd, not just cameras.

## Layout and what to eat

The container stalls ring a central open area with communal tables and often live music on a small stage. Food stands run down one side, drink and craft stalls down another.

- Grilled seafood skewers and whole fish
- Thai sweets (khanom) served in small portions
- Fresh-cut fruit cups
- Handmade jewelry, clothing and small craft items from independent sellers

Walk the full loop once before ordering. Portions are small and shareable, so it rewards grazing across five or six stalls rather than committing to one.

## When to go

Chillva runs Monday through Saturday, 5pm to 11pm, and is closed all day Sunday. Weekend evenings between 6pm and 10pm are the busiest stretch, when tour groups and local crowds overlap and tables fill fast.

Arrive right at 5pm on a weekday if you want breathing room and first pick of what's fresh off the grill. By 7pm on a Friday or Saturday, the communal tables are mostly claimed and the queue at popular grill stalls starts to stretch.

Because it's closed Sundays, don't build a Sunday-night itinerary around it. Plan for a weeknight instead if crowds bother you.

## Ordering, seating and getting around the crowd

This is a well-visited market now, not a quiet local secret, so plan around the crowd rather than expecting to avoid it. Most stalls are cash-based; small bills help since change can be slow at busy grill stands. Order at the stall counter, take a number or wait for your name, and carry food back to a shared table yourself. Nobody assigns seating, so claim a table before you queue for food if the market is already filling up.

Live music and the container-bar setup mean it gets loud after 8pm, especially on weekends, which suits groups but is worth knowing if you want conversation over dinner. Grab an early plate and eat first if you'd rather talk than shout.

Chillva is a short taxi or grab ride from Phuket Town's old quarter, and it pairs naturally with an earlier walk through the Sino-Portuguese shophouse streets before heading over for dinner once the market opens at 5.
