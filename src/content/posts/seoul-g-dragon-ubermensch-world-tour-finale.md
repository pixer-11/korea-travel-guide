## A dome beside the Anyangcheon

Gocheok Sky Dome sits on the bank of the Anyangcheon stream in southwest Seoul. From the walkway it looks like a grey steel shell set down between apartment blocks and an elevated rail line. It opened in 2015 as Korea's first domed baseball stadium. On ordinary days it is home to the Kiwoom Heroes.

For a concert the field is filled with floor seating and the stands rise steeply on three sides. The roof is closed, so the sound stays inside and nothing depends on the sky. That matters in December. It is one of the few venues in Seoul large enough for a stadium-scale show that keeps the winter out.

## The tour this finale closes

This finale is the last stop of G-Dragon's 'Übermensch' world tour. *Übermensch* is the album he released in 2025, his first solo studio record in more than a decade. The tour carried it to arenas and stadiums abroad before coming back to Seoul.

G-Dragon, born Kwon Ji-yong, made his name as the leader of BIGBANG. Since the mid-2000s he has been one of K-pop's best-known producers and fashion figures. A hometown finale for an artist like this tends to draw fans from all over Asia and further away, and that shapes the whole day around the dome.

## Dates, tickets and where they live

The concerts fall in December 2026 at Gocheok Sky Dome. The official tour announcements from G-Dragon's management and the tour's ticketing partner carry the exact dates, seating maps and sale rules. Those sources are the reference point for anything about timing or price.

Big K-pop shows in Seoul usually sell through Korean ticketing platforms. These often split sales between a domestic window and a separate global window for buyers outside Korea. Fans abroad typically use the global page, which runs in English and takes international cards. Resale outside the official channel is a well-known risk for K-pop tickets. Name-matched tickets bought second-hand can be refused at the gate.

## Guil Station, Exit 2, and the bridge

Most people reach the dome by subway. **Guil Station** on **Line 1** is the closest stop. **Exit 2** leads onto a pedestrian route across the Anyangcheon, and the walk to the dome takes a few minutes. On concert days the whole route turns into one slow stream of people with lightsticks.

Line 1 is Seoul's oldest line and its branches can confuse visitors. Not every train stops where you expect. The trains that serve Guil run through **Sindorim** and **Yeongdeungpo**, which link to Line 2 and the rest of the network. Guil is only a stop or two from Sindorim.

- **From Hongdae or Gangnam:** take Line 2 to Sindorim and change to Line 1 towards Guil.
- **From central Seoul (Seoul Station, City Hall):** Line 1 runs directly towards Guil.
- **By taxi:** the drivers know "Gocheok Dome" (고척돔, *Gocheok-dom*), but the roads around the venue clog up badly after a show.

## Gate day at Gocheok: IDs, merch and lightsticks

Korean concert entry runs on paperwork. Tickets are commonly tied to the buyer's name, and staff often compare ID at entry or at the ticket booth. Foreign visitors usually carry their passport rather than a photocopy. Collection booths for physical tickets and global-window passes set up outside the dome, and those lines move one person at a time.

The official merchandise booth, called the "MD" booth in Korean fan slang, draws the longest queue of the day. Fans often join it hours before the show starts. Items such as the official lightstick can sell out partway through. Card and mobile payment are normal at the booth.

Inside, a few habits are common to most dome shows in Seoul:

- **Lightsticks** are often synced wirelessly to the show, so they change colour on their own once paired to your seat.
- **Fan chants** are shouted in set places in the songs, and nobody minds visitors joining late.
- **Filming rules** are announced on screen. Staff do act on recording bans.
- **Seating** follows the zone and number on the ticket. On the floor, sections are fenced and people stay in their own.

## December on the Anyangcheon

Seoul in December is properly cold. Daytime temperatures often sit near or below freezing, and the open walkway by the stream catches the wind. The show itself is under a roof. Everything around it is outside: the merch line, the ticket booths, the queue to get in.

Fans who wait for hours usually wear thermal layers and gloves that work with a phone screen. Many carry the disposable heat packs (핫팩, *hat-paek*) sold at every convenience store in Korea. Once you are in your seat, those layers become a lap pile, because a full dome warms up quickly. Lockers near the venue fill fast on big concert days.

## After the encore

Getting out of the dome takes a while. Thousands of people head for the same Line 1 platform at Guil, and station staff sometimes control the flow at the gates. Some fans walk towards **Gaebong Station** instead. Others wait half an hour in a nearby café or restaurant until the crowd thins.

For dinner and somewhere to stay, visitors usually look one or two stops up the line. **Yeongdeungpo** has the Times Square mall and plenty of late-opening barbecue and chicken places. **Sindorim** has the D-Cube City complex and hotels right on the Line 1 and Line 2 interchange. Fans planning to explore Seoul for several days often stay in **Hongdae**, which is direct on Line 2 from Sindorim and stays lively well after the show ends.

The neighbourhood right around the dome is mostly residential. Food options grow thinner the closer you get to the stadium. The convenience stores along the streets near Guil Station are the reliable choice for a hot drink or a triangle kimbap during the merch wait.
