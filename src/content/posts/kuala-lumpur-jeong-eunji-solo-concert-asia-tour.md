## One voice, one standing hall

Apink concerts are big group shows. This one is different.

On October 11, 2026, Jeong Eunji brings her solo Asia tour to Zepp Kuala Lumpur. It is her first solo concert in the city. The room is a purpose-built live house, so most of the audience is close to the stage and her voice carries the whole night without five other members beside her.

That changes what you are there for. A group show is built around choreography and fan chants. A solo vocalist's set leans toward ballads, live phrasing and the talk between songs. The tour has not shared a setlist here, so treat any song list you see online as fan speculation.

## Who is on stage

Most international fans know Eunji as Apink's main vocalist. The group debuted in 2011, and its fandom is called Pink Panda. Many Malaysian concertgoers came to her through acting first.

- **Reply 1997** (2012): her breakout drama role as Sung Si-won, a die-hard fan of the idol group H.O.T.
- **"All For You"**: her duet with co-star Seo In-guk from that drama's soundtrack.
- **Solo music**: she debuted as a soloist in 2016 with the mini album *Dream* and its title track "Hopefully Sky".

You will likely see Apink lightsticks in the crowd. Many solo-show fans are long-time Pink Pandas.

## Inside Zepp Kuala Lumpur

Zepp Kuala Lumpur belongs to the Japanese Zepp chain of live-music halls. It opened in 2022 as part of BBCC, the mixed-use development built on the site of the old Pudu Prison. The hall is a black-box space with a raised stage at one end. The floor can be cleared for standing or filled with seats, depending on how each show is set up.

The layout shapes your night. On a standing floor, the people in the front rows are usually those who joined the queue earliest. Elevated rear sections give shorter fans a clear sightline. Your ticket shows which zone you are in, and entry is usually called zone by zone.

## The Hang Tuah approach

Rail is the easiest way in. Zepp sits inside BBCC on Jalan Hang Tuah, next to Hang Tuah station. Three lines stop there:

- **LRT Ampang Line**
- **LRT Sri Petaling Line**
- **KL Monorail**, which also stops in Bukit Bintang and KL Sentral

From the west, Merdeka MRT station on the Kajang Line is also within walking distance. On the way you pass the base of Merdeka 118, which is hard to miss. A Touch 'n Go card works on every line, and most visitors tap in with one instead of queuing for tokens.

E-hailing apps such as Grab are the usual fallback. After the encore, though, Jalan Hang Tuah fills with cars at the same moment. Many fans walk to the station or a nearby mall entrance before calling a car, which spares them the jam at the kerb.

## Lightsticks, freebies and the doors queue

Malaysian K-pop crowds share a set of habits. Knowing them makes the evening easier.

- **Queues form early.** On standing floors, fans typically line up an hour or more before doors. Numbered tickets set the entry order inside each zone.
- **Free gifts get handed out.** Fans often make and give away photocards, stickers and slogan banners near the entrance. Accept them with a thank-you. They are gifts, not merch for sale.
- **Slogan moments are planned.** Fan projects sometimes ask the crowd to raise a banner during one particular song. The person handing you the banner usually tells you which song.
- **Phones and cameras:** each show sets its own rules on filming and professional cameras. Staff point them out at the door, and the crowd follows them closely.
- **Merch lines** often open well before doors. Sizes and popular items tend to sell out first.

Malaysian crowds are polite and mostly bilingual. You will hear English, Malay, Cantonese and Korean fan chants in the same queue. Cards and e-wallets are accepted almost everywhere in BBCC.

## Before the show: from Pudu to Petaling Street

The neighbourhood is a large part of the night. Two of KL's best-known places to eat are within walking distance of the venue.

- **Petaling Street (Chinatown)**: about ten minutes on foot past Merdeka station. Stalls sell chee cheong fun, claypot dishes and herbal drinks, and a covered market runs down the middle.
- **Jalan Alor**: north toward Bukit Bintang. This evening food street is lined with plastic tables, grilled stingray and satay smoke.
- **LaLaport BBCC**: the mall in the same development as Zepp, with air-conditioned food courts and Japanese chains. It is the easiest choice if rain arrives.

The old Pudu Prison gate stood nearby until the site was redeveloped. Few traces remain, but it explains the long, walled plot that BBCC now fills.

## October in Kuala Lumpur

October falls in the inter-monsoon period. Late-afternoon thunderstorms are common, and they can be sudden and heavy. Fans waiting in an outdoor queue usually carry a compact umbrella or a poncho. Many also keep a dry layer in a bag, because the hall's air-conditioning feels cold after the humid air outside.

Evenings stay warm. Light clothing and closed shoes suit a packed standing floor. If you are flying in, Bukit Bintang and the KL Sentral area are where most out-of-town fans stay. Both are one or two rail stops from Hang Tuah.
