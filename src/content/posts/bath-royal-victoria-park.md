## Below the Crescent's curve

A long Georgian arc of honey-coloured Bath stone runs along the top of the lawn. That is the Royal Crescent, and Royal Victoria Park spreads out beneath it. Between the two is a ha-ha, a sunken wall that keeps the Crescent's private lawn separate from the public grass without blocking the view.

The park opened in 1830. Princess Victoria, then 11, opened it herself, and it was the first park to carry her name. An obelisk to her stands near the Royal Avenue entrance. Lime and plane trees line the main drives, and the grass rolls west and downhill towards the River Avon side of the city.

## What fills an afternoon here

The park covers enough ground for several separate visits in one day. You'll find:

- **Botanical Gardens** in the north-west corner, with rock gardens, a pond and labelled specimen trees. It has its own gate and the hours change with the season.
- **Boating pond** for small hired boats in the warmer months.
- **Skate park** and a big **adventure playground** next to it.
- **Tennis courts** and golf courses for casual rounds.
- **Great Dell**, a wooded hollow with tall conifers, quieter than the open lawns.

On calm summer evenings, hot-air balloons sometimes take off from the lawns and float out over the city roofs. Give it an hour if you're just walking through to the Crescent. Allow half a day with children or a picnic.

## Reaching Marlborough Lane

The park sits on the western edge of central Bath. From Bath Spa station it's a flat walk of about 20–25 minutes, either through the centre past the Circus or along the Upper Bristol Road.

The Circus and the Royal Crescent are both a few minutes away on foot, and many visitors link the three in one loop. Drivers will find pay-and-display bays on the park's internal roads, with tariffs on the machines. Bath's centre is tight for cars, though, and the city's park-and-ride buses usually make more sense.

## Getting the lawn without the crowd

Google currently lists the park as open 24 hours a day, every day of the week. Facilities inside it, like the Botanical Gardens, the boating pond and the courts, keep their own seasonal hours, so check the council's site for this month's times.

Weekends get busy. Try not to turn up between 10am and 7pm on a Saturday or Sunday, when the lawns below the Crescent fill with picnic blankets and the playground queues build. The quietest weekend window is 7am–8am, when you'll mostly share the paths with dog walkers and runners. On weekdays the measured lull comes late, 10pm–11pm.

A few habits worth copying:

- Spread out on the lawn below the ha-ha, not on the Crescent's private grass above it, which belongs to the residents.
- Photograph the Crescent from low on the slope. The curve fits in the frame better from there than from the pavement up top.
- Bring your picnic from the centre. Kiosk and café options inside the park are limited and run on seasonal hours.
- Book tennis courts ahead on summer weekends rather than hoping one is free.

A late-evening walk on a weekday gives you the lit Crescent with almost nobody else on the grass.
