## Eight voices on a Roman summer stage

It is one night, and it is July. On July 8, 2026, ATEEZ brings its world tour to Rome as part of Rock in Roma. That puts an eight-member K-pop act inside a festival better known for rock, rap and Italian headliners.

ATEEZ debuted in 2018 under KQ Entertainment. They built a reputation on stage performance more than anything else: tight, physical choreography and a live show that leans on volume. Their fans call themselves ATINY. A Rome date pulls them in from across Italy and from neighbouring countries too.

## Rock in Roma and its stages

Rock in Roma is the city's big outdoor summer concert series. It runs show by show rather than as one weekend festival, so each night has its own crowd and its own ticket. Its longtime home is the Ippodromo delle Capannelle, a horse-racing track off the Via Appia Nuova in south-east Rome.

The festival has also staged shows at other Roman venues over the years. The stage for a given night is printed on the ticket. Rock in Roma lists it alongside the date on its official site.

At the racecourse, you are standing on flat, open ground with the stands and the track around you. There is no roof and little natural shade. The ground fills from the barrier backwards, and food and drink stalls line the edges of the arena.

## How ATINY do a Rome show

K-pop concerts have their own etiquette. Italian festival crowds have theirs. On this night the two overlap, and knowing both makes the evening easier.

- **Lightsticks:** the official ATEEZ lightstick is known as the Lightiny. Fans hold it up for the whole set, and at many tour stops it syncs to the show's lighting.
- **Fan chants:** ATINY shout member names and set lines during the songs. Fans usually learn them from the group's official chant guides before the show.
- **Banners and slogans:** small printed slogans held up at agreed moments are common. Large signs that block the view behind are frowned on.
- **Free merch swaps:** many fans hand out photocards, stickers or keyrings they made themselves, known as "freebies". People swap them along the queue.
- **Language:** a simple "grazie" goes down well with staff at the gates and stalls. Most security and bar staff work in Italian first.

At the bars, Italian festivals often run on a token or prepaid-card system rather than cash at each counter. How it works on the night is explained at the venue. Card payment is widely accepted in Rome, while tipping is not expected for a drink handed over a counter.

## July after dark: Rome's late, hot concert hours

Rome in July is hot. Daytime highs regularly push past 30°C, and the city stays warm long after sunset. Sunset in early July comes around 8:45pm, so most of any summer show plays out in dusk and then darkness.

Romans move to that rhythm. They eat late, go out late, and spend the hot afternoon indoors where they can. Concertgoers usually do the same, keeping the slow heat of the afternoon for waiting and saving their energy for the set.

What fans typically bring to an open-air July show in Rome:

- a refillable water bottle, within whatever the venue's bag rules allow
- a hat or cap for the hours before sunset
- sunscreen, since the ground has almost no shade
- a power bank, because lightsticks, tickets and photos all run on phones and batteries
- a light layer for the late-night trip home

Rome's public drinking fountains, the cast-iron *nasoni*, run all over the city centre. Locals refill bottles at them all summer.

## Getting to the south-east of the city

If the show is at Capannelle, you are heading well out from the historic centre. The racecourse sits beyond the ring road in south-east Rome, past the Appian Way area.

Two public transport routes serve that side of the city:

- **Metro Line A** runs south-east from Termini towards Anagnina, with buses running on towards the Via Appia Nuova.
- **Regional train FL4** stops at Capannelle station, a short walk from the racecourse.

After a big show, taxis and ride apps around the edge of Rome are under heavy demand. Many fans plan their ride home around the last Metro A trains, or share a car with friends from the queue. If your ticket names a different Rock in Roma venue, the route changes completely. The address on the ticket is the one that matters.

## Making a day of it in Rome

A mid-week concert date gives visiting fans a reason to spend a few days in Rome. Most stay near Termini, in Monti or in San Giovanni. All three are on Metro Line A or close to it, the line heading towards the south-east.

The morning before a show is when the cool hours are. The Colosseum and the Roman Forum sit a short walk from Monti. The Baths of Caracalla and the start of the Appian Way are on the same south-eastern side of the city as Capannelle. The Pantheon and Trevi Fountain are more pleasant early in the day, before the July heat builds.

For food, Rome's pasta classics are easy to find near any of those bases: *cacio e pepe*, *carbonara* and *amatriciana*. Romans eat dinner late, so a post-show meal is not unusual in the centre. A slice of *pizza al taglio*, sold by weight, makes a quick pre-show lunch.
