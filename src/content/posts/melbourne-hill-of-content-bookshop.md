## Bourke Street's quiet end

Most visitors see Bourke Street as the mall: department stores, buskers and trams. Walk uphill past Swanston and Russell and it changes. At this end the street is lined with theatres and old espresso bars, and the State Parliament steps close off the view at Spring Street.

Hill of Content sits here at Shop 1/32 Bourke St, a general bookshop with every genre on the shelves. It's easy to walk past on the way to somewhere else. People who go in tend to praise the booksellers more than anything else: they know the stock and will talk you through it.

## Reaching the top of Bourke Street

The shop is in the CBD's Free Tram Zone, so getting there is easy.

- **Train:** Parliament Station on the City Loop. Take the Spring Street exit and walk a couple of minutes down Bourke Street.
- **Tram:** routes 86 and 96 run along Bourke Street. Get off at the Spring Street end.
- **On foot:** about 15 minutes east from Bourke Street Mall, uphill the whole way.

Close by you'll find the Princess Theatre and Parliament House on Spring Street, Pellegrini's Espresso Bar a few doors down at 66 Bourke St, and Chinatown one block north on Little Bourke Street. Give the shop 30–60 minutes and longer if you start chatting to the staff.

## Browsing with the booksellers

This is a shop where it pays to ask. Name a book you loved or a trip you're planning and the staff will suggest something. That's quicker than searching the shelves, and you'll find things an algorithm wouldn't show you.

- **Ask about Australian writing.** It's the obvious thing to carry home from a Melbourne bookshop, and the booksellers can point you past the airport bestsellers.
- **Ask whether they can order a book in** if it isn't on the shelf. Leave time for it to arrive before you fly out.
- **Pay by card or tap.** Contactless is normal in Melbourne shops, and nobody tips in a bookshop.

Browsing is part of the deal and nobody hurries you. Keep your voice down, though, because the people next to you are reading.

## Timing your visit around the theatres

At the moment the shop opens at 10am Monday to Saturday and at 11am on Sunday. It closes at 7pm Monday to Wednesday, 8pm Thursday to Saturday and 6pm on Sunday.

The late close from Thursday to Saturday is the useful part. With the Princess Theatre and other Spring Street venues nearby, you can browse here before an evening show and then get a coffee at Pellegrini's. Remember that Sunday is the shortest day. If you're heading over on a Sunday afternoon after the Fitzroy Gardens or the Treasury Gardens, leave yourself enough time before 6pm.

Weekday mornings just after opening are a sensible choice if you want the booksellers' full attention. Office workers from the Spring Street end of the city usually only come in at lunchtime.
