Piazza Marina looks like an ordinary rectangular square from a distance, until you spot the canopy rising above the perimeter railings. That's Giardino Garibaldi, and the trees are the reason it exists on anyone's list at all.

## The fig trees are the whole point

Two Ficus macrophylla, Moreton Bay figs planted in the 1860s, dominate the garden. Their aerial roots have grown outward for over a century, forming buttresses and arches thick enough to look architectural rather than botanical.

Locals sometimes call this Piazza Garibaldi interchangeably with the square itself, since the garden fills its centre. Benches sit in the shade beneath the canopy, and the ground beneath is dense with root structures rather than lawn. It's one of the most photographed tree canopies in the historic centre, so expect company.

## What else is inside

Beyond the figs, the garden holds a formal 19th-century layout: gravel paths, iron fencing, ornamental fountains, and a scatter of marble busts honouring Risorgimento-era figures, fitting given the park's name honours Garibaldi himself. A monument to the Palermo uprising of 1860 stands near one entrance.

It's compact, not a place to plan a picnic around. Most visitors walk the loop, look up at the canopy, read a plaque or two, and move on within twenty minutes.

## Getting there and timing your visit

The garden sits on Piazza Marina in the Kalsa quarter, a short walk from the waterfront Foro Italico and directly beside Palazzo Chiaramonte-Steri, the old Inquisition palace that's now a University of Palermo museum. Via Alloro and the Vucciria market area are both within a ten-minute walk.

- Hours: daily, 7:00 AM to 8:00 PM, every day of the week
- Entry: free, gated but unstaffed
- Time needed: 15-20 minutes
- Nearest landmark: Palazzo Chiaramonte-Steri, on the piazza's east side

Because it's gated to the opening hours above, don't route an evening stroll here after 8pm expecting access; you'll be looking through railings at the trees rather than walking under them. Morning light through the canopy, shortly after the 7am opening, is the best window for photos before tour groups from the cruise-ship crowd start arriving later in the day.

## Fitting it into a Kalsa walk

This is a very well-visited stop on the standard Kalsa walking route, not a quiet corner, so treat it as a five-minute pause rather than a destination. Combine it with Palazzo Chiaramonte-Steri next door, then continue toward the Vucciria market or down to the Foro Italico waterfront.

There's no ticket booth and no queue system: you walk in through the pedestrian gates, circle the paths, and walk out. Sundays bring flea-market stalls to parts of Piazza Marina itself, which makes the square livelier but the garden itself no busier than any other day, since its footprint and gates stay the same regardless of what's happening outside them.
