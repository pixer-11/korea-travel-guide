## A city rebuilt around one square

Mendoza was redrawn after the 1861 earthquake. Plaza Independencia became the centre of the new grid.

It covers four full city blocks, bounded by Chile, Rivadavia, Patricias Mendocinas and Espejo. Wide paths cut through it under tall plane trees, and irrigation channels (*acequias*) run along the kerbs outside. Those channels are what keep a desert city this green.

The semicircular fountain sits at the centre. Benches line the paths, and vendors set up along the walkways. It's one of the most-reviewed public spaces in Argentina, so expect company at any hour.

## What's on and under the plaza

The plaza has several layers to it. Look for:

- **Museo Municipal de Arte Moderno**, built below the plaza itself. Look for the entrance set into the ground.
- **Teatro Independencia**, the city's old theatre, on the Espejo side.
- **The Park Hyatt**, in the restored former Plaza Hotel, facing the square on Chile.
- **The craft fair**, with artisan stalls along the paths, mainly at weekends.
- **Paseo Sarmiento**, the pedestrian street of cafés that runs east from the plaza toward Avenida San Martín.

## The five-plaza walk

From the plaza, Mendoza's layout makes for an easy loop. One block off each corner sits a smaller satellite square:

- **Plaza España**, with blue-and-white tilework.
- **Plaza Italia**, the most heavily planted of the four.
- **Plaza Chile**, which honours Argentina's neighbour across the Andes.
- **Plaza San Martín**, closest to the banking district.

Walking all five takes about one to two hours at a relaxed pace. It's flat, mostly shaded, and covers the whole historic grid of the post-earthquake city. Avenida San Martín, the main commercial street, is a few blocks east.

## Keeping Mendoza's hours

The square never shuts, but the city around it keeps a different clock.

- **Siesta is real.** Many shops and museums close in the early afternoon and reopen in the evening. Check the museum's and theatre's hours before you go, because they set their own schedules.
- **Evenings are when people come out.** Families and couples fill the paths after the heat breaks, and dinner in Mendoza rarely starts before 9pm.
- **Weekends bring the stalls.** If you want the craft fair, plan your visit for Saturday or Sunday.
- **Paying vendors:** small stalls often prefer cash in pesos. Cafés on Paseo Sarmiento usually take cards, and in Argentina a tip of around ten percent is customary at sit-down places.
- **Watch the acequias.** These open channels sit right at the kerb edge, and visitors step into them after dark more often than you'd think.
- **Stay alert in crowds.** Keep phones out of back pockets, as you would in any busy main square.

The plaza is also the city's stage. During the Fiesta Nacional de la Vendimia, the grape-harvest festival in late summer, events and parades pass through the centre. Expect crowds and road closures if your trip falls in that season.
