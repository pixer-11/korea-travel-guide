## A pause on the Neergarh road

This is a viewpoint, not a building. It sits on Neergarh Waterfall Road near the village of Kyarki, where the road climbs into the forested hills above Rishikesh. Most visitors are on their way up to the falls, so the lookout gives you a reason to stop and look back down the valley.

Don't expect a ticket booth or a gift shop, because none is listed. What you get is height and space. The Ganga runs through these foothills of the Garhwal Himalaya, and from the ridge roads the river towns of Rishikesh are laid out far below you.

It gets only a few dozen reviews online, but they're consistently warm. That makes it one of the quieter stops around a town where Laxman Jhula and the ghats are packed most of the day.

## Getting up to Kyarki

Start from the Tapovan and Laxman Jhula side of Rishikesh. Take the Badrinath highway north along the river, then turn uphill onto the Neergarh Waterfall Road. Ask for "Neergarh jharna" (*jharna* is Hindi for waterfall) and drivers will know the road.

Your options:

- **Hired auto or taxi** from Tapovan or Laxman Jhula. Agree on a return trip with waiting time, because empty vehicles rarely pass on the hill road.
- **Rented scooter.** Rental shops are common in Tapovan. The road has tight bends, so only ride it if you're comfortable on mountain roads.
- **On foot** from the waterfall area, if you're fit and the weather's cool. Keep to the road edge.

For longer journeys, the nearest railhead is Yog Nagari Rishikesh station. The nearest airport is Jolly Grant at Dehradun.

## Timing the view

The season matters more than the hour. From October to March the air is usually at its clearest and the hills stand out sharply. Pre-monsoon heat often leaves a haze over the valley.

From July to September the monsoon turns the slopes green and swells the waterfalls below. It also brings landslides and slippery surfaces to Uttarakhand's hill roads. In those months, check road conditions in Rishikesh before you head up.

Light is softer early and late in the day. That's reasoning about the sun, not a promise about crowds. No opening hours are published for this spot, so treat it as open-air and check locally if you plan to arrive near dark.

## Making a half-day of it

The lookout works best as one stop in a loop, not as a destination on its own. Nearby:

- **Neer Garh waterfall**, a set of cascades and pools reached by a walking trail from the road. The entry fee is posted at the gate.
- **Laxman Jhula and Tapovan**, with cafés, yoga schools and the riverside walk, back at the bottom of the hill.
- **Ram Jhula and Swarg Ashram**, a short way downstream, where you'll find the ghats and evening aarti.
- **The Beatles Ashram** (Chaurasi Kutia) on the Swarg Ashram side, which is good for a slower afternoon.

A practical plan is to go up in the morning, stop at the viewpoint, walk to the falls, then come down to Tapovan for lunch.

## Stopping safely on a ridge road

The road is the main hazard here, not the view. Pull fully off the carriageway before you get out, and never stop on a blind bend. Drivers here sound their horns going into curves, and you should too if you're riding.

Rhesus monkeys are common around Rishikesh. Keep snacks inside a closed bag and don't feed them. Fed monkeys get bold quickly, and a grabbed phone at a cliff edge is gone for good.

Pack what you need, since nothing is listed at the lookout:

- Water, and a layer for the cooler air higher up
- Shoes with grip for any dusty or wet shoulder
- A bag for your own rubbish

## Manners in a hill village

Kyarki is a village where people live and work, not a resort, so behave like a guest. If you want to photograph people, houses or livestock, ask first. A nod and a gesture at the camera is understood everywhere.

Rishikesh is a pilgrimage town, and meat and alcohol are traditionally kept out of its centre. Keep that tone on the hill road too. Dress modestly and keep your music off.

UPI payments by QR code are widely accepted in town. Carry some small notes anyway, because signal can drop on the hill road and drivers out here often prefer cash. Settle any waiting-time arrangement before the vehicle leaves Tapovan, not at the top.
