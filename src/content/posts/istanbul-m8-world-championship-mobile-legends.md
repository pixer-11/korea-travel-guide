## A world final a long way from home

M8 lands somewhere new. Every earlier M-series World Championship was held in Southeast Asia, in cities like Jakarta, Manila and Kuala Lumpur, where Mobile Legends: Bang Bang fills arenas.

Istanbul is a different stage. The game's biggest followings sit in Indonesia, the Philippines and Malaysia, with strong support across East Asia too. That means many people in the stands are travelling a long way, and the city's own crowd is meeting the tournament for the first time.

The M8 World Championship is the top international MLBB tournament of the year. Teams that qualify through regional leagues meet in one bracket for the world title. Philippine and Indonesian sides won the early editions, and that regional rivalry still drives most of the noise in the arena.

## Dates: five weeks across the New Year

M8 runs from December 17, 2026 to January 23, 2027. That is a long event by esports standards, and it straddles Christmas, New Year's Eve and the first weeks of January.

Earlier M-series editions built from a wildcard round through group or Swiss-style play into a knockout bracket, with the grand final on the last day. M8's own match-by-match schedule sits with Moonton. Most travelling fans pick a slice of the event rather than all five weeks:

- **Opening days:** the widest field, and the most teams on the schedule each day.
- **Late knockouts and grand final:** the fewest matches, the loudest rooms, and the heaviest demand for seats.
- **The New Year stretch:** Istanbul's own celebrations overlap with the bracket.

## Tickets and venues

The facts are simple. M8 is held across venues in Istanbul, not one single arena. Moonton publishes the venue for each stage and the ticket links on its official MLBB esports channels, and that is where the authoritative information lives.

Because play moves between venues, the stage of the event you attend decides where you sleep. Fans at earlier M-series events typically chose hotels near transit rather than near one building. In Istanbul that means staying close to a metro or Marmaray station.

Prices are not something this guide prints. They sit on the official ticketing pages linked from Moonton's channels.

## New Year's Eve in the middle of a bracket

This is the quirk that sets M8 apart. Few world championships put a major city's biggest night of the year inside the tournament window.

On New Year's Eve, Istanbul crowds gather along the Bosphorus shoreline and around Taksim Square for fireworks. Ferries and central metro stations get crowded, and some roads near the waterfront close. Visitors planning a match day near December 31 usually leave extra travel time and avoid the waterfront routes late in the evening.

Christmas is not a public holiday in Turkey, so ordinary city life carries on through late December. Shops, restaurants and transit run as normal.

## Getting around Istanbul in winter

Istanbul spreads across two continents, and the Bosphorus splits it. Where your venue sits, European side or Asian side, shapes every journey.

- **Istanbul Airport (IST):** on the European side, linked to the city by the M11 metro line and Havaist airport buses.
- **Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW):** on the Asian side, linked by the M4 metro line.
- **Istanbulkart:** the reloadable transit card covering metro, tram, Marmaray, buses and city ferries. Machines at stations sell and top it up.
- **Marmaray:** the rail line that runs under the Bosphorus, usually faster than crossing by road at rush hour.

Winter here is cool and damp, with grey skies, rain and an occasional snowfall. Temperatures typically sit in single digits Celsius. Ferry decks feel cold in the wind, so most people ride inside.

## In the stands: how M-series crowds cheer

MLBB crowds are loud and organised. Fans bring national flags, team jerseys and handmade signs, and cosplayers dressed as heroes from the game are a normal sight in the queue.

A few habits travelling fans tend to follow:

- **Team chants carry across the room.** Filipino and Indonesian fans often chant in their own languages; joining in is welcome.
- **Signs stay below head height** so the people behind can see the stage.
- **Bag checks at the door** are standard at large esports events, so fans usually travel light.
- **Phones up for big plays, down for the rest.** A full match can run long, and the screen behind you does not need your camera.

Outside the arena, Turkish courtesy goes a long way. "Merhaba" is hello and "teşekkür ederim" is thank you. Cards are accepted almost everywhere in central Istanbul, though small cash still helps at simit carts and corner kiosks. In restaurants, diners usually round up or leave around ten percent.

## Between match days

Long gaps between your team's matches are where Istanbul pays off. The old city in Sultanahmet holds Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque and Topkapı Palace within walking distance of each other. The Grand Bazaar is a short tram ride along the T1 line.

Across the Golden Horn, Galata Tower overlooks the Karaköy waterfront. Many visitors take the public ferry from Eminönü or Karaköy to Kadıköy on the Asian side for an inexpensive Bosphorus crossing. Kadıköy's market streets are full of fish stalls, pickle shops and meyhanes.

Mosques remain places of worship during esports season too. Visitors take their shoes off at the entrance, women cover their hair, and nobody walks in during prayer times. A cup of çay in a glass tulip, handed over on a small saucer, is the city's standard warm-up on a cold walk back.
