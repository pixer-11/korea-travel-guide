## A harbourfront built as a stage set

Fisherman's Wharf looks like a film lot. It opened in the mid-2000s as one of Macau's first themed leisure complexes, and it lines the Outer Harbour on Av. Dr. Sun Yat Sen in NAPE. The buildings copy places from around the world: pastel Mediterranean terraces, Amsterdam-style gables, a Tang Dynasty quarter with red columns and tiled roofs.

The grounds are open around the clock, so you can walk through at any hour. The biggest landmarks are easy to spot:

- **The Roman Amphitheatre**, a stone-coloured copy of a Colosseum facing the water and used for events
- **Rocks Hotel**, a Victorian-style building with gaslamp detailing on the waterfront
- **Legend Wharf**, a row of brightly painted European-style frontages full of shops and restaurants
- **Babylon Casino**, behind the harbour promenade

It sees heavy tourist traffic and gets mixed reviews. Some love the theatrical setting, while others notice that parts of it look tired or half empty.

## Walking in from the ferry

Most visitors arrive on foot. The Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal, where boats from Hong Kong dock, is a walk of roughly ten minutes away along the waterfront, so it makes an easy first or last stop on a day trip.

From the Wharf you can keep going along the NAPE reclaimed land. Head south past the hotel towers and the bronze Kun Iam statue to reach the Macau Science Center. Head inland and you are in Macau peninsula's casino district, where taxis queue outside the big hotel entrances.

## Timing your loop

Give it one to two hours unless you are eating here. During the day, the harbour views run across to the Taipa skyline and the bridges. After dark the facades are floodlit, and that is when most photos get taken.

The site is open 24 hours, but individual shops and restaurants keep their own hours, so check the venue before you go. There is little shade on the promenade, so a midday walk in Macau's humid summer is hard going.

## Getting around the Wharf without fuss

The Wharf is popular, so plan around the crowds. Ferry arrivals and tour groups move through together; let a coach group pass and the photo spots clear within minutes.

- **Money:** Macau patacas and Hong Kong dollars are both widely accepted at roughly par, but change usually comes back in patacas. Most restaurants and hotel outlets take cards. Small kiosks may want cash.
- **Casino rules:** the gaming floor admits only visitors aged 21 and over. Staff at the door may ask for ID, and you can't take photos inside.
- **Prices:** menus and any attraction tickets show their own prices. Check the posted board or the official site rather than relying on an old figure.
- **Language:** Cantonese is the main language. English is common in hotels, while menus at smaller outlets are often in Chinese and Portuguese, so pointing works.
- **Tipping:** you usually don't need to. Hotel restaurants often add a service charge, so check the bill before leaving extra.

For a meal with fewer coach crowds, eat at the hotel restaurants rather than the snack kiosks near the entrance.
