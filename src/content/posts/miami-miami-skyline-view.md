## Brickell from across the water

The city is on the other side of the bay. From this stretch of Virginia Key's western shore, Brickell's towers and the downtown skyline stand together in one band. Biscayne Bay fills the gap between you and them, flat and open.

It's a viewpoint, not a venue. You won't find a ticket booth or a gift shop, just the shoreline beside the Rickenbacker Causeway. The rise of the William Powell Bridge frames the view to one side. Few visitors know to stop here, so it stays calm and well rated by the people who do.

## Getting onto the causeway

The address is 3401 Rickenbacker Causeway, Miami, FL 33149. It's on Virginia Key, between the mainland and Key Biscayne. Driving from Brickell, take the causeway east, cross the bridge and look for the shoreline on your right. It's close to the Rusty Pelican and Miami Marine Stadium.

- **Toll:** the causeway has a toll plaza. The current rate is posted there and on Miami-Dade County's site.
- **Bus:** Metrobus runs a route from Brickell out to Key Biscayne. Check the Miami-Dade Transit planner for the current route number and stops.
- **Bike:** the causeway has a wide shoulder lane. It's one of the most-ridden routes in Miami.
- **Time needed:** 20–45 minutes for photos and a look along the water.

## When the light works

The view faces west, so the sun goes down behind the towers. In the late afternoon the glass catches the low light. After dark the skyline lights up across the bay, so arriving just before sunset gets you both.

Mornings are flatter for photos because the sun is behind you and the towers sit in full light. That's fine for a clear record shot but less atmospheric. Summer brings afternoon thunderstorms over the bay, and from the open shoreline you can watch them build over the city. Leave before the lightning reaches you.

## Doing the Key Biscayne run properly

People who use this shoreline treat it as one stop on a longer run out the causeway. Here's how to fit it in:

- **Watch for cyclists.** Weekend mornings bring groups of road riders onto the causeway. Signal early and pull off slowly.
- **Park only where it's marked.** Stopping on the causeway shoulder is risky and can get you a ticket. Use the designated pull-offs.
- **Bring water and shade.** There's no café at the viewpoint itself. The Rusty Pelican, just along the shore, is a sit-down restaurant with the same skyline view.
- **Leave nothing behind.** The shoreline is open mangrove and bay edge, with no staffed bins.

Once you've seen the view, carry on east along the causeway. Hobie Beach (also called Windsurfer Beach) is a shallow, sandy spot where people launch kayaks and windsurfers. Further out, Crandon Park and Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park have the island's full beaches. The Cape Florida Lighthouse is at the southern tip of Key Biscayne.

On the way back, Miami Marine Stadium is next door. It's a 1963 concrete grandstand on the water, well known for its graffiti and its sweeping cantilevered roof. On the return drive, the skyline sits ahead of you as you come down off the bridge.
