## A circle drawn for the camera

Putra Square looks like a compass laid flat on the ground. It is a wide round plaza of pale paving, with a star-shaped centre and a ring of flagpoles. On one side stands the rose-granite dome of Putra Mosque, and on the hill above, the green dome of Perdana Putra.

This is the city's emblem, the spot every Putrajaya postcard is framed from. That means company. It ranks among the most visited stops in the federal capital, and on weekend afternoons tour coaches and families crowd the circle. Plan around that and the square gives you room to see why it was laid out this way.

## Reading the star from the middle

Begin at the centre. The star motif radiates outward and lines up your sightlines with the buildings around it, and walking its points is the best way to understand the plan. The flags snap above you, and the water features soften the hard expanse of stone.

Turn slowly and the plan reveals itself:

- **Perdana Putra**: the Prime Minister's office complex, crowned with a green onion dome, closing the axis on higher ground.
- **Putra Mosque (Masjid Putra)**: finished in 1999, faced in rose-tinted granite, with a minaret rising over Putrajaya Lake.
- **Persiaran Perdana**: the long ceremonial boulevard running away from the square and through the government precinct.

The scale is deliberate. Putrajaya was planned from scratch in the 1990s as Malaysia's administrative capital, and this plaza is where the plan makes its loudest statement.

## The 7am window

Timing matters more than anything else here. The quietest stretch is usually 7am–9am, on weekdays and weekends alike. Sunrise in Malaysia falls around 7am through most of the year. Arriving then gives you low sun on the mosque's pink stone and a flag ring with nobody in your frame.

Try not to come on a weekend between 2pm and 10pm. That is when the square is typically at its busiest, with coach groups, wedding shoots and families all sharing the same paving. Midday on any day brings a second problem: the open granite holds the heat and offers almost no shade.

The square currently opens at 6am and closes at 10pm, every day this week. Hours can change, so check before a late-evening trip.

## Getting out from Kuala Lumpur

Putrajaya sits about 25km south of central Kuala Lumpur. The simplest rail route is the KLIA Transit from KL Sentral to Putrajaya & Cyberjaya station. From there, a Grab ride to the square takes only a few minutes.

Many visitors simply take a Grab straight from the city, which avoids the transfer. If you drive, follow signs for Presint 1 and the mosque. The address is Presint 1, 62000 Putrajaya. Inside the city, the boulevards are wide but distances between landmarks are longer than they look on a map.

## Crossing into Putra Mosque

The mosque is the square's most popular side trip, and it has its own rules. Its visiting hours are separate from the square's. They close to tourists during the five daily prayers and for longer around Friday midday prayers, so check the posted times at the entrance before you walk over.

What to expect at the door:

- Non-Muslim visitors are given robes to wear over their clothes. Women also cover their hair.
- Shoes come off before the prayer areas. Leave them on the racks provided.
- Keep your voice low and don't walk in front of anyone who is praying.
- Ask before photographing people, especially worshippers.

Even on the square itself, modest clothing (covered shoulders and knees) is the courteous choice. You are standing between a mosque and the head of government's office.

## The square's unwritten rules

Most people treat the plaza as a photo stage and move on quickly. Here is what makes it go smoothly:

- **Pay by app.** Grab is the normal way to get around Putrajaya, and Touch 'n Go eWallet is widely accepted.
- **Leave the drone in the bag.** This is a government precinct, and flying near official buildings needs permits.
- **Bring water.** The circle is exposed, and the walk to the lakefront adds distance.
- **Share the frame.** Wedding couples often use the square as a backdrop, so step aside for a minute.

Security staff watch the approach to Perdana Putra closely. Stay on the public paving and don't wander up the access roads.

## Turning the square into a half-day

Most people spend 30–45 minutes on the square. Adding the mosque and the waterfront makes it a satisfying morning. Putrajaya Lake is right beside the mosque. Walk the promenade to see the city's bridges and the government buildings reflected in the water.

Persiaran Perdana makes a good next stretch, either on foot in the cool early hours or by Grab once the sun climbs. In the evening, Perdana Putra and the mosque are floodlit. A visit on a weekday after dusk, before the 10pm close, gives you the lit domes without the weekend crush.
