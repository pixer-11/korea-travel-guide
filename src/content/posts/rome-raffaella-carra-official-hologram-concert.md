## A Carrà night in EUR

One date, one room: Atlantico, December 28, 2026. The show is a hologram concert built around Raffaella Carrà, the entertainer who taught Italy the *Tuca Tuca* in the early 1970s and later fronted *Carràmba! Che sorpresa* on Rai.

She died in July 2021. This tribute puts her image back on a stage in the capital, in the gap between Christmas and New Year, when Rome is full of returning families and holiday visitors.

## Who comes, and why it matters

Carrà's appeal never belonged to one generation. Her catalogue includes:

- *Tanti auguri* (the "Com'è bello far l'amore da Trieste in giù" song)
- *A far l'amore comincia tu*
- *Rumore*
- *Fiesta*
- *Pedro*

She was a superstar in Spain and Latin America as well as Italy. She is also a long-standing LGBTQ+ icon, and the blonde bob plus sequins are an easy tribute outfit. Expect a mixed audience: grandparents who watched *Canzonissima*, and younger fans who know her through *Ballo ballo* and dance remixes.

## Finding Atlantico

Atlantico is a concert club on Viale dell'Oceano Atlantico in EUR. EUR is the planned district of travertine-clad boulevards south of the historic centre, laid out under Mussolini for a world's fair that never happened.

Metro Line B runs from Termini to the EUR stations. Most concertgoers use EUR Fermi and then walk or take a short bus or taxi ride to the venue. The district's landmarks make easy reference points:

- the Palazzo della Civiltà Italiana, the "Square Colosseum"
- the artificial lake by EUR Fermi
- the Nuvola convention centre on Viale Cristoforo Colombo

From the centre, a taxi follows Via Cristoforo Colombo almost the whole way.

## Doing the Atlantico night properly

Atlantico is a club-format room, so fans usually treat the evening as a standing gig, not a seated theatre show. Anyone who wants a clear view of the projection tends to come in near the start of doors and hold a spot in the middle of the floor. Hologram effects lose impact at sharp side angles.

Some practical habits:

- **Paying:** cards and phone payments are normal at Roman venue bars. A few coins are still handy for buses.
- **Getting home:** after shows in EUR, crowds head for taxi ranks and ride apps (FreeNow, itTaxi) at the same moment. Many attendees walk back toward Viale Europa or Cristoforo Colombo before hailing a ride.
- **Singing along:** Italians sing every word, in Italian. Learning the chorus of *Tanti auguri* is half the fun.
- **Dress:** late-December Rome is cold after dark. Most people wear layers they can leave at the cloakroom.

For dinner, EUR's restaurants around Viale Europa are the natural pre-show stop. The pizzerias of Garbatella sit two Metro B stops back toward the centre.
