## Why go

You step off the RER and the first thing that hits you is the sheer scale of the place before you've even reached the gates — gilded ironwork, a cobbled courtyard the size of a small town square, and a facade that keeps unfolding as you walk toward it. This is Louis XIV's old hunting lodge turned into the most extravagant royal address in Europe, and it still shows: gold leaf catching the light on the gates, marble busts lining the entrance, the low murmur of a few thousand people all craning their necks at once. It's one of the most-reviewed attractions in the country, and for good reason — few places anywhere pack this much sculpted history, painted ceiling, and manicured garden into a single visit.

## Getting there

Versailles sits at Place d'Armes in the town of Versailles, about 20km southwest of central Paris. The easiest route is RER C to Versailles Château – Rive Gauche station, which drops you a short, well-signed walk from the palace gates — follow the crowd, you genuinely cannot get lost. Trains run frequently from central Paris stops like Saint-Michel Notre-Dame, Invalides, and Champ de Mars, and the ride takes roughly 35–45 minutes depending on where you board. A standard Paris Métro/RER ticket covering Zone 4 works for this; a regular city-only ticket does not, so check the fare before you tap in. If you'd rather skip the train, SNCF services from Gare Montparnasse or Gare Saint-Lazare to Versailles Chantiers also work, followed by a 20-minute walk or short bus ride.

## What to see

The Hall of Mirrors is the reason most people come, and it earns the hype: seventeen mirrored arches facing seventeen garden windows, chandeliers doubling their light back into the room, courtiers' portraits and gilded stucco crowding every surface. But don't stop there. The King's and Queen's State Apartments walk you through room after room of brocade, painted ceilings, and canopied beds where the business of absolute monarchy actually happened. Outside, the gardens designed by André Le Nôtre stretch out in geometric parterres, fountains, and the long Grand Canal, where you can rent a rowboat or just walk the gravel paths in relative quiet compared to the palace interior. Further out, the Trianon palaces and Marie-Antoinette's rustic Hameau (her mock-peasant village) are a 20–30 minute walk or a quick ride on the little tourist train — worth it if you have a full day, since the crowds thin out considerably the farther you get from the main building.

## When to go

Versailles is closed on Mondays, and open 9am–5:30pm Tuesday through Sunday — plan around that, because there's no early or late slot to fall back on. Weekends are the worst crunch, with the courtyard and state apartments busiest between 11am and 5pm; if you can only come on a Saturday or Sunday, get there right at opening or hold out until the last hour or two before closing. Weekdays, especially Tuesday, Wednesday, and Friday, tend to breathe easier. On days when the Musical Fountains Show runs (spring through autumn, on a published seasonal schedule), the gardens get busier but it's genuinely worth timing a visit around — check the official calendar before you go since it doesn't run daily.

## How to visit like a local

Book a timed-entry ticket online before you go — walk-up ticket lines at the palace itself can eat over an hour of your day, and pre-booking sidesteps that entirely. Head straight for the Hall of Mirrors and State Apartments first thing in the morning, then save the gardens, Trianon estate, and Hameau for the early afternoon once the tour-bus crowds have settled into the main palace. Most transactions here — tickets, food carts, garden boat rental — take card as easily as cash, so don't over-prepare with cash you won't need. The etiquette mistake most visitors make is trying to "do" everything — palace, gardens, both Trianons — in two rushed hours; locals and regular visitors treat it as a full-day outing, often bringing a picnic for the gardens rather than eating on-site. If mobility is a concern, note that the estate is enormous and mostly outdoors — comfortable walking is unavoidable, timed tickets or not.
