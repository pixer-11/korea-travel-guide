## A Sunday Morning on the Lawns

Alice Springs is quiet on a Sunday. Most of Todd Mall's shops open late or not at all, and the town's one pedestrian street stays mostly still. The exception is the stretch near the Council Lawns, where the market currently sets up its gazebos in its temporary location.

What you'll find there is a small-town market in the desert. Expect trestle tables under shade cloth, food stalls, local crafts and art, with people moving slowly between them. It's modest in scale. That modesty is the appeal: you can see the whole thing, buy breakfast and still have most of the morning left for Alice.

## When It Actually Runs

Get this part right first. Google currently lists the market as **Sunday only, 9am–1pm**, with every other day closed. There are no weekday or Saturday sessions to fall back on.

Some practical consequences:

- **Arrive Saturday at the latest** if you're driving in from Uluru or the West MacDonnell Ranges and want to see it.
- **Go early rather than late.** Stalls start packing up as 1pm gets closer, and food can sell out.
- **Check the calendar before you go.** Market schedules in the Red Centre can shift with the seasons and with events, and this one has already moved site once. The organisers and Alice Springs Town Council post current dates.

## Finding the Market and What's Around It

Todd Mall runs north–south through the CBD, parallel to the dry bed of the Todd River. The market's current home on the Council Lawns sits beside the Town Council offices, so you're never more than a short walk from anything in the centre.

Pair the market with these places within walking distance:

- **Adelaide House**, on Todd Mall itself. It's the town's first hospital, designed by John Flynn, founder of the Royal Flying Doctor Service.
- **Aboriginal art galleries** lining the mall, including Papunya Tula Artists, the Western Desert art movement's cooperative.
- **Anzac Hill**, at the northern edge of town, for a short climb and a view over Alice and the MacDonnell Ranges.
- **Araluen Cultural Precinct**, a short drive west, if you want a fuller morning of art and museums.

Gallery and museum hours vary, and some keep reduced hours on Sundays, so check each one before building a route around it.

## Shopping the Stalls Without Regret

Buying here works best if you treat it as a conversation. Stallholders at a market this size are usually the makers or growers themselves, so ask who made something and where. People in Alice are generally happy to talk about their work.

Aboriginal art deserves extra care. Ask whether the artist is named and how they're paid, and look for galleries and sellers who follow the Indigenous Art Code. If a piece interests you but the details are vague, walk the hundred metres to the mall's community-run galleries and compare.

On payment, bring cash and a card. Plenty of stalls take tap-and-go now, but smaller tables and food vans don't always, and the nearest ATM may be in a queue when you need it. Prices are on the tables or you can ask the stallholder. Polite questions are fine, but heavy haggling isn't normal at Australian markets.

## Dressing for the Desert Morning

Central Australia changes a lot between seasons, even in a four-hour window. From roughly June to August, mornings can start near freezing and be warm by midday. Wear layers you can strip off and carry.

From November to March it's the other way round. By late morning the heat is serious, and the lawns offer only patchy shade. Bring a hat, sunscreen and a refillable water bottle, and do your browsing in the first part of the session.

## Making a Morning of It

The market suits a slow start to a day in town. Allow about an hour to walk every stall and eat something. Then head along the mall to the galleries or Adelaide House while you're still in walking mode.

If you have a car, this also makes a good base before a drive out. The Alice Springs Telegraph Station Historical Reserve sits just north of town. Simpsons Gap, in the West MacDonnell Ranges, is an easy run west for the afternoon. Either one turns a two-hour market visit into a proper Red Centre day without much planning.
