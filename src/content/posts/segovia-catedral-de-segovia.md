## What you're looking at

This is the last major Gothic cathedral built in Spain, finished in the 1500s and 1600s after the previous one, closer to the Alcázar, was wrecked in a revolt. Locals still call it "the Lady of Cathedrals" for its scale and its pale limestone towers, which top out over 88 meters. Inside, the nave is unusually tall and narrow, with ribbed vaulting that pulls your eye straight up rather than forward.

The stained glass is the headline feature the reviews single out: window after window dated to the 1300s, glowing amber and blue depending on the hour. Side chapels hold retablos, tombs and paintings accumulated over five centuries, so the walk around the ambulatory turns into a small art crawl.

## Cloister, museum, tower

The cloister is older than the current building; it was moved stone by stone from the earlier cathedral and rebuilt here. Off it sits a small museum with tapestries, silverwork and choir books, included in the general admission.

A separate tower climb is usually offered for an extra fee, with guided timed slots rather than free access, and it's the best vantage point over Segovia's rooftops toward the aqueduct. Check current tower ticket times at the door since slots run on a schedule, not continuously.

- Nave and chapels: 20–30 minutes
- Cloister and museum: 15–20 minutes
- Tower climb (if running): add 30–45 minutes

## Timing your visit around the crowds

This is one of Segovia's most-visited sites, so it never feels empty, but the hours themselves stay consistent: 9am to 9pm on weekdays and weekends alike is the calm stretch, while Saturdays and Sundays from noon onward pull the heaviest foot traffic as day-trippers from Madrid arrive.

Sunday opens late, at 1:30pm, which actually works in your favor if you come right at opening before the afternoon crowd builds. On any other day, arriving close to 9am gets you the nave largely to yourself before tour groups start filing in.

## Getting in and moving through respectfully

Entry is by ticket at a booth just inside the main door on Plaza Mayor side; card payment is standard practice here. There's no need to book far ahead outside of peak summer weekends, but buying on-site does mean queuing behind whichever tour bus arrived first.

- Dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered, as it's an active place of worship
- Keep voices low near the choir and altar during services
- Photography is generally fine without flash; tripods are usually not
- Follow the marked one-way route through chapels and cloister rather than backtracking

Mass is still held here, so check posted times if you want to avoid touring mid-service, or attend if you'd rather experience the space as it's actually used.
