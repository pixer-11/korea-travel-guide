Tashkent has hundreds of places serving plov, but Caravan is the one people mention first when the conversation turns to where to get it done properly. The restaurant occupies a large, multi-level building built around the idea of Eastern hospitality on a grand scale: carved wooden ceilings, low seating nooks, and dining rooms that feel more like a merchant's house than a modern restaurant.

That scale is the point. Caravan isn't a single room but a series of them, spread across floors and connected by staircases and open galleries, so a party of four and a party of forty can both be accommodated without either feeling out of place.

## The two plovs

Plov is the reason Caravan's name comes up in almost every list of where to eat in Tashkent. The kitchen is known for preparing it in two distinct styles, a detail that sets it apart from restaurants that serve just one version and call it a day.

Uzbek plov varies enormously by region and household, built around rice, carrots, meat and oil cooked together in a single wide pot called a kazan. Caravan's reputation rests on giving diners a chance to compare two takes on that dish in one sitting, which is unusual even by Tashkent's plov-obsessed standards.

Order it as your centerpiece rather than a side thought. It's typically brought out communally, meant for sharing across the table rather than plated individually.

## Wooden interiors and summer terraces

Inside, the restaurant leans into a traditional Uzbek aesthetic: dark wood paneling, carved columns, low tables, and décor that nods to caravanserai architecture, the roadside inns that once sheltered Silk Road traders. It's an interior built for long, multi-course meals rather than fast turnover.

When the weather cooperates, the summer terraces open up and become the more sought-after seating. Outdoor dining in Tashkent's warmer months is a big part of the city's restaurant culture, and Caravan's terraces are set up to handle large groups celebrating birthdays, anniversaries and business dinners alike.

Ask for terrace seating specifically if you're visiting between late spring and early autumn; indoor rooms are the fallback once temperatures drop.

## Why celebrities keep showing up

Caravan has built a reputation as a favorite stop for visiting celebrities and prominent guests, which has only added to its profile within Tashkent. That doesn't mean it's stuffy or exclusive by design; it means the restaurant is used to hosting high-profile groups without disrupting service for everyone else.

For a regular visitor, the practical takeaway is that the restaurant can get busy on evenings and weekends, particularly when there's a private event underway. Building in flexibility around your reservation time is worth doing.

## Booking a table without a headache

Given its size and popularity, Caravan is not the kind of place you show up to and expect an immediate table on a Friday night. A phone or online reservation ahead of time is the standard way locals and repeat visitors handle it, especially for groups.

- Call or book ahead, particularly for dinner service and weekends
- Mention if you want terrace seating specifically
- Groups celebrating a special occasion should flag it when booking, since the restaurant regularly hosts larger parties
- Confirm current opening hours directly with the restaurant before you go, as these can shift seasonally

## Getting there and what to expect

Caravan sits within Tashkent proper, and like most of the city's better-known restaurants, it's easiest reached by taxi or a ride-hailing app rather than on foot, especially if you're coming from one of the outer districts. Traffic patterns in the city mean it's worth building in extra time for the ride, particularly around the evening rush.

Once inside, expect a full sit-down meal rather than a quick bite: multiple courses, unhurried service, and a menu built around Uzbek staples beyond just the plov. Bring an appetite and, ideally, a group to share with, since much of Uzbek dining culture, and Caravan's format specifically, is built around communal platters rather than individual plates.

Dress is smart-casual rather than formal, in keeping with a restaurant that hosts everything from family celebrations to visiting dignitaries. If in doubt about current hours, prices or reservation policy, a quick call ahead saves any disappointment at the door.
