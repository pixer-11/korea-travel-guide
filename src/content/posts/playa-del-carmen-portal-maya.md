## An arch built from bodies

Look at it from the sand and the arch rises over 50 feet. Up close it turns out to be human figures. Men and women twist upward and lean into each other, shaped like a gust of wind and a curl of water, and they meet at the top to form a gateway.

It stands in Parque Fundadores, the small seafront plaza where Avenida Juárez meets the beach. The shape works as a frame. Stand on the plaza side and the Caribbean fills the opening. Walk around to the sand and the town shows through instead.

There is no ticket, gate or fence, and the plaza never closes. Plan on 20 to 30 minutes, or longer if you stay for the beach next to it.

## Getting to Parque Fundadores

Everything in central Playa leads to this corner. The address is Av Benito Juárez 251, Centro.

- **From Quinta Avenida (5th Avenue):** walk to the Juárez intersection and turn toward the sea. The arch is a block away.
- **From the ADO bus terminal on Quinta Avenida:** the terminal is at that same Juárez corner, so it's about a two-minute walk.
- **From the Cozumel ferry pier:** walk north along the shore for a few minutes.
- **By colectivo from Tulum or Cancún:** ride to the end of Juárez and walk toward the water.

Skip driving. Parking in Centro is tight, and the last stretch is pedestrian streets anyway.

## Beating the weekend crowd

The sculpture is one of the most-photographed spots in town. When people are around, a line forms for the classic shot through the arch. Measured foot traffic shows the plaza is quietest from **7am to 8am**, on weekdays and weekends alike. At that hour the beach faces east into the morning light and the arch is mostly yours.

On weekends, avoid **12pm to 11pm**. The plaza fills for most of the day and stays full long after dark, with street performers and evening crowds spilling off Quinta Avenida. If a weekend is your only option, go early or settle for a wide shot from the sand.

## Manners at the plaza

The plaza has its own unwritten rules.

- **Voladores:** Parque Fundadores has a tall pole where Voladores de Papantla perform the flying ritual. When they come down, someone passes around a hat. If you watched, tip in pesos. Small notes and coins are the norm.
- **Photo vendors and performers:** people in costume will offer to pose with you. Agree on the payment before the photo, not after.
- **The sculpture:** you'll see people sitting on the base. Climbing the figures is another matter. The bronze-toned surface gets hot by midday, and security does tell climbers to get down.
- **Taking turns:** when it's busy, people queue informally for the centred shot. Take a few frames and step aside. You can get a wider angle from the beach without waiting.

Playa Fundadores starts right behind the arch. It's the easiest place in Centro for a quick swim, and Quinta Avenida's restaurants are a block inland when you're done.
