## Getting your bearings first

Telok Blangah Hill Park is not actually in Kampong Glam. It sits on Singapore's southern ridge, closer to HarbourFront and Mount Faber, a good half-hour taxi or MRT-plus-walk from the Arab Street shophouses and Sultan Mosque.

If you're basing yourself in Kampong Glam, treat this as a half-day excursion rather than a stroll after lunch. The nearest MRT is HarbourFront (Circle and North East lines), from which the park is a short uphill walk.

## What the 34 hectares actually give you

This is one of three linked parks forming the Southern Ridges, a green spine that runs from Mount Faber down to Telok Blangah and on to Kent Ridge. The terrain rolls rather than flattens, so expect gentle climbs through secondary rainforest.

You'll pass patches of tall rain trees, the odd colonial-era relic, and clearings where the canopy opens onto the harbour. The signature structure here is the Sky Garden, a lookout tower that lifts you above the treeline for a clear sweep of the port and the sea beyond.

Come dusk, that same view fills with container ship lights and the glow of Sentosa in the distance. It's one of the few spots in the city where you get skyline and shipping lanes in the same frame.

## Linking up with Henderson Waves and Mount Faber

Most visitors don't stop at Telok Blangah Hill Park alone; they walk it as one leg of the Southern Ridges trail. From here a boardwalk carries you toward Henderson Waves, Singapore's highest pedestrian bridge, its timber slats curving in a distinctive wave shape.

Cross that and you're climbing toward Mount Faber Park, where a cable car station offers a ride down to Sentosa or HarbourFront if your legs are done. Plan for roughly 1.5 to 2 hours if you're doing the full stretch at an easy pace, longer if you stop often for photos.

Wear shoes you don't mind sweating in. Even shaded sections stay warm and humid through the day.

## When to go

The park is open 24 hours, every day, so there's no gate to time your visit around. On weekends, the quietest stretch runs 7am to 9am, before the day's heat and the walking groups build up.

Crowds pick up steadily from mid-morning and stay busy right through to 11pm on weekends, since the boardwalks and viewing decks are lit and popular with evening joggers and photographers. If you want the Sky Garden view without other tripods in your shot, early morning is your best bet.

Midweek mornings tend to be calmer still, though exact quiet hours on weekdays aren't tracked as precisely as weekends.

## How to visit like a local

Singaporeans use this park for exercise more than sightseeing, so the etiquette follows fitness-park norms rather than tourist-site ones.

- Keep to the left on shared boardwalks; joggers and cyclists move fast on the connector paths.
- Bring your own water. Vending and food options are sparse until you reach the restaurant near the park's edge or amenities closer to Mount Faber.
- Don't expect food stalls mid-trail; this isn't a hawker-adjacent park like those closer to housing estates.
- Check the weather before setting out. Afternoon thunderstorms are common and the canopy offers only partial cover.

The most common visitor mistake is treating this as a quick stop. Because it's well-known and rated highly, people assume a fifteen-minute look will do, then realize the real reward is the connected walk toward Henderson Waves and Mount Faber. Give it the time, not just the glance.

## Getting back

If you've walked the full ridge to Mount Faber, the cable car down to HarbourFront or Sentosa saves your knees. Otherwise, retrace your steps to Telok Blangah Hill Park and pick up a taxi or ride-share from the nearest road access point, since MRT stations sit at either end of the ridge rather than in the middle.

Factor the return trip into your day if you're heading back to Kampong Glam for dinner; it's not a quick hop.
