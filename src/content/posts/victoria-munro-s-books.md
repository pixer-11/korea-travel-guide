## A bank vault turned into a reading room

Look up before you look at the shelves. Munro's occupies a neoclassical bank building on Government Street, and the old banking hall has been left mostly as it was. A coffered ceiling sits far above the stacks, and stone columns run along the front.

That height changes how you shop. You walk past tables of new releases laid out across the open floor, with tall cases around the walls, and the room stays bright and quiet even when it is full. Large fabric hangings by the Victoria textile artist Carole Sabiston hang high up and soften the stone.

## The Munro name, and why it matters here

Jim Munro opened the shop in 1963 with his then-wife Alice Munro. She went on to become one of Canada's most celebrated short-story writers and won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2013. The store moved into this building in 1984.

Plenty of people come because of that connection, and the Canadian fiction shelves are an obvious place to start. The store's reputation rests on the range of the stock, though, not on the literary link. You'll find:

- Canadian and Pacific Northwest writing, including local history and nature titles
- A broad general stock of fiction, poetry and non-fiction
- A children's section
- Cookbooks, travel and art books suited to browsing on the tables

## Finding it on Government Street

Victoria has no metro, and downtown is small enough that you won't need one. From the Inner Harbour, where floatplanes and the Victoria Clipper from Seattle dock, walk north up Government Street for about 10 minutes. The Parliament Buildings and the Fairmont Empress will be behind you.

If you're arriving on BC Ferries at Swartz Bay, BC Transit's route 70 runs into downtown. Once you're on Government Street, Munro's is between Fort Street and Bastion Square. Its stone front looks nothing like the souvenir shops on either side, so it's hard to miss.

## Browsing without the elbows

This is a well-known stop, not a secret. On summer days, when cruise passengers and harbour visitors walk up Government Street, the floor can feel busy by mid-afternoon. Arriving right at the 9:30am opening usually means you get the front tables to yourself.

Thursday and Friday are worth remembering. On those days the store currently stays open until 7:30pm, not 6pm, so you can come after dinner once the day-trippers have gone. Other habits that help:

- **Ask at the desk.** Staff are known for their recommendations, and a specific request ("a novel set on Vancouver Island") gets a better answer than "something good".
- **Order what isn't on the shelf.** Like most independent bookstores, they can special-order titles. That's handy if you're in town for a few days.
- **Pay the usual Canadian way.** Contactless card payment is standard, and nobody tips in a bookshop.
- **Keep your voice down.** The stone hall echoes, and people really do read here.
- **Check the weight.** If you're flying home, look at your baggage allowance before you buy three hardcover art books.

## Making an afternoon of the block

Munro's sits in the most walkable stretch of the old town. You can fit it into a couple of hours on foot without planning much.

- **Bastion Square** is around the corner, a cobbled square with seasonal market stalls and the old courthouse building.
- **Murchie's**, the long-running Victoria tea merchant, is a few doors along Government Street if you want a pot of tea with your new book.
- **Chinatown and Fan Tan Alley** are about 10 minutes north on foot. The alley is one of the narrowest streets in Canada.
- **The Inner Harbour** is back to the south and makes a good last stop. Find a bench along the causeway and start reading.

## When it fits your trip

Munro's works in any weather, and Victoria gets plenty of grey, damp days outside summer. That makes it a good rainy-morning stop between the harbour and the museums. Most people stay 45 minutes to an hour. Serious readers stay longer.

The shop opens every day of the week at the moment, but check the store's own site around public holidays. If you can only fit in one bookstore on Vancouver Island, this building makes the choice easy.
