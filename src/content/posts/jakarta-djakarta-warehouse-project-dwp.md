## Three nights on an old airfield

DWP fills a former runway with bass. JIExpo Kemayoran stands on land that was once Kemayoran Airport, Jakarta's main airport until it closed in the 1980s. Today the grounds are a sprawl of exhibition halls, open lots and wide service roads. Across three nights, December 11-13, 2026, the festival turns all of it into a multi-stage EDM city.

The scale is the point. DWP calls itself Southeast Asia's biggest EDM festival, and the layout shows it. The stages sit far enough apart that walking between them takes real time. Each one carries its own sound and crowd, so a single night can swing from main-stage anthems to something darker in a hall.

## What the festival is

DWP began in Jakarta in 2008. Since then it has grown into the region's flagship dance-music weekend, drawing top international DJs and a large travelling crowd from Singapore, Malaysia, Australia and beyond. The 2026 edition brings it back to JIExpo Kemayoran.

Expect a production-heavy festival rather than a boutique one. Its reputation rests on:

- Big-name headliners spread across several stages
- Large-format stage builds with lighting and screens
- Three consecutive nights, so many people treat it as a full long weekend
- A strongly international audience mixed with Jakarta's own club scene

The full lineup and stage schedule belong to the organiser, and they live on the festival's official site and social channels.

## Reaching JIExpo Kemayoran

Getting there is mostly a traffic question. JIExpo sits in Kemayoran, north of Jakarta's central business district around Sudirman and Thamrin. On a festival night the roads around the venue load up fast.

Most attendees use ride-hailing:

- **Grab and Gojek** are the default; both offer cars and motorbike taxis
- **Motorbike taxis (GoRide, GrabBike)** cut through jams far faster than cars, which matters at the end of the night
- **Taxis** from Blue Bird are the familiar metered option

International visitors land at Soekarno-Hatta International Airport (CGK), west of the city. The drive into Kemayoran can take well over an hour when traffic is heavy, so festival-goers flying in on day one usually leave a wide margin.

## Getting through the night at DWP

The crowd has rhythms of its own. Regulars tend to settle on a meeting point in the first hour, because phone signal across a packed exhibition ground can drop at peak moments. Groups usually pick a fixed landmark rather than "by the main stage".

Payment in Jakarta leans heavily cashless. E-wallets such as GoPay, OVO and DANA are everywhere in daily life, and cards work at most larger vendors. Visitors from abroad often link a card to Grab or Gojek before they land.

Some local realities matter more than at festivals elsewhere:

- **Indonesia's drug laws are among the strictest in the world.** Penalties are severe, and foreigners have no exemption.
- **Security screening** at the gates is thorough, and queues build at the main entrances when the headline sets near.
- **Leaving** is the hardest part: the ride-hailing pickup zones get crowded, and many people walk a few streets out before ordering a ride.

## The weather and what people wear

December is rainy season in Jakarta. Heavy evening downpours are common, and the city stays hot and humid even after dark. Outdoor stages and the walks between halls take most of that weather.

Experienced attendees dress for it:

- Light, quick-drying clothes rather than heavy fabrics
- Closed shoes that can handle wet ground and a long night on concrete
- A compact poncho, since umbrellas are awkward in a crowd
- A small, secure bag for phone and ID

Water and rest breaks matter more than usual. The humidity wears people down well before the last set, especially over three nights in a row.

## Where people stay

Location decides how much of the night goes on traffic. Hotels in Kemayoran and the nearby Gunung Sahari and Ancol areas sit closest to the venue. A short ride home at the end of the night is worth a lot in this city.

Many international visitors base themselves in central Jakarta instead, around Thamrin, Sudirman or Menteng. Those areas give easier access to restaurants, malls and the rest of the city on the days between festival nights. The trade-off is a longer ride to and from JIExpo each evening.

For daytime recovery, the Kota Tua old town with its Fatahillah Square lies north-west of Kemayoran. The National Monument (Monas) sits in Merdeka Square in central Jakarta. Both make an easy afternoon before the gates open again.
