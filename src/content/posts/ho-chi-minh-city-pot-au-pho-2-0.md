## What makes this phở different

Peter Cuong Franklin isn't a street-food cook. He trained in fine dining before turning that discipline on Vietnam's most familiar dish.

The original iteration of his concept made international headlines for a bowl of phở priced at $100, built with luxury ingredients and slow, technical broth work rather than the usual overnight stockpot. Pot au Phở 2.0 is his newer location, carrying that idea forward rather than repeating it.

Expect the same core logic: phở treated as a canvas for technique, not a fixed formula. The kitchen leans on classical French method (the "pot au feu" pun in the name is deliberate) layered onto Vietnamese broth-building.

## Where it sits in the city

This is Ho Chi Minh City, so the restaurant exists inside a food scene that runs from plastic-stool street stalls to serious tasting menus within a few blocks of each other. Pot au Phở 2.0 sits firmly at the upscale end of that spectrum.

Don't expect a stall setup. This is a sit-down restaurant experience, positioned closer to a chef's-table dinner than a quick bowl-and-go lunch.

## Before you go

The menu, pricing, and exact hours aren't things to guess at here, and specifics change as new locations bed in.

- Check the restaurant's official page or a current booking platform for today's hours
- Reserve ahead: buzzy new openings from a chef with this profile fill fast
- Ask when you book whether the tasting-style format or an à la carte phở option is running that night
- Confirm the address before heading out, since "2.0" signals this is a newer, separate location from the original spot

Treat this as a planned dinner, not a walk-in stop between sights.

## Ordering and etiquette here

This isn't a bowl-in-ten-minutes counter. Budget real time at the table, the way you would for a chef-driven tasting menu anywhere else in the world.

- Book ahead rather than arriving on spec; a trending new opening from a known chef draws walk-away crowds
- Ask your server how the phở is meant to be eaten here: some elevated versions change the components (broth, noodle, garnish) so the usual squeeze-of-lime-and-dig-in habit may not apply
- Dress a notch above street-food casual; the room and price tier both sit closer to fine dining than to a phở stall
- If you loved the original "$100 phở" concept, ask staff directly how this menu differs before ordering, since 2.0 suggests evolution, not a copy

Come hungry for the story as much as the bowl. Franklin's whole project is a conversation with phở's usual rules, and the value here is watching a familiar dish get taken apart and rebuilt with unfamiliar tools.
