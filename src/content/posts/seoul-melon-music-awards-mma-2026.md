## Eighteen years of Melon's big night

The 18th Melon Music Awards is set for November 14–16, 2026. Melon is South Korea's largest music streaming service and is owned by Kakao Entertainment. Its awards rank among the year's biggest K-pop ceremonies, so the night tends to work as a year-end summary of the charts.

The night is built around stage performances as much as trophies. Groups usually perform staging made for this show rather than repeating their music-show routines. Fan sections are often filled with matching official lightsticks, so an artist's colours can light up a whole block of seats.

## Inside Gocheok Sky Dome

Gocheok Sky Dome opened in 2015 as Korea's first domed baseball stadium, and the Kiwoom Heroes play their home games there. It sits beside the Anyangcheon stream in Guro-gu, southwest Seoul. The fixed roof means mid-November cold and rain stay outside, which matters in a month when Seoul evenings are often near freezing.

Concerts and awards shows use a stage set up across the field, with seats on the floor and in the curved tiers of the stands. Because the stands rise steeply, upper-tier seats look down on the whole lighting rig. That view suits a show where the staging is half the point.

## Getting to Guil and getting home

**Guil Station (Line 1)** is the closest stop. Leave by **Exit 2** and cross the bridge over Anyangcheon; the dome is a few minutes' walk away. Other options:

- **Gaebong Station (Line 1)**, one stop south, is a longer walk but less crowded.
- **Sindorim Station (Lines 1 and 2)** is two stops away and is where most people transfer.
- **Yeongdeungpo**, **Yeouido** and **Guro Digital Complex** are where visitors usually stay. All three are a short ride from the dome.

Once the show ends, Guil's platforms fill up quickly. Many attendees walk along the stream to Gaebong, or take a taxi from the main road and avoid the station rush.

## How fans work a K-pop awards night

Korean fan culture has its own routine, and international visitors pick it up fast. What attendees typically do:

- **Carry an official lightstick.** Many are linked to the venue's system and change colour together during sets.
- **Collect free fan goods.** Fan clubs often give out banners and slogan towels near the gates before doors open.
- **Keep the camera small.** Large K-pop shows in Korea commonly ban professional cameras with detachable lenses, and staff check bags at entry.
- **Use T-money.** A rechargeable T-money card covers the subway and buses and works at convenience-store tills around the dome.

Ticketing is the step international fans struggle with most. Korean platforms often require local identity or phone verification. Overseas fans therefore usually go through a platform's global site or a ticket proxy service. Past editions sold through Melon's own ticketing platform. The organiser publishes the official dates, venue and ticket channels, and resale outside those channels is a common source of invalid tickets.

Inside, fan chants follow fixed scripts for each song, and fandoms practise them online beforehand. Food and drink rules follow venue policy, so most fans eat at the Guil and Gocheok restaurant strips nearby rather than in their seats.
