## A coffee stop four floors up

Most museum cafés sit by the exit. This one is upstairs. Papakape has opened a branch inside the National Museum of Fine Arts, on the 4th floor Sen. Benigno Aquino Jr. Foyer. That puts it in the middle of your gallery route, not after it.

The building is the old Legislative Building on Padre Burgos Avenue, a neoclassical block of columns and pale stone that once held the Philippine Senate. Now it holds the national collection of Filipino painting and sculpture. Papakape is a Filipino-inspired coffee brand with a community focus, and it brings cream sodas and coffee to one of Manila's most visited cultural landmarks.

## Getting to the foyer

The museum sits on the Rizal Park side of Ermita, facing Agrifina Circle. The closest rail option is LRT Line 1:

- **Central Terminal Station**: walk south past Manila City Hall, then about 10–15 minutes on foot.
- **United Nations Station**: a similar walk north along Taft Avenue toward the park.
- **By Grab or taxi**: ask for the National Museum of Fine Arts on Padre Burgos Avenue, not the Anthropology building across the circle.

Expect a security check at the entrance. From there, go up to the 4th floor and look for the Sen. Benigno Aquino Jr. Foyer.

## Pairing coffee with the collection

The café is a break, so plan the galleries around it. The museum's best-known work, Juan Luna's *Spoliarium*, hangs in a hall of its own on a lower floor. It is a huge canvas of dead gladiators being dragged away, and it draws the densest crowds in the building. See it first, then climb to the foyer for a cream soda before the upper galleries.

Give the whole visit two to three hours. That is enough for the main halls, a seated drink and a slow walk through the old Senate Session Hall. If you have energy left, the National Museum of Anthropology and the National Museum of Natural History are a short walk across Agrifina Circle. Intramuros is about 15 minutes away on foot.

## Using a café inside a national museum

Museum rules come first here. Food and drinks normally stay in the café area, so finish your cup before going back into the galleries. Staff watch closely near the paintings, and a cup in hand next to the *Spoliarium* will get you stopped.

A few practical habits help:

- **Check two schedules.** The café and the museum may keep different hours. The museum has closed on some weekdays in the past, so confirm on the National Museum of the Philippines' official channels and Papakape's social pages.
- **Ask about reservations.** It is unlikely you will need one for a coffee, but the branch is new, so confirm it if you are bringing a group.
- **Ask what's local.** Papakape is built around Filipino-inspired drinks. Asking staff which cream soda or coffee they recommend is the quickest way through a menu you don't know.
- **Go early.** School groups and weekend families fill Manila's free-entry museums. Arriving at opening usually puts you ahead of the tour buses, and you get the foyer before it fills up.
- **Payment.** Bring some cash in pesos in case card or e-wallet payments such as GCash aren't working yet at a newly opened counter. Prices are posted at the counter.

Leave large bags at your hotel if you can. A light day pack makes the stairs, the security check and a seat in the foyer much easier.
