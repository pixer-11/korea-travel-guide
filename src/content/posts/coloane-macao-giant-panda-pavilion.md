## Bamboo, glass and a hillside in Coloane

Behind the glass, a panda is sitting on its haunches. It strips a bamboo stalk with its teeth and drops the woody leftovers into a pile beside it. That slow, methodical eating is most of what you came for, and at the Macao Giant Panda Pavilion you can watch it from a few metres away.

The pavilion is built into the green slope of Seac Pai Van Park in Coloane, Macau's quietest district. The bears live in indoor and outdoor habitats, and you look into both through large viewing windows. Education exhibits explain how pandas live in the wild, and the gift shop by the exit leans heavily on plush toys.

## Why the pandas are in Macau

The pavilion opened in 2011. It was built for a pair of giant pandas given to Macau by China's central government to mark the tenth anniversary of the 1999 handover. In 2016 the pavilion had twin cubs, the first giant pandas ever born in Macau, which gave it a lasting place in local affection.

The pandas living here have changed over the years, so check the pavilion's official information to see who is currently in residence. It is a well-known attraction rather than a secret. Macau families treat it as a standard weekend outing, and visitors staying in Cotai add it on easily.

## The one o'clock gap

The schedule here is unusual. The pavilion opens at 10am and closes from 1pm to 2pm, then reopens until 5pm. That applies every day from Tuesday to Sunday, and Monday is closed all day.

If you turn up at 12:45pm, you get a short visit before staff clear the building. A late morning bus from Cotai can easily leave you with too little time.

- **Best window:** from 10am. Pandas are typically fed and active in the morning.
- **Lunch break:** use the hour to walk Seac Pai Van Park or eat in Coloane, then come back at 2pm.
- **Late afternoon:** pandas spend a lot of the day sleeping, and a curled-up black-and-white back is a common sight before 5pm.
- **Monday:** closed, so plan Coloane around another day.

## Getting to Seac Pai Van Park

The pavilion's address is Estrada de Seac Pai Van, the road that runs between Cotai and Coloane Village. Several public buses stop at Seac Pai Van Park, including routes 21A and 26A from the Macau Peninsula and Cotai. Check the route on the Macau transport bureau's map before you set off.

A taxi from the big Cotai resorts is a short ride. Ask for Seac Pai Van Park, or show the Chinese name, 石排灣郊野公園, if the driver hesitates. Inside the park, follow the signs up the slope to the pavilion. The paths climb a little, so allow a few minutes on foot.

## Watching without the scrum

Visits are controlled. Staff manage how many people are inside the viewing areas at once, so on a busy weekend there may be a short wait before you go through. Entry details and the current ticket price are on the official Seac Pai Van Park information and at the gate. Read them there, not on old blog posts.

- **No flash.** Photography is welcome, but flash bothers the animals and staff will stop you.
- **Keep your voice down near the glass.** Banging on it or calling to the pandas is frowned on, and children are told the same.
- **Move along the window.** People shuffle sideways so everyone gets a turn at the front, especially where a panda is close to the glass.
- **Arrive at opening on weekends.** Because 10am is also feeding time, it usually beats the family crowds that build later.

## How long to give it

The pavilion itself takes 30 minutes to an hour, depending on how long you stay at each window. Spend some time on the education exhibits as well. They explain bamboo diets, breeding and conservation in plain language, and they work well with children who have already spent a few minutes watching a panda eat.

To make a half day of it, add the rest of Seac Pai Van Park. Its shaded paths and hillside planting are a relief after the casino floors of Cotai, and some of Coloane's walking trails start nearby.

## After the pandas: the rest of Coloane

Coloane rewards a slower pace. Coloane Village is a short ride south. Its low Portuguese-era buildings, the small Chapel of St. Francis Xavier and the waterfront are a good place to spend the lunch break or the afternoon.

Further east, Hac Sa Beach has a long stretch of dark sand. Higher up on Coloane Hill stands the tall white A-Ma statue. Together with the pavilion, these make a full day that feels nothing like the Macau of the Cotai Strip.
