## Desert rock on the waterfront

The show is on Sunday, December 20, 2026. Queens of the Stone Age bring their tour to Spark Arena, Auckland's largest indoor concert room. The band formed in Palm Desert, California, in the mid-1990s, and Josh Homme has led it from the start.

The sound is heavy, groove-locked riffing with a dry sense of humour. The singles most people know are:

- "No One Knows"
- "Go With the Flow"
- "Little Sister"
- "Make It Wit Chu"

## Inside Spark Arena

The arena is a single enclosed bowl. A flat floor faces the stage and raked tiers of seating wrap around three sides. Floor tickets put you standing in the crush. Tier seats give you a clean view down onto the lights and the band.

Concourses ring the bowl behind the seating, with bars and food counters along them. The building sits in Quay Park, between the rail lines and Ports of Auckland's container wharves. Look out from the forecourt and you see cranes, not the city. In late December Auckland is in early summer, so it's usually still light as the crowd gathers outside.

## Getting to Mahuhu Crescent and back

Most people come on foot from downtown. From the Britomart transport hub at the bottom of Queen Street, the walk east along Quay Street or Beach Road takes about 10 minutes and is flat the whole way. The Strand station is closer still, on the Parnell side.

- **Train:** Britomart is the end of the line for every Auckland suburban route. On a Sunday night, services run less often than on weekdays.
- **Bus:** many city routes finish at or near Britomart.
- **Ferry:** the Downtown Ferry Terminal is a short walk west of Britomart, so Devonport and Waiheke are reachable. Late Sunday sailings are limited.
- **Car:** paid parking is limited around the arena, and traffic on Quay Street backs up after the encore.

Visitors who come from outside the city usually stay in the CBD, Britannia Quarter or Parnell. All three are within walking distance, so there's no transport to plan for the trip home.

## Doing a Spark Arena night the Auckland way

The pre-show routine is fairly settled. Fans tend to eat and drink downtown first, in the Britomart precinct or along Viaduct Harbour, and then walk over together. The arena's own bars have queues at the start and between sets. That's why many people only buy drinks inside once the headliner is on.

Floor fans who want the barrier usually line up outside well before doors. Seated ticket holders tend to wander in during the support act. After the show, thousands of people walk back towards Britomart at the same moment. Locals often hang back for a drink in Parnell or the CBD and let the platforms clear. Rideshare pickups are easier a few blocks from the arena than at the front doors.
