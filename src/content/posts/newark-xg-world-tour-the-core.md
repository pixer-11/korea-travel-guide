## From Manhattan to Lafayette Street

Rail is the easy answer here. Newark Penn Station is the hub, and from its platforms the arena is a short walk east through downtown.

Most fans coming from New York use one of these:

- **PATH** from World Trade Center, which ends at Newark Penn
- **NJ Transit** trains from New York Penn Station, the quickest ride on the Northeast Corridor and North Jersey Coast lines
- **Amtrak**, which also stops at Newark Penn, for anyone coming up from Philadelphia or Washington

From **Newark Liberty International Airport**, the AirTrain links to its own NJ Transit station, and one stop on the train reaches Newark Penn. Fans flying in for the show often land, ride in, and never touch a car.

Driving works too. The arena is surrounded by paid garages and surface lots, and traffic on the surrounding streets thickens as the crowd converges. Many drivers end up parking further out and walking the last stretch.

## Dinner across the tracks in the Ironbound

Walk out the other side of Newark Penn Station and you are in the Ironbound. It is Newark's old Portuguese and Brazilian neighbourhood, and Ferry Street is its spine. The street is lined with churrascarias, Portuguese seafood houses and bakeries selling pastéis de nata.

The food comes in big portions:

- **Rodízio** at Brazilian grills, where servers carve skewered meats at the table
- **Mariscada**, a Portuguese seafood stew of shellfish in a tomato and wine broth
- **Pork alentejana**, pork and clams cooked together
- **Sangria** by the pitcher, which is how most tables order it

Because the neighbourhood sits right beside the station, it works on either side of the show. Some fans eat there before walking over to Lafayette Street. Others cross back after the encore and order a late plate while the platforms clear.
