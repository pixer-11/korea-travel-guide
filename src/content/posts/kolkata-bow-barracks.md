## Red brick, green shutters, washing lines

Bow Barracks is a street to walk, not a site with a gate. Rows of three-storey red-brick blocks face each other across a narrow lane off Bow Street. They were built around the First World War as housing for soldiers. Balconies stack up above you, many with painted railings, potted plants and clothes drying in the air.

Today the blocks belong to one of Kolkata's last concentrated Anglo-Indian communities. The families who live here are the reason to come. You will hear English spoken across balconies and see Christian images beside doorways. On many ground floors, people sit outside and watch the lane.

## Walking in from Bowbazar

The enclave sits between Bentinck Street and Chittaranjan Avenue, close to Bowbazar's rows of gold jewellery shops. The easiest arrival is by metro:

- **Central** (Blue Line): the shortest walk, crossing Bowbazar Street.
- **Chandni Chowk** (Blue Line): a slightly longer walk south through the electronics market.
- **Esplanade**: a longer walk, but it links easily with the rest of a central Kolkata day.

The lane itself takes 30 to 45 minutes to explore slowly. The old Chinatown at Tiretti Bazaar is only a few streets north, and its early-morning Chinese breakfast stalls pair naturally with a morning visit here. Lalbazar, the colonial-era police headquarters, is also within walking distance.

## When the lane is worth timing

Bow Barracks is open 24 hours, since it is simply a street. When you go still matters. It is calmest between 7am and 10am on weekdays and weekends alike. That window gives you soft light on the brickwork and space to photograph the facades without crowds.

Try not to arrive between 1pm and 11pm on weekends. That is when visitor numbers peak and the narrow lane fills. Christmas week is the exception worth crowding for. Residents string lights and stars between the blocks, and on Christmas Eve the lane becomes a street party. Families also sell homemade fruit cake and wines, ginger and grape among them, from their doorways.

## Being a good guest on Bow Street

Everything here is somebody's front door. The community is used to visitors, and it is used to visitors who behave badly too. A few habits make the difference:

- **Ask before photographing people**, especially older residents on their steps. Buildings and street decorations are fair game from the lane.
- **Stay out of the stairwells** and courtyards unless someone invites you in. They are private homes, not galleries.
- **Keep the lane clear.** Residents drive scooters and carry shopping through it, so do not set up tripods in the middle.
- **Buy directly if something is for sale.** At Christmas, cakes and wine are sold from homes, usually for cash or UPI. The seller tells you the price at the door.

Nobody runs this as an attraction and there are no guides or ticket booths. Keep your voice low early in the morning, when people are opening shutters and heading to work. If a resident starts a conversation, take the time for it. Local stories of the barracks and the Anglo-Indian families who stayed are the real reason to visit.
