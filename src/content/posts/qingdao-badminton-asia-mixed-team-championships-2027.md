## Six days of team badminton on the Yellow Sea

This tournament is about the team, not the player. Each tie is a set of matches that covers every discipline: men's singles, women's singles, men's doubles, women's doubles and mixed doubles. A nation needs depth across all five to win, and that is why the scoreboard can swing so hard between sessions.

The 2027 edition runs from January 12 to 17 in Qingdao, the port city on the Shandong coast. Interest across East Asia is strong. Crowds follow China, Japan, Korea, Indonesia and their rivals, and a home crowd backs China. In a team event, a single match can decide a whole tie, and that keeps the arena tense well after the headline singles finish.

## How the week usually unfolds

The early days are group play. Several ties run back to back, and a session ticket often gives you more than one nation. It is the cheapest way to see a lot of badminton in one sitting, and it is when the smaller federations get their court time.

The later days narrow down to the knockout rounds. The final weekend of January 16–17 is when the biggest ties land. These sessions tend to draw the fullest stands, especially when China is playing.

- **Group stage:** long sessions, lots of nations, easier seating
- **Knockouts:** shorter, louder, the matches most fans travel for
- **Final day:** the deciding tie, with the trophy presented courtside

Badminton Asia publishes the official draw, the order of play and the venue details on its own site. Ticket links appear there as well.

## Cheering in a Chinese arena

Watching here has its own rhythm. During a rally the hall goes nearly silent, because players need to hear the shuttle off the strings. Then the point ends and the noise comes all at once: clapping, drums in the home sections and chants of "Jiāyóu!" (加油, literally "add oil"). It is the standard Chinese cheer, and visiting fans who join in are generally welcomed.

A few habits help:

- **Pay by phone.** Concession stands and taxis in Qingdao run almost entirely on Alipay and WeChat Pay. Both apps accept foreign bank cards once you set them up.
- **Carry your passport.** Ticketed events in China often tie entry to ID, and foreigners use the passport number.
- **No flash.** Flash photography distracts the players, and stewards act on it fast.
- **Move between points.** Ushers hold people at the aisle ends until a rally finishes.

The Chinese name for the event is 亚洲羽毛球混合团体锦标赛. Having it in your phone helps with taxi drivers and hotel staff.

## Getting to Qingdao in mid-January

Most international visitors fly into Qingdao Jiaodong International Airport, which lies well northwest of the old city. The Qingdao Metro links the airport with the urban area. Taxis and ride-hailing on DiDi are simple to use if you want a door-to-door ride to the arena district.

From Beijing or Shanghai, China's high-speed rail network reaches Qingdao North Station and the central Qingdao Station. The red-roofed German-era buildings are clustered around the central station, close to Zhanqiao Pier.

Winter on this coast means cold, dry days and a wind straight off the Yellow Sea. Temperatures hover around freezing. Visitors usually wear layers they can take off, since the arena is heated and the walk outside is not.

## Between sessions: the city around the courts

Qingdao rewards the free mornings before a tie. The **Tsingtao Brewery Museum** on Dengzhou Road is housed in the original German brewery building from 1903 and serves the beer at the end of the tour. **Zhanqiao Pier** runs out into the bay with its octagonal Huilan Pavilion at the tip. Walk east and you reach the villa streets of **Badaguan**, where each road is lined with a different kind of tree.

Farther along the shore, **May Fourth Square** has the big red spiral sculpture and views across Fushan Bay toward the Olympic Sailing Center. If you have a full rest day, **Laoshan** sits on the coast east of the city. It is a granite mountain with Taoist temples on its slopes, and the bare winter trails are quiet.

Dinner in Qingdao means seafood. Clams stir-fried with chilli (蛤蜊, *gala* in the local accent) are the city's signature. They go with fresh draught Tsingtao, which some neighbourhood shops still sell by the bag.

## Where the details live

Badminton Asia is the continental governing body and runs the championships. It publishes the dates, the venue, the draw and the ticket links on its official site. Results and match reports from each session go out on the same site and on the organisation's social channels.

Travelling fans usually build their trip around the final weekend. Many base themselves near the venue, or near the metro line that serves it, which keeps late-evening finishes simple.
