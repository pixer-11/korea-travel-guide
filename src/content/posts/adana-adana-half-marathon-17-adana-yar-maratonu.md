## A winter race in a summer city

Adana has a reputation for heat. In July and August the Çukurova plain is one of the hottest places in Turkey, and nobody sensible runs 21 kilometres through it at midday.

January is a different city. The 17th Adana Half Marathon takes place on January 10, 2027, when mornings on the plain are cool and days are usually mild. That makes the race one of the more comfortable stops on Turkey's winter road-running circuit.

## Why Adana runs in January

The timing is the whole point. Adana sits on the Seyhan River at the head of a wide, flat delta, with the Taurus Mountains to the north. That geography traps summer heat and humidity, so a road race here only makes sense in the cool months.

In January, early mornings can feel properly cold, especially before the sun clears the rooftops. Daytime temperatures usually climb to comfortable running weather. Runners typically line up in a throwaway layer or a bin bag and shed it in the first kilometre.

Rain is possible in a Mediterranean winter, though it rarely lasts all day. A light shell in the kit bag covers most of what the morning can do.

## Course, start area and entries

The verified facts don't include a start line or route, so this guide won't guess at one. The organiser's official race page carries the course map, start and finish location, and registration details.

What is general to Adana: it is flat. The city centre spreads across low ground on both banks of the Seyhan, so road races here tend to be friendly to anyone chasing a time. The riverside promenades, the Stone Bridge (Taşköprü) and the Sabancı Central Mosque are the landmarks most runners use to get their bearings.

At Turkish road races, runners usually collect their race kit and bib (göğüs numarası) at a kit distribution point (kit dağıtımı) before race day. Details for this edition sit with the organiser.

## Getting to Adana and finding your way

Adana is Turkey's fifth or sixth largest city, so it's easy to reach. Domestic flights connect it with Istanbul and Ankara, and the airport serving Adana sits outside the city. Long-distance buses run into the main otogar from across the country.

Inside the city, the Adana Metro runs a single line north to south. Taxis and city buses cover most other journeys, and road closures on race morning usually make walking the simplest way to reach the start.

Visiting runners usually stay in the central districts of Seyhan or Çukurova. Both are within reach of the river and its bridges.

## Liver at dawn, kebab at dusk

Adana eats meat for breakfast, and race weekend is a good excuse to join in. The local morning tradition is ciğer: skewered lamb liver grilled over charcoal and served with flatbread, raw onion and herbs. Most runners save it for the morning after.

After the finish, the obvious order is Adana kebabı. This is the hand-minced lamb kebab pressed onto a wide flat skewer, and it's named after the city. Here is how ordering usually works:

- **Ask for it acılı or acısız.** That means spicy or mild. In Adana, spicy is the default.
- **Expect the side plates first.** Usually that's onion with sumac, parsley, grilled peppers and tomatoes, plus a stack of thin lavaş.
- **Order şalgam.** This fermented purple carrot and turnip drink comes acılı or acısız too, and locals swear by it with kebab.
- **Order by the portion (porsiyon).** One portion is a full meal. Hungry runners order a portion and a half (bir buçuk).

Cards are taken widely in sit-down restaurants, but small ciğer stalls often prefer cash. Rounding up the bill or leaving small change is the usual way to tip. The current prices are on the menu board or printed menu.

## The rest of the weekend

Adana rewards a spare day, and most of what's worth seeing is walkable on tired legs:

- **Taşköprü (Stone Bridge):** a Roman-era bridge over the Seyhan. It's among the oldest bridges in the world still standing in a city, now closed to cars and an easy shakeout walk.
- **Sabancı Merkez Camii:** the huge riverside mosque with six minarets, one of the largest in Turkey. Visitors cover shoulders and knees, women cover their hair, and everyone stays outside during prayer.
- **Büyük Saat (Great Clock Tower):** the 19th-century Ottoman clock tower in the old centre. Around it lie the covered bazaars and the coppersmiths' lane, Kazancılar Çarşısı.
- **Seyhan Dam Lake:** a large reservoir on the northern edge of the city, with lakeside paths for a recovery walk.
- **Varda Viaduct:** a stone railway bridge in the foothills north of Adana, well known from the opening chase in the James Bond film *Skyfall*. It's a half-day trip by car.

Most runners spend two or three nights. That leaves one day for the race and one for the bridges, the bazaar and a long lunch of kebab.
