## Finding it in Bellevue, not Seattle

Despite the name, this temple stands in Bellevue, on 148th Ave SE, about a 20-minute drive east of downtown Seattle across Lake Washington. It belongs to The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and serves the wider Puget Sound region. The building rises with a single spire topped by the golden Angel Moroni statue, visible from the surrounding residential streets well before you reach the gate.

Free visitor parking sits on-site, and the grounds are landscaped with manicured lawns, seasonal flower beds, and reflecting pools that frame the exterior. Most people spend 20 to 40 minutes here, walking the grounds and reading the visitor information near the entrance.

## Hours that actually work

The temple is closed both Sunday and Monday, unlike most tourist sites that shut midweek. Tuesday, Wednesday, and Saturday it opens at 5:30 AM; Thursday and Friday it opens later, at 6:30 AM. Every open day it closes at 8:15 PM, giving a consistent evening window across the week.

Because the interior is reserved for church ordinances and not open to the general public, your visit centers on the grounds and exterior architecture rather than a guided tour inside. Confirm hours before driving out, since holiday closures and maintenance days aren't reflected in the standard weekly schedule.

## Beating the weekend rush

Weekends see steady visitor traffic from 8am to 8pm, largely families and church members attending services or bringing children to see the grounds. If you want a quieter walk, aim for a weekday morning right at opening, either the 5:30 AM slot on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Saturday, or the 6:30 AM slot Thursday and Friday.

Early light also flatters the white exterior and grounds for photos, with fewer cars in the small parking area. Evenings near the 8:15 PM close are another lower-traffic option, especially outside the Saturday peak.

## Grounds etiquette worth knowing

This is an active house of worship, not a museum, so dress and behavior expectations lean modest and quiet. Visitors are welcome to walk the exterior grounds respectfully; loud conversation, drones, or picnicking on the lawns aren't appropriate here.

Photography of the exterior and grounds is generally fine, but be mindful of people arriving for services or ceremonies. There's no admission fee and no ticket to book, since the grounds are open to the public during listed hours without reservation.

If you're combining this with other Bellevue stops, the Bellevue Botanical Garden and downtown Bellevue's shopping district are both within a short drive, making it easy to pair a quiet morning here with an afternoon elsewhere.
