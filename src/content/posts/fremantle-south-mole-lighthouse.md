## A green light at the harbour mouth

The road narrows, then the land runs out. South Mole is a long rock breakwater pushing west into the Indian Ocean. The small lighthouse at its tip marks the southern edge of the channel into Fremantle Harbour.

Look north across the water and you'll see its partner on North Mole. The two lights frame the gap that every container ship, cruise liner and Rottnest ferry has to pass through. It's a working navigation light, so you look at it from outside rather than climbing it.

What you come for is the setting:

- Boulders stacked on either side of the mole
- Open ocean to the west
- Cranes and wharves of the inner harbour to the east
- A clear line of sight to the horizon

## Walking or driving the mole

The listed address is 1 Fleet St, at the West End of Fremantle near the WA Maritime Museum. From there the access road runs out along the mole, and you can drive or walk all the way to the light.

Driving is the easy option if you're short on time or carrying fishing gear. Parking is informal and spread along the mole's edge. Walking is better if you want to stop and watch the water on both sides.

From Fremantle Station, at the end of the Fremantle Line from Perth, allow a decent walk. The route heads through the West End toward the Maritime Museum and then out along the breakwater. Once you're on the mole there's no shade, so cover up properly in summer.

## Watching the ships come through

Timing your visit to a ship movement is the experience you won't get anywhere else in Fremantle. The channel between the two moles is narrow. Large vessels pass close enough that you can read the names on their hulls and watch the tugs working alongside.

Rottnest ferries run through the same gap several times a day, heading out toward the island on the horizon. Fremantle Ports publishes upcoming shipping movements, which is worth a look before you go if you'd like to time your visit to an arrival or departure.

Between ships, the view is all water. Watch for the swell breaking on the seaward boulders and the colour shift where river water meets the sea.

## Sunset, the Doctor and the weekend rush

The mole faces west, so sunset drops straight into the ocean ahead of you. Google lists the site as open 24 hours at the moment. Port access can still change, so obey any gates or signage on the day.

Weekends have a clear pattern. It's usually quietest between 12pm and 2pm. Try not to arrive between 7am and 9am, when early fishers and walkers fill the road. Also avoid 2pm–11pm, the long afternoon and evening stretch when most people drive out for sunset and stay into the night.

In summer the afternoon sea breeze, known locally as the Fremantle Doctor, usually arrives after lunch. Out on an exposed breakwater it can be strong. A midday visit dodges both the crowd and the worst of the wind. Bring a layer if you're staying for sunset.

## Sharing the rocks with the rod-and-bucket crowd

The mole is mainly a fishing spot, and the lighthouse is a bonus. Most people out there have a rod, a bucket and a folding chair. Leave them room, and look behind you before you swing a bag or a camera tripod near someone's line.

A few habits that keep it pleasant:

- **Stay off the outer boulders** when swell is running; they're slippery and waves wash over them.
- **Drive slowly** on the access road, as people walk on it and car doors open without warning.
- **Take your rubbish back**, including bait bags and fishing line, which tangles seabirds.
- **Keep drones grounded** unless you've checked the rules; this is an active port with vessel traffic.

There's no kiosk or café out on the mole. Bring water and anything else you need from town. Because it's under the radar compared with the West End's main sights, you'll often have long stretches of the walk to yourself, even when the town centre is full.

## Before and after: the West End end of Fremantle

South Mole is part of the town, so it pairs easily with the West End. The WA Maritime Museum sits near the start of the mole, and its galleries cover the harbour you've just been looking at.

Close by:

- **Bathers Beach**, a short sandy stretch just south of the harbour mouth
- **The Round House**, Western Australia's oldest public building, on the headland above the beach
- **Fishing Boat Harbour**, the working marina known for its fish and chips along the waterfront
- **Victoria Quay and the B Shed**, where Rottnest ferries depart

Do the Round House and Bathers Beach in the late morning, then walk out to the lighthouse for the weekend lull from 12pm to 2pm. Alternatively, finish with fish and chips at Fishing Boat Harbour after sunset.

## How long to give it

Plan for about an hour if you walk out, take in the view and walk back. Allow longer if you're waiting for a ship or staying for sunset. Driving out for a quick look takes far less, but you'll miss the slow change of the view from harbour cranes to open sea.

On a calm, clear day you can see Rottnest Island from the tip. On a wild one, the spray off the boulders is reason enough to go.
