## A dock that works as the lake's front door

Shuishe Pier is where Sun Moon Lake starts for most people. It sits on Mingsheng Street in Shuishe Village, on the lake's western shore. Floating walkways run out from the shore to a row of moored passenger boats. Across the water, forested ridges rise straight out of the lake and fold back into the mountains of Nantou County.

This is not a quiet corner. It's among the most-reviewed spots on the lake, and it's consistently well rated. Buses unload here, bike shops line the streets behind it, and restaurants plus a waterfront bar face the water. Come for the boats and the views, and plan your timing around everyone else doing the same.

## The three-pier loop

This loop is the reason the pier exists. Ferries from Shuishe cross to two other docks, and together they make a triangle across the lake:

- **Shuishe Pier**: the village hub, with hotels, food and the bus terminal.
- **Xuanguang Pier**: a short climb up to Xuanguang Temple. The stall near the dock selling tea eggs simmered in mushroom-and-tea broth is a lake institution.
- **Ita Thao Pier**: the Thao community's village on the east shore, with a pedestrian street of snack stalls and the lower station of the Sun Moon Lake Ropeway.

Each crossing is short. From the deck you get the clearest view of Lalu Island, the small, sacred islet of the Thao people that sits low in the water between the piers. Most people ride the full loop and spend an hour or two at each stop. Budget half a day if you want to climb to the temple and ride the ropeway.

## Getting to Shuishe

Buses do most of the work. Nantou Bus runs services from Taichung High Speed Rail Station and Taichung city to Sun Moon Lake, ending in Shuishe a short walk from the pier. From Taipei, direct Kuo-Kuang coaches make the trip in a few hours, and the mountain road is part of the ride.

Most of these buses accept an EasyCard, so you can tap on rather than count coins. If you're driving, Shuishe has public car parks near the village. They fill quickly on weekends and holidays, so arrive early or plan to walk in from a lot further out.

Some bus and ferry companies sell combined passes that bundle the boat loop with transport. Check the current options at the Taichung HSR bus counter or the official Sun Moon Lake tourism site before you buy.

## South on the Xiangshan bike path

The shore path is the best thing to do without a boat. From Shuishe, a dedicated cycling track follows the water south toward Xiangshan. Stretches of it run on boardwalks built out over the lake, and it has often been listed among the most scenic bike routes in Asia.

Rental shops cluster in the streets just behind the pier, with standard and electric bikes. The ride ends at the Xiangshan Visitor Center, designed by Norihiko Dan. It's a long, curving concrete building with a grassed roof you can walk up for a view back across the lake. There and back takes a relaxed couple of hours with stops.

If you'd rather walk, the first boardwalk section south of the pier is pleasant on foot. It thins out quickly once you're away from the village.

## After the day boats stop

The pier never closes, even when the ferries aren't running. That changes how you should use it. Once the tour groups head back to Taichung in the late afternoon, the walkways clear out and the village slows down. The waterfront bar and the restaurants facing the lake become the main event.

Shuishe is on the west shore, so dawn is the other reward. The sun comes up behind the eastern ridges and lights the water in front of you. Morning mist often lies on the lake here, especially in the cooler months. If you're staying in the village, walking down before breakfast is the easiest win of the trip.

Ferry schedules change by season, so confirm the first and last departures at the ticket booths or on the operators' posted boards. Don't assume a boat is running just because the pier is open.

## Buying tickets and boarding like a regular

Several ferry companies work from Shuishe, and each has its own booth near the head of the pier. They mostly sell the same loop. Here's how it works:

- **Keep your ticket.** Loop tickets are usually valid for hop-on, hop-off travel all day. You'll show them again at Xuanguang and Ita Thao.
- **Stay with your company.** A ticket bought from one operator is generally only good on that operator's boats. Look for its flag or name when boarding at the other piers.
- **Check posted fares.** Prices and pass options are posted at each booth. Compare them there rather than trusting what a tout on the street tells you.
- **Cash still matters.** Card and EasyCard acceptance varies between booths and small stalls, so carry some Taiwanese dollars for tea eggs and snacks.

Tour buses cluster in the late morning and early afternoon, especially on weekends and Taiwanese public holidays. Taking the first boats of the day usually means shorter boarding lines and a less crowded deck. Queues form in lines on the pontoon, and staff wave groups aboard in order. Hold your place and let passengers off before stepping on.

## Beyond the pier

Shuishe is a good base for the rest of the lake. The round-lake bus stops here and continues north to Wenwu Temple, whose terraced halls look out over the water. From Ita Thao, the ropeway climbs over the ridge to the Formosan Aboriginal Culture Village.

If you only have a day, do this: take an early boat loop, have lunch at Ita Thao, then ride the bike path south from Shuishe in the afternoon light. Stay the night if you can and catch the lake at dawn.
