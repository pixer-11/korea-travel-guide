## Thirteen heavens in the scrub

The road gives way to open ranch country, mesquite and cactus. Out here, in a valley above the Laja River basin, Cañada de la Virgen's stone platforms rise from the scrub. This is a remote archaeological complex of several pyramid structures, first built in the 7th century. It still gets far fewer visitors than the colonial streets back in town.

The centrepiece is the House of the Thirteen Heavens, a stepped pyramid with a sunken patio in front. Archaeologists read it as an astronomical instrument: its alignments track the movements of the sun and moon across the horizon. Nearby stands the circular House of the Wind, a rounded shape that sets it apart from the squared platforms around it.

## Getting out to Km 9.5

No public bus serves the site, so you need a car, a taxi, or an organised tour from San Miguel.

- **Route:** take the San Miguel de Allende–Guanajuato highway and turn off at Km 9.5.
- **Final stretch:** an unpaved access road leads to the visitor area. Drive it slowly in a low car.
- **Taxi:** agree the return pickup time before you get out. Phone signal can be weak this far out.
- **Tours:** several San Miguel operators run half-day trips with a guide. A guide helps, because the signage on site is limited.

From the visitor area you go most of the way by site transport. The last stretch is a walk along a path through the scrub.

## Walking the ceremonial core

Expect two to three hours from leaving town to getting back. About an hour of that is on the ground. The walking is mostly level, but the path is stony and there's almost no shade.

What to bring:

- A hat and sunscreen. The site sits at high altitude, and the midday sun on bare rock is strong.
- More water than you think you need.
- Closed shoes for loose gravel.
- Binoculars or a zoom lens to pick out details on the upper tiers of the pyramid.

Look for the stairway on the main pyramid's front and the enclosed patio below it. Stand in the patio and face east. The alignment that gives the House of the Thirteen Heavens its reputation makes most sense from there.

## Timing the trip and site manners

Hours are 10am to 5:30pm, Tuesday to Sunday, and the site is closed every Monday. Don't plan a Monday outing; it's the most common way visitors waste the drive. Because you go part of the way by transport and then walk, arrive well before closing. Entering around 10am also beats the strongest afternoon heat.

Rules here follow Mexico's national archaeological sites:

- Stay on the marked paths.
- Don't climb any structure unless staff direct you to.
- Leave stones where they lie.
- Drones and tripods usually need prior permission, so ask at the entrance before you set one up.

Entry prices are posted at the ticket point. Bring pesos in small notes in case the card terminal isn't working out here. Afterwards, you can pair the morning with the hot-spring pools along the same Guanajuato road. Or drive back for a late lunch around San Miguel's Jardín Principal.
