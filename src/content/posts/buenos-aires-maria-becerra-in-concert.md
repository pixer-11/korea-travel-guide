## Two weeks of "La Nena de Argentina"

Maria Becerra takes over Movistar Arena from November 13 to 27, 2026. Rather than one stadium night, it is a run of indoor shows spread across two weeks.

She grew up in Quilmes, in the southern Greater Buenos Aires sprawl. She built her audience on YouTube before music took over, and albums like *Animal* and *La Nena de Argentina* turned her into one of the country's biggest pop and urbano acts. In a hometown crowd, the singalongs to "Corazón Vacío" and "Automático" tend to be loud enough to drown her out.

## Inside Movistar Arena

The arena opened in 2019. It is a roofed, purpose-built concert hall at Humboldt 450, on the Villa Crespo side of the Chacarita border, so the shows don't depend on spring weather. Tickets split into two broad types:

- **Campo:** the standing floor in front of the stage
- **Platea:** tiered seating that rings the floor, with numbered seats

The ticket links for every date sit on the arena's official site and on Maria Becerra's own channels. That is where each show's section map lives too.

## Getting to Villa Crespo

Subte Line B is the easiest way in. Get off at **Dorrego**, then walk a few blocks south along Humboldt to the arena. On a show night the route is easy to follow, because the whole crowd walks it.

Other options:

- **Bus:** the colectivos along Avenida Corrientes and Avenida Córdoba pass nearby
- **Rail:** Federico Lacroze station (Urquiza line) is a longer walk north-west
- **Ride apps:** Uber, Cabify and DiDi all operate in the city

At the end of the night, many fans walk to Corrientes rather than queue at the station entrance.

Fans staying in Palermo Soho or Palermo Hollywood cross Avenida Juan B. Justo on foot in roughly 20 minutes. Those bars and parrillas are where many fans eat before the show and drink after it.

## Doing a show night the porteño way

Buenos Aires runs late, and concert nights follow the city's clock. Dinner at 10pm is normal here, so the eating usually happens after the encore rather than before it. Outside the arena, street vendors work the pavement selling choripán, drinks and unofficial merch.

How people pay and behave:

- **Paying:** card and the Mercado Pago QR app are the default almost everywhere, and many small vendors also take cash pesos
- **Ordering:** ask for "un choripán" or "una birra"
- **Campo fans:** they usually queue along the arena perimeter well before doors, because the barrier spots go first
- **Platea ticket holders:** they tend to arrive closer to the start time, since their seats are numbered
- **Chants:** expect the crowd to chant her name between songs

Within the area, the Cementerio de la Chacarita and the Mercado de Pulgas antiques market on Avenida Dorrego both sit within walking distance. They make a good daytime pairing for visitors with a free afternoon on a show day.
