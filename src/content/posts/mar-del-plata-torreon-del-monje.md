## A castle that was built as a lookout

It looks medieval, but it isn't. Torreón del Monje went up in 1904, a private lookout built for the businessman Ernesto Tornquist on a rocky outcrop above the Atlantic. Its battlements, turret and stone walls are a seaside folly. For more than a century, it has been the shape everyone in Mar del Plata recognises.

The name comes from a local legend about a monk, told in several versions. Nobody needs the story to enjoy the place, though. The draw is plain: a stone tower on the rocks, the South Atlantic breaking right below it, and the whole curve of the city's coast laid out to the north.

## Crossing the bridge over the coast road

The approach is part of the visit. A stone footbridge arches over the coastal boulevard, so you reach the tower from the slope of Loma Stella Maris without dodging traffic. Below the bridge, cars and buses run along the seafront. Ahead of you, the tower stands on its outcrop with nothing behind it but water.

That bridge is where most of the photographs get taken. Stand at its middle to frame the tower against the sea. Walk onto the terraces and look back for the opposite view, the city's apartment blocks stacked along the bay. Give yourself time on both sides. The angle changes a lot over a few metres.

## Sunrise on an east-facing shore

There's a quirk of geography here. Mar del Plata faces east, so the sun comes up out of the ocean, not down into it. The tower currently opens at 7am every day, and in the colder months, when sunrise is later, that means you can be on the terrace for first light.

In summer the sun rises well before the doors open. You can still watch it from the bridge and the coastal walkway outside. Opening hours change through the year, so check the current schedule before you plan a dawn visit around them.

Late afternoon has its own pull. The light swings behind you and turns the stone warm, and the bay goes gold toward the Casino. Closing time is currently 7pm Monday to Thursday and 8pm Friday to Sunday, so weekends give you an extra hour.

## Coffee with a view: the café-restaurant

Inside and on the terraces there's a café-restaurant, mid-range in price, with tables facing the water. Many people treat it as a coffee stop on a coastal walk. Others make it a proper meal. Either way you're paying partly for the setting, and it's one of the most-reviewed places in the city.

The menu and prices are posted at the venue, so look there before you sit. The building also hosts functions, which means parts of it may now and then be closed for a private event. If you have your heart set on a particular table or terrace, ask at the entrance when you arrive.

## Beating the crowds at the railings

This is one of Mar del Plata's best-known stops, and it gets busy. The city is Argentina's classic summer resort, and January and February bring the heaviest crowds to the whole seafront. If you can come in March, autumn or spring, you'll have a much easier time.

A few habits help:

- **Come early.** The 7am opening is well ahead of the day-trippers and families who come for the afternoon walk.
- **Avoid the obvious meal times.** Argentines lunch late and dine later still. Mid-morning coffee is usually calmer than the long weekend lunch.
- **Order at the table.** Service in Argentine cafés is usually table service, so take a seat and wait for a waiter rather than queueing at a counter.
- **Tipping.** Leaving around ten percent in cash for the waiter is the usual custom, even when you pay the bill by card.
- **Say it right.** Locals call it simply "el Torreón". Asking for "el Torreón del Monje" gets you pointed the right way by anyone.

Bring a layer even in summer. The wind comes straight off the Atlantic here, and the terraces have no shelter from it.

## Walking the coast in either direction

The tower is best taken as part of a walk. Head north along the seafront and you reach the Bristol beaches and the grand old Casino and Hotel Provincial, the city's main promenade. It's a flat, easy stroll with the sea on your right the whole way.

Head south and the coast turns rockier. Playa Varese sits just past the tower, a small sheltered cove between two breakwaters, and it's popular with families because the water is calmer. Beyond it, the road rounds Cabo Corrientes toward Playa Grande, with surfers and the more upmarket beach clubs.

Uphill, Loma Stella Maris is worth the climb. The Stella Maris church sits on the hill, and the Torre Tanque, the old water tower, has its own lookout over the city. Combine all three and you get a half-day loop of views from sea level to the top of the hill.

## How long to give it

For the bridge, the terraces and photos, plan on 30 to 45 minutes. If you add a coffee or a meal, allow an hour and a half or more. Walking from the Casino along the seafront and back with a stop here fills a relaxed morning.

Taxis and ride-hailing cars drop off on the coast road below the bridge. Several city bus routes also run along the seafront. If you're staying anywhere near the centre or the Bristol beaches, walking is the best way to arrive: you see the tower from a long way off, rising from the rocks ahead of you.
