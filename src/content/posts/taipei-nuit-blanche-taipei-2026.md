## A city that stays up on purpose

Most of Taipei winds down after the night markets close. Nuit Blanche Taipei is the one weekend each year when art keeps going through the dark. It is an annual all-night arts festival with free admission, modelled on the Paris Nuit Blanche that started in 2002. Installations, performances and light works fill public space from the afternoon into the following day.

The 2026 edition was themed "Night ka shi". The name plays on *nakashi* (那卡西), the roaming musicians with guitar and accordion who once worked the hot-spring inns of Beitou. It suggested a night of song, wandering and a bit of nostalgia, though the programme itself belonged to the organiser.

## Dates and the overnight shape

Nuit Blanche Taipei 2026 was set for November 7 to November 8, 2026. That was a week later than the previous editions at Expo Park, which ran from the afternoon of October 31 into November 1.

The announced format matched earlier years: an afternoon start, then straight through the night into the second day. The organiser handled the dates, venue, programme and running times, along with any registration for individual works.

## Taipei Expo Park and the Yuanshan approach

Recent editions have been held at Taipei Expo Park, the green belt left over from the 2010 Taipei International Flora Expo. It spreads out from Yuanshan Station (R14, red Tamsui-Xinyi Line). Exit 1 drops you straight into the park grounds.

The area is big and split into zones. Inside you will find:

- The Expo Dome and the flora pavilions
- Open lawns near the Zhongshan Soccer Stadium
- Fine Arts Park across Zhongshan North Road, home to the **Taipei Fine Arts Museum**

Going from one end to the other is a real walk. A full loop of the installations usually takes a few hours rather than a quick pass.

## Free entry, and what that means for crowds

The 2026 edition had no gate charge and no general ticket. Free entry changes how a night like this feels. People tend to come and go all night, families leave after the early evening shows, and a younger crowd moves in as it gets late.

The flip side is that the most photographed works usually draw queues. Attendees tend to go to the headline installations before dark or long after midnight. In the early evening they mostly wander the edges.

## Moving through the night at Yuanshan

A few local habits shape a night out at Yuanshan:

- **EasyCard.** The same stored-value card works on the MRT, buses and YouBike, and at the convenience stores around the station.
- **Last trains.** Regular MRT service ends around midnight. People staying for the small hours usually switch to taxis, ride-hailing or YouBike, whose docks are by the station exits.
- **Weather.** November nights in Taipei are mild but damp, and the northeast monsoon often brings drizzle. A compact umbrella is the usual kit.
- **Queueing.** Lines at installations and food trucks form quietly, in single file, and people keep to them. Flash photography and touching the works are generally frowned on.

Volunteers in event vests typically direct the crowds and hand out maps. Most visitors use the printed or online map rather than just following the crowd.

## Before and after the art

Yuanshan sits next to some of north Taipei's most rewarding older streets. A short walk west leads to **Dalongdong Baoan Temple**, with its carved stone dragon columns, and the quieter **Taipei Confucius Temple** next door.

For food, **Shilin Night Market** is one MRT stop north at Jiantan Station, and it stays lively late. **Beitou**, the home of the nakashi tradition behind the 2026 theme, sits further up the same red line. Its hot-spring district is where the tradition took root.
