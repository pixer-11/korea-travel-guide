## ANOTR's NO ART lands in India

This edition was billed as a first for the country. NO ART is an international dance music festival built around the Dutch act ANOTR, whose groove-led house has filled clubs and open-air stages across Europe. Mumbai was chosen for its India debut.

The festival was set for **Wednesday, November 25, 2026**. Midweek nights in Mumbai usually draw people straight from work, with a slow start the next morning. The event was first set for March and then postponed; November was the rescheduled date.

Indian event gates usually ask for a printed or phone ticket plus government photo ID, and international visitors normally carry their passport for this. Drinking ages in Maharashtra apply at the bar: 21 for beer and 25 for spirits. Bar staff at large events often ask for proof of age.

## Mumbai in late November

Late November falls in Mumbai's dry season. The monsoon is long over, humidity drops and evenings are warm without being sticky. That makes it the most comfortable stretch of the year for a night spent dancing.

Visitors flying in arrive at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport. Many stay in Bandra or Juhu, the western suburbs where much of the city's nightlife is based. Others stay in Colaba or Lower Parel, depending on where an event is. Traffic across the city is heavy at the evening rush, and a cross-town ride can take well over an hour.

## Getting home at 2am in Mumbai

Getting home is harder than getting there. Most festival-goers in Mumbai rely on Uber and Ola, and surge pricing and slow pickups are normal when thousands of people leave at once. Groups usually walk a block or two from the gates before requesting, because drivers find riders more easily away from the crush.

Some things that trip up first-timers:

- **Auto-rickshaws** don't run in South Mumbai. Below Bandra and Sion, the options are a black-and-yellow kaali-peeli taxi or an app cab.
- **Suburban trains** on the Western and Central lines stop running in the small hours. Few people use them after a late set.
- **Payment** is mostly UPI, scanned from a QR code at bars and food stalls. Cards are widely accepted. Foreign visitors without an Indian bank account usually carry a card and some rupees in cash.
- **Water:** sealed bottled water is the norm, and festival bars sell it.

Dress codes at Mumbai dance events are relaxed: trainers, light cotton and one layer for air-conditioned rooms. People usually keep phones in a front pocket or a zipped bag, as at any big crowd.

Visitors to late-night events in Mumbai often stay on in the city. Marine Drive at dawn, the Gateway of India in Colaba and the cafés of Bandra's Pali Hill are the usual places for a slow recovery morning.
