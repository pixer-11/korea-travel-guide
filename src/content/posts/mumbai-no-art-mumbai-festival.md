## ANOTR's NO ART lands in India

This is a first for the country. NO ART is an international dance music festival built around the Dutch act ANOTR, whose groove-led house has filled clubs and open-air stages across Europe. Mumbai hosts its India debut.

The date is **Wednesday, November 25, 2026**. That is a midweek night, so most of the crowd comes straight from work or takes the next morning slow. The event was first set for March and then postponed; November is the rescheduled date.

## Dates, tickets and where it happens

Here is what is known:

- **Date:** November 25, 2026
- **City:** Mumbai, Maharashtra
- **Headliner:** ANOTR
- **Venue and ticket links:** published by the organiser on its official site

Indian event gates usually ask for a printed or phone ticket plus government photo ID, and international visitors normally carry their passport for this. Drinking ages in Maharashtra also apply at the bar: 21 for beer and 25 for spirits. Bar staff at large events often ask for proof of age.

## Mumbai in late November

Late November falls in Mumbai's dry season. The monsoon is long over, humidity drops and evenings are warm without being sticky. That makes it the most comfortable stretch of the year for a night spent dancing.

Visitors flying in arrive at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport. Many stay in Bandra or Juhu, the western suburbs where much of the city's nightlife is based. Others stay in Colaba or Lower Parel, depending on where the venue sits. Traffic across the city is heavy at the evening rush, and a cross-town ride can take well over an hour.

## Getting home at 2am in Mumbai

Getting home takes more planning than getting there. Most festival-goers lean on Uber and Ola, though surge pricing and slow pickups are normal when thousands of people leave together. Groups usually walk a block or two from the gates before requesting, because drivers find you more easily away from the crush.

Some things that trip up first-timers:

- **Auto-rickshaws** don't run in South Mumbai. Below Bandra and Sion you need a black-and-yellow kaali-peeli taxi or an app cab.
- **Suburban trains** on the Western and Central lines stop running in the small hours. Few people use them after a late set.
- **Payment** is mostly UPI, scanned from a QR code at bars and food stalls. Cards are widely accepted. Foreign visitors without an Indian bank account carry a card and some rupees in cash.
- **Water:** stick to sealed bottled water, which festival bars sell.

Dress codes at Mumbai dance events are relaxed: trainers, light cotton and one layer for air-conditioned rooms. Phones go in a front pocket or a zipped bag, as at any big crowd.

After the event, plenty of visitors stay on in the city. Marine Drive at dawn, the Gateway of India in Colaba and the cafés of Bandra's Pali Hill are the usual places for a slow recovery morning.
