## A town market on Mabini Street

At 5:00 AM the shutters go up on Mabini Street. By the time Iloilo City is getting to work, Oton Public Market has been selling for hours. This is a working municipal market in the town proper, in the Poblacion area of Oton. It is there to stock kitchens, not to entertain visitors.

That is the reason to come. Visitors to Iloilo tend to stick to the city markets, like La Paz and Central Market downtown. Oton gives you the same everyday Ilonggo routine at a smaller scale, and you won't find a souvenir stand in the aisles.

## What you'll find inside

Like most Philippine public markets, it is split by trade. Expect a wet section on concrete floors that get hosed down all day, and drier aisles for everything else. Oton sits on the coast of the Iloilo Strait, so seafood gets a lot of space.

Things to look for:

- **Fresh fish and shellfish**, sold by the kilo off tiled or steel counters
- **Pork and chicken**, cut to order while you wait
- **Vegetables and fruit** in season, often sold by the pile or bundle
- **Dried fish (*uga*)**, a Visayan pantry staple
- **Rice, spices and sari-sari goods** in the dry aisles
- **Kakanin**, the sticky rice cakes eaten as a morning or afternoon snack

Small eateries (*carinderias*) are a normal feature of town markets here. If you see pots of viands behind glass, point at what you want and it comes with rice.

## Getting to Oton

Oton is the next town west of Iloilo City. It lies along the coastal highway that carries on to Tigbauan, Guimbal and eventually Antique. From the city, catch a jeepney with "Oton" on the signboard. They run from the Molo and Mandurriao side of town.

Tell the driver you're going to the *palengke* (market) or the *plaza*, and he'll let you off in the town centre. Grab cars and taxis also make the trip. That's handy if you plan to be there right at 5:00 AM, before the jeepney traffic builds.

## Shopping the aisles like a regular

This is a cash market, so bring small peso bills and coins. Vendors at the start of the day often can't break a large note. Some stalls take GCash, though you shouldn't count on it. Nobody tips in a market.

A few habits make things easier:

- **Ask first, then buy.** "*Tag-pila ini?*" (how much is this?) in Hiligaynon usually gets a smile. English and Tagalog work too.
- **Bargain gently.** Asking for a small discount (*tawad*) on a bulk buy is normal. Hard haggling over a single fish is not.
- **Ask to take photos.** People are working. A nod towards your camera before you shoot is basic courtesy, and most vendors say yes.
- **Wear shoes you can wash.** The wet section really is wet.
- **Carry a reusable bag.** You'll leave with fewer plastic bags that way.

Buyers who come back to the same stall become *suki* (regular customers). Even on one visit, sticking with one friendly vendor for several items often gets you better treatment than hopping between stalls.

## Timing your visit

The market is open every day from 5:00 AM to 6:00 PM, with the same hours on weekends. Get there early for the widest choice of seafood, because the overnight and dawn catch sells first. By late afternoon the wet section has thinned out, and some stalls are packing up well before 6:00 PM.

If you're only browsing and eating, mid-morning is fine. Start with a snack of kakanin and coffee, then walk through while vendors still have full counters.

## Around the plaza afterwards

The market is a few minutes from the town plaza and the Parish Church of St. Bartholomew. The town has deep roots: Oton was one of the earliest Spanish-era settlements on Panay. It is also where the gold Oton Death Mask was dug up. The mask dates from before the Spanish arrived and is now held by the National Museum of the Philippines.

To make a half day of it, pair the market with the church and plaza. Then head back into Iloilo City for lunch, or carry on west along the coast towards Tigbauan and Miagao. Miagao Church is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
