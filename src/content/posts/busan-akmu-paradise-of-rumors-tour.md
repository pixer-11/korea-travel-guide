Two nights, one city, and a sibling duo who built a career on songs that sound like a private joke between a brother and sister. AKMU brings the Paradise of Rumors tour to BEXCO in Busan on December 19 and 20, 2026. For many fans outside the capital, this is the closest the tour comes to home.

## Two siblings, one stage

AKMU is Lee Chan-hyuk and Lee Su-hyun. They are a brother and sister who first reached a national audience by winning SBS's *K-pop Star 2* in 2013. Chan-hyuk writes and produces their songs. Su-hyun's light, clear voice carries most of the melodies, and the two of them harmonise in a way that is hard to fake.

That balance is the reason people go. Their catalogue runs from the bouncy debut-era "200%" and "Give Love" to the slower heartbreak of "How can I love the heartbreak, you're the one I love". It also includes "Nakka", their duet with IU, and the 2023 single "Love Lee". ## BEXCO: what the venue actually is

BEXCO stands for Busan Exhibition and Convention Center. It is a large complex in U-dong, Haeundae-gu, built for trade fairs and conferences, and it hosts big concerts in its exhibition halls. The concert hall is a converted exhibition space, not a purpose-built arena. That shapes the night in a few ways:

- The floor is flat, so sightlines from the back depend on the staging and screens
- Entry, merchandise and ticket collection usually happen in the wide halls and plazas outside the performance space
- Queues tend to form outdoors, so December wind off the water is part of the wait

The complex sits in Centum City. This district of glass towers, wide boulevards and department stores was built on reclaimed land along the Suyeong River.

## Getting to Centum City

Centum City Station on Busan Metro Line 2 (green) is the stop most concertgoers use. The exhibition halls are a short walk away. The Donghae Line, Korail's commuter rail along the east coast, also stops nearby at BEXCO Station.

From Seoul, the KTX to Busan Station takes roughly two and a half to three hours. From Busan Station, Line 1 connects to Line 2 at Seomyeon. Ride east toward Jangsan and get off at Centum City. Gimhae International Airport links to the city by the light rail and the subway network.

A T-money card or a contactless bank card covers the subway and buses. After a concert lets out, thousands of people head to the same platform at the same moment. Many fans walk a stop or two along the river, or wait out the first surge over food in Centum City.

## Wristbands, queues and fan etiquette

K-pop concert days in Korea run on a quiet set of rules. Fans who have done it before follow them without thinking. The organiser sets the terms for each show and prints them with the ticket, but these patterns are typical:

- **Ticket collection needs ID.** Korean concerts commonly match the ticket to the buyer's name. Overseas fans usually carry their passport.
- **Merch lines start well before doors.** Official goods often sell out in popular sizes, so dedicated fans queue for merch first and food second.
- **Filming rules are strict.** Many Korean concerts ban recording the performance. Staff do enforce it.
- **Singing along is expected.** AKMU songs have choruses built for a crowd, and Korean audiences join in loudly while staying quiet during spoken moments.
- **Fan-made slogans and banners** are common, along with small gifts left at designated support areas when the organiser allows them.

Most fans pay for snacks, transport and convenience-store runs by card or phone. Tipping is not part of Korean service culture.

## December by the Suyeong River

Mid-December in Busan is milder than Seoul, but the coast is windy. Evenings around Centum City feel colder than the thermometer suggests. Fans in the outdoor queues usually wear layers, a scarf and something warm on their hands, because most of the waiting happens outside.

The season adds something too. Busan usually lights up for the holidays in December. The Christmas Tree Festival along Gwangbok-ro in Nampo-dong and the winter light displays at Haeundae are regular fixtures. A concert weekend this close to Christmas means the city is already dressed for it.

## Before doors and after the encore

Centum City is built for killing an afternoon. Shinsegae Centum City, one of the largest department stores in the world, sits next to the station and has a food hall, a spa complex and cinemas. Across the road, APEC Naru Park runs along the Suyeong River, a flat walk with views of the Gwangan Bridge.

Two beaches are within easy reach:

- **Haeundae Beach**, a few stops east on Line 2, with the Haeundae Market alley for fish cakes and hotteok
- **Gwangalli Beach**, across the river, where the Gwangan Bridge is lit at night and the waterfront is lined with bars and cafés

For a post-show meal, Busan's signature *dwaeji gukbap* (pork and rice soup) is the classic choice. It is served in a stone bowl with chives, salted shrimp and kimchi on the side. Many gukbap shops around Seomyeon and Haeundae keep late hours, which makes them a natural finish to a concert night.

## Where fans stay for a two-night run

Fans attending both nights usually base themselves close by, in Centum City, Haeundae or Gwangalli. Haeundae has the widest choice of hotels and is one subway ride from the venue. Gwangalli tends to suit smaller budgets and comes with the bridge view.

Seomyeon, Busan's central downtown interchange, works for fans arriving by KTX. It sits on both Line 1 and Line 2 and is busy late into the night. Nampo-dong and the Jagalchi Fish Market area are further from BEXCO but put the Christmas lights and the old port within walking distance.
