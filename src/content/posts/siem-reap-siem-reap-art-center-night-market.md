## What's actually here

This isn't a temple souvenir strip; it's a covered grid of stalls off Hospital Street, close enough to Pub Street to fold into an evening loop. Vendors sell silk scarves, silver jewelry, wood carvings, printed t-shirts and krama scarves. Between the stalls, plastic-chair massage bays offer foot and shoulder rubs while you sit facing the aisle.

Food stalls cluster near the edges: skewered meats, fruit shakes, fried noodles. Prices aren't marked, so expect to haggle on crafts and clothing; massage prices are usually posted or quoted upfront. With a rating of 4.2 across more than 6,000 reviews, it's one of the more consistently reviewed markets in town, not an under-the-radar find.

## Hours and getting there

Open 4pm to 11pm Monday through Saturday. Closed all day Sunday, which trips up visitors who assume every market runs nightly.

The address resolves to Hospital Street in central Siem Reap. Most travelers walk from Pub Street or the Old Market area, ten to fifteen minutes on foot. Tuk-tuks from anywhere in the central tourist zone run a short, cheap fare; agree the price before getting in, since meters don't exist here.

## Beating the crowds on Hospital Street

This market draws heavy foot traffic most evenings, so plan around it rather than hoping to dodge it entirely.

- Arrive right at the 4pm opening for the loosest aisles and first pick of stock
- Expect the thickest crush from roughly 7pm to 9pm, when dinner crowds from Pub Street spill over
- After 9:30pm, vendors start discounting slow-moving stock to avoid packing it back up
- Weeknights run thinner than Friday and Saturday

Cash rules here; small US dollar bills or Cambodian riel work, and few stalls take cards. Bargaining is expected but keep it good-natured: counter at roughly half the first quote and meet in the middle rather than pushing for rock-bottom.

## What to skip and what to linger over

Skip the mass-produced fridge magnets and printed shirts near the entrance; nearly identical stock repeats at every stall for the first few rows. Push further in for the carved wood pieces and silver jewelry stalls, where quality and price vary more, so it pays to compare a few before buying.

The massage bays are a genuine draw after a day of temple-hopping at Angkor. Rates are cheap by international standards and sessions run in open-air view of the market, not private rooms, so don't expect quiet. Bring small bills for tipping if the service is good; it's not automatically added.

Pair the visit with Pub Street itself, a two-minute walk away, for a full night-market-to-bar-crawl evening. Since the market shuts at 11pm and is dark on Sundays, build your Siem Reap nightlife plans around a Monday-to-Saturday visit.
