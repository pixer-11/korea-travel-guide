## A Victorian hall that still trades

Fremantle Markets is limestone and brick on the outside: a Victorian market hall from 1897, with its name set into the gable over the South Terrace entrance. Inside, the long roof trusses sit over narrow aisles of stalls, packed close enough that two prams can barely pass.

It is one of the most-reviewed attractions in Western Australia and consistently well rated. Expect company. The main hall is mostly makers and traders: jewellery, clothing, soaps, prints, Australiana and souvenirs. The produce and food side is where locals do their weekend shopping.

What to look for:
- Fruit, vegetables and deli goods in the produce area
- Hot food stalls for an easy lunch
- Handmade crafts and clothes from small sellers
- Buskers working the South Terrace and Henderson Street frontages

## Getting to the corner of South Terrace

The easiest way from Perth is the train. The Fremantle line ends at Fremantle Station, and from there you walk south along Market Street onto South Terrace in about 10 minutes. The free Fremantle CAT bus also loops past the area, which helps if you are coming back from the harbour.

Driving works, but Fremantle's weekend parking fills fast. Paid parking is spread across council car parks and street bays, with charges and limits posted on each meter. A ferry also runs between Perth's Barrack Street Jetty and Fremantle. Check Transperth for the current timetable before relying on it.

## Working the aisles

Weekends get crowded because everyone else has the same free day. Arriving close to the 9am opening usually means room to browse before the lunch crowd turns up. On Friday the hall is often calmer, since most people are at work.

How the stalls operate:
- **Paying:** tap-and-go cards are standard. Carry a little cash for the odd small stall that only takes notes.
- **Prices:** marked prices are fixed. Haggling is not part of Australian market culture, though some sellers will do a deal if you buy several items.
- **Tipping:** not expected at food stalls.
- **Bags:** bring your own. Western Australia phased out lightweight plastic bags, and you will be carrying produce.
- **Timing:** the hours now are Friday to Sunday, 9am–6pm. Public holidays can change this, so check the official site before a long-weekend trip.

If you plan to buy produce, do the craft hall first and the fruit and veg last. You won't be lugging a bag of mangoes past the jewellery stalls.

## Making a day of Fremantle

The market fits neatly into a walking loop of the West End and its surroundings:

- **Fremantle Prison:** a UNESCO-listed convict site, a short walk uphill on The Terrace. Tours run all day, so book ahead.
- **South Terrace "Cappuccino Strip":** a run of footpath cafés starting right outside the market doors.
- **Fishing Boat Harbour:** about 10 minutes' walk west, where locals go for fish and chips by the water.
- **The Round House:** Western Australia's oldest surviving public building, a small limestone gaol on a headland at the end of High Street.
- **Esplanade Park:** a lawn between the market and the harbour, good for eating something you bought in the hall.

Allow an hour or two in the market, then half a day for the prison and harbour on foot.
