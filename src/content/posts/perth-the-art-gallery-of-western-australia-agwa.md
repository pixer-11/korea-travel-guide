## Concrete, glass and a plaza

The building makes its case before you reach the door. AGWA's main gallery is a 1979 Brutalist block of angled concrete, set on the open paving of the Perth Cultural Centre between the CBD and Northbridge. The State Library of Western Australia and WA Museum Boola Bardip share the same plaza, so one walk covers three institutions.

Inside, the rooms are pale and sparse. The work gets the space, and the displays mix classic and contemporary pieces from the region. The state collection spans colonial painting to present-day practice. Look out for:

- Aboriginal art, which makes up one of the gallery's major collecting areas
- Frederick McCubbin's *Down on His Luck* (1889), a cornerstone of Australian painting
- Contemporary Western Australian artists, shown on rotation

## Walking in from Perth Station

The gallery is effectively beside the railway. From Perth Station, cross north toward the Cultural Centre and you are on the plaza within minutes. Perth Underground and the Perth Busport are also close by. Driving is the weaker option, since city parking fills up. Coming by train also means Northbridge's bars and restaurants are right there once the gallery shuts at 5pm.

## Beating the weekend crowd

Timing matters here. Crowds build across the whole weekend opening, from 10am to 5pm, so no Saturday or Sunday slot is reliably calm. If you can only come at the weekend, go straight to the special exhibition, then wander the permanent collection as the rooms clear. Weekdays are the better bet. The gallery keeps the same 10am to 5pm hours every day of the week, Monday included.

How it works on the door:

- General entry to the collection is free. Ticketed exhibitions are priced on AGWA's official site and at the front desk.
- Book popular exhibitions online ahead of weekend visits, as timed sessions can sell out.
- Large bags may need to go to the cloakroom, and photography rules are set by each exhibition, so check the signs.
- Since doors close at 5pm, arrive by 2pm for a full circuit.

## Up on the AGWA Rooftop

End the visit on the roof. The AGWA Rooftop opened in 2021 and brought the old concrete top into use. It has a bar, commissioned works by Aboriginal artists and views across the CBD skyline. The bar's hours can differ from the galleries, so check them before planning sundown drinks.

Back at ground level, WA Museum Boola Bardip is across the plaza if you have energy left. The State Library, on the same paving, is a quiet place to sit. For a meal, Northbridge's William Street and James Street are a few minutes north on foot. Two to three hours covers the collection and the roof. Add a morning if you're pairing it with the museum.
