## A street of makers beside the parklands

Little Stanley Street is a short strip of cafés and restaurants on the edge of South Bank Parklands. Three days a week, a line of market stalls takes over its footpaths. On one side you have the restaurant terraces. Over the trees, a short walk away, are the lagoon at Streets Beach and the Wheel of Brisbane.

This is The Collective Markets. It's an outdoor makers' market, and it sticks to its brief: locally made crafts, jewellery, art, home decor and clothing. Don't come expecting a farmers' market or a food hall. You come to look at objects and talk to the people who made them.

The market is well known in Brisbane, yet it rarely makes the city's headline lists. That's because visitors heading for the lagoon and the museums at the Cultural Centre walk straight past the end of the street. Once you know it's here, it becomes an easy hour to add to any day at South Bank.

## Three sessions, three different markets

The timetable is the market's most important quirk. Get it wrong and you'll find an ordinary restaurant street.

- **Friday:** 5:00–9:00pm, an evening-only session
- **Saturday:** 10:00am–9:00pm, the longest day, running from morning into night
- **Sunday:** 9:00am–4:00pm, the earliest start, and it packs up mid-afternoon
- **Monday to Thursday:** closed

Each session has its own feel. Friday is an after-work market with the stalls lit up against the dark. Saturday runs for eleven hours, so you can come in daylight or go to dinner on Little Stanley Street and browse afterwards.

Sunday is the daytime-only option. Don't plan a Sunday evening visit, because the stalls are gone by 4pm.

## What's on the stalls

The goods fall into the categories the market itself promotes. These are the things to look for:

- **Jewellery:** often the most crowded stalls, since the pieces are small and easy to pack
- **Art and prints:** good for flat souvenirs that survive a suitcase
- **Home decor:** homewares and small decorative pieces
- **Clothing:** locally made pieces, not chain-store stock
- **Crafts:** the catch-all for everything handmade that doesn't fit the other categories

The point is that it's made locally. Stallholders change between weekends, so treat each visit as a fresh look rather than a return trip to one shop. If you see something you love, buy it then. It may not be there next week.

## Getting to Little Stanley Street

South Bank is one of the easiest parts of Brisbane to reach without a car. You have several options:

- **On foot from the CBD:** cross the Victoria Bridge from the end of Queen Street, or take the Goodwill Bridge from the Botanic Gardens side. Both drop you at the parklands within a few minutes.
- **By train:** South Bank and South Brisbane stations on the Queensland Rail network are both close.
- **By bus:** Cultural Centre busway station sits at the northern end of the precinct.
- **By ferry:** the river ferries stop at South Bank, just below the parklands.

Once you're in the parklands, walk towards the Wheel of Brisbane and the lagoon. Little Stanley Street runs parallel to the parklands on the inland side.

## Buying from the person who made it

At most stalls, the person behind the table is the maker, or someone close to them. That changes how you shop.

- **Ask questions.** Asking how something was made, or what it's made from, is a normal opener. It's often the best part of the visit.
- **Don't haggle.** Bargaining isn't part of Australian market culture. The price on the tag is the price, and pushing it reads as rude.
- **Pay by tap.** Card and phone payments are standard across Australia, and small vendors commonly take them. A little cash is a useful backup for any stall without a reader.
- **Ask before photographing work.** Designers can be protective of original pieces. A quick question gets you a yes far more often than you'd think.

Prices aren't standard across stalls. Each maker sets their own and usually labels the item or the display.

## Dodging the weekend crush

The numbers here are blunt. On weekends the market is at its busiest from 9am to 9pm, which covers the entire Saturday and Sunday trading day. So there's no quiet weekend window to aim for.

If you want space to browse and time to talk to stallholders, Friday evening is the only session outside that weekend window. That makes it the best bet for a calmer look. If you can only come at the weekend, expect shoulder-to-shoulder footpaths and plan around them.

On a weekend, a few things help:

- Bring a tote bag, not a backpack. It's easier to manoeuvre between stalls.
- Do one quick lap first, then go back to the stalls that caught your eye.
- Leave the pram or large bags at your accommodation if you can.

## Around the stalls: South Bank on foot

The market fills an hour, and the rest of South Bank fills the day. All of these are within a short walk of Little Stanley Street:

- **Streets Beach:** the free, man-made lagoon beach, patrolled by lifeguards, in the middle of the parklands
- **The Grand Arbour:** a long, curving walkway covered in bougainvillea that runs through the parklands
- **Nepal Peace Pagoda:** a carved timber pagoda originally built for World Expo 88
- **Wheel of Brisbane:** the observation wheel at the end of the street
- **Cultural Centre:** GOMA, the Queensland Art Gallery, the Queensland Museum and QPAC, at the northern end of the precinct

The best pairing is a Saturday. Spend the morning at the galleries or in the lagoon, then go to the market in the late afternoon and stay on Little Stanley Street for dinner. On a Sunday, do it the other way round: go to the market first, before it closes at 4pm, then cool off at Streets Beach.

Brisbane summers are hot and humid, so the evening sessions on Friday and Saturday are the more comfortable choice from December to February. From May to September the weather is mild and dry, and the Sunday morning start is at its best.
