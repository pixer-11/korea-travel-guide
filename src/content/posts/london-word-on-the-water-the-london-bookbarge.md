The shelves start before you reach the boat. Books are set out on the towpath beside the hull, and joggers, cyclists and dog walkers pass between them and the water. Then you step aboard and the shop carries on inside.

Word On The Water calls itself The London Bookbarge, and it is exactly that: a bookshop on a canal barge on the Regent's Canal at King's Cross. It is well known and consistently well rated, so expect company. Treat it as a stop on a canal walk, not a secret.

## A shop with a waterline

There are shelves inside and outside, and the range of titles is wide. You will not find a chain store's tidy front tables here. Browsing works best if you come without a list and let the spines decide.

Inside, the space is set by the shape of the hull rather than by a shopfitter. That makes it feel more like someone's crowded study than a retail floor. Outside, the towpath display is the quickest way in, and it is where most people start.

A few things help the browse go well:

- Take your backpack off before stepping aboard; aisles on a boat are tight.
- Hold the handrail or doorframe. The barge floats, and you may feel it move.
- Put books back where you found them, spine out, so the next person can find them too.

## Walking in from King's Cross St Pancras

King's Cross St Pancras Underground station is the easiest approach. It serves the Northern, Piccadilly, Victoria, Circle, Hammersmith & City and Metropolitan lines, plus mainline trains and Eurostar at St Pancras International.

From the station, head north up King's Boulevard towards Granary Square. That takes about 10 minutes on foot. The square ends in wide stone steps that run down to the Regent's Canal, and the address is the towpath itself (London N1C 4LW). Walk down to the water and look along the moored boats.

Coming from Camden, the canal makes a better route. Walk east along the towpath from Camden Lock for roughly half an hour. You pass houseboats, the backs of old warehouses and the gasholders before King's Cross opens up ahead.

## Sharing the towpath politely

This is a working canal path, and the shop is only one of its users. The way people behave here comes from that.

- **Keep the path clear.** Cyclists use the towpath and it narrows near the boats. Browse the outdoor shelves close to the barge, not across the walkway.
- **Watch the edge.** There are no railings between the towpath and the water. Keep a hand on small children.
- **Ask before photographing inside.** Snapping the exterior from the towpath is normal. Inside, it is someone's shop in a small space, so buy something or ask first.
- **Pay at the counter on board.** Check there how they take payment rather than assuming; carrying both a card and a little cash covers you either way.
- **Move along when it fills.** If the inside is packed, browse outside for a few minutes and step in when it thins out.

## Building an afternoon around it

The bookbarge makes a good centre for half a day at King's Cross. Several things sit within a few minutes' walk:

- **Granary Square**, directly above the canal, with its grid of ground fountains and the Granary Building, home to Central Saint Martins art school.
- **Coal Drops Yard**, the Victorian coal warehouses next door, now shops and restaurants under their kissing rooflines.
- **Gasholder Park**, a lawn set inside a restored cast-iron gasholder frame on the canal bank.
- **Camley Street Natural Park**, a small nature reserve on the far side of the canal.
- **The British Library** on Euston Road, a 10–15 minute walk south, if one bookshop isn't enough for you.

Sitting on the Granary Square steps with a new paperback is a very King's Cross way to finish. On a dry day the steps fill with people doing exactly that.

## When to go

Right now the shop opens at 12pm and closes at 7pm, every day of the week. Mornings are therefore out: arriving at 10am finds the boat shut, however nice the towpath looks. Hours on a boat business can change, so check the latest listing before a special trip.

Weekday afternoons tend to be easier than weekends. King's Cross fills with weekend visitors to Coal Drops Yard, and the towpath gets busier with them. If you can't avoid a Saturday, come soon after noon.

Weather matters more here than at most bookshops. The outdoor shelves are half the experience, and a wet day keeps everyone crammed inside. Give yourself 30–45 minutes to browse properly, more if you plan to read on the steps afterwards.
