## A Tuesday night in the 19th

Le Zénith hosts the Paris stop. On October 20, 2026, Jay Park brings his 2026 world tour to this arena on the north-east edge of Paris, with LNGSHOT sharing the bill. The tour launched earlier with a MIDEM LIVE show in Cannes, so Paris comes after the opening, not at the start.

The date falls on a Tuesday. That shapes the evening: fans come straight from work or class, the Villette lawns are mostly empty of weekend picnickers, and the late Metro home matters more than it would on a Saturday.

## The artist and the bill

Jay Park is a Korean-American rapper, singer and producer. He first became known in Korea as a member of 2PM, then left the group and built a solo career in hip-hop and R&B. He also built record labels, founding AOMG and H1GHR MUSIC and later MORE VISION.

That background explains who turns up. The audience at his shows usually mixes long-time K-pop fans with people who follow Korean hip-hop and R&B. LNGSHOT is the other name on the poster for this tour. Expect both acts' fans in the room, with lightsticks, printed banners and matching merch.

## Inside the tent that was meant to be temporary

Le Zénith opened in 1984. Architects Philippe Chaix and Jean-Paul Morel designed it as a stopgap concert hall: a steel frame covered in a stretched fabric membrane, meant to stand for a few years. Paris kept it, and it became the model for a whole network of Zénith arenas around France.

From the outside it still looks like a big grey tent on the edge of the park. Inside, a standing floor (the *fosse*) faces the stage and banks of tiered seating rise behind it. The hall holds a little over 6,000 people. That is large enough for a world-tour production, and the back rows still feel close to the stage.

Which areas are open varies from show to show. Your ticket category shows where you'll be: on the standing floor or in the tiered seats.

## Getting to Parc de la Villette

The Zénith sits inside Parc de la Villette, so every route ends with a short walk through the park. The main options:

- **Metro Line 5, Porte de Pantin:** the closest station, a few minutes' walk from the southern park entrance.
- **Metro Line 7, Porte de la Villette:** at the northern end of the park, beside the Cité des Sciences. The walk is longer and crosses the Canal de l'Ourcq.
- **Tram T3b, Porte de Pantin:** useful if you're coming from elsewhere on the city's outer ring.

Most visitors stay in central Paris and ride Line 5 out. It runs direct from Gare du Nord, Gare de l'Est, République and Bastille, so the trip is easy from most of the city's hotel districts.

## The Villette ritual: canal, Philharmonie, then the show

Parc de la Villette is a former slaughterhouse district turned into a cultural park. That makes the walk to the show worth taking slowly. Bernard Tschumi's red steel *folies* are scattered across the lawns. The Philharmonie de Paris, clad in grey metal, stands near the Porte de Pantin entrance.

Fans with a free afternoon often fill it here:

- **Cité des Sciences et de l'Industrie:** a large science museum at the park's northern end.
- **La Géode:** the mirrored steel sphere beside it, which houses a giant-screen cinema.
- **Grande Halle de la Villette:** the 19th-century iron-and-glass former cattle market, now used for exhibitions and events.
- **Canal de l'Ourcq:** runs through the middle of the park. Its towpath leads south-west towards the Bassin de la Villette and the canal-side bars around Stalingrad.

On a Tuesday in late October it gets dark early. The canal path lit by streetlights is the usual way back towards the city for people who want a drink after the encore.

## Getting through the doors at Le Zénith

Paris venues run bag searches at the entrance as standard. People who come with a small bag or nothing at all get through fastest. Large backpacks and anything resembling a camera rig are the items most likely to cause trouble at security.

K-pop crowds usually line up well before the doors open, especially fans hoping for a place near the front of the standing floor. Fans often trade freebies in the queue: photocards, stickers and fan-made banners. People do this freely, and it's a friendly way to start talking in a mixed French and international crowd.

Inside, card payment and contactless are normal at the bars and merch stands, as they are across Paris. A few useful French words:

- *fosse*: the standing floor
- *gradins*: the tiered seats
- *vestiaire*: the cloakroom, if one is running that night
- *sortie*: the exit

When the show ends, most of the crowd heads for Porte de Pantin at once. Walking north through the park to Porte de la Villette on Line 7 is the usual way around the crush.
