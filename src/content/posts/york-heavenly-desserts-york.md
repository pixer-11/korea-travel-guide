## Pudding on the road to the Minster

Blake Street is short, and most people walk it fast. It runs from St Helen's Square up to Duncombe Place, where York Minster's west front fills the end of the view. Heavenly Desserts sits at numbers 19-21, on the stretch most visitors use going to the cathedral and coming back.

That position matters more than any decor. You don't plan a detour here; you pass it after a walk on the walls or an evening show, and the question becomes whether you have room for a waffle. It's a sit-down dessert restaurant, not a takeaway counter, so expect to take a table and stay a while.

It is well known and consistently well rated, with a long trail of reviews behind it. Don't come looking for a secret. Come for a proper pudding in the middle of the old city, and time your visit so you're not stuck behind a Saturday queue.

## Finding 19-21 Blake Street

The address is 19-21 Blake St, York YO1 8QJ. Inside the walls everything is close, and walking is the sensible way to get there.

- **From York railway station:** cross Lendal Bridge, go up Museum Street and turn right into Blake Street. It takes roughly 10–15 minutes.
- **From York Minster:** walk down from Duncombe Place. It's a couple of minutes.
- **From the Shambles or Stonegate:** head for St Helen's Square, then up Blake Street.

Driving into the centre is slow, and the city-centre car parks fill up on weekends. Park and Ride buses from the edge of the city drop you within easy walking distance.

## Waffles, fondue and sundaes

The menu is built around three things: creative waffles, chocolate fondue and ice-cream sundaes. Everything else plays a supporting role. Prices sit in the mid-range for a sit-down dessert, more than a cone from a kiosk but not a fine-dining bill. The menu at the table has current prices.

The fondue is the one to share. It's a pot of melted chocolate for dipping, and it works best between two or more people with a pot of tea or coffee. The waffles and sundaes are single-plate desserts. They come loaded, so one each is usually plenty.

Treat it as the whole sweet course rather than a nibble after a big dinner. If you've just had three courses at a restaurant on Petergate, sharing one plate is the more realistic plan.

## The 9pm sitting on weekends

This is the place's odd timing. Most of York's daytime cafés have shut by early evening, but Heavenly Desserts currently stays open until 11pm Sunday to Friday and until 11:30pm on Saturday. That makes it one of the few places near the Minster where you can sit down for pudding late at night.

On weekends the busiest stretch runs from 5pm to 9pm, as dinner crowds and families fill the tables. After that it changes. The quietest weekend window is 9pm–11pm, which suits a dessert after a show at the Theatre Royal on St Leonard's Place, a few minutes' walk away.

Late evening is also when Blake Street looks its best. The Minster is a short walk up the road, and the walk there after your waffle is quieter than at any point during the day.

## Getting a table without the weekend wait

Timing does most of the work here. Weekends between 10am and 2pm and between 5pm and 9pm are the busiest, and that's when you're most likely to wait at the door. If your trip runs over a weekend, push dessert to after 9pm.

On weekdays it's calmest from 10am to 11am, right after the doors open. That's an odd hour for a sundae, but it's a good one for coffee and a waffle before Stonegate fills up.

A few habits that will make it go smoothly:

- **Check booking before you go.** The current booking policy is on the official site or the Google listing. Check it before a busy Saturday or a group visit.
- **Pay by card or phone.** Contactless is standard across York city centre, and you'll rarely need cash.
- **Tip as you would at a British restaurant.** It's table service, and around 10% is the usual norm. Read your bill first: some places add a service charge automatically, and then no extra tip is expected.
- **Order sharing plates for groups.** Fondue for the table plus one waffle often beats a full dessert each.

## Before and after: the streets around Blake Street

Blake Street sits between several of York's most visited corners. Pairing dessert with one of them makes the visit worthwhile.

- **York Minster:** up the road at Duncombe Place. Do the cathedral and the tower climb first, then come back down for pudding.
- **St Helen's Square and the Mansion House:** at the bottom of Blake Street. The Lord Mayor's Georgian residence faces the square.
- **Stonegate:** off St Helen's Square, one of the city's best-preserved medieval streets. It leads on towards Petergate.
- **Museum Gardens and the ruins of St Mary's Abbey:** a short walk down Museum Street towards the river. Good for walking off a sundae.
- **York Art Gallery and Bootham Bar:** both at Exhibition Square, a couple of minutes away. From Bootham Bar you can climb onto the city walls.

The gardens, the gallery and the Minster all change their hours through the year. Check their official sites for this month's times before you build an evening around them.

## Is it for you?

It suits anyone who wants dessert to be the whole outing: families after a day of sightseeing, couples after a show, friends sharing a fondue. It also works if you're staying inside the walls and want somewhere to sit after most cafés have closed.

It's the wrong choice if you want a quiet, traditional afternoon tea. For that, York's tea rooms around St Helen's Square do the job better. Heavenly Desserts is about chocolate, waffles and ice cream, served late, a few minutes from the Minster.
