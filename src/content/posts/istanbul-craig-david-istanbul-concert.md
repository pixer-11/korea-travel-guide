## What the night is

Craig David has been making records since the turn of the millennium. His debut album *Born to Do It* gave him "Fill Me In", "7 Days" and "Walking Away". Later, his TS5 DJ-and-vocals format put him back in front of club crowds in the mid-2010s.

Nobody outside his team knows which songs he plays on a given night, and this guide won't guess. What you can count on is an outdoor amphitheatre in central Istanbul on a Sunday evening in autumn. You'll be with an audience that grew up on the early singles, and a fair share of people who found him later.

## The venue: a 1940s amphitheatre in Harbiye

The theatre opened in 1947. It is named after Cemil Topuzlu, a surgeon who served as mayor of Istanbul in the early twentieth century. It's built like a classical open-air theatre:

- **Tiered stone seating** rising in a semicircle from the stage
- **No roof**, so the sky is your ceiling and the weather is part of the show
- **Raked sightlines**, so even the upper rows look down onto the stage rather than across heads

Istanbul has used it for big summer and autumn concerts for decades. The Istanbul Jazz Festival is a regular tenant, and touring international acts often play here. It holds a few thousand people, so it feels far smaller than a stadium show. The back rows still feel connected to the stage.

## October on the stone steps

This is the part people forget to plan for. A mid-October evening in Istanbul is usually mild by day and noticeably cooler after dark. You'll be sitting on stone that has spent the afternoon losing its warmth.

Pack for an outdoor evening, not a club:

- **A jacket or warm layer**, even if the afternoon felt like summer
- **Something to sit on**, like a folded scarf or thin cushion, if the venue's rules allow it
- **A compact rain layer**, since autumn showers happen and there is no roof
- **A charged phone**, because you'll likely need it for transport or a taxi app afterwards

Check the organiser's page for the bag policy before you bring anything bulky. Security checks at the gate move faster when you travel light.

## Getting to Harbiye

The theatre sits in Harbiye, part of the Şişli district, on the stretch between Taksim and Nişantaşı. Its immediate neighbours are easy to find:

**By metro:** the M2 line is your friend. **Osmanbey Station** is the closest stop, roughly a ten-minute walk south along Halaskargazi Caddesi. **Taksim Station**, also on the M2, is a similar walk north through Cumhuriyet Caddesi.

**From the Bosphorus side:** take the tram or a ferry to Kabataş. From there, the F1 funicular climbs to Taksim, and you walk on to Harbiye.

**By taxi:** ask for "Harbiye Açıkhava Tiyatrosu", the venue's Turkish name. Drivers know it better by that than by the English.

## Getting in and out on a concert night

Locals move around concert nights in Harbiye in a few predictable ways, and copying them saves real time.

**Use an Istanbulkart.** This is the rechargeable transit card used on the metro, funicular, trams and ferries. Load it before the show so you're not queuing at a machine with a few thousand other people afterwards.

**Plan the exit, not the arrival.** Getting there is easy. Leaving is when everyone hits Halaskargazi and Cumhuriyet at once. Walking up to Taksim or Osmanbey is usually quicker than waiting for a taxi at the gate. October 11 is a Sunday, so many people are heading home for a Monday workday.

**Hail taxis through an app.** Turks commonly use BiTaksi to call licensed yellow cabs. Booking one from a side street a few minutes from the venue beats fighting for a cab on the main road. Uber in Istanbul also books licensed taxis.

**Buy tickets from one source only.** Turkish concerts are commonly sold through national platforms such as Biletix. For this show, use whichever outlet the official announcement links to. Resold tickets passed around on social media are a common way to get turned away at the gate.

**Pay by card.** Contactless card payment is normal across central Istanbul, including at cafés and kiosks around Harbiye. Keep a little Turkish lira for small street vendors anyway.

## Before and after: Nişantaşı and Taksim

You're between two very different Istanbuls, and both are within walking distance.

**Nişantaşı**, just north past Osmanbey, is the polished one. It has boutique-lined streets like Abdi İpekçi Caddesi, plus cafés and restaurants that suit an early dinner before the show. Teşvikiye Mosque sits at its centre.

**Taksim and İstiklal Caddesi**, to the south, stay busy late. That makes them the obvious choice if you want a drink or a late bite after the encore. The pedestrian avenue runs from Taksim Square down toward Galata.

If you have daylight to spare, take a walk through **Maçka Park** before the show. It's the green slope just behind the venue and an easy break from the traffic.
