## A hotel dining room with a pedigree

This is a restaurant inside a hotel. Arraval sits within Hotel Casa Teva, a boutique hotel in El Raval, the dense, multicultural strip of the old city between La Rambla and Avinguda del Paral·lel. Of all the city's new restaurants, it is the one people are citing most.

The reason is who is cooking. Three chefs run the kitchen, and all three trained under Jordi Vilà, the chef behind Alkímia, his Michelin-starred restaurant in the Fàbrica Moritz building on Ronda de Sant Antoni. You get that lineage in a looser setting, in a neighbourhood with more street art than white tablecloths.

## What lands on the table

The cooking is Catalan market cuisine, which Catalans call *cuina de mercat*. The style starts with what the stalls have that week, so the menu follows the season rather than a fixed list of signatures. Arraval has not published a permanent dish list that we can vouch for, so read the current menu when you sit down.

The Vilà connection usually shows up as precise technique applied to familiar Catalan ingredients. The Raval side brings a bit of restlessness, the same creative streak you see in the barrio's galleries and small bars. If you want to know what the menu is drawing on, two of the city's best-known markets are close by:

- **La Boqueria**, on La Rambla at the Raval's eastern edge
- **Mercat de Sant Antoni**, just past the neighbourhood's western boundary

## Booking a table at Arraval

Plan this one. A restaurant this talked-about and this new fills up quickly, and walk-in luck is a poor bet. Book directly through the restaurant or the hotel, and confirm opening days and hours at the same time. We were not given a schedule, and new places often change theirs in their first months.

Keep local timing in mind:

- **Lunch** in Barcelona usually starts around 2pm, and the main meal of the day is often eaten then.
- **Dinner** rarely gets going before 9pm. A 7:30pm booking will put you among the first diners.
- **Paying**: cards are standard. You ask for *la cuenta, si us plau* (Catalan) or *por favor* (Spanish).
- **Tipping**: it is not obligatory in Spain. Leaving loose change or rounding up for good service is normal. Fifteen to twenty percent is not.
- **Water**: tap water is safe to drink. Ask for *aigua de l'aixeta* if you don't want bottled.

A word or two of Catalan is noticed and appreciated here. *Bon profit* is what you'll hear when plates arrive.

## Around the Raval before or after

The neighbourhood is walkable, and it is close to everything in Ciutat Vella. The Raval's edges are served by Liceu (L3) on La Rambla and Sant Antoni (L2) to the west. Check the hotel's exact address for the closest exit.

Worth pairing with a meal:

- **MACBA**, the contemporary art museum. Its white Richard Meier building faces a plaza full of skateboarders.
- **CCCB**, the culture centre next door, for exhibitions and talks.
- **Rambla del Raval**, a broad palm-lined promenade with Fernando Botero's oversized bronze cat at one end.
- **Sant Pau del Camp**, a Romanesque monastery church and one of the oldest in Barcelona.

Some of the Raval's narrower streets feel rough after dark. Keep your phone and bag close, the same way you would on La Rambla.
