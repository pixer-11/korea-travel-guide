## A Hebei band on Mount Eden Road

On November 1, 2026, a band best known for a song about Shijiazhuang plays Auckland. Omnipotent Youth Society (万能青年旅店, usually shortened to Wanqing) brings its Australia and New Zealand tour to the Powerstation, a club-sized room near the bottom of Mount Eden Road.

That is an unusual chance to see them up close. At home in China, Wanqing play big festival stages. Here, the room holds roughly a thousand people, and nobody stands far from the trumpet.

## The trumpet in "Kill That Shijiazhuang Person"

Start with the song everyone waits for. "杀死那个石家庄人" ("Kill That Shijiazhuang Person") closes out the band's 2010 debut album. It is a slow, heavy portrait of a worn-down industrial city, and it ends in a long trumpet line that Chinese rock fans can hum from memory.

The band formed in Shijiazhuang, Hebei province, in the 1990s. They released almost nothing for a decade, then put out the self-titled debut that made them famous. A second album, 冀西南林路行 (*Ji Xi Nan Lin Lu Xing*), followed in 2020, after another long gap.

What you hear is patient and dense:

- Guitar lines that build over several minutes rather than verses and choruses
- Brass, especially trumpet, carrying the melody where a singer usually would
- Lyrics in Mandarin, poetic and oblique, often about Hebei towns and ordinary lives
- Songs that run long, so a set covers fewer tracks than a pop show

Newcomers who know none of this still tend to follow the songs. The trumpet does most of the explaining.

## Inside the Powerstation

The Powerstation is one of Auckland's long-running live music venues. It has hosted touring rock and indie acts since the late 1980s. The layout is simple: a flat standing floor in front of the stage, a bar, and a raised upstairs area looking down on the crowd.

The floor is where the sound is loudest and the sing-alongs happen. Upstairs gives a clearer view of the whole band, useful at a show where the brass and guitar players matter as much as the vocalist. Shorter fans often head there for sightlines.

Door times, age restrictions and the bag policy sit on the Powerstation's own listing for each show. Doors usually open well before the band takes the stage, as at most club gigs.

## Getting to 33 Mount Eden Road

The venue sits at 33 Mount Eden Road, on the edge of Eden Terrace. It is close enough to the central city to walk.

- **On foot:** from Karangahape Road, the walk down past Symonds Street takes roughly 15 minutes.
- **By bus:** the frequent Mount Eden Road buses run past the venue from the city centre.
- **By rideshare or taxi:** the drop-off is right on Mount Eden Road, which gets busy at the end of a show.
- **By car:** street parking nearby is limited, and most attendees skip driving.

After the encore, many people walk back up to Karangahape Road or into the city instead of waiting for a car outside.

## Singing along at the Powerstation

The crowd sets the tone at a Wanqing show. Auckland has a large Chinese community, including many students and young professionals. A good share of the audience is likely to know every Mandarin line. They usually sing whole verses back at the band, and the trumpet solo in the closing song often gets a roar.

A few things smooth the night:

- **ID at the bar.** New Zealand's drinking age is 18. Bar staff accept a passport, a New Zealand driver licence or a Kiwi Access card. Overseas licences may be refused.
- **Paying.** Card and contactless payment are standard at New Zealand venues. Tipping bar staff is not expected.
- **Phones.** Fans film the big moments, but a raised phone through a whole song blocks the people behind you on a flat floor.
- **Song titles.** Fans usually call the songs by their Chinese names. The debut album's track 秦皇岛 ("Qinhuangdao"), named for the seaside city, is one people often request.

If you don't speak Mandarin, look up English translations of the lyrics beforehand. It changes how the long, slow songs land.

## Before and after on Karangahape Road

The Powerstation's neighbourhood is well set up for a gig night. Karangahape Road, known locally as K Road, is a short walk north. It is lined with bars, late-opening eateries and small music venues, and it is where much of the crowd ends up afterwards.

In the other direction, Mount Eden Road climbs toward Mount Eden village. The village has cafés and restaurants that suit an earlier dinner. Above it rises Maungawhau / Mount Eden, a volcanic cone with a deep crater and a view across the whole isthmus. It is best walked in daylight, before the show.

Visitors without a car usually stay in the central city or around K Road. From there the venue is a walk or a single bus ride away.
