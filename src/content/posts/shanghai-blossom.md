Plaza 66 is not where you'd expect to find Chaoshan comfort food. The tower rises over Nanjing West Road, and its lower floors are given over to luxury houses. Ride the lifts past them, though, and Blossom serves the braised, briny cooking of China's south-east coast. It sits a few floors above all that polished stone.

The pitch is simple. Blossom is the lower-commitment sibling of Amazing Chinese Cuisine, a Michelin one-star restaurant. It shares some of the same signature dishes, and you don't have to plan a whole evening around a tasting-level bill.

## A Michelin kitchen's dishes without the occasion

Some starred restaurants are places you save up for. Blossom exists so you don't have to.

It takes dishes that made Amazing Chinese Cuisine's reputation and puts them in a more casual room at a friendlier price point. For a visitor, that's useful in practice. You get food with a serious pedigree, and you can treat it as a weeknight dinner rather than a booking you build a trip around.

It's also new, and Shanghai notices new openings fast. Expect it to be talked about and busy, especially while it's still trending.

## The beef offal pot and the grouper

Two dishes connect Blossom to its parent kitchen. Order both.

- **Chaoshan beef offal pot.** Beef is central to Chaoshan cooking. The Shantou region is known across China for its beef hotpot and its habit of using the whole animal. An offal pot is the deep, slow-cooked side of that tradition: tripe and other cuts that need time and a well-built broth.
- **Pickled-mustard-greens grouper.** Preserved mustard greens are a staple of the Chaoshan pantry. Their sour, salty edge cuts through the sweetness of a white-fleshed fish like grouper.

Between them, these two show the region's two big strengths, its beef and its seafood. They're dishes for sharing, so they work best with at least two or three people around the table.

## Why Chaoshan food is worth the trip

Chaoshan, also called Teochew, is the coastal corner of eastern Guangdong around Chaozhou and Shantou. Its food is lighter-handed than most Chinese regional cooking and built around freshness and good stock.

Chaoshan cooks are known for braising, for preserved vegetables, and for treating beef and fish with real precision. Gongfu tea, poured in tiny cups, is part of the region's dining culture too. In Shanghai, where local cooking leans sweet and soy-dark, a Chaoshan table is a real change of pace.

If you've already eaten your way through xiaolongbao and red-braised pork, this is a good next step. You stay in China but cook from a different coastline.

## Finding it inside Plaza 66

Plaza 66 stands at 1266 Nanjing West Road in Jing'an, one of Shanghai's main shopping and office districts.

The nearest metro is Nanjing West Road Station, an interchange for Lines 2, 12 and 13. Follow the signs for Plaza 66, and from the station it's a short walk along the main road. Taxis and ride-hailing drivers all know the building by name: say "Hènglóng Guǎngchǎng" (恒隆广场).

Inside, Blossom is on the upper floors rather than at street level. Look for the mall directory or ask the information desk, then take the lift up. Allow a few extra minutes, because big Shanghai malls can mean a long walk between lift banks.

## Booking, paying and ordering on Nanjing West Road

New Shanghai restaurants fill quickly, and walk-ins at peak meal times can mean a wait. A few habits make the evening go more smoothly.

- **Book ahead.** Locals usually reserve through Dianping (大众点评) or the restaurant's WeChat account. If you can't use either, ask your hotel concierge to call. Confirm the opening hours at the same time; they weren't available when we checked.
- **Set up mobile payment.** Shanghai runs on Alipay and WeChat Pay. Both now accept many international cards, so link yours before the trip. Large malls generally take bank cards too, but don't rely on that.
- **Skip the tip.** Tipping isn't customary at mainland Chinese restaurants.
- **Order to share.** Dishes go in the middle of the table for everyone. Order one or two fewer plates than there are people, plus a staple like rice, then add more once you've seen the portions.

Many Shanghai restaurants take orders by QR code at the table. If Blossom does, a translation app handles the menu easily, and staff in a Plaza 66 restaurant will usually help if you point.

## Making an evening of Jing'an

Blossom fits easily into a day around Jing'an, and most of it is on foot.

- **Jing'an Temple** is a short walk west along Nanjing West Road. Its gilded roofs sit among glass towers, and it's lovely just before dinner.
- **Jing'an Park**, across the road from the temple, is a leafy place to sit for half an hour.
- **Nanjing West Road** itself links several large malls, including Plaza 66 and Jing'an Kerry Centre, so it works as a rainy-day route.
- **Wujiang Road**, close to Nanjing West Road Station, is a pedestrian lane of small snack shops if you want something light after dinner.

For a walk afterwards, head south from Jing'an into the plane-tree streets of the former French Concession. Change at Nanjing West Road Station for a quick ride anywhere else.

## Who Blossom suits

This is a good choice when you want something better than a food-court meal but don't need a white-tablecloth ceremony. It suits a first night in Shanghai, a dinner between shopping stops, or a group that wants to try a regional cuisine they don't know yet.

Plan on about an hour and a half at the table, longer if you add tea and a second round of dishes.
