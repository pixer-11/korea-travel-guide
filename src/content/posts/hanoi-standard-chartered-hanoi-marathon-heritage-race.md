## Racing the Old Quarter

Few marathons send runners through streets this old. The Heritage Race course was routed into Hanoi's Old Quarter, a grid of narrow trade streets that grew up beside Hoan Kiem Lake. Many of those streets are still named for the goods once sold on them.

On an ordinary day the narrow shophouse fronts face motorbikes parked on the pavement and plastic stools at the kerb. On a race morning the roads close and runners fill them instead. The marathon was AIMS/World Athletics certified, so a finishing time here counted as a properly measured result.

## Running Hanoi in November

November is the start of Hanoi's dry season. Air is cooler and less humid than in the summer months, which makes it one of the better times of year to run a long distance here. Air quality varies from day to day in Hanoi, and live AQI apps track it closely.

A long race weekend fits well around these Old Quarter landmarks:

- **Hoan Kiem Lake** and the red Huc Bridge to Ngoc Son Temple
- **Dong Xuan Market**, the large covered market at the quarter's north edge
- **Temple of Literature**, a short ride west
- **St Joseph's Cathedral**, just southwest of the lake

The Old Quarter is compact and easy to cover on foot. Road closures on race mornings make taxis unreliable, so hotels within walking distance of the course are the easiest base.

## Getting around race weekend

Hanoi's streets work by their own rules, and runners who know them have an easier weekend.

- **Crossing the street:** the trick is a steady, predictable pace with no sudden stops. Motorbikes steer around pedestrians; a sudden dash is what causes accidents.
- **Transport:** Grab is the standard ride-hailing app for cars and motorbike taxis. Detours or no service are common near closed roads on race mornings.
- **Paying:** street stalls and pho counters mostly take cash in Vietnamese dong. Hotels, cafés and race expos are more likely to take cards.
- **Fuelling:** pho and bánh mì are breakfast foods here, and many stalls open early. Runners usually stick to familiar food before a race and save the street food for afterwards.
- **Spectating:** supporters usually pick a spot on the course before roads close. Crossing the route once a race starts can mean long waits.

People don't usually tip at street stalls. Leaving small change at sit-down restaurants is appreciated but not expected.
