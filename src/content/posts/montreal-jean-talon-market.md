## Aisles that change with the season

Rows of stalls run under long roofs, each one stacked with crates. In June the crates hold Québec strawberries. In August they fill with corn, and by October you get pumpkins, apples and a lot of squash. The market has been trading here since 1933. Over the years the farm stalls have been joined by shops along the edges selling meat, fish, cheese and bread.

You can tell it's a working market because vendors put out cut fruit to taste. Maple products, Québec cheeses and preserves sit beside the produce, so you can fill a picnic or a suitcase from one loop. In the cold months enclosures go up around the central stalls and the market keeps trading.

Locals and tourists both come here in large numbers. Expect company.

## Reaching Henri-Julien

The market's address is 7070 Avenue Henri-Julien in Petite-Italie.

- **Metro:** De Castelnau (Blue Line) is the closest station, a short walk east. Jean-Talon station (Orange and Blue lines) is a little farther and handy if you're coming from downtown on the Orange Line.
- **Bike:** BIXI docks are spread around the neighbourhood, and the Saint-Laurent and Saint-Denis corridors bring you close.
- **Car:** the market has its own parking, but at weekends it fills just when the crowds peak.

For a full look at the stalls, plan on 1–2 hours. Add another hour if you're eating your way around.

## Beating the weekend crush

The crowd figures are clear. Weekdays are calmest from 8am to 11am. At weekends the quiet window is just 8am–9am. From 11am to 6pm on Saturdays and Sundays the aisles are shoulder to shoulder and the strollers barely move, so a late-morning weekend visit is the one to avoid.

At the moment it opens at 8am every day. It closes at 6pm Monday to Saturday and an hour earlier on Sunday, at 5pm. Market hours can shift through the year, so check the official site before an evening visit. Turning up at opening also means you get the best of the berries and pastries before they sell out.

## Shopping the stalls properly

Most of the trading happens in French, but nearly every vendor will switch to English. Start with "bonjour" and you'll be served faster.

- **Let the vendor pick.** At many produce stalls the staff fill your basket, so point and ask instead of handling the fruit.
- **Taste first.** If samples are out, they're meant for you. Trying something before you buy is normal here.
- **Pay how you like.** Most stalls take cards or tap, but a few small farm stands prefer cash. Prices are written on chalkboards or cards at each stall.
- **Bring a bag.** Shoppers here carry reusable totes or wheeled carts. A sturdy bag keeps peaches from being crushed on the metro.

Afterwards, walk south into Little Italy for espresso on Boulevard Saint-Laurent. You can also visit the Church of Notre-Dame-de-la-Défense, a few blocks away, known for its painted ceiling.
