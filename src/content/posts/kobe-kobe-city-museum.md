## The building is the first exhibit

Kobe City Museum occupies a 1935 neoclassical structure built as the Bank of Japan's Kobe branch. Stone columns and a heavy granite facade front onto Kyomachi, in Chuo Ward's old foreign concession grid. The bank vault survives in the basement, now used for storage and occasional display cases. Inside, the layout still reads as a banking hall: high ceilings, a central hall ringed by side galleries.

## What's on the walls

The permanent collection centers on Namban art, the 16th- and 17th-century Japanese paintings and screens depicting early contact with Portuguese and Dutch traders. Kobe was a key port for that exchange, so the pieces aren't generic imports; they're locally relevant history. Alongside them: archaeological finds from the Kobe area, old maps of the port, and documents tracing the city's growth from fishing village to treaty port.

Special exhibitions rotate through a separate gallery and usually carry their own admission on top of the permanent collection fee. Budget 60 to 90 minutes for the permanent galleries alone; add 30 to 45 minutes if a special exhibition is running.

## Hours, days, and getting there

Check the schedule before you go, since it varies by day.

- Closed Mondays
- Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Sunday: 9:30am–5:30pm
- Friday and Saturday: 9:30am–8:00pm

The museum is a short walk from both Sannomiya Station (JR, Hankyu, Hanshin, and subway lines) and Motomachi Station (JR and Hanshin), each about 10 minutes on foot through the Kyomachi and Sakaemachi shopping streets. Kobe City Hall and the Ijinkan foreign-residence district are both within easy walking range, so this pairs naturally with a half-day loop through Kitano or the harbor.

## Reading the room like a regular visitor

This is a well-known, well-reviewed museum, not a quiet back-street find, so plan around when other visitors show up rather than expecting to have it to yourself. Weekday mornings right at the 9:30am opening tend to be calmer than afternoons, since tour groups and school visits usually cluster after 11am. The Friday and Saturday evening hours, open until 8pm, are a genuine advantage: locals use this window after work, and it's noticeably less crowded than a Saturday afternoon.

- Buy tickets at the counter inside; no need to book ahead for the permanent collection
- Special exhibitions sometimes require a separate timed ticket during peak runs, so check the museum's own signage or site if a popular show is on
- Coin lockers near the entrance are worth using if you're carrying shopping bags from Kyomachi's arcades
- Explanatory labels are bilingual (Japanese and English) throughout the permanent galleries

Confirm current admission prices before visiting; special exhibitions typically add a separate fee beyond the base entry cost.
