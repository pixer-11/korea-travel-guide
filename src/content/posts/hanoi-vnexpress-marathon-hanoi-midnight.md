## A race that starts as Hanoi winds down

Most marathons start at dawn. This one is run in the dark.

The VNExpress Marathon Hanoi Midnight is the night edition of the running series organised by VnExpress, one of Vietnam's largest online newspapers. The race takes place on November 29, 2026. Runners line up under streetlights in a city better known for its daytime motorbike traffic, on roads that are closed off for the race.

It draws runners from all over Vietnam and from abroad. For many international entrants it is a reason to see Hanoi at an hour when its streets are unusually empty.

## Date, distances and where the details live

The date is fixed: November 29, 2026, which falls on a Sunday. Everything else sits with the organiser.

VnExpress publishes the race distances, start and finish area, wave schedule, cut-off times and registration links on its official VnExpress Marathon site. The race guide for runners is also where the course map and the bib-collection arrangements appear. This guide deliberately names no start time or price, because neither is given in our facts.

For a midnight race, read the date and start time together. A late start can mean the race begins one calendar evening and finishes the next morning. The organiser's schedule is the authority on which hour belongs to which day.

## Running the capital after midnight

Night running in Hanoi changes how the city feels underfoot.

By day, the streets around the centre are full of scooters, street vendors and pavements used as kitchens and parking. After midnight much of that has been packed away. Shopfronts are shuttered and the big boulevards are wide open. The tree-lined streets of the French-era districts are lit mostly by streetlamps.

There are a few practical results of a night start:

- **Headlamps are rarely needed** on lit city roads, but a light reflective layer helps on the walk to the start.
- **Fuelling shifts.** Runners usually eat a normal dinner in the early evening, then something small and familiar a few hours before the gun.
- **Sleep is the hard part.** Many entrants rest or nap through the afternoon rather than trying to stay awake all day.
- **Getting home** happens in the small hours, so most runners settle their ride back to the hotel before the race (see below).

## Late-November weather and what to wear

November is one of Hanoi's most comfortable months for running. The summer heat and humidity are gone and the dry season is setting in. Nights at the end of the month are typically cool rather than cold.

That makes kit simple. Most runners race in shorts and a singlet or tee. They wear a throwaway long-sleeve or a bin bag in the start pen and shed it once they warm up. Humidity can still be noticeable, so race-day hydration matters even when the air feels fresh.

Spectators standing still at night tend to feel the chill more than runners. A light jacket is the usual choice for anyone cheering from the kerb.

## Cheering, paying and moving around on race night

A few local habits make race night smoother.

**The cheer.** Vietnamese spectators shout *"Cố lên!"* (roughly "keep going!"). Learning it costs nothing, and runners hear it all along a Vietnamese course.

**Getting to the start.** Grab, the ride-hailing app used across Vietnam, is how most visitors move around Hanoi, by car or motorbike taxi. Road closures for the race can push drop-off points away from the start area. Runners usually allow a walking buffer from the nearest open street.

**Paying.** Small street stalls and noodle shops mostly run on Vietnamese dong in cash. Hotels, cafés and Grab take cards or app payment. Vietnamese people pay through apps such as MoMo, but most visitors can't easily set those up.

**Crossing streets after the race.** Once roads reopen, traffic returns fast. Locals cross at a steady, predictable walk so riders can flow around them. Stopping suddenly or dashing is what causes trouble.

**Bib collection.** Large Vietnamese races usually hand out bibs at a collection event in the days before the race. The organiser's race guide gives the venue and the ID required. Overseas runners usually arrive in Hanoi a day or two before the race for this reason.

## Where runners stay, and the bowl afterwards

Location decides how easy the night is.

**Hoan Kiem and the Old Quarter** are the usual base for visitors. Hotels and guesthouses are packed into a walkable grid around Hoan Kiem Lake. Grab rides out to most central venues are short. On weekend evenings the streets around the lake become a pedestrian zone. That makes Friday or Saturday a gentle shakeout walk before race night.

**Ba Dinh**, to the west, is quieter and greener. It holds the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex and the One Pillar Pagoda, a sensible sightseeing loop for tired legs on the following days.

As for the finish, Hanoi is the home of *phở*, and the city's breakfast culture starts early. A bowl of beef phở or *bún chả* (grilled pork with noodles and herbs) is the classic recovery meal once stalls open in the morning. For something warm and sweet, Hanoi's egg coffee (*cà phê trứng*) is a local speciality worth the walk.

## Making a trip of it

A late-November race leaves good days around it.

Most international runners add two or three days in Hanoi. The Temple of Literature, the Old Quarter's trade streets and West Lake fill the city days easily. Ha Long Bay and Ninh Binh are the classic overnight or day trips for anyone with longer, ideally after the legs have recovered rather than before.

The race itself stays the anchor. Visitors usually build the trip around that one long night on November 29, 2026, then spend the following mornings eating.
