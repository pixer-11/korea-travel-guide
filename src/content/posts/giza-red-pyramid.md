## Sneferu's smooth-sided experiment

It is the first pyramid built with true smooth sides. Pharaoh Sneferu built it around 2600 BC at Dahshur, the royal necropolis serving ancient Memphis. Before this one he had built the Bent Pyramid, whose slope changes partway up.

The Red Pyramid rises about 105 metres at a shallower angle of roughly 43 degrees. Most of its white limestone casing was stripped long ago. What's left is the rust-pink stone underneath, and that colour gave the pyramid its name.

From the sand around the base you look up at long, even courses of weathered blocks. Desert stretches away on every side. The Bent Pyramid sits in plain view to the south, close enough to visit on the same trip.

## Down the passage to the chambers

You enter on the north face. A stairway built against the masonry takes you up about 28 metres to the opening. From there a narrow passage slopes down roughly 60 metres into the rock.

The ceiling is low, so most adults go down bent double, gripping wooden handrails over slatted boards. At the bottom are two antechambers with tall corbelled roofs, where each stone course steps inward over the one below. A wooden staircase then climbs to the burial chamber.

Visitors often mention a sharp ammonia smell down there and stale, warm air. If tight spaces bother you, think it through before you start the descent. The climb back up is the harder half.

## Reaching Dahshur

No metro or train serves the site. Most people hire a driver or book a ride-hail car from Cairo or the Giza plateau. The drive south runs through farmland and palm groves.

Dahshur fits easily into a day with:

- **Saqqara**, with the Step Pyramid of Djoser, a short drive north
- **Memphis** open-air museum and its colossus of Ramesses II
- **The Bent Pyramid**, inside the same Dahshur site

Allow 1–1.5 hours at the Red Pyramid, including the descent. Add another hour if you also walk to the Bent Pyramid.

## Timing the climb and the descent

At the moment it opens 9am–5pm every day of the week. Hours at Egyptian antiquities sites change with the season and around holidays, so check the official antiquities site before you set out.

It's usually quietest between 3pm and 5pm, both on weekdays and at weekends. Weekends between 10am and 1pm are when it typically fills up. Arriving then can mean a queue on that single narrow passage, with people climbing up past you as you go down.

Tickets are sold at the site gate, and the current price is posted there. A few practical habits help:

- **Carry small Egyptian pound notes.** Guardians at the entrance or in the chambers may expect baksheesh (a small tip), for example for holding a torch or pointing out details.
- **Bring water and a hat.** There is almost no shade on the walk up the north face.
- **Wear clothes you can crouch in.** Dust from the passage gets onto knees and palms.
- **Go down backwards on the steepest stretch** if facing forward feels unstable. Many visitors do.
