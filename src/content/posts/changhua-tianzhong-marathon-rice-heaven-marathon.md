## A marathon run through a rice bowl

Tianzhong sits on the flat paddy land of southern Changhua County. The town's name means roughly "in the fields", and the marathon takes that literally. For most of the route, runners are on narrow farm roads with irrigation channels on one side and rice paddies stretching off toward the low ridge of the Bagua Mountains on the other.

The race is widely counted among Taiwan's top five running events. It has earned that ranking without big-city scenery. People talk about it because of what happens along the roadside, and because a whole farming township treats the day as its own festival.

## When and where it happens

The 2026 edition was set for early November. That is the start of central Taiwan's drier season, when mornings on the plain are usually mild. Once the sun clears the mountains, though, the open fields offer almost no shade.

The organiser set the exact race date, the start and finish location, the distances and registration. It also handled the race-day schedule, bag drop and cut-off rules.

## Getting to Tianzhong

Tianzhong is easier to reach than its rural setting suggests. There are two main rail options, plus a larger base nearby:

- **Taiwan High Speed Rail, Changhua Station**: this station is in Tianzhong Township. It is on the main north–south high-speed line between Taipei and Kaohsiung.
- **Taiwan Railway, Tianzhong Station**: a conventional station on the Western Line in the town centre. It is the more walkable choice for the race area.
- **Changhua City**: the county seat, further north on the Western Line. It has more hotels for runners who stay over the night before.

On race weekends, roads around the town typically close for the course. Visiting runners usually travel by train rather than driving in.

## The roadside feast

This is why runners come back. Residents of the villages along the route set up their own tables and hand out food and drink. These tables are separate from the official aid stations, and the race is known across Taiwan for this hospitality.

What turns up depends on the household. Changhua County is famous for *bawan*, its big translucent meatballs, and local snacks and fruit are typical. Many runners slow down to take it all in, and some treat the second half of the course as a tasting walk.

Cheering is part of the same tradition. Families stand at farm gates, temple squares and road junctions, and local groups bring drums and music to some stretches. The countryside here is not empty on race day.

## Running among the paddies

The terrain is mostly flat farmland, with long straight stretches between villages. Flat sounds easy, but it means few landmarks to break up the distance. It also means full exposure to sun and wind across open fields.

Runners chasing a time usually start strong in the cooler early hours, before the heat builds over the fields. Runners who come for the experience take it slower and save energy for the food tables. Both are normal at Tianzhong, and they share the same roads.

## Race-day manners in Tianzhong

How runners behave matters to the people who host them. These habits go down well:

- **Thanks in Mandarin or Taiwanese.** A quick *xièxie* (thank you) or *jiāyóu* (the usual cheer, roughly "keep going") is customary, and residents often say it back.
- **Taking only what you'll eat.** Food on the home-run tables is offered by the households themselves. Taking a bite and leaving the rest is seen as wasteful.
- **Using the bins.** The course runs past working farms and irrigation ditches. Cups and wrappers dropped there end up in someone's field.
- **Keeping the farm roads clear.** Lanes are narrow, so runners step to the side for photos with the paddies behind them rather than stopping mid-road.
- **Cash in town.** The small eateries and stalls around Tianzhong Station are more often cash-only than places in Taipei.

## Around Tianzhong after the finish

Most visiting runners stay in town for a meal and then head home by train. With an extra day, Changhua County is worth exploring.

**Changhua City** has the Great Buddha on Bagua Mountain and the round Fan-shaped Roundhouse, a historic railway locomotive shed. **Lukang**, toward the coast, is an old port town with Longshan Temple, Tianhou Temple and lanes of traditional shops. Both are a short train or bus ride from Tianzhong, so a race weekend can easily become a longer stay.
