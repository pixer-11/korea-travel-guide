## The edge of the plateau

The road simply runs out. Repeater Station Road climbs the Springbrook plateau, passes a scatter of holiday cabins and bush, and stops at a small car park. Beyond that is a track, and beyond the track the land falls away into New South Wales.

Best of All Lookout is the southern lip of Springbrook, a remnant of the huge Tweed shield volcano that erupted here more than 20 million years ago. You stand on the rim of what's left of it and look down into the eroded caldera. Few lookouts in southeast Queensland give you the geology this plainly.

The rainforest here belongs to the Gondwana Rainforests of Australia World Heritage Area. That status explains the trees you walk under on the way in.

## The walk under the Antarctic beeches

The track is short: roughly 600 metres return on a formed path, an easy 20–30 minutes at a slow pace. Mostly it's a stroll in deep shade. Moss grows over everything, and tree ferns lean across the path.

About halfway along you reach the Antarctic beeches (*Nothofagus moorei*). These gnarled, multi-trunked trees are a living link to the cool forests of Gondwana. Their roots spill across the ground in thick knuckles, and the trunks are furred with moss and lichen. Many visitors stop longer here than at the view.

- Stay on the path: the roots are fragile and the ground is often slick.
- After rain, check your ankles for leeches. They are common in Springbrook's wet forest.
- The path suits most walkers, though the damp surface calls for shoes with some grip.

## Reading the view: Wollumbin to the sea

The forest ends at a railed platform. Directly south, the sharp peak of Wollumbin (Mount Warning) rises from the middle of the caldera floor. It's the volcano's old central plug, and it is a sacred place for the Bundjalung people.

Below you, the Tweed Valley spreads out in a patchwork of cane fields, farmland and small towns. Further out, on a clear day, the coastline runs towards Byron Bay. Turn your head and the cliffs and forested ridges of the caldera rim curve away on both sides.

On clear mornings the light comes in low across the valley. Cloud often pools in the lowlands while the plateau sits above it.

## Getting up the mountain

You need a car. No public transport runs to the lookout, and tour operators are the only alternative if you don't drive.

From the coast, head inland through Mudgeeraba and take Springbrook Road up the escarpment. It climbs in tight, narrow bends, so take it slowly and watch for cyclists. At the top, follow the signs onto Repeater Station Road and drive to its end. Allow roughly an hour or more from central Gold Coast, depending on where you start.

The car park is small. That matters a great deal on weekends, as the next section explains.

## Beating the weekend car park

The lookout is well known but still feels like a find: it sits at the far end of a dead-end road, past many of Springbrook's busier stops. The pressure point is parking, not the platform.

- **Avoid weekends between 9am and 7pm.** That's typically when the car park fills and people queue at the railing for photos.
- **Go on a weekday for daylight views.** Early in the morning, the plateau is at its quietest before the day-trippers drive up.
- **The quietest windows are at night:** usually 8pm–10pm on weekdays and 7pm–11pm on weekends. Google lists the lookout as open 24 hours at the moment. With no town lights nearby, a clear night up here can be dark enough for good stargazing. Bring a torch for the forest track.
- **Take turns at the railing.** The platform is compact, so step back once you've had your look.
- **Leave nothing behind.** This is a national park with no bins on the track, so carry out your rubbish.

Before you drive up, check the Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service website for track closures. Storms and maintenance sometimes shut sections of Springbrook.

## Mist, cold and the plateau's weather

Springbrook sits at roughly 900–1,000 metres. It's noticeably cooler and wetter than the beaches below. Pack a warm layer even if the coast is sweltering.

Cloud is the lookout's great gamble. Some days the whole valley vanishes into white, and the view is the inside of a cloud. Check the weather radar or a webcam forecast before committing. If the plateau is clagged in, the beech walk is still atmospheric, but don't count on seeing Wollumbin.

The drier, clearer months of winter usually give the sharpest long-distance views towards the coast.

## Building a Springbrook day

Best of All is a natural last stop: the road ends here, so you'll pass the plateau's other sights on the way. Worth linking together:

- **Purling Brook Falls:** a tall waterfall with a circuit walk down into the gorge.
- **Canyon Lookout:** views straight down the valley towards the coast.
- **Goomoolahra Falls:** a short stop close to Repeater Station Road, where the water drops off the plateau edge.
- **Natural Bridge:** a separate section of the park, reached by a different road. Famous for its rock arch and glow-worms after dark.

Give the lookout itself about an hour with time under the beeches. A loop of the plateau's main stops fills a comfortable half day from the coast.
