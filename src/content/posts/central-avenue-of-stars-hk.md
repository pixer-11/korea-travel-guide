## Why go

This isn't a museum you tour — it's a stretch of harbourfront you walk along, slowly, with Hong Kong Island glowing across the water. The draw is twofold: the skyline view and the tribute to a century of Hong Kong film.

Plaques set into the pavement carry the handprints of stars like Jackie Chan, Bruce Lee, and Anita Mui. A bronze Bruce Lee statue, fist raised mid-kick, is the spot everyone lines up to photograph.

## Getting there

The Avenue runs along the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront, easiest reached on foot from the Star Ferry Pier after crossing from Central by ferry — a ride that's an attraction in itself. Alternatively, take the MTR to East Tsim Sha Tsui Station and follow signs for the Avenue of Stars or Salisbury Road.

- **By ferry**: Star Ferry from Central Pier, then walk east along the harbourfront.
- **By MTR**: East Tsim Sha Tsui Station, exit toward Salisbury Road.
- **On foot**: connects directly to the Hong Kong Cultural Centre and Museum of Art promenade.

## What to see and do

Budget 45 minutes to an hour for a full walk end to end, longer if you're queuing for photos. The handprint plaques run in a loose chronological line, so you can trace Hong Kong cinema's history just by walking.

Beyond the Bruce Lee statue, look for:

- The Golden Bauhinia-style lighting rig, lit for the nightly *A Symphony of Lights* show
- Handprints of directors and stars spanning decades of Hong Kong film
- Unobstructed views of the Central skyline and Victoria Harbour

If you're there after dark, the multimedia light show over the harbour runs most evenings — check current showtimes locally, as they shift.

## When to go

The promenade is open 24 hours, every day, so timing is entirely up to you. Crowd patterns, though, are sharp and worth planning around.

On both weekdays and weekends, the calm window is **7am to 9am** — joggers, a few photographers, little else. Weekend afternoons are the opposite: crowds build steadily from 3pm and stay heavy until 11pm.

Sunset is genuinely the best light for photos, but it also overlaps with the busiest hours. If you want the statue shot without twenty people in frame, morning is the trade-off.

## How to visit like a local

This is a very popular stop, not a hidden one — the trick is beating the crowds rather than avoiding them altogether. Come right at 7am and you'll get the harbour, the statue, and the plaques almost to yourself.

- **Payment**: none needed — the Avenue itself is free and unticketed.
- **Photos at the Bruce Lee statue**: expect a short wait midday; early morning or after midnight it's usually clear.
- **Etiquette**: step around the handprint plaques rather than over them — many visitors treat them like a walk of fame and photograph each one.
- **Common mistake**: arriving right at sunset expecting space. That's exactly when weekend crowds peak, especially near showtime for the light display.
- **Combine it with**: the Star Ferry crossing, the Museum of Art next door, or a slow dinner in Tsim Sha Tsui once the crowds thin after 11pm.

Bring layers if you're going for sunrise — the harbourfront wind picks up early, even when the day turns warm later.

## Nearby and practical notes

The Avenue sits within easy walking distance of Tsim Sha Tsui's main sights, so it pairs naturally with a half-day itinerary rather than standing alone. The Hong Kong Cultural Centre, Space Museum, and Clock Tower are all a few minutes' walk east.

There's no admission fee and no closing time, which makes it one of the few Hong Kong attractions you can genuinely visit on a whim — whether that's a pre-breakfast walk or a last stop after a late dinner across the harbour.
