## Bare rock where the river meets the bay

Cape Merry sits at the end of Cape Merry Road, on the west side of the Churchill River mouth. The ground is glacier-scoured Precambrian rock, rounded and cracked and patched with lichen. There are hardly any trees. Across the channel stands Prince of Wales Fort, the Hudson's Bay Company's 18th-century stone fortress.

On the point is the Cape Merry Battery, a low stone gun emplacement built in the 1740s to cover the river entrance together with the fort. Cannon still sit in it. It's part of Prince of Wales Fort National Historic Site, run by Parks Canada. Give it 45 minutes to an hour, or longer when the belugas are running.

## What to look for from the point

There's more on the water than on the land.

- **Beluga whales:** thousands come into the Churchill estuary in summer, mostly July and August. Their white backs surface close to the rocks.
- **Prince of Wales Fort:** you can only reach it by boat tour, and from here you see its walls end to end.
- **Hudson Bay itself:** open water in summer. In late autumn it starts to freeze from the shoreline.
- **The grain elevator:** Churchill's port skyline rises behind you, and it's the easiest way to get your bearings.
- **Polar bears:** they travel this coast. You might see one from the point, and that's exactly why the next section matters.

## Bears, light and the quiet hours

Don't walk out from town. Bears move along this shoreline, especially in October and November while they wait for the bay to freeze, and the rocks give them plenty of cover. Get here by car, taxi or tour van. In summer Parks Canada usually staffs the site. Outside that season, nobody is watching for you.

Google currently lists the site as open 24 hours. That only means it's an open outdoor site, not that staff are there. Parks Canada's website has this season's interpretive programme.

Crowds are easy to plan around:
- **Quietest on weekdays:** 8pm–9pm.
- **Quietest on weekends:** 3pm–7pm.
- **Avoid on weekends:** 8am–9am, 12pm–1pm, 2pm–3pm and 7pm–11pm, when the point fills up.

July evenings stay light late at this latitude, so an after-dinner visit works well.

## Standing on the rocks Churchill-style

Before you open the door, look along the shore from inside the vehicle. Park close, stay close, and don't wander behind outcrops you can't see over. Keep your group together. Read the bear warning signs at the site. If staff tell you to get back in the vehicle, do it straight away and don't ask why.

The wind off the bay is cold even in July. Rock near the water gets slick. Bring:
- a windproof layer and a hat
- insect repellent for summer biting flies
- binoculars for the belugas and the fort walls

Churchill has no road connection. You arrive on the VIA Rail train from Winnipeg or by air. Pair Cape Merry with a boat tour from the port, which takes you out to the belugas and across to Prince of Wales Fort. You can finish at the Itsanitaq Museum in town for Inuit carving and Arctic history.
