## Four voices, one arena by the old runway

MAMAMOO is a group built on vocals. Solar, Moonbyul, Wheein and Hwasa debuted in 2014 under RBW, and they made their name on live harmonies rather than flawless lip-sync. That reputation is why fans travel for their concerts and don't just stream them.

The 4WARD tour brings them to Kai Tak Arena in Hong Kong as part of their 2026 world schedule. The title is a play on four members moving forward together. For many longtime MooMoos, as the fandom calls itself, that idea carries real weight.

## Kai Tak Arena, explained

The arena sits inside Kai Tak Sports Park. The park was built on land that once belonged to Kai Tak Airport, where jets famously banked low over Kowloon City's rooftops until the airport closed in 1998. It is now Hong Kong's newest major events complex.

The park has three main venues:

- **Kai Tak Stadium**, the large retractable-roof bowl used for stadium-scale shows
- **Kai Tak Arena**, the indoor venue hosting this concert, sized for arena tours rather than stadium crowds
- **Kai Tak Youth Sports Ground**, a smaller outdoor ground

Because it is indoors, the arena feels closer than a stadium. Even from the upper tiers, you can follow the members' faces on the screens and hear the harmonies cleanly. Rain and Hong Kong's humidity stay outside the door.

## Getting to Kai Tak by MTR

The MTR is how most concertgoers get there. Two stations on the **Tuen Ma Line** serve the sports park:

- **Kai Tak Station**, to the north of the park
- **Sung Wong Toi Station**, closer to the Kowloon City side

From Central or Tsim Sha Tsui, the usual route is to change onto the Tuen Ma Line at Hung Hom or Ho Man Tin. From there it is only a few stops. Most people tap in with an Octopus card or a contactless bank card, which saves queuing at ticket machines when the post-show crowd floods the platforms.

Taxis do serve the area, but the roads around the park clog after a full-house event. Most fans head straight for the MTR when the encore ends.

## How MooMoos do concert night

A MAMAMOO show runs on audience participation. Fans usually learn the official fanchants before the concert, especially for the big singles and the songs with spoken-word breaks. The chants are shouted in Korean, and the cheering in the hall is loud and well timed.

What attendees usually bring or do:

- **The official lightstick.** It syncs with the show's lighting, so the seating blocks change colour together.
- **Merch queues before doors.** Fans typically get to the merchandise booths hours before showtime, because popular items sell out.
- **Slogans and banners.** Handheld fan slogans come out during ballads and the closing songs.
- **Photocard trading.** This often happens around the plaza outside the arena before the show.

Inside, staff generally enforce the venue's rules on professional cameras and recording equipment. Fans who want to keep their gear usually bring only a phone.

## Eating before and after in Kowloon City

The sports park sits next to Kowloon City, one of Hong Kong's best neighbourhoods for casual eating. The old streets around **South Wall Road** and **Nga Tsin Wai Road** are packed with Thai restaurants and grocers. That reflects a long-established Thai community here.

Many fans eat there before heading to the arena, since a filling dinner is easier than queuing at concession stands. Cha chaan tengs and dessert shops round out the options.

With spare daylight, **Kowloon Walled City Park** is walkable from the neighbourhood. Its gardens sit on the site of the notorious former walled enclave. **Sung Wong Toi Garden**, with its inscribed historic rock, is closer still to the MTR station of the same name.
