Late January is the one stretch of the Abu Dhabi year when an afternoon in an open stadium feels pleasant rather than punishing. Daytime temperatures usually sit in the low-to-mid twenties Celsius. Evenings turn cool enough that people bring a light layer. That climate is the reason a women's tennis tournament can sit here on the calendar at all.

## A week on the Gulf swing

The 2027 edition runs from Sunday 31 January to Saturday 6 February. It falls just after the Australian Open, at the start of the WTA's Middle East run that continues in Doha and Dubai. Players come from Melbourne still in match form, and some come in search of ranking points they missed there.

The tournament is a recent addition to the women's tour. Belinda Bencic won the first edition in 2023. Earlier editions sat at WTA 500 level, which puts it one step below the big combined events. In practice that means a compact draw where top-20 names regularly turn up in the first round.

Mubadala, the Abu Dhabi sovereign investment company, is the title sponsor. Its name is also on the men's exhibition held in the city every December.

## The courts at Zayed Sports City

Earlier editions were held at the International Tennis Centre inside Zayed Sports City, the large sports complex in the city's inland southern districts. It sits alongside Zayed Sports City Stadium. The tennis centre has an open-air centre court bowl ringed with steep seating, plus outside courts where you can stand close enough to hear the ball off the strings.

The surface is outdoor hard court. Afternoon sessions are played in full sun with almost no shade on the higher rows. Evening sessions are played under floodlights after the temperature drops.

The organiser publishes the 2027 venue, daily schedule and ticket links on the tournament's official site. That is the place the order of play appears each evening for the following day.

## Tickets and sessions

Tournament tickets are usually sold by session or by day, and they are priced and listed on the official tournament site. The early rounds, from Sunday through midweek, carry the most matches per ticket. A grounds-level pass often lets you wander the outside courts as well as centre court.

The later days narrow the field:

- **Quarter-finals:** the busiest single day for centre court.
- **Semi-finals:** fewer matches, usually the strongest crowds.
- **Saturday 6 February:** the closing day, when a tournament's final is normally played.

Fans who care about seeing particular players tend to buy for the opening days. By the weekend the names are no longer guaranteed to be in the draw.

## Courtside manners in Abu Dhabi

Tennis has its own rules of movement, and ushers here enforce them. Spectators enter and leave the seating bowl only at changeovers, every odd game. Nobody stands or talks while a point is in play. Phone flashes are off during rallies.

Some practical habits regulars follow:

- **Dress:** UAE public venues expect shoulders and knees to be covered. Light long sleeves also double as sun protection on afternoon sessions.
- **Sun:** hats and sunglasses are standard in the upper rows, and sunscreen goes on before the first match, not after.
- **Payment:** cards and phone payments are accepted almost everywhere in Abu Dhabi, including food stalls at sports venues.
- **Weekends:** the UAE weekend is Saturday and Sunday, so both ends of this tournament week fall on days off for local fans.

Players often sign autographs near the outside courts after practice and early matches. Fans who hope for one usually wait by the courtside rails with a large ball or programme.

## Getting there and away

Most visitors reach Zayed Sports City by taxi or ride-hail app; Careem and Uber both operate in Abu Dhabi. From the Corniche and downtown it is a straightforward drive south. From Yas Island or Saadiyat the ride is longer but runs mostly on the motorway.

Abu Dhabi has no metro, and its public buses are slow for a night-session exit. After the last match, ride-hail pickup queues build quickly at the gates. Many attendees walk a few minutes out of the complex before requesting a car.

Hotels around the Corniche, Al Maryah Island and the Grand Mosque area keep travel times short. Abu Dhabi International Airport (Zayed International) is roughly half an hour's drive from Zayed Sports City.

## Filling the gaps between sessions

The tournament week leaves mornings free for anyone holding evening tickets. The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is the closest major sight, a short taxi ride away, and its white marble courtyard is at its best in soft morning light. Visitors to the mosque follow a strict modest-dress code, the same clothing that works at the tennis.

Further afield:

- **Louvre Abu Dhabi** on Saadiyat Island, under Jean Nouvel's perforated dome.
- **Qasr Al Hosn**, the city's oldest stone building, downtown.
- **The Corniche**, a long seafront promenade that suits an evening walk after a day session.

February is also high season for Abu Dhabi's desert trips, so a dune excursion fits neatly into a rest day between rounds.
