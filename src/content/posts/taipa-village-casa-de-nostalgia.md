## A green house facing the old shoreline

Avenida da Praia means "beach avenue." The beach is long gone.

What's left is a promenade lined with old trees and a row of colonial-era houses painted in mint green with white trim. Their verandas face a stretch of reclaimed land and ponds that used to be open water. Casa de Nostalgia is one of these houses, run as a small museum.

Across the ponds, the towers of the Cotai Strip fill the horizon. That view is the reason to come. A 1920s house behind you, the casino resorts of reclaimed Cotai in front of you, and the whole story of how Taipa changed fits between the two.

## What the Taipa Houses are

The houses along this promenade went up in 1921. They were homes for Macanese families and Portuguese civil servants back when Taipa was a separate island with a small fishing and firecracker economy. Today they are grouped together as the Taipa Houses-Museum, and each house has its own theme.

Casa de Nostalgia is the house given over to the past itself. It is a domestic-scale museum, so expect a handful of rooms and not a gallery wing. Exhibits are refreshed from time to time. Check the panel at the door or the Macao Government Tourism Office listings to see what's on before you build a trip around it.

The architecture deserves a look in its own right:

- **Raised verandas** with balustrades, built to catch the sea breeze before air conditioning
- **Tall shuttered windows** set in thick, pale-trimmed walls
- **High ceilings** that keep the rooms cool even in Macau's humid summers
- **Front steps** that lead down to the promenade and the old waterline

## Getting to Avenida da Praia

Taipa Village is on the north side of Cotai, and it's easy to reach without a taxi.

- **Macau LRT, Taipa Line:** Pai Kok station is the closest stop to the old village. From there it's a short walk downhill toward the waterfront.
- **On foot from the Cotai resorts:** From The Venetian Macao, walk north past the ponds. The green houses are visible well before you reach them.
- **Free resort shuttles:** Many casino hotels run shuttles from the ferry terminals and border crossings into Cotai. Take one, then walk the rest.
- **Bus:** Plenty of public routes stop around the edge of Taipa Village. Check the current route numbers at the stop, because they change.

The address is Avenida da Praia, plus code 5H36+M3J. Put that code into a map app and it will drop the pin on the promenade itself.

## Timing your visit around the closed Monday

The house is shut every Monday. People who fly in for a long weekend get caught by this more than anything else.

Tuesday to Sunday it opens from 10am to 7pm, with the same hours every day. There's no short day to plan around. The village fills up with day-trippers from the afternoon onwards, when visitors drift over from the Cotai resorts. A morning visit to the house followed by lunch on Rua do Cunha usually sidesteps the thickest of that.

Late afternoon is the other good window. Arrive by about 5pm and you'll have time inside before the 7pm close. Then you can stay on the promenade as the lights switch on across the strip.

If the lotus ponds in front of the houses matter to you, come in summer. That's when the lotus blooms, and it has long been the season for flower displays along this stretch.

## Moving through the house without missing it

This is a quiet museum. It is not yet on most visitors' Macau lists, and it shows: you'll often have a room to yourself.

- **Read the signage first.** Labels are usually in Chinese, Portuguese and English. The panel at the entrance explains the current theme, and the rooms make more sense after it.
- **Look for the photography rules.** Rules vary by exhibit and are posted inside. If you can't see a notice, ask the attendant.
- **Keep your voice down.** The rooms are small and sound carries. Staff tend to stand close by.
- **Leave big bags at the hotel.** Doorways and corridors are narrow, and a backpack swung round in a period room is a real risk.
- **Pay attention to the floor plan.** How the rooms connect tells you as much about Macanese domestic life as the objects do.

Many visitors stop at the facades and never go inside. If you do the same, you'll have walked past the part of the green row that actually explains it.

## Up the steps to Carmel and down to Rua do Cunha

The house is the start of a loop through the village, not the whole day.

Right above the houses, up a flight of stone steps, stands the **Church of Our Lady of Carmel**, a pale yellow church from 1885. Its small garden looks down over the green roofs, and couples use it as a backdrop for wedding photos. Walk inland and you reach **Rua do Cunha**, a narrow pedestrian lane of bakeries and snack counters.

What to look out for along the lane:

- **Pork chop buns**, Macau's signature snack: a fried chop in a crusty roll
- **Almond cookies** in the bakery windows, often with free samples on the counter
- **Serradura**, the Macanese "sawdust" pudding of cream layered with crushed biscuit
- **Portuguese egg tarts**, sold warm from the trays

Restaurants in the lanes around **Largo do Camões** serve Macanese cooking. Look for minchi, African chicken and other dishes that come from the territory's Portuguese and Asian mix.

## Paying and getting around the village

Macau uses the pataca, but Hong Kong dollars are taken almost everywhere in Taipa Village at roughly par. Change usually comes back in patacas. Card and mobile payments work in most shops, while some small snack stalls prefer cash.

For admission details at Casa de Nostalgia, check the notice at the entrance or the official Macau tourism site, since arrangements for the Taipa Houses can change. Tipping isn't expected at museum counters. In sit-down restaurants, look at the bill first, because many already add a service charge.

The village is compact and hilly, and the lanes are cobbled with Portuguese-style calçada paving. Everything here is within a 15-minute walk of the green houses. Leave the taxi until you head back to Cotai.
