## STEAL THE WIND reaches Kyushu

One date, one dome. On December 19, 2026, TXT (Tomorrow X Together) play Mizuho PayPay Dome as part of their 'STEAL THE WIND' tour. That puts all five members, Soobin, Yeonjun, Beomgyu, Taehyun and Huening Kai, under the largest roof in Kyushu.

The group debuted under Big Hit Music in 2019, and Japanese dome dates have since become a regular part of their touring. A dome stop is the scale where MOA, the fandom, fill the stands with synced lightsticks. Every tier of seating turns into one moving field of colour.

## Inside Mizuho PayPay Dome

The building sits on reclaimed land facing Hakata Bay. Opened in 1993, it was Japan's first stadium with a retractable roof, and it is home ground of the Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks baseball team. In December the roof stays shut, so the cold off the bay stays outside.

The surrounding area has plenty to fill the hours before doors:

- **Fukuoka Tower**, a mirrored 234m lattice a few minutes' walk west
- **MARK IS Fukuoka Momochi**, the shopping mall attached to the dome, with food courts and cafés
- **Momochi Seaside Park**, a man-made beach that ends at the bay

## Getting to Jigyohama

The Fukuoka City Subway Airport Line (Kuko Line) does most of the work. Tojinmachi Station is the usual stop, followed by a flat walk of roughly 15 minutes north through a residential grid. Ohorikoen Station, one stop east, is a slightly longer walk and pulls some of the crowd away.

Travel times on the same line are short:

- **Fukuoka Airport**: direct, about 15 minutes to Tojinmachi
- **Hakata Station**: around 11 minutes, for shinkansen arrivals
- **Tenjin**: a few stops, the city's shopping centre

Visitors usually stay in Tenjin or Hakata, where hotel stock is deepest. Nishitetsu buses and taxis also serve the dome. After the encore, the walk to Tojinmachi turns into a slow-moving river of people, so many fans walk on to Ohorikoen or eat nearby while the platforms clear.

## Dome-day etiquette, Fukuoka style

Japanese dome concerts run on order. Tickets for shows of this size are typically allocated by lottery and issued as smartphone e-tickets, often name-registered, with ID checks at the gates. The tour's organiser and its ticketing partners publish the entry rules, and staff at the gates enforce them as written.

What attendees usually do:

- **Tap in with an IC card** (Hayakaken, Suica or similar) to skip ticket machines on the crowded return trip
- **Queue for official merch** at the dome's goods area hours before doors, since lightsticks and tour goods draw the longest lines of the day
- **Follow the posted photo and filming rules**, which Japanese venues apply strictly
- **Stay at the seat number on the ticket**, as moving between blocks is not tolerated

Fukuoka's yatai food stalls along the Naka River in Nakasu and in Tenjin are open late on most nights. Many fans finish the night there with a bowl of Hakata tonkotsu ramen.
