## Forty-one years of Grasshopper

This is an anniversary show. Grasshopper (草蜢) are Calvin Choy, Remus Choy and Edmond So, the trio that came up in mid-1980s Hong Kong and never really stopped. The Three in Love Concert 2026 marks their 41st year and runs over two nights, October 24 and 25, 2026, at The Venetian Arena in Macau.

Their story is tied to Canto-pop's golden age. They started out dancing alongside Anita Mui before their own hits took over. Songs like 失戀 and 半點心 are karaoke staples across Hong Kong and Guangdong. The crowd at a Grasshopper show tends to know every word and every hand move, and many of them have done so since the cassette years.

## Inside The Venetian Arena

The Venetian Arena is a full indoor bowl. It holds roughly 15,000 people, which makes it one of the largest concert halls in the Pearl River Delta. Seats rise steeply on three sides of the floor, so even the upper tiers look down on the stage instead of across at it.

You reach it through the resort itself. That means a walk past the gaming floor, under painted ceilings and along the indoor canals of The Venetian Macao. The arena entrance sits off the main concourse, and staff route concert crowds along roped lanes on show nights. Before the show, people queue for merchandise in the foyer.

## Crossing to Cotai

Most of the audience travels in from Hong Kong or mainland China, and the routes are well worn:

- **Ferry from Hong Kong:** Cotai Water Jet sailings land at Taipa Ferry Terminal, a short ride from the Strip.
- **Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge:** shuttle buses cross from the Hong Kong port to the Macau port in under an hour.
- **Free resort shuttles:** the Sands group runs buses from Taipa Ferry Terminal, the Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal and the bridge port to The Venetian.
- **Light Rapid Transit:** the Taipa Line's Cotai West station is a short walk from the resort.

From the Zhuhai side, Gongbei crossing at the Border Gate is the usual route. Taxis or buses cover the rest.

## How a concert night runs in Cotai

On concert nights Cotai fills with fans, so a few habits matter. Fans typically arrive well before doors to buy merchandise and to get through the bag check, and in the resort's food courts it is hard to find a seat around dinner time. A lot of people eat at the Grand Canal Shoppes on the upper level, then walk straight down.

The local currency is the pataca. Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted on the Strip, usually at near-par, though change comes back in patacas. Inside the resort, cards and mobile wallets work almost everywhere.

At the show, people sing along loudly, which is normal and expected. They also stay in their seats until the encores finish, and Grasshopper are known for those.

The crush comes after the last song. Thousands of people head for the same shuttle bays and taxi rank at once. Many fans stay overnight in Cotai rather than queue for the last ferries or bridge buses. Others hang back for half an hour in the shopping mall until the lines thin.

## Staying and eating around the Strip

A two-night run makes Cotai an easy weekend. The Venetian, The Parisian Macao, The Londoner and Four Seasons are all linked by covered walkways, so you can walk from your room to the arena without stepping outside. On the other side of the road, Galaxy Macau and City of Dreams add more choice.

Taipa Village is about a fifteen-minute walk from the Strip. Its narrow lanes, especially Rua do Cunha, are packed with almond cookie shops, pork chop bun stalls and Macanese restaurants. Fans often eat there in the afternoon before the show. Further on, the Taipa Houses-Museum and its row of green colonial villas suit a slower morning after the second night.

## Where the details live

Shows at The Venetian Arena are generally ticketed through Cotai Ticketing, the box office run by the resort's operator.
