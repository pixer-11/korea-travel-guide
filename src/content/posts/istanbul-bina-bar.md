## Why Bina Bar is worth the detour

Kadıköy has never been short on bars, but Bina Bar is the one everyone on the Asian side seems to be talking about right now. Part of the appeal is architectural: this is a converted multi-story building — "bina" literally means "building" in Turkish — that stacks its rooms and terraces on top of each other rather than spreading out across one flat floor plan. You feel the change in register as you climb: each level has its own light, its own noise level, its own reason to linger.

But the real hook, the thing everyone posts about before they've even ordered a drink, is the entrance. You don't just walk into Bina Bar — you cross a garden first, and in the middle of it sits a shallow pool lit from below, presided over by a stone lion statue that looks like it wandered in from a much older building. It's theatrical in a way that's rare for a neighborhood bar, and it sets the tone: this is a place designed to be looked at as much as sat in.

## Getting there

Bina Bar sits in Kadıköy, the district on Istanbul's Asian shore that's spent the last decade turning into the city's most reliably interesting nightlife and food quarter. If you're coming from the European side, the classic approach is the ferry — the short crossing from Eminönü, Karaköy, or Beşiktaş to Kadıköy pier is cheap, scenic, and honestly part of the experience, especially at dusk when the water turns orange and the Bosphorus traffic slows. From the ferry terminal, the bar bars and meyhanes of Kadıköy's backstreets — around Moda, Yasa Caddesi, and Bahariye — are all a walkable 10–15 minutes away. If you're coming by metro, the M4 line connects to Kadıköy directly, putting you within easy walking distance of the neighborhood's bar streets. Because exact street addresses and walking routes for newer venues can shift as areas develop, it's worth pulling up the pin on a map app before you set out rather than relying on memory of "somewhere near Moda."

## What to expect once inside

Three floors gives Bina Bar room to do different things at different heights — ground-level energy near the entrance and garden, then progressively different moods as you go up, which is typical of Kadıköy's converted-building bars where the ground floor handles the crowd and the upper floors reward people willing to climb for a quieter corner or a better view. Expect the kind of drinks list and crowd you'd find at Kadıköy's other buzzed-about spots: cocktails, local beer, a soundtrack that shifts as the night goes on. Because this is a recently-opened, fast-trending place, treat any specific menu or price information you see online as provisional — confirm directly with the venue or a current listing before you go, rather than planning around details that may already be out of date.

## When to go

Kadıköy's bar scene runs late, and a dramatic, photogenic entrance like Bina Bar's tends to pull weekend crowds fast once word spreads on social media. If you want to actually appreciate the garden and pool without jostling for a photo, an early evening arrival — before the after-dinner crush — is your best bet. Weeknights will generally be calmer than Friday or Saturday, when a talked-about new opening like this one is likely to fill up quickly and stay full.

## How to visit like a local

Kadıköy regulars treat bar-hopping as the whole point of the night, not a single stop — so don't feel obligated to camp at Bina Bar all evening; wander the surrounding streets before or after. Payment across Istanbul's newer bars is overwhelmingly by card, though it's still smart to carry some cash in case of a system hiccup. Tipping isn't obligatory but rounding up or leaving small change is common and appreciated. If the place is trending as hard as its reputation suggests, don't assume you can walk in and get a table on a weekend night — a quick call or check of their social media for reservation policy beforehand saves you a wasted trip. And resist the urge to just photograph the lion-and-pool entrance and leave — the different floors are part of what makes the place worth the visit, so give yourself time to actually explore upward.

## Nearby, while you're in the area

Kadıköy rewards lingering: the Tuesday and Friday Salı Pazarı market, the seaside promenade toward Moda with its teahouses and sunset views, and the dense grid of meyhanes around Kadıfe Sokak (nicknamed "Barlar Sokağı," or Bar Street) are all within easy reach on foot, so it's easy to fold Bina Bar into a longer evening rather than treating it as a standalone destination.
