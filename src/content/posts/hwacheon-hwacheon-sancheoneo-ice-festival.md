## A town that fishes through the floor

In January, the Hwacheon River becomes the festival venue. Thousands of holes are drilled in tidy grid rows across the frozen stream in Hwacheon-eup, a small county seat in northern Gangwon Province, close to the DMZ. The 2027 edition runs from January 9 to 31.

Sancheoneo is the Korean name for mountain trout, and the river is stocked with them beneath the ice. Anglers sit on low stools beside holes about the width of a dinner plate. They jig short rods with small lures and watch the dark water for a flash of silver.

Overnight temperatures here regularly drop below -10°C. In warm winters the opening has been pushed back in the past because the ice was too thin to hold crowds.

## On the ice and in town

The ice fishing grounds are the main event. Around them, the programme usually includes:

- **Barehanded trout catch:** participants in festival T-shirts and shorts wade into a pool of near-freezing water and grab trout by hand, often stuffing them down their shirts.
- **Grill zones:** caught fish are gutted, salted, wrapped in foil and roasted over charcoal, or sliced raw at sashimi stalls.
- **Ice and snow play:** long sled runs, ice bobsleds and skating on cleared sections of the river.
- **Sancheoneo Lantern Street:** thousands of fish-shaped lanterns are strung above the main street of town and lit after dark.
- **Ice sculpture plaza:** a large indoor hall of carved ice, a warmer break from the riverbank.

A day here typically covers two or three hours of fishing, a grilled-trout lunch and a walk under the lanterns.

## Getting up to Hwacheon

Intercity buses run from Dong Seoul Bus Terminal, beside Gangbyeon Station on Seoul Subway Line 2, to Hwacheon Intercity Bus Terminal. The ride takes roughly two and a half to three hours. The festival grounds are a short walk from the terminal.

The other route goes through Chuncheon. The ITX-Cheongchun train runs from Yongsan and Cheongnyangni in Seoul to Chuncheon Station. From there, local buses and taxis cover the remaining 40-odd minutes north. Hwacheon has limited lodging, so many visitors sleep in Chuncheon and treat the festival as a day trip. Seoul tour operators also run packaged day trips with transport and fishing entry bundled together.

## Lures, hand warmers and the voucher trick

The festival's official site carries the ticket options and fishing-zone details, and on-site booths sell entry at the grounds. Part of a fishing ticket usually comes back as a Hwacheon local gift voucher. Regulars spend it at the market stalls and restaurants in town rather than leaving with it unused. Current prices are posted on the official site and at the ticket booths.

Korean families come equipped:

- folding stools and foam pads to sit on the ice
- disposable hand warmers (*hatpaek*), sold in every convenience store in town
- spare gloves, because wet ones freeze fast
- a thermos of something hot

Rods and lures are sold right by the river, so few people bring their own. Patience matters more than gear. Experienced anglers move the lure in slow, small twitches and leave the hole alone rather than stirring it. Weekdays tend to be less packed than weekends, when Seoul day-trippers and tour buses arrive in force.
