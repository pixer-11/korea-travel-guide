## Curves of stone on the Quebec shore

From the lookouts behind Parliament Hill, the museum looks like a riverbank shaped by wind. Douglas Cardinal designed its long, rounded walls of pale limestone to suggest land carved by glaciers and water. Not one hard corner faces the river.

The building opened in 1989 as the Canadian Museum of Civilization and took its current name in 2013. It is one of the most-visited museums in the country. Expect company, especially in summer and on school-trip weekdays.

## The Grand Hall and what follows

Start with the Grand Hall. It is a long, curving gallery with a glass wall several storeys high on one side. On the other stands a row of towering Pacific Northwest Coast totem poles in front of reconstructed house fronts. Through the glass, the Parliament buildings sit framed across the water.

It is the largest indoor display of totem poles in the world, and the scale only lands once you are standing at the base of one. Give it at least half an hour before moving on.

The other main galleries:

- **Canadian History Hall:** opened in 2017, it runs chronologically from the earliest Indigenous presence to the present across three linked galleries.
- **First Peoples Hall:** Indigenous histories, art and objects from across the country, organised around living cultures rather than artefacts alone.
- **Canadian Children's Museum:** a separate hands-on wing inside the same building, aimed at younger kids.
- **Theatre:** the large-format screen listed with the museum; check the official site for what is currently showing.

## Walking over from Ottawa

The best approach is on foot. From the National Gallery of Canada or Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica, cross the Alexandra Bridge. Its walkway gives open views upriver to Parliament Hill and the Library of Parliament. From Parliament itself the walk takes roughly 20 minutes.

Crossing the bridge puts you in Quebec, though the time zone does not change. STO buses run from downtown Ottawa into Gatineau if you would rather ride. Drivers will find underground parking beneath the museum, with rates posted at the entrance.

## Planning your hours inside

At the moment the museum opens at 9am every day and closes at 5pm, except Thursday, when it stays open until 7pm. Museum schedules shift with the seasons and around holidays. Confirm this month's hours on the official site before you set out.

Three to four hours covers the major halls without rushing. Families heading for the Children's Museum should budget extra, because younger visitors rarely want to leave. Ticket prices are listed on the official website and at the admissions desk.

## Getting through the galleries like a regular

A few habits make the day smoother:

- **Book online.** Timed tickets bought ahead let you skip the admissions line, which is the main bottleneck on busy days.
- **Go bilingual.** Labels are in French and English. Staff greet you with "Bonjour, hello" and switch to whichever language you answer in.
- **Do the Grand Hall first.** Arriving right at opening usually puts you ahead of school groups and tour buses, so you get the totem poles with fewer people in your photos.
- **Use the Thursday evening.** The later close leaves time for a full visit after a day of sightseeing in Ottawa.
- **Check the coat room.** Ottawa winters mean heavy coats, and the galleries are warm.

## Before or after, along the river

The museum grounds slope down to the Ottawa River, and paths along the water give the classic postcard view of Parliament Hill. Back on the Ottawa side, the National Gallery of Canada, the ByWard Market and the Rideau Canal locks are all within walking distance of the bridge. That makes it easy to pair this museum with a full day downtown.
