## A hotel built for one view

Every window worth having points west. The Chateau stands at the end of Lake Louise Drive, roughly 4 km uphill from Lake Louise village. Its long façade faces the turquoise lake, with Mount Victoria and Victoria Glacier closing off the far end of the valley.

Canadian Pacific Railway opened a wooden chalet here in 1890 to fill its trains. Most of what you see now went up between 1913 and the mid-1920s, after fire destroyed the old timber wing in 1924. It is a big, heavily visited place. Treat it as a landmark with a lobby, not a quiet retreat.

The editorial line covers the facilities: several restaurants, an indoor pool, a spa and outdoor activities run from the property.

## Getting up the hill

The drive takes about 45 minutes from Banff and around two hours from Calgary, both via the Trans-Canada Highway (Highway 1). You need a Parks Canada pass to stop anywhere in the park.

- **Hotel guests:** valet parking at the front entrance.
- **Day visitors:** the paid Parks Canada lakeshore lot beside the hotel, which fills early on summer days.
- **Shuttle:** the Parks Canada shuttle runs from the Park and Ride at Lake Louise Ski Resort. Book seats ahead in peak season.
- **Moraine Lake:** about 14 km away. Its road has been closed to private cars since 2023, so get there by shuttle or a licensed bus.

## Walking out from the front door

The trails start at the hotel steps. The flat **Lakeshore Trail** runs along the north shore to the far end of the lake and back, and it suits anyone.

- **Lake Agnes Tea House:** a steady climb of about 3.5 km one way to a log teahouse above Mirror Lake.
- **Plain of Six Glaciers:** follows the lakeshore and keeps going toward the glacier, with a second teahouse near the top.
- **Canoes:** rented from the boathouse once the ice goes out, usually in early June. Lines are normal here.

From roughly November to May the lake is frozen. The hotel clears a skating rink on the ice, and the Lake Louise Ski Resort sits across the valley. Snowshoeing the shoreline is the quietest way to see the glacier.

## Doing the Chateau without staying

Non-guests are welcome in the public areas. A few habits make it smoother:

- **Book afternoon tea.** Tea in the Lakeview Lounge, where the windows face the glacier, books up, so reserve ahead instead of hoping for a walk-in seat.
- **Book dinner too.** The Walliser Stube serves Swiss-style fondue in a wood-panelled room and needs a reservation in high season.
- **Pay by card.** Cards are standard everywhere, and tipping runs at the usual Canadian 15–20% in the restaurants.
- **Keep to the public areas.** Guest floors and the pool are for residents only. Lounges, shops and the lakefront are open to all.
- **Go early or late.** Tour coaches and shuttles arrive through the middle of the day. If you are up at first light or still there in the evening, the shoreline in front of the hotel is calmer and the lake is often still enough to reflect the glacier.

Bring a warm layer even in July. The hotel sits at about 1,750 m, and the air coming off the glacier keeps the shore cool.
