Two days, open pasture, and the cool season's first cold nights. Big Mountain Music Festival 2026 takes place on **December 12-13, 2026** at **Chokchai Farm 3** in Nakhon Ratchasima province. Thai music fans know it simply as "Big Mountain," and it is one of the largest outdoor music festivals the country holds each year.

The setting is ranch country. Chokchai Farm is a long-established dairy and cattle operation on the Khao Yai side of the province. For one weekend its grassland becomes a temporary village of stages, food stalls and tents.

## What the weekend looks like

It is a festival you walk, not one you stand still at. Big Mountain has always been built around several stages spread across open ground. Fans move between them all day, so walking from one set to the next is part of the experience.

The music leans heavily Thai: rock, pop, indie, luk thung crossovers and the bands a whole generation grew up singing along to. If you don't speak Thai, expect to be surrounded by thousands of people who know every word. That mass sing-along is the reason many people come back year after year.

What you'll typically find on site:

- Several stages playing at the same time, from big main-stage acts to smaller tents
- Long rows of food and drink stalls selling Thai street-food staples
- Camping areas for people who stay through both days
- Open grass where groups spread mats between sets

The organiser publishes the lineup, stage map and set times through its official festival channels. This guide doesn't list artists or times, because none are given here.

## Getting to Chokchai Farm 3

The farm is in Pak Chong district. That is the gateway town for Khao Yai National Park, not Nakhon Ratchasima city (Korat) itself. Pak Chong is roughly a three-hour drive northeast of Bangkok along Highway 2, the Mittraphap Road. Korat city is further east, about an hour or more beyond.

Most festival-goers come from Bangkok, and they travel in one of these ways:

- **By car:** the most common choice, usually with a full car of friends and a shared drive up Highway 2.
- **By train:** the State Railway's Northeastern line stops at Pak Chong station, with a taxi or songthaew for the last stretch to the farm.
- **By van or bus:** minivans and buses run from Bangkok to Pak Chong, and local transport covers the final leg.

Traffic on Highway 2 is heavy on festival weekends. Many attendees build an extra hour or two into their drive, especially on the Saturday morning.

## The Khao Yai cold snap

December is the reason Big Mountain feels the way it does. It falls in Thailand's cool season, and the hills around Khao Yai sit higher than Bangkok. Days are warm and sunny on open pasture with very little shade. After dark the temperature drops sharply.

This is the festival's real timing quirk. People who arrive in shorts and sandals at midday often spend the late headline sets wrapped in a borrowed blanket. Regulars pack for two climates:

- A hat and sunscreen for the afternoon sun on the grass
- A hoodie, fleece or light jacket for the night
- A scarf or buff, which also helps against the dust
- Closed shoes that can handle grass, dirt and a lot of walking

Campers generally bring a proper sleeping bag rather than a thin beach blanket. The pre-dawn hours on the farm are the coldest part of the weekend.

## Sharing the grass: how Thai festival crowds work

Big Mountain crowds are friendly and organised in groups. Most people come with a gang of friends, pick a patch of grass near a favourite stage and lay down a mat as a base. Stepping around a mat is polite. Walking across it is not.

Cashless payment is everywhere in Thailand, and Thai QR payments through banking apps are the everyday way to pay at stalls. Visitors without a Thai bank account usually carry Thai baht in small notes as well. Tipping isn't expected at food stalls.

A few habits worth copying:

- Groups pick a fixed meeting point, such as a stage sign or a food stall, because phone signal can struggle when a big crowd is packed in one place.
- When a much-loved song starts, the whole field sings. Join in, even if you only know the chorus.
- Large crowds near the main stage stay calm and good-natured. People usually ask "khor thot" (excuse me) as they edge through.
- Fans often wear band shirts or matching group outfits, which also makes friends easier to spot.

## Where people stay

Pak Chong and the Khao Yai area have a big stock of resorts, homestays and hotels, because Bangkok weekenders fill them all year. During the festival, rooms close to the farm are in demand. Attendees who don't camp often stay further along Highway 2 or on the roads leading toward the national park, then drive or take a taxi in.

Camping on site is the other classic choice. It saves the late-night journey after the final set. It also means sleeping through the coldest hours outdoors, so a proper sleeping bag matters.

## Making a trip of it

Many visitors add a day or two either side of the festival weekend. **Khao Yai National Park**, a UNESCO-listed forest, is close by. It is known for waterfalls such as Haew Suwat and for spotting wild elephants, gibbons and hornbills on its trails and roads.

The Pak Chong area also has vineyards, cafés and farm-stay attractions aimed at Bangkok day-trippers. Chokchai Farm is a well-known stop for its dairy products and ranch-style tours. Further east, Korat city has the Thao Suranari Monument and access to the Khmer temple ruins at **Phimai Historical Park**.
