## A gallery inside a university

It's a working campus gallery, not a tourist hall. Lawrence Wilson Art Gallery sits on the University of Western Australia's Crawley campus, off Stirling Highway. Students cut across the lawns outside, and the sandstone of Winthrop Hall and the Reid Library is a few minutes' walk away.

Inside, the program draws on the UWA Art Collection, one of the state's major holdings of Western Australian and Australian art. The gallery also cares for the Cruthers Collection of Women's Art, a large body of work by Australian women artists. Exhibitions rotate, so what's on the walls changes between visits.

It's a quiet place. A good number of Perth visitors never realise it's there, and you can usually look at the work without anyone in front of you.

## Reaching Crawley

Crawley is around 5km west of the city centre, between Kings Park and the Swan River.

- **Bus:** Transperth's high-frequency 950 runs along this corridor to UWA. Several other routes from the CBD also stop on Stirling Highway or Mounts Bay Road by the campus. SmartRider cards and contactless bank cards work on the readers.
- **Car:** campus parking is ticketed and enforced. Read the signs at the bay before you leave the car.
- **On foot or bike:** the riverside path from the city along Mounts Bay Road is flat and follows the water the whole way.

Once you're on campus, follow the gallery signage or ask at any building reception. The grounds are big, and the gallery isn't obvious from the highway.

## Timing a visit to the UWA gallery

The hours are short, so plan around them. At the moment the gallery opens 11am–4pm, Tuesday to Saturday, and is closed Sundays and Mondays. Weekend plans therefore have to use Saturday.

University galleries often change their hours between exhibitions and over the summer and midyear breaks. Check the official UWA gallery site for this month's times before you make the trip.

- **Arrive by about 2:30pm** so you have a full hour before the 4pm close.
- **Look for public programs.** The gallery runs talks and openings tied to its exhibitions, and the website lists them.
- **Ask before photographing.** Rules on photos vary by exhibition, and lent works often carry restrictions.
- **Bags:** large backpacks may need to stay at the front desk, as in most galleries.

The front-of-house staff are often university people, and many know the collection well. Asking them which piece to see first is normal, and the answers are usually good.

## Matilda Bay and the rest of the afternoon

Pair the gallery with the campus grounds and the foreshore below them. Before you leave UWA, walk through the Sunken Garden and past the Winthrop Hall arcades. Both are popular backdrops for photos and weddings.

Then head downhill to Matilda Bay Reserve on the Swan River:

- **Crawley Edge Boatshed:** the small blue boatshed standing in the water, one of Perth's most photographed spots.
- **Matilda Bay foreshore:** shaded lawns and river views east to the city skyline.
- **Kings Park:** the park's western edge is a short walk or ride uphill from campus. It has bushland trails and the Western Australian Botanic Garden.

A sensible half day runs like this: gallery from opening at 11am, lunch around campus or the bay, then the boatshed and the foreshore in the afternoon light. It works on any weekday from Tuesday or on Saturday, the days the doors are currently open.
