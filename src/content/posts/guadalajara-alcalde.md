## A star for Jalisco

Alcalde is a Michelin-starred restaurant in Guadalajara. The star came in 2026, the year the guide expanded into Jalisco, and it put the city on the fine-dining map alongside Mexico City and Oaxaca.

The kitchen works from regional Mexican ingredients. That means the cooking points at Jalisco's own produce and traditions, not imported luxury. For a visitor, Alcalde gives a sit-down answer to a question Guadalajara usually answers at street level: what does this state's food taste like when a kitchen slows down and refines it?

## What to expect at the table

Treat it as a planned evening, not a walk-in meal. A Michelin star means demand, and a newly awarded one usually means more demand than before.

We haven't been given a current menu or format, so we won't describe dishes. Before you book, check these with the restaurant:

- Whether it serves à la carte, a tasting menu, or both
- Current opening days and service times
- How dietary restrictions are handled, and how much notice they need
- Whether there is a dress expectation

The context helps you read the menu. Jalisco is the home of tequila and the blue agave country around it. It also gave the country birria and the torta ahogada. Expect a kitchen in this state to talk about its sources.

## Booking, paying and tipping

Reserve first. The surest route is the restaurant's own website or social channels. Confirm the date, the party size and any deposit policy in writing.

Some habits for fine dining in Guadalajara:

- **Language:** staff at restaurants at this level usually handle English. A few words of Spanish ("una reservación para dos", "la cuenta, por favor") still go down well.
- **Paying:** cards are standard at upscale restaurants in Mexico. Carry a little cash in pesos for tips, valets or drivers anyway.
- **Tipping:** 10 to 15 percent is customary in Mexico, and more for exceptional service. Check the bill, because some places add a service charge.
- **The bill:** it won't arrive until you ask. Waiters consider it rude to rush a table.
- **Pacing:** dinner in Mexico runs late, and a long, unhurried meal is normal.

## Getting there and making a night of it

Use Uber, DiDi or a licensed taxi rather than driving. Parking in central Guadalajara is tight, and a ride lets you drink tequila with dinner. From Guadalajara International Airport (GDL), the trip into the city usually takes 30 to 45 minutes, depending on traffic.

Confirm the exact street address when you book. Then build the rest of the evening around it. Colonia Americana and Avenida Chapultepec, west of the historic centre, are packed with mezcal and cocktail bars for a drink before or after dinner.

Coming for a weekend? Pair Alcalde with a daytime trip to the town of Tequila, a little over an hour away by road. You'll see the agave fields there, then taste what the region does with them when you're back in the city.
