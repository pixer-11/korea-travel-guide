## Boardwalk, river, masts

The Wharf faces the river, not the surf. It runs along the Mooloolah River just upstream of the river mouth. Restaurant decks look over the marina's hulls and rigging instead of breaking waves, and the water slaps quietly against the pylons.

It's one of the Sunshine Coast's best-known waterfront spots and gets a steady stream of visitors, so don't expect it to yourself. What you get is a compact strip where dinner, an aquarium and a boat trip all sit within a few minutes' walk.

## What sits along the water

The anchor is **SEA LIFE Sunshine Coast**, the aquarium that older visitors still remember as UnderWater World. Around it the precinct is mostly about eating and drinking, with decks and outdoor tables facing the marina.

What you'll find here:

- **SEA LIFE Sunshine Coast**: walk-through tunnels and seal presentations. Allow about two hours.
- **Waterfront dining**: casual cafés through to sit-down restaurants, many with river views.
- **Cruise and whale-watching departures**: operators run trips from the Mooloolaba harbour area. Humpback season runs roughly June to November.
- **The marina itself**: free to walk along, and the cheapest show in town.

Tenancies change over time. Check the precinct's official site for the current list of restaurants before you set your heart on one.

## Getting to Parkyn Parade

Most visitors drive. Mooloolaba is roughly 90 minutes north of Brisbane via the Bruce Highway and the Sunshine Motorway. Sunshine Coast Airport at Marcoola is about a 25-minute drive away.

No train comes into Mooloolaba. Translink buses do serve the town, so use the Translink journey planner for current routes. The precinct has its own car parking off Parkyn Parade. The rules and charges are posted on signs at the entrance, so read them before you walk off.

From the Mooloolaba Esplanade and the main surf beach, it's an easy flat walk of around 20 minutes south-west along Parkyn Parade. On the way you pass the seafood shops that have long supplied the town's prawn and fish trade.

## Reading the day at The Wharf

The precinct's listed hours currently run 6:30am to 11pm daily, but that's the outer limit. Individual cafés, restaurants and SEA LIFE each set their own times. Check the venue you want, not just the precinct.

Early morning suits a coffee and a slow walk past the boats. Fishing and charter boats often head out around then too. Midday brings families heading to the aquarium, especially in Queensland school holidays.

Late afternoon is when the decks earn their reputation. The light goes soft over the marina and dinner bookings start to fill.

## Booking, paying and finding a table

Behave here the way you would anywhere on the Queensland coast. The specifics:

- **Pay by tap.** Contactless card and phone payments are standard. Some places add a surcharge on public holidays or for card payments, and when they do it's printed on the menu.
- **Tipping is optional.** Wages already cover service in Australia. Rounding up or leaving a little for great service is welcome but never expected.
- **Book dinner on weekends.** Waterfront tables at sunset go first. Booking ahead online is normal practice, not fussy.
- **Buy SEA LIFE tickets online.** Attractions in this chain usually sell timed entry on their website, which saves a wait at the desk in holiday periods.
- **Order at the counter** in casual cafés. Many run order-and-pay at the till, with a number to take to your table.

If you only want the view, buy a drink or an ice cream and walk the marina edge. You don't have to commit to a full meal to enjoy the water.

## Making a day of Mooloolaba

The Wharf works best as one stop on a loop rather than a day on its own. These are all within a short drive or walk:

- **Mooloolaba Beach**: the patrolled main beach, with the Esplanade's cafés behind it.
- **The Spit and the river mouth**: calm water, families swimming and boats coming through the harbour entrance.
- **Point Cartwright**: the headland and lighthouse across the river, with lookouts toward the coast.
- **Alexandra Headland**: the next beach north, linked by the coastal path.

A sensible order is a morning swim at Mooloolaba Beach, the aquarium over the middle of the day, then a walk back to The Wharf for an early dinner on the water.
