## A quieter rock above the valley

This lookout gets far less traffic than the Malahat's better-known stops. It doesn't come up in the usual Victoria itineraries, but the people who do find it rate it consistently well. That makes it a good half-day escape from the city.

The name tells you what you're walking towards: an exposed rock with a viewpoint. Plan on bare stone underfoot, open sky, and a view across the forested Cowichan Valley country on southern Vancouver Island.

## Driving up from Victoria

Your route north is Highway 1, the Trans-Canada.

- **Route:** leave Victoria on Highway 1 and climb the Malahat, the forested ridge between Greater Victoria and the Cowichan Valley.
- **Address to enter:** Cowichan Valley, BC V8H 1B7. Put the name "Split Rock Lookout" into your map app as well, because a rural postcode alone can land you in the wrong spot.
- **Transport:** a car is the practical option. Don't count on a bus dropping you near a viewpoint like this.
- **Signal:** download offline maps before the climb, since coverage can drop in the hills.

The Malahat stretch has steep grades and sharp curves. Use the official pull-outs only, and don't brake hard for a view from the highway itself.

## Picking your light and your weather

Google currently shows the lookout as open 24 hours every day. In practice, daylight is your real schedule. There is no point being on an exposed rock after dark.

- **Clear days** give you the long view. Coastal fog and low cloud can sit on the ridge and hide everything below.
- **Wet rock** gets slippery, especially from November to March, when the island gets most of its rain.
- **Summer evenings** give you long, late light. Pack a layer anyway, because exposed ground cools fast once the sun drops.

Allow 1–2 hours at the lookout itself, plus the drive each way. You can pair it with other Cowichan Valley stops to make a full day.

## Keeping the rock the way you found it

This is a low-key spot, and how you behave there decides whether it stays that way. There's no gate, no attendant and no kiosk. Bring everything you need and carry all of it back out.

- **Footwear:** wear shoes with real grip. Smooth rock, moss and loose gravel are a bad mix in trainers.
- **Edges:** stay well back from drop-offs, and keep children and dogs close.
- **Plants:** stick to bare rock and worn paths. The thin soil and mosses on Vancouver Island outcrops recover slowly once they're trampled.
- **Fire:** BC often brings in wildfire restrictions in summer. Check the current BC Wildfire Service notices before you carry a stove or anything that burns.
- **Parking:** park so you don't block anyone else's access, and leave nothing visible in the car.
- **Noise:** keep it down. Part of the appeal is how quiet it is.

If a parking area is full when you arrive, come back another day rather than squeezing in along the road shoulder.
