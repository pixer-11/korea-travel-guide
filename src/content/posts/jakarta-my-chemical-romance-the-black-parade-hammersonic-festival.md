## A hospital-ward album in an 80,000-seat bowl

The show was billed as The Black Parade from start to finish. That meant a single flatline tone, then "The End.", then the marching drum that opens "Welcome to the Black Parade", through to "Famous Last Words" and the hidden "Blood". The setting was Jakarta International Stadium, a steel-and-glass bowl built for tens of thousands.

The show was set for November 22, 2026. It was announced as the closing night of the band's Southeast Asia run, the kind of finale that draws travelling fans from Singapore, Manila and Bangkok. The show sat under the Hammersonic banner. Hammersonic is Indonesia's long-running heavy music festival, and its crowd knows how to hold a pit for hours.

## Where the stadium actually is

Jakarta International Stadium (JIS) is in Papanggo, Tanjung Priok, in North Jakarta. That puts it near the port and well north of the Sudirman and Thamrin hotel strip. It opened in 2022 as the home ground of Persija Jakarta. Its retractable roof and stacked tiers are visible from the toll roads that loop past it.

The distance matters more than the map suggests. From central Jakarta, the trip north can be quick on a clear evening and painfully slow at rush hour. A wide time buffer is the norm for evening events here.

- **Ride-hailing:** Grab and Gojek both serve the stadium. Gojek's motorbike option (GoRide) cuts through traffic much faster than a car.
- **TransJakarta:** the city bus network has run services to JIS on event days, with routes shown in the TransJakarta app.
- **Staying nearby:** hotels around Kelapa Gading or Ancol are much closer than a base in South Jakarta.

## Tickets and the paperwork around them

Tickets were sold through the official ticketing partner linked from the Hammersonic and My Chemical Romance sites, in categories with seating maps. Resale screenshots posted on social media are a common scam around big Jakarta concerts.

Indonesian promoters often use named e-tickets. Some ask fans to swap them for a wristband, and some check ID at entry. Where the name has to match, the passport or KTP the ticket was booked under is what gets checked.

## Getting through the gates on the night

Most of a stadium evening comes down to handling the crowd and the heat. These are the habits experienced Jakarta concertgoers stick to.

- **Cashless payment.** QRIS, Indonesia's standard QR payment, is everywhere, and most venue vendors take it along with e-wallets like GoPay and OVO. Regulars load their apps before arriving.
- **Bag rules.** Stadium shows usually limit bag size and ban outside food, drinks and umbrellas. A small clear bag, or none at all, gets through security faster.
- **The exit.** After stadium shows, ride-hailing pickup outside JIS turns into gridlock. Walking ten or fifteen minutes away and setting the pin on a named street is the usual workaround.
- **Meeting points.** Mobile data can slow down when a full stadium is on one cell network, so groups agree on a landmark in advance.

A few words of Bahasa Indonesia go a long way with staff and drivers. *Terima kasih* means thank you, and *permisi* means excuse me when squeezing through a row.

## Dressing for a November night in Jakarta

November falls in Jakarta's rainy season. Heavy downpours in the afternoon and evening are normal, and the humidity stays high after dark. The roof is retractable, but it isn't always closed. Packable ponchos are the usual answer where umbrellas are banned.

The Black Parade era is known for marching-band jackets and white face paint. Heavy costume layers in tropical humidity call for water breaks. Comfortable closed shoes suit a crowded stadium floor.

## Turning the trip into more than one night

North Jakarta has enough nearby to fill a day around a show. Ancol Dreamland is on the waterfront to the west. Just past it is Kota Tua, the old Dutch colonial quarter around Fatahillah Square, home to the Jakarta History Museum. Further west, the old Sunda Kelapa harbour still has wooden pinisi schooners tied up along the quay.

Kelapa Gading, east of the stadium, is a dining district known for its malls and Chinese-Indonesian restaurants. It works well for a late meal while the traffic around JIS clears. That traffic typically takes an hour or more to ease after a stadium show.
