## A market built for cold nights

Da Lat Market is the town's commercial centre. The main concrete hall dates from the early 1960s. It stands at the foot of Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, where the road drops toward Xuan Huong Lake, with Hoa Binh Square and its old cinema just uphill.

This is one of Da Lat's best-known stops, so don't expect it to yourself. Highland produce is what makes it different from lowland Vietnamese markets. At 1,500 metres, Da Lat grows strawberries, artichokes, avocados and cut flowers, and all of them end up here.

## Working the floors

The ground floor is dense with dried and candied goods, stacked in open trays and sealed bags. Look for:

- **Mứt** (candied fruit): strawberry, persimmon, ginger, sweet potato
- **Trà atisô**: artichoke tea, a Da Lat speciality
- Dried persimmons, often sold in gift boxes
- Strawberry jam and mulberry syrup
- Coffee from the surrounding Lâm Đồng plantations

Fresh fruit, vegetables and flowers spill out around the edges of the building. The upper level has seated food stalls serving rice and noodle dishes, which is handy when the sun drops and the temperature goes with it. Allow about an hour for a proper loop, longer if you stop to eat.

## After dark on the steps

At night the market spreads into the surrounding streets. Vendors set up along the stairs and roads in front of the main hall, and tiny plastic stools appear on the pavement. The food to find:

- **Bánh tráng nướng**: rice paper grilled over charcoal with egg, spring onion and dried shrimp, often called "Da Lat pizza"
- **Sữa đậu nành**: hot soy milk, sometimes served alongside bánh bò or other small cakes
- Grilled skewers and sweet potatoes from charcoal braziers

Clothing stalls sell knitted hats, scarves and fleece jackets, because Da Lat nights run cool all year. If you packed for Saigon heat, this is the place to fix that.

## Bargaining and paying at Chợ Đà Lạt

A few habits make the visit smoother:

- **Cash rules.** Carry small Vietnamese đồng notes. Some stalls show a QR code for bank-app payment, but don't count on it.
- **Bargain on clothes and souvenirs, not food.** Knitwear and trinkets usually have room to negotiate. Snacks and drinks at night stalls are generally fixed.
- **Taste before buying dried fruit.** Vendors routinely offer samples. Ask for the per-kilo price and have a portion weighed rather than grabbing pre-packed bags.
- **Sit where you order.** At the night stalls, find a stool, point at what the neighbouring table has, and pay when you leave.
- **Watch your bag.** The steps get packed in the evening, so keep phones and wallets in front pockets.

Weekend evenings bring domestic tourists up from Ho Chi Minh City, so the street area fills up. A weekday evening, or a daytime visit to the indoor hall, gives you more room to browse. Hotels in central Da Lat are mostly within walking distance. From Lien Khuong Airport, about 30 km south, a taxi or airport shuttle drops you in the centre.
