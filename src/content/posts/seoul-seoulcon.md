## Four days on a silver curve

SEOULCon is a New Year's countdown and culture event. It takes place at Dongdaemun Design Plaza, the building everyone in Seoul calls DDP, from December 29, 2026 to January 1, 2027.

The setting does a lot of the work. DDP is Zaha Hadid's long, low swell of aluminium panels, with no straight facade and hardly a right angle anywhere. After dark its skin picks up whatever light is thrown at it, which is why a countdown here looks unlike one in a plaza ringed by office towers.

The event straddles the turn of the year. That means the last days of 2026, the countdown to midnight on December 31, and New Year's Day itself, all within one footprint in Jung-gu.

## The building you are walking through

DDP opened in 2014 on the site of the old Dongdaemun Stadium. It is less a single hall than a landscape. There are grassed roofs you can climb, sloping walkways, and interior ramps that spiral between levels instead of stairs.

Inside the grounds is Dongdaemun History & Culture Park. Here excavated stone foundations of Joseon-era structures sit in the open, including remains of the Igansumun, a water gate in the old Seoul City Wall. A couple of stadium floodlight towers were kept as relics. It is worth a slow lap between programme sessions, because most crowds walk straight past it.

The complex has several named zones, including the Art Hall and the Museum. Each SEOULCon session's location within DDP sits on the event's own schedule rather than anywhere general.

## Getting to DDP

The subway lands you right at the venue.

- **Station:** Dongdaemun History & Culture Park (동대문역사문화공원역), served by Lines 2, 4 and 5.
- **Exit 1** leads into DDP's lower level, so you stay underground almost to the door.
- **Dongdaemun Station** (Lines 1 and 4) is one stop north and suits anyone coming from the Heunginjimun Gate side.
- **From Myeongdong:** Line 4, two stops.
- **From Hongdae or Gangnam:** Line 2 runs straight through, no transfer.

Driving makes little sense on countdown night. Roads around Dongdaemun get heavy with taxis and buses, and the subway is quicker in every direction.

## Countdown night at Dongdaemun

The central night is December 31. Seoul in late December is properly cold: nights below freezing are normal, and wind channels along the open plazas around DDP.

People who stand outside for a countdown here usually dress for hours, not minutes. Expect thermal layers, a padded long coat (the knee-length "long padding" you'll see on half the city), gloves and a hat. Convenience stores nearby sell disposable hand warmers called *hatpaek* (핫팩), and plenty of attendees slip a pair into each pocket.

Seoul has a second, older midnight ritual a short ride away. At Bosingak Pavilion in Jongno, the city bell is rung 33 times to welcome the new year, and that crowd is enormous. SEOULCon at DDP is a different, design-district version of the same night. Most people pick one or the other for midnight, not both.

## Moving through the crowds after midnight

The hard part of a Seoul countdown is going home. A few habits make it smoother.

- **Pay with T-money.** A rechargeable transport card (티머니), sold and topped up at convenience stores, avoids ticket-machine queues. Many foreign credit cards also tap through gates directly.
- **Taxi apps beat hailing.** Kakao T is the standard app. Right after midnight, though, demand around Dongdaemun swamps supply, and many people walk a few blocks away and request a car from there.
- **Know the Owl buses.** Seoul's late-night buses carry route numbers starting with "N" and run after the subway stops.
- **Extended subway service:** in past years the city has kept trains running later on New Year's Eve, but that is decided year by year.

Korean crowds queue in tidy lines at station gates and escalators. Pushing forward is noticed. Standing on the right of escalators is common, though the city officially asks riders to stand on both sides.

## Dongdaemun's after-hours shopping streets

DDP sits inside one of the few districts in Seoul built to stay awake. The fashion malls around it, such as Doota and the wholesale buildings across Cheonggyecheon-ro, keep famously late hours. Wholesale floors trade through the night for retail buyers. Hours over the New Year holiday can differ from the usual pattern, so treat any one store as a maybe.

On the Dongdaemun Station side, the alleys off Heunginjimun hold a cluster of Central Asian and Russian restaurants and shops. Signs there are in Cyrillic as often as Hangul. It is a useful place for a hot meal that isn't fried chicken.

## Around DDP between sessions

A multi-day event leaves daylight to fill, and the neighbourhood rewards walking.

- **Heunginjimun Gate (Dongdaemun):** the great eastern gate of the old city, about ten minutes' walk north.
- **Seoul City Wall, Naksan section:** the wall climbs from the gate up Naksan, with city views from Naksan Park after a steady uphill walk.
- **Ihwa Mural Village:** the hillside lanes just below Naksan.
- **Gwangjang Market:** one subway stop west on Line 1 to Jongno 5-ga, or a walk along the Cheonggyecheon stream. Go for *bindaetteok* (mung bean pancakes) and *mayak gimbap* eaten at the counter stalls.
- **Cheonggyecheon:** the restored stream runs west from Dongdaemun toward City Hall, a flat walk below street level.

New Year's Day is a holiday in Korea. Some smaller restaurants close or shorten hours, while markets and chain cafés generally stay busier.
