## A temple on a suburban road

Balmoral Road is an ordinary Auckland street. Houses and traffic run along it between Dominion Road and Sandringham Road, and then you reach a Hindu temple at number 254. Bharatiya Mandir is a place of daily worship first. Visitors are welcome, but it doesn't run as a tourist site.

It's also a quieter find than Auckland's big sights. It is well known within the city's Hindu community and consistently well rated by visitors, yet it rarely shows up on sightseeing lists. You come here to watch a living religious tradition up close. Don't expect a museum.

## Timing your visit around the hours

The schedule has a gap, so check it first. Currently the temple keeps these hours:

- **Monday–Friday:** 8am–1pm, then 4pm–8:30pm. The doors are shut from 1pm to 4pm.
- **Saturday–Sunday:** 8am–8:30pm, with no midday break.

On a weekday, a visitor turning up at 2pm finds the doors shut. If your only free slot is mid-afternoon, go at the weekend. Hours can change for festivals and holy days, so check before making a special trip.

Evening is the more atmospheric time. That's when families drop in after work and the evening prayers take place. Mornings tend to be quieter and more contemplative. Major festivals draw much larger crowds, especially Diwali, Navratri and Janmashtami. Go then if you want to see celebrations, but avoid those dates if you want calm.

Plan on 30–45 minutes. Stay longer if you sit through a prayer service.

## Inside without causing offence

The etiquette is the same as at most Hindu temples, and following it matters more than anything else on this page:

- **Shoes off** before you enter the prayer hall. Leave them where the other shoes are.
- **Cover shoulders and knees.** Loose, modest clothing is the safe choice for everyone.
- **Keep your voice low** and switch your phone to silent.
- **Ask before photographing** anything inside, and never photograph people praying or the deities without clear permission.
- **Don't point your feet** at the shrines when you sit, and don't step over anyone sitting on the floor.
- **Accept prasad (blessed food) with your right hand** if it is offered, and eat it rather than throwing it away.

Donations are voluntary. If you want to leave one, use the donation box rather than handing money to anyone. Not sure where to stand or how close you can go? Do what the worshippers around you do, or quietly ask someone. Regulars at a community temple are usually happy to explain.

## Getting to Sandringham and what's nearby

The temple sits in Sandringham, a central suburb a short drive southwest of the city centre. Sandringham Road and Dominion Road both carry frequent buses from the central city. Either one puts you within walking distance on Balmoral Road. Check Auckland Transport's journey planner for current routes. If you're driving, the surrounding streets are residential, so park considerately and expect it to be tighter at festival times.

The neighbourhood suits a half-day out:

- **Sandringham Road shops:** this stretch is known for South Asian restaurants, sweet shops and grocers. It's the obvious stop for a meal after evening prayers.
- **Dominion Road:** to the east, it's one of Auckland's best-known strips for Chinese and other Asian food.
- **Maungawhau / Mount Eden:** the volcanic cone a short drive northeast has city-wide views.
- **Eden Park:** the national rugby stadium is close by. Expect heavy traffic on match days.

Pairing an evening temple visit with dinner on Sandringham Road makes a full evening in this part of the city.
