## One Sunday night in Kallang

RIIZE bring their 'RIIZING LOUD' world tour to Singapore on 24 January 2027. The show takes place at Singapore Indoor Stadium, the arena with the pointed roof at the edge of the Kallang Basin. It's the only Singapore date in the facts we have.

The SM Entertainment boy band debuted in September 2023 with "Get a Guitar". Their sound mixes guitar-led pop with sharp, heavy choreography, and their fans go by BRIIZE. Singapore has long been a regular stop for K-pop tours in Southeast Asia. A Sunday show means plenty of fans fly in from Malaysia and Indonesia for the weekend.

## Under the pyramid roof

You'll spot the venue from the expressway. Japanese architect Kenzo Tange designed it, and it opened in 1989. Its sloping, pagoda-like roof still makes it the easiest building to pick out in the Kallang skyline.

Inside, it's a fully enclosed, air-conditioned bowl. Seating tiers rise steeply on all sides, so even the upper rows feel fairly close to a stage at one end. Outside it's warm and humid, and inside the air conditioning runs cold. Concertgoers often carry a light layer for the hours in their seats.

The stadium is part of the Singapore Sports Hub. That puts it next to:

- the National Stadium, under its huge retractable dome
- the OCBC Aquatic Centre
- Kallang Wave Mall, which has food courts and restaurants under one roof
- the Kallang Basin waterfront, a flat promenade fans use as a meeting point

## Getting to Kallang by MRT

Most fans come by train. **Stadium MRT (CC6, Circle Line)** is the closest station, a few minutes' walk from the stadium doors. Signs point you to the Sports Hub as soon as you come up from the platform.

Two other stations work as alternatives:

- **Kallang MRT (EW10, East-West Line):** a longer walk over the river, handy if you're coming from Bugis, City Hall or the east.
- **Mountbatten MRT (CC7, Circle Line):** one stop further, and quieter when everyone leaves Stadium station at once.

Taxis and ride-hailing cars drop off around the Sports Hub. After a full-house show, pickup queues get long. That's why many fans walk to a station one stop away instead.

## Concert-night habits around the Sports Hub

K-pop nights at Kallang follow a familiar pattern. Fans tend to gather on the forecourt hours before doors. Groups trade handmade "freebies" like photocards, stickers and keychains, usually laid out on a small table or passed hand to hand. Taking something and offering a small item back is the polite thing to do.

Some things that help on the night:

- **Paying for the MRT:** tap a contactless bank card or phone at the gates. Visitors don't need a separate transit card.
- **Eating and drinking:** both are banned on MRT trains and inside stations, and the rule is enforced. Riders usually finish their bubble tea outside the fare gates.
- **Queueing:** Singapore queues are orderly and single-file. Merch, food stalls and the MRT platform after the show all work the same way.
- **Lightsticks:** most fans bring the official one, which links to the show's lighting. Everyone holds them up at shoulder height so the people behind can see.

January falls in the northeast monsoon, so short, heavy afternoon showers are common. Fans waiting outdoors usually carry a compact umbrella.

## Before doors and after the encore

Kallang Wave Mall is where most people eat before the show. It sits right beside the stadium, and its food court and casual chains fill up with fans in tour shirts on concert days.

If you'd like a proper meal, there are other options:

- **Old Airport Road Food Centre:** one of Singapore's best-known hawker centres, a short taxi ride east.
- **Geylang:** late-night eating lies just beyond Kallang. It's good for zi char and hot bowls after the encore.

Some fans stay nearby in Kallang or Lavender, a short ride from the Sports Hub. Others base themselves around Bugis and City Hall, which the Circle and East-West Lines connect back to the stadium.
