## Midnight over the Thames

The countdown happens on the water. Fireworks go up from the river around the London Eye, and Big Ben's chimes across from Westminster set the timing for midnight. The skyline behind does a lot of the work: the Eye's wheel, the Houses of Parliament and the bridges all sit inside the same frame.

For 2026 the event starts at 8:00pm on December 31 along the River Thames and South Bank. It carries on past midnight into January 1, 2027. Much of the evening is spent waiting in fenced riverside zones. Most people come for the display itself.

## Tickets and viewing zones

The riverside is not open standing. Since 2014 the official viewing areas along both banks have been ticketed. Without a ticket you cannot get into the enclosures closest to the display. The Mayor of London's office runs the event and publishes the dates, venue and ticket links on its official site.

What the zones usually look like:

- **South Bank**, around the London Eye and Jubilee Gardens, closest to the launch point
- **Victoria Embankment**, on the north bank, looking across at the Eye
- **Areas near Westminster and Waterloo bridges**, sightlines differ from zone to zone

Riverside restaurants, hotels and Thames cruise operators sell their own New Year's Eve packages, and these are separate from the Mayor's tickets. The BBC shows the display live on BBC One every year, so people without tickets often watch it on TV.

## Getting through the gates and home again

Each ticket is tied to one entry point. Visitors find that gate rather than heading for the river and hoping. Every entrance has security screening and bag searches, and the queues move slowly through the evening. Zones can close once they are full, which is why ticket holders tend to come in during the evening and not in the last minutes to midnight.

Getting around central London on the night:

- **Stations:** Westminster, Embankment, Waterloo and Charing Cross are the closest. On the night, some close or go exit-only to manage crowds.
- **Roads:** many streets around Whitehall and the South Bank close to traffic, so taxis and ride-hailing cars can't get anywhere near the river.
- **After midnight:** Transport for London usually runs extra services through the night. Crowds head for stations further from the river, such as Southwark or Piccadilly Circus, to skip the worst queues at the nearest ones.

Locals often walk 15–20 minutes away from the river before they try to get on a train.

## What the night is like

Expect hours of standing in the cold. There are no seats in the public zones, and late December on the Embankment is damp and windy. Attendees tend to dress in layers and come in having already eaten. Once inside, leaving and coming back in is rarely simple.

Count on about four to five hours, from the start of the evening to the end of the crowds leaving afterwards. The fireworks are the short part of the night. Getting to your spot and getting home afterwards take far longer.
