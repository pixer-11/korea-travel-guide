## A grass-walled pyramid on the Seine

Picture a pyramid with lawn on its sides. Accor Arena's sloping walls are planted with grass, and the steel-framed roof rises above the Right Bank in eastern Paris. This is the building where aespa brings the SYNK: COMPLÆXITY world tour in February 2027.

The SM Entertainment group has four members: Karina, Giselle, Winter and Ningning. They debuted in November 2020 with "Black Mamba" and built a catalogue that includes "Next Level", "Savage", "Supernova", "Armageddon" and "Whiplash". SYNK is the name of their concert series. Earlier legs ran as HYPER LINE and PARALLEL LINE.

## The arena and the night

The show is held at Accor Arena, 8 Boulevard de Bercy, in February 2027. It is one of the largest indoor concert venues in France. It opened in 1984 as the Palais Omnisports de Paris-Bercy, and Parisians still call it "Bercy". A major renovation in 2015 rebuilt the concourses and seating.

For concerts the room is usually split two ways:

- **Fosse**: the standing floor in front of the stage
- **Gradins**: numbered seats in the tiers that wrap around the bowl

Those two French words appear on Accor Arena tickets. Knowing them saves confusion at the gates. The arena's website is home to its event calendar and ticket sales for every show it hosts.

## Getting to Bercy

The Métro takes you almost to the door. **Bercy** station on Lines 6 and 14 opens onto the arena forecourt. Line 14 is driverless and fast. It runs through Châtelet, Madeleine and Saint-Lazare, and since its 2024 extension it reaches Orly airport too.

Other useful approaches:

- **Gare de Lyon** (RER A and D, Métro Lines 1 and 14): a walk of about 10–15 minutes east along the boulevard
- **Cour Saint-Émilion** (Line 14): one stop on, beside Bercy Village
- **Quai de la Gare** (Line 6): across the Seine, linked by the Passerelle Simone-de-Beauvoir footbridge

Parking near the arena fills fast on concert nights, so most fans come by public transport.

## How a Bercy show night works

On K-pop nights the forecourt fills with fans. Many carry the official aespa lightstick, and fans of the group call themselves MY. Merchandise stands draw long lines, and plenty of attendees treat buying merch as a separate trip before the show itself.

What attendees usually do:

- **Travel light.** Every entrance has a security check with bag inspection, and small bags get through quicker.
- **Carry phones charged.** Accor Arena tickets are commonly digital, scanned at the gate.
- **Learn the fanchants.** K-pop audiences shout set calls during songs, and Paris crowds join in loudly.
- **Expect fan projects.** Fan groups often hand out banners or slogans to hold up at a planned moment.

Fosse holders who want a spot near the barrier usually queue for hours. Seated ticket holders tend to turn up closer to doors, since their places are fixed. A little French goes a long way with staff: "Où est l'entrée ?" means "where is the entrance?"

## Around Bercy before and after

The arena backs onto **Parc de Bercy**, a long modern garden laid out on the site of the old wine warehouses. Its lawns and terraces run east toward **Bercy Village**. There, the stone wine storehouses (chais) on Cour Saint-Émilion now hold restaurants and shops, a handy pre-show dinner strip.

Also within a few minutes' walk:

- **La Cinémathèque française**, in a Frank Gehry building at the park's edge
- **Passerelle Simone-de-Beauvoir**, the curving footbridge to the Bibliothèque nationale (BnF) on the Left Bank
- **Ministère de l'Économie**, the huge finance ministry building jutting over the riverbank

After the encore, crowds pour into Bercy station at once. Many fans walk to Gare de Lyon or Cour Saint-Émilion to skip the crush. The Métro runs later on Friday and Saturday nights than during the week.

## Where fans usually stay

The 12th arrondissement puts the arena within walking distance. Hotels cluster around **Gare de Lyon**, which also handles TGV trains from Lyon, Marseille and the south. Visitors who prefer central Paris often stay along Line 14, since one ride connects Châtelet or Saint-Lazare to Bercy in minutes.

February in Paris is cold and often damp. That matters most to anyone spending hours in the outdoor queue. Fans who have done it before bring layers they can stuff into a small bag once inside.
