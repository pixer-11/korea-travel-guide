## A dance-heavy solo show on Mercedes-Platz

TAEMIN plays Berlin on November 18, 2026. He debuted with SHINee in 2008 and went solo in 2014 with "Danger". Since then his name has been tied to choreography-led stagecraft, on tracks like "Move", "Criminal" and "Guilty".

This is a solo concert, not a SHINee group show. Nothing in our facts names a setlist, support act or start time, so this guide doesn't guess at any of them.

## Inside Uber Eats Music Hall

The venue opened in 2018 as Verti Music Hall. It sits on Mercedes-Platz, right beside the much larger Uber Arena. Inside, a flat standing floor faces the stage and tiered seating rises behind and above it.

The room holds a few thousand people. That's small for a K-pop soloist of his profile. Even the back of the floor is close enough to make out the footwork, which counts for a lot at a TAEMIN show.

The plaza around the hall is a modern entertainment block, with restaurants, bars and a cinema all within a few steps of the doors. Concert crowds tend to fill those places in the hours before a show. The plaza works as the meeting point for groups who have travelled in from other countries.

## Getting to Friedrichshain

The simplest route is **Warschauer Straße**. Both the S-Bahn lines (S3, S5, S7, S75) and the U1 stop there, and the walk south to Mercedes-Platz takes under ten minutes.

- **Ostbahnhof** (S-Bahn, regional trains) is a slightly longer walk west along the river.
- **East Side Gallery**, the longest surviving stretch of the Berlin Wall, runs along Mühlenstraße between the venue and the Spree.
- **Oberbaum Bridge**, the red-brick double-deck bridge across to Kreuzberg, is a few minutes east of the hall.

Visitors coming in for the show usually stay in Friedrichshain or across the river in Kreuzberg. From both, walking or the U1 gets you home after the encore without a taxi. Mid-November evenings in Berlin are cold and dark early, so fans queuing outside wear proper winter layers.

## How the floor and the fandom work

At K-pop shows in Berlin, standing-floor ticket holders usually queue outside well before doors open. That's because floor spots go in order of arrival. Seated ticket holders tend to come later, since their place is already fixed.

- **Lightsticks:** fans who own SHINee's official lightstick usually bring it, and a sea of them is what you'll see from the tiers.
- **Fan projects:** international fan groups often hand out banners or slogans at the queue for a coordinated moment during the show. They usually announce these on fan social accounts.
- **Merch:** official merchandise sells at stands inside the venue. The longest lines tend to form right after doors open.
- **Bags and entry:** the venue publishes its bag-size and entry rules on its website. Berlin venues are generally strict about large backpacks.
- **Chanting:** the crowd typically sings and chants along in Korean during the familiar songs. Romanised fanchant guides circulate on fan accounts.

German venues use a cloakroom (*Garderobe*) system. In November most attendees use it rather than carrying a winter coat on a packed floor. After the show, the crowd mostly heads straight back to Warschauer Straße, so some fans wait out the first rush over a drink on Mercedes-Platz.
