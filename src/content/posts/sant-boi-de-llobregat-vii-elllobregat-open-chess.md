## Six days, one hall, 150 boards' worth of silence

The seventh edition runs from December 3 to 8, 2026. Around 150 players sit down in Sant Boi de Llobregat, a town in the Baix Llobregat district on the river's west bank, a short ride from central Barcelona.

The event is part of the **FIDE Circuit**, the ranking series where results at qualifying opens feed into a yearly standings table. That status matters to titled players chasing points. It also means every game is rated, so the field usually mixes grandmasters and masters with club players looking for a strong rated event.

The dates fall across Spain's December *puente*. Constitution Day (December 6) and the Immaculate Conception (December 8) are both national holidays. Amateurs who work weekdays often use that long weekend to play the full schedule.

## Reaching Sant Boi from Barcelona

Sant Boi is close enough that most visitors stay in the city and commute.

- **FGC train:** the Llobregat–Anoia line from Plaça Espanya stops at Sant Boi station, the most direct link to the town centre.
- **Metro L8:** this FGC-run line ends at Molí Nou–Ciutat Cooperativa, on Sant Boi's eastern side.
- **By air:** Barcelona–El Prat Airport sits a few kilometres south across the Llobregat delta, so the venue is easy to reach for players flying in.
- **By car:** the B-23 and C-32 motorways pass the town.

Players on a budget often book rooms in Sant Boi itself or in neighbouring Cornellà, skipping the daily trip in from Barcelona.

## Inside the playing hall

The organisers set the venue, the round times and the entry fees for players. This guide gives no figures for any of them. At FIDE-rated opens across Spain, spectators can usually watch the top boards from behind a rope or barrier. Live game boards often run on screens or online for those who cannot attend.

The FIDE Laws of Chess shape how the room works:

- **Phones:** players may not carry an electronic device in the playing area. Spectators are normally expected to switch theirs off or leave the hall.
- **Silence:** talking near the boards, even in a whisper, gets you asked to leave. Players analyse in a separate room or outside.
- **Distance:** onlookers stand back from the tables and never comment on positions.

Catalan chess culture is strong. The Federació Catalana d'Escacs runs a dense circuit of weekend opens, and announcements at local events are often given in Catalan first. *Escacs* is Catalan for chess, and *partida* means a game.

## Between rounds in Sant Boi

The town has enough to fill a free afternoon.

- **Termes Romanes:** the remains of a Roman bath complex in the old centre, among the best preserved in Catalonia.
- **Can Barraquer:** the house linked to Rafael Casanova, the hero of Barcelona's 1714 siege, who died in Sant Boi. He is buried in the parish church of Sant Baldiri.
- **Parc Agrari del Baix Llobregat:** the farmland of the delta, flat and good for walking.

Restaurants in Catalonia generally serve lunch from around 1:30pm and dinner after 8:30pm. That suits a morning or afternoon round better than it suits an evening one. Card payment is accepted almost everywhere. Tipping is modest: rounding up or leaving small change is normal.
