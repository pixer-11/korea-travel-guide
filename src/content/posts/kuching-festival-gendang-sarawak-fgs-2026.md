## Drums on the Sarawak River

The *gendang* is a hand drum, and this festival is built around it. The 2026 edition was set for five nights, from October 28 to November 1, 2026, with the Kuching Waterfront hosting Sarawak's traditional drumming alongside two Malay performing arts, Bermukun and Bertandak.

Bermukun pairs drum rhythms with sung verses, usually *pantun*, the Malay quatrain form. Bertandak is the dance that moves to the same beat. The programme also announced delegations from Indonesian Kalimantan and Brunei. All three places share Borneo and much of the same Malay musical tradition, so the line-up put variations of one drumming culture from three sides of the border on a single stage.

## Finding the stage at Kuching Waterfront

The waterfront is a paved promenade along the south bank of the Sarawak River, facing the old city. Behind it runs Main Bazaar, a row of shophouses selling handicrafts and pepper. Across the water are:

- **Fort Margherita**, the white Brooke-era fort
- **The Sarawak State Legislative Assembly building**, with its golden umbrella-shaped roof
- **Darul Hana Bridge**, the S-shaped pedestrian bridge linking both banks

Kuching International Airport sits south of the city, a short taxi or Grab ride from the waterfront. Most hotels in the old centre are walkable to the promenade. From the north bank, Darul Hana Bridge and the *tambang*, the small wooden river ferry, both cross to the south side. The festival's stage position along the promenade can change between editions.

## Tickets, timing and weather

Late October falls at the start of Kuching's wetter northeast monsoon season. Heavy afternoon and evening downpours are common, and an open-air riverfront offers little shelter. The programme spread its Sarawak, Kalimantan and Brunei performances across five evenings rather than a single night.

## Watching a gendang night properly

These are cultural performances first, so audience habits follow Malay community norms.

- **Modest dress.** Covered shoulders and knees are respectful, and sensible for mosquitoes by the river.
- **A clear floor.** Bertandak needs space. Audiences stand back from the dancers' path.
- **No flash.** Bright flashes during a performance distract both drummers and dancers.
- **The verses.** Much of Bermukun's appeal is in the sung pantun, which carries best to those standing close enough to hear the singer, not just the drums.

For getting around, Grab is the standard ride-hailing app in Kuching and is easy to use from the waterfront. Food stalls and small shops along Main Bazaar and Carpenter Street often prefer cash, so ringgit in hand is useful for snacks.

The old town is the place to eat before an evening on the waterfront. Sarawak laksa, made with a sambal-based broth rather than curry, is a breakfast and lunch staple. Kolo mee, springy noodles tossed rather than served in soup, is easy to find. After dark, the lit-up Assembly building across the river makes the walk along the promenade worth taking slowly.
