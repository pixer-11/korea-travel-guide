## A harbour built for walking

Masts are the first thing you see across the water at Abu Tig. White yachts and motor cruisers sit stern-in along the quays. Behind them, low ochre and cream buildings with arches and small domes face the boats, and the ground floors are mostly restaurants, cafés and shops.

This is El Gouna's main marina. El Gouna is a planned resort town built around a web of lagoons, about a half-hour's drive up the coast from central Hurghada. The marina feels like a small Mediterranean harbour moved to the Red Sea. You can walk the whole promenade, and the main draw is that very few cars come down to the waterline.

## Getting up the coast

Abu Tig sits in El Gouna, not in Hurghada's downtown or Sakkala districts. Plan the trip around that.

- **From Hurghada city:** take a taxi or book a car through a ride-hailing app. The drive north along the coast road usually takes 30–40 minutes, depending on where you start.
- **From Hurghada International Airport:** the airport is south of the city, so allow a little longer. It is still a single straight run up the coast.
- **At the town entrance:** cars stop at a gate when they enter El Gouna. If non-residents are charged anything, it is posted there.
- **Once inside El Gouna:** tuk-tuks and the town's shuttle buses link the marina with Downtown and the beaches. You can skip taxis altogether.

## What the quay actually offers

The marina is a place to wander and eat. It has no ticketed sights. Here is what you will find on an ordinary visit:

- **The promenade loop.** A paved walkway follows the water past moored boats. It is flat, so strollers and wheelchairs manage it easily.
- **Waterside dining.** Most restaurants put tables right at the quay edge. The cuisines lean international, so seafood, pasta and grills all turn up.
- **Boat departures.** Day trips for snorkelling, diving and cruising often leave from El Gouna's marinas. Confirm with your operator which quay you are meeting at.
- **Evening light.** The building façades are lit after dark and the boats reflect in the still water. Most people prefer the harbour at this hour.

## Choosing your hours

Arrive late in the afternoon. During the day the sun hits the white paving and the water hard, and from June to September midday heat on the Red Sea coast is punishing. October to April is far more comfortable for a long walk.

The promenade fills up as dinner service begins, so arriving before dark gets you a waterfront table without much effort. Expect a gentler pace than the restaurant strips of central Hurghada. It works well as a slower evening away from the city's busier strips, and two or three hours is usually enough.

## How evenings work at Abu Tig

Tables are not formally allocated. Restaurants along the quay often have someone out front with a menu, and you are welcome to read a few before choosing. A polite "la, shukran" (no, thank you) is enough to keep walking. On weekend evenings and in holiday weeks it makes sense to book the restaurant you want ahead, especially for a quay-edge table.

Paying is straightforward:

- **Cards** are widely accepted at El Gouna's sit-down restaurants. Carry some Egyptian pounds anyway for tuk-tuks and small purchases.
- **Service charge** is often already on the bill. Check before adding more.
- **Tipping** (baksheesh) is customary in Egypt. Leaving a little extra in cash for good service is normal, even when service is included.

Dress is resort-casual. Swimwear belongs on the beach, so bring a layer to put on for the promenade. Winter evenings can turn cool once the sun goes down.

## Pairing it with the rest of El Gouna

The marina is only one corner of the town, and it combines easily with the rest of it. A short tuk-tuk ride away, El Gouna's Downtown has a cluster of shops and cafés around small squares. Its architecture matches the marina's domes and arches.

Kitesurfers come to the lagoons and to the beaches on El Gouna's northern edge for the steady wind. If you are staying in central Hurghada, a good day out is a morning on the beach or the water, then Downtown, then dinner at Abu Tig before the drive back south.
