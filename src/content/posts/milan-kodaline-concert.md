## A Dublin band in a Milan warehouse

Kodaline plays Alcatraz Milano on December 3, 2026. The Irish four-piece from Swords, north County Dublin, made its name on big, open-hearted choruses. Songs like "All I Want" and "High Hopes" became the slow-dance and sing-along tracks of a whole decade.

Alcatraz suits that sound. It is one of Milan's best-known mid-sized concert halls, a broad industrial space where most of the crowd stands on one flat floor. When the band reaches a chorus, the room usually ends up singing it back loudly enough to compete with the PA.

## Where Alcatraz sits and how fans reach it

The venue is at Via Valtellina 25. That puts it on the northern edge of the Isola district, between Porta Garibaldi and the Maciachini area. Shiny new towers give way to older workshops and apartment blocks here.

Most concertgoers use the metro:

- **Maciachini (M3, yellow line):** the closest stop, roughly a 10-minute walk south along Via Valtellina.
- **Garibaldi FS (M2 green and M5 lilac lines):** around 15–20 minutes on foot through Isola. It is also a mainline and suburban rail hub, handy for anyone staying outside the centre.
- **Taxi or ride-hail:** common after the show. A cluster of cars usually waits on the surrounding streets once the crowd spills out.

From the Duomo, the M3 runs straight north to Maciachini with no change. That makes the centre the simplest base for most visitors.

## Queues, coats and paying at the bar

Italian gigs follow a few habits worth knowing.

- **The door line:** fans with general-admission tickets who want a spot near the barrier typically queue on Via Valtellina well before doors. Bag checks happen at the entrance, so the line moves in bursts.
- **Guardaroba:** Milan clubs commonly run a staffed cloakroom. On a December night, the queue for it at closing is often longer than the one at the door. Some regulars skip it and carry a light jacket instead.
- **Tickets:** the ticket is usually shown on a phone at the gate. Named tickets are common in Italy, so attendees keep an ID that matches the booking name on them.
- **Ordering:** the bar is a point-and-pay counter. A simple "una birra, per favore" works fine, and table-service tipping does not apply at a gig bar.

Milan crowds are enthusiastic but polite. Phones go up for the big singles, then mostly come down again.

## Before and after in Isola

Isola is one of the better neighbourhoods in Milan for a pre-gig meal. Via Borsieri and the streets around Piazzale Archinto are lined with trattorias, pizzerias and wine bars. The neighbourhood's aperitivo habit means a drink often arrives with small plates on the side.

A short walk south brings you to the glass towers of Piazza Gae Aulenti and the Bosco Verticale, the two plant-covered residential towers. Both are lit after dark and sit on the natural walk between Garibaldi and the venue.

Fans heading back to the centre usually walk to Garibaldi or Maciachini together after the encore. The streets stay busy for a while as the hall empties.
