## What Fashion Street actually is

This is a line of roughly 400 stalls under identical green tarpaulins, running along Mahapalika Marg between Churchgate and CST. Officially it's Mahatma Jyotiba Phule Market, but nobody calls it that. Stalls sell export-surplus and export-reject clothing: jeans, t-shirts, kurtas, bags, the overflow and rejects from garment factories that stitch for foreign labels. Sizes run inconsistent since batches vary stall to stall.

## Getting there and getting your bearings

Churchgate station (Western Railway terminus) is a five-minute walk; CST is ten minutes the other direction. The market runs opposite Bombay Gymkhana and St. Xavier's College, along the pavement facing Cross Maidan. There's no single entrance: you just start walking the row and stalls blur into each other for close to a kilometre.

- Nearest stations: Churchgate (Western Line) or CST (Central Line)
- Landmark to aim for: Bombay Gymkhana, New Marine Lines side
- Best stretch for clothing vs. accessories: clothing dominates the CST end, bags and belts cluster nearer Churchgate

## Bargaining and the rhythm of the stalls

Prices aren't fixed and nobody expects you to pay the first number quoted. Vendors open high for anyone who looks like a first-timer, so counter at roughly half and settle somewhere in between; check the market's own price tags if a stall has them, since some now do.

Cash is still king here, though more stalls now take UPI transfers via QR code taped to the counter. Carry a small folded bag; you'll be handed loose items, not gift-wrapped ones. Try clothes over what you're wearing since there are no changing rooms.

The market runs 10am to 10pm every day, no weekly closure. Weekday mornings, 10am to 1pm, are the quietest window, and weekend mornings before noon come close. Avoid the 2pm to 9pm weekend stretch, when the pavement fills shoulder to shoulder and browsing turns into shuffling.

## What to skip, what to actually buy

Skip anything a vendor insists is "original brand" at a market price; it isn't, and that's not the point of shopping here anyway. The genuine draw is basic wardrobe stock: plain t-shirts, denim, cotton kurtas, summer dresses, at prices well below any mall.

- Check stitching and zips before paying; quality varies stall to stall
- Don't expect exchanges or returns once you've walked off
- Combine the visit with the Oval Maidan and Kala Ghoda gallery strip nearby, both walkable from the same stretch of road

Fashion Street draws heavy footfall year-round, so this isn't a quiet find; treat the crowd as part of the experience rather than something to dodge entirely.
