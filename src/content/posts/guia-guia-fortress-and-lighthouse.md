## A fort above the casinos

Guia Hill is the peninsula's high point. From the top, the gilded towers of the Cotai and NAPE casino strips look like scale models. The Portuguese built the fortress here between 1622 and 1638, and its stone walls still ring the summit.

Inside the walls stand three things worth your time:

- **The lighthouse**, a 15-metre stone tower lit in 1865 and widely cited as the first modern lighthouse on the China coast.
- **The Chapel of Our Lady of Guia**, a small whitewashed church dating from the fort's founding.
- **The typhoon signal mast**, still used to warn the city when storm signals go up.

The whole complex belongs to the Historic Centre of Macao, listed by UNESCO in 2005.

## The frescoes and the tunnels

The chapel is the reason to slow down. Restoration work in the late 1990s uncovered frescoes beneath layers of later plaster. They blend Catholic imagery with Chinese motifs, including figures in Chinese dress and cloud patterns borrowed from local painting. The space is small, so you can study the walls from a few steps away.

Below the ramparts, a network of military tunnels and shelters cut in the 1930s is partly open, with a small exhibition. The lighthouse interior usually stays closed, so plan to admire it from the courtyard. The walls give clear sightlines over the Outer Harbour, the Taipa bridges and, on clearer days, out toward the Pearl River estuary.

## Up the hill: cable car or footpath

The easy route starts at **Flora Garden** (Jardim da Flora), a formal colonial garden at the hill's foot. From there the **Guia Cable Car** climbs to the summit in a ride of only a couple of minutes, one of the shortest public cable cars anywhere. The cable car runs its own schedule, separate from the fortress, so check it before you count on it.

On foot, a paved road climbs from Estrada do Engenheiro Trigo, the address on the gate. Shaded paths circle the hill, and the climb takes roughly 15 to 20 minutes at a steady pace. Taxis can drop you near the top if the heat is punishing.

## Timing the climb and minding the chapel

The fortress opens at 10am and closes at 5pm daily, including weekends. Since it stays off most tour itineraries, it rarely feels packed. Still, arriving close to 10am gets you the cooler morning and softer light on the lighthouse. Don't start up after about 4pm: you need time for the chapel before the gates shut.

A few habits that matter here:

- **Treat the chapel as a church.** Keep your voice down, don't sit on the altar steps, and skip the flash near the frescoes.
- **Carry water.** There's little to buy on the summit, and the climb is exposed in summer.
- **Watch the mast.** If typhoon signals are hoisted, the city may close outdoor sites, so check official announcements before you go.
- **Pair it sensibly.** Flora Garden at the bottom is the natural start or finish. Guia Hill's walking paths link down toward the Tap Seac area if you want to keep going on foot.

Give the summit about an hour. Add another 30 minutes if you walk up instead of riding.
