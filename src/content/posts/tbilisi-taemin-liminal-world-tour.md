## A December night at the Sports Palace

Tbilisi is an unusual stop for a K-pop tour. On December 9, 2026, Taemin brings his LiMiNal World Tour to the Tbilisi Sports Palace. The venue is a round, domed concrete arena that opened in 1961 and is better known as the home of Georgian basketball.

That history shapes the night. The seating is steep and close to the floor, so even the upper tiers look down onto the stage rather than across a field. For a solo performer whose shows are built around choreography, that view counts for a lot.

## Who Taemin is and what LiMiNal means

Taemin joined SHINee as its youngest member in 2008. He started his solo career in 2014 with "Danger" and went on to build a catalogue known for exact, theatrical dance. Fans point to "Move", "Criminal", "Idea" and "Guilty" as the tracks that set the standard.

The LiMiNal World Tour is his solo touring run. The title suggests in-between states, which fits a performer who has spent his career between boy-band polish and something stranger. This guide does not guess at the setlist or the staging. Both are the artist's to reveal, and fans tend to prefer the surprise.

## Reaching the arena on concert night

Most concert-goers get to the Sports Palace by taxi. Bolt is the ride-hailing app people in Tbilisi use most, and it lets you avoid haggling with street taxis or running into a language barrier. Pickup after the show is slower, because thousands of people open the same app at once.

December evenings in Tbilisi are cold but rarely brutal, usually a few degrees above freezing. The queue outside is the cold part of the night. Fans tend to bring a layer they can carry or stuff into a bag once they are inside the heated hall.

## Lightsticks, fan chants and the merch line

A K-pop crowd has its own customs, and the Tbilisi audience mixes local fans with people who travel in from across the region. Knowing the routine makes the night easier.

- **Lightsticks:** the official SHINee lightstick is the standard accessory, and the crowd's sea of light is part of the show.
- **Fan chants:** fans shout set lines during certain songs. Learning a few from fan videos lets you join the loudest moments.
- **Freebies:** fans often hand out small printed cards, stickers or slogan banners in the queue. Taking one and saying thank you is normal.
- **Merch:** the official stand usually draws the longest line of the night, and many people visit it before the show to avoid the rush at the end.
- **Filming:** phones go up for favourite songs. Holding yours low and not blocking the row behind you is the courtesy everyone expects.

Card payment is common across Tbilisi, and the local currency is the Georgian lari. A few words of Georgian always go down well: *gamarjoba* (hello) and *madloba* (thank you).

## Making a weekend of it in Tbilisi

Many fans flying in for one concert stay several days, and the city rewards it. Old Town is compact and walkable. From there a cable car rises to Narikala Fortress, which looks down over the river and the rooftops.

Below the fortress, the domed brick sulphur baths of Abanotubani are a good way to warm up on a December day. Rustaveli Avenue has the Opera House and the National Museum. Fabrika, a Soviet sewing factory turned hostel and courtyard of bars, is where many younger visitors spend their evenings.

Georgia lets citizens of many countries enter visa-free for long stays. That is one reason Tbilisi pulls in fans from neighbouring countries for a show like this.
