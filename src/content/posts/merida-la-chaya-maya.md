## A corner of Santa Lucía

Mérida's street grid makes this easy to find. La Chaya Maya sits at Calle 57 and Calle 62, at the edge of Parque Santa Lucía. The park is a small arcaded square known for its *sillas confidentes*, the S-shaped stone "love seats" built for two people to sit facing each other.

The Plaza Grande and the cathedral are a short walk south along Calle 60. Paseo de Montejo starts farther north. That puts lunch here within walking distance of most first-day sightseeing in the Centro.

The prices are mid-range, and it is one of the most-reviewed restaurants in the city. Expect visitors from all over, not a quiet neighbourhood spot.

## What to look for on a Yucatecan menu

The name gives you a clue. Chaya is a leafy green from the Yucatán, often blended into a cold drink called *agua de chaya*. The peninsula's cooking has its own vocabulary, so it pays to know these words before you open the menu:

- **Cochinita pibil**: pork marinated in achiote and sour orange, then slow-cooked
- **Poc chuc**: thin grilled pork with pickled red onion
- **Sopa de lima**: chicken broth sharpened with the local lime and topped with crisp tortilla strips
- **Relleno negro**: turkey in a black paste of charred chiles
- **Papadzules**: tortillas filled with egg and covered in pumpkin-seed sauce
- **Panuchos and salbutes**: small fried tortillas topped with shredded meat

Habanero salsa is the regional heat. Taste a small amount before you spoon it on.

## Getting a table without the dinner wait

The doors open at 7am and close at 11pm, seven days a week. A place this well known fills up at peak meal times. Breakfast and the gap between lunch and dinner are your best chances of walking straight in.

At busy times you put your name down with the host at the door and wait outside. Groups get seated faster if they're willing to split across two tables. Don't take an empty table without asking.

If the Thursday evening dates line up, plan around the park itself. Parque Santa Lucía hosts the long-running Serenata Yucateca on Thursday nights, with trova music and folk dance. Eat early, then walk out the door to it.

## Paying, tipping and ordering in Spanish

The table service follows normal Mexican habits:

- **Ordering:** Staff are used to visitors, but a few words help. "*Para tomar*" introduces your drink order, and "*sin picante*" asks for no heat.
- **The bill:** It does not come until you ask. Say "*la cuenta, por favor*" or make a writing gesture in the air.
- **Tipping:** Tips (*propina*) of 10 to 15 percent are customary. Check your bill to see whether service is already included.
- **Paying:** Ask whether they take cards before you order. Carry some pesos for the tip and for the street vendors around the park.
- **Prices:** These are printed on the menu at the table.

Allow about an hour for a full meal. Afterwards, walk south on Calle 60 to Parque Hidalgo and the Plaza Grande. It's a good digestive stroll, with plenty of shade in the colonnades.
