## Stakes, legend and coloured light

The cave's name is its story. In Vietnamese it is Hang Đầu Gỗ, the "Cave of Wooden Stakes." Legend says General Tran Hung Dao hid the sharpened stakes here that he planted in the Bach Dang River in 1288. When the tide dropped, the stakes wrecked the Mongol fleet.

French visitors called it the Grotte des Merveilles, the Grotto of Wonders. Today it runs as a guided route past stalactites and stalagmites lit in shifting colours. That lighting divides people: some love the drama, others would rather see bare rock. It is one of the best-known cave stops in the bay, so expect company.

## Reaching Dau Go Island

You cannot drive or walk to this cave. Every visit starts on a boat.

- **From Bai Chay:** tourist boats and day cruises leave from the Bai Chay side of Ha Long City. The standard bay route stops at Dau Go Island.
- **From Tuan Chau:** many overnight cruises sail from Tuan Chau Island marina and include the cave on day one.
- **Pairing:** Thien Cung Cave (Heavenly Palace) sits on the same island, a short walk away. Most itineraries visit both.

From the landing pier, a steep stone staircase climbs the cliff to the cave mouth. The top gives you a view over the moored boats and the karst islands beyond. Inside, the path is paved but damp and uneven in places.

## Timing around the crowds

Google lists the cave as open 24 hours every day. In practice, you arrive whenever your boat does.

- **Avoid:** weekends from 8am to 5pm. Tour groups stack up on the stairs and the narrower passages then.
- **Quietest:** 6pm to 11pm on both weekdays and weekends, going by measured foot traffic. Few boats schedule a cave stop that late, so ask your operator before counting on it.
- **Next best:** a weekday departure. Weekday daytime crowds run lighter than weekend peaks.

Allow 30 to 45 minutes for Dau Go itself. Add roughly the same again if you are doing Thien Cung too.

## Moving through Hang Đầu Gỗ with a group

Entry is almost always packaged with your boat. On a cruise, the cave is part of the itinerary. On a public route boat, you buy the bay route ticket at the wharf, and the price is posted at the ticket counter. Carry some Vietnamese dong for drinks and souvenirs at the pier stalls.

The cave moves in one direction, with groups following each other through. If a large group is ahead, let it get a chamber's length in front before you walk on. You get the formations to yourself for a minute, and your photos come out clearer.

- **Humidity:** the air inside is warm and damp, so wear breathable clothes.
- **Shoes:** grippy soles help on the wet steps and the polished floor.
- **Touching:** keep your hands off the formations. Skin oils stain the limestone, and guides will ask you to stop.
- **Guides:** guides on private or small-group boats usually tell the Tran Hung Dao legend at the stake-shaped formations. A small tip at the end is common but not expected.

Drones and tripods slow down the line on the stairs. Most visitors shoot handheld and save the long exposures for the view from the entrance.
