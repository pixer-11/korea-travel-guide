## Sunday, November 1, 2026

The date is set: Sunday, November 1, 2026. The Hangzhou Marathon is an internationally recognised road race. It has been part of the city's calendar since the late 1980s, which makes it one of China's longer-running marathons.

The start sits near Zhejiang Huanglong Sports Centre, the big stadium complex in Xihu District. Picture the wide boulevards around the stadium filled with runners in their corrals before dawn. Start waves, distances and cut-off times can change from one edition to the next. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site before you build a trip around them.

## Getting a bib

Paying for a place is not the whole process. Large Chinese marathons usually take registrations during a set window and then allocate places, often by ballot. Results are published afterwards and confirmed runners then pay.

Treat the official race website and its official social channels as the only sources. Reseller listings and "guaranteed entry" offers are a risk, because bibs at Chinese races are normally tied to the runner's ID.

What international runners usually need:

- A passport whose details match your registration exactly
- Any medical or fitness declaration the organisers ask for
- Collection of your race pack in person at the expo, with the same passport
- A Chinese visa or visa-free entry eligibility, checked separately with your nearest Chinese embassy

Prices for each distance are listed on the registration page. That figure is the one to trust.

## Huanglong at first light

The start area is a sports precinct, not a park. Zhejiang Huanglong Sports Centre is a large stadium with a curved, cable-supported roof. Around it are broad avenues that can hold thousands of runners, plus baggage trucks and long rows of portable toilets.

The metro is the easy way in. Huanglong Sports Centre station sits beside the venue on Hangzhou Metro Lines 3 and 10. On race morning, roads around the start close early, so taxis and ride-hailing cars get stuck in diversions.

If you're arriving in the city:

- **Hangzhou East Railway Station** is the main high-speed rail hub, with metro links across the city
- **Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport** is southeast of the centre and connected by metro
- **Wulin Square** and the streets north of West Lake put you a short metro ride from the start

## The lakeside miles

What makes Hangzhou different is West Lake. The course has traditionally run along the lake's shoreline roads, with willow-lined banks, causeways and low hills on the far side. Few big-city marathons give you that much water and green.

Exact routing can shift between editions, so study the official course map rather than older race reports. Check where the hills are. Hangzhou is mostly flat, but the roads near the lake pass close to Baoshi Hill and other wooded slopes.

The city's own geography helps you read the map. The Qiantang River runs along the south and east. The old commercial core is north and east of the lake. Huanglong is northwest. Once you know those four points, the kilometre markers make sense.

## Cheering "jiayou": race-day manners

Learn one word before you go: 加油, *jiāyóu*. It literally means "add oil", and spectators shout it at every runner on the course. Shout it back at the volunteers and you'll get a grin.

**Paying.** Hangzhou is Alipay's home city. Almost everything runs on mobile payment: cafés, metro gates, convenience stores near the start. Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with an international card before you fly. Both apps accept foreign cards, and they will save your race weekend.

**Getting in.** Marathon-morning metro trains fill with runners, so allow extra time at the station security checks. Bags are scanned on the Hangzhou Metro every day, not just on race days.

**On the course.** Water and refreshment stations are staffed by volunteers who hand cups out at arm's length. Take one without stopping dead, and drop the empties in the bins or zones marked after the table. Runners in China also carry small flags and wear team shirts. If a group runs past in matching colours, it's a running club, and they'll be loud.

**Spectating.** Watching from the West Lake shoreline is popular with locals out for their Sunday walk. Pick a spot you can reach on foot or by metro. Crossing the course is controlled, so stay on one side of it.

## A weekend around the race

It's worth staying an extra day or two. November is a comfortable month to walk the city, and the start area is a short distance from some of Hangzhou's best-known sights.

Near Huanglong:

- **Yellow Dragon Cave** (Huanglong Dong), a small garden in a fold of hillside, which gives the area its name
- **Baoshi Hill** and **Baochu Pagoda**, a short climb with views straight down over West Lake
- **Zhejiang University's Yuquan campus**, a leafy stretch at the foot of the hills

For recovery days:

- **Su Causeway** and **Bai Causeway**, flat walkways across West Lake that are easy on tired legs
- **Lingyin Temple** and the Feilai Feng rock carvings in the western hills
- **Longjing** tea villages, the home of Dragon Well green tea, terraced into the slopes southwest of the lake
- **Hefang Street**, the restored old shopping street near Wushan Square

After a marathon, Hangzhou food is easy on the stomach. Look for West Lake fish in vinegar sauce, Dongpo pork and shrimp stir-fried with Longjing tea leaves. Menus and prices are posted at each restaurant.
