## Bronze founders on Prapokklao Road

Three bronze kings stand shoulder to shoulder on a raised plinth. Behind them is the white, colonnaded former provincial hall, now the Chiang Mai City Arts & Cultural Centre. A paved forecourt opens onto Prapokklao Road, the north–south street through the middle of the moated Old City.

King Mengrai stands in the centre. He founded Chiang Mai in 1296 as the capital of the Lanna kingdom. Beside him are his allies: King Ramkhamhaeng of Sukhothai and King Ngam Muang of Phayao. Tradition says the three chose the site together.

## Getting here and when to come

No rail line runs to the Old City, so you'll arrive on foot or by road:

- **Walking:** about 15 minutes west of Tha Phae Gate through the lanes, or north up Prapokklao Road from Wat Chedi Luang.
- **Songthaew:** flag a red shared truck and say "Anusawari Sam Kasat". Agree the fare with the driver before you get in.
- **Grab or Bolt:** set the drop-off to the arts centre.

The forecourt never closes. Avoid weekends from 3pm–11pm, the busiest period, when tour groups and evening crowds fill the space in front of the plinth.

Morning light falls on the statues' faces. After dark the monument is floodlit. That's worth seeing if you're coming on a weekday, when the square is quieter.

## Offerings at the plinth

Treat this as a shrine, not just a photo stop. Thais come to pay respect to King Mengrai in particular. At the base you'll usually find marigold garlands, candles and incense, and people kneeling there. Keep out of the way of anyone who is praying.

- **Stay off the plinth.** Don't climb it, lean on it or sit on the steps for photos.
- **Keep your feet down.** Pointing your soles at the kings, or at anyone kneeling, is rude.
- **Dress as you would for a temple.** Shoulders and knees covered is safest, and you'll need the same for Wat Chedi Luang nearby.
- **To make an offering,** buy flowers or incense from a vendor. Kneel, wai with your palms together, then place it at the base. Nobody expects you to do this.

Photos are fine from the forecourt. Group selfies with arms around the kings are not.

## Museums and temples within a few blocks

Most visitors spend 15 minutes here, but the monument sits among some of the Old City's best-known sights.

- **Chiang Mai City Arts & Cultural Centre:** right behind the statues. Its galleries tell the Lanna story from prehistory to the modern city. Plan about an hour.
- **Chiang Mai Historical Centre:** the building behind the arts centre, with the remains of an old temple wall under glass.
- **Lanna Folklife Museum:** across Prapokklao Road, covering northern Thai craft, textiles and ritual.
- **Wat Chedi Luang:** a short walk south on Prapokklao Road, with its huge, partly ruined brick chedi. Wat Phan Tao, a teak viharn, is next door.
- **Sunday Walking Street:** the Sunday evening market along Ratchadamnoen Road, a few blocks south. Stalls often spill onto Prapokklao Road too, which adds to the crowds at the monument that night.

You can walk the monument, the arts centre and Wat Chedi Luang in one morning. Doing it before the heat builds is easiest.
