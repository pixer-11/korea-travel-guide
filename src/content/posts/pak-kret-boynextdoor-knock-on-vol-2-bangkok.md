## One night at IMPACT Arena

The Knock On Vol.2 Tour stops just outside Bangkok. BOYNEXTDOOR, the K-pop group from HYBE's KOZ Entertainment label, play IMPACT Arena on January 30, 2027. The arena is technically outside the capital: it sits in Muang Thong Thani, a planned district in Pak Kret, Nonthaburi province.

That address matters more than it looks. IMPACT is a whole compound rather than a single hall. Exhibition halls, the Thunder Dome, hotels and a lake share the same grounds. On a concert night the arena is the loudest room on the site, but rarely the only thing happening.

## The room itself

IMPACT Arena is a large indoor venue, roughly a rectangular box with a flat floor and steeply raked tiers wrapped around three sides. It is one of the standard stops for K-pop tours in Thailand. Thai audiences know its sightlines well, and the upper tiers still face the stage head-on rather than from the side.

Air conditioning keeps the interior cool, which surprises people coming in from the forecourt. January falls in Bangkok's cooler, drier season, so the waiting outside is far kinder than at a mid-year show. Expect a sea of lightsticks once the house lights drop. In a room this size, synchronised lightsticks make up much of the visual show.

## Getting to Muang Thong Thani

Most of the work of the night is in the journey. Muang Thong Thani lies off Chaeng Watthana Road, north of central Bangkok. Your main options:

- **MRT Pink Line**: the monorail runs along Chaeng Watthana Road and serves Muang Thong Thani. It links to the BTS Sukhumvit Line at Wat Phra Sri Mahathat and to the MRT Purple Line at Khae Rai.
- **Taxi or Grab**: direct from central Bangkok, though the expressways and Chaeng Watthana Road clog badly around big events.
- **From Don Mueang Airport**: the closest airport, a short drive east, handy for fans flying in on low-cost carriers from around Asia.

Leaving is slower than arriving. When an arena crowd empties at once, ride-hailing prices surge and pickup points jam. Fans often walk into the wider complex or wait out the first rush before calling a car.

## Where fans stay and eat

The cleanest option is sleeping on site. Novotel Bangkok IMPACT sits inside the complex, a walk from the arena doors. Other fans base themselves along Chaeng Watthana Road or near Don Mueang. Both cut the trip to minutes rather than an hour across the city.

Food is easy to find around the halls. The compound has convenience stores, food courts and casual restaurants that cater to exhibition crowds. Street vendors also gather on event days. Around the lake you will find a cluster of cheaper Thai restaurants used by office workers and locals in the district.

## Queues, freebies and fan etiquette at IMPACT

Thai K-pop fandom runs on generosity. Before big IMPACT shows, fans set up tables and hand out free goods to other fans: photo cards, stickers, slogan banners, snacks. It is a long-standing custom in Thailand. Accepting something with a smile and a "khop khun ka/khrap" (thank you) is the expected response, and plenty of people bring small things of their own to swap.

Official merchandise has its own line, usually the longest one on the grounds. Lines are orderly and self-policed, and cutting in is noticed quickly. Thai fans are generally quiet and cooperative in queues, then loud inside.

A few practical habits are normal here:

- **Payment**: Thai QR payment (PromptPay) is everywhere. Cards work at most official stalls, and cash covers the street vendors.
- **Bags**: arena security checks bags at the entrance, so attendees tend to carry small ones.
- **Phone charge**: e-tickets, QR payments and a Grab home all depend on one battery, so many people carry a power bank.
- **Chants**: Thai fans learn the Korean fan chants and shout them in unison, and newcomers who know them blend right in.

## Tickets and seating

Ticket details for this date sit with the tour's promoter and its ticketing partner, not with the venue. This guide makes no claim about prices, seat zones or showtimes. Seating at IMPACT Arena shows usually splits into standing or seated floor zones plus numbered tier seats. Resale through unofficial sellers is common around big K-pop dates in Bangkok, and so are scams, which is why Thai fans tend to stick to official channels.
