## Twenty-nine floors above Peking Road

Your evening starts in a lift. One Peking is the curved glass tower at 1 Peking Road, at the harbour end of Tsim Sha Tsui. The street below is all luxury storefronts and tour groups heading for Harbour City.

Canton Library sits on the 29th floor. Up there the noise of Kowloon drops away, and the dining room feels removed from the crush at street level. That height is part of the point. You come here for Cantonese cooking with some distance from the dim sum trolley and the banquet hall.

## What "modern-heritage" means on the plate

The restaurant describes its focus as Lingnan cuisine. Lingnan is the old name for the region south of the Nanling Mountains, covering Guangdong and Hong Kong. It is the home of Cantonese cooking in its widest sense.

That tradition values a few things above all:

- Freshness, especially in seafood and vegetables
- *Wok hei*, the smoky "breath of the wok" from very high heat
- Long-simmered soups and careful roasting
- Restraint with seasoning, so the main ingredient leads

Canton Library takes that heritage and serves it in a modern setting. It is not built around round tables and twelve-course wedding menus. Expect a format closer to a contemporary restaurant, where you choose what to eat rather than working through a set banquet. The menu changes and is still settling in, so check the restaurant's own channels for current dishes.

## Getting to One Peking

The walk is short and easy to find:

- **MTR:** Tsim Sha Tsui Station (Tsuen Wan Line) is a few minutes away on foot. East Tsim Sha Tsui Station (Tuen Ma Line) is a similar distance.
- **Star Ferry:** Ride across from Central and you land at Tsim Sha Tsui pier, a short stroll from Peking Road. At night this is the best way to arrive.
- **Taxi:** Say "One Peking, Peking Road, Tsim Sha Tsui". Most drivers know the tower by name.

Enter through the ground-floor lobby and take the lifts to 29. The lobby staff can point you to the right bank of lifts if you are unsure.

## Booking, paying and ordering

Treat a reservation as essential. A new restaurant on a high floor of a famous tower draws curious diners quickly. Walk-ins on a Friday or Saturday night are a gamble. Book through the restaurant's official channels and confirm opening hours at the same time, since a recent opening may still adjust its schedule.

When you book, ask about window seating. One Peking faces Victoria Harbour, and a table with a view is worth requesting early.

Paying works the Hong Kong way:

- Most sit-down restaurants of this level add a service charge to the bill, so check the total before tipping anything extra.
- Credit cards are standard; ask on arrival if you plan to use a specific one.
- Leaving loose change on top is a small courtesy, not an expectation.

Cantonese meals are shared. Dishes go in the middle and everyone takes from them, so order as a table rather than one plate each. Tea arrives early. If your pot runs dry, leave the lid propped open or set on its side, and staff will refill it. When someone pours tea for you, tap two fingers on the table as a quiet thank-you.

## Before and after dinner

Tsim Sha Tsui gives you a full evening around the meal. The harbourfront promenade sits right at the bottom of Peking Road. From there you can reach these spots on foot:

- **The Clock Tower**, the red-brick and granite remnant of the old Kowloon-Canton Railway terminus
- **Hong Kong Cultural Centre**, with its sloping, windowless tiled walls
- **Avenue of Stars**, the waterfront walkway facing Hong Kong Island's skyline
- **Harbour City**, the giant mall along Canton Road, for a wet-weather alternative

The skyline's nightly light show, *A Symphony of Lights*, plays across the harbour in the evening. Check the Hong Kong Tourism Board's site for the current schedule and plan dinner either side of it.

## Who will get the most out of it

Canton Library suits people who already know Cantonese food and want to see it treated with care in a modern room. It also works for first-time visitors who want one polished Cantonese dinner instead of a noisy dim sum hall.

Allow around two hours for a relaxed dinner, longer if you add the waterfront walk. Dress smart-casual. It is a tower-top dining room, not a cha chaan teng.

If you are after cheap noodles or a quick bowl of wonton, head elsewhere in Kowloon. This is a place to sit down, share a table of dishes and take your time.
