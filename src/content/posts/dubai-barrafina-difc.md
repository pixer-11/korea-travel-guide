## A London counter, rebuilt in Gate Village

Barrafina started in Soho as a tapas bar. In London the name means sitting at a counter, watching plates leave the pass and ordering a few at a time. On January 26, 2026, the brand opened its first site outside the UK, and it chose Gate Village in DIFC.

That choice makes sense. Gate Village is the low-rise cluster of sandstone-coloured buildings behind The Gate, DIFC's arch-shaped landmark. Galleries and restaurants share shaded courtyards there. Between them, they turn a finance district into one of Dubai's busiest dinner neighbourhoods once offices empty.

## What "counter-led" means for your evening

A counter changes the meal. You face the cooking, not the room, and the conversation runs sideways with whoever you came with. Tapas suit this format because dishes arrive in small portions, one after another, rather than as a single main course.

The menu for the Dubai site was not part of what we could verify. Rather than guess at dishes, read the current menu on the restaurant's own channels before you go. Expect the Spanish small-plate structure the London bars are known for, and a pace you control by ordering in rounds.

- **Best for:** two people, or a solo diner who likes watching a kitchen
- **Less ideal for:** large groups wanting one long table and a single conversation
- **Plan on:** roughly 90 minutes for a relaxed tapas dinner, longer if you keep ordering

## Getting to Gate Village

The Dubai Metro Red Line is the simplest route if you are staying in Downtown or along Sheikh Zayed Road. Two stations serve the area:

- **Financial Centre Station:** the closest stop for The Gate and the southern end of DIFC
- **Emirates Towers Station:** a short walk to the north side of the district

From either station, walk towards The Gate building. Gate Village sits directly behind it, reached through its courtyards. Taxis and ride-hailing apps drop at the DIFC road entrances, and there is paid parking in the district's underground car parks. Give yourself a few minutes extra on your first visit, because the Gate Village building numbers can confuse newcomers.

## Booking, queuing and paying

Book first, and check that you can. Barrafina's London bars built their name partly on walk-in queues at the counter. If walk-ins are accepted, arriving close to opening usually beats the after-work crowd that fills DIFC in the evening.

Some practical points for dining in DIFC:

- **Payment:** cards and contactless phones are standard across Dubai restaurants
- **Service charge:** check the bill before tipping, since some Dubai venues add one; if none is included, around 10% is a common gesture
- **Weekends:** the UAE weekend is Saturday and Sunday, and DIFC dinner tables fill fastest on Thursday and Friday nights
- **Dress:** smart casual fits Gate Village; beachwear and gym kit do not

At a counter, order a couple of plates at a time. Then add more as they land. It keeps food hot and stops the counter in front of you overcrowding.

## Before or after the meal

Gate Village rewards a slow walk. The courtyards hold several contemporary art galleries, and their windows are worth a look on the way in. DIFC also runs public art installations around its walkways.

For a longer evening, these are close by:

- **Dubai Mall and the Burj Khalifa:** one Metro stop south to Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall Station
- **Emirates Towers:** a short walk north, with their own restaurant and bar options
- **Zabeel and Za'abeel Park:** across Sheikh Zayed Road, for daylight hours

## Is it worth the trip?

If you know Barrafina from London, this is the brand's first chance to be judged somewhere else. If you don't, the format is the reason to go. It is a counter where you eat close to the cooking, in a district that is well set up for an evening on foot.

Since it is new and has drawn crowds since opening, things can change quickly. Confirm hours, booking rules and the current menu before you commit an evening to it.
