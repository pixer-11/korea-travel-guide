## The December temperature swing

This is the detail that catches first-timers. December afternoons in Sharm are usually warm enough for the beach and a swim. After dark, though, desert air cools quickly, and an open-air dance floor near the mountains can feel properly chilly by the small hours.

Festival-goers usually dress in layers. The typical kit:

- A light jacket or hoodie for after midnight
- Closed shoes, because outdoor sites are often sandy or gravelly
- Sunscreen and a hat for the daytime before the music starts
- A refillable bottle, since dry air dehydrates you faster than you notice

The sea stays swimmable in December. Plenty of people spend the day snorkelling and the night dancing, then sleep late.

## Landing at SSH and moving around town

Sharm El Sheikh International Airport (SSH) sits north of the main resort strip. It is a short drive to Naama Bay and Nabq Bay. Many European cities have direct flights, especially in winter, and Cairo has frequent domestic connections.

Sharm is spread out along the coast, not walkable end to end. Getting around usually means:

- Hotel shuttles, which most resorts run to Naama Bay and the Old Market
- Taxis, which are plentiful along the main roads
- Ride-hailing apps, which work in parts of town
- Organiser shuttles, which festivals often run from central points; the official site describes any transport offered

By road, Cairo is a long drive across the Sinai, roughly seven hours or more. Most visitors fly.

## Picking a base: Naama Bay, Old Sharm or Nabq

Where you sleep shapes the whole trip. Pick it with the venue location in mind.

**Naama Bay** is the classic centre. It has a pedestrian promenade lined with restaurants, bars and dive shops, and a sandy beach. It is the easiest base for nightlife and taxis.

**Old Sharm (Sharm El Maya)** is home to the Old Market. You'll find spice stalls, shisha cafés and the large white Al Mustafa Mosque here. It feels more like a working town and sits close to the marina.

**Nabq Bay** lies north towards the airport. It is quieter and dominated by big all-inclusive resorts. **Hadaba**, on the cliffs south of Naama, has sea views and good access to the reefs. **Soho Square**, near Sharks Bay, is an open-air entertainment complex that is popular in the evenings.

## Getting around Sinai etiquette

Sharm is relaxed by Egyptian standards, but local norms still apply away from the resorts. These are the habits that make a festival trip smoother.

**Paying.** The Egyptian pound is the everyday currency. Taxis, market stalls and small cafés are easiest in cash. Hotels and larger venues generally take cards. Taxi drivers usually agree a fare before setting off, and people who skip that step tend to argue at the end.

**Tipping.** Small tips, known as *baksheesh*, are part of daily life. Hotel porters, drivers, dive guides and anyone who helps with bags usually get one. Keeping small notes on you makes this painless.

**Dress and behaviour.** Festival clothes are normal at the venue and the resort. In the Old Market or near mosques, visitors usually cover their shoulders and knees. Alcohol is served in hotels and licensed venues, not on the street. Public drunkenness away from those spaces draws attention.

**Language.** A few Arabic words go a long way. *Shukran* means thank you, and *la, shukran* (no, thank you) is the polite way to wave off a persistent shop pitch at the market.

**Entry stamps.** Visa rules depend on your passport. For years, many nationalities staying only within the South Sinai resorts have been able to use a Sinai-only entry stamp. Egypt's official e-visa portal sets out the current options.

## The recovery days: reefs, desert and a mountain

Most people who fly in for a December festival tack on a few days. Sharm's surroundings are some of the best in the Red Sea.

- **Ras Mohammed National Park**, at the very tip of the peninsula, has reef walls that are famous with divers and snorkellers. Day boats and road trips leave from Sharm.
- **Tiran Island** and its offshore reefs lie in the strait north of town. They are a standard boat trip from Sharm marina.
- **Naama Bay's house reef areas** suit an easy morning snorkel when nobody has slept much.
- **Mount Sinai and St Catherine's Monastery** are a few hours' drive inland. Overnight trips leave Sharm to reach the summit for sunrise, and the peak is genuinely cold in December.
- **Dahab** is a laid-back dive town up the coast with the Blue Hole and a waterfront of low-key cafés. It makes a good day or overnight trip.

A typical festival trip runs four to seven days: one or two nights of music, with sea and desert on either side.
