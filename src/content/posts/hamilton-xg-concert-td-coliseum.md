## Seven voices, one arena floor

XG plays TD Coliseum on November 12, 2026. This is the group that broke out in 2022 with "Tippy Toes" and built a global fanbase on hard choreography and English-language hip-hop and R&B. Its seven members are Jurin, Chisa, Hinata, Harvey, Juria, Maya and Cocona.

They are Japanese, trained in a K-pop style system, and they rap as much as they sing. Fans call themselves ALPHAZ. Expect a crowd that knows every formation change in "Left Right" and "Shooting Star" and treats the dance breaks as the main event.

## The building on York Boulevard

TD Coliseum is the new name for the arena long known as Copps Coliseum, later FirstOntario Centre. It sits at the corner of York Boulevard and Bay Street North in downtown Hamilton. Oak View Group led a major renovation that rebuilt the bowl, concourses and hospitality spaces, and the arena reopened under the TD name in late 2025.

The arena connects to the Jackson Square shopping complex next door. FirstOntario Concert Hall stands a short walk away on Main Street West. That cluster makes the block the city's main entertainment district on show nights.

For a seven-member group with big floor formations, the arena's raised end-stage setup suits the show well. Seats along the sides give a clean view of the choreography. Floor spots put you closer but lower, so you see faces rather than patterns.

## Getting to downtown Hamilton

GO Transit is the main rail link. Two stations serve the area:

- **Hamilton GO Centre** on Hunter Street East, about a 15-minute walk south and east of the arena
- **West Harbour GO**, on the Lakeshore West line, a walk of roughly 15–20 minutes downhill toward the bay

From Toronto, the Lakeshore West line runs along the lake through Oakville and Burlington. Fans coming from Union Station usually take the train rather than face the QEW traffic. On weeknights the late return trains run less often, so people tend to note the last departure before the encore.

Hamilton Street Railway (HSR) buses run along King Street and Main Street, both a few blocks from the arena. Drivers use the downtown parkades around Jackson Square and York Boulevard. Those lots fill on concert nights, and the exits clog for a while after the show.

## Before and after the show

Hess Village, a small pedestrian strip of bars and patios at Hess and George Streets, is about ten minutes' walk west. James Street North has independent restaurants, coffee shops and galleries, and it is the usual pre-show pick for a sit-down meal.

Visitors who stay overnight tend to book downtown hotels near the arena rather than out by the highway, so the walk home avoids the parking queue. If you have a free morning, Bayfront Park and the waterfront trail sit at the foot of Bay Street North, near West Harbour GO.
