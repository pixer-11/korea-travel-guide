## A valley of trees wired with light

By day in winter, the garden is bare branches and frost. After dusk, it turns into something else.

The Garden of Morning Calm sits in a fold of hills in Sang-myeon, Gapyeong County. A horticulture professor, Han Sang-kyung, founded it in 1996, and its winter light festival has become one of the best-known seasonal displays in the Seoul region. This edition runs from December 4, 2026 to March 14, 2027.

Coloured LEDs wrap the tree trunks and run along hedges and lawns. Strings of light hang over the paths, so you walk through tunnels and across slopes that glow in blocks of colour. The garden is terraced into a hillside, which means many paths climb, and from the higher ground you look down over the lit beds below.

## Reaching Achimgoyo Sumogwon from Seoul

There is no station at the gate. The usual trip is a train and then a road leg.

- **Train:** the Gyeongchun Line and the ITX-Cheongchun both run from Seoul to Cheongpyeong Station and Gapyeong Station.
- **Bus:** local buses leave Cheongpyeong Station for the garden. The Gapyeong City Tour bus loop also stops there.
- **Taxi:** taxis wait outside both stations and are the simplest choice after dark.
- **Car:** the garden has its own parking. Drivers come in via the roads around Cheongpyeong Lake.

Lots of visitors combine it with Nami Island or Petite France, which are both a short drive away in the same county. Day-tour operators in Seoul often sell that combination.

## Walking the lights after dark

Most visitors time their arrival for late afternoon. That way they see the garden's layout in daylight, then stay as the lights come on at dusk. The full loop usually takes 1.5–2 hours at a strolling pace.

- **Tickets:** the garden sells admission at its gate, and prices are posted there and on its official site. Korean card payments work almost everywhere, and a T-money card covers the local buses.
- **Clothing:** winter nights in Gapyeong regularly drop below freezing, and the slopes catch the wind. Locals come in long padded coats, gloves and hats, and many carry hand warmers.
- **Footing:** paths can ice over, so shoes with grip matter more than style.
- **Etiquette:** visitors keep to the paths and off the planted beds. Couples and families pause at the light arches for photos, then move on so the next group can step in.

Weekend evenings and Korean holidays draw the biggest crowds from Seoul. Visitors who want quieter paths tend to go on weekdays.

## Where the festival details live

The Garden of Morning Calm runs the festival itself. Its official website publishes the dates, the nightly lighting hours and the admission information. Lighting schedules and closing times often shift across a festival season that runs this long, and the garden's site has each month's hours.

Most visitors treat the festival as a day trip from Seoul and return on the last trains from Cheongpyeong or Gapyeong. Some stay overnight in Gapyeong instead, at the pensions and guesthouses along the Bukhan River and Cheongpyeong Lake. That makes it easy to stay until the lights go off.
