## Why this goose has a following

You smell it before you see it: charcoal smoke and rendered fat drifting onto Hennessy Road. Behind the glass, whole geese hang in a row, skin lacquered the colour of dark honey. This is what draws people off a busy Wan Chai sidewalk and into a queue.

Kam's built its reputation on a technique borrowed from a Michelin-starred lineage — the founder trained at Yung Kee before striking out on his own. The goose here is roasted to order in small batches, never left sitting under a heat lamp.

## What to order

The roast goose is the reason you're here, and it's sold by portion size, so decide how hungry you are before you reach the counter.

- Roast goose (quarter, half, or whole) — order this above all else
- Char siu (BBQ pork) — glossy, slightly charred at the edges
- Roast goose noodles or rice — the easiest single-person option
- Goose blood and offal soup — for the more adventurous

Ask for a mix of leg and breast meat if you're sharing; the leg is fattier and more tender, the breast leaner with more bite from the skin.

## The scene inside

Inside, it's bright, functional, and loud in the way busy Cantonese restaurants are loud — clattering plates, servers calling orders, the chop of a cleaver against a wooden block near the window. Tables turn over fast.

Don't expect a leisurely, lingering meal. Locals and tourists alike treat this as efficient, purposeful eating: order, eat, pay, go. That's part of the charm if you let it be.

## Getting there

Kam's Roast Goose sits at 226 Hennessy Road in Wan Chai, an easy walk from Wan Chai MTR Station (Island Line) — take Exit A3 and it's a few minutes on foot along Hennessy Road. It's also walkable from Causeway Bay if you're combining it with shopping there.

The restaurant is open daily from 11:30am to 9:30pm, with no midday closure, so you don't need to time a break between lunch and dinner service.

## When to go

This is a very popular, well-reviewed spot, so expect a wait at peak hours regardless of day. Weekday lunch (12pm–2pm) and early weekend dinner tend to be the busiest stretches.

On weekends, the calmest window measured runs from noon through 9pm — steadier than the sharp lunch spike you'd expect. Arriving right at 11:30am opening, or after 2:30pm on weekdays, generally means less standing around.

## How to visit like a local

Order at the counter or point at the goose in the window if the menu language is a barrier — staff are used to this and will steer you toward a portion size that suits your group.

- Pay by cash or card; both are accepted, but having small bills speeds things up during a rush
- Don't linger over the menu once seated — regulars order within a minute or two, and tables are in demand
- The biggest tourist mistake is over-ordering a whole goose for two people; a half portion easily feeds two with rice or noodles
- Tipping isn't expected beyond any service charge already added to the bill
- If the queue outside looks long, ask if takeaway is faster — many regulars grab sliced goose to go rather than wait for a table

Go in expecting noise, speed, and a plate of goose that justifies the wait. This isn't a place for a quiet, drawn-out dinner — it's a place to eat well, quickly, and get on with your evening in Wan Chai.
