## Eight players, one desert week

The season ends here. The WTA Finals is the women's tour's year-end championship, and qualification comes from the season's results: the top eight ranked singles players and the top eight doubles teams make the field.

The 2026 edition runs November 8-15 at Indian Wells Tennis Garden. It was originally set for Riyadh and moved to California because of safety concerns. Singles uses a round-robin format. Two groups of four play each other, the top two from each group reach the semifinals, and the week closes with the final.

## Inside Indian Wells Tennis Garden

You already know this venue if you watch tennis. Every March it hosts the BNP Paribas Open, the event players and fans call "Tennis Paradise". Stadium 1 is the main court. It is one of the largest tennis-only stadiums in the world, a steep concrete bowl with the Santa Rosa and San Jacinto mountains rising behind the upper rows.

In March the grounds are packed with dozens of matches a day. The Finals is different. It is a short, focused card of a few singles and doubles matches per session, so seat choice matters more than wandering between outside courts. Upper rows in Stadium 1 still give a clean, full view of the court. Lower bowl seats put you close enough to hear the footwork.

## Getting to the Coachella Valley

Indian Wells is a small resort city between Palm Desert and La Quinta, about two hours' drive east of Los Angeles on Interstate 10.

- **Fly:** Palm Springs International Airport (PSP) is the closest, roughly half an hour's drive away.
- **Bigger hubs:** Ontario (ONT) and Los Angeles (LAX) have more flights but mean a longer drive.
- **Stay:** the Hyatt Regency Indian Wells and Renaissance Indian Wells resorts sit beside the Garden. Palm Desert, La Quinta and Palm Springs have more options.
- **On site:** most fans drive or take a rideshare. The Garden has large surface parking lots for its March crowds.

Public transport in the valley is thin, so visitors without a car usually book rideshares to cover the gap.

## Changeovers, layers and the night sessions

Tennis crowds follow strict seating rules. Ushers hold you at the portal while a point or game is in play. People take their seats and leave during changeovers, which come after the first game of a set and then after every odd-numbered game. Phones stay silent, and flash photography during points is not allowed.

The desert in November swings hard between day and night. Afternoons are usually warm and very sunny. Once the sun drops behind the mountains, the temperature can fall fast in an open-air stadium. Regulars come to day sessions with sunglasses and a hat, and to night sessions with a jacket.

Most attendees eat on site or in the restaurants along Highway 111. For fans who want to see more than one match, a day session and a night session together make a full day at the Garden.
