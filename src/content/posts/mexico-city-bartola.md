## Broken cement and red sauce

Bartola is a dinner that comes with a crowd. The room is finished in broken, raw-looking cement, a deliberately unfinished surface that throws noise back at you. Expect volume. Conversation happens leaning in, and that is the point of the place.

The kitchen leans Italian, by way of Mexico City. Two dishes carry its reputation:

- **Chicken parm**, the breaded, sauced and cheese-topped cutlet.
- **Rigatoni cacio e pepe**, ridged tubes in the Roman pecorino-and-black-pepper sauce.

Order one of those first. Anything else on the menu changes, so read what is printed on the night.

## Who you'll be sitting next to

People-watching here is half the draw. Bartola fills with a mix of expats and Mexico City locals, and the bar side keeps the room moving between tables. Dress follows Roma Norte norms: put-together, never formal.

This is not the spot for a quiet anniversary or a business conversation. Pick it for a group dinner, a first night in the city, or drinks that turn into pasta. If you want calm, Roma Norte has plenty of quieter rooms a few blocks away.

## Finding it in Roma Norte

Roma Norte is the leafy, early-20th-century grid west of Avenida Cuauhtémoc and east of Avenida Insurgentes. Its streets are lined with Porfirian-era mansions turned into restaurants, galleries and bars. Avenida Álvaro Obregón, with its tree-shaded central walkway, runs through the middle.

Getting there:

- **Metro:** Insurgentes station (Line 1, pink) sits at the neighbourhood's northwest corner.
- **Metrobús:** Line 1 runs down Avenida Insurgentes, with stops along Roma's western edge.
- **Ride-hail:** Uber and DiDi are the default way back at night for most visitors.

Check Bartola's own listing for the exact address before setting off. Pair the evening with a walk past Plaza Río de Janeiro and its replica of Michelangelo's David, or cross into Condesa for Parque México beforehand.

## Getting a table without a scene

Treat Bartola as a place you plan for. It is recently opened and trending, so tables go fast and hours may still shift. Confirm opening times and booking options on its official website or social accounts before you go. Do not rely on an old listing.

How the evening works in practice:

- **Book ahead** where reservations are offered. Arriving early in the evening, before the dinner rush builds, gives a walk-in better odds.
- **Paying:** cards are widely accepted in Roma Norte restaurants. Staff usually bring a handheld terminal to the table.
- **Tipping:** the *propina* is customary in Mexico City, typically 10 to 15 percent. The terminal often asks you to choose the tip before you pay. Check the bill first in case service is already included.
- **Asking for the bill:** say "la cuenta, por favor." Mexican waiters rarely bring it until you ask.

Dinner in Mexico City runs late, and a table booked for early evening will feel empty next to the same room a few hours later. Choose your slot by the atmosphere you want.
