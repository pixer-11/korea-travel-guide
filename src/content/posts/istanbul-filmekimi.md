Ten days in October, a handful of cinemas, and a programme made up of the year's festival-circuit films. That's Filmekimi, Istanbul's autumn film showcase. It's one of the main cultural events of the city's season. The 2026 edition runs **October 9-18**, with screenings at **Atlas 1948** and three other cinemas around Istanbul.

You won't find an outdoor stage, wristbands or a festival village here. You get dark rooms, subtitles and queues on the pavement. Fitting several films into one day is entirely normal.

## What Filmekimi actually is

Filmekimi is a city showcase, not a competition. It's organised by İKSV, the Istanbul Foundation for Culture and Arts, which also runs the Istanbul Film Festival in spring. In autumn the job is curation: films that have played the major international festivals during the year, brought to Istanbul audiences over a short, packed run.

The ten-day format shapes everything. Most titles get only a screening or two. Miss one and you may wait months for a release in Turkey, or it may never arrive.

## The Atlas 1948 screen on İstiklal

Atlas 1948 is the venue most people mean when they talk about Filmekimi. It's on **İstiklal Caddesi**, the long pedestrian street running from Taksim Square down towards Tünel. You go in through **Atlas Pasajı**, one of the old covered arcades off the street.

The cinema takes its name from the year it opened. After a long restoration it reopened as part of the Istanbul Cinema Museum, which shares the building. Arrive early and you can look around the museum before your film starts.

Inside, it's a single proper auditorium, not a multiplex box. That's a big part of why festival screenings here feel like an event. Expect a full room for the most talked-about titles.

## Getting to the cinemas

For Atlas 1948, the M2 metro is the simplest option:

- **Taksim station (M2)**: come up at Taksim Square and walk down İstiklal. It's a short, flat walk.
- **Şişhane station (M2)**: useful if you're coming from the Galata or Karaköy side. Walk up through Tünel onto the lower end of İstiklal.
- **Tünel funicular**: the historic underground funicular climbs from Karaköy to the bottom of İstiklal.
- **Nostalgic tram**: the red heritage tram runs the length of İstiklal. It's slow, but handy on tired legs.

Load an **Istanbulkart**, the city's tap-on transit card, so one card covers the metro, funicular and tram. Filmekimi uses three other cinemas as well, so check the address of each screening on your ticket. Don't assume every film is at Atlas.

## Booking without missing out

Tickets are the whole game at Filmekimi. A short run with limited screenings means the films people are talking about sell out, and the opening weekend sells out fastest. Confirm timing and tickets on the official İKSV Filmekimi site. That's where the programme, venues and booking details are published, and it's the source to trust over any reseller.

Some habits that help:

- **Build a shortlist first.** Read the full programme, rank your top five, then book in order. Don't wait until every slot looks perfect.
- **Check the subtitle line on each listing.** Films are generally shown in their original language. Whether English subtitles are included varies by title, so read the listing before you book.
- **Note the venue on every ticket.** With four cinemas, it's easy to plan two films back to back at venues that are a real journey apart.
- **Keep the confirmation on your phone.** Have your ticket ready to scan at the door so the queue keeps moving.

Ticket prices are on the official site and at the box office. Card payment is standard almost everywhere in central Istanbul.

## Doing a festival day like an Istanbul regular

Istanbul audiences take Filmekimi seriously, and a few unwritten rules make the day go smoothly.

Arrive with time to spare. The crowds on İstiklal can slow a five-minute walk to fifteen, especially in the evening. Festival screenings tend to start on time, and walking in after the lights go down means fumbling for a seat in a full house.

Phones go away completely, and that includes the glow of checking messages. People stay seated through the credits more than you might expect, since festival films often end on a final shot people want to see out. If a director or guest is introducing the film, the room will usually be quiet and ready by the scheduled time.

Between films, do what everyone else does: step out into the arcade or onto the street, grab a tea or a quick bite, and argue about what you've just seen. A simit from a street cart is the classic way to keep going between screenings.

## Between screenings on İstiklal

Atlas 1948's location is a big part of Filmekimi's appeal. The gaps between films land you on one of the city's busiest streets. A few things fit into an hour or two:

- **Istanbul Cinema Museum**: in the same building as the cinema, the obvious first stop.
- **Çiçek Pasajı**: the old "Flower Passage" arcade further down İstiklal, lined with meyhane restaurants.
- **Galatasaray High School gates**: a landmark on the street and a common meeting point.
- **Galata Tower**: downhill past Tünel, a good walk if you have a longer gap.
- **Taksim Square**: at the top of the street, where the metro is.

İstiklal is crowded from late afternoon through the night. If you have a ninety-minute gap, choose one stop rather than trying to fit in three.

## Planning your ten days

Mid-October suits a festival like this. Evenings cool down, which makes a night of back-to-back films indoors feel natural.

On how many films to see: two a day is comfortable for most visitors, while three makes a proper festival marathon. Weekday screenings are usually easier to book than weekend ones, purely because more people can make weekends.

If you're in Istanbul for only part of the run, start with the programme and plan the rest of your trip around it. The films are fixed, while the city's other sights will still be there the day after. Before you travel, confirm the dates, the venue for each screening and your tickets on the official Filmekimi site.
