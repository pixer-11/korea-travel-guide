## A weekend named for the underworld

Mictlan was the Aztec land of the dead. It was ruled by Mictlantecuhtli, and souls crossed nine levels to reach it. The festival took that name for a Day of the Dead-themed cultural event set for October 24-25, 2026, a Saturday and Sunday.

The dates fell a week before the family altars of November 1 and 2. By late October the city has usually started to fill up for the season:

- marigolds (*cempasúchil*) on market stalls
- sugar skulls in the bakeries
- *pan de muerto* stacked in the panaderías

## The ground around El Ángel

The festival's setting was the Angel of Independence. The gilded Victory figure stands on a tall column in the middle of a roundabout on Paseo de la Reforma. Antonio Rivas Mercado designed it, and it was inaugurated in 1910 for the centenary of independence. Its base holds a mausoleum with the remains of independence heroes, which suited a festival about the dead.

Reforma is wide and lined with trees, with office towers along both sides. Zona Rosa lies just south of the roundabout and Colonia Juárez is next to it. Colonia Cuauhtémoc sits to the north. Between them there is no shortage of taquerías, cafés and hotels within a few blocks. Chapultepec Park and the Museo Nacional de Antropología are about half an hour's walk west along the boulevard.

## Getting to Reforma

The Metrobús is the most direct way in. Line 7 runs along Reforma and has a stop at El Ángel. Line 1 runs down Avenida Insurgentes, a short walk east.

- **Metro Insurgentes (Line 1):** sits on the Glorieta de Insurgentes roundabout, about 10 minutes south on foot through Zona Rosa.
- **Metro Sevilla (Line 1):** sits on Chapultepec avenue, a similar walk.
- **Taxi or ride-hailing:** an option, but street closures around the roundabout often leave cars stuck in traffic. Most people get dropped a few blocks away.

On Sunday mornings Reforma usually closes to cars for the city's *Muévete en Bici* cycling programme. Many visitors rent an Ecobici bike or simply walk the boulevard.

## Doing Día de Muertos properly at Mictlan

Behaviour matters at Day of the Dead events. The holiday is a family remembrance first, and the costumes come second.

- **Catrina face paint:** this is common and welcome. Street artists usually set up around big Reforma events. A sombrero worn as a joke isn't welcome.
- **Altars (*ofrendas*):** the photos, candles, water and food on an altar are offerings to particular dead people. Visitors photograph them but don't touch or move anything.
- **Payment:** street vendors and face painters mostly take cash in pesos. Card readers are more common at fixed stalls.
- **Spanish that helps:** *¿Cuánto cuesta?* (how much?), *una calaverita* (a small sugar skull), *con permiso* (excuse me, used when squeezing through a crowd).

Crowds on Reforma can get dense at weekends, so locals usually agree on a meeting point in advance. The Angel's column, visible from blocks away, is the usual choice.
