## A quiet room off the main square

Plaza Amelia Wilkes is the old town square of Cabo San Lucas. It's a small square of benches and shade trees a few blocks back from the marina's restaurant strip. The Museum of Natural History stands on the square itself, so you'll find it as soon as you reach the plaza.

This is a small place. The displays cover the geological and archaeological history of Baja California Sur. That means how the peninsula formed and what traces people left on it long before the resort hotels went up. It gets far less traffic than the boat tours at the marina, and that makes it a good short break from the waterfront.

## What's inside, and how long it takes

There are two broad themes:

- **Geology:** how the southern tip of the Baja peninsula was shaped. It's the same rock and coastline you'll see at Land's End and El Arco.
- **Archaeology:** the human past of the Cape region, whose original inhabitants were the Pericú people.

Allow 30 to 60 minutes. Labels may be mainly in Spanish, so a translation app with a camera mode helps. The museum is useful background before a water taxi out to the Arch. Once you've seen it, the granite headland means more than a photo backdrop.

## Timing your stop on Plaza Amelia Wilkes

The museum is open **10am to 3pm, Monday through Saturday**. It is **closed on Sunday**, which catches out weekend visitors. Because it shuts at 3pm, an after-lunch stroll from the marina can arrive too late.

Here's how to fit it in:

- **Before a boat trip:** walk up from the marina at 10am, then head back down for a midday departure.
- **On a hot day:** come in the morning and save the exposed beach time for later.
- **Cruise passengers:** ship tenders land at the marina. The plaza is close enough to fit the museum around a short port day, as long as it isn't Sunday.

## Visiting the Museo de Historia Natural

Locals call it the **Museo de Historia Natural**. Ask for it by that name if you need directions, and a simple "¿Dónde está el museo?" on the plaza will get you there. Check the entry arrangements at the door, because they aren't posted anywhere reliable online. Carry some pesos for the door and the plaza's snack stands, since small vendors in Centro don't always take cards.

The museum building and its exhibits deserve quiet attention. Keep your voice down, and ask before photographing anything if there's no sign. Afterwards, stay on the plaza for a while. The streets around Plaza Amelia Wilkes are the oldest part of town, with smaller cafés and shops that feel slower than the marina's bar row.

From the plaza, these are all an easy walk:

- **Cabo San Lucas marina:** water taxis and glass-bottom boats to El Arco and Lover's Beach leave from here.
- **Medano Beach:** the town's main swimming beach, east of the marina.
- **Centro's side streets:** good for an unhurried lunch once the museum closes.
