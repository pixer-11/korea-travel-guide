## A Victorian layout that still works

The paths here were laid out for strolling. Alexandra Park opened in 1870, and its broad promenades and tree-lined walks still follow that Victorian plan. Inside the 60 acres you'll find a lake, open lawns, sports facilities and children's play areas.

It sits roughly three kilometres south of the city centre, where Whalley Range meets Moss Side. Princess Road runs along its eastern side. Chorlton is a short walk west if you want food or pubs afterwards.

## What fills the 60 acres

Most visits loop the lake first. From there, the park splits into distinct zones:

- **The lake:** the visual centre, with waterfowl and benches around the edge.
- **Sports areas:** pitches and courts that local clubs and casual players use.
- **Play areas:** fenced spaces for younger children.
- **Open grass:** wide lawns that fill with picnics and ball games when the weather allows.

An unhurried loop of the perimeter and lake takes about an hour. Allow longer if you have kids at the playground or you're watching a match.

## Carnival weekend and other big days

The park is best known for the Manchester Caribbean Carnival. It has grown out of the Moss Side community and is held here each August, with sound systems, food stalls and costumed processions. On carnival weekend the park works as a festival site rather than a quiet green space, so check the official carnival website for dates, access and road closures.

Outside carnival, the park's character comes from regular local use. You'll see joggers, dog walkers, families and five-a-side games. It is well-known across south Manchester rather than tucked away, and on a sunny weekend it shows.

## Timing your walk around Alexandra Park

Gates are listed as open 6am to midnight, seven days a week. That leaves plenty of room to avoid the busy periods:

- **Weekdays:** quiet across the whole 7am–11pm window, so almost any time suits.
- **Weekends:** also quiet from 7am–11pm by our crowd data, but the busiest stretch is 8am–6pm. Try not to arrive mid-morning or mid-afternoon on a Saturday or Sunday if you want an empty bench by the lake.
- **Early starts:** 6am to 8am at weekends gives you the paths before the crowds build.

A few habits help you blend in. Keep dogs under control near the play areas and the lake birds. Use the bins, or take your litter home after a picnic, because the lawns get heavy use. Cyclists share paths with walkers, so keep left and stay alert on the main promenades.

Getting there is simplest by bus. Services along Princess Road run between the city centre and the park's eastern edge. If you drive, look for street parking on the residential roads around Russell Street (M16 7JL) and read the signs, because restrictions vary by street.

After dark, the open lawns are poorly lit compared with the main paths. Stick to the promenades for an evening walk, and leave well before the midnight close.
