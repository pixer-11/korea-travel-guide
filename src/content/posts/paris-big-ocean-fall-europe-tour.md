## Three voices, two languages on stage

Big Ocean is not a typical K-pop boy group. The trio of Chanyeon, PJ and Jiseok debuted in Korea in 2024 as a group whose members are hearing-impaired. Sign language is built into their choreography, so the hands carry the lyrics as much as the voices do.

That changes what you watch for. Gestures land on the beat, and lines of Korean Sign Language or American Sign Language turn up inside the dance breaks. On October 31, 2026, that show comes to the Alhambra in Paris as one stop on the group's Fall Europe Tour.

## The Alhambra on rue Yves Toudic

The name is old Paris music-hall history. Today's Alhambra is a mid-sized concert room on rue Yves Toudic, a narrow street a few minutes' walk from Place de la République. It's close enough to the canal that most people walk the last stretch along the water.

The scale is the main draw. Big arena tours put you a long way from the stage. This room puts you close enough to read hands and faces, which matters for a group whose performance depends on both.

Expect a standing floor, which French venues call *la fosse*. Your sightline depends on how far forward you get and how tall the people in front of you are. Smaller fans usually drift to the sides, where the floor is less packed and the view across the stage is often clearer.

## Getting to the 10th arrondissement

The metro gets you closest, and every route ends with a short walk.

- **Jacques Bonsergent (Line 5):** the nearest station, a short walk east toward the canal.
- **République (Lines 3, 5, 8, 9, 11):** a big interchange with more exits. Leave on the north-east side of the square and head for rue du Faubourg du Temple or the canal.
- **Gare de l'Est and Gare du Nord:** walkable for anyone arriving by train from London, Brussels or eastern France.
- **Vélib' bikes:** docks line the canal, though the stands near République empty fast on busy evenings.

Driving makes little sense here. Street parking in the 10th is scarce, and the canal-side streets are tight. Most fans travelling from outside Paris stay near République, Gare de l'Est or along the canal, so the walk home after the encore is a short one.

## Halloween falls on show night

The date carries more weight than usual. October 31, 2026 is a Saturday and Halloween. Sunday 1 November is Toussaint (All Saints' Day), a public holiday in France.

That has a few practical effects:

- **The neighbourhood is busy.** The bars along Canal Saint-Martin and around République are full on any Saturday, and Halloween adds costumed groups to the pavements.
- **It's dark early.** France has turned its clocks back by then, so night falls by early evening, long before the show finishes.
- **Sunday runs on holiday hours.** Many shops close on Toussaint and public transport keeps a reduced timetable. Fans heading out of Paris the next day often take that into account.
- **Costumes turn up.** K-pop crowds in Europe often dress up on Halloween dates. A coordinated outfit or a nod to a Big Ocean concept fits in, but anything bulky makes a crowded standing floor harder work.

## Inside a K-pop night at the Alhambra

Standing K-pop shows in Paris draw a pavement queue well before doors open. Places on the floor go roughly in the order people come through the door. Fans who care about being at the barrier are usually the ones lined up along rue Yves Toudic in the afternoon, with snacks and a phone battery pack.

Security follows the same routine as every Paris venue under the national Vigipirate rules. Staff search bags at the entrance and sometimes pat people down. Small bags move through fastest. Large backpacks and anything resembling a tool tend to cause delays.

A few habits make the night go smoothly:

- **Pay by card.** Contactless works almost everywhere in Paris, including at venue bars, and tipping at the bar is not expected.
- **Use the cloakroom.** It's called the *vestiaire*. A coat is a burden in a hot standing room even on a cool October night.
- **Learn the visual applause.** In Deaf communities, people raise both hands and twist them in the air instead of clapping. Many Big Ocean fans do it at shows, and it fits this band in a way plain cheering does not.
- **Learn a line or two.** A short signed "thank you" or "I love you" held up toward the stage tends to be seen and answered.
- **Bring your lightstick, keep phones low.** Lightsticks are part of the culture. A phone held high for the whole set blocks the people behind you, and in a room this size they notice.

Venue staff speak French first and usually manage basic English. "*Bonsoir*" before any question gets a warmer reply than starting in English.

## Before doors and after the encore

The canal is the obvious place to fill time. Fans who aren't queuing usually spend it on the stretch of Canal Saint-Martin between the iron footbridges and the locks near rue de Lancry. The quays are lined with cafés, wine bars and quick-food counters. Sitting on the stone edge of the canal with a takeaway is completely normal here.

Place de la République is the other meeting point. It's a wide open square with the statue of Marianne in the middle, easy to find, and the obvious spot to regroup with friends after the crowd spills out. From there, Lines 3, 5, 8, 9 and 11 spread across the city.

For food after the show, the streets around Faubourg du Temple and the edge of Belleville have late-opening counters and restaurants. On a Halloween Saturday they are busy but rarely closed.
