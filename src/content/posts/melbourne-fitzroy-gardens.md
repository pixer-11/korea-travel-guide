## Elm avenues on a flag-shaped plan

Look at a map before you walk in. The main paths cross the lawns in a pattern resembling the Union Jack, a Victorian-era layout that still shapes how you move through the park. Mature elms line those diagonals and form long, shaded tunnels in summer. In autumn they turn yellow and drop their leaves across the gravel.

This is one of Melbourne's most visited green spaces. It sits on the eastern edge of the CBD grid, so office workers, tour groups and wedding parties all share it. The park itself never closes. The buildings inside keep their own hours.

## Five stops worth walking to

The gardens are compact enough to cover in a single loop. These are the fixed points to aim for:

- **Cooks' Cottage**: the Yorkshire family home of Captain James Cook's parents. It was shipped from Great Ayton, England, and rebuilt here in 1934. It has a cottage garden and an interior you pay to enter.
- **The Conservatory**: a Spanish Mission-style glasshouse from 1930 with seasonal floral displays that are changed through the year.
- **Tudor Village**: a miniature English village given by the people of Lambeth, London, as thanks for food parcels Melbourne sent after the Second World War.
- **The Fairies' Tree**: an old red gum stump carved with fairies, gnomes and Australian animals by sculptor Ola Cohn in the 1930s.
- **River God fountain and the fern gully**: a cooler, damper corner of the park, good for a slower lap.

## Beating the weekend crowds

Timing matters more here than anywhere else in East Melbourne. Weekends are busiest from 10am to 7pm, when picnic groups take over the lawns and queues build at Cooks' Cottage. Arrive between 7am and 8am on a Saturday or Sunday and the elm avenues are mostly empty. Joggers and dog walkers will be your only company.

On weekdays the quietest time is 10pm to 11pm. That suits a late walk under the trees, but the cottage and Conservatory will be shut.

Some practical habits:

- Check the City of Melbourne website for current Cooks' Cottage and Conservatory hours and ticketing before you go. Prices are posted there and at the cottage door.
- The Conservatory is a popular wedding-photo backdrop. Give shoots space and keep moving through the glasshouse.
- Melbourne runs on contactless card payment. Park cafés and kiosks expect a tap rather than cash, and tipping is not expected.
- Bring your own picnic. The lawns between the diagonals are where locals spread out, and you can sit on the grass anywhere it isn't fenced for reseeding.

## Getting in and moving on

The address is Wellington Parade, East Melbourne. Parliament Station on the City Loop is the closest train stop: take the Macarthur Street exit and walk east a few minutes. Jolimont Station sits just past the park's southeast corner. Trams 48 and 75 run along Wellington Parade on the southern edge, and the free City Circle tram runs nearby on Spring Street.

From here, Treasury Gardens sits just across Lansdowne Street to the west. St Patrick's Cathedral is a short walk north on Cathedral Place. Head south through Yarra Park and you reach the Melbourne Cricket Ground in about 15 minutes, which makes the gardens an easy walk before a match.
