## A Turkish rapper's first night in Uzbekistan

LVBEL C5 has never played Uzbekistan before. On December 9, 2026, he plays Humo Arena in Tashkent, and it is his first concert anywhere in the country.

That makes this a debut, not a tour stop on a familiar route. Most of the crowd has only heard these tracks through headphones and phone speakers. Hearing them through a full arena rig, with several thousand people shouting the hooks back, is a different experience.

## Why the lyrics land in Tashkent

Turkish and Uzbek are both Turkic languages. They share a large stock of basic vocabulary and the same grammar built from stacked suffixes, so many Uzbek listeners catch more of a Turkish verse than an outsider would expect.

Turkish music and TV series have also had a long run in Uzbekistan. A Turkish rapper drawing an arena crowd here makes sense. Expect many of the fans to know the choruses word for word, and some to know entire verses.

Visitors who speak neither language still get the show. Rap crowds communicate through rhythm: the bounce on the drop, the call-and-response, the phone torches during slower tracks.

## The venue: Humo Arena

Humo Arena is Tashkent's large indoor arena. It was built for ice hockey and is home to the city's professional hockey team, and it also hosts major concerts and touring acts.

For a concert, the ice is covered and the floor turns into a standing or seated area in front of the stage. Tiered seating rises around it on the other sides. As in most hockey-style bowls, the upper tiers give a full view of the stage and lights. The floor is where the energy is.

- **Floor:** closest to the stage and the most physical part of a rap show
- **Lower tiers:** a clear view and a seat to come back to
- **Upper tiers:** a wide view of the production, further from the artist

The exact layout and ticket categories for this concert come from the promoter, not the venue.

## Getting to Humo Arena

Tashkent is easy to cross in the evening. The city has two main options for concertgoers.

The **Tashkent Metro** is cheap, frequent and famous for its decorated Soviet-era stations, many of them lined with marble, mosaics and chandeliers. It is a sensible way to beat road traffic on the way in.

**Yandex Go** is the ride-hailing app most people in Tashkent use. Fares are set in the app, so there is no haggling. After a big arena show, though, hundreds of people request cars at once. Many attendees walk a few streets away from the arena and request their car from there, where pickups are easier.

Visitors flying in arrive at Tashkent International Airport (Islam Karimov), a short drive from the city centre. Most international guests stay in central Tashkent around Amir Temur Square, which puts the metro and plenty of hotels within reach.

## Concert night etiquette in Tashkent

A few local habits make the evening smoother.

- **Payment:** Uzbekistan runs on the som. Local cards (Uzcard and Humo) are everywhere. International Visa and Mastercard work at many hotels and larger venues but not at every food stall or kiosk, so visitors usually carry some som in cash.
- **Getting in:** Arena concerts here involve bag checks and security lines at the doors. Fans travelling light get through fastest, and most people come to the doors with their e-ticket or printed ticket already open.
- **Dress for December:** Tashkent winters are cold, often hovering around freezing in the evening. Inside it is warm, so layers beat a single heavy coat, especially on the floor.
- **Phones:** Filming is common at rap shows here, as everywhere. Fans on the floor usually keep it to a chorus or two so the people behind them can see.

Uzbekistan is a socially conservative country, but arena concerts are relaxed. Normal concert clothing is fine. Public drinking is a different matter from what some European visitors are used to, so most of the night's energy comes from the music itself.

## Making a trip of it

Many visitors from abroad turn a Tashkent concert into a longer stay. The city rewards a day or two either side of the show.

- **Chorsu Bazaar:** the big domed market in the old city, selling spices, dried fruit, bread and nuts
- **Khast Imam Complex:** the religious heart of old Tashkent, with madrasas and a library holding an early Quran manuscript
- **Plov:** Uzbekistan's national rice dish, traditionally cooked in huge kazan pots and usually eaten at lunchtime

Samarkand and Bukhara are both reachable by high-speed Afrosiyob train, so plenty of travellers fold the concert into a wider Silk Road route.
