## Two nights on the Rangsit campus

NCT 127's Bangkok stop isn't in Bangkok. The NEO CITY: THE REDLINE world tour plays **Thammasat Stadium** in Pathum Thani province on **October 31 and November 1, 2026**. That's two consecutive nights at a university sports ground out on the city's northern edge.

The address changes how the day works. You aren't stepping off the BTS onto a stadium concourse. You're crossing a big campus of lecture halls, dormitories and wide car parks, and the stadium bowl rises out of it. Getting there and getting home take as much planning as the setlist does for most fans.

## Where Thammasat Stadium actually sits

The stadium is on Thammasat University's **Rangsit campus** in Khlong Luang district. That's roughly 40 kilometres north of central Bangkok, just off Phahonyothin Road. It was built for the **1998 Asian Games**, which Bangkok hosted. It's a proper athletics venue: an oval running track around the pitch and a covered main grandstand facing open stands.

A few landmarks help you get your bearings:

- **Don Mueang International Airport** is the closest airport, south of the campus along the same corridor.
- **Future Park Rangsit** and its neighbour **Zpell** form one of the biggest mall complexes in the north of the city. Fans often use them as a place to meet up, eat and wait out the afternoon.
- **Rangsit**, the commercial district around the old canal junction, has most of the nearby hotels.

## The Red Line to THE REDLINE

The coincidence is easy to spot. Bangkok's suburban rail line is also called the Red Line. The **SRT Dark Red Line** runs north from Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal (Bang Sue). It stops at Don Mueang and ends at **Rangsit station**, so it's the fastest way out of the city before the evening traffic builds.

Here's the catch: it ends at Rangsit, not at the stadium. From Rangsit station the campus is still a road trip north. Fans usually finish the journey by Grab, Bolt or metered taxi, or share a ride with friends they've met in fan group chats. The line connects with the MRT Blue Line at Bang Sue. That makes it a sensible route from anywhere in the city.

Plenty of fans skip the train altogether. Ride-hailing straight from central Bangkok is simple, but on a concert evening Phahonyothin Road and the Vibhavadi expressway corridor fill up quickly. Travel times stretch well beyond the off-peak estimate your app shows.

## Inside the bowl on concert night

This is an open-air stadium, and that matters at the end of October. Bangkok's rainy season is winding down, but evening showers still happen. Only the main grandstand has a roof. Fans in the open stands and on the floor usually carry a thin poncho instead of an umbrella, since umbrellas block the view and stadium staff often don't allow them.

Even after dark, the air stays warm and humid. Compact handheld fans, cooling towels and refillable water bottles are standard kit for Thai concertgoers, for when the venue's rules allow them. Seating, gate allocation and bag rules depend on how the stage is set up for each tour. They're printed on your ticket and in the promoter's entry guidelines, not in this guide.

When the lights go down, the stands turn green. NCTzens bring the official NCT lightstick, and the sync-controlled ones change colour together across the stadium during the show.

## Show day the Thai NCTzen way

Thai K-pop fandom runs a show day almost like a festival. Knowing how it works saves you time and money.

- **Merch (MD) queues** start long before doors. Fans usually split up, with one friend in the merch line and another holding a spot near the gate.
- **Freebies** are a big part of the culture. Fans set up outside the venue and hand out photocards, stickers, fans and snacks they've made themselves. Carry a small bag for what you're given. A simple thank-you, *khop khun kha* (women) or *khop khun khrap* (men), goes a long way.
- **Paying** is mostly by **PromptPay QR** or cash. Snack and drink stalls around the campus may not take foreign cards, so small Thai baht notes make things easier.
- **Ride-hailing** apps are what almost everyone uses for the last stretch. Pickup points jam up after the show, so fans often walk a little way out of the crowd before they book.

Queues are taken seriously. Cutting in, or saving places for a big group that turns up late, gets a frosty reaction. Thai fans are also known for cleaning up after themselves, and they often carry their rubbish out of the stands when the show ends.

## After the encore

The hardest part of a Thammasat show is getting home. Tens of thousands of people leave a suburban campus at the same moment, and there's no rail station at the gate. Expect a wait.

Fans tend to handle it in one of three ways:

- **Wait it out.** Some stay on campus or head to an all-night spot in Rangsit until the ride-hailing surge dies down, then book a car.
- **Pre-arrange a driver.** Groups often hire a private driver or van for the round trip and agree on a meeting point away from the main gates.
- **Stay close.** Fans going on both nights often book a hotel in Rangsit or near Future Park. That way the trip home is a short ride instead of an hour on the expressway.

The last Red Line trains of the night may have gone by the time you reach Rangsit station. Many fans treat the train as a way to get there and don't count on it for the ride back.

## Where fans base themselves

There are two choices, and each has a trade-off. **Central Bangkok** gives you nightlife, food and the full city for the days around the show. The price is a long commute. **Rangsit and Pathum Thani** put you minutes from the stadium, with mall food courts and local restaurants nearby, but there's less to do in the evenings.

International fans flying into **Don Mueang** sometimes split the difference. They spend concert nights up north, then move into the city to see the rest of Bangkok. With two shows on back-to-back nights, plenty of fans go to both. Staying near Rangsit makes the second evening far less tiring.
