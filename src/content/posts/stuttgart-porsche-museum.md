## A white block on three legs

Look up as you leave the S-Bahn platform. A huge angular white building, opened in 2009, sits on three V-shaped concrete supports above Porscheplatz, so the exhibition halls appear to hover over the roundabout. Vienna firm Delugan Meissl designed it. Across the road is Werk 1, the Zuffenhausen plant where Porsche still builds sports cars.

You enter at street level, under the floating mass. An escalator carries you up into the exhibition hall, which is one open, bright space with sloping floors. Polished cars sit on low white plinths.

## What's inside

The collection follows Porsche from the early 20th century to today's race cars. Only part of the fleet is on show at any one time, and the selection changes, so treat these as likely highlights rather than a guarantee:

- **The Lohner-Porsche "P1"**, Ferdinand Porsche's electric carriage from 1898
- **The 356 "No. 1" Roadster**, the 1948 car that started the brand
- **911 generations**, which together trace the evolution of the rear-engined silhouette
- **The 917**, Porsche's Le Mans winner, plus a wall of later Le Mans and endurance racers
- **Themed displays** on engines, design studies and the company's founding family

On the ground floor, glass walls look into the museum workshop, where technicians maintain historic cars that are still driven. The Christophorus restaurant sits up in the building too. Check its hours separately, because they don't follow the museum's.

## Getting to Zuffenhausen

From Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof, take the S6 toward Weil der Stadt. Get off at Neuwirtshaus (Porscheplatz), roughly ten minutes out. The museum stands directly across from the station exit.

Driving works too, since there is a car park underneath the building. The Mercedes-Benz Museum is on the other side of the city in Untertürkheim. Many visitors pair the two, but they are 30–40 minutes apart by public transport, so plan on one each half of the day.

## Beating the weekend wave

The museum is one of Stuttgart's best-known attractions, and the crowds show it. Weekends between 11am and 5pm are when it is busiest, with school groups and families filling the escalator and the lines for tickets.

- **Go on a weekday** if you can. Tuesday to Friday it opens at 9am, and arriving at opening usually puts you ahead of the tour coaches.
- **Don't come on Monday.** It is closed all day.
- **Buy tickets online** from the official Porsche Museum site. Current prices are posted there and at the foyer counter. Card payment is standard.
- **Factory tours** of Werk 1 run separately and must be booked in advance through the museum. Don't expect to join one on the day.
- **Walk the hall in date order.** The layout is built as a timeline, so starting at the oldest cars makes the 911 section easier to follow.
- **Leave time for the shop** on the way out. It sells scale models, books and branded clothing, and it fills up at the same times as the galleries.

The last admissions come before the 6pm close. On a weekend, arriving in the late afternoon gives you the end of the crowd curve but very little time. A full visit runs two to three hours, so a weekday morning remains the better plan.
