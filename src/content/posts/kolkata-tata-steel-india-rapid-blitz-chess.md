## Five days of fast chess

The clocks here move quickly. Tata Steel India Rapid & Blitz Chess runs in Kolkata from January 7 to 11, 2026. It is the Indian sister event to the long-running Tata Steel Chess tournament in Wijk aan Zee, and it uses the two fast formats instead of classical play.

Rapid games come first. Blitz follows, where each player has only minutes for the whole game and the last moves often go to whoever has more seconds left. Each year the field includes top international grandmasters, with former world champion Viswanathan Anand among the names the tournament draws. Recent editions have also run a women's section next to the open event.

## Getting into the hall

Tata Steel organises the tournament and publishes the venue, round times and entry details on its official tournament site. Recent editions were staged at Dhono Dhanye Auditorium in Alipore, in south Kolkata, a large modern hall near Alipore Zoo.

Alipore has no metro station of its own. Most spectators use app cabs or the city's yellow Ambassador taxis. The nearest stops on Metro Line 1 (Blue Line) are Rabindra Sadan and Jatin Das Park, each a short taxi ride away. January is Kolkata's driest and coolest month, so evenings near the Maidan are mild enough to walk for a while after the last round.

## Etiquette in the playing hall

Watching elite chess is mostly a silent business. These are the usual conventions:

- **Phones go on silent or off.** A ringing phone near a board can cost a player the game under FIDE rules, and spectators are held to the same expectation.
- **No flash photography.** Photographers usually get a short window at the start of a round, then leave.
- **Talk happens outside.** Analysis, predictions and arguments belong in the foyer or the commentary area, not the playing zone.
- **Watch the screens.** Spectators usually follow the moves on large displays with live boards, since the tables themselves sit behind a barrier.
- **Pay digitally.** UPI apps such as Google Pay and PhonePe are standard at Kolkata food stalls and cafés, and cash still works almost everywhere.

Blitz days are usually the loudest, from the foyer at least. Rounds are short and the turnover is fast, so fans tend to come for the final rounds of the closing session, when the standings are settled.

## Around the venue in Alipore

Alipore is one of Kolkata's greener, quieter districts, and it makes an easy half-day alongside the chess.

- **Alipore Zoological Garden**: one of India's oldest zoos, open since 1876, close to the auditorium.
- **National Library at Belvedere Estate**: the former residence of the Lieutenant-Governor of Bengal, set in wide grounds.
- **Victoria Memorial**: the white marble hall at the southern edge of the Maidan, a short drive north.
- **Kalighat Temple**: Kolkata's most important Kali temple, a few minutes south by taxi.

Park Street, with its old restaurants and bakeries, is a common dinner stop for visitors staying central. Chess fans from abroad usually stay around Park Street or Esplanade, where hotels sit close to the Blue Line metro.
