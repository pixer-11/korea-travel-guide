## A bookshop where Brighton turns into Hove

Western Road changes its mind halfway along. At the Clock Tower end it is all chain shops, phone cases and crowds heading for Churchill Square. Keep walking west and the shopfronts narrow and the traffic eases. Somewhere past the city boundary the street turns into Hove.

City Books sits on this quieter stretch at number 23. It is a bookshop, plainly and completely, with no café or gift range advertised in its name. Reviewers rate it consistently highly, yet relatively few visitors to Brighton ever walk far enough west to find it.

That is the appeal. You can browse here without the elbow-to-elbow feel of the Lanes on a summer weekend.

## Finding number 23 Western Road

The address reads Hove BN3 1AF, which tells you which side of the line you are on. The shop sits in the run of buildings between the Brighton end of Western Road and Palmeira Square. From there the road carries on west and becomes Church Road, Hove's main shopping street.

Look south down the side streets as you walk. The cream-painted terraces of Brunswick Town start almost immediately, and the sea sits at the bottom of each road. That view is your landmark: if you can see the Regency terraces, you are close.

## Getting there without a car

Parking along Western Road is tight and largely metered, so most people arrive on foot or by bus.

- **From Brighton station:** walk down Queen's Road to the Clock Tower, then head west along Western Road. Allow around 25 minutes at a steady pace.
- **From Hove station:** walk south through the residential streets toward the sea. It takes roughly 15 minutes.
- **By bus:** a large share of Brighton & Hove's routes run along Western Road. Ask for stops near Palmeira Square or Brunswick Square.
- **From the seafront:** climb any street north from Hove Lawns, such as the roads either side of Brunswick Square, and you reach Western Road in a few minutes.

## Buying a book the Hove way

An independent bookshop works on simple, slightly old-fashioned courtesies. Most of them are about the shop staying in business.

- **Pay at the counter by card or phone.** Contactless is the norm across British shops, so you will rarely need cash.
- **Don't photograph covers to buy online later.** It is the one habit that reliably irritates booksellers. If a title isn't in stock, ask whether they can order it in. Many UK independents do, often within a few days.
- **Ask for a recommendation.** Booksellers in small shops usually know the shelves well. Give them a book you liked and see where they send you.
- **Mind your bag.** Small shops have tight aisles, so carry backpacks in your hand rather than on your back.
- **Book tokens:** if you are carrying a UK book token or gift card, ask at the till whether it is accepted rather than assuming.

Browsing in silence is fine. Nobody expects you to buy on your first visit, but leaving with something is how places like this survive.

## Working around the short Sunday

The hours are generous on weekdays. At the moment City Books opens at 9:30am Monday to Saturday and closes at 5:30pm. Sunday runs shorter, from 11am to 4:30pm, so a late Sunday lunch on the seafront can easily eat your window.

The early weekday opening is worth knowing about. Get there at 9:30am and you can browse before your first coffee, then walk down to the beach while the rest of Brighton is still waking up. Hours can change around bank holidays and Christmas, so check before a special trip.

## Brunswick Town, two minutes south

The best thing about this address is what lies just below it. Brunswick Town is a planned Regency estate built in the 1820s and 1830s. Its showpiece is Brunswick Square, a long U of cream stucco terraces with bow fronts and balconies that opens onto the sea.

Take your new book there. Benches line the gardens and the lawns along the seafront. The Regency Town House at 13 Brunswick Square is a heritage project restoring one of the terrace houses. It opens on selected days, so check its own site before you plan around it.

Continue west along the coast and you reach Adelaide Crescent and Palmeira Square. These are some of the grandest stretches of stucco in the city, and they are rarely crowded.

## A slow afternoon from the shop to the sea

The whole outing fits into a relaxed half day. Here is one way to put it together:

1. **Start at City Books.** Allow 30–45 minutes if you like to read first pages before you choose.
2. **Walk south through Brunswick Square** to the promenade.
3. **Head east along the seafront** toward the skeletal remains of the West Pier. It is about a 15-minute stroll, with the beach huts and Hove Lawns behind you.
4. **Or head west** along the Hove promenade toward the colourful beach huts. This stretch is calmer and better for actually reading on a bench.
5. **Loop back up Church Road** if you want more of Hove's independent shops and cafés before heading home.

Brighton's famous sights stay within reach without taking over. The Lanes, the Royal Pavilion and the Palace Pier are all east along Western Road and North Street, a straightforward walk once you have finished on the quieter side of town.
