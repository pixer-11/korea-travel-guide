## A wall of white stupas on the steppe

The wall comes into view first. It is a long, low square of whitewashed stupas strung along the flat grass of the Orkhon Valley, with green-tiled temple roofs rising inside. Tradition gives the wall 108 stupas, a sacred number in Buddhism.

This is Mongolia's earliest surviving Buddhist monastery, founded in 1585 by Abtai Sain Khan. It is also one of the very few that came through the 1939 purges partly intact. Most of what stood here was destroyed. What survived is the reason you came this far.

## Erdenet or Kharkhorin? Getting your bearings

First, a correction to the listing. Erdene Zuu is not in Erdenet, the copper-mining city in the north. It sits on the edge of **Kharkhorin**, a small town in Övörkhangai province in central Mongolia. Someone who sets off for Erdenet expecting the monastery will be several hours' drive away from it.

Most visitors come from Ulaanbaatar. The trip is roughly 360 km west on paved road, which usually means five to six hours by car. Public buses to Kharkhorin leave from the Dragon bus terminal on the west side of the capital. Most travellers, though, arrive on a driver-and-guide tour that combines the monastery with the Orkhon Valley.

Once you are in Kharkhorin, the monastery is at the southern edge of town. You can walk there from most guesthouses and ger camps.

## Three temples, a gold stupa and Karakorum's stones

The monastery was built on the site of Karakorum, the Mongol Empire's capital under Ögedei Khan in the 13th century. Much of its stone was taken from the ruined city. It was rebuilt and expanded in the 18th century, and in 2004 it became part of the UNESCO-listed Orkhon Valley Cultural Landscape.

Inside the wall, the main buildings to find are:

- **The three Zuu temples** (Baruun, Dund and Zuun: West, Central and East), built in Chinese style with green-glazed roofs. Inside are painted statues, thangkas and masks.
- **The Golden Prayer Stupa** (1799), which pilgrims circle.
- **Lavrin Süm**, the white Tibetan-style temple where resident monks hold services.
- **Wide open grass** where dozens of other temples stood before 1939. The empty space between the buildings shows you how much was lost.

## The 9am–10am window

Timing matters more here than you might expect. The monastery is still under the radar by world-heritage standards, but tour minivans out of Ulaanbaatar tend to arrive in clusters. The crowd figures are clear: the calmest hour is 9am–10am, on weekdays and weekends alike.

The squeeze is 11am–2pm at weekends. That is when groups line up at the temple doors and the inner courtyard fills. If you stay overnight in Kharkhorin, be at the gate close to the current 9am opening. You will get the Zuu temples almost to yourself.

Opening hours are currently 9am–6pm every day of the week. Hours at heritage sites like this one shift with the season, and winter on the steppe is long. Check the official listing or ask at your guesthouse before you plan around a late visit.

## Walking clockwise with the pilgrims

Erdene Zuu is a museum and an active monastery at the same time. Behave as you would at any working temple.

- **Walk clockwise.** That goes for circling the Golden Stupa, turning prayer wheels and walking around temple interiors. Mongolian pilgrims always move this way.
- **Take your hat off** when you go into a temple, and keep your voice down if a service is running in Lavrin Süm.
- **Don't point your feet** at altars or monks if you sit down. Don't touch the statues.
- **Ask about photography at the entrance.** Rules for shooting inside the temples differ from the grounds, and permission may be sold separately. The current terms are posted at the ticket point.
- **Dress modestly,** with shoulders and knees covered. It is respectful, and practical in the wind.

Local guides at the gate can take you through the temples in English. If you have no Mongolian, they are the easiest way to follow the iconography. Ask at the entrance what is on offer that day.

## Karakorum beyond the wall

Plan on keeping the afternoon free. Very little of the imperial city is still standing, but enough survives to fill a day.

- **The stone turtle**, just outside the monastery wall. It is one of the granite turtles that once marked the boundaries of Karakorum.
- **Kharakhorum Museum**, a short walk away in town. It has finds from the excavated capital and a scale model of the city.
- **The Mongol Empire monument** on the ridge above town. Three large ceramic panels map the empires of different eras, and the view takes in the whole valley.
- **The Phallic Rock**, a stone carving on the hillside outside town. Visitors tend to find it a funny side trip.

## Planning the day

Allow two to three hours inside the walls. That is long enough for the three Zuu temples, Lavrin Süm and a slow lap of the stupa wall. Add another half day for the museum and the monuments.

The Orkhon Valley is open, high ground, so the wind finds you easily even in summer. Bring a layer, sun protection and water, because there is little shade between the temples. Gravel and grass cover most of the grounds, so wear shoes you can walk comfortably in.

Staying a night in Kharkhorin is the one choice that fixes almost everything. You can be inside at 9am, leave before the midday rush, and drive on into the Orkhon Valley rested instead of after a dawn start from Ulaanbaatar.
