## Two venues in one night

Billie Jean works in shifts. Earlier in the evening it is a stylish cocktail bar, a place to sit and talk with a drink. Later the music goes up and the same room becomes a late-night club.

That split is the main reason to go. You can start with conversation and stay for the dancing without moving across Seomyeon at midnight. The place opened recently, and local word already puts it among Busan's hottest clubs. A reputation like that tends to bring bigger crowds as the weeks go on.

## Seomyeon after dark

Seomyeon is Busan's main downtown nightlife district. It sits in Busanjin-gu, between the old port area and the northern suburbs. The streets around Seomyeon Station fill with Korean barbecue restaurants, pojangmacha-style drinking tents, karaoke rooms and multi-storey bars, often stacked several to a building.

This matters for planning. You will never be far from dinner or a second stop. Seomyeon Food Alley and Bujeon Market are both close for an early meal. The Jeonpo café street, a short walk away, is good for a late coffee before the bar fills up.

## Finding the door

Seomyeon Station is the transfer point for Busan Metro Line 1 (orange) and Line 2 (green). That makes it one of the easiest stations in the city to reach from Haeundae, Nampo-dong or Busan Station. The underground shopping arcade has many numbered exits, so choose your exit before you surface.

## Getting in at a Seomyeon club

Korean clubs run on a few rules that catch visitors out. Expect these here as well:

- **Bring your passport.** Doors check ID, and a foreign driver's licence is not always accepted. Korea's legal drinking age is 19, calculated by birth year rather than birthday.
- **Dress up a notch.** Seomyeon clubs often turn away sportswear and flip-flops. Smart-casual is the safe choice.
- **Confirm reservations ahead.** A table policy or entry rules can change once a place gets busy. Message the venue through its social channels before you go.
- **Don't tip.** Tipping isn't customary in Korea. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, and many places also take Korean mobile payments.

Any entry charge and the drink prices should be listed at the door or on the menu. Check them there, not in an older online post.

## Getting home

Busan Metro stops running around midnight, which is well before most club nights end. Look up the last train from Seomyeon for your line before you head in. Otherwise plan for a taxi.

Taxis wait along the main roads around Seomyeon Station late at night. The Kakao T app lets you book one with a pinned pickup point, which helps when the street is busy at closing time. If your hotel is in Haeundae, the ride crosses the city. Keep your hotel's address saved in Korean to show the driver.
