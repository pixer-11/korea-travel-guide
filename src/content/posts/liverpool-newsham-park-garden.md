## A Victorian park on the city's east side

Newsham Park was planned around one big idea: open space. Laid out in the 1860s and founded in 1868, it was one of a ring of parks Liverpool built so its fast-growing neighbourhoods could breathe. You walk in from Gardner's Drive onto broad tarmac paths, with mature trees on each side and lawns running down to the water.

It isn't a formal garden with clipped beds around every corner. It's a working neighbourhood park on a generous Victorian scale. Dog walkers, joggers and families with buggies all use the same wide loops, and grand buildings look over the green from the edges.

## Around the lake

The lake is what most people come for. It holds fish, and anglers set up along the banks with rods, nets and folding chairs. Waterfowl gather near the paths, especially where people tend to stop.

A full loop of the water is the simplest way to get a feel for the place. Things to look out for on the way round:

- **The angling banks**: give people fishing some room and don't throw bread right beside their lines.
- **Open lawns**: big enough for a kickabout or a picnic blanket without crowding anyone.
- **The playground**: handy if you have children with you, and a good place to meet up.
- **Tree-lined avenues**: the old planting makes these the shadiest paths on a warm afternoon.

## The landmark buildings on the edge

The buildings that frame the park are a big part of its character. Victorian Liverpool liked to set large institutions and grand houses beside its green spaces, and Newsham is a clear example. Red brick and stone frontages rise above the treeline at several points around the perimeter.

The best known is the former Seamen's Orphanage, which later became Newsham Park Hospital, a large Gothic block next to the park. It is privately owned and is not open for wandering. View it from the public paths and don't climb fences. Elsewhere on the edges, Victorian villas show the kind of address the park was meant to give the area.

## Getting to Tuebrook

The park sits in L6, east of the city centre and between the Kensington and Tuebrook districts. It's an easy bus ride out from the centre along the main roads heading east towards West Derby. Use the Merseytravel journey planner to find the current route numbers and the nearest stop to Gardner's Drive.

On foot from Lime Street it's a walk of roughly 40 minutes, mostly through ordinary residential streets. There's on-street parking around the perimeter roads if you're driving. Read the signs carefully, because restrictions change from street to street.

## How Liverpool uses the park

This is a local park first, and the etiquette follows from that. Keep to the paths near the angling pegs. Dogs are everywhere, so keep yours under control near the playground and the wildfowl. Fishing has its own permit arrangements, so check with the city council before you bring a rod.

There's no admission charge or ticket desk. You just walk in. Facilities are limited, so bring your own drinks and snacks, and take your rubbish home if the bins are full. Mornings after the 7am opening tend to belong to runners and dog walkers. Sunny weekend afternoons draw families to the lawns and playground.

Treat it as a daytime walk. The listed hours run until 10pm, but the paths are long and some of the edges are quiet after dark. A circuit before dusk is the more comfortable choice for a first visit.

## Pairing it with Anfield and Stanley Park

Newsham fits neatly into a day in north and east Liverpool. Anfield, home of Liverpool FC, is a short bus ride or a walk of about half an hour north. Stanley Park is next to the stadium, and it's another Victorian park, which makes a good comparison with Newsham.

On a match day the whole district fills up, and buses and roads towards Anfield get very busy. If you'd rather have the paths to yourself, choose a non-match day, or come to Newsham in the morning before the crowds move north. Allow one to two hours for the park itself.
