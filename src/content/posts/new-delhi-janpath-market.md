## A pavement shop off the Connaught Place circle

Janpath is a shopping street, not a building. Walk south from the Connaught Place circle and the pavement narrows, with stalls packed along the kerb and small shopfronts behind them. Fabric hangs from awnings, and folded stacks spill onto trestle tables. Vendors call out to anyone who slows down.

This is one of Delhi's best-known and most-reviewed markets, so don't come expecting a discovery. Families shop here, office workers from Connaught Place come on their breaks, and backpackers work down the row with lists. That crowd is part of what makes it useful. There's a lot of stock, and there's plenty of competition over it.

## What fills the stalls

The range is wide, but it follows a pattern. Scan for these:

- **Clothing:** cotton kurtas, printed skirts, harem trousers and export-surplus shirts, often in piles you dig through
- **Textiles:** block-printed bedcovers, cushion covers, stoles and embroidered shawls
- **Mirror-work and embroidered bags** in the Gujarati and Rajasthani style
- **Tibetan-style goods:** silver and turquoise jewellery, prayer flags, singing bowls and thangka paintings, mostly at the stretch often called the Tibetan Market
- **Kashmiri crafts:** papier-mâché boxes and woollen shawls
- **Small souvenirs:** brass figurines, incense, fridge magnets, leather-bound notebooks

Quality runs from genuinely good to throwaway, sometimes on the same table. Rub the fabric between your fingers, check the seams and look inside bags for loose threads. Be sceptical when a seller says "pure pashmina" or "real silver".

## Reaching Janpath by metro

The metro beats sitting in Connaught Place traffic. Janpath Metro Station on the Violet Line leaves you at the southern end of the street. Rajiv Chowk, where the Yellow and Blue Lines cross, sits under Connaught Place, and from there it's a short walk to the northern end.

Auto-rickshaws and app cabs can drop you on Janpath Road. The pickup points are congested in the evenings, though, so walking to the metro is usually quicker. Give yourself at least 90 minutes to browse the whole strip without hurrying, and more if you want to shop seriously.

## Bargaining on Janpath without getting worn down

Haggling is expected at most stalls, and the first price is where the conversation starts. Plenty of locals begin well below it and walk away calmly if they don't get close. You don't need a script, but these habits help:

- Know what things cost first. The **Central Cottage Industries Emporium**, also on Janpath, sells crafts at fixed prices, and that makes it a good benchmark.
- Ask for the price of several items together. Bundling usually brings the price per piece down.
- Keep it friendly. A smile and "thoda kam karo?" ("make it a bit less?") goes further than acting offended.
- Carry small notes. Many stalls take UPI mobile payments, but a visitor without an Indian bank account will often find cash simpler.

Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket or a zipped bag, as you would in any crowded Delhi street. Touts may offer to take you to a "government shop" nearby. A polite no, and keep walking.

## When to go and when to stay away

Timing makes more difference here than anywhere else in Connaught Place. Janpath Market is open every day from 11am to 8pm. It's quietest from 11am to 1pm on weekdays and from 11am to 2pm on weekends. At those times vendors have room to talk, and you can walk the pavement without edging sideways.

Try not to arrive between 4pm and 8pm at weekends. That's when the crowds peak, the pavement jams and bargaining turns into shouting. Delhi's summer afternoons are harsh on an open street, so from October to March you can browse comfortably for longer.

## Around the corner from Janpath

The market sits in a dense patch of central Delhi, so it pairs well with other stops:

- **Connaught Place:** the Georgian-style colonnaded circle, with cafés and restaurants for a break
- **Palika Bazaar:** an underground market beneath the Connaught Place gardens, mostly electronics and clothing
- **Jantar Mantar:** the 18th-century astronomical observatory built by Maharaja Jai Singh II, a short walk west on Sansad Marg
- **Agrasen ki Baoli:** a stepwell off Hailey Road, east of Janpath
- **Hanuman Mandir** and the state emporiums on Baba Kharak Singh Marg, where each Indian state sells its crafts at fixed prices

A good half-day: Jantar Mantar in the morning, Janpath in the quieter late-morning window, then lunch in Connaught Place.
