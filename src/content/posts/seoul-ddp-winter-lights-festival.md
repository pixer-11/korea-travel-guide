## Light on a Building With No Corners

Dongdaemun Design Plaza has almost no straight lines. Zaha Hadid designed it, and it opened in 2014. Its long silver shell is made of tens of thousands of aluminium panels that bend from wall into roof without a visible seam. For the DDP Winter Lights Festival, from December 17 to 31, 2026, that shell becomes the screen.

Projections and illuminations run across the curves, so images stretch and fold as they travel along the facade. The format has drawn more than a million visitors since 2019. Because the surface is so long, people walking past see a different part of the picture than people standing still.

## Dates, Tickets and the Venue

- **Dates:** December 17–31, 2026, ending on New Year's Eve.
- **Venue:** Dongdaemun Design Plaza, 281 Eulji-ro, Jung-gu, Seoul.
- **Tickets and show times:** these come from the festival organiser. Nothing here confirms whether watching the facade needs a ticket, so no price or programme is given in this guide.

The building is open on every side, with plazas and walkways going around it. The surrounding Dongdaemun History & Culture Park adds paths and excavated foundations of the old city wall. People spread out across this space instead of packing into a single viewing area.

## Getting to Dongdaemun

The subway is the most direct way in. Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station is served by Line 2, Line 4 and Line 5. Exit 1 leads straight into the DDP complex, so you barely walk outside before you see the panels.

Dongdaemun Station (Lines 1 and 4) is one stop north. From there it is a short walk past Heunginjimun, the old East Gate of Seoul. The gate is lit at night too, and many visitors pair the two.

December evenings in Seoul are often below freezing, and the plaza is exposed to wind. Most visitors come in padded coats. They duck into the building's interior halls between viewings to warm up.

## Watching the Facade the Way Seoul Does

Seoul crowds at light shows tend to keep moving. Most people do a slow loop of the building rather than holding one spot. The curved walls look completely different from the park side than from the Eulji-ro side, so a full circuit is worth the cold.

Photo habits are polite but determined:

- Phones go up for short clips, then down again, so the people behind can see.
- Tripods appear at the edges of the plaza rather than in the walkways.
- The raised paths on the grassy roof give the widest angle over the lit panels.

Food is close by. The Dongdaemun wholesale fashion district runs late, and malls like Doota sit across the road with food courts. Gwangjang Market is about one subway stop west, and pojangmacha street stalls serve tteokbokki and fish-cake skewers in broth. Payment is almost always by card or T-money. Tipping is not part of Korean custom.

The final night, December 31, falls on New Year's Eve. Central Seoul is at its busiest then, and the trains around the countdown are the most packed of the year. Visitors who want room to watch usually come on a weeknight in the festival's first week.
