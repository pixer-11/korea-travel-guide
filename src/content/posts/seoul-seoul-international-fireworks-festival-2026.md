## A river used as a launch pad

The Han is wide at Yeouido. That width is the reason this show works. Barges sit out on the water, the glass towers of Yeouido stand behind the park, and the bridges on either side give the bursts something to frame them.

Hanwha Group has hosted the festival since 2000. It is the best-known fireworks show in Korea, built around international teams who each fire a set in turn. Reported crowds have run close to a million people, which tells you how the day goes: a big chunk of the city tries to fit onto one stretch of grass.

## October 3, 2026: a Saturday and a holiday

The 2026 edition is announced for **Saturday, October 3, 2026**. That date is also Gaecheonjeol, National Foundation Day, a public holiday in Korea. Expect families, office groups and couples all on the same riverbank, many of them claiming spots well before dark.

Exact timings, team line-ups and any reserved-seating arrangements are set by the organisers. Confirm timing, venue details and tickets on the official site before you plan the day around them. This guide gives no show times or prices because none were supplied.

## Getting to Yeouido without the crush

Take the subway. Roads around Yeouido are closed or jammed on festival night, and taxis are almost useless near the park.

- **Yeouinaru Station (Line 5)** opens almost straight onto Yeouido Hangang Park. It is the closest station and the most congested one.
- **Yeouido Station (Lines 5 and 9)** is a short walk inland. It is a sensible alternative when Yeouinaru is packed.
- **Mapo Station (Line 5)**, across the river, puts you on the north bank facing the show.

In past years, trains have sometimes skipped Yeouinaru during peak crowds. Have a second station in mind. Load a T-money card beforehand so you don't end up queuing at a ticket machine with everyone else.

## Where to watch along the Han

The main lawn at Yeouido Hangang Park gets you closest to the barges. It also fills first. Seoul has other good angles, though:

- **Ichon Hangang Park**, east along the south bank, looks back toward the show with fewer people packed in.
- **Nodeul Island**, in the middle of the river beneath Hangang Bridge, puts water on every side of you.
- **The north bank around Mapo** lets you watch the bursts over Yeouido's towers, which makes for strong photos.
- **The bridges** look tempting, but police often keep pedestrians moving, so don't plan to stand on one for the whole show.

Wherever you end up, an open view of the sky over the water matters more than being close.

## Claiming a patch of riverbank

People really do stake out their spots. The grass at Yeouido fills with picnic mats from the afternoon, and arriving shortly before the show usually means standing behind thousands of people. Bring a mat or a folding sheet. Convenience stores along the park sell them, along with drinks and snacks, but queues build fast.

Eating on the riverbank is normal. Fried chicken and beer (chimaek) is the classic Han River picnic, and delivery riders come to designated pickup points by the parks. Pay for that by app or card. Card and mobile payment work almost everywhere in Seoul, including at park convenience stores.

Some etiquette matters on a night this crowded:

- Keep your mat to the size of your group. People hold spots for friends, but sprawling across a wide area causes friction.
- Pack out your rubbish. Volunteers and bins are there, but people judge leaving litter on the grass harshly.
- Use the toilets early, because the lines grow long as the evening goes on.
- Bring a layer. October nights by the river turn cool once the sun goes down.

## After the last shell

Leaving is the hardest part of the night. Everyone heads for the same few stations at once, and staff set up crowd control at the entrances. If you can wait, stay on your mat for a while and let the first wave clear. Alternatively, walk inland to Yeouido Station or across Mapo Bridge to the north bank, where trains are less crowded.

Yeouido is also worth seeing by day. The 63 Building stands at the eastern end of the island, and The Hyundai Seoul department store is a short walk from Yeouido Station. Both are easy ways to fill the afternoon before you head down to the river.
