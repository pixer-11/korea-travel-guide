## Three nights at Hua Mak

BTS bring their 360° stadium World Tour 'ARIRANG' to Bangkok for three nights. The announced dates are December 3, 5 and 6, 2026, and all three are at Rajamangala National Stadium.

The tour takes its name from "Arirang", the Korean folk song that is close to an unofficial national anthem. For a group whose fanbase calls itself ARMY, the name signals a show built around where BTS come from. Start times, set details and support acts are not part of the announcement. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site.

## Rajamangala in the round

Rajamangala is Thailand's national stadium, a concrete bowl built for the 1998 Asian Games. It sits inside the Hua Mak Sports Complex on Bangkok's eastern side. It holds roughly 50,000 people, which makes it the city's default venue for the biggest touring acts.

A 360° stage changes how the bowl works. There is no "back of the stadium" behind the stage, because the stage is in the middle and the stands face it from every side. The upper tiers look down on the floor like a theatre in the round, and side sections are closer to the action than at a standard end-stage concert.

The stands are steep and largely uncovered. December evenings in Bangkok are usually dry, which helps an open-air show. The concrete still holds heat from the afternoon, so do not expect a chilly night.

## Getting to Bang Kapi and back

The stadium is a long way from Sukhumvit and Silom, and no rail line stops at its gates. Most fans use Hua Mak as their rail hub. The Airport Rail Link and the MRT Yellow Line both have stations there, and from there it is a taxi, motorbike taxi or long walk to the stadium.

Your options, roughly from most to least predictable:

- **Rail to Hua Mak, then the last stretch on foot or by motorbike taxi.** Slow, but you are not stuck in traffic for most of the trip.
- **Grab or Bolt straight to the stadium.** Easy going in. Coming out, surge pricing and gridlock on Ramkhamhaeng Road are normal after big shows.
- **A metered taxi.** Fine for the trip there. After the show, drivers often refuse the meter or refuse the trip.

The real problem is the end of the night. When 50,000 people leave at the same moment, the roads around the complex jam. A common tactic is to walk 15 to 20 minutes away from the stadium before booking a ride. Ramkhamhaeng University and the Hua Mak station area are the usual spots to head for.

## Tickets without getting burned

Buy only through the ticketing partner named on BTS's official tour announcements. The announcement does not give prices or seating maps here, so check the official site for the plan and price categories. In a 360° layout, "side" and "rear" sections are not the bad seats they would be at an end-stage show.

Resale scams around K-pop stadium shows in Bangkok are common. They usually involve screenshots of e-tickets, Facebook and X sellers asking for a bank transfer, and "transfer later" promises. If the official channel offers no named-ticket transfer, a ticket bought this way may not get you in. Bring the ID that matches your booking, because ID checks at the gate are routine for major K-pop tours.

## Concert night with Thai ARMY

Thai K-pop crowds have their own habits, and joining in is half the fun.

- **Bring the ARMY Bomb.** The official lightstick is usually synced wirelessly to the show, so the whole bowl changes colour together. Pair it the way the official app tells you, and register your seat number if the app asks for it.
- **Learn the fanchants.** The Korean fanchants for the main songs are published by the fandom, and Thai fans shout them word for word.
- **Expect free gifts outside.** Fan clubs hand out free items such as stickers, photo cards, fans and printed banners. People queue politely for them, and you are not expected to pay. A small gift of your own to swap goes down well.
- **Pay at the stalls with PromptPay or cash.** Food and drink vendors around the complex mostly use Thai QR payment. Foreign cards are less reliable there than at a mall.

Arrive with time to spare. Official merchandise queues at stadium shows often start hours before the gates open. Bag rules change from tour to tour, so check the official notice before you pack. Professional cameras and large power banks are often banned.

## The December 5 holiday and the night off

The schedule has a gap. Shows run December 3, then skip the 4th, then return on the 5th and 6th. The second show also falls on 5 December, a Thai national holiday. It marks the birthday of the late King Bhumibol and is also National Father's Day.

The holiday helps and hurts at the same time. Many Bangkok residents have the day off, so the city empties a little and daytime traffic is lighter than on a normal Saturday. Government buildings close, though, and public areas near the Grand Palace and Sanam Luang may host ceremonies. You may also see yellow flags and portraits of the late king around the city. Be respectful near them, in the same way locals are.

You can use December 4 to recover, or to do the parts of Bangkok fans usually tack on:

- **Siam Square and the malls around Siam BTS station.** Siam Paragon, Siam Center and the side streets are where K-pop shops and fan-café events usually happen.
- **The Chao Phraya River by express boat.** It's a cool, slow afternoon after a long night in the stands.
- **An early night.** Two back-to-back stadium shows on the 5th and 6th are hard on the voice and the feet.

## Before you leave the hotel

A short checklist for each show night:

- Ticket in the official app, plus the matching ID
- A charged phone, and your Grab or Bolt account set up in advance
- ARMY Bomb with fresh batteries, already paired
- Water and a hand fan for the hot concrete stands, if bag rules allow them
- A meeting point agreed with friends, because mobile data often struggles once the bowl is full

Confirm dates, gate times and entry rules on the official BTS tour channels before you go.
