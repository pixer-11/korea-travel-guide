## Skeletons under a stone roof

A sauropod's neck stretches across the hall where you walk in. The building is a neo-baroque sandstone block that opened in 1907. It was built for the Senckenberg Nature Research Society, which has run natural history research in Frankfurt since 1817. That research history shows in the floors above: drawers, dioramas and labelled specimens stacked into tall glass cases.

The dinosaur hall is the reason most people come. You stand at rib height among mounted skeletons and casts. The Edmontosaurus "mummy", preserved with impressions of its skin, is the one specimen to seek out. The giant anaconda from the museum's own summary is coiled in the reptile displays upstairs.

## What to find on each floor

The collection is spread over several levels, so pick your targets before you start.

- **Dinosaurs:** sauropods, a Tyrannosaurus rex and the Edmontosaurus with skin impressions, all on the ground floor.
- **Messel Pit fossils:** flattened early horses, bats and birds from the UNESCO-listed shale quarry near Darmstadt. Some still show fur and stomach contents.
- **Whales and marine life:** large skeletons hung overhead.
- **Mammal and bird dioramas:** taxidermy displays in the old-style glass halls.
- **Reptiles:** home of the anaconda.

Two to three hours covers the highlights. With children who stop at every drawer, plan on half a day.

## Timing your visit around the crowds

On weekdays, the measured foot traffic is quiet from 9am to 6pm. In practice that means the whole day the doors are open. Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday run 9am–5pm. Wednesday stays open until 8pm, which suits an evening visit after work or after a day at the Messe.

Weekends are different. Saturday and Sunday it opens 9am–6pm, and only the first hour, 9am–10am, is calm. Try not to arrive between 11am and 5pm, when families fill the dinosaur hall and the cloakroom queue backs up. If a weekend is your only option, come at opening and see the dinosaurs first, before the rest of the visitors arrive.

## Getting in and moving through

Take the U-Bahn to **Bockenheimer Warte** (lines U4, U6 and U7). The museum is across the square, next to the medieval watchtower the station is named after. Messe Frankfurt's fairgrounds are a short walk south. The Palmengarten botanical garden is about 15 minutes' walk north through Westend, which makes a good second stop.

Ticket prices are posted at the entrance desk and on the official Senckenberg website. Buy online to skip the ticket line on weekends. Card payment is common, but carry a little cash for lockers or the café in case you need it.

German museum habits apply. Coats and large bags go in the cloakroom or lockers before you enter the halls. Labels are mainly in German, with English on the major displays, so the museum app or an audio guide helps in the side galleries. Photography without flash is generally fine. Check the signs at temporary exhibitions, which can have their own rules.
