## A Saturday in November, an arena on the M2

Korn play the Unipol Forum in Assago on Saturday, November 21, 2026. The arena sits on Milan's south-western edge, past the ring road, among the office blocks and hotels of the Milanofiori business district.

Most of the crowd comes out from the city on the metro. By the last stops the green M2 carriages are full of black band shirts, and the walk from the station to the arena is short enough to follow without a map.

This is a full indoor arena show. That means one big room, a floor in front of the stage and tiered seating rising on three sides.

## The band behind the bagpipes

Korn formed in Bakersfield, California, in 1993. Their self-titled 1994 debut is widely credited with starting nu-metal: low-tuned seven-string guitars, a slapping, clattering bass tone and Jonathan Davis's lyrics, which move between a whisper and a scream.

The records that followed made them arena headliners worldwide. These include *Life Is Peachy*, *Follow the Leader* and *Issues*, along with singles like "Freak on a Leash", "Got the Life" and "Falling Away from Me".

Two details long-time fans watch for:

- **The mic stand.** Davis's biomechanical microphone stand was designed by H.R. Giger, the Swiss artist behind the creature in *Alien*.
- **The bagpipes.** Davis plays them on stage, a habit that goes back to "Shoots and Ladders" on the first album.

## Inside the Unipol Forum

The Forum opened in 1990 and has had several names. Older Milanese still call it the Forum di Assago or the Mediolanum Forum. It is one of the largest indoor concert arenas in Italy, and it is also the home court of Olimpia Milano, the city's basketball club.

The ticket categories use Italian terms, so it helps to know what they mean:

- **Parterre:** the standing floor in front of the stage.
- **Tribuna** or **anello:** the tiered seating sections, usually with numbered seats (*posto numerato*).
- **Ingresso:** the entrance gate printed on your ticket. The arena has several of them, and using the right one saves a lap of the building.

Ticket sales go through the concert organiser and the sales channels the Unipol Forum lists for its events. Ticket prices appear at the point of sale, not here.

## Riding the M2 fork to Assago

Metro line M2 splits in two at Famagosta. One branch runs to Abbiategrasso, the other to Assago. Fans heading to the show get on a train signed **Assago Milanofiori Forum**, not Abbiategrasso. If an Abbiategrasso train arrives first, riders usually stay on until Famagosta and change there.

Useful connections onto the M2:

- **Milano Centrale:** the main rail station is on the M2, so there is no change needed.
- **Cadorna:** the Malpensa Express airport train ends here, with an interchange to the M2.
- **Sant'Ambrogio:** the interchange with M4, the line from Linate Airport.
- **Porta Genova and Garibaldi:** both on the M2, and both handy if you're staying in the Navigli or Isola areas.

Drivers come in on the Tangenziale Ovest ring road, and there is parking at the arena and around Milanofiori. After a sold-out arena show, though, the car parks and the junctions empty slowly.

## The last-train squeeze after the encore

This is the timing quirk of any late show at Assago. The Forum is near the end of a metro branch, and Milan's metro winds down around midnight. When the house lights come up, thousands of people walk to the same small station at the same moment.

Attendees usually handle it in one of three ways:

- **Leave during the final song.** This beats the platform rush but means missing the encore.
- **Wait it out.** Some fans let the first surge pass with a drink in Milanofiori, then make for the station.
- **Stay nearby.** Others book a room in the Milanofiori hotels, which are a short walk from the arena, and skip the journey back entirely.

Taxis wait outside after big shows, but the queue is long. App-based ride options in Milan are more limited than in many other European cities.

## Getting through the doors, Forum-style

Italian arena entry has its own rhythm. Lines build at each *ingresso* before doors open, and security does bag checks at the gate. Large bags, professional cameras and glass bottles are the things arenas typically turn away. Fans usually travel light with a small bag or none at all.

Inside, cards and contactless payments are standard at the bars and the merchandise stands. At the bar, *una birra* and a pointed finger at the tap get you served.

The merch queue on the concourse is longest just before the band's set and right after it. Fans who want a shirt in their size tend to go when doors first open.

Parterre fans who want to stand near the barrier usually get to the Forum well before doors and queue outside. People with numbered tribuna seats can turn up much later, because their place is waiting for them.

## Making a weekend of it

Late November in Milan is cold, grey and often damp. The big Christmas lights usually go up around the Duomo and the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II at about this time. Most out-of-town fans stay in the city centre rather than in Assago, because the M2 links the centre directly to the arena.

For a pre-show meal, the Navigli canal district is two stops from Famagosta at Porta Genova and has dense rows of bars and trattorias. For something closer to the arena, the Milanofiori complex has places to eat within walking distance of the Forum doors.
