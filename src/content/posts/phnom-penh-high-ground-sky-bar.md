## Two floors, one view

High Ground Sky Bar occupies the 25th and 26th levels of a tower in BKK1, Phnom Penh's district of embassies, boutique hotels and glass-fronted condos. The 25th floor holds the indoor bar, built for air-conditioned comfort and cocktail service. Climb to 26 and you're on an open-air terrace with a 360-degree sweep of the city, no columns or walls breaking the horizon.

That split matters practically. Humid nights or sudden rain push the crowd downstairs to the enclosed bar; clear evenings empty the terrace onto plush seating for the sunset crowd. Both floors share the same drinks list and kitchen, so you're not choosing between experiences, just weather and mood.

## Why BKK1, why now

BKK1 is Phnom Penh's densest cluster of high-rises, which is precisely why a 360-degree view works here rather than in the low-rise riverside core. The neighborhood is walkable, lined with cafes and design shops, and close to Independence Monument. High Ground is a recent opening, part of a wave of towers finally tall enough to give Phnom Penh a proper sky-bar skyline.

The kitchen runs a creative international menu built to pair with signature cocktails rather than stand alone as a full dinner destination. Expect small, shareable plates designed for grazing over a few rounds, not a sit-down tasting menu. Because it's new, the lineup is still finding its rhythm, so treat any specific dish name you hear secondhand as unconfirmed.

## Booking your table

Reservations are the sensible move for a spot this new and this high. Rooftop bars in tall BKK1 towers fill their terrace seating fast once word spreads, and walk-ins risk the indoor floor only.

- Call or book online ahead, especially for sunset hours
- Ask specifically for terrace seating on the 26th floor if the view is the point
- Confirm current opening hours and reservation policy directly with the venue before you go, since both can change at a recently launched bar
- Have a backup plan for the indoor bar if the terrace is fully booked

## Getting the terrace at its best

Arriving before sunset gives you the best odds of a terrace seat before the after-work crowd builds. Phnom Penh's light fades fast near the equator, so plan to be seated at least twenty minutes before dusk if the sky matters to you. Dress code at towers like this tends toward smart-casual rather than beach wear; sandals and shorts can work against you at the door.

Payment at Phnom Penh's upscale bars is almost always by card, with US dollars accepted alongside Cambodian riel in cash if you need it. Tipping isn't obligatory but rounding up or leaving small change is common practice at bars aimed at this crowd. Because the bar is new, double-check whether a service charge is already added before tipping again.
