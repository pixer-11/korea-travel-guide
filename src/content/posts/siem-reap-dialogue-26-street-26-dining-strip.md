Street 26 is not the road most Siem Reap itineraries mention first. That's changing. Wat Bo Village, a former quiet residential pocket east of the river, has spent the last couple of years filling in with small-format cafés and concept bars, and Dialogue 26 is one of the addresses locals now point to when they explain why.

## What Dialogue 26 actually is

It's a dual-identity venue rather than a single restaurant. Mornings and afternoons, it runs as a café and brunch spot, the kind of place suited to working through an iced coffee and something off a brunch menu at a slow pace.

Once the sun drops, the room resets. The same space becomes a speakeasy-style cocktail bar, with the low light and hidden-door theatrics that label implies. Two visits, two different rooms, one address.

## Why Wat Bo Village is the story

For years, Pub Street and the Old Market grid absorbed almost all of Siem Reap's evening energy. Wat Bo has been quietly building an alternative.

Street 26 in particular has filled with independent cafés and small eateries over a short stretch of time, enough that outlets covering Asia's food scene have started naming the neighbourhood outright as a rival to the old tourist core. Dialogue 26 is part of that build-out, not a lone outpost.

That matters for how you plan your evening. Rather than treating this as a single stop, treat Street 26 as a short crawl: a coffee or brunch somewhere by day, a wander past new shopfronts in the late afternoon, cocktails somewhere with a door policy after dark.

## Getting there and what to expect on arrival

Wat Bo Village sits on the east bank of the Siem Reap River, an easy tuk-tuk ride from Pub Street or the Old Market, generally short enough that drivers won't need much more than the street name and neighbourhood to place it.

Because this is a newly trending address rather than an established institution, don't assume walk-in ease at night. Speakeasy-format bars often work on limited seating or a soft reservation system precisely to protect the atmosphere they're selling.

- Call ahead or check their social channels for the night's hours before heading over
- Ask your tuk-tuk driver for "Street 26, Wat Bo" rather than the venue name alone, since new openings take time to register with local drivers
- If arriving for brunch, note that café hours and bar hours are separate windows, not one continuous open

## Reading the room: café by day, bar by night

The split identity is worth understanding before you arrive, because showing up expecting one mode and finding the other is the easiest way to be disappointed.

Daytime Dialogue 26 is built for lingering: laptops, conversation, the unhurried pace typical of Southeast Asian café culture where nobody rushes your table. Night mode trades that for the choreography of a cocktail bar built around a concept, the sort of place where the entrance itself and the first pour are part of the pitch.

If you only have one window in your schedule, pick based on mood rather than convenience. Brunch suits a slow first half of the day before temple visits; the bar suits an evening when Pub Street's volume feels like too much.

## Ordering and etiquette on Street 26

Cambodia runs increasingly cashless in tourist-facing venues, and a concept bar in a trending neighbourhood is a safe bet to accept cards, but carry small riel or dollar notes as backup since new openings can have inconsistent card readers.

Tipping isn't compulsory but rounding up or leaving a small note is standard practice at Siem Reap's café and bar scene, appreciated rather than expected.

Because exact menus and prices for a recently opened venue change quickly, don't lock in specific dish or drink expectations in advance. Ask staff what's currently on when you sit down, and treat the visit as exploratory rather than scripted, which suits the neighbourhood's whole appeal anyway.

## Building it into a Siem Reap day

Pair Street 26 with a broader Wat Bo wander rather than a single dedicated trip. Walk the side streets near the pagoda that gives the village its name, note which shopfronts are new, and let Dialogue 26 be one stop among several rather than the whole excursion.

Given how fast this pocket of the city is filling in, the specific lineup of cafés and bars on the street a year from now may look different from today. That's part of the appeal of visiting while it's still forming.
