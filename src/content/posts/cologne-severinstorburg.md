## A gate that outlived its wall

Stand on Chlodwigplatz and look north. A heavy stone gatehouse rises out of the traffic, with trams sliding past its flanks. This is Severinstorburg, the southern entrance to medieval Cologne, and its oldest fabric dates back to the 12th century.

Cologne once had twelve of these gates in its great semicircular wall. Most of the wall was pulled down in the late 19th century, when the city spread outward along the Ring boulevards. Only three gates survived: this one, the Eigelsteintorburg to the north and the Hahnentorburg to the west.

## Getting to Chlodwigplatz

The address is Chlodwigplatz 19, 50678 Köln, and the gate is the square's centrepiece, so you can't miss it.

- **By Stadtbahn:** Chlodwigplatz station sits right underneath, served by lines 15 and 16. Follow the exits up to street level and the tower is in front of you.
- **On foot from the Dom:** walk south down Hohe Straße and on into Severinstraße. It takes roughly 30 to 40 minutes, and the gate closes off the far end of the street like a full stop.
- **From the river:** the Rheinauhafen and the Chocolate Museum are an easy walk east.

## What you actually see

Most visitors get Severinstorburg from the outside, and that's the honest way to plan it. Walk a full loop around the base. From Severinstraße you see the side that faced the old city. From the Chlodwigplatz side you see the face that met travellers coming up from the south, and it looks far more defensive.

Look at how thick the walls are compared with the buildings around it. Look up at the narrow window openings too. Give yourself 15 to 20 minutes and you'll have taken it in properly, with time for photos from across the square.

The interior holds three rooms that are hired out for weddings, parties and corporate events. That's why the doors are often shut to casual visitors, or closed off for a private booking. If seeing the inside matters to you, contact the venue before you go. Don't count on walking in.

## Around the Vringsveedel

The streets behind the gate are Cologne's Severinsviertel. Locals call it the "Vringsveedel", after Vring, the Kölsch nickname for Saint Severin. Severinstraße runs north from the gate and is lined with ordinary neighbourhood shops, bakeries and pubs. It feels very different from the tourist stretch around the cathedral.

Nearby, by name:

- **St. Severin:** the Romanesque basilica of the gate's patron saint, a few minutes' walk up Severinstraße.
- **Volksgarten:** a large park with a pond, a short walk south-west.
- **Rheinauhafen:** the old harbour, with the three Kranhäuser and the Chocolate Museum.
- **Bayenturm:** another surviving fortification tower, down on the Rhine.

Linking these makes a good half-day walk through the Südstadt, and the gate makes a natural start or finish.

## Handling the square and the Südstadt pubs

Chlodwigplatz is a busy junction, not a pedestrian plaza. Trams have right of way and come through quietly, so cross at the marked points rather than stepping out for a photo angle. A loop of the tower on the pavement is safer and gives you better views anyway.

If there's a wedding party outside, the venue is in use. Take your pictures from across the square and leave the doorway to the guests.

In the brewpubs and corner bars on Severinstraße, Kölsch arrives in small, straight 0.2-litre glasses called Stangen. The waiter, known as the Köbes, keeps bringing fresh ones until you put your beer mat on top of the glass. Your tally goes on the mat as pencil marks.

Many smaller places still prefer cash, though card is spreading. Ask before you order if you only have plastic. Rounding up the bill is the normal way to tip.

## When to come

This is a quieter stop than the cathedral, and you rarely have to plan around tour groups. Weekday mornings suit photographers, before the square fills with people running errands. Late afternoon is good if you want to go straight on to a Kölsch on Severinstraße afterwards.

Around Carnival the whole Südstadt is a party zone, and the area around the gate gets packed with costumed crowds. That's either the best time to come or the time to avoid it, depending on what you're after. Either way, check the dates and street closures before travelling.
