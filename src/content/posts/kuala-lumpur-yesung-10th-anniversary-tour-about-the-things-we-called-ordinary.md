## Ten years of Yesung on his own

Yesung's solo career started in April 2016. That was when the Super Junior main vocalist released his first mini album, *Here I Am*, after more than a decade singing inside one of K-pop's largest groups.

*ABOUT THE THINGS WE CALLED ORDINARY* marks those ten years. The Kuala Lumpur show falls on Saturday, November 7, 2026, at Zepp Kuala Lumpur. On that date the city becomes one stop on an anniversary tour, so this night is about his own catalogue and not a Super Junior group concert.

His solo work has always leaned on ballads and his voice: the quiet build of "Here I Am", the rainy-day mood of "Paper Umbrella", and the lighter, brighter "Pink Magic". Nobody outside the production knows the setlist. The tour's name points toward memory and small, everyday moments, though, and fans who know his discography generally expect a vocal-heavy evening.

## Inside Zepp Kuala Lumpur

Zepp Kuala Lumpur belongs to the Japanese chain of mid-sized live houses. It opened in 2022 inside the BBCC development, next to Mitsui Shopping Park LaLaport.

The layout follows the Zepp pattern:

- A flat main floor, usually standing for K-pop solo shows
- A raised tier of fixed seats at the back
- A stage you can see clearly from almost anywhere, because the room is far smaller than an arena

At full standing capacity it holds roughly 2,500 people. For a singer with Yesung's following, that's close quarters. Even the back of the floor is within reach of the stage, and you can hear the voice itself instead of a stadium echo.

## Rail to BBCC: Hang Tuah and Merdeka

Getting there by rail is simple. **Hang Tuah station** serves three lines: the LRT Ampang line, the LRT Sri Petaling line and the KL Monorail. A covered link runs from the station into the BBCC complex.

**Merdeka MRT station** on the Kajang line is the other option. You come out by Merdeka 118, the tall spire you can see from most of the city, and the walk to BBCC is short.

Coming from KLIA, most visitors take the KLIA Ekspres to KL Sentral. From there it's one change to the Monorail toward Hang Tuah. Grab is the usual ride-hailing app here. On a concert night, cars dropping off around Jalan Hang Tuah add to the ordinary Saturday traffic in Bukit Bintang.

## Queue numbers, lightsticks and the floor

Most of what makes a Zepp night work happens in the queue. Standing tickets at Zepp venues in Asia usually carry an entry number. Staff line the floor holders up and let them in by number block, so where you end up on the floor depends on that number, not on what time you got there.

What attendees usually do:

- **Fans with good numbers** often wait in the shaded areas of LaLaport until their block is called.
- **ELFs**, as Super Junior fans call themselves, bring the official sapphire-blue Super Junior lightstick. You'll also see Yesung-specific banners and slogans held up during the ballads.
- **Fan-made freebies**, like photocards, stickers and small printed slogans, often get handed out along the queue. People swap them politely and say thank you.
- **Bags** stay small. Malaysian venues usually search bags at the door, and big camera gear is normally kept out.

Inside, the crowd stays quiet during the ballads and sings back on the choruses fans know. Malaysia's K-pop audiences have a reputation for loud, coordinated fan chants. Many of the fans around you will know the chant parts for Yesung's songs.

Card and Touch 'n Go eWallet payments work almost everywhere in BBCC and LaLaport. Merchandise booths at K-pop events usually take cards as well.

## A Saturday night in Bukit Bintang

Because the show falls on a Saturday, the rest of the evening fills in easily. LaLaport is right next door, and its food court and restaurant floors are where most concert-goers eat before doors. That's the easiest option if you want to stay close to the queue.

A little further out:

- **Jalan Alor**, the hawker street in Bukit Bintang, runs well into the night. It's a common after-show stop for satay, grilled stingray and noodles.
- **Petaling Street** in Chinatown is a short ride away. Its covered market and the kopitiams around it are good for breakfast the next morning.
- **Pavilion Kuala Lumpur** and the shopping strip along Jalan Bukit Bintang are within walking distance or one Monorail stop.

## November weather and the walk home

Early November sits in Kuala Lumpur's inter-monsoon period. Late afternoons often bring short, heavy thunderstorms, and the evenings stay warm and humid.

The covered link between Hang Tuah station and BBCC matters on those evenings. Fans heading home usually take the rail instead of waiting outside for a Grab while every other concert-goer leaves at once. After the encore, the Monorail and LRT platforms at Hang Tuah fill quickly. Merdeka MRT, a few minutes' walk away, is the usual alternative for anyone going toward the Kajang line.

## Staying near the venue

Visitors from out of town usually base themselves in Bukit Bintang, near Pavilion and Jalan Alor, or in Chinatown around Petaling Street. Both are one or two stops from Hang Tuah and close to plenty of food.

KL Sentral is the other common choice for fans flying in and out the same weekend. It's the KLIA Ekspres terminus and sits on the Monorail line that runs straight to Hang Tuah.
