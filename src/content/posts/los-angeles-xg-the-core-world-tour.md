## A Thursday night on Figueroa

The date matters more than it looks. XG brings The Core World Tour to Crypto.com Arena on November 5, 2026, which is a Thursday. The show lands on a working night, when downtown is still clearing out after the evening commute.

The arena stands at 1111 South Figueroa Street in South Park, the southern end of downtown Los Angeles. Its curved glass and metal front faces the open plaza of L.A. Live. The Los Angeles Convention Center sits right beside it, and the I-110 and I-10 freeways meet a few blocks away.

## Seven voices, mostly in English

XG is a seven-member group from Japan: Jurin, Chisa, Hinata, Harvey, Juria, Maya and Cocona. They debuted in 2022 with "Tippy Toes" on XGALX, the label set up by producer Simon, who works as JAKOPS. Most of their music is sung and rapped in English. It leans more toward hip-hop and R&B than bright idol pop.

Their fans call themselves ALPHAZ. The catalogue they built their reputation on includes "Shooting Star", "Left Right", "Woke Up" and "Puppet Show". The rap-heavy choreography of those songs is why their live shows get talked about. Nobody has published a setlist for this date, so this guide doesn't guess at one.

## Inside the bowl that used to be Staples Center

The building opened in 1999 as Staples Center and was renamed Crypto.com Arena in 2021. It is home to the Lakers, the Sparks and the Kings, and it holds around 20,000 people for concerts. It is the biggest indoor room on a typical Los Angeles arena tour.

The seating stacks up in three main tiers:

- **Floor:** in front of the stage, standing or seated depending on how the show is set up
- **100 level:** the lower bowl, closest to the stage on the sides
- **300 level:** the upper bowl, steep and high, with a full view of the stage and lighting

Before doors, fans usually wander Star Plaza outside the main entrance. The bronze statues there include Magic Johnson, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Wayne Gretzky and Kobe Bryant, and they're a popular place to meet up.

## Tickets, dates and where they live

The organiser publishes the tour dates and ticket links on XG's official site. Crypto.com Arena sells its events through AXS, and its tickets are mobile, held in the AXS app or a phone wallet. Resale tickets bought outside the official channels are where fake and duplicate tickets usually turn up.

The arena posts its bag policy and its list of banned items on its own website. Those rules apply to every event in the building. Banners, light sticks and large signs are the things fans most often have questions about, so the venue's page is the place to look before packing.

## The Metro beats the garage

The A and E lines stop at **Pico station**, about a five-minute walk south of the arena. From Hollywood, the Westside or Long Beach, most fans take the train rather than face the freeway traffic on a weeknight.

Other options:

- **7th St/Metro Center:** a hub for the A, B, D and E lines, roughly a 15-minute walk up Figueroa
- **Driving:** L.A. Live runs garages and surface lots around the complex. Leaving after the show is slow, because the whole crowd heads for the same freeway ramps at once.
- **Rideshare:** L.A. Live has set pickup areas, and they get crowded right after a show ends

Plenty of fans walk a few blocks away from the arena before they request a ride. Pickups are much quicker once you're clear of the crowd.

## Merch lines, outfits and the walk back to Pico

Concert nights at this arena run on phones. You get in by scanning a mobile ticket, and most food and drink stands take cards and mobile payments. Fans usually have their tickets loaded before they reach the security line, because the plaza signal slows down when thousands of people arrive at once.

Merch is usually the longest wait of the night. Fans at big arena shows tend to queue at the stands well before doors, then spend the rest of the time on the plaza. A lot of ALPHAZ show up in outfits based on a member's stage look or a music video. Sometimes fan groups hand out small items or coordinate a chant.

At the end of the night, most of the crowd walks south down Figueroa to Pico station. The trains fill up fast. The people who stop for a snack at L.A. Live first usually catch an emptier train.

## A Japanese supper three Metro stops away

Little Tokyo is the obvious place to go before or after a show by a Japanese group. Take the A or E line north from Pico to **Little Tokyo/Arts District station**. Since the Regional Connector opened, it's a direct ride with no change.

Around the station you'll find:

- **Japanese Village Plaza:** a pedestrian lane of shops, sweet stalls and noodle counters
- **Japanese American National Museum:** a short walk from the station on 1st Street
- **Ramen and izakaya spots:** they line 1st and 2nd Streets, and the popular ones are known for their queues

Closer to the arena, the **Grammy Museum** sits inside L.A. Live itself. It's a good choice if you want something indoors while you wait for doors. Visitors coming from out of town often stay at the JW Marriott or the Ritz-Carlton in the L.A. Live towers, which puts the arena within walking distance at both ends of the night.
