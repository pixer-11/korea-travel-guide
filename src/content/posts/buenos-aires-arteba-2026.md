## Three days at La Rural

arteBA 2026 runs from October 30 to November 1, 2026. It is Argentina's leading contemporary art fair, and it fills La Rural, the exhibition grounds of the Sociedad Rural Argentina in Palermo, with gallery booths and the works collectors pick out each year.

The fair dates from 1991, and Fundación arteBA, a non-profit, has organised it ever since. Over the decades it has grown into the main meeting point for the Argentine art market. Booths come from established Buenos Aires galleries, from younger spaces and from galleries elsewhere in Latin America.

## Reaching Plaza Italia

La Rural sits at the corner of Avenida Sarmiento and Avenida Santa Fe, facing Plaza Italia. The grounds also back onto Avenida Cerviño.

- **Subte:** Line D to Plaza Italia. The exit puts you across the avenue from the main gate.
- **Bus:** a long list of colectivo lines stops at Plaza Italia. You pay with a SUBE card, which also covers the Subte.
- **Taxi or app:** drivers know it as "La Rural, Plaza Italia". On Santa Fe the traffic slows to a crawl at weekends.

Most visitors stay in Palermo, so the fair is a walk or a short ride away. Recoleta, one Subte line over, is the other usual base.

## Working the aisles

Expect a long afternoon. Pavilions at La Rural are big halls with concrete floors, and the booths are laid out in a grid of aisles. Most visitors spend three to four hours there and still take a second lap.

- **Pace:** walk every aisle once, then go back to the booths you liked.
- **Programme:** talks and curated sections usually run alongside the commercial booths. You will find them on the fair map at the entrance.
- **Weekend:** Saturday and Sunday bring families and students, so the aisles fill up more than on the Friday.
- **Food:** the grounds have cafés. Bigger meals are a short walk away on Santa Fe and in Palermo Soho.

Behind the fair is the Jardín Botánico Carlos Thays, which gives you shade and a quiet path after hours under hall lighting. MALBA, the Museo de Arte Latinoamericano on Avenida Figueroa Alcorta, is about a 20-minute walk away. Plenty of fairgoers make it a second stop the same weekend.

## Talking to galleries in Spanish

Booths are run by gallerists, and they will expect questions. Opening with "Hola, buenas" before asking anything is basic courtesy in Buenos Aires.

- Works often have no visible price. Collectors ask "¿Me pasás el precio?" or ask for the booth's price list, the *lista de precios*.
- The artist's name and the year are usually on a small label next to each piece. Gallerists are happy to walk you through a series.
- Asking before you photograph a piece up close is polite. Some galleries say no to particular works.
- Payment for purchases is arranged with the gallery directly. Larger sales are often finished after the fair.

Evenings in Buenos Aires start late. Dinner before 9pm feels early to most porteños, so visitors who stay until the fair closes still eat on local time. Galleries in Palermo and Villa Crespo sometimes hold openings over fair weekend, and fairgoers pass word about them from booth to booth.
