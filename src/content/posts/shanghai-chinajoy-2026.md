## Why go

ChinaJoy (中国国际数码互动娱乐展览会) is China's flagship expo for gaming, esports, and digital entertainment, drawing major domestic and international publishers, hardware makers, and streaming platforms. It's less a trade show for casual browsers and more a full-blown industry-and-fan spectacle: elaborate booth builds, playable demos of unreleased titles, esports tournament stages, voice-actor and idol appearances, and the cosplay culture the event is famous for. If you're into gaming culture, hardware launches, or just want to see Chinese pop-entertainment fandom at full volume, this is one of the best windows into it anywhere in Asia.

## Getting there

ChinaJoy is held at the **Shanghai New International Expo Centre (SNIEC)**, in Pudong, on Longyang Road. The venue sits directly above **Longyang Road Station**, served by **Metro Line 2** and **Line 7** — take Line 2 if you're coming from the Bund/People's Square side of the city, or Line 7 from the north/west. Exit toward SNIEC (signage inside the station points directly to the expo halls) and you'll walk straight into the exhibition complex without needing to surface far from the entrances. If you're staying near Pudong airport or Hongqiao, factor in that Longyang Road is a mid-length metro ride from both, so budget 45–60 minutes door-to-door depending on your hotel.

## What to expect inside

SNIEC is a sprawling multi-hall complex, and ChinaJoy typically spreads across several of them — a business-to-business (BTB) hall for industry deals and a business-to-consumer (BTC) hall for the public-facing show floor, plus dedicated esports and stage areas. Past editions have featured booths from major console, mobile, and PC game publishers, hardware and peripheral brands, and livestreaming platforms, alongside cosplay competitions and autograph sessions. Because exact exhibitor lineups, ticket categories, and hall maps are only finalized closer to the show, treat any specific brand or stage list you see online as provisional until confirmed on ChinaJoy's official channels.

## When to go

The 2026 dates are **July 31 to August 3**, which places the show squarely in Shanghai's hottest, most humid stretch of summer — expect high temperatures and the possibility of sudden thunderstorms, so plan for both sun and rain. Within the four days, the show is generally split between trade/industry-heavy hours early in the run and consumer-heavy crowds on the weekend days, when attendance peaks. If your schedule allows flexibility, an early-morning arrival on a weekday tends to mean shorter queues and cooler temperatures for the outdoor walk from the metro.

## Tickets and practical prep

Ticketing details, price tiers, and exact opening hours for 2026 had not been locked in as of this writing — always confirm dates, venue hall assignments, and ticket purchase links on the official ChinaJoy website or verified ticketing partners before you travel. Chinese expo tickets are frequently sold online in advance and can sell out or shift to on-site-only sales depending on demand, so build in a buffer day and don't assume walk-up tickets will be available.

## How to visit like a local

Seasoned expo-goers in Shanghai treat ChinaJoy as a crowd-management exercise first. Arrive at or before the official opening time — queues at SNIEC's entrances build fast once doors open, and the popular BTC hall stages and cosplay photo areas get congested by mid-morning. Payment inside is almost entirely mobile: Alipay and WeChat Pay are the default for food stalls, merchandise, and any on-site purchases, and vendors may not readily accept cash or foreign cards, so link a payment app before you go rather than relying on a wallet full of yuan. Lunch lines at the venue's food courts peak sharply around noon; eating slightly earlier or later avoids the worst of it. The most common visitor mistake is underestimating the walking distance and heat inside a hall complex this large — wear breathable clothing and carry water, since the halls can feel far apart even though they're technically one venue. If you want photos with cosplayers, ask permission first; it's expected courtesy rather than a rule, but it's consistently how it's done. Finally, don't try to "see everything" in one day — locals who attend annually usually pick one or two halls or themes per day rather than rushing the whole floor.
