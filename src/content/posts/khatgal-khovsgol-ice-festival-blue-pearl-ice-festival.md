## A festival on a metre of ice

The stage is the lake itself. Lake Khövsgöl runs about 136 km north toward the Russian border and holds most of Mongolia's surface fresh water. That clarity earned it the name "Blue Pearl". By early March, the ice off Khatgal is thick enough to carry horses, sledges and crowds.

The 2027 edition runs from 2 to 3 March, out on the ice near Khatgal. What fills those two days:

- **Ice sculptures** carved from blocks cut out of the lake
- **Horse-sledding races**, with Mongolian horses pulling sledges across the frozen surface
- **Tug-of-war on the ice**, where the footing is part of the contest

Most of it happens in open air with the larch-covered shoreline behind. Spectators stand along the edges of the cleared race lanes.

## Reaching Khatgal in winter

Khatgal sits at the southern end of the lake in Khövsgöl Province. The usual route is a domestic flight from Ulaanbaatar to Mörön, the provincial capital, then roughly 100 km north by road. The overland drive from Ulaanbaatar takes most of two days on winter roads.

Many international visitors come on a packaged trip from Ulaanbaatar, which bundles the flight, a driver and a heated ger or lodge in Khatgal. Plenty of lakeside camps shut for winter, so beds near the festival are limited. Independent travellers usually hire a driver in Mörön for the final stretch.

## Dressing for the lake in March

Early March on Khövsgöl is deep winter. Nights regularly fall far below −20°C, and wind across open ice makes daytime feel colder than the thermometer suggests. Watching races means standing still for long stretches, the hardest condition to dress for.

What festival-goers typically wear:

- Insulated boots rated well below freezing, with felt liners or thick socks
- Several thin layers under a long down parka
- A face covering, hat and mittens rather than gloves
- Sunglasses, because sun on white ice is fierce

Phone batteries die fast in this cold. Visitors usually keep spares inside a jacket, close to the body.

## Etiquette among the herders and horsemen

Festival crowds are mostly local families, herders and riders from around the province. Small courtesies matter here:

- Accept tea, food or anything handed to you with the right hand, the left lightly supporting the right elbow.
- Salty milk tea (*süütei tsai*) is the standard warm-up; taking at least a sip of whatever is offered is polite.
- Ask before photographing people, especially riders and anyone in traditional *deel* robes.
- Keep clear of the sled lanes. Horses at full pace on ice cannot stop quickly.

Cash in Mongolian tögrög is how small purchases work in Khatgal. Card terminals are scarce, and Mörön has the nearest dependable banks. Inside a ger, guests move clockwise and avoid stepping on the threshold. Most visitors stay the full two days, because the races and the sculpture judging fall on different stretches of the programme.
