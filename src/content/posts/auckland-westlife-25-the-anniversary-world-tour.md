## Twenty-five years of standing up on cue

Westlife built a career on one move. Five young men from Sligo, perched on stools, holding a ballad until the key change, then rising to their feet together. The group formed in 1998, and "Swear It Again" became their first UK number one the following year.

What followed was a run of number ones that few pop acts have matched. Think "Flying Without Wings", "My Love", "Uptown Girl", "World of Our Own" and "You Raise Me Up". The group split in 2012 and reunited in 2018. This anniversary tour celebrates that whole arc, and it brings Westlife back to New Zealand in November 2026.

## Spark Arena, Quay Park

The Auckland show is held at Spark Arena. It's the city's main indoor arena, set in the Quay Park precinct on Mahuhu Crescent, just east of the downtown grid and close to the port.

Inside, it's a classic enclosed bowl. Tiered seating wraps around a flat floor, and an end stage faces the far stands for most concerts. A ballad show like this one suits the room well. Every seat looks down at the stage, and a few thousand phone torches lighting up in the upper tiers is the arena's familiar closing picture.

The floor is the place for people who want to be close. The raised tiers give you a clean view of the whole lighting rig and the crowd swaying below.

## Getting to Mahuhu Crescent

Train is the easy answer. The arena is roughly a ten-minute walk from the city's central rail and ferry hub at Britomart. Follow the waterfront east along Quay Street and turn up toward Quay Park.

- **Train:** every suburban line ends at the Britomart hub downtown. Walk east from there.
- **Ferry:** services from Devonport, Waiheke and the North Shore come into the Downtown Ferry Terminal, next to Britomart.
- **Bus:** many city routes stop along Customs Street and Quay Street, within walking distance.
- **Car:** central parking is limited and traffic thickens before big arena shows. Many fans park further out and ride the train in.
- **Payment on transport:** Auckland's buses, trains and ferries use the AT HOP card and contactless bank cards.

At the end of the night, thousands of people leave at once. The walk back to Britomart is the busiest stretch, and taxis and ride-shares usually take a while to get through the Quay Park streets.

## Doors, bags and the big singalong

Arena nights in Auckland follow a well-worn pattern. Fans typically arrive an hour or so before doors, queue along the forecourt, and pass through bag screening at the entrance. Spark Arena publishes its bag-size and prohibited-items rules on its website. Small bags move through fastest.

Inside, paying works the New Zealand way:

- **Cards and contactless** are the norm at food and merch stands. Many Auckland venues barely use cash now.
- **Tipping** isn't expected in New Zealand, at the bar or anywhere else.
- **ID:** bar staff ask for photo ID from anyone who looks young. Overseas visitors usually carry a passport, as a foreign driving licence isn't always accepted.

The etiquette is relaxed but well understood. This crowd sings. It sings every word of the ballads, and loudest at the key change, when the whole arena tends to stand up along with the stage. Merch queues are longest before the show and in the interval, so plenty of fans buy on the way out.

## Before and after: Britomart to the Viaduct

You walk past most of the pre-show options on your way from the train. The Britomart precinct's restored brick warehouse blocks hold restaurants and bars along Galway and Tyler Streets. Commercial Bay, the shopping and dining complex on Queen Street at the harbour end, has a large food hall upstairs that's good for a quick meal.

Further west, Viaduct Harbour and Wynyard Quarter line the water with bars and seafood restaurants. They're a twenty-minute stroll from the arena. Plenty of fans finish the evening there, especially on a warm November night when Auckland's late spring evenings stay light well past dinner.

## Staying the night downtown

If you're coming from Hamilton, Tauranga, Wellington or across the Tasman, staying in the city centre makes the night much simpler. Hotels around Britomart, Queen Street and the Viaduct are all within walking distance of Spark Arena, so there's no late transport to deal with.

November is a lively month in Auckland, with spring events and the start of the summer cruise-ship season filling downtown beds. Travellers on a budget often look at Parnell or Ponsonby instead. Both are a short ride from the centre, and Parnell is close enough to walk.

A Westlife show runs on nostalgia, so give yourself time for the day after. Ferries from the Downtown terminal reach Devonport in about twelve minutes, or Waiheke Island's beaches and vineyards in under an hour. Either makes an easy morning before the trip home.
