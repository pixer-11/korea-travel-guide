## Paper in every direction

West Street is short. It runs between Washington Street and Tremont Street, with the shopping traffic of Downtown Crossing at one end and Boston Common at the other. Partway along, three floors of used books and magazines fill Brattle Book Shop. Beside it, an open lot is lined with carts of more books, out in the open air.

Brattle is well known, and it doesn't pretend to be undiscovered. It is one of the oldest antiquarian bookshops in the United States. The business traces its roots to 1825, and the Gloss family has run it since 1949. Expect browsers, tourists following a map and serious collectors all working the same shelves.

## The lot next door

The outdoor sale lot is what makes Brattle different from most city bookshops. Rolling carts and shelving units stand in the open space beside the building. They are packed spine-out with secondhand paperbacks, hardcovers, old textbooks and odd volumes. You browse standing up, under the sky, with the brick walls of the neighbouring buildings around you.

Prices out here are set by the cart or section, and signs show you which is which. Take your finds inside to pay. The lot depends on the weather. On wet or wintry days the carts may be covered or not out at all, so an outdoor rummage is best planned for a dry day in the warmer months.

- **Bring:** a tote bag, because finds from the lot add up fast
- **Look for:** mixed shelves, where fiction, history and oddities often sit side by side
- **Allow:** 20–30 minutes for the lot on its own, more if you dig

## Climbing the three floors

Inside, the shop gets more specialised as you climb. The lower floors hold most of the general stock: used books sorted by subject, plus the magazines the shop is known for. The shelves run close to the ceiling and the aisles are narrow, so expect to step aside for other browsers.

The top floor is for the rarer material. Brattle has a reputation for first editions, signed copies, antiquarian volumes and older printed ephemera. This is the room for slower browsing. Many items here are valuable, so ask staff before handling anything fragile or kept behind glass.

Even if you're not shopping for rare books, the climb is worth it. Each floor shows a different layer of what passes through a secondhand trade this old.

## Getting to West Street

Downtown Crossing is the nearest stop and serves both the Orange Line and the Red Line. From the station, walk along Washington Street and turn onto West Street. The shop is a few minutes away on foot.

Park Street station (Red and Green Lines) is a similar walk from the other side. Come out at the corner of Boston Common, head down Tremont Street and turn onto West Street. Driving into Downtown Crossing is slow and parking is limited. The T is much simpler.

## Beating the Saturday rush

Saturday is when Brattle fills up. Crowds typically build from 10am to 5pm, which covers nearly the whole trading day. The shop currently opens at 9am, so arriving right at opening gives you the first hour before the weekend peak. You also get first look at the outdoor carts.

Remember that Brattle is currently closed on Sundays. Plenty of weekend visitors find that out at a locked door. If you're in Boston on a Monday-to-Friday trip, a weekday visit is the easier choice for the narrow upstairs aisles. Closing time is currently 5:30pm every open day, so don't leave it until late afternoon.

## Browsing etiquette on three floors

How you behave matters in a shop this packed. A few habits make a visit smoother:

- **Pay inside, once.** Gather books from the lot and every floor, then settle up at the counter in one go.
- **Leave books on the shelf you found them on,** or hand them to staff. Misplaced books are hard to find again in a shop this size.
- **Ask before you open the rare stuff.** On the top floor, staff will take items out for you, and that is the expected routine.
- **Ask for what you're hunting.** Staff know the stock, and a named author or subject gets you further than wandering.
- **Check payment at the till** before you build a big stack, especially if you only carry cards.

The shop also buys books. If you're thinking of selling a collection, contact the shop first rather than turning up with boxes.

## A short loop around Brattle

West Street puts you within a short walk of downtown Boston's historic sites, so an hour at Brattle fits easily into a wider walk. Head toward Tremont Street and you reach Boston Common, the country's oldest public park. The Freedom Trail runs past the same corner.

Nearby stops worth pairing with the shop:

- **Boston Common and the Public Garden,** a few minutes west
- **Park Street Church and the Granary Burying Ground,** along Tremont Street, where Paul Revere and John Hancock are buried
- **Old South Meeting House,** a short walk east, where colonists met before the Boston Tea Party
- **Boston Athenæum,** the historic membership library on Beacon Street, an easy add-on for a bookish day

One practical route is to start at Brattle when it opens and spend an hour or so on the lot and the three floors. Then walk your bag of books across to the Common for a bench and a first look through what you bought.
