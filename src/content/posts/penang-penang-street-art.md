## Paint on Old Shophouse Walls

Start on Armenian Street (Lebuh Armenian). Two painted children ride a real, rusting bicycle bolted to the plaster there. That piece, *Children on a Bicycle*, came from Lithuanian artist Ernest Zacharevic. He painted it for the 2012 George Town Festival, and it set off the mural trail you walk today.

His other famous wall is on Ah Quee Street. A painted boy sits on a real motorbike parked against the wall. Elsewhere, black wrought-iron cartoons are fixed to corners across the grid. Each one carries a caption about the street's history: the trades, the nicknames, the clan feuds.

The murals are outdoors and open 24 hours. The Google pin sits at 316 Beach Street, but the works are spread through the whole old quarter. George Town and Melaka were listed together by UNESCO in 2008. Some older murals have faded badly in the tropical sun, and that fading is part of the look now.

## Routing the Walk

Give it two to three hours on foot. The core grid is compact.

- **By ferry:** boats from Butterworth dock at Weld Quay, a short walk from the mural lanes.
- **By bus:** Rapid Penang's free CAT shuttle loops the heritage zone.
- **By Grab:** ride-hailing is the usual way around the island. Ask for Armenian Street as your drop-off.

A sensible loop runs Armenian Street, Cannon Street, Ah Quee Street, then down to Beach Street. Pick up a free heritage map at your guesthouse, or check the official tourism site for the current mural list.

## Getting the Shot Without the Crowd

This is one of Penang's most visited sights, and the crowd data is clear. The lanes are quietest from 7am to 9am on weekdays and weekends alike. The morning light is softer then, and the pavement hasn't soaked up the day's heat yet.

Try to avoid 10am to 11pm on weekends. Queues build at *Children on a Bicycle* and the motorbike boy, and tour groups stack up behind each other. On weekday afternoons you can stand in the shaded five-foot-ways and wait for a gap.

The etiquette is simple:

- **Take turns.** People line up loosely at each mural, so step aside once you have your frame.
- **Mind the doorways.** Many murals are on homes and working shops, so don't block entrances or lean on shutters.
- **Only use the props meant for you.** The bicycle and motorbike are part of the art; other parked bikes belong to residents.
- **Watch the traffic.** Cars and scooters still use these narrow streets while you're posing.

## Nearby Stops on the Same Route

The mural lanes run straight into George Town's big heritage sites.

- **Khoo Kongsi:** an ornate clan temple off Cannon Street, a few minutes' walk from the Armenian Street murals.
- **Kapitan Keling Mosque:** on Jalan Masjid Kapitan Keling, on the edge of Little India.
- **Chew Jetty:** one of the Weld Quay clan jetties, with stilt houses over the water. It is close to the ferry.
- **Cheong Fatt Tze Mansion:** the indigo-blue mansion on Leith Street. Check the official site for tour times.

Hawker stalls and kopitiam cafés line the same streets, and small stalls usually expect cash. Plan a break partway through, because by late morning the heat shortens most people's stamina.
