## A wok on the ridge

From the harbourfront you can pick out the building's shape. The Peak Tower's roof curves up at both ends like a bowl on a stand, and people have compared it to a wok since Terry Farrell's design opened in 1997. It sits at 128 Peak Road, on the saddle just below the summit of Victoria Peak.

Inside, it works like a vertical mall. The Peak Tram arrives at the bottom, escalators take you up past souvenir shops and restaurants, and the open-air Sky Terrace 428 is on the roof. The name is its height: 428 metres above sea level. From there you look down over the towers of Central and Admiralty, across Victoria Harbour to Kowloon, and over to the hills beyond.

## Riding up from Central

Getting there is part of the visit. Most people take the Peak Tram from the lower terminus on Garden Road in Central, a short uphill walk from St John's Cathedral. From Central MTR station (Island Line and Tsuen Wan Line), use Exit J2 and follow the signs through Chater Garden. Allow 10–15 minutes on foot.

The tram climbs steeply, and the buildings outside the windows appear to lean as it goes. Sit on the right-hand side going up for the harbour view. Your other options:

- **Bus 15** from Central (Exchange Square) winds up the hairpins on Peak Road and stops right under the tower.
- **Taxi** from anywhere in Central drops you at the Peak's main entrance.
- **On foot**, the Old Peak Road path from Mid-Levels is a steep, sweaty hour through the trees.

## Inside the tower

The tower has several floors with different uses. The lower levels hold gift shops, Peak Tram merchandise and chain eateries. Madame Tussauds Hong Kong also has a floor here, with wax figures of Cantopop stars and film actors alongside the international names.

On the upper levels you'll find sit-down restaurants with windows facing the harbour, which are worth booking if you want a table at dusk. Sky Terrace 428 is ticketed separately from the building. Check the official site for current prices and terrace hours, because the terrace doesn't necessarily keep the same hours as the tower.

## Beating the tram queue

This is one of Hong Kong's busiest sights, and the queue is the part most visitors complain about. Here is how to keep the waiting short:

- **Go early.** The building currently opens at 7am. If you arrive soon after, you'll usually be ahead of the tour groups and day-trippers who come late morning.
- **Pick a weekday.** On weekends and public holidays, local families join the tourists.
- **Use an Octopus card** for the tram and bus. Tapping in is the normal way to pay, and it saves you a stop at the ticket window.
- **Ride the bus down.** The queue for the tram back down can be as long as the one going up, especially after dark. Bus 15 back to Central often moves faster.
- **Mind the sunset crush.** Everyone wants the moment the city lights come on, so the terrace railing fills up well before dusk.

Sunset is popular for a reason, and the evening view is the one on the postcards. You'll have more space for photos if you get up in daylight and stay into the evening. With the building open until 11pm at the moment, you aren't racing a closing time.

## The free view next door

You don't have to go up to the roof to see the harbour. Leave the tower, cross to Lugard Road and follow the flat, shaded path around the shoulder of the hill. In a few minutes it opens onto a railing with the same Central and Kowloon skyline. Many photographers prefer this angle.

If you keep going, Lugard Road joins Harlech Road in a loop of about 3.5 kilometres. It takes about an hour at a gentle pace and ends back at the tower. Across the plaza is The Peak Galleria, a second mall with more places to eat and its own viewing deck.

## How long to give it

Plan on half a day if you're doing the tram and the terrace with a meal, and longer if you add the Lugard Road loop. A quick visit of an hour or two is possible, but only if the queues are on your side.

Bring a light layer. The Peak is noticeably cooler and windier than the streets of Central, and low cloud sometimes sits on the ridge and hides the view completely. Look up at the summit before you set off. If it's covered in mist, leave it for another day.
