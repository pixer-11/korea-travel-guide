Barcelona's food scene resets every few years around a single new opening, and right now that opening is Mineral. It's the new project from chef Oliver Peña, who cut his teeth at Teatro — itself the successor to the legendary Tickets, the tapas playground that helped define the city's modern dining boom. That pedigree is doing a lot of the talking before you've even sat down.

Word has spread fast among locals who track the city's chef moves closely. When someone leaves a kitchen with that lineage to open their own place, Barcelona pays attention, and reservation books fill up accordingly.

## The pedigree behind the plates

Teatro closed relatively recently, and Peña's move to strike out on his own is being read as one of the more significant restaurant stories in the city this year. Chefs who trained under the Adrià-adjacent Tickets/Teatro school tend to bring a particular sensibility: playful technique, a respect for Catalan produce, and a willingness to bend a classic dish sideways without losing its soul.

That's the expectation Mineral is walking into.
