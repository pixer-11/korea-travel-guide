## A market you have to dig through

Nobody walks straight through Snoopers Paradise. You step in off a narrow pedestrian lane and the room runs back much further than the shopfront suggests. It is divided into dozens of small dealer pitches, and each one is packed with someone else's idea of what is worth keeping.

The pleasure here is rummaging, not browsing. Nothing is grouped by theme across the floor, so a rail of old coats can sit beside a crate of records. Plan on at least an hour. Anyone who likes to lift every lid can easily lose two.

## Finding it on Kensington Gardens

The address is 7/8 Kensington Gardens, BN1 4AL. That puts it in the North Laine, the grid of narrow streets between Brighton station and the seafront. Kensington Gardens is a short pedestrian lane between North Road and Gloucester Road, and it runs parallel to Sydney Street and Gardner Street.

On foot from Brighton station:

- Leave by the main front entrance onto Queens Road.
- Walk downhill and turn left into the North Laine streets around Trafalgar Street or North Road.
- The walk takes about 10 minutes and is all downhill, which makes the way back with heavy bags harder work.

Don't drive. Parking in the North Laine is limited and costly, and the lanes are busy with walkers. Most city buses stop along North Street or the Western Road corridor, both a few minutes away.

## What turns up on the stalls

Each pitch belongs to a different dealer, so stock changes all the time and no two visits are alike. That's the whole point. Expect the broad mix of a multi-dealer vintage emporium, not a curated boutique:

- Vintage clothing, coats and accessories
- Vinyl records and old books
- Glassware, ceramics and kitchenalia
- Small furniture, lamps and homewares
- Prints, postcards, toys and odd bits of ephemera

The variety is the reason it holds such a strong reputation with over a thousand reviews. If you have something particular in mind, go round every pitch before you decide. The same kind of item often appears on three stalls in three different conditions.

## When to go snooping

Timing changes the whole experience. The aisles between pitches are narrow, and a crowded room makes it hard to stop and look.

At weekends the calmest stretch is 11am to 12pm. On Sundays the doors don't open until 11:15am, so arrive at opening. Try not to turn up between 12pm and 5pm on a Saturday or Sunday. That is the busiest period, and the lanes outside fill with weekend shoppers too.

Weekdays are generally easier, with the doors open from 10:15am to 5:45pm. Keep Sunday's hours in mind: it shuts at 4:45pm, an hour earlier than the rest of the week, so a late-afternoon plan won't work that day.

## Buying from dealers who aren't there

Most stallholders in a market like this don't stand at their pitch. Items usually carry a handwritten ticket with the dealer's code, and you pay at the front counter, not to a person at the stall. Hold onto your find, or ask staff to put it by, while you carry on looking.

A few habits help:

- Check condition yourself. Open drawers, look over glass for chips and try on clothes where you can.
- If you want to haggle, ask politely at the counter. Staff can sometimes contact the dealer, but nobody owes you a discount.
- Measure your space at home before falling for furniture, and think about how you'll get it to the station.
- Prices are on each item's ticket, so read the label rather than guessing.

It's a quieter find than the seafront attractions. Treat it as a shop, not a theme park: handle things with care and put them back where they came from.

## Turning it into a North Laine afternoon

Snoopers Paradise sits among some of Brighton's best walking streets. Once you've finished, these are all close by:

- **Kensington Gardens itself**, a run of small independent shops on the same lane
- **Sydney Street and Gardner Street**, one block away, with more independent shops and cafés
- **Brighton Dome and the Royal Pavilion**, about five minutes' walk southeast across Church Street
- **The seafront and Brighton Palace Pier**, roughly 15 minutes downhill through The Lanes

Starting with an 11am snoop and then drifting toward the Pavilion is a neat way to have the market before the crowds and the town after it.
