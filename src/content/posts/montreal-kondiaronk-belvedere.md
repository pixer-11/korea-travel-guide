## A balcony over downtown

The city stops at the balustrade. Below the stone railing, Montreal's downtown towers stand in a tight cluster, close enough to pick out Place Ville Marie's cross-shaped tower. Past them, the St. Lawrence River runs as a flat band to the south.

On a clear day the view goes further. The isolated Monteregian hills, such as Mont Saint-Bruno and Mont Saint-Hilaire, rise out of the plain beyond the river. It's one of the most visited viewpoints in the city, and it earns that reputation without needing an entry gate or a ticket.

## The terrace, the chalet and the name

The belvedere is a broad, semicircular paved plaza set in front of the Mount Royal Chalet. The chalet is a stone pavilion from 1932 with a grand hall inside. Its doors are your best bet for public washrooms and a break from the wind, but check what's open on the day you go.

The terrace was named in 1997 after Kondiaronk, the Wendat chief whose diplomacy was central to the Great Peace of Montreal of 1701. That treaty brought together the French and some forty Indigenous nations. Mount Royal Park itself was laid out by Frederick Law Olmsted, the landscape architect behind New York's Central Park, and opened in 1876.

## Choosing your way up

There's no road to the edge itself, so everyone finishes on foot. Pick your route by how much climbing you want:

- **The Peel staircase:** Start at Peel station (Green Line), walk uphill on Rue Peel past Avenue des Pins, and pick up the trail and long wooden staircase at the top. Expect a steep 20–30 minutes from the bottom.
- **Olmsted Road (Chemin Olmsted):** A wide gravel path that zigzags gently up the mountain. It's longer, but it suits strollers, older legs and anyone who'd rather not climb stairs.
- **Bus 11 from Mont-Royal station (Orange Line):** Ride over the mountain on Voie Camillien-Houde and Chemin Remembrance, get off near Beaver Lake (Lac aux Castors) or Maison Smith, and walk through the woods for about 15–20 minutes.
- **By car:** There are paid lots near Beaver Lake and Maison Smith. The rates are posted at the pay stations. From there it's the same forest walk.

Give the visit 1.5–2 hours in total if you walk up from downtown and back. Allow longer if you add the nearby Mount Royal Cross, a short walk through the trees, or a loop past Beaver Lake.

## On the stairs and at the railing

The Peel staircase works as a free gym for many Montrealers, who run it in laps. Keep right on the steps and pause on the landings rather than mid-flight, so runners can pass. Most signs on the mountain are in French. "Belvédère Kondiaronk" and "Chalet du Mont-Royal" are the names to look for.

The terrace is popular, so beating the crowds is mostly about timing. The park opens at 6am. Arriving early on a weekday usually means a clear stretch of railing before tour groups and the afternoon walkers turn up. Weekend afternoons in summer are the hardest time to get a photo without people in it.

The belvedere is free and there's nothing to pay at the top. Do bring a card or some cash if you're planning on bus fare or parking. Dogs on a leash are common, and so are picnics on the low walls. Take your rubbish back down with you.

## Season by season

The view changes a lot with the calendar. In late September and October, the slopes below turn red and orange around the skyline. In summer, the leafy trees frame the towers, and the plaza fills with walkers, cyclists and the odd busker.

Winter strips the trees bare and opens up the sightlines. Packed snow makes the staircase slippery, so lots of people take Olmsted Road in boots or snowshoes instead. Beaver Lake nearby is a classic winter stop when conditions allow.

## When the lights come on

The park currently stays open until midnight, so you can watch the downtown towers light up after dusk. Hours on Mount Royal can shift through the year, and the official Les amis de la montagne and City of Montréal sites carry the current schedule.

After dark, use the lit Olmsted Road or the main paths rather than the narrow trails, and bring a phone torch for the forest stretches. Coming down the Peel stairs at night lands you back in the downtown streets.
