## Eight days of fast tables

You hear table tennis before you understand it. The light double tick of the ball, bat and table, comes too fast for the eye to follow. At a world youth championship that sound comes from many tables at once, each one fenced off by low barriers, with umpires on high chairs and coaches leaning forward between points.

The 2026 World Table Tennis Youth Championships ran in Istanbul from November 21 to 28, 2026. The players are teenagers. Several of them go on to play senior world tour events within a few years, so this is often where fans first see a future star.

## Who fills the stands

The crowd is unusually international. China, Japan, South Korea and Chinese Taipei bring strong followings, and their supporters tend to sit together in blocks with flags and coordinated chants. European federations travel well too, and Turkish fans make up the home crowd.

The sport's format shapes the week:

- **Early days:** many tables run at once, so one seat can give you three or four matches in view.
- **Middle days:** the draw narrows and there are fewer, longer sessions.
- **Final days:** play usually moves to a single show court, with the medal matches under brighter lights.

## Tickets and the venue

The ITTF, the sport's world governing body, publishes the event's venue, schedule and ticket information on its official site and World Table Tennis channels. The same place carries draws and live results through the week.

Youth championships are generally priced well below senior world events, and early rounds at junior tournaments often draw thin crowds. Supporters usually find the first days the easiest for walking in and moving between tables. Finals sessions are the ones that fill.

## Getting around Istanbul in late November

Istanbul has two airports. Istanbul Airport (IST) sits on the European side, northwest of the city. Sabiha Gökçen (SAW) is on the Asian side. Where you land matters, because crossing the Bosphorus at rush hour can take a long time.

Visitors usually rely on public transport:

- **Istanbulkart:** the rechargeable card that works on metro, tram, Marmaray, buses and ferries.
- **Marmaray:** the rail line under the Bosphorus linking the European and Asian sides.
- **Ferries:** cheap, frequent, and often faster than a taxi across the water.
- **M11 metro:** connects Istanbul Airport to the city's metro network.

Late November in Istanbul is cool and often damp, with short days and frequent rain. Fans typically carry a waterproof layer for the walk from the station. Indoor halls stay warm, so layers you can take off make the long sessions easier.

## Watching table tennis in the hall

Table tennis has firm crowd manners. Learn them and you'll fit in right away.

- **Stay still during rallies.** Movement behind the server's eyeline is distracting. Regulars move between seats only when a point ends.
- **Cheer between points, not during them.** A shout of "Cho!" from Japanese fans, or "Jiayou" (加油) from Chinese supporters, comes after the point, never in the middle of a rally.
- **No flash photography.** The white ball is hard enough to track without it.
- **Walk the barriers on early days.** Fans often stand at the edge of the field of play between matches to watch warm-ups up close.
- **Respect the towel break.** Every six points, players stop to dry off. That pause is when people talk, reach for water or swap seats.

In the city, card payment is accepted almost everywhere. Small cash still helps at simit carts and for tipping. Rounding up or leaving around 10% is normal in sit-down restaurants.

## Filling the evenings

Sessions end in time for dinner, and fans make good use of it. Sultanahmet puts the Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque and the Basilica Cistern within a short walk of each other. Karaköy and Galata, across the Golden Horn, have meyhanes (taverns serving meze and rakı) and the Galata Tower.

On the Asian side, Kadıköy's market streets fill up after dark with fish stalls, pickle shops and small bars. A ferry back across the Bosphorus at night, with a glass of çay bought on board, is one of the city's cheapest pleasures. Visiting teams often take one on their rest days.
