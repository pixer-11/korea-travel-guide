## Two days at the end of spring

Primavera Sound Buenos Aires runs on Saturday 28 and Sunday 29 November 2026. It is the Argentine edition of a festival that started in Barcelona in 2001 and now runs under the same name in several cities. Across the two days, Argentine artists share the bill with international acts.

Late November is the end of spring in the southern hemisphere. Days are long and usually warm, and the sun sets close to 8pm, so the early evening sets play in daylight. The festival also suits a city where dinner at 10pm is normal and nights out finish late.

## Where this edition comes from

The original Primavera Sound grew up on the Parc del Fòrum waterfront in Barcelona. It made its name by booking indie, electronic, pop, hip-hop and Latin acts on the same weekend, without treating any of them as the side attraction.

The brand reached Porto in 2012. In 2022 it came to South America, with editions in Buenos Aires, Santiago and São Paulo. The Buenos Aires event draws on a deep local scene, from rock nacional to the trap and urbano artists who now fill stadiums across Latin America.

## Dates, tickets and the venue

Here are the core facts for this edition:

- **Dates:** Saturday 28 and Sunday 29 November 2026
- **City:** Buenos Aires, Argentina
- **Format:** a two-day festival with national and international artists
- **Organiser:** Primavera Sound, which publishes the venue, lineup and ticket links on its official site

Earlier Buenos Aires editions were held at large open-air sites within the city. They were not in a remote field. For people coming from abroad, that means a city hotel and a normal ride home at night, with no need for camping.

## Getting around Buenos Aires that weekend

International flights land at Ezeiza (EZE), about 35 km southwest of the centre. Domestic and many regional flights use Aeroparque Jorge Newbery (AEP). It sits on the Río de la Plata at the edge of Palermo, a short ride from most hotel districts.

To ride the Subte and the colectivo buses, you need a rechargeable SUBE card. The Subte shuts down before most headline sets finish. For the trip home, festival-goers usually rely on Uber, Cabify and DiDi, all of which work in the city, or on radio taxis.

Most visitors stay in one of these areas:

- **Palermo Soho and Palermo Hollywood:** cobbled blocks of bars and restaurants, busy at night
- **Recoleta:** quieter streets lined with grand apartment buildings, good for sleeping off a late finish
- **San Telmo:** old colonial streets, antique shops and tango bars

## Festival nights, porteño style

Locals plan a night out around the clock. Lunch is long, dinner starts late, and bars stay full past midnight. Many festival-goers eat a proper meal at a parrilla in the afternoon and graze inside the grounds after that.

How the city pays and talks:

- **Payment:** cards are widely accepted, and Mercado Pago QR codes sit on counters everywhere, from kiosks to cafés
- **Tipping:** around 10% in restaurants is customary, often left in cash on the table
- **Useful words:** *entrada* (ticket), *pulsera* (wristband), *recital* (the local word for a concert), *baño* (toilet)
- **Sun:** the November sun is strong, so most people bring sunscreen and a hat for the daylight sets

Sunday 29 November is also market day in San Telmo. The Feria de San Telmo stretches along Calle Defensa with antiques, leather and street performers. It is an easy morning out before the second day of the festival.

## Stretching it into a long weekend

Many visitors add a few days in the city on either side of the festival. These sights are close to the main hotel areas:

- **Cementerio de la Recoleta:** a walled city of marble mausoleums that includes Eva Perón's family tomb
- **MALBA:** the Museum of Latin American Art in Palermo, home to works by Frida Kahlo and Xul Solar
- **El Rosedal:** the rose garden in the Bosques de Palermo, usually in bloom in November
- **Caminito, La Boca:** a short street of brightly painted tin houses next to the Boca Juniors stadium

Each one fills a morning without much travel. That leaves the afternoon free for a nap before the gates open.
