## A walled garden on Stuttgart's northern slope

You come in through a gate from a residential street, and the city falls out of sight. Gravel and stone paths wind around water, a pavilion and a small waterfall. Every turn frames a new view, which is the whole point of a classical Chinese garden.

It was laid out for the 1993 International Garden Exhibition (IGA) and is known in Chinese as Qingyinyuan, the "Garden of Tranquility". It is small. Most visitors walk the full circuit in 30 to 45 minutes, and longer only if they sit down.

The hillside location helps. From the garden's upper edge you look out over Stuttgart's valley basin and rooftops.

## Getting up Birkenwaldstraße

The address is Birkenwaldstraße 65, 70174 Stuttgart, in the Stuttgart-Nord district. Stuttgart is built in a bowl, so from the centre it is uphill all the way.

- **On foot:** from Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof, head north and climb through the residential streets of the Nordbahnhof side. The walk is steep and takes roughly 20 minutes.
- **By public transport:** buses run up into the northern hillside. Check the VVS journey planner for the current stop nearest Birkenwaldstraße.
- **By car:** parking is on-street in a residential area and limited. Public transport makes more sense.

## When the paths are quiet

The garden opens at 7am and closes at 8pm, seven days a week, with the same hours every day.

The quietest window is 7am to 9am, on weekdays and weekends alike. At that hour you are likely to share the pavilion with a jogger or two and nobody else. Low morning light also falls across the water then, which makes for clean photos without people in the frame.

Try not to arrive between 12pm and 7pm on a weekend. That is the peak, and on a site this size the narrow paths and the best viewpoints fill up quickly. For an afternoon visit, a weekday is a much safer bet.

## Keeping the "tranquility" in Qingyinyuan

This is a well-known stop. Expect other visitors, and remember that the garden's name sets the tone.

- **Keep your voice down.** Sound carries over still water, and people come to sit in silence.
- **Stay on the paths.** The planting and stonework are part of the design, not shortcuts.
- **Take turns at the pavilion.** It is the obvious photo spot. Step aside once you have your shot.
- **Read the signs at the gate.** Rules on dogs, bikes and food are posted there, so check them before you go in.

To make a half-day of it, the garden sits on the same northern heights as Höhenpark Killesberg and the Weissenhof Estate, the 1927 modernist housing showcase with buildings by Le Corbusier and Mies van der Rohe. Go to the garden first while it is quiet, then carry on uphill.
