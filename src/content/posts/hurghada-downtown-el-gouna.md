## A town centre built on lagoons

El Gouna does not feel like Hurghada. The resort town was developed from the late 1980s on reclaimed lagoons north of the city, and its downtown is the compact grid where everything converges. Buildings here are low, domed and arched, plastered in ochre and sand tones that borrow from Nubian and Upper Egyptian village architecture.

Narrow lanes open onto small squares, with Tamr Henna Square as the usual reference point. You will find restaurant terraces, shisha cafés, souvenir and clothing shops, and pharmacies within a few minutes' walk of one another. Compared with Hurghada's Sheraton Road strip, it is quieter, cleaner and easier to cross on foot.

## Reaching downtown from Hurghada

Downtown El Gouna sits roughly 25 km north of central Hurghada. Hurghada International Airport is about 30–40 minutes by road. Your options:

- **Taxi or ride-hailing** from Hurghada; agree on the drop point "downtown" or "Tamr Henna" before you set off.
- **Hotel shuttles**, which many Hurghada hotels run on fixed days.
- **The El Gouna bus and tuk-tuks** once you are inside the town, linking downtown with Abu Tig Marina, the beaches and the hotels.

The town is entered through a gated checkpoint. Day visitors not staying in El Gouna may be asked for an entry ticket, and the rules change, so ask your hotel or check at the gate on arrival.

## Eating and wandering after dark

Prices sit in the mid-range: above Hurghada's local eateries, below the marina's waterfront dining. The menus mix Egyptian staples such as koshari, grilled kofta, molokhia and fresh Red Sea fish with pizza, burgers and international plates aimed at European visitors.

Daytime heat empties the lanes for much of the year, especially from June to September. Once the sun drops, terraces fill and the squares become the town's living room. Plan one to two hours: a slow loop of the lanes, dinner, then mint tea or a shisha. From downtown, a short tuk-tuk ride takes you on to Abu Tig Marina for a drink by the yachts.

## Getting the evening right in El Gouna

Downtown still runs at a quieter pitch than much of the Red Sea coast, and it rewards a slower pace. A few habits help:

- **Pay with a mix.** Most restaurants take cards, but tuk-tuks and small kiosks prefer Egyptian pounds in cash.
- **Tip around 10%** at sit-down restaurants if service is not already on the bill; check the receipt first.
- **Order tea the Egyptian way.** Ask for *shai bil na'na* (mint tea) and say *shukran* (thank you).
- **Dress modestly in the lanes.** Swimwear belongs on the beach. Cover up when you head into town.
- **Book on Thursday and Friday nights** in high season (October to April), when Cairo weekenders arrive and the popular terraces fill.

Some shopkeepers expect gentle haggling, though less than in Hurghada's old Dahar souk. Fixed-price boutiques usually display tags; where they do not, ask before you pick anything up.
