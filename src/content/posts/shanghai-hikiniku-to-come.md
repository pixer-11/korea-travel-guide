## Why everyone's talking about it

Shanghai's dining scene churns through trends fast, but the queue outside Hikiniku To Come has held steady since the day FUFU opened its doors on Donghu Lu. The name translates roughly to "ground meat is coming" — a cheeky nod to the Japanese hambāgu tradition of grilled minced-meat patties, distinct from the Western burger despite the shared DNA. This is the Shanghai outpost of a Tokyo-born concept, and it's the kind of opening that gets photographed and shared before the paint has even dried on the walls of the mall around it.

What makes it worth the wait isn't novelty alone. The kitchen grills its patties — made from wagyu — over charcoal, the kind of live-fire cooking that's still relatively rare for this style of dish in Shanghai. You crack a raw egg over the top yourself, letting the yolk melt into the meat and sear-charred crust for a texture that goes from crisp to custardy in one bite. Rice refills are unlimited, which turns what could be a dainty small plate into a genuinely filling, DIY-assembly meal.
