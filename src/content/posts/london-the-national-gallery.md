## Up the steps from Trafalgar Square

Climb the portico steps and look back. Nelson's Column rises straight ahead, St Martin-in-the-Fields stands to your left, and Whitehall runs off towards Parliament. The long stone front behind you is William Wilkins' building, finished in the 1830s. Its columns and small dome cover the whole north side of the square.

Inside the main entrance, look down. The landings are covered in Boris Anrep's mosaics, and plenty of visitors walk across them without noticing. Above, the galleries open one into the next under skylit ceilings. The walls are coloured and the gilt frames are heavy. It's a building made for walking slowly.

## Seven centuries in one loop

The collection runs roughly in chronological order. It takes you from the gold-backed altarpieces of the 1200s up to the early 1900s, and you can follow European painting across those centuries without going back on yourself. Some famous paintings worth finding:

- **Jan van Eyck, *The Arnolfini Portrait***: notice the tiny convex mirror on the back wall.
- **Hans Holbein, *The Ambassadors***: stand off to the right and the smear at the bottom turns into a skull.
- **Leonardo da Vinci, *The Virgin of the Rocks***
- **Velázquez, *The Rokeby Venus***
- **Turner, *The Fighting Temeraire***, and **Constable, *The Hay Wain***, two classics of British painting
- **Van Gogh, *Sunflowers***, which usually has the biggest crowd in front of it

Paintings get moved or lent out from time to time. Check the online floor plan or ask at the information desk before you set off after one particular work.

## Timing your visit

Opening hours are currently 10am–6pm Saturday to Thursday, with a late close at 9pm on Fridays. Museums change their hours around holidays and special events, so check the official site before a specific trip.

Crowds usually follow a clear pattern. On any day, the quietest stretch is 10am–11am, so arriving at opening pays off. On weekdays it tends to quieten again from 5pm. On Fridays, 5pm–7pm is a good window, with three late hours still left before closing.

Try to avoid weekends between 12pm and 5pm. Those afternoons bring the crowds, and the people around *Sunflowers* can stand several deep.

## Getting to the portico

The gallery's address is Trafalgar Square, WC2N 5DN, and most routes end on the square:

- **Charing Cross** (Northern and Bakerloo lines, plus mainline trains): the closest station. Follow the signs for Trafalgar Square.
- **Leicester Square** (Northern and Piccadilly lines): a short walk down Charing Cross Road.
- **Embankment** (District, Circle, Northern and Bakerloo lines): walk up Villiers Street or Craven Street, which is handy if you're coming from the river.

Lots of buses stop around the square. Walking from Covent Garden or Piccadilly Circus takes a few minutes.

## How the gallery works inside

There's no ticket desk for the permanent collection, so you can walk straight in. Expect a security check and a bag search at the door. Large suitcases won't make it through, so leave heavy luggage at your hotel.

Some practical things to know:

- **Book exhibitions in advance.** The big temporary shows are ticketed and the popular ones sell out. Prices are listed on the official site.
- **Look out for donation points.** Most take contactless cards. Entry is free and the gallery relies on donations, so giving is up to you.
- **Pick a few rooms.** The collection is too big for one visit. Trying to see it all in two hours usually leaves people tired out by the Venetian rooms. Choose two or three periods and leave the rest for another day.
- **Use the benches.** Many rooms have a bench facing one major work. Sitting there for ten minutes shows you more than walking past twenty paintings.
- **Photos:** non-flash photography for personal use is generally allowed in the permanent collection. Exhibitions often have their own rules, so look for the signs.

Plan on two to three hours for a focused visit. A Friday evening is a good option if your days are full: the rooms are quieter and the square is lit up outside when you leave.

## After the last room

The National Portrait Gallery is right around the corner, so you can easily see both on one long morning. Across the square, St Martin-in-the-Fields has a café in its crypt and holds regular concerts in the church. Head south down Whitehall and you'll reach Horse Guards and Westminster on foot. North of the gallery, Leicester Square and the theatres of the West End begin almost immediately.
