## A White Spire on River Terrace

River Terrace follows the top of the Kangaroo Point peninsula, and one stretch of it holds a pale stone temple with a single spire. This is the Brisbane Australia Temple of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, dedicated in 2003. A gilded figure of the angel Moroni stands on top of the spire, as it does on most of the church's temples.

From the street you get trimmed lawns, clipped hedges and a building that gleams against the city skyline across the water. Many Brisbane visitors walk or cycle straight past it on the way to the cliffs. That works in your favour: this is one of the calmer stops on the peninsula.

## What You Can and Can't See

Know this before you go. The interior is not open to the public. Temples differ from the church's ordinary Sunday meetinghouses. Entry is limited to church members who hold a current temple recommend, and they come for ordinances such as marriages, which members call sealings.

For everyone else, the visit happens outdoors:

- The landscaped grounds and paths around the building
- The front facade and spire, which make the best photographs
- Views across the Brisbane River toward the CBD from this side of Kangaroo Point

Plan on 20 to 30 minutes unless you are bringing a book to a bench. Most people pair it with a longer walk along the peninsula.

## Timing Around the Temple Schedule

The posted hours belong to the temple. Check them before you set out, because two days are fully closed:

- Monday: closed
- Tuesday: 4:30pm to 8:15pm only
- Wednesday to Friday: 7:30am to 8:15pm
- Saturday: 6:30am to 7:15pm, closing an hour earlier
- Sunday: closed

Try not to come on a Saturday between 8am and 7pm. That is the busiest window of the week. The car park fills, and you may find yourself stepping around wedding parties having photos taken on the lawns.

A weekday morning after the 7:30am opening is the better choice. Wednesday to Friday evenings are good too, when the white stone picks up the last of the light. On Tuesdays, nothing opens until 4:30pm.

## Etiquette on the Temple Grounds

The grounds are open to visitors, but you are on sacred ground. A few habits make the visit easy for you and for the members arriving to worship:

- **Dress modestly.** Members arrive in their Sunday best. Beachwear or gym gear stands out here, even on a hot Brisbane afternoon.
- **Keep your voice down.** Treat the lawns as you would a cathedral forecourt, not a park picnic spot.
- **Photograph the outside, not the people.** Exterior shots are normal. Ask before photographing a bridal couple or a family.
- **Stay off the doorstep.** Staff at the entrance check recommends, and walking up to the doors uninvited causes confusion.
- **No drone flights, no picnics, no smoking.** These are the standard expectations on Latter-day Saint temple grounds.

If you are curious about the faith, the church runs public meetinghouses around Brisbane. Those are open to visitors at Sunday services, unlike the temple.

## Getting to Kangaroo Point

The temple is at 200 River Terrace, Kangaroo Point QLD 4169, just across the river from the CBD.

- **On foot from the city:** cross the Kangaroo Point Green Bridge or the Story Bridge, then follow River Terrace along the clifftop.
- **By ferry:** CityCat and cross-river ferries stop at several terminals on the peninsula. Most are a short uphill walk from River Terrace.
- **By bike:** the riverside paths link up with the Brisbane Riverwalk network, and River Terrace has a cycle lane.
- **By car:** temple parking is meant for people attending, so don't count on it on a Saturday.

## Nearby on the Peninsula

The temple fits into a wider afternoon on Kangaroo Point. Close by:

- **Kangaroo Point Cliffs:** old volcanic-rock quarry faces with climbers on them by day and floodlights after dark, plus picnic lawns and stairs down to the river.
- **Story Bridge:** the steel cantilever bridge at the northern tip of the peninsula, with footpaths across and a guided climb.
- **South Bank:** a ferry hop or a walk across the Goodwill Bridge brings you to the Parklands, the Streets Beach lagoon and the Cultural Centre galleries.

The light works best if you see the temple first and then walk to the cliffs as the sun sets behind the CBD towers. It is a quiet start to a busy evening by the river.
