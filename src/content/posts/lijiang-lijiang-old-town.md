## Why Lijiang Old Town still feels like a find

Most visitors picture Lijiang as a postcard, but the town itself rewards patience. Wander a few lanes off the main drag and the souvenir noise fades fast. What's left is running water, grey tile roofs, and the low murmur of a place that's been continuously lived in for over 800 years.

This is the historic seat of the Naxi people, and their matrilineal Dongba culture still shapes the architecture and street life here. It's a UNESCO World Heritage Site, but with only a few hundred reviews logged online relative to its size, it hasn't been flattened into a checklist stop the way some Chinese heritage towns have.

## Getting there and getting oriented

Lijiang Sanyi Airport sits about 25–28km from the old town, roughly a 40-minute taxi or shuttle bus ride. Lijiang Railway Station, with high-speed links to Kunming and Dali, is closer, around 20 minutes away.

Once you arrive, ditch any idea of a grid. The old town is built on a slope beside Elephant Hill, with canals fed by the Yuquan River branching into hundreds of narrow alleys.

- Sifang Square is the natural center and easiest place to reorient if lost
- Mu Family Mansion marks the southern edge, worth the climb for roof-tile views
- Wangu Tower on the hillside gives you the best overview of the rooftops below

## What to see and do inside the walls

Start at Sifang Square in the early morning, before tour groups fill it. Follow the water uphill — locals have long used the canal flow to judge direction, since the current runs from northwest to southeast.

Duck into the residential lanes east of the square, where laundry still hangs over stone bridges and older residents sit on their doorsteps. The Black Dragon Pool, at the old town's northern edge, frames Jade Dragon Snow Mountain on clear days — go for the reflection shot in still morning air.

In the evening, the wooden balconies along the main canal light up with red lanterns, and you'll hear snatches of Naxi folk music drifting from small courtyard bars.

## What to eat

Naxi cuisine is heavier and more herbal than the Yunnan food you'll find elsewhere in the province.

- Baba: a flaky Naxi flatbread, savory or sweet, sold from street-side griddles
- Lijiang smoked bacon (larou): stewed with local greens or hot pot
- Yak cheese skewers: grilled and dipped in chili powder, sold near the main squares
- Sour plum drinks (suanmeitang): a local cooling drink for the afternoon heat

## When to go

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) bring mild days and clearer mountain views. Summer afternoons see sudden rain, so carry a light shell rather than an umbrella for the alleys.

Try to be inside the gates by 8am. By mid-morning, day-trip buses from Kunming and Dali start unloading, and the main lanes narrow with foot traffic.

## How to visit like a local

Skip the daytime rush and come back after 9pm instead. The lantern light on the canals is at its best once the tour groups have retreated to their hotels, and the alleys empty out enough to actually hear the water.

Pay by mobile where you can — Alipay and WeChat Pay are standard even at small snack stalls, and cash change can be slow to find. Cards are rarely accepted outside larger shops.

If you're visiting certain paid sights or staying overnight in a old-town guesthouse, you may be charged a old town "protection fee" — this funds the site's upkeep and is usually collected once, not per entry, so keep the ticket or receipt.

The mistake most first-timers make is treating Lijiang as a two-hour photo stop. Stay a night instead. Once the day-trippers leave, the town reverts to something closer to what it actually is: a working neighborhood with grandmothers airing quilts and cats asleep on warm stone steps.

Learn a few Naxi or Mandarin basics before you go — a simple "ni hao" (hello) or "xie xie" (thank you) at a family-run baba stall goes further here than in more heavily touristed corners of China.
