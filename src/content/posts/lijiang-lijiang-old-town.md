## Why Lijiang Old Town still surprises you

You expect a museum piece. What you get is a living neighborhood.

Lijiang's old town — Dayan, to use its proper name — has been continuously inhabited by the Naxi people for over 800 years, its street grid unusually organized around water rather than a central axis. Snowmelt from Yulong Snow Mountain is channeled through a network of canals that splits and re-splits under nearly every lane, so you're forever crossing tiny stone bridges without quite meaning to.

The cobblestones are local marble, polished smooth and faintly reflective after centuries of foot traffic. No cars can follow you in — the alleys are too narrow and too crooked, which is exactly why getting lost here feels productive rather than frustrating.

## Getting there and getting oriented

Lijiang Sanyi International Airport sits about 25–30 minutes by taxi or airport shuttle from the old town's edge. High-speed rail from Kunming (roughly 4 hours) is the other common route, arriving at Lijiang Railway Station, itself around 20 minutes away by taxi.

There's no single "entrance" — the old town is an open district you simply walk into. Most visitors orient themselves from Sifang Square, the paved market plaza at the historic center, or from the Old Town's north gate near Jade Spring Park.

Drop a pin before you go. Street names change block to block and phone signal inside the stone-walled lanes can be patchy.

## What to see and do

Start at Sifang Square in the cool part of the morning, then let the canals guide you outward.

- **Jade Spring Park (Black Dragon Pool)** — a short walk north, with the classic postcard view of Yulong Snow Mountain reflected in the pool.
- **Mu Family Mansion** — the restored residence of the Naxi hereditary chieftains, worth the climb for the tiled roofscape it reveals.
- **Wangu Tower** — the highest point in the old town, reachable by a short uphill walk, giving a full rooftop panorama.
- **Naxi orchestra performances** — traditional Dongjing music is still performed most evenings in venues near the square; ask locally for the current schedule.

Budget a full day for the old town alone, two if you want to fold in Black Dragon Pool and a slower pace through the residential lanes east of the main tourist strip, where laundry hangs over the water and shopfronts give way to actual front doors.

## When to go

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) bring the clearest mountain views and the mildest weather. Summer is greener but wetter, with afternoon downpours common.

Chinese national holidays — especially Golden Week in early October — bring heavy domestic tourism; the lanes near Sifang Square can genuinely jam up. Outside those windows, this remains a comparatively under-the-radar stop relative to Lijiang's bigger set-piece sights.

Early morning, before the day-tour buses arrive, and evening after they leave are consistently the calmest hours to walk the core streets.

## How to visit like a local

Skip the map app once you're inside — it will fight the crooked alleys and lose. Instead, navigate by landmark: uphill toward Wangu Tower, downhill and toward running water to find your way back to the square.

Mobile payment (Alipay or WeChat Pay) is standard even at small stalls, though setting up a foreign-linked account beforehand saves fumbling for cash later. Bargaining is expected at souvenir stalls, less so at food counters — watch what other customers pay before you offer.

Try Naxi-style baba (a flaky grilled flatbread) from a street stall rather than a sit-down restaurant menu; it's cheaper and closer to how it's actually eaten. The biggest mistake visitors make is treating the old town as a single afternoon photo stop — staying overnight inside its walls, after the tour groups bus back out, is when the canals and lantern-lit lanes actually feel like themselves.

Some homestays and boutique inns operate directly inside restored courtyard houses, which is worth seeking out over a hotel outside the walls.
