## Inside the stupa wall

A square of white wall runs along the open steppe, topped every few metres by a stupa. That wall is Erdene Zuu. Abtai Sain Khan founded the monastery in 1585, and much of it was built from stone and brick taken from the ruins of Karakorum, the 13th-century Mongol capital that once stood here.

Most of the enclosure is open grass. The surviving buildings sit in clusters across it, which is a result of the 1937 purges, when most of the site's temples were destroyed. It now forms part of the Orkhon Valley Cultural Landscape, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## What to see

Pace yourself. The interiors are compact, but the walking between them takes time.

- **The three Zuu temples** (Baruun Zuu, Gol Zuu, Zuun Zuu): Chinese-style halls with green-tiled roofs, holding statues of the Buddha at different ages, plus thangkas and masks.
- **Lavrin Süm**: a whitewashed Tibetan-style temple where monks still hold services.
- **The Golden Stupa**: the large gilded stupa in the middle of the grounds.
- **The gates and wall**: walk part of the circuit to see how far the stupas run.

Give yourself 1.5–2 hours. Add another hour if you also visit the Kharakhorum Museum, a short walk south, which covers the old capital through excavated finds. One of Karakorum's stone turtles sits on a low rise just outside the wall.

## Timing and getting to Kharkhorin

Kharkhorin is roughly 360km west of Ulaanbaatar, a full day's drive on paved road. Buses leave from the capital's Dragon bus station. Most visitors come with a tour or a hired driver and stay overnight in a ger camp.

At the moment the monastery is open 9am–6pm daily. Temple hours change with the season, so check before you plan around a late visit.

Go when it opens. The quietest window is 9am–10am on both weekdays and weekends. Try not to arrive between 11am and 2pm on a weekend, when tour groups fill the small temple interiors. Summer brings the most visitors overall, and the steppe wind is cold for much of the rest of the year.

## Moving through a working monastery

Erdene Zuu is still a place of worship, not a museum.

- Walk clockwise around the temples and stupas, the way pilgrims do.
- Don't point your feet at altars, and step over thresholds rather than on them.
- Photography inside the temples is often restricted or ticketed separately, so ask at the entrance first.
- If a service is underway in Lavrin Süm, stand along the side and stay quiet. Offerings and prayer wheels belong to worshippers, so watch before you join in.
- Entry and photo permit prices are posted at the gate. Carry Mongolian tögrög, because card acceptance in Kharkhorin is patchy.

Shoulders and knees should be covered. A windproof layer is useful too: the grounds are open, with little shade or shelter between buildings.
