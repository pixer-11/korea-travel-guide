## Daniel Caesar in Núñez on December 5, 2026

The Toronto singer plays an indoor room. That matters for a voice like his. Daniel Caesar performs at Estadio Obras Sanitarias in Buenos Aires on **December 5, 2026**. The arena is a basketball venue that has hosted rock shows for decades, so you hear the falsetto up close instead of across a football pitch.

December is early summer in Buenos Aires. The evenings are long and warm, and the walk along Avenida del Libertador to the doors is usually done in a T-shirt.

## The songs that fill the room

Caesar's catalogue is built for quiet singalongs. His 2017 debut *Freudian* gave him "Get You" with Kali Uchis and "Best Part" with H.E.R. The second song won a Grammy and is still the one a whole arena knows by heart.

Later records pushed further:

- ***Case Study 01*** (2019): moodier and more experimental, with "Cyanide" and "Love Again".
- ***NEVER ENOUGH*** (2023): includes "Always" and "Let Me Go".
- **Features**: "Peaches" with Justin Bieber and Giveon brought him a far wider pop audience.

## Inside the Templo del Rock

Obras is a sports hall first. Tiered seating rises around a rectangular floor where the basketball court sits. For concerts that floor becomes the standing *campo*, with the stage at one end. The stands, called *plateas*, wrap the remaining sides, so even the back rows feel close to the stage.

Its nickname comes from its history. Argentine rock bands and touring international acts have played here since the late 1970s. Local fans treat a show at Obras as something of a rite of passage. The building is plain concrete and functional, and it sounds better than it looks.

## Reaching Obras without a car

The arena sits at Avenida del Libertador 7395, close to River Plate's Estadio Monumental. On a night when both venues have events, the area jams quickly.

The usual routes:

- **Tren Mitre**: the Tigre branch from Retiro stops at Núñez station. From there it's a walk of roughly ten blocks toward the river.
- **Subte Line D**: the terminus, Congreso de Tucumán, sits on Avenida Cabildo. From there it's a longer walk east, or a short taxi ride.
- **Colectivos**: plenty of city bus lines run along Libertador and stop near the arena.

Trains, Subte and buses all run on the rechargeable SUBE card. You can't pay cash on board. After the show, most people walk back toward Cabildo or call a ride-hail app from a side street, away from the crush on Libertador.

## How a show night at Obras usually runs

Porteño crowds have their own routines. People often eat before the show at a *parrilla* or pizzería along Cabildo or Libertador. Groups also hold a *previa*, a few drinks at someone's flat. Dinner after a concert is normal here, since kitchens in Buenos Aires keep cooking well past ten.

The floor works on first come, first in. Fans who want the barrier usually queue outside along the avenue long before doors. Seated ticket holders tend to arrive later.

Most of the neighbourhood is apartment blocks, so the interest is nearby rather than on your doorstep. Parque de la Memoria, a memorial park and sculpture garden on the Río de la Plata, is a short ride north along the Costanera. It's worth a slow afternoon before an evening show.
