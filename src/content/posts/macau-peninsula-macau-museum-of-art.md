## Galleries above the reclaimed waterfront

The Macau Museum of Art is a quiet place. It occupies part of the Macao Cultural Centre, the large modern complex on Avenida Xian Xing Hai. The building faces the Outer Harbour, on land reclaimed in the 1990s, and the museum opened in 1999.

The galleries are spacious, with room to step back from a long scroll. They are laid out across several floors. Expect these strands:

- **Chinese painting and calligraphy**, including hanging scrolls and album leaves
- **Ceramics**, with Guangdong's Shiwan pottery among the strengths
- **Historical pictures of Macau and the China coast**, made during the trade era
- **Rotating temporary shows**, often loaned from mainland or overseas museums

Plan on 1.5 to 2 hours if you read the labels. Labels are in Chinese, Portuguese and English.

## Reaching Avenida Xian Xing Hai

You won't find a metro station nearby, so most visitors walk or take a taxi. The Kun Iam Statue, the bronze goddess on the seawall, is a short walk along the waterfront. The Macao Science Center's cone-shaped building is a little further east along the same harbour.

From the casino strip around the Grand Lisboa and Wynn, it is a flat walk south-east through the NAPE grid. Allow roughly 15 to 20 minutes. Taxi drivers know the museum best by its Chinese name, **澳門藝術博物館**. The Portuguese name is **Museu de Arte de Macau**.

## Timing your visit

The museum is open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 7pm, with the same hours every open day. **It is closed all day Monday**, and a lot of visitors only find this out at the door.

Try not to arrive after 11am on a Saturday or Sunday. Weekends stay at their busiest from 11am until closing at 7pm. A weekday visit avoids that crowd completely. Arriving at 10am on a weekend also gets you ahead of it.

If you come late in the day, give yourself time before the 7pm close. Then walk out to the Kun Iam Statue and the harbour promenade as the light drops.

## Making the most of the Cultural Centre visit

This is one of the city's quieter cultural stops, well away from the queues at the Ruins of St. Paul's. Locals don't treat it as a favourite hangout; it is simply calmer than the old-town sights. Here is how to get the most out of it:

- **Check the temporary show first.** The museum website lists current exhibitions, and some visitors come for those alone. Admission details are posted there and at the front desk, so check before you go.
- **Look for photo signs in each gallery.** Rules can differ between the permanent collection and loaned exhibits.
- **Keep your voice low.** The large galleries carry sound, and other visitors come for long, slow looking.
- **Pair it with the waterfront.** Combine the museum with the Kun Iam Statue and the Science Center for a half day in NAPE. The Macau Tower is a short taxi ride south-west.

Payment at the shop or nearby cafés is in Macau patacas. Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted across the city, and so are cards. If you want something from the shop, ask at the counter which payment methods it takes before you choose.
