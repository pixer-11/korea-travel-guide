## Five rings, one medal plaza

This is where the 2010 medals were handed out. During the Vancouver Winter Olympics, Whistler Olympic Plaza held the nightly medal ceremonies for events staged on the mountains above. The large rings sculpture stayed behind as the plaza's permanent marker. Today it is the most-photographed spot on Village Stroll.

The rings sit on a broad open square at the edge of the pedestrian village. Mountains rise behind them on clear days. In winter the plaza usually runs an outdoor skating rink, and in summer the lawn and stage host concerts and festivals. Either way, the rings remain the fixed point.

## Reaching Village Stroll

There is no road access to the rings themselves. Whistler Village is car-free, so you arrive on foot along Village Stroll, the cobbled walkway linking the shops and hotels.

- **From Vancouver:** roughly two hours north on Highway 99, the Sea-to-Sky Highway.
- **Parking:** the municipal Day Lots sit on the edge of the village. Rates are posted at the lot pay stations.
- **From the gondolas:** a few minutes' walk from the base of Whistler and Blackcomb.
- **By bus:** local transit stops at Whistler Village Gateway Loop, a short stroll away.

## Getting the shot without a crowd

Timing matters more here than anywhere else in the village. On weekends the plaza is calmest from 7am to 8am and again from 12pm to 1pm. Try not to arrive between 1pm and 11pm on a Saturday or Sunday. That is when skiers come off the hill, families gather, and a line forms for photos in front of the rings.

The sculpture is a public artwork in an open plaza, so there is no ticket, no gate and no closing time. That makes the 7am slot easy to manage. Grab a coffee on the Stroll, get your shot in soft morning light, and you will still beat the first lift crowds.

Expect a loose, informal queue in the busy hours. Groups take turns standing in front of the rings, so keep your session short when others are waiting. Offering to take a photo for the group behind you is common, and they will usually do the same for you. Climbing on the sculpture is discouraged.

For the full ring with the peaks above it, stand well back toward the far side of the plaza. A wide-angle setting helps. In winter the rink boards can get into the frame, so shoot from a slight angle.

## Around the plaza

Plan on 15–30 minutes for the rings, and longer if the rink or a summer concert is on. The Whistler Public Library sits right beside the plaza and has warm seating on cold days. The Audain Art Museum is a short walk away on Blackcomb Way. Its collection of British Columbia art, including Northwest Coast First Nations works, makes a good indoor follow-up.

If you want more Olympic history, the 2010 venues are spread around the valley. Whistler Creekside hosted the alpine skiing. The Whistler Sliding Centre on Blackcomb's lower slopes ran bobsleigh, luge and skeleton. Whistler Olympic Park in the Callaghan Valley held the Nordic events.
