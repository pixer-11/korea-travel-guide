## A voice that never left

Few singers still fill halls three decades after their death. Teresa Teng, the Taiwanese star who died in 1995, is one of them. Her ballads still play in coffee shops, taxis and karaoke rooms across Chinese-speaking Asia, and Malaysia is no exception.

*As If She Were Here* is a tribute series built around Chen Jia, a singer known for interpreting Teng's repertoire. It runs from November 7 to 29, 2026, a long residency rather than a one-night stop. That gives the season a different feel: audiences here tend to be families spanning three generations, many of whom know every lyric.

Teng's catalogue is the draw. Even casual listeners recognise songs like these:

- **月亮代表我的心** (*The Moon Represents My Heart*)
- **甜蜜蜜** (*Tian Mi Mi*)
- **小城故事** (*Small Town Story*)

## Inside Dewan Filharmonik PETRONAS

The hall sits between the twin towers. It is a shoebox-shaped auditorium, the classic form for acoustic music, and it is the home of the Malaysian Philharmonic Orchestra. The interior is warm timber, with ornate ceiling work drawing on Malaysian and Islamic geometric motifs.

Above the stage, a large pipe organ rises across the back wall. The room is intimate by arena standards, so even the upper tiers feel close to the singer. For a voice-led tribute show, that matters more than spectacle.

Acoustics favour unamplified detail. Breaths, a soft vibrato and the tail of a held note carry clearly to the back rows.

## Reaching KLCC on a concert night

The simplest route is the LRT. Take the Kelana Jaya Line to **KLCC Station**, then follow the underground link into Suria KLCC, the mall beneath the towers. Signs inside point to the concert hall's entrance at the towers' base.

Other options:

- **Grab or taxi**: drop-off at the Suria KLCC entrances on Jalan Ampang is the usual choice.
- **Driving**: the KLCC complex has large underground car parks, though exiting after a full house takes time.
- **Walking**: from Bukit Bintang, the covered pedestrian walkway to KLCC runs through the Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre side and keeps you out of the rain.

November falls in Kuala Lumpur's wetter months, with heavy late-afternoon downpours common. The indoor LRT and walkway routes are the ones most attendees use for that reason.

## Etiquette at the Filharmonik

This hall keeps a more formal tone than most KL venues. It has a long-standing dress code, and smart casual is the norm: concertgoers generally avoid shorts, sleeveless tops and flip-flops. Office clothes or a neat batik shirt fit right in.

Doors close once the performance begins. Latecomers usually wait for a suitable break before ushers seat them, so regulars tend to arrive with time to spare and use the foyer instead.

A few house habits worth knowing:

- Photography and recording during the performance are not allowed, and ushers do enforce it.
- Phones go fully silent, not just on vibrate, in a room this acoustically live.
- Singing along is tempting with songs this familiar. Audiences typically hold back until an encore or until the performer invites it.

## Before the curtain and after the encore

The towers give you an easy evening around the show. Suria KLCC has food courts and sit-down restaurants across several floors, from quick noodles to full dinners. Many concertgoers eat there and walk straight to the hall.

Behind the mall, **KLCC Park** stretches out with jogging paths and lawns. The **Lake Symphony** fountain sits on the esplanade facing the park, and its evening water shows draw a crowd of photographers.

After the show, the towers are lit against the night sky. The park side gives the classic straight-up view. For a later meal, Jalan Alor's street-food strip in Bukit Bintang is a short ride or a covered walk away.

Allow roughly an evening for the whole outing: dinner, the concert and a stroll outside. With a run this long, many visitors fit it into a broader KL stay rather than planning a trip around a single night.
