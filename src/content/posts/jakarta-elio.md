Plaza Senayan is one of Jakarta's older upscale malls, the kind of place with marble floors and a roster of tenants that's shifted over decades, so a restaurant here generating this much conversation is worth noticing. Elio is what's doing it right now. It's built around a coastal Mediterranean idea, but not one country's coast: the menu pulls from Greece, Southern Italy, Spain, Portugal and the Middle East at once, which is a wider net than most "Mediterranean" restaurants in this city usually cast.

## What's actually on the table

The dishes people keep mentioning are lamb ribs, kofta skewers and mezze platters, the kind of food built for a table of four or more rather than a solo weeknight dinner. That mezze-first structure means:

- Expect shared starters to arrive first and set the pace of the meal
- Grilled proteins like the lamb ribs and kofta read as the centerpiece, not a side
- The format rewards ordering across regions rather than sticking to one cuisine

If you're used to Jakarta's more singular Mediterranean spots, Italian trattorias or Lebanese grills that stay in their lane, Elio's pitch is that it doesn't pick a lane. Whether that lands as coherent or scattered on any given visit is the kind of thing worth judging for yourself rather than taking a stranger's word for.

## Finding it inside Plaza Senayan

Plaza Senayan sits in the Senayan area of South Jakarta, near the Gelora Bung Karno sports complex, and is well known enough that any rideshare driver or taxi will know it by name. Because it's a mall restaurant rather than a standalone address, the practical challenge isn't the neighborhood, it's the floor and unit number inside a large multi-level building.

Before you go, look up Elio's current floor location through the mall directory or the restaurant's own listing, since mall tenants shift levels more often than street-level restaurants do. Parking is structured mall parking, which in Jakarta traffic is usually the easier option compared to trying to street-park nearby.

## Why this opening is getting attention

Jakarta's dining scene has no shortage of new restaurants, but not many get called one of the city's most talked-about openings. Part of what separates Elio is the cross-regional concept itself: coastal Mediterranean cooking spanning four distinct culinary traditions is a genuinely different proposition from the single-country Mediterranean restaurants that dominate the city's fine-dining conversation. That kind of positioning tends to draw both serious diners and the social-media crowd, which is worth knowing going in.

## The kofta and lamb ribs question

If you only remember two dishes going in, make them the kofta skewers and the lamb ribs, since those are the two dishes most consistently mentioned by people describing the place. Kofta, spiced ground meat formed onto skewers and grilled, is a Middle Eastern staple that Elio is using as one of its anchor dishes rather than an afterthought on a mezze list.

Lamb ribs sit on the heavier, slower-cooked end of the menu, the kind of dish that suggests a kitchen willing to commit real time to a single centerpiece rather than turning tables fast.

Order both if your group size allows it, and treat the rest of the mezze platter as the connective tissue between them rather than the main event.

## Booking a table without a wasted trip

Because Elio is trending and sits inside a busy mall, walking in and hoping for a table is a riskier bet here than at a quieter standalone restaurant. Call ahead or use whatever online reservation channel the restaurant currently lists, and reconfirm your reservation, the operating hours and the exact floor location on the day of your visit. Mall restaurant hours can also shift around mall-wide events or holidays in ways that a standalone restaurant's wouldn't, so don't rely on hours you saw online weeks ago.

If you're going as a larger group specifically for the shared-plate format, mention the group size when booking. Restaurants built around sharing platters often seat groups differently than couples or solo diners, and a kitchen expecting a two-top will plate very differently than one expecting six.

## Pacing a meal built for sharing

Treat an Elio meal the way you'd treat a proper mezze dinner anywhere around the Mediterranean: start slow, order in waves, and don't rush the grilled centerpieces out alongside the cold starters. Ask your server how the kitchen prefers the courses paced, since a menu spanning four culinary traditions can be plated all at once or staggered depending on how the kitchen has set up service.

Splitting the lamb ribs and kofta across the table rather than ordering one of each per person keeps the meal feeling like the coastal, communal format it's aiming for, rather than a plated Western-style dinner that happens to have Mediterranean flavors.

Come hungry and come with people willing to share plates rather than guard their own order, since that's clearly the format this kitchen has designed around.
