Wanaka is a small town that fills up every ski season and every summer. Nearly everyone staying here needs food at some point. For many of them, the answer is a supermarket on Dunmore Street, a couple of blocks back from the lake.

New World Wānaka is not a secret. It's one of the most-reviewed shops in town and well known to visitors. It is still the most useful stop of a Wanaka trip, as long as you time it well.

## Why the supermarket shapes your Wanaka days

Dining out in a resort town adds up quickly. Most holiday rentals, motels and campervans here come with a kitchen, and this is where those kitchens get filled.

New World is one of New Zealand's two big national supermarket brands. Its stores belong to the Foodstuffs cooperative and each one is run by a local owner-operator. In practice you get a full-range supermarket rather than a convenience shop: fresh produce, meat, dairy, bread, household basics, beer and wine.

## Finding 20 Dunmore Street

Dunmore Street runs through Wanaka's compact town centre and meets Ardmore Street, the lakefront road. From the waterfront by Roys Bay, it's a short walk uphill. That makes it easy to combine with a stroll along the lake or a coffee in town.

If you're driving in from Queenstown over the Cardrona Valley Road, or from Haast Pass in the north, you finish right in the centre. Many visitors stop here on the way in, before they check in. That habit is one reason the store gets busy in the afternoon.

## The 7am window before Roys Peak

The doors currently open at 7am every day. Early starts are normal in Wanaka. The Roys Peak Track is a long, steep, full-day climb. The road to Mount Aspiring National Park and the Rob Roy Glacier track is long and unsealed. The ski fields at Treble Cone and Cardrona fill their car parks early in winter.

So the first hours after opening work twice over. They're among the quietest of the day, and you can pick up lunch and snacks before you head out. A typical trail bag might hold:

- Bread, cheese and fruit for a summit lunch
- Snack bars and chocolate for the climb
- Bottled water if your accommodation tap water doesn't suit your bottle
- Sunscreen, which you'll need on exposed tracks like Roys Peak

One timing note: Roys Peak closes every year for lambing, from 1 October to 10 November. Check the Department of Conservation website before you build a morning around it.

## Paying, bagging and the ID rule

New Zealand supermarkets have their own habits, and knowing them saves you an awkward moment at the till.

- **Bring a bag.** Single-use plastic shopping bags have been banned nationally since 2019. Bring a reusable bag or buy one at the checkout.
- **Card is normal.** Contactless card payment and local EFTPOS are standard. There's no tipping in supermarkets, or anywhere much in New Zealand.
- **Clubcard pricing.** Some shelf tags show a lower Clubcard price for members of New World's loyalty scheme. The regular price is on the same tag, so read both.
- **Alcohol needs proper ID.** Supermarkets here sell beer and wine but not spirits. Staff ask for ID from anyone who looks under 25. Accepted ID is a passport, a New Zealand driver licence or a Kiwi Access card. An overseas driver licence usually won't do, so carry your passport if you plan to buy wine.

Self-checkouts are common in New World stores. Alcohol bought at one still needs a staff member to check your ID.

## Stocking a lakeside dinner

Wanaka's evening ritual is eating outdoors by the water as the light fades over the mountains. The lawns along the Roys Bay foreshore are a short walk from Dunmore Street. You don't need a reservation, just a bag of groceries.

The town sits in Central Otago, one of the country's best-known pinot noir regions. It's worth looking along the wine shelves for local bottles. Pair one with cheese, bread and fruit and you have the classic Wanaka picnic.

If you're camping, remember that freedom camping around Lake Wānaka is tightly controlled. Most sites are limited to certified self-contained vehicles. Buy food for where you're actually allowed to stay, and take your rubbish with you.

## Beating the weekend crush

The pattern is clear. On both weekdays and weekends, the quietest times are 7am–10am and 8pm–10pm. On weekends, the store is busiest from 11am to 6pm.

That afternoon peak lines up with the moment visitors roll in from Queenstown and day hikers come off the hills. Aisles get crowded and checkout queues grow. Avoid it if you can.

There are two simple ways round it:

- **Shop early.** Combine the trip with your morning departure and you're done before 10am.
- **Shop late.** Doors currently close at 10pm, so after dinner, from 8pm, is a calm time for tomorrow's supplies.

In the peak ski and summer holiday weeks, expect busy spells beyond these hours too. Hours can change on public holidays, so check the store's listing before planning around them.

## Around the block

Everything near the store is walkable, which is why a quick shop fits so easily into a Wanaka day.

- **The lakefront and Roys Bay:** a few minutes downhill, with lawns, a beach and the lakeside path.
- **That Wānaka Tree:** the much-photographed willow growing in the water at the western end of Roys Bay, a pleasant walk along the shore.
- **Wanaka town centre:** cafés, outdoor gear shops and restaurants along Ardmore, Helwick and Dunmore streets.
- **Puzzling World:** the illusion-and-maze attraction on the road out of town towards Cardrona, a short drive away.

Do your shopping, walk down to the water, and the hard part of the day is already done.
