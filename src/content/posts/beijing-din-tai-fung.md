## Eighteen folds on Xinyuan Xili

You come here for one dumpling. Din Tai Fung began as a cooking-oil shop in Taipei and became famous for xiaolongbao, the small steamed soup dumpling. The chain says each one is pleated with eighteen folds. The Beijing branch is on Xinyuan Xili Zhongjie, a residential and embassy-district street in Chaoyang, and it is consistently well rated.

It doesn't have the global crowds of the Taipei flagship. With only a modest number of online reviews, this branch is a quieter find than its name suggests. It's mid-range: more than a noodle stall, much less than a hotel dining room.

## The xiaolongbao and what goes around them

Start with the classic pork xiaolongbao. They arrive in a bamboo steamer basket, thin-skinned, so the broth inside shows a little through the dough. Order one basket for every one or two people, then build the rest of the meal around it.

Dishes the chain is known for, though menus vary by branch:

- **Pork xiaolongbao**, the benchmark
- **Crab and pork xiaolongbao**, richer and slightly sweet
- **Shrimp and pork wontons in chilli oil**, for heat
- **Shrimp fried rice**, plain and well made
- **Braised beef noodle soup**, the Taiwanese standard
- **Marinated cucumber**, to cut the richness
- **Sweet taro or red bean buns** for dessert

Look at the menu for anything seasonal or specific to the mainland. Branches often add regional dishes alongside the core list.

## Spoon, ginger, vinegar: the soup-dumpling ritual

A xiaolongbao holds hot broth, and biting straight in is how people burn their mouths. Each table gets a small dish for shredded ginger plus black vinegar and soy sauce. Din Tai Fung suggests roughly three parts vinegar to one part soy.

1. Lift the dumpling by its pleated top knot with your chopsticks.
2. Set it in your soup spoon. Don't let it hang from the chopsticks, or it tears.
3. Bite a small hole in the side and sip the broth.
4. Add a few strands of ginger dipped in the sauce, then eat the rest in one or two bites.

At many branches the dumpling makers work behind a glass window, weighing and folding. If this one has a window, take a look while you wait.

## Finding the door in Chaoyang

The restaurant is in Xinyuanli, a quiet area north of the Third Ring Road's Liangmaqiao stretch. The nearest subway is **Liangmaqiao Station on Line 10**, and the restaurant is a short walk or quick taxi ride from there. Check the exit on a map app before you set off, because the station is spread out.

The easiest way in is a ride-hailing app with the Chinese address pasted in: **朝阳区新源西里中街24号**. Taxi drivers rarely read English addresses. Show the characters on your screen and you'll get there without a phone call.

## Ordering and paying at the table

Din Tai Fung branches usually hand you a printed order sheet, or a QR code to order on your phone. You mark quantities against each dish and pass it back. If the sheet is only in Chinese, ask for an English or picture menu. Staff at chain branches are used to the request.

Payment works like the rest of mainland China:

- **WeChat Pay and Alipay** are the default. International visitors can now link a foreign card to either app, so set this up before you travel.
- Cards and cash may be accepted, but don't rely on them alone.
- **Tipping isn't customary** in mainland restaurants, so you don't need to add anything.

Baskets come out whenever they're ready, not in courses. Share everything from the middle of the table, which is how the menu is meant to be eaten. Order a second basket of xiaolongbao partway through, once you know your appetite. That beats ordering too much at the start and letting the dumplings go cold.

## Getting a table without the rush

At the moment, hours run from 10:30am to 10:30pm every day of the week, so you have plenty of choice. In most Beijing restaurants the pressure comes at standard meal times: around noon for office lunches and from early evening for dinner. Sitting down shortly after opening, or in the mid-afternoon lull, usually beats those peaks.

Weekend lunches in this residential area are likely to fill with families. If you're a group of six or more, it's worth asking about reserving. Smaller parties can generally just walk in. The long hours also make it a reliable late dinner, with last orders before the 10:30pm close.

## After the dumplings

The **Liangma River** runs close by, and its landscaped banks around Liangmaqiao make a good walk after a heavy meal. The embassy streets of the Sanlitun diplomatic area are nearby too, as is the **Lufthansa Center** (Yansha) shopping complex, a familiar Chaoyang landmark.

For the evening, **Sanlitun** is a short taxi ride south. It has Taikoo Li's shopping lanes and the area's bars. Ninety minutes is about right for the meal itself, so you can pair it with either one.
