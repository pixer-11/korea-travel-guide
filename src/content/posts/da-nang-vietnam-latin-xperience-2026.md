## Three days of salsa and bachata

Vietnam Latin Xperience 2026 runs October 2-4, 2026, in Da Nang. It is an annual festival built around two partner dances, salsa and bachata. The dates fall from Friday to Sunday, so a long weekend covers all of it.

Festivals in this genre usually split the day in two. Workshops run in daylight and social dancing goes late into the night. The organisers set the exact schedule, venue and ticket passes, so confirm timing, venue and tickets on the official site before booking anything that can't be refunded.

## Landing in Da Nang

Da Nang is one of the easiest cities in Vietnam to reach, and its airport sits almost inside town.

- **By air:** Da Nang International Airport (DAD) is roughly 15 minutes by car from the Han River centre. It has frequent domestic flights from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, plus regional international routes.
- **By rail:** Reunification Express trains stop at Da Nang Station, west of the river.
- **Getting around:** Grab is the default app for cars and motorbike taxis, and it works well for late rides home after a social.

There are two main places to stay. The east bank runs along My Khe Beach and Vo Nguyen Giap street. The west bank is the city centre around the Han River. Choose the bank the venue is on and your 2am rides home stay short.

## Floorcraft and manners at the socials

Social dance etiquette at an international festival is fairly universal. A few habits will make you welcome on the floor:

- **Asking:** anyone can ask anyone. Offer a hand and make eye contact. A "no thanks" needs no explanation, from either side.
- **Partners:** you do not need to arrive with one. Socials are built on rotating partners.
- **Thanking:** say thank you and walk your partner off the floor at the end of a set.
- **Space:** keep salsa slots and bachata frames compact when the room fills, and lead away from collisions.
- **Shoes:** wear suede- or leather-soled dance shoes. Rubber street soles grip and strain knees.
- **Sweat:** central Vietnam is warm and humid in early October. Pack two or three spare shirts, a small towel and deodorant for each night.

Carry Vietnamese dong in cash for street food and small shops. Hotels and Grab accept cards. Tipping isn't customary in Vietnam, but people appreciate it.

## Between workshops: weather and the city

October falls inside central Vietnam's rainy season, which runs roughly September to December. Heavy downpours and occasional typhoons are possible, so get travel insurance and keep day plans flexible.

On the free mornings, try these:

- **My Khe Beach:** a long, flat sand beach on the east bank, good for a recovery swim.
- **Son Tra Peninsula:** a forested headland and home to Linh Ung Pagoda with its giant white Lady Buddha statue.
- **Marble Mountains:** limestone hills with caves and shrines, south of the city.
- **Hoi An Ancient Town:** about 30 km south, roughly 45 minutes by car.
- **Dragon Bridge:** it breathes fire and water on Saturday and Sunday evenings, which fits before a late social.

To eat, try mì Quảng (turmeric noodles) and bánh xèo (crisp rice pancakes) around Han Market.
