## A Thursday night at the national stadium

Mid-December is early summer in Auckland. The sun sets late, so the opening part of an evening show usually plays out in daylight over the stands. The grass where the All Blacks play becomes a standing floor, and the seated tiers rise around three sides of it.

Eden Park has history of its own. It hosted the Rugby World Cup finals in 1987 and 2011, and the stands are steep and close to the field. That shape suits a stadium rock show, because even the upper rows feel near the stage.

## Getting to Eden Park without a car

Eden Park is in a residential pocket, and the surrounding streets are typically closed or restricted on major event nights. Driving in is the slow option. The main routes in:

- **Kingsland station** (Western Line): the closest station, a short walk down Sandringham Road to the stadium.
- **Morningside station** (Western Line): one stop further out, a quieter walk in from the north-west side.
- **Dominion Road and Sandringham Road buses**: they run in from the city and stop within walking distance of the gates.
- **On foot from the city**: around 4km from the CBD. Plenty of fans walk it on a warm December evening, mostly through Newton and Kingsland.

After the encore, the queue for Kingsland station is long and is managed with barriers. Many fans walk to Dominion Road and get a bus or rideshare from there.

## Eating and drinking before the show

Kingsland is the pre-gig neighbourhood. New North Road has a strip of bars, cafés and takeaways a few minutes' walk from the stadium. On concert nights it fills with band T-shirts from late afternoon.

Dominion Road, on the eastern side, is Auckland's best-known street for Chinese food. Dumpling houses and noodle shops line it for several kilometres. Mt Eden Village, further up, is calmer and has more sit-down restaurants.

Most visitors from out of town stay in the CBD or around Ponsonby. Both are an easy train, bus or walk away, and both still have food and bars open after the show.

Stadium entry follows a fixed routine. Eden Park enforces a bag size limit and searches bags at the gates, and the stadium's own site sets out what is allowed in. Fans who bring only a phone, a card and a light layer get through fastest.

A few habits make the night easier:

- **Paying**: inside the stadium, people pay for food, drinks and merchandise by card or phone.
- **Tipping**: New Zealanders don't usually tip at bars or food stands, and nobody expects it.
- **Merchandise**: tour stands open with the gates. The lines are shortest while the support act is on.

- **Water**: bars and water points get busy at the changeover before the headliner. People who need a refill tend to go during the support set.

Fans with floor tickets usually stay put once they have a spot. Leaving the floor means pushing back through a dense crowd.

## The band, then and now

Guns N' Roses formed in Los Angeles in 1985. Their 1987 debut, *Appetite for Destruction*, is one of the best-selling debut albums of all time. It gave them "Welcome to the Jungle", "Paradise City" and "Sweet Child o' Mine".

Axl Rose, Slash and Duff McKagan reunited in 2016, and the band has toured stadiums worldwide in the years since. Their shows are known for running long. Fans going to Eden Park usually plan their trip home around a late finish on a weeknight, with Friday work or flights the next morning.
