## Six hundred stalls under one roof

Picture an exhibition hall built for trade fairs, now filled with Christmas shopping. At the World Bazaar Festival, the World Trade Center Metro Manila becomes a grid of booths, with more than 600 exhibitors along aisles you can walk for hours. It is billed as the country's largest annual holiday charity bazaar.

The format is the Filipino Christmas bazaar on a very large scale. Small sellers, family businesses and brands set up side by side in rented booths. Shoppers drift from one to the next with bags looped over both wrists. Holiday entertainment runs alongside the shopping, so the hall works as an outing and not only a place to run errands.

Because it is a charity bazaar, every purchase feeds a bigger cause. That is part of why it has become an annual December fixture rather than a one-off sale.

## Dates for the 2026 edition

The 2026 edition runs from Friday, December 11 to Sunday, December 20. That is ten straight days, with two full weekends in the middle of the Christmas run-up.

The organiser publishes the opening hours, entry details and any ticket links on its official site and social pages. This guide gives no daily times because none were supplied to us.

## Two weekends, one payday

The calendar shapes this bazaar more than most events. In the Philippines, the 15th of the month is a standard payday. December also brings the legally mandated 13th-month pay, so many shoppers have extra money in their pockets that week. December 15, 2026 falls on a Tuesday, right in the middle of the bazaar.

Then there is Simbang Gabi. Its nine dawn Masses begin on December 16, and from then on the whole city feels the countdown to Christmas.

How the ten days usually play out:

- **Opening weekend (Dec 11–13):** first pick of the stock, with booths fully stocked
- **Midweek (Dec 14–17):** office workers come after their shifts, and payday shoppers come around the 15th
- **Closing weekend (Dec 18–20):** last-minute gift buying, and families coming on Saturday and Sunday

Weekdays are usually easier to move around in than weekends at Manila holiday fairs. That is general reasoning about how these fairs work, not crowd data for this one.

## The World Trade Center Metro Manila

The venue sits where Sen. Gil Puyat Avenue (still widely called Buendia) meets Diosdado Macapagal Boulevard. This is reclaimed bayside land in Pasay. It belongs to the same long strip as the Cultural Center of the Philippines complex and the Bay City district around SM Mall of Asia.

The building is a purpose-built convention and exhibition centre. Its large open hall is the reason a fair of this size fits under one roof. Inside, the experience is about aisles and booth numbers rather than streets. Most visitors simply walk it row by row.

## Reaching the bay end of Buendia

No rail line stops at the door. The closest rail link is Gil Puyat Station on LRT Line 1, on Taft Avenue, a few kilometres east along the same avenue. From there, visitors usually take a short taxi or Grab ride west, or a jeepney along Buendia heading toward the bay.

Grab and taxis are how most people arrive, especially those leaving with full shopping bags. Plenty of drivers head for the building's own drop-off area. Others get dropped at SM Mall of Asia and make the short trip over from there.

December traffic in Metro Manila is notoriously heavy. Roxas Boulevard, EDSA and the roads around Mall of Asia slow right down in the evenings and on weekends. Locals build that into their plans and often time their trips around rush hour.

## Paying, bargaining and hauling it home

This part of the trip has its own habits. Booths at Filipino bazaars are run by independent sellers, so payment varies from table to table.

- **Cash** works almost everywhere, and small notes make change easier at busy booths
- **E-wallets** like GCash and Maya are widely accepted by small sellers, often through a printed QR code taped to the table
- **Cards** are hit and miss, more common at brand booths than at small family stalls

A polite request for a discount is normal, especially if you buy several items from one seller. Ask "Last price na po?" with a smile. The "po" makes it respectful. Do not push hard on prices; many stalls are small businesses, and the event is a charity bazaar.

Regular shoppers bring a large foldable bag or a small trolley. With 600-plus booths, the bags pile up fast. Many visitors start with a lap of the whole hall before buying, because the same kind of item often turns up again three aisles over.

## Making a day of it on the bay

The bazaar sits in one of the easiest parts of Manila to fill a day. The best-known neighbour is SM Mall of Asia, a few minutes away, with its bayside promenade facing the sunset over Manila Bay. Many shoppers finish the day there with dinner.

Just north is the Cultural Center of the Philippines complex. Its theatres sometimes run holiday programmes in December. Further up Roxas Boulevard you reach Malate and Ermita, with Rizal Park and Intramuros beyond them for anyone giving the trip a full day.

Most people spend three to four hours inside the bazaar itself. That is enough to walk every aisle, stop for the entertainment and shop properly. Serious gift buyers often come back on a second day.
