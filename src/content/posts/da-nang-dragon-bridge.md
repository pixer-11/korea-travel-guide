## A steel dragon over the Han

This is six lanes of traffic under a yellow steel spine. The dragon's body rises and falls in arches along the 666-metre span, and its head sits at the eastern end, facing the sea. Dragon Bridge opened on 29 March 2013, the anniversary of Da Nang's liberation in 1975. It quickly became the city's best-known landmark.

After dark, thousands of LEDs run along the body and change colour through the night. It gets heavy traffic from both visitors and commuters, so don't expect it to yourself. Plan around the crowds instead.

## When the dragon breathes

The show happens on weekend nights. On Saturday and Sunday evenings the head shoots out bursts of fire, then sprays water over the road below. The show has long been held at 9pm, but it can be cancelled on some holidays or in bad weather, so confirm locally that day.

The weekend busiest hours are 6pm to 11pm, which covers the whole show window. If you come for the fire, arrive well before it starts. On weeknights the lights run without the show, and the pavements are much easier to walk.

## Where to stand, and getting soaked

Where you stand changes what you get. Your main options:

- **East bank, beside the head** (An Hai side): closest to the fire. Also the zone that gets drenched by the water blast.
- **On the bridge's pedestrian walkways**: close-up views of the arches, with traffic passing right beside you.
- **West bank riverfront on Bach Dang Street**: a side-on view of the full length, and you stay dry.
- **A Han River boat**: cruises leave from the Bach Dang riverfront. Timing one for show night puts you on the water underneath it.

Nearby, you have three good options for before or after:

- The **Museum of Cham Sculpture** is at the western end of the bridge.
- The **Carp Transforming into Dragon** statue and the **Love Lock Bridge** are a short walk north along the east bank.
- **Han Market** is up the west bank on Bach Dang.
- **My Khe Beach** is roughly two kilometres straight east of the head along Nguyen Van Linh.

## Show-night tactics

Locals treat the show as a quick stop. Many ride up by motorbike, watch from the kerb and leave as soon as the water ends. That's the easiest way to do it:

- **Book a Grab ahead or walk.** Pickups near the head are chaotic right after the show, so walk a few blocks off the bridge before hailing.
- **Protect your phone.** If you stand near the head, the water carries further than you'd expect. A zip bag or a quick retreat saves your camera.
- **Hold your spot.** The kerb space near the head fills well before the show. Once you're there, stay put.
- **Watch the traffic.** The walkways sit right beside moving motorbikes. Don't step into the road for a better angle.
- **Come back on a weeknight** to walk the full span and look at the dragon's scales without being in a crowd.

The bridge is open to walkers around the clock. A crossing takes about fifteen minutes, or half an hour if you stop for photos.
