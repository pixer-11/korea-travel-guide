## What's actually here

This is a residential-district park, not a monument. Rue de Rueil sits south of Colmar's canal-laced centre, past the ring of boulevards that mark the old town's edge.

Expect lawns, mature trees, gravel paths and benches rather than fountains or statuary. It reads as a place built for dog walks, prams and lunch breaks, not for sightseeing itineraries.

That's precisely its value: a spot to decompress between the crowded lanes of Petite Venise and the half-timbered squares everyone else is photographing.

## Getting there and timing it right

Rue de Rueil is walkable from the centre but not adjacent to it; plan on 15-20 minutes on foot from the Koïfhus or Place de la Cathédrale. Cycling is faster if you've rented a bike, as Colmar's flat and compact.

The park opens at 7:30am and closes at 7:30pm every day of the week, so evening picnics after a late dinner won't work.

Weekend crowd patterns are measured here: the calmest hours tend to fall in the morning on Saturdays and Sundays. Afternoons from 2pm to 7pm draw more local traffic, mostly families, so aim for the morning window if you want the lawns to yourself.

## Why it's rated so well

A 4.5-star average across 215 reviews is high for a park with no headline feature. That score comes from upkeep and calm, not spectacle: reviewers consistently note tidy grass, shade in summer, and an absence of the tour-group noise that fills Colmar's centre by mid-morning.

It's still under-the-radar by Colmar standards. No souvenir stalls, no ticket booth, no queue. Visitors who do find it tend to be staying nearby rather than following a guidebook detour.

## Fitting it into a Colmar day

Treat this as a pause, not a destination in itself. Pair it with a walk through the quieter southern streets rather than looping back through Petite Venise a second time.

**Practical notes:**
- Free entry, no booking, open 7:30am-7:30pm daily
- Bring your own coffee or snacks; no kiosk on site
- Benches and shade trees make it workable even in Alsace's warmer summer afternoons
- Best paired with a slow walk back into town rather than a special trip out

Locals use it as a shortcut between residential streets as much as a destination, so don't expect a landscaped centrepiece. What you get instead is fifteen minutes of quiet grass with nobody trying to sell you anything, which after a morning in the tourist core of Colmar is its own kind of relief.
