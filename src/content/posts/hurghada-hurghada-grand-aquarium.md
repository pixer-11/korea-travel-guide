## Why it earns a morning

You get the Red Sea without getting wet. The tanks bring the reef fish you would otherwise need a boat and a mask to see to eye level, and the underwater tunnel puts them above your head. That makes it a sensible plan for non-swimmers, small children and anyone who has had too much sun.

It goes beyond fish. Freshwater tanks sit alongside the marine ones, and a Bedouin life exhibit covers the desert side of the region. There is also a zoo section and a restaurant, so one ticket fills a half-day. It is one of the most-reviewed attractions in Hurghada and is consistently well rated, which also means it is rarely empty.

## Finding it on Villages Road

The address is Villages Road, Hurghada 1, on the main road that links the resort hotels along the coast. Most visitors come one of three ways:

- **Taxi or ride-hail** from any hotel on the strip. Agree the fare before you set off if the car has no meter.
- **Hotel tour desk packages**, which usually bundle entry with a return transfer.
- **Local microbuses** running along Villages Road, the cheapest option but the hardest one if you don't speak Arabic.

Ask your driver for "the Grand Aquarium" by name. On Villages Road that is clearer than a street number.

## Walking the route through

The layout works as a one-way loop. Smaller themed tanks come first, the tunnel is the centrepiece, and the Bedouin and zoo sections follow before you reach the restaurant. Allow 2–3 hours if you read the labels. Families with young children often take longer.

- **Tunnel:** take your time here. It is the part most people come for, and there is a lot to look at overhead.
- **Photos:** switch off your flash at the glass. It bounces straight back and bothers the animals.
- **Restaurant:** handy for a mid-visit break, so you don't need to leave the site to eat.

## Beating the tour-group wave

Being very popular shapes how you should time it. Hotel excursions tend to arrive in batches once transfers have done their morning rounds. Getting in close to the 9am opening usually means a quieter tunnel before the coaches unload.

- **Tickets:** current prices are on the official site and at the ticket window. Compare them with your hotel's package price, which may include transfer.
- **Payment:** carry some Egyptian pounds for taxis and small purchases, even if the ticket desk takes cards.
- **Tipping:** small tips, or *baksheesh*, are normal in Egypt for drivers and anyone who helps you. Keep low-value notes handy.
- **Heat:** it works well as a midday escape. Spend the cool early hours on the beach, then head indoors when the sun is at its strongest, and leave time before closing.

Opening times can shift with the season and on holidays. The 9am–6pm schedule showing at the moment is this week's, so confirm it before you book a driver.
