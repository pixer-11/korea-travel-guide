Paradise City sits a few minutes' drive from Incheon International Airport, on Yeongjongdo island, which makes it one of the easier concert venues to combine with a flight in or out of Korea. My Chemical Romance's November 7, 2026 date there put a mid-2000s American rock band in a venue better known for its casino, art collection, and spa than for guitar music, an unusual booking outside the usual K-pop touring circuit.

## Getting to Paradise City

Paradise City is inside the Incheon airport free economic zone, connected to Terminal 1 by a free shuttle bus that runs frequently and takes about 10 minutes.

- From Terminal 1: shuttle bus, roughly 10 minutes
- From Terminal 2: shuttle bus, slightly longer, plus an inter-terminal transfer for arrivals at T2
- From central Seoul: AREX airport express train to Incheon Airport, then the shuttle, or a taxi that can take over an hour depending on traffic

From Seoul proper, the journey takes at least 70-90 minutes each way, and late trains and shuttles thin out after midnight.

## What kind of venue this is

Paradise City isn't a purpose-built rock arena. It's a resort complex with multiple halls and event spaces, so the room used for this show was to be sized and configured specifically for the concert rather than being a fixed, year-round music venue.

In halls like these, sightlines, standing versus seated sections, and bag policies can differ from what you'd find at a dedicated arena like Incheon's own indoor stadiums. For Korean concerts, the hall name, floor plan, seating chart and entry gates usually appear on the ticket listing itself.

## Buying tickets

Tickets for touring international acts in Korea are typically sold through Korean ticketing platforms such as Interpark or Melon Ticket, sometimes with an international resale or verified-fan step for high-demand shows.

- Korean concert tickets are often split into seated, standing, or mixed sections, which affects both price and entry times
- Third-party resale sites often charge above face value
- Korean ticketing platforms usually require a local phone number or ID verification, which catches some overseas fans off guard

## Making a day (or two) of it

Because Paradise City sits right by the airport rather than in downtown Incheon or Seoul, a show there suits a dedicated fly-in visit or the start or end of a longer Korea trip. The resort itself has an art wing (Paradise Art Space), an indoor beach club-style pool complex, and several restaurants, so the hours before a show there need not be dead time.

Incheon's Songdo district and central Seoul are both under two hours away, which makes a same-day return possible without an airport-adjacent hotel.

## Fitting the show around flights

Because the venue is essentially on airport grounds, a red-eye out after the encore is a genuinely workable combination here, as long as the flight isn't scheduled too tightly against the show's finish. Crowds leaving a concert hit the shuttle and AREX at the same time, so three hours between the end of a show and check-in cutoff is a sensible margin.

Same-day arrivals face immigration and baggage claim at a major international airport, which can take 45 minutes to an hour before reaching the shuttle stop.
