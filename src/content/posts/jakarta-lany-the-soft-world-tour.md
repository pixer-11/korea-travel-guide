## Two nights of LANY in Jakarta

LANY's Jakarta visit was planned as two nights. The Los Angeles pop band brought The Soft World Tour to the city's calendar for October 29 and 30, 2026, part of a run through Asia and Australia. The band's catalogue is built around songs that made them a fixture of Indonesian playlists, including "ILYSB" and "Malibu Nights".

Few touring acts book a second Jakarta night, so two dates in one city pointed to strong demand.

The Indonesian dates were handled by an official local promoter alongside LANY's own tour announcements.

## Thursday or Friday: picking your night

The two dates fell on a Thursday and a Friday. In Jakarta, that difference shapes an evening more than visitors might think.

- **Thursday, October 29:** a working night, when getting across the city before doors means leaving early. Afterwards, the roads usually empty out faster than on a weekend eve.
- **Friday, October 30:** the start of the weekend in a city where Friday evening traffic is famously heavy. The upside is no alarm the next morning, and late-night food is easier to justify.
- **Flying in:** for visitors from elsewhere in Indonesia or the region, a Friday show leaves Saturday to recover. A Thursday show leaves Friday for the flight home.

Fans with tickets for both nights often sit or stand somewhere different on each. Seeing the stage from two angles is half the reason fans double up.

## Venue and tickets

The venue mattered more than usual, because Jakarta is a city of more than ten million people and a wrong guess puts you on the wrong side of it.

Jakarta's large international shows are spread widely. Some play the Senayan sports complex in Central Jakarta, and others play convention halls in the satellite cities to the south and west. The difference can be an hour or more in a car, which makes the choice of hotel area important.

Tickets came from LANY and the official Indonesian promoter, in several price categories. Resale offers on social media and from touts (*calo* in Indonesian) are the main way people lose money on Jakarta concerts.

## Crossing Jakarta on show night

Traffic in Jakarta is the real opening act. Trips take far longer than the map suggests, especially on Friday.

The main options:

- **MRT Jakarta:** the North–South line runs from Lebak Bulus to Bundaran HI along Jalan Sudirman and Jalan Thamrin. It is the fastest way through the central business corridor for venues near it.
- **TransJakarta:** the busway network has dedicated lanes on main roads. It is cheap and wide-reaching, but crowded at rush hour.
- **KRL Commuterline:** the suburban rail network is useful for venues out in satellite cities such as Tangerang.
- **Grab and Gojek:** ride-hailing cars and motorbike taxis (*ojek*) are everywhere. A motorbike slips through jams that hold cars still.

After a big concert, thousands of people open the same apps at once. Prices surge and drivers struggle to reach the gates. Walking ten minutes away from the venue before requesting a ride usually means a quicker pickup.

Late October sits at the turn into Jakarta's wet season, and heavy evening downpours are common. A light rain jacket is easier than a soaked commute home.

## Redeeming tickets the Jakarta way

Indonesian concerts have their own routine at the gate, and knowing it makes the queue less stressful.

- **ID that matches the ticket.** Local promoters commonly tie tickets to the buyer's name and check identification at redemption or entry. Foreign visitors use a passport.
- **Wristband exchange.** Many Jakarta shows swap e-tickets for wristbands at a redemption booth, sometimes on a separate day or at a separate time.
- **Digital payment.** Merch stands and food vendors at Jakarta events generally take QRIS codes and e-wallets such as GoPay, OVO and DANA. Many also take cards. Some rupiah in cash helps when a signal drops.
- **Bag rules.** Promoters usually restrict large bags, professional cameras and outside food and drink.

On the floor, Jakarta crowds typically sing every word, loudly and in English. Standing-area fans queue early for barrier spots. An area near the sound desk usually gives a clear view with more elbow room.

## The songs the room waits for

LANY's catalogue rewards fans who know the early records. "ILYSB" is the one Indonesian crowds sing first. Its looping synth hook and short title made it a lasting favourite with Indonesian listeners.

"Malibu Nights" goes the other way: slow, piano-led and heartbroken. It is the kind of song where phone torches come up across an arena. Frontman Paul Klein has a habit of talking to the crowd between songs, often pausing to address the city directly.

The setlist was not fixed in advance. Fans following the tour through earlier Asian stops were the quickest guide to what the band was playing on this leg.

## Making a night of it

Jakarta's street food runs late, and the areas around big venues usually fill with carts and warung stalls on show nights. Nasi goreng, sate ayam and martabak are the staples.

For anyone staying over, a hotel on the same side of the city as the venue means a short ride back at midnight rather than a long one through Jakarta's toll roads.
