## A 1928 house built for a statesman

Start at the gate and look up. Nelly Garden Mansion sits back from E. Lopez Street behind its own grounds, which already sets it apart in Jaro. Most old houses here stand right on the road.

It was built in 1928 for Don Vicente Lopez, a statesman from one of Iloilo's most prominent families. The style is beaux-arts, the formal European look of that era. Expect symmetry, columns and a grand, deliberate front. The house is widely known as the "Queen of Iloilo Heritage Houses", and the name comes from Nelly, the Lopez daughter it honours.

It gets far fewer visitors than the cathedral a few minutes away. Tour groups heading for Jaro Plaza often pass it, which is a big part of its appeal.

## What you actually see inside

This is a guided visit, not a museum you wander alone. A tour takes you through a house built for entertaining on a large scale. Beaux-arts interiors of the period were designed to impress, so look for:

- The proportions of the main rooms, with ceilings high enough to catch air in a tropical city
- The staircase and how it frames the entrance
- Period furniture and family details that show this was, and is, a lived-in home
- The view back over the grounds that gave the mansion its "garden" name

What you'll see depends on the guide and on what the family opens that day. Treat the list above as what to watch for, not a guarantee. The visit is less about any single object. It's about standing inside a complete house from Iloilo's sugar-era peak.

## Sugar money on E. Lopez Street

The street matters as much as the house. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, sugar from Panay and neighbouring Negros made a small circle of Ilonggo families very rich. Much of that money went into houses in Jaro, then a separate town and now a district of Iloilo City.

E. Lopez Street became a row of those homes. As you walk it, you'll pass other ancestral houses in various states: some restored, some reused, some fading behind their gates. Nelly Garden is the grandest of them and one of the few you can tour.

Seeing the street first helps the tour make sense. It shows why one family built on this scale, and who they built it to impress.

## Weekdays only: timing your visit

The schedule is strict. Tours run Monday to Friday, 9am to 4pm, and the mansion is closed on Saturday and Sunday. That catches weekend visitors out more than anything else.

Aim to arrive in the morning or early afternoon, well before 4pm, so your tour isn't rushed. An hour covers the house comfortably. Add another hour or two if you want to walk E. Lopez Street and Jaro Plaza as well.

Iloilo's midday heat is real. A morning start lets you tour the house before the plaza walk gets uncomfortable.

## Getting to Jaro

Jaro lies north of Iloilo's City Proper, a short ride from downtown hotels and from the Iloilo River esplanade area.

- **Jeepney:** Routes marked "Jaro" run from the City Proper. Ask the driver for E. Lopez Street or Jaro Plaza, then walk.
- **Grab or taxi:** The simplest option. Enter "Nelly Garden Mansion, E. Lopez St, Jaro" as the address.
- **From Jaro Plaza:** If you're already at the cathedral, the mansion is close enough to walk along E. Lopez Street.

Iloilo International Airport is in Cabatuan, well outside the city. From the airport, head into the city first and then go on to Jaro.

## Visiting a house that's still a family home

Nelly Garden is still owned by the Lopez family, and that shapes the etiquette. It isn't a state-run site with a ticket booth and fixed tours every half hour.

- **Arrange ahead.** Confirm through the mansion's current contact channels before you go. Turning up unannounced can mean waiting or being turned away, even on a weekday.
- **Ask about photos first.** Rules can vary from room to room. The guide will tell you.
- **Dress modestly.** It's a private residence in a conservative, church-centred district, so skip beachwear.
- **Carry pesos in cash.** Ask about current tour fees when you book. We don't list them here because they can change.
- **Use Hiligaygon courtesies if you can.** Ilonggo is famously soft-spoken, and a "salamat" (thank you) to your guide goes a long way.

Follow the guide's lead and stay with the group. Treat the rooms the way you'd treat a friend's grandparents' house. That's the right tone.

## Pairing it with Jaro Plaza

End your visit at the plaza. Jaro Metropolitan Cathedral, the National Shrine of Our Lady of the Candles, faces the square. Its bell tower stands apart on the opposite side, an unusual layout for a Philippine church.

Every 2 February, the Feast of Our Lady of the Candles (the Candelaria) fills Jaro with processions and crowds. It's one of the region's biggest religious festivals. If your trip falls then, check that the mansion is running tours, and expect traffic around the plaza.

In a single weekday morning you can see the house, the street and the cathedral. Together they give you a clear picture of old Jaro and the sugar families who built it.
