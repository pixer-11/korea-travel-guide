## A side street off the Lanes

Nile Street is short and easy to miss. It sits among the narrow alleys of The Lanes, Brighton's old twitten quarter, where jewellers and independent shops press close together. Number 10 is a few minutes on foot from Brighton Square and the Royal Pavilion, and about ten minutes downhill to the seafront.

Because it's tucked off the main shopping streets, it can feel like a quieter find, even though plenty of people know it. The café describes itself as minimalist and fresh. Expect a pared-back room rather than seaside kitsch, and a menu built on breakfasts, lunches and food to take away.

## What the day looks like here

It opens at 9am every day, but closing times change through the week:

- **Monday to Wednesday and Sunday:** 9am to 5pm
- **Thursday to Saturday:** 9am to 11pm

On early-close days, plan for breakfast or lunch. A late-afternoon drop-in after 5pm on a Monday will find the door locked. Thursday, Friday and Saturday are the evenings to come. Those late hours make it a dinner option in a part of town where many daytime cafés shut early.

Prices are mid-range. Current prices are on the menu at the counter and table, so check them there rather than trusting third-party listings.

## Timing it to miss the rush

Weekends fill up for most of the day. Try not to arrive between 11am and 9pm on Saturday or Sunday, because that's the busiest stretch. On a weekend, your best chance of a table is right at 9am opening, before the brunch crowd walks in from the station.

On weekdays the quietest hour is 5pm–6pm. That only works on Thursday and Friday, since the café closes at 5pm on other weekdays. An early dinner at 5pm on a Thursday is the calmest way to eat here.

## Ordering, paying and getting there

A few habits make the visit smoother:

- **Pay by card.** Contactless card and phone payments are standard in Brighton cafés, so you rarely need cash.
- **Tipping is optional.** Check the bill first. Some UK restaurants add a discretionary service charge, and if there isn't one, leaving about 10% for table service is normal.
- **Take-out is an option.** If every table is full, order to go and walk five minutes to the Pavilion Gardens to eat.
- **Booking:** if you're coming for a Friday or Saturday evening, check the café's own channels to see whether it takes reservations.

**Getting there:** from Brighton station, walk south down Queen's Road and West Street, then turn into The Lanes. It takes about 15 minutes, all downhill. Parking in the centre is limited and expensive. The Churchill Square car parks are the nearest large ones, but arriving by train or on the city's buses is easier.

**Nearby:** combine a meal with the Royal Pavilion, browsing the North Laine shops to the north, or a walk to Brighton Palace Pier afterwards.
