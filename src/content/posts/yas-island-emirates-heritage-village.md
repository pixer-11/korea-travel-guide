## Palm fronds against a glass skyline

Behind you, the towers of the Corniche line up across the water. In front of you are low walls the colour of sand, houses roofed with palm fronds, and a watchtower. Emirates Heritage Village rebuilds the coastal and desert settlements that came before oil, and it does this on reclaimed land that looks straight at the city those settlements turned into.

It is one of the most-reviewed cultural stops in Abu Dhabi and it is consistently well rated. It also draws a steady flow of school groups, families and tour coaches. Don't expect a quiet corner. Expect a compact, walkable lesson in how people lived here before air conditioning.

## What you'll find inside the walls

The village is small and you can walk it in a loop. Look for these:

- **Barasti huts**, made of woven date-palm fronds. Their shape let sea breezes pass through.
- **A Bedouin goat-hair tent** (*bait al sha'ar*), furnished with floor cushions and rugs.
- **A falaj channel**, showing the irrigation system that carried water to the oases.
- **Small museum rooms** with pottery, jewellery, weapons, fishing gear and household objects.
- **Craft workshops and souq stalls**, where artisans often demonstrate weaving, pottery or metalwork.

The village describes itself as a venue for a "regular roster of events and exhibitions". What's on changes, especially around National Day and the winter festival season. Check the Emirates Heritage Club's channels before you go if you want to catch a specific demonstration.

Allow 60 to 90 minutes. Add another half hour if you plan to walk the seafront afterwards.

## Getting there from Yas Island

Abu Dhabi has no metro, so you'll be driving or taking a car. From Yas Island, a taxi or a Careem or Uber ride follows the E12 and the Corniche west to the Breakwater. Without heavy traffic it takes 30 to 40 minutes.

Ask for Marina Mall or the Abu Dhabi Theatre. The village is on Breakwater Road next to both, so drivers know the turn-off. If you're driving yourself, Marina Mall's car park is the easiest place to leave the car before walking over. Public buses run along the Corniche, but from Yas they take far longer than a car.

## Reading the Friday timetable

Six days a week, the hours are simple: 9am to 4pm. Friday is split into two sessions, 7:30am to 12pm and then 3pm to 9pm. The gap covers midday Friday prayers. Turn up at 1pm on a Friday and the gates will be closed.

That split timetable actually helps you. Weekday visits finish at 4pm, before sunset, so Friday's evening session is the only time you can be inside as the skyline lights up across the water. The rest of the week, go early. From October to April, mornings are comfortable. In summer, the shade among the huts disappears by late morning.

Ramadan and public holidays can change the hours. Confirm them before you set off.

## Manners in a working heritage site

This is a cultural site run by the Emirates Heritage Club, not a theme park. A few habits make the visit go better:

- **Dress modestly.** Cover your shoulders and knees, as you would in the old souqs. It also protects you from the sun.
- **Ask before photographing people.** The craftspeople at the workshops are working. A quick gesture toward your camera and a nod back is usually all it takes, and the answer is often yes.
- **Accept the coffee if it's offered.** Hosts sometimes pour *gahwa*, cardamom-scented Arabic coffee, from a brass *dallah*. Take the small cup with your right hand. When you've had enough, give the cup a gentle shake.
- **Learn one greeting.** "As-salamu alaykum" gets a warm reply. "Shukran" means thank you.

There is no admission figure in our facts. Check the official listing or ask at the gate. Souq stall owners set their own prices, so keep some small notes for little purchases.

## Pairing it with the Breakwater

You're already at one end of the Corniche, so the village makes a good half-day with what's around it. Marina Mall is next door if you need air conditioning or lunch. The Breakwater promenade gives you one of the widest views back toward the city's towers.

Head east and the Corniche beach and its long cycling and walking path run for kilometres. A short drive west brings you to Emirates Palace and Qasr Al Watan. On a Friday, you could see those during the midday closure, then come back for the 3pm reopening and stay for the lights over the water.
