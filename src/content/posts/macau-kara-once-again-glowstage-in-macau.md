## One night, one stage, Cotai

The date is Saturday, November 28, 2026. KARA brings its 「Once Again」GlowStage concert to Broadway Macau for a single night in Cotai. That's the reclaimed strip of land between Taipa and Coloane where Macau's newer resorts stand side by side.

The title suits the group. KARA debuted in 2007 and helped carry K-pop into Japan with songs like "Mister" and "Lupin". The members went separate ways in the late 2010s. They came back together for the 15th-anniversary album *Move Again* in 2022. A show called "Once Again" reads as the next step in that return.

## The venue: Broadway Macau

Don't expect a stadium. Broadway Macau is a lower-rise, street-facing complex, smaller and more casual than the glass towers of Galaxy Macau next door. The two are linked, so you can walk between them under cover. That matters in late November, when evenings in Macau turn cool and breezy.

The main draw outside the concert is Broadway Food Street. It's a row of street-food and casual restaurant counters serving Macanese and Hong Kong staples:

- pork chop buns
- egg tarts
- curry fish balls
- wonton noodles

Concertgoers often eat here before the show, since it's a short walk from the performance space.

## Getting to Cotai

Most international visitors come in through Hong Kong. You have two main routes:

- **By ferry:** boats from Hong Kong dock at the Taipa Ferry Terminal, the closest crossing point to Cotai.
- **By road:** cross-border buses run over the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge to the Macau port at the bridge's end.

From mainland China, the Lotus Bridge crossing at Hengqin also sits close to Cotai.

Within Macau, taxis and the Light Rapid Transit's Taipa line both serve the Cotai resort strip. The big resorts also run their own shuttle buses from the main entry points. Routes have changed in recent years, so the hotel concierge or the transport desk at the port has the current shuttle map. After the show, taxi queues at the Galaxy end of the complex usually move faster than trying to flag a car on the road.

## Moving through the resort like a regular

Macau resorts have a few quirks that catch first-timers out.

- **Money:** the Macau pataca is the local currency, but Hong Kong dollars are accepted almost everywhere, usually at a near one-to-one rate. Change often comes back in patacas, which are hard to spend outside Macau. Cards work across the resort; small food stalls are the likeliest to want cash.
- **The casino floor is 21-plus.** Under Macau law, younger fans can't cut through the gaming areas. Security checks IDs at the entrances, so groups with teenagers stick to the shopping and dining corridors.
- **Language:** Cantonese is the everyday language and Portuguese appears on official signs. Resort staff handle English and Mandarin easily, and many concert crowds in Macau also include Korean-speaking fans.
- **Fan culture:** KARA's fandom is called Kamilia. Expect fans in matching merchandise, fan-made slogans held up during ballads, and a crowd that knows the "Mister" hip-dance chorus by heart.

## Making a weekend of it

A Saturday show leaves Sunday free, and most visiting fans stay on the Cotai strip for that reason. There's plenty within walking distance or a short taxi ride:

- **Taipa Village:** narrow lanes of pastel Portuguese-era houses and Rua do Cunha's snack shops, about ten minutes by car.
- **Coloane Village:** quieter and further south. It has the Chapel of St. Francis Xavier and Lord Stow's Bakery, famous for its Portuguese-style egg tart.
- **The historic centre on the peninsula:** Senado Square and the Ruins of St. Paul's, both part of the UNESCO-listed Historic Centre of Macau, a bridge-crossing away.

Late November is one of Macau's most comfortable months. The summer humidity has gone, typhoon season has passed, and walking between sights is easy. A light jacket covers the harbour-side breeze after dark.
