## A K-pop production lands at Lansdowne

K-POP INFINITY is a staged show, not a festival. It is billed as a cinematic K-pop stage production, built around big visuals and choreography, and it is touring internationally. The Ottawa date on Friday, November 13, 2026 is its first stop in Ontario.

The organiser does not publish a full performer list on this page, so this guide names none. The organiser's official channels carry the show format, running order and ticket links.

## Getting to TD Place

TD Place sits inside Lansdowne, the redeveloped exhibition grounds on Bank Street between the Glebe and Old Ottawa South. The Rideau Canal runs along its eastern edge. Aberdeen Pavilion, the 1898 exhibition hall locals call the "Cattle Castle", stands in the middle of the site.

- **Bus:** OC Transpo buses run the length of Bank Street and stop at the Lansdowne gates. Many fans ride the O-Train into downtown and then take a Bank Street bus south.
- **Car:** Lansdowne has an underground garage beneath the site. Concert nights fill it quickly, and Glebe side streets carry residential parking limits.
- **On foot:** Downtown hotels near Bank and Somerset are roughly a half-hour walk away along Bank Street.

## Fan habits on show night

K-pop crowds come with their own rituals, and they work much the same in Ottawa as anywhere else.

- **Lightsticks:** fans bring official lightsticks from their favourite groups. The arena goes dark between numbers and the lightsticks fill the gaps.
- **Fan chants:** regulars know the call-and-response lines for the big songs. Joining in is welcome, and nobody minds if you just listen.
- **Freebies and trades:** fans often swap photocards, stickers and fan-made slogans outside the gates before the show. This is a gift culture, and people trade or give rather than sell.
- **Merch:** the merchandise stand usually has the longest queue of the night. Fans typically get in line as soon as the doors open.
- **Phones:** short clips are normal. Holding a tablet up for a whole song blocks the people behind you, and that annoys them.

Ontario's sales tax is added at the till, not shown in the price. At the sit-down restaurants along Bank Street, a 15–20% tip is the norm.

## Before and after in the Glebe

The concert is on a Friday, so the restaurants and bars on and around Lansdowne are busy all evening. The pedestrian streets inside Lansdowne have chain and independent places within a few minutes' walk of the doors. Further north, Bank Street through the Glebe has a long run of cafés, pubs and noodle shops between Lansdowne and the Queensway.

Mid-November in Ottawa is usually cold, with evening temperatures near or below freezing. Fans who queue outside for merch or trades tend to wear proper winter layers. Coat-check options at events like this are limited, so plenty of people carry a coat that packs down small.

Fans from Toronto and Montréal often make it a weekend. Ottawa is roughly four to five hours' drive from Toronto, or a VIA Rail trip. From Montréal it is about two hours by road. Visitors who stay over usually book downtown or in the ByWard Market, with Parliament Hill and the canal pathways close by for Saturday.
