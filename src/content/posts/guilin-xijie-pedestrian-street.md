## Why go

Xijie isn't a manufactured "old street" — it genuinely is one. Records place a market here since the Sui dynasty, and the current stone-paved lane still runs past centuries-old brick facades, now filled with tea shops, bakeries and bars instead of rice merchants.

What makes it work is the setting. Karst limestone towers rise directly behind the rooftops, so even a five-minute walk feels staged against a landscape you'd otherwise pay for a river cruise to see.

## Getting there

Xijie sits in Yangshuo County, part of greater Guilin prefecture, not downtown Guilin city itself. From Guilin city, buses run regularly to Yangshuo's long-distance bus station, a journey of roughly 70-90 minutes depending on traffic and route.

From there it's a 10-15 minute walk or short taxi ride to the street itself, which runs down to the Li River waterfront. Many travelers combine the trip with a Li River cruise from Guilin to Yangshuo, arriving by boat and finding Xijie a short stroll from the dock.

## What to see and do

The street itself is the attraction — plan to wander rather than tick off sights.

- Ming and Qing dynasty shopfront architecture, now housing shops rather than museums
- Riverside terraces along the Li River at the street's southern end, with karst peaks as backdrop
- Local snacks like beer fish (啤酒鱼) and stuffed rice noodles sold from small storefronts
- Craft and textile stalls selling batik, embroidery and calligraphy
- Bars and cafés that fill the upper floors, many with balconies over the pedestrian flow

Give it two to three hours if you're eating and shopping, less if you're just passing through en route to cycling the countryside or a river cruise.

## When to go

Late afternoon into early evening is the sweet spot. The light softens over the karst peaks, lanterns and shopfront lights start to glow, and the street shifts from a daytime shopping strip into something closer to a social scene.

Mornings are quieter and better if you want photos of the architecture without a crowd of pedestrians in the frame. Avoid the middle of the day in summer, when the stone paving traps heat and the street is at its most crowded with day-trip groups.

## How to visit like a local

Xijie is a well-known stop, not an undiscovered one, so the useful local knowledge is about timing rather than secrecy.

- Come at breakfast or mid-morning, before tour buses arrive from Guilin, if you want the street closer to empty
- Pay by mobile (WeChat Pay or Alipay) — nearly every stall and café expects it, and small vendors may not have change for large bills
- Bargain gently at craft stalls; fixed-price cafés and restaurants don't expect haggling
- Order beer fish at a sit-down restaurant rather than a street stall for a proper portion, and expect to share it
- The common tourist mistake is treating Xijie as a full-day destination — most visitors are happier pairing a couple of hours here with a river cruise, bike ride to Moon Hill, or an evening at the Impression Sanjie Liu light show nearby

Confirm current opening hours for specific shops and restaurants before you go, as small operators vary their schedules seasonally.

## Nearby

Once you're done with Xijie, Yangshuo's countryside is the real draw. Rent a bicycle and ride out toward Moon Hill or the rice terraces along the Yulong River, both reachable within 30-40 minutes. The Li River docks near the street's end are also the starting or ending point for many river cruises back toward Guilin, so Xijie works well as a bookend to a longer day rather than a standalone trip.
