## Two towers and a ring at the top

From the plaza below, it looks unfinished. Two slab towers rise side by side and meet only at the very top, where a ring-shaped observatory bridges the gap. Hiroshi Hara designed it, and it was completed in 1993. It is about 173 metres tall, and the joined crown is called the Floating Garden Observatory.

The approach is part of the visit. A lift carries you partway up. Then a glass-tubed escalator crosses the open air between the towers, with nothing but the drop underneath. Above the enclosed observatory floor sits the rooftop: a circular open-air walkway with the wind coming straight off the Yodo River side and no glass between you and Osaka.

## Getting to Ōyodonaka

The building sits at 1-chōme-1-88 Ōyodonaka, northwest of the Umeda rail hub. Your options:

- **JR Osaka Station:** leave from the north side and follow the signs for the Sky Building. It's about 10 minutes on foot, partly through an underground passage that runs beneath the tracks.
- **Umeda Station (Midosuji Line) or Hankyu Umeda:** expect a slightly longer walk west through the station complex.
- **Signage:** look for "Umeda Sky Building" or "Floating Garden" (空中庭園) on the overhead maps.

The ticket price is posted at the counter upstairs and on the official site. Allow 60–90 minutes for the ascent, both decks and a slow loop of the rooftop.

## Timing the view

The building is very popular, and the escalator tube becomes a queue when crowds peak. At weekends, try not to arrive between 1pm and 4pm, when lines are longest. On weekend mornings it's calmest from 10am to 11am, and again from 8pm to 10pm.

On weekdays the quietest stretch is 9pm–10pm. That's also when the city below is fully lit and the rooftop's circular walkway gives an unbroken night panorama. Google currently lists 9:30am–10pm every day, but observatory hours and last entry change through the year and on holidays. Check the official site for this month's times before planning a late visit.

## Riding up without the scrum

- **Go up, then down:** the observatory floor fills first. Walk straight up to the rooftop deck, circle it, then come back down to the indoor windows once the crowd has moved.
- **Secure loose items:** the roof is fully open and exposed to wind. Put hats and phones on straps away before you step out.
- **Look down from the escalator:** the glass tube gives the best view of the gap between the towers. Stand on the side facing the opposite tower.
- **Eat in the basement:** Takimi Koji, on the lower level, is a recreated old Osaka lane of small restaurants. It's an easy dinner before a late ascent.
- **Use the garden at the base:** the planted grounds at the foot of the towers are free to walk. They're the best spot for a photo of the whole building, including the bridge at the top.

Afterwards, Grand Front Osaka and the HEP Five Ferris wheel are both a short walk back toward the station.
