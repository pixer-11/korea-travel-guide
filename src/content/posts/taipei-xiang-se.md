It's the administrative heart of Taipei, home to Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall and a grid of residential lanes that empty out once government offices close for the day. Xiang Se sits on one of those quiet streets, the kind of address you'd walk past without a second look if you didn't already know it was there.

That combination, chef trained in globally recognized tasting-menu kitchens returning to reinterpret his own culinary heritage, is the actual draw here, more than any single dish you could name without having eaten there yourself.

## Reading the menu like a Hakka-Taiwanese-European hybrid

Without a published menu to draw from, the honest guidance is to go in expecting technique-forward plating, layered on top of ingredients and flavor bases rooted in Taiwanese and Hakka cooking traditions, rather than a straightforward "fusion" plate that splits the difference.

Hakka cuisine in Taiwan leans on preserved and fermented components, stir-fried mountain vegetables, and pork prepared in ways that reward slow technique. European fine-dining training tends to bring sauce work, precise temperature control, and course pacing. Expect the kitchen to be translating between those two vocabularies rather than picking one.

Ask your server about the day's format when you sit down. A chef with this background is likely to change composition and pacing based on what's in season and what the kitchen is testing that week.
