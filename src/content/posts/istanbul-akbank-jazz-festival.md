## What the 36th edition covers

Akbank Jazz Festival has run in Istanbul since the late 1980s, and the 2026 edition was set for September 28 to October 13. That span covered over two weeks, longer than a typical single-venue festival, with shows staggered across the city rather than stacked into one weekend.

The announced programme mixed international touring acts and Turkish jazz musicians, spread across evenings rather than a continuous daily program. The festival has historically paired big-name headliners with smaller late-night sets, so a single visit can cover both a seated concert and a club show.

## Where the shows happen

Two venues anchored this edition: Babylon Bomonti and Salon İKSV.

- **Babylon Bomonti** sits in the Bomonti district, inside the former Bomonti beer factory complex, a mid-size concert hall known for its sound system and standing-and-seated hybrid layout.
- **Salon İKSV** is on İstiklal Caddesi in Beyoğlu, run by the İstanbul Foundation for Culture and Arts (İKSV), the same organization behind the festival itself; it's a smaller, seated room suited to acoustic and small-ensemble sets.

Other venues sometimes join in a given year, so not every show sits in these two rooms. Each concert listing carried its own address.

## Tickets and what they cost

Tickets for the 2026 edition were sold per concert, not as a bundled festival pass, through Biletix and the İKSV/festival website. Prices varied by artist and venue size: a Salon İKSV seated show typically costs less than a headline concert at Babylon Bomonti. Some free or discounted student sets have appeared in past editions, but that's never guaranteed year to year.

Salon İKSV is a small room, and popular acts there tend to sell out faster than the larger Babylon Bomonti shows. Venue assignments and set times at the festival have historically been subject to change even after they're first posted.

## Getting there and planning your night

Bomonti is reachable by metro (Şişli-Mecidiyeköy on the M2 line, then a short walk or taxi) or a direct taxi from central Beyoğlu; it's not a walkable distance from İstiklal. Salon İKSV, by contrast, is a straightforward walk from Taksim Square down İstiklal Caddesi, about 10 to 15 minutes on foot.

- Babylon Bomonti is partly standing-room, so at popular headline shows the earliest arrivals usually end up closest to the stage.
- Salon İKSV has assigned seats, so there's less of a rush, though latecomers slip into seats mid-set in a small, quiet room.
- Both venues have food and drink inside, but neither is set up as a full dinner stop; Bomonti and Beyoğlu have plenty of restaurants nearby.

The festival was planned as an evening affair, leaving the days free for the usual Sultanahmet or Bosphorus sightseeing.
