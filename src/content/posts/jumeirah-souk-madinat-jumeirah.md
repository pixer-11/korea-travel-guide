## Under the timber roof

Look up first. The alleys of Souk Madinat Jumeirah run beneath dark wooden beams and lattice screens, and lanterns hang in rows overhead. Sand-coloured walls rise into wind towers, the old Gulf cooling chimneys known as barjeel. Here they are mostly for show, because the whole souk is air-conditioned.

Be clear about what this is before you go. It was purpose-built in the 2000s as part of the Madinat Jumeirah resort, next to the Al Qasr and Mina A'Salam hotels. It is a mall dressed as an old bazaar, not a working trading souk like the ones in Deira. That is the whole appeal: the look of a souk with none of the heat.

It is also one of the most-reviewed places in Jumeirah, and well over 25,000 visitors have rated it consistently well. Expect company.

## Getting to Al Sufouh

The souk sits on King Salman Bin Abdulaziz Al Saud Street in Al Sufouh 1, on the beach side of the city. No metro station is within walking distance. Most visitors come by taxi or ride-hailing app.

- **By metro plus taxi:** take the Red Line to Mall of the Emirates Station, then a short taxi ride towards the coast.
- **By taxi from Downtown or the Marina:** ask for "Souk Madinat Jumeirah"; every Dubai driver knows it.
- **By car:** the resort has parking and valet. Check the current terms at the entrance, as they change.

## Beating the crowds

The quietest windows are 10am to 2pm on weekdays and 10am to 1pm on weekends. At those hours the lanes are wide open and shopkeepers have time to talk you through their stock. Doors open at 10am daily, so an arrival right at opening gives you the arcades almost to yourself.

Try not to turn up on a weekend between 4pm and 11pm. That stretch is the peak, and waterside tables fill fast. The UAE weekend is Saturday and Sunday.

If you want the Burj Al Arab at dusk, aim for a weekday evening. Most people want that view, and on weekends it comes with the heaviest crowds.

## Browsing, paying and dressing for the souk

The shops lean towards souvenirs and decorative pieces. You will find pierced metal lamps, pashminas, carpets, spices, perfume oils and Arabic-style homeware. Most stores show fixed prices, and hard bargaining is less expected here than in the older souks. A polite question about a better price for several items is still normal at the smaller stalls.

- **Paying:** cards and contactless are accepted almost everywhere, including at restaurants.
- **Tipping:** check the bill for a service charge first. If there isn't one, leaving about 10 percent for good service is customary.
- **Dress:** this is a family space in a conservative country. Covered shoulders and knees are the safe choice, and swimwear stays at the beach.
- **Photos:** the architecture is fair game. Ask before photographing staff or other visitors, especially women.

Shipping carpets or large lamps home is common, and many shops arrange it. Get the costs and delivery times in writing before you pay.

## Eating by the waterways

Restaurants and cafés line the canals and courtyards, and several have terraces facing the water and the Burj Al Arab. Cuisines range from Middle Eastern to international. Menus with current prices are posted at each entrance, so you can compare as you walk.

For a terrace table at sunset, particularly on a weekend, book ahead. Walk-ins do much better during the quiet midday window. The canals themselves carry abra-style boats that serve the resort. Ask at the resort about current access rather than assuming you can get on.

The Madinat Theatre is also inside the complex. Check its programme if you want to add a show to dinner.

## Beyond the souk walls

The souk works best as one stop on a coastal afternoon. The Burj Al Arab is visible from the waterfront terraces and only minutes away by car. Nearby beaches include Umm Suqeim's Sunset Beach, known for its straight-on view of the sail-shaped hotel. Kite Beach is a little further up the coast.

Two hours covers a proper browse and a drink by the water. Add dinner and it becomes an evening. Mall of the Emirates, with Ski Dubai inside, is a short drive inland if the weather pushes you back indoors.
