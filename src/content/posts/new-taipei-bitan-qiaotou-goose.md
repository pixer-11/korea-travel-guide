## What Bitan Qiaotou Goose actually is

This is a smoked-goose specialist near Bitan (Green Lake), the riverside area of Xindian District that most visitors know for its suspension bridge and paddle boats. The kitchen runs a single short shift, about four hours, and closes the moment the goose runs out. It was named among the newest additions to the MICHELIN Guide Taiwan 2026, which is a sharp jump in profile for a stall that keeps such a small daily output.

Smoked goose (煙燻鵝) is a Taiwanese classic built on slow smoking over a fragrant wood or tea base, then a quick pass to crisp the skin before slicing. The draw here is the same dish other Taipei-area smoked-goose shops built reputations on, but with a reputation for consistency tight enough that regulars plan their afternoon around it. Expect the meat cut to order and served warm, not reheated from a steam table.

## Getting to Bitan

Bitan sits at the southern end of Xindian, reachable via Xindian Station on the Songshan-Xindian Line, the green line's southern terminus. From the station it is a short walk down to the riverside promenade where the bridge and boat rentals are.

The goose stall trades on foot traffic from this same riverside crowd, so treat it as a stop before or after a walk across the suspension bridge rather than a separate trip. New Taipei's MRT system makes this an easy half-day add-on to a Taipei itinerary; there is no need for a car.

## Why it sells out by early afternoon

A four-hour opening window with no guarantee of a full afternoon shift means demand outruns supply most days. Regulars who know the pattern arrive as close to opening as they can manage rather than gambling on lunchtime.

Because hours, days of operation and any reservation system are exactly the kind of details that shift as a small operation adjusts to sudden attention, confirm the current schedule through the restaurant's own listing or the MICHELIN Guide Taiwan page before you set out. Do not assume yesterday's hours hold today.

## Queueing without wasting the day

The stall's whole rhythm is built around scarcity, so plan around it rather than around a fixed mealtime.

- Check today's opening time the morning you plan to go; a four-hour window shifts easily with supply.
- Arrive at or before opening if you want to avoid a sold-out sign; this is not a place where a 1 p.m. lunch works.
- Have a backup planned around Bitan, the boats or the old street, so an early sellout doesn't waste the trip.
- Bring cash as a fallback; small food stalls in Taiwan often prefer it even where cards are accepted.
- Treat the smoked goose as the anchor of a Bitan half-day, not a quick detour, since the whole point is not to rush the queue.
