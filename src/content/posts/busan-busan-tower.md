## Why go

Busan Tower isn't the tallest structure in the city anymore, but it's still the best-placed one. At 118 meters, perched on Yongdusan Park's hilltop in the middle of Jung-gu, it puts you above the tangle of Nampo-dong's alleys and gives you the harbor in one unbroken sweep.

On a clear afternoon you can trace the container cranes at Busan Port, the curve of the coast toward Gwangalli, and the low green hills that ring the city. At night the deck turns into something else entirely — a glass box hanging over a carpet of neon signage and ferry lights.

## Getting there

The tower sits inside Yongdusan Park, and the walk up is part of the experience. From Nampo Station (Busan Metro Line 1), take Exit 1 or 7 and follow the signs toward the park — it's about a 10-minute uphill walk through Nampo-dong's shopping streets.

If the climb doesn't appeal, an escalator and elevator system runs up from street level near the BIFF Square side. Either way, you'll pass through the park's plaza, with its fountain and statues, before reaching the tower base.

## What to see and do

The observation deck wraps the full circumference of the tower, so there's no bad side — just walk the loop and let the view change.

- Look north for Busan Station and the hillside neighborhoods climbing behind it
- Look south toward the harbor, Yeongdo Island, and the bridges linking them
- Check the glass floor sections if you want a small jolt of vertigo
- Stop at the café level for a coffee with the same view, minus the ticket line

Below the deck, Yongdusan Park itself is worth a slow lap — there's a bell pavilion, a statue of Admiral Yi Sun-sin, and shaded benches that locals use for lunch breaks year-round.

## When to go

The tower runs 10am to 10pm every day of the week, so there's no early-morning or late-night rush to plan around. Weekday visits are calm pretty much the entire time it's open.

Weekends are a different story. Crowds build steadily and peak between noon and 8pm — that's the window to avoid if you don't want to share the deck with several tour groups at once. If you're only free on a Saturday or Sunday, aim for after 9pm; the last hour before closing is consistently the quietest stretch of the weekend.

Sunset is the obvious draw, and it does deliver, but it's also when the deck is most crowded on any given day. If photographs matter more to you than the sunset itself, come an hour or two after dark instead — the city lights are already on and the crowd has usually moved on.

## How to visit like a local

Busan Tower is firmly on the tourist circuit now, so don't expect a quiet secret spot — plan around the crowds rather than hoping to dodge them entirely.

- Buy tickets at the base kiosk or via the machines; card payment is standard, cash still works
- Weekday daytime hours are the calmest window all week — no need to rush the opening bell
- Avoid Saturday and Sunday between noon and 8pm, when tour groups stack up at the elevator
- If weekends are your only option, go after 9pm for a near-empty deck before closing
- Skip the taxi and walk up through Yongdusan Park — it's how most locals reach the tower anyway
- Combine the visit with a wander through nearby BIFF Square or Gukje Market, both a short walk downhill

The most common mistake is treating this as a quick five-minute photo stop. Give yourself a full hour: the walk through the park, a slow loop of the deck, and a coffee at the café level if you're not in a rush. That's enough time to actually notice how the light changes across the harbor rather than just checking the box.
