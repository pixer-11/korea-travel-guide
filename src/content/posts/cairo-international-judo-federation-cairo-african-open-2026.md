## Seven categories, one long day of throws

Judo rewards the patient spectator. At the International Judo Federation Cairo African Open 2026, the day opens with several mats running at once. Pairs of athletes in white and blue judogi bow in, grip up, and try to end it with a single clean throw.

The event carries 2026 in its name, but the announced date is January 20, 2027. It draws competitors from across Africa and beyond, along with the coaches, team staff and fans who follow the circuit from city to city.

## Where a Continental Open sits

Continental Opens are a tier of the IJF World Tour below the Grand Prix and Grand Slam events. They are the working end of the circuit. Athletes go to them to gather world-ranking points, test themselves against new opponents and move up toward bigger draws.

For a spectator, that means a mix of names. Established international fighters chasing points share the bracket with younger judoka in their first senior seasons. Egypt has long been one of the stronger judo nations in Africa, so home athletes tend to get the loudest support in the hall.

## How the mat works

The structure is easy to follow once you know it. Each weight category runs as a knockout bracket, and fighters who lose to an eventual finalist go into the repechage. That is why every category awards two bronze medals.

A few things help you follow the scoring:

- **Ippon** ends the contest at once. It is usually a throw that lands the opponent flat on the back with control.
- **Waza-ari** is a smaller score. Two of them add up to ippon.
- **Golden score** decides a contest still level after regulation time, with the first score winning.
- **Shido** penalties go to passivity and illegal grips. A third shido loses the contest.

Preliminary rounds on the circuit usually fill the earlier hours. The repechage, bronze and gold contests are grouped into a final block, when the number of mats in use drops and the whole hall watches one contest.

## Getting to the hall and getting in

The International Judo Federation publishes the venue, the session schedule and any spectator ticketing for the Cairo African Open on ijf.org. The IJF also streams World Tour events, so fans abroad usually follow along on its live channel.

Cairo is large, and its traffic is slow at the best of times. Visitors typically get around by ride-hailing apps such as Uber and Careem, which are widely used in the city. The Cairo Metro covers the main corridors and avoids the road traffic, though most visitors still finish the trip by car.

People who only want the medal contests usually turn up for the final block, not the morning preliminaries. Some fans stay for the whole day and pick their favourite categories from the draw.

## Manners in the stands

Judo has its own etiquette, and the crowd tends to follow it. Athletes bow at the edge of the mat and again to each other, and the referee runs the contest with hand signals, not shouts. Spectators cheer throws and big moments but go quiet during the formal bows.

A few habits of regular judo audiences:

- Seating near a single mat gives you the clearest view during the busy preliminary rounds.
- Coaches sit in marked chairs beside the mat, and fans leave that area to them.
- At the medal ceremonies, fans usually stand for the anthem of the gold medallist.
- Egyptian fans are vocal. Chanting for home athletes is normal, and visiting supporters bring flags too.

Friday is the main day of prayer in Egypt, and many offices and shops keep shorter or different hours around midday. This matters if your trip around the event takes in the city's mosques and markets.

## Cairo in late January

January is one of the more comfortable months in Cairo. Days are mild and dry, and evenings turn cool enough that people carry a jacket or light sweater. That makes it a good season to add sightseeing on either side of competition day.

Most visitors pair the trip with the classic sights:

- **The Giza Pyramids and the Great Sphinx**, on the city's western edge.
- **The Egyptian Museum** on Tahrir Square, and the Grand Egyptian Museum near Giza.
- **Islamic Cairo**, including the Al-Azhar Mosque and the Khan el-Khalili bazaar.
- **The Citadel of Saladin** with the Mosque of Muhammad Ali on its hilltop.

Judo fans usually treat competition day as one full day and spread the sights over the days around it. Cairo's distances and traffic make it hard to pack the pyramids and a full tournament session into a single day.
