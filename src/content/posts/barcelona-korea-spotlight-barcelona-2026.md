## A Wednesday night of K-pop in Barcelona

Korea Spotlight comes back to Barcelona on Wednesday, November 25, 2026. It is a showcase, not a single-artist tour stop. The format exists to put Korean acts in front of European audiences and industry.

The event is organised with South Korea's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism through KOCCA, the Korea Creative Content Agency. That government backing explains the format: a curated bill, a mid-size room, and a focus on introducing acts as much as celebrating them. This guide does not name performers, set times or prices, because none were given to us.

## Razzmatazz and Paral·lel 62

The venue is listed as Razzmatazz (Paral·lel 62), and the two names point to different parts of town. Paral·lel 62 is the hall at number 62 on Avinguda del Paral·lel, the old theatre avenue between Poble Sec and Sant Antoni. Razzmatazz's own club complex is across the city in Poblenou.

Attendees follow the address printed on their ticket. If it reads Paral·lel 62, you are on a broad avenue lined with old music-hall theatres. El Molino, with its red windmill façade, sits a short walk along the same stretch.

## Tickets and the official word

KOCCA and its Korea Spotlight programme publish the lineup, door times and ticket links through their own official channels. Those channels are the source for anything this page leaves out. Resale listings for K-pop shows are common, and fans usually stick to the official ticketing link the organiser names.

Showcases built around several acts tend to draw fans of one group alongside curious newcomers. The evening tends to feel more varied than a solo concert, and more relaxed about who knows which song.

## Getting to the Paral·lel and home again

The avenue is one of the easiest places in Barcelona to reach by metro. Two stations sit close to number 62:

- **Paral·lel** (L2 and L3), at the Plaça de Raquel Meller end, also has the funicular up to Montjuïc
- **Poble Sec** (L3), a few blocks further along the avenue
- **Drassanes** (L3), near the bottom of La Rambla, if you are walking in from the old town

Most visitors on a few days' stay buy a T-casual, the ten-journey metro and bus card, from station machines. On weeknights the metro stops running around midnight, so late finishers usually rely on the NitBus night buses or a taxi along the Paral·lel itself. Line 3 is known for pickpockets, and a phone in a back pocket does not stay there long.

## How the crowd works at a K-pop showcase

K-pop audiences in Barcelona behave much like K-pop audiences anywhere. Fans who follow a particular group usually turn up holding that group's official lightstick. Many also learn the fan chants, the shouted call-and-response lines timed to a song's intro or chorus. If you don't know them, the people around you will carry it.

A few habits make the night smoother:

- **Standing rooms fill from the front.** Fans who want the barrier usually queue well before doors.
- **Fan-made freebies get passed hand to hand**, things like photocards, stickers and printed slogans. Taking one is fine; a thank-you is expected.
- **Card and contactless payment are standard** at Barcelona venues and bars. Cash is rarely needed.
- **A little Catalan or Spanish goes down well** at the bar. "Bona nit" is goodnight, "gràcies" is thank you.

Filming whole songs on a raised phone blocks the people behind you. Most venues set their own camera rules, and staff enforce them.

## Before and after in Poble Sec

Barcelona eats late, and a showcase night works around that. Dinner at 9pm counts as early here. Poble Sec, the neighbourhood rising behind the avenue towards Montjuïc, is where many concertgoers eat before or after a show.

- **Carrer de Blai**, a pedestrian street of pintxos bars where small bites on bread are counted by the toothpick
- **Mercat de Sant Antoni**, the restored iron-and-glass market a short walk north, for a daytime wander
- **Montjuïc**, reached by the funicular from Paral·lel station, for the Fundació Joan Miró and city views before the evening

In late November the evenings are cool rather than cold. Most people get by with a light jacket over whatever they plan to wear in a warm, packed room.
