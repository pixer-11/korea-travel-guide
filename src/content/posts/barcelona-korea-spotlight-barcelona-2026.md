## Getting to the Paral·lel and home again

The avenue is one of the easiest places in Barcelona to reach by metro. Two stations sit close to number 62, and a third is a walk away:

- **Paral·lel** (L2 and L3), at the Plaça de Raquel Meller end, also has the funicular up to Montjuïc
- **Poble Sec** (L3), a few blocks further along the avenue
- **Drassanes** (L3), near the bottom of La Rambla, for those walking in from the old town

Most visitors on a few days' stay buy a T-casual, the ten-journey metro and bus card, from station machines. On weeknights the metro stops running around midnight, so late finishers usually rely on the NitBus night buses or a taxi along the Paral·lel itself. As on any crowded metro line, pickpockets are active, and a phone or wallet is safer in a front pocket or zipped bag than somewhere easy to lift.

## How the crowd works at a K-pop showcase

K-pop audiences in Barcelona behave much like K-pop audiences anywhere. Fans who follow a particular group usually turn up holding that group's official lightstick. Many also learn the fan chants, the shouted call-and-response lines timed to a song's intro or chorus. Newcomers who don't know them get carried along by the people around them.

A few habits make the night smoother:

- **Standing rooms fill from the front.** Fans who want the barrier usually queue well before doors.
- **Fan-made freebies get passed hand to hand**, things like photocards, stickers and printed slogans. Taking one is fine; a thank-you is customary.
- **Card and contactless payment are standard** at Barcelona venues and bars. Cash is rarely needed.
- **A little Catalan or Spanish goes down well** at the bar. "Bona nit" is goodnight, "gràcies" is thank you.

Filming whole songs on a raised phone blocks the people behind. Most venues set their own camera rules, and staff enforce them.

## Before and after in Poble Sec

Barcelona eats late, and a showcase night works around that. Dinner at 9pm counts as early here. Poble Sec, the neighbourhood rising behind the avenue towards Montjuïc, is where many concertgoers eat before or after a show.

- **Carrer de Blai**, a pedestrian street of pintxos bars where small bites on bread are counted by the toothpick
- **Mercat de Sant Antoni**, the restored iron-and-glass market a short walk north, for a daytime wander
- **Montjuïc**, reached by the funicular from Paral·lel station, for the Fundació Joan Miró and city views before the evening

In late November the evenings are cool rather than cold. Most people get by with a light jacket over whatever they wear in a warm, packed room.
