## Fourteen floors above Manchester

You don't find Sister Moon from the street. You get to it by going up through the Treehouse Hotel to the 14th floor, where the restaurant sits well above the city's red-brick rooflines.

It is one of Manchester's newer openings and draws plenty of talk. It arrived with a wider food and drink expansion at the hotel, so the restaurant shares the upper floors with a new rooftop bar and lounge. That makes the whole top of the building a destination of its own, not just a place for hotel guests to eat.

## What "Southeast Asian barbecue" signals

The label tells you the direction: food cooked over heat, with the flavours of the region. The cooking traditions across Southeast Asia lean on a few things:

- skewers and grilled meats
- marinades built on lemongrass, chilli, lime and fish sauce
- dishes meant for sharing across the table

Sister Moon hasn't published a menu we can confirm here, so treat that list as a guide to the style, not a promise of particular dishes. Look at the menu online before you go, especially if you have dietary needs. Barbecue-led kitchens can have fewer options for vegetarians than you'd expect.

## Getting a table on the 14th floor

A newly opened rooftop restaurant fills up fast. Here's how to avoid turning up to a full room:

- **Book ahead.** Use the restaurant's or hotel's official booking page. For a Friday or Saturday evening, book days ahead, not hours.
- **Confirm the hours before you go.** New restaurants often change their service times in the first months.
- **Read the bill before tipping.** In Manchester, as in most of the UK, many restaurants add a discretionary service charge to the bill. If it's there, you don't need to tip again. If it isn't, leaving a tip for good service is normal.
- **Pay by card.** Contactless card payment is standard in city-centre restaurants across the UK.
- **Ask about the bar.** If you're early, ask whether you can wait at the hotel's rooftop bar. That's better than waiting in the lobby.

Groups should book even further ahead. A table for six or more on a weekend evening is the hardest to get somewhere this new.

## Making a night of the Treehouse rooftop

The restaurant is one part of the plan. The new rooftop bar and lounge sit in the same expansion, so you can easily start with a drink, eat, and finish higher up without leaving the building.

Timing matters more up here than at street level. Manchester's sunsets vary a lot by season: before 4pm in midwinter and after 9pm in late June. Book a table that falls around dusk if you want to see the city lights come on. Check the forecast too, because low cloud can hide the skyline.

Allow two to three hours for dinner plus a drink before or after. Wear what you'd wear to any smart-casual city restaurant. Manchester weather changes fast, so bring a layer in case any of the rooftop space is outdoors.
