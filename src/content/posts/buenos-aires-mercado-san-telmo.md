## A city block under one iron roof

Mercado San Telmo dates from 1897. It was designed by the Italian-born architect Juan Antonio Buschiazzo to feed the European immigrants arriving in the south of the city. Its wrought-iron frame and glass roof still cover the original hall, and the market was declared a National Historic Monument in 2000.

It fills most of a block between Bolívar, Defensa, Carlos Calvo and Estados Unidos. You can enter from several streets. Inside, the aisles form a grid, with greengrocers and butchers close to the centre and antique dealers spreading out toward the edges.

It is one of the most-reviewed sights in San Telmo and is consistently well rated. Don't expect a quiet corner the guidebooks missed. It's an everyday working market that has become a stop on most visitors' first walk through the old quarter.

## What you'll find in the aisles

The mix changes stall by stall, so a lap of the whole hall before you buy pays off. The usual finds:

- **Antiques and curios:** old soda siphons, enamel signs, vinyl records, cameras and tango-era postcards.
- **Fresh produce and butchers:** these are the stalls that kept the market running for a century.
- **Coffee counters and casual food:** empanadas, sandwiches and other quick plates, eaten standing or at shared tables.
- **Vintage clothing and small crafts:** mostly near the Defensa side.

Allow 45 minutes to an hour for the market itself. Add more if you're eating, or if you're pairing it with the streets around Plaza Dorrego, one block south.

## Getting to Bolívar 970

San Telmo has no subte station of its own, so you walk the last stretch. The closest options are:

- **Independencia (Lines C and E):** about ten minutes' walk east along Estados Unidos.
- **Belgrano (Line E):** a short walk south down Bolívar.
- **From Plaza de Mayo:** walk about 15 minutes south along Defensa, past shopfronts and colonial-era houses.

Taxis and ride-hailing cars can drop you on Bolívar. Defensa is pedestrian-heavy on Sundays and hard to drive.

## Haggling, paying and the Sunday crush

The market's hours are currently 9am–8pm every day of the week. Each vendor sets their own schedule, though, so some antique stalls open late or skip quiet days. Check before making a special trip for one dealer.

On Sundays the Feria de San Pedro Telmo takes over Defensa and Plaza Dorrego, and much of that crowd flows into the market. If you want room to look properly, come on a weekday morning soon after opening. If you're here for the Sunday atmosphere, do the market first and the street fair second.

How to pay and bargain:

- **Pesos:** carry cash in small notes. Many stalls also take bank transfers or Mercado Pago QR codes. Card acceptance varies, so ask first.
- **Haggling:** polite bargaining is normal at antique stalls. It is not done at food or produce counters. Ask "¿Es su mejor precio?" rather than naming a low number.
- **Prices:** they're usually chalked or tagged at each stall. If nothing is marked, ask before anyone wraps your purchase.
- **Pickpockets:** keep your phone and wallet in front of you in the packed aisles, especially on Sundays.
