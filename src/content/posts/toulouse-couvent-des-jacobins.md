## Palm-tree vaults, not palm trees

The nave is the reason people come. A single row of seven slim columns runs down the middle of the church, each one fanning out at the top into 22 stone ribs, like an avenue of palm trees carved in rock. The last column, called the palmier, is the widest vault span in medieval architecture still standing. Light comes through tall stained-glass windows in reds and blues, original to the 13th and 14th centuries when the Dominican order built this as their mother church.

Thomas Aquinas is buried here, in a chest tomb beneath the altar, moved to Toulouse in 1369. The Dominicans founded their order in this city in 1215, and this convent became their intellectual base; Aquinas taught and wrote for the order before his death in 1274. That relic is why art historians and pilgrims both show up.

## The cloister and garden

Step out the side door into a covered cloister walk, arcaded on all four sides around a rectangular garden. It's quieter than the nave, planted with clipped box hedges and a few trees, and gives you the best angle to photograph the octagonal bell tower from below. A side chapel, the Chapelle Saint-Antonin, has 14th-century frescoes on its ceiling depicting the Apocalypse, easy to miss if you don't look up.

## Hours, tickets, getting there

- **Open:** Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, 10am–6pm
- **Closed:** Monday and Thursday
- **Metro:** Capitole station (Line A), 3-minute walk
- **Nearby:** Place du Capitole and the Capitole itself, a 5-minute walk
- Confirm current admission prices before you go; the church interior has historically been free, with a small fee for the cloister.

Weekends are busy across the full 10am–6pm stretch, so don't expect a lull on a Saturday afternoon. If your schedule allows a weekday, Tuesday or Wednesday morning gives you a better shot at photographing the nave without other visitors in frame.

## Getting in without the crowd

This is one of Toulouse's most visited monuments, so treat it like a popular sight, not a quiet find. Arriving right when doors open at 10am on a weekday usually beats the tour groups that build through midday. Keep your voice low inside; it's a working sacred space as well as a monument, and Mass or events occasionally close sections without notice.

- Wear layers: the stone interior stays cool even in Toulouse's hot summers.
- Look up in the Chapelle Saint-Antonin for the fresco cycle.
- Walk the cloister last; it's the natural spot to linger before heading back to Place du Capitole for coffee.
- Photography is generally fine without flash, but check posted signs at the entrance for current rules.
