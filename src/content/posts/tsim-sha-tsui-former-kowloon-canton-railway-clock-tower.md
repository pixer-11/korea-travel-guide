## A clock tower whose station is gone

The trains left decades ago. The tower stayed.

This red-brick tower was finished in 1915 as part of Kowloon Station, the southern terminus of the Kowloon-Canton Railway. Passengers boarded here for the line north towards Canton, now Guangzhou. For half a century this patch of Tsim Sha Tsui was where the railway met the harbour and the ferries.

In the 1970s the terminus moved east to Hung Hom, and the old station building was demolished. Public campaigning saved the tower, and it was later declared a monument. Today it stands by itself on the promenade, with the curved roofs of the Hong Kong Cultural Centre behind it where the platforms used to be.

## Brick, granite and four faces

Get close and look at what it is made of. The shaft is red brick with bands of pale granite, and the four faces each carry a clock. Above them sits a small cupola and a tall lightning rod. The whole thing rises roughly 44 metres, which used to be tall here. Next to the harbourfront hotels it now looks modest.

The tower is closed to visitors. You look at it from the paved plaza at its base, where low benches and planters face the water. From there Victoria Harbour opens straight across to the Hong Kong Island skyline, with Central and Wan Chai lined up along the far shore.

Walk all the way around. From the Salisbury Road side, the tower is framed by The Peninsula hotel across the street. From the harbour side, you get it against open sky and the ferries.

## Reaching the bottom of Salisbury Road

The tower sits at the very bottom of Kowloon, where Salisbury Road meets the waterfront. Getting there is simple:

- **MTR:** Tsim Sha Tsui Station (Tsuen Wan Line). Take Exit E, walk down to Salisbury Road, then head towards the harbour. It is a short walk past The Peninsula.
- **MTR alternative:** East Tsim Sha Tsui Station (Tuen Ma Line) connects underground to the Salisbury Road side of the district.
- **Star Ferry:** Boats from Central and Wan Chai dock at the Tsim Sha Tsui pier, a few steps from the tower's base.
- **Bus:** The bus terminus beside the Star Ferry pier is next to the plaza.

Arriving by Star Ferry is the best way in. The tower comes into view from the deck as the boat crosses, and you step off almost underneath it.

## The 7am rule for weekends

Timing matters more here than at most monuments. The plaza is a free, open space beside a ferry pier and one of the city's main harbour viewpoints, so it fills up fast.

On weekends the quietest window is usually 7am–8am. At that hour the plaza is mostly joggers and ferry commuters, and you can photograph the tower without strangers in the frame. The early light on the island skyline is the other reward.

Don't plan a relaxed weekend visit between 3pm and 11pm. That is typically when the crowd peaks: tour groups, families and couples waiting for the harbour to light up. You can still see the tower then, but expect to queue for any clean photo angle.

The forecast has no quietest weekday window. Still, a weekday morning is a safer choice than a weekend evening.

## Moving with the promenade crowd

Most people come to the tower as part of a harbour stroll, so a few habits help:

- **Carry an Octopus card.** Hong Kong runs on it. It pays for the Star Ferry, the MTR and the buses, and you tap it at the pier turnstiles with no ticket queue.
- **Shoot and step aside.** The base of the tower is the busiest photo spot. Locals take their shot and move on, so don't set up a tripod in the walkway at peak hours.
- **Keep left on the stairs and ramps** near the ferry pier, where the commuter flow is fastest at rush hours.
- **Don't climb the planters or railings.** It is a declared monument, and staff do ask people to get down.
- **Bring water.** The plaza has little shade. On humid summer afternoons, people shelter under the Cultural Centre's overhangs instead.

If you want the evening light show on the harbour, check the Hong Kong Tourism Board's site for the current schedule. Then claim a spot on the promenade railing well before it starts, because the spaces near the tower go first.

## Walking out from the tower

The tower is a good starting point for a walk along the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront, and most sights are within ten minutes:

- **Star Ferry Pier, Tsim Sha Tsui:** the crossing to Central is short, cheap and still one of the best views of the harbour.
- **Hong Kong Cultural Centre:** the windowless, tiled concert-hall complex directly behind the tower.
- **Hong Kong Space Museum:** the white egg-shaped dome across the plaza.
- **Hong Kong Museum of Art:** on the waterfront just east of the Cultural Centre.
- **Avenue of Stars:** continue east along the promenade for film-star handprints and an unbroken skyline view.
- **The Peninsula:** the grand 1920s hotel across Salisbury Road, famous for its afternoon tea.

Give the tower itself 15–30 minutes. With the promenade and a ferry crossing, it easily becomes a half-day.

## Why it still draws a crowd

There is nothing to buy and nothing to enter, yet it is one of the most-reviewed landmarks in Kowloon. It is the last physical trace of the station that first tied Hong Kong to the mainland by rail. It also sits right where the city's best free harbour view begins. Come at 7am on a weekend, and that view is almost all yours.
