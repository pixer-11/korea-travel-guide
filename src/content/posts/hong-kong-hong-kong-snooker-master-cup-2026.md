## Five weeks of baize in Kowloon

The calendar is long. Play opens on Sunday, November 8, 2026 and closes on Saturday, December 12, 2026. That gives you roughly five weeks in which to catch a session, not a single weekend you have to plan a trip around.

The Billiard Sports Council of Hong Kong China organises the event. It is the city's governing body for snooker and billiards, so the council handles the draw, the schedule and spectator entry. ## Finding the Mongkok General Snooker Club

The venue is the Mongkok General Snooker Club, and as the name says, it is in Mong Kok. This is one of the most crowded districts on earth, a grid of neon signs, street stalls and stacked commercial buildings on the Kowloon peninsula.

- **MTR:** Mong Kok Station, on the Tsuen Wan Line (red) and Kwun Tong Line (green). Prince Edward Station, one stop north, also serves the area.
- **Payment:** an Octopus card covers the MTR, buses and most convenience stores.
- **Navigation:** many Mong Kok venues sit on upper floors, so the street-level door can be narrow and easy to walk past. Visitors usually look for the building's lobby directory rather than a shopfront.

## Watching snooker without breaking the silence

Snooker has stricter spectator etiquette than almost any other sport. These are the rules regulars follow:

- **Stay still during a shot.** People move, sit down or leave only between frames, or when a player is walking around the table.
- **Phones on silent.** Camera shutters and flash bother players at the table, so photos are taken quietly or not at all.
- **Applause is measured.** People clap a big break or a hard pot, but there is no chanting or calling out.
- **Language:** snooker in Cantonese is *toi kau* (桌球). Hong Kong snooker culture is mostly Cantonese-speaking, though English is widely understood around an organised event.

Because the event runs for weeks, early-round weekday sessions in a club setting tend to feel close and informal. Later stages usually draw a denser crowd.

## Mong Kok around a session

Late autumn is Hong Kong's dry season, with cooler, less humid days than summer. That makes the walk between frames and dinner comfortable. All of these are a short walk from the station:

- **Ladies' Market (Tung Choi Street):** a long run of stalls selling clothing, phone cases and souvenirs, busiest in the evening.
- **Goldfish Market:** the northern end of Tung Choi Street, lined with shops displaying fish in hanging plastic bags.
- **Fa Yuen Street ("Sneakers Street"):** shop after shop of trainers.
- **Flower Market Road and Yuen Po Street Bird Garden:** near Prince Edward, quieter by day.
- **Langham Place:** a tall mall on Argyle Street, with indoor seating and food courts if the weather turns.

Mong Kok also has dense clusters of cha chaan teng (Hong Kong-style cafés) serving milk tea, pineapple buns and macaroni soup. Spectators usually grab a quick meal between sessions without leaving the neighbourhood.
