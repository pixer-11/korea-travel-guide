## The comeback in numbers

BIGBANG last played Australia in 2017. This stadium show on October 31, 2026, ends that wait. The date falls on a Saturday and on Halloween, so the western suburbs fill with two different kinds of crowd at once.

The year also marks two decades since the group debuted under YG Entertainment in 2006. The catalogue covers "Haru Haru", "Fantastic Baby" and "Bang Bang Bang", which helped carry K-pop into stadiums worldwide. The organiser publishes the lineup, set times and ticket links on its official site. This guide names no performers or prices beyond what the announcement states.

## Inside Accor Stadium

This is no club gig. Accor Stadium was built as Stadium Australia for the 2000 Sydney Olympics and seats around 80,000 people. Most of the bowl has two tiers of seating, with a field floor that takes standing or seated sections for concerts.

Things to know about the layout:

- Gates sit around the whole oval, so your ticket's gate letter decides which side of the stadium you walk to.
- Upper-tier seats look over the full stage production, and the floor puts you close to it.
- The roof covers most seats but not the field, which matters for floor ticket holders if it rains.

## Getting to Sydney Olympic Park

The stadium is in Sydney Olympic Park, about 15 km west of the CBD. On weekdays, Olympic Park station runs as a shuttle from Lidcombe on the T7 line. On major event days, extra direct trains usually run from Central. The station opens straight onto Olympic Boulevard, a few minutes' walk from the stadium.

Payment works the same as everywhere on Sydney Trains. You tap an Opal card or a contactless bank card on the readers at the gates, then tap off at the other end. Visitors from interstate or overseas usually stay in the CBD or in Parramatta. Parramatta is a short train ride north-west and generally sits closer to the stadium.

## Light sticks, fan chants and the walk out

BIGBANG's fan club is called VIP. Its crown-shaped official light stick lights up whole sections of seating once the stage lights drop. Fans typically arrive an hour or more before doors to buy merchandise, because the stall queues form well before the show. Many come dressed in group-themed outfits, and the Halloween date adds costumes to that.

Crowd etiquette at K-pop stadium shows is consistent:

- Fan chants follow set patterns for each song, and long-time VIPs know them by heart.
- Filming phones are usually held at chest height so people behind can see.
- Fans often trade handmade fan favours like photocards and stickers on the concourse before the show.

Getting home is the slowest part of the night. After stadium events, Olympic Park station uses queue pens to load trains one at a time. Many attendees wait in the stadium for the first rush to clear. Others walk to the bus stops along Olympic Boulevard instead.
