## One night in Zuoying

TREASURE's New Wave: Live tour stops in Kaohsiung on September 26, 2026. The venue is Kaohsiung Arena (高雄巨蛋, Gāoxióng Jùdàn), a dome-roofed hall that opened in 2008 and seats roughly 15,000 for concerts. Its oval shell and curved roof are hard to miss on Bo'ai 2nd Road in Zuoying District, in northern Kaohsiung.

This is the tour's only Kaohsiung date. Fans from Taipei, Taichung and abroad usually travel down for it rather than relying on a second chance. The tour's promoter publishes the ticket links and seating plans, and the ticket terms set the entry rules.

## Getting to the dome

Rail does the work here. The arena has its own stop on the Kaohsiung MRT:

- **MRT Red Line, Kaohsiung Arena Station (R14):** the arena stands across Bo'ai 2nd Road from the station.
- **From Taiwan High Speed Rail:** the line ends at Zuoying (Xinzuoying) Station, which connects to MRT Zuoying (R16). From there it is two stops south to R14.
- **From Kaohsiung International Airport:** the airport sits on the same Red Line (R4), so it is a direct ride north with no change.

After the encore the station platforms fill quickly. Many attendees walk one stop south toward Aozihdi (R13) or wait out the crush at the shopping plaza next door. For anyone staying near the arena, the MRT trip back to Zuoying for a morning HSR train takes only a few minutes.

## How Teume do concert day here

TREASURE's fandom, Teume, treats concert day in Taiwan as a full-day event. The habits are fairly consistent:

- **Fan freebies:** fans set up outside the arena handing out photocards, stickers and printed goods. Swapping is common, and a small bag of your own to trade is welcome.
- **Lightsticks:** most of the crowd carries the official TREASURE lightstick. Some concerts sync it to the show over Bluetooth, which turns the whole floor into one block of colour.
- **Transport cards:** almost everyone taps through the MRT gates with an EasyCard or iPASS, which skips the ticket-machine queue after the show. Both cards also work at convenience stores.
- **Language:** staff instructions are in Mandarin. "Rùchǎng" (入場) on a sign means entry, and the gate and block numbers are printed on your ticket.

Camera and recording rules come from the ticket terms and the venue, not from the fans around you. Security staff do enforce them during the show.

## Around the arena before and after

The arena sits among places to eat. **Hanshin Arena** (漢神巨蛋), the department store and mall directly beside it, has a food court and restaurants on its lower floors. It's also the obvious air-conditioned place to wait in late-September heat.

A short walk away, **Ruifeng Night Market** (瑞豐夜市) runs on several evenings a week. Its stalls sell grilled squid, stinky tofu and bubble tea packed tightly between narrow aisles. It doesn't open every night, so post-show plans there depend on the weekday.

For a daytime detour, **Lotus Pond** in Zuoying, with the Dragon and Tiger Pagodas standing over the water, is a short taxi ride north. Visitors staying more than one night usually pair the concert with **Pier-2 Art Center** and the harbour around **Sizihwan** (R1 or the Light Rail). Both are about 20 minutes south by MRT.
