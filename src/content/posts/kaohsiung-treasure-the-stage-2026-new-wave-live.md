## One night in Zuoying

TREASURE's New Wave: Live tour was set to stop in Kaohsiung on September 26, 2026. The venue is Kaohsiung Arena (高雄巨蛋, Gāoxióng Jùdàn), a dome-roofed hall that opened in 2008 and seats roughly 15,000 for concerts. Its oval shell and curved roof are hard to miss on Bo'ai 2nd Road in Zuoying District, in northern Kaohsiung.

It was the tour's only Kaohsiung date. With no second show in the city, fans from Taipei, Taichung and abroad typically travel down for nights like this. Entry rules for concerts here come from the ticket terms.

## Getting to the dome

Rail does the work here. The arena has its own stop on the Kaohsiung MRT:

- **MRT Red Line, Kaohsiung Arena Station (R14):** the arena stands across Bo'ai 2nd Road from the station.
- **From Taiwan High Speed Rail:** the line ends at Zuoying (Xinzuoying) Station, which connects to MRT Zuoying (R16). From there it is two stops south to R14.
- **From Kaohsiung International Airport:** the airport sits on the same Red Line (R4), so it is a direct ride north with no change.

After a big show the station platforms fill quickly. Many concertgoers walk one stop south toward Aozihdi (R13) or wait out the crush at the shopping plaza next door. From the arena, the MRT trip back to Zuoying for a morning HSR train takes only a few minutes.

## How Teume do concert day here

TREASURE's fandom, Teume, treats concert day in Taiwan as a full-day event. The habits are fairly consistent:

- **Fan freebies:** fans set up outside the arena handing out photocards, stickers and printed goods. Swapping is common, and people who bring their own items to trade are welcome.
- **Lightsticks:** most of the crowd carries the official TREASURE lightstick. Some concerts sync it to the show over Bluetooth, which turns the whole floor into one block of colour.
- **Transport cards:** almost everyone taps through the MRT gates with an EasyCard or iPASS, which skips the ticket-machine queue after the show. Both cards also work at convenience stores.
- **Language:** staff instructions are in Mandarin. "Rùchǎng" (入場) on a sign means entry, and the gate and block numbers are printed on the ticket.

Camera and recording rules come from the ticket terms and the venue, not from the fans nearby. Security staff at the arena enforce them during shows.

## Around the arena before and after

The arena sits among places to eat. **Hanshin Arena** (漢神巨蛋), the department store and mall directly beside it, has a food court and restaurants on its lower floors. It's also the obvious air-conditioned refuge from late-September heat.

A short walk away, **Ruifeng Night Market** (瑞豐夜市) runs on several evenings a week. Its stalls sell grilled squid, stinky tofu and bubble tea packed tightly between narrow aisles. It doesn't open every night of the week.

For a daytime detour, **Lotus Pond** in Zuoying, with the Dragon and Tiger Pagodas standing over the water, is a short taxi ride north. Visitors staying more than one night often pair a concert with **Pier-2 Art Center** and the harbour around **Sizihwan** (R1 or the Light Rail). Both are about 20 minutes south by MRT.
