## Folk songs, turned up

Picture a saz line from an old village song played on electric bağlama. Behind it sit a fuzz bass, a drum kit and a wash of organ. That collision is Altın Gün. The band formed in Amsterdam in 2016 around Dutch bassist Jasper Verhulst, who wanted to play the Turkish psych records he had fallen for.

Most of their catalogue reworks Anatolian folk and the 1970s Anadolu rock of artists like Neşet Ertaş, Aşık Veysel and Selda Bağcan. Their second album, *Gece*, was nominated for the Grammy for Best World Music Album. After it came *Yol*, *Dönüş* and *Aşk*.

## The Istanbul date and the room

The show is on Friday, November 13, 2026, at Volkswagen Arena. A Turkish crowd changes the night. In Amsterdam or London much of the audience comes for the groove. In Istanbul, many people grew up with the original versions of these songs and know every word.

Volkswagen Arena is part of the UNIQ Istanbul complex in Maslak, the glass-tower business district on the European side of the city. It is an indoor arena built for concerts, so the wet, cool weather Istanbul usually has in November stays outside. The band's official website has a tour page with the date and ticket links.

## Getting to Maslak

The M2 metro is the obvious route. It runs north from Yenikapı through Şişhane, Taksim, Osmanbey and Levent. The closest stop is **İTÜ–Ayazağa**, and from there it is a short walk to the arena.

- **From Taksim or Beyoğlu:** M2 straight north, no change, roughly 20 minutes.
- **From the old city (Sultanahmet):** T1 tram to Kabataş, then the F1 funicular up to Taksim, then the M2.
- **From the Asian side:** Marmaray or a ferry across, then the M2 from Yenikapı or Taksim.

Plenty of people avoid taxis to this show for a reason. Friday evening around Maslak and Büyükdere Caddesi is among the slowest traffic in the city. After the encore the metro platform fills fast, so some fans hang around the complex for a while before heading home.

## Singing along at an Altın Gün show

Regulars at these shows tend to share a few habits.

- **Learn a chorus.** The crowd usually sings the folk refrains back at the stage. "Goca Dünya", "Cemalim" and "Tatlı Dile Güler Yüze" are the most likely singalongs. Knowing even one makes the night.
- **Expect dancing.** Some of the reworked songs are dance tunes at heart. When a regional rhythm kicks in, fans sometimes link arms in a loose halay line at the back of the floor.
- **Use an Istanbulkart.** The rechargeable transit card covers the metro, tram, funicular and ferries. Kiosks and machines at major stations sell and top it up, and contactless bank cards also work at many gates.
- **Dress in layers.** It is warm inside a full arena and damp outside it. Standing in a coat for two hours is miserable.

Turks say "Altın Gün" as *ahl-TUHN gyun*. It means "golden day". It is also the name of a Turkish women's savings circle, which is part of the joke.

## Before and after the gig

There isn't much in Maslak besides offices and malls. Most visitors eat somewhere else first and ride up. The neighbourhoods along the M2 are the usual picks.

- **Şişhane and Karaköy:** meyhanes with meze and rakı, plus the Galata Tower area a few minutes' walk away.
- **Nişantaşı (Osmanbey station):** cafés and restaurants on the streets off Abdi İpekçi Caddesi.
- **Levent:** the Kanyon and Metrocity malls, right at the station, for a quick meal close to the venue.

Visitors in town just for the concert usually stay in Beyoğlu or Şişli. Both are on the M2, so it is a direct ride home after the show.

## Hearing the records first

To get the most out of the setlist, listen to the originals as well as the covers. Neşet Ertaş's recordings and the Turkish psych compilations from the 1970s show where the arrangements come from.

*Gece* is the album most first-timers start with, and the one that brought the band its Grammy nomination. *Yol* and *Dönüş*, both released in 2021, lean more on synths and drum machines. The live show tends to fold both sides together.
