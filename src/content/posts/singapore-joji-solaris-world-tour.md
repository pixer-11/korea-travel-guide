## Two nights out east

Joji was set to play Singapore twice. The Solaris World Tour stop at Arena @ Expo was announced for Saturday 21 and Sunday 22 November 2026. Both nights were at the same venue.

Joji is the stage name of George Miller, the Japanese-Australian singer whose slow, hazy songs have racked up billions of streams. His catalogue runs through *Ballads 1*, *Nectar* and *Smithereens*. It includes "Slow Dancing in the Dark" and "Glimpse of Us", the piano ballad that became one of 2022's biggest songs. Fans fly in from across Southeast Asia for dates like these.

## Where Arena @ Expo sits

The venue belongs to Singapore EXPO, the city's largest exhibition complex. It is on the eastern edge of the island, between Tampines and Changi. The complex is a run of huge, flat-roofed halls with long covered walkways between them. On show nights, those walkways become the queue.

It is a long way from Marina Bay and Orchard Road, roughly half an hour or more by train from the centre. It is not a quick hop from most central hotels.

## Getting there and back

The MRT is the easiest option. Expo station serves two lines:

- **Downtown Line (blue)**: Expo is the eastern terminus. It runs direct from Bugis, Chinatown and Bayfront.
- **East-West Line, Changi Airport branch (green)**: the change is at Tanah Merah. Changi Airport is one stop the other way.

Station signs point to Singapore EXPO. The walk to the halls is short and mostly covered, which matters in November. That month brings the northeast monsoon, and heavy evening showers are common.

Contactless bank cards and phones work at the MRT gates, so a separate travel card is not needed. Last train times vary by line and are posted by SMRT and SBS Transit. Grab and Gojek both work here. Pickup points around the halls get crowded when thousands of people leave together, so walking back to the station is often quicker.

## Arriving early, eating first

Changi City Point, a mall right beside Expo station, is the obvious pre-show stop. It has a food court and chain restaurants, and its air-conditioning is welcome before a long queue.

For hawker food, Bedok and Tampines are a few MRT stops away. Their markets serve Singapore staples like chicken rice, laksa and char kway teow. Jewel Changi Airport is also close enough to fill the hours around a show on a travel day.

## Queue rules at a Singapore concert

Singapore runs concerts tightly, and door checks are usually strict.

- **Official tickets**: tickets for these shows were sold through the tour's official ticketing partner. Resale scams are common in Singapore, and the police warn about them before almost every big concert.
- **E-tickets**: mobile signal often gets patchy when a crowd packs into the queue.
- **Bags and cameras**: large bags, professional cameras and detachable-lens gear are often turned away at Singapore shows.
- **Photo ID**: some ticket categories require it, and so do the bars.

People here queue in orderly lines, and pushing forward is frowned on. Merchandise stands usually draw their longest lines just before doors open, and they tend to be quieter early or after the show. Card and mobile payments are accepted almost everywhere, though a little cash is useful if a reader goes down.

The two dates suited different trips. The Sunday show fitted people flying out on Monday morning, because Changi is so close. The Saturday show left Sunday free for recovery, or for East Coast Park, a short taxi ride south.

Hotels near the airport or in Tampines cut out the long cross-island ride altogether.
