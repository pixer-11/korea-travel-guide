## Two nights out east

Joji plays Singapore twice. The Solaris World Tour stops at Arena @ Expo on Saturday 21 and Sunday 22 November 2026. Both nights are at the same venue, so pick whichever fits your trip.

Joji is the stage name of George Miller, the Japanese-Australian singer whose slow, hazy songs have racked up billions of streams. His catalogue runs through *Ballads 1*, *Nectar* and *Smithereens*. It includes "Slow Dancing in the Dark" and "Glimpse of Us", the piano ballad that became one of 2022's biggest songs. Fans fly in from across Southeast Asia for dates like these.

## Where Arena @ Expo sits

The venue belongs to Singapore EXPO, the city's largest exhibition complex. It is on the eastern edge of the island, between Tampines and Changi. The complex is a run of huge, flat-roofed halls with long covered walkways between them. On show nights, those walkways become the queue.

It is a long way from Marina Bay and Orchard Road, roughly half an hour or more by train from the centre. Plan your evening around that distance rather than treating it as a quick hop from your hotel.

## Getting there and back

The MRT is the easiest option. Expo station serves two lines:

- **Downtown Line (blue)**: Expo is the eastern terminus. It runs direct from Bugis, Chinatown and Bayfront.
- **East-West Line, Changi Airport branch (green)**: change at Tanah Merah. Changi Airport is one stop the other way.

Follow the station signs for Singapore EXPO. The walk to the halls is short and mostly covered, which matters in November. That month brings the northeast monsoon, and heavy evening showers are common.

You can tap a contactless bank card or phone at the MRT gates, so there is no need to buy a separate travel card. After the show, check the last train times for your line on the SMRT or SBS Transit website before the encore. Grab and Gojek both work here. Pickup points around the halls get crowded once thousands of people leave together, so walking back to the station is often quicker.

## Arriving early, eating first

Changi City Point, a mall right beside Expo station, is the obvious pre-show stop. It has a food court and chain restaurants, and its air-conditioning is welcome if you plan to queue early.

For hawker food, Bedok and Tampines are a few MRT stops away. Their markets serve Singapore staples like chicken rice, laksa and char kway teow. If you are flying in or out the same day, Jewel Changi Airport is close enough to fill the hours around a show.

## Queue rules at a Singapore concert

Singapore runs concerts tightly, and a little preparation saves you trouble at the door.

- **Buy only from the official ticketing partner** listed on the tour site. Resale scams are common in Singapore, and the police warn about them before almost every big concert.
- **Have your e-ticket downloaded** before you reach the halls. Mobile signal gets patchy when a crowd packs into the queue.
- **Check the bag and camera policy** on the official event page. Large bags, professional cameras and detachable-lens gear are often turned away at Singapore shows.
- **Carry photo ID**. Some ticket categories need it, and so does the bar if you plan to drink.

People here queue in orderly lines, and pushing forward is frowned on. Merchandise stands usually draw their longest lines just before doors open, so shopping early or after the show tends to be easier. Card and mobile payments are accepted almost everywhere, but a little cash is useful if a reader goes down.

## Before you book

Choose your night first, then your seat. The Sunday show suits people flying out Monday morning, because Changi is so close. The Saturday show leaves Sunday free for recovery, or for East Coast Park, a short taxi ride south.

If you are staying near the airport or in Tampines, you can skip the long cross-island ride altogether. Wherever you stay, confirm timing, ticket categories and venue rules on the official site, because those details belong to the organiser and can change.
