## Five days of rights deals and reading crowds

Most of the world's book business meets here. Rick Steves calls it a "huge book fair and publishers' convention", and both halves of that are accurate. The 2026 edition was set for **Wednesday 7 to Sunday 11 October**.

On the weekdays, the halls are given over to publishers, literary agents, translators and rights scouts. They sit at small tables in back-to-back meetings, buying and selling translation and film rights. Saturday and Sunday belong to readers, students, school groups and costumed cosplayers.

Frankfurt has hosted book trading since the 15th century, when Gutenberg's press was working in nearby Mainz. The modern fair dates from 1949. Each year a Guest of Honour country has its own pavilion and a programme of author events.

## Getting to Messe Frankfurt

The fair occupies the **Messe Frankfurt** exhibition grounds, between the city centre and Bockenheim. Public transport beats driving here.

- **S-Bahn:** lines S3, S4, S5 and S6 to *Frankfurt (Main) Messe*.
- **U-Bahn:** U4 to *Festhalle/Messe*, close to the City entrance by the Festhalle.
- **From the airport:** the S-Bahn runs into the city, with a change on some routes. Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof is one stop from Messe on the S-Bahn.
- **On foot:** the Hauptbahnhof is a straightforward walk away when the trains are packed.

Hotels across Frankfurt and as far out as Mainz, Darmstadt and Wiesbaden usually fill up during fair week, and prices go up. Many visitors stay in a neighbouring city on a direct train line.

## Working the halls

The grounds are big. Covered moving walkways (the *Via Mobile*) connect the halls, and getting from one end to the other still takes time. Most visitors focus on a few halls rather than the whole site.

- **The online hall plan** shows where exhibitors are. Their locations change every year.
- **Cloakrooms** take bags, which fill up fast with catalogues and free proofs.
- **The Agora** is the open-air square between the halls, with food stands, outdoor stages and seating.
- **The Guest of Honour pavilion** usually has the most designed space on the grounds.

On the final Sunday of the fair, the Peace Prize of the German Book Trade (*Friedenspreis des Deutschen Buchhandels*) is traditionally presented in the Paulskirche in the old town. The ceremony is broadcast, and it marks the end of fair week for much of the city.

## Fair etiquette, trade and public

On trade days, everything works by appointment. Agents and rights staff book meetings in tight half-hour slots weeks in advance. Their stand tables are not for walk-up pitches. Approaching agents unannounced at the fair rarely works for authors looking for representation. Agents prefer queries by email beforehand.

On public days, the rules relax. Stands sell books, publishers hold signings, and author talks fill the stages. Popular signings usually build queues well before they start. The event programme appears on the official app and website.

- Ticket type sets which days a visitor can enter. Trade and public tickets are not the same.
- Online tickets are digital. Box office queues at the entrances are typically slow on Saturday morning.
- Many visitors come to Saturday and Sunday in costume. The cosplay crowd is a regular part of the weekend.
- Food lines in the halls usually get long at lunchtime, so many visitors carry water and a snack.
