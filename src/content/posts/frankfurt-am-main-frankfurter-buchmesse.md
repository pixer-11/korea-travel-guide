## Five days of rights deals and reading crowds

Most of the world's book business meets here. Rick Steves calls it a "huge book fair and publishers' convention", and both halves of that are accurate. The 2026 edition runs **Wednesday 7 to Sunday 11 October**.

Publishers, literary agents, translators and rights scouts fill the halls on the weekdays. They sit at small tables in back-to-back meetings, buying and selling translation and film rights. On Saturday and Sunday the halls fill with readers, students, school groups and costumed cosplayers.

Frankfurt has hosted book trading since the 15th century, when Gutenberg's press was working in nearby Mainz. The modern fair dates from 1949. Each year a Guest of Honour country has its own pavilion and a programme of author events.

## Getting to Messe Frankfurt

The fair fills the **Messe Frankfurt** exhibition grounds, between the city centre and Bockenheim. Public transport beats driving here.

- **S-Bahn:** lines S3, S4, S5 and S6 to *Frankfurt (Main) Messe*.
- **U-Bahn:** U4 to *Festhalle/Messe*, close to the City entrance by the Festhalle.
- **From the airport:** take the S-Bahn into the city and change if needed. Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof is one stop from Messe on the S-Bahn.
- **On foot:** the Hauptbahnhof is a straightforward walk away if the trains are packed.

Hotels across Frankfurt and as far out as Mainz, Darmstadt and Wiesbaden fill up during fair week, and prices go up. Book early, or stay in a neighbouring city on a direct train line.

## Working the halls

The grounds are big. Covered moving walkways (the *Via Mobile*) connect the halls, and getting from one end to the other still takes time. Plan around a few halls, not the whole site.

- **Check the online hall plan first.** Exhibitor locations change every year.
- **Use the cloakrooms.** Bags fill up fast with catalogues and free proofs.
- **Find the Agora.** This open-air square between the halls has food stands, outdoor stages and seating.
- **Look for the Guest of Honour pavilion.** It usually has the most designed space on the grounds.

On the final Sunday, the Peace Prize of the German Book Trade (*Friedenspreis des Deutschen Buchhandels*) is presented in the Paulskirche in the old town. The ceremony is broadcast, and it marks the end of fair week for much of the city.

## Fair etiquette, trade and public

On trade days, everything runs on appointments. Agents and rights staff book meetings in tight half-hour slots weeks in advance. Their stand tables are not for walk-up pitches. If you are an author looking for representation, the fair is the wrong place to approach agents unannounced. Send queries by email beforehand.

On public days, the rules relax. Stands sell books, publishers run signings, and author talks fill the stages. Popular signings build queues well before they start, so check the event programme on the official app or website and get there early.

- Your ticket type sets which days you can enter. Trade and public tickets are not the same.
- Buy online and bring the digital ticket. Box office queues at the entrances are slow on Saturday morning.
- Many visitors get to Saturday and Sunday in costume. The cosplay crowd is a regular part of the weekend.
- Carry water and a snack. Food lines in the halls get long at lunchtime.

Confirm timing, ticket categories and entry rules on the official Frankfurter Buchmesse site before you travel.
