## A façade the length of the plaza

Most cathedrals face their square end-on. Arequipa's runs sideways along the whole northern edge of the Plaza de Armas, so a single long wall of white sillar forms one side of the square. Sillar is the pale volcanic stone the city is built from, and it gives Arequipa its nickname, the White City.

Two bell towers stand at either end. The present neoclassical building dates from rebuilding after a fire in 1844, and further repairs followed several earthquakes. The 2001 quake brought down one of the towers, and it has since been rebuilt. From the plaza's palm-lined centre you can usually see El Misti rising behind the cathedral's roofline on a clear day.

## What to look for inside

The interior is wide, bright and spare compared with the gilded baroque churches of Cusco. Look for these:

- **The pulpit**: carved wood by the French artist Buisine-Rigot, with the devil crushed beneath it at the base.
- **The organ**: a large Belgian-made instrument, often described as one of the biggest in South America.
- **The museum**: the on-site collection of religious objects, vestments and artwork, visited separately from the main nave.

Guided museum tours often include the roof. Up there, between the towers, you look out to the three volcanoes, El Misti, Chachani and Pichu Pichu, and down over the plaza. Allow about 20 minutes for the nave alone and around an hour if you add the museum and roof.

## Timing around Mass

The listed hours are this week's worship schedule: currently 7:00–10:00 AM and 5:00–6:00 PM every day. Outside those windows the doors are generally shut. Church hours can change for feast days and Holy Week, so check the posted notice at the door.

Remember that these are Mass hours, not tourist slots. During a service, stay at the back, keep your voice low and put the camera away. The museum keeps its own schedule, and its prices are posted at its entrance. If you want the volcano views from the roof, ask there first.

## Inside a working church

This is the seat of the Archdiocese of Arequipa, and locals come here to pray. Behave the way they do:

- **Dress**: shoulders and knees covered. Men take off hats as they step inside.
- **Photos**: no flash, and none at all while Mass is under way.
- **Movement**: stay out of the central aisle during services and don't walk in front of the altar.
- **Language**: Mass is said in Spanish. A quiet *permiso* gets you past people in the pews.

## Around the Plaza de Armas

The other three sides of the square are the *portales*, stone arcades with upstairs balconies. At dusk, those balconies look straight across at the cathedral's floodlit façade. The Iglesia de la Compañía, with its intricately carved Jesuit façade, sits on the plaza's southeast corner.

Santa Catalina Monastery is a few blocks north, an easy walk for the same morning. Arriving for the 7:00 AM opening, then walking on to Santa Catalina, gives you two of the city's main sights before midday.
