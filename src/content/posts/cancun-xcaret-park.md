## Water That Runs Through Rock

Xcaret starts underground. Wearing a life vest, you float down rivers that run through limestone tunnels. Daylight comes in through gaps in the jungle overhead, then disappears where the rock closes in again. The water is fresh and clear and noticeably cool after the Riviera Maya heat.

This is the Yucatán's geology on show. The whole peninsula sits on porous limestone, and that rock is what makes cenotes and underground channels possible. Xcaret's operators shaped these natural features into a park. It runs along the Caribbean shore, on the site of an ancient Maya port called Polé.

## How a 13-Hour Day Fits Together

The park is open from 8:30 AM to 10:00 PM every day of the week, and people really do use all of it. Don't treat it as a half-day stop. The site is large and the paths wind, and most visitors spread it out like this:

- **Morning:** the underground rivers, while you have the most energy
- **Midday:** snorkelling in the sheltered inlet, then a long lunch somewhere shaded
- **Afternoon:** the animal areas, such as the butterfly pavilion, the aviary, the coral reef aquarium and the sea turtle enclosures
- **Late afternoon:** the small Maya ruins, the Mexican folk art displays and the hillside Mexican cemetery with its rows of painted tombs
- **Evening:** the big show in the open-air theatre

The cemetery is easy to miss and worth finding. It's a terraced hill of colourful graves, built to show how Mexico treats death as something to remember, not something to hide from.

## Getting Down From Cancun

The address says Playa del Carmen, not Cancun. That catches out a lot of first-time visitors. The park is roughly 75 km south of the Cancun hotel zone on Federal Highway 307, which runs straight down the coast. Without traffic, the drive takes about an hour.

You have three realistic choices:

- **Drive:** the turn-off is well signposted and there's parking on site
- **Bus:** take an ADO coach from Cancun's downtown terminal to Playa del Carmen, then a short taxi ride south
- **Park transport:** many ticket packages include pickup from Cancun hotels, so check when you book

Leave room in your schedule for the trip home if you stay for the night show. You'll be on the highway late.

## Working Xcaret Without Losing the Day

This is one of the most visited attractions in the Mexican Caribbean, and it's very well reviewed. Don't expect a quiet corner. Expect a lot of company, and plan for it.

- **Book ahead online.** Buying in advance means less time at the gate, and the official Xcaret site lists current packages and prices.
- **Get there for the 8:30 AM opening.** The rivers usually fill up as tour coaches from Cancun and Playa del Carmen arrive through the morning.
- **Use only biodegradable sunscreen.** Ordinary sunscreen is banned in the rivers and inlet to protect the reef and freshwater systems.
- **Pack light for the rivers.** You go in at one end and come out at another, so leave valuables in a locker and carry only what's allowed.
- **Pay in pesos or by card.** Mexican pesos are the local currency and cards are widely accepted inside. Small notes help with tips at restaurants.

Locals say "shka-ret" for the name. Staff speak English, but a "gracias" goes a long way.

## When the Lights Go Down

The evening show, *Xcaret México Espectacular*, is a big reason people stay until closing. It's held in a large theatre and runs through Mexican history on stage, from the Maya ball game, played by knocking a heavy rubber ball with the hips, to regional folk dances and music from across the country. The cast is large and the costumes are elaborate.

Get to the theatre a little early so you can choose your seat. After a day in the water, the show gives you a couple of hours sitting down before the drive back.

## What to Bring for Rock, Reef and Sun

This kit makes the day easier:

- **Water shoes**, for the river exits and the rocky inlet
- **A quick-dry layer**, because the river water and the evening air can feel cold
- **A hat**, since many of the paths between areas have no shade
- **A dry bag or waterproof pouch** for your phone, if the rules allow it where you're going

If one day isn't enough, the same company runs Xel-Há and Xplor nearby on the same stretch of coast. Playa del Carmen's Quinta Avenida is a short drive north, which makes an easy dinner stop on the evenings you don't stay for the show.
