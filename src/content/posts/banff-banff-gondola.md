Four people, a glass box, and a cable that climbs steeply up the forested face of Sulphur Mountain. The town of Banff shrinks below you to a grid of rooftops beside the Bow River. Mount Rundle's tilted slab fills the window opposite. About eight minutes later the cabin slides into the upper terminal, and you step out onto a ridge at roughly 2,281 metres.

## Riding up Sulphur Mountain

The base station sits on Mountain Avenue, next to the Banff Upper Hot Springs and about 4 km uphill from Banff Avenue. The cabins are fully enclosed four-seaters that run on a continuous loop. Parties are seated together, so a couple may share with strangers when it's busy.

The ride is smooth and quiet, as the editorial summary puts it: calm. Facing downhill on the way up gives you the valley opening out behind you. Facing uphill shows the line of cabins climbing towards the summit building. If heights bother you, the uphill seat feels steadier.

## What waits at the summit

The upper complex is a multi-level building with viewing decks, places to eat and an interpretive museum about the mountain's geology, wildlife and human history. From the decks you can pick out:

- Mount Rundle and Cascade Mountain flanking the town
- The Bow Valley curving north-west towards Lake Louise
- Lake Minnewanka glinting to the north-east on clear days
- The Fairmont Banff Springs hotel in the trees below

The real draw is the boardwalk. It runs roughly 1 km along the ridge to Sanson's Peak, with stairs and lookouts the whole way. At the end stands a small stone weather observatory from 1903, linked to Norman Sanson, who climbed up for decades to record conditions. Bighorn sheep sometimes graze near the walkway, so keep your distance and never feed them.

## Booking a cabin and beating the lines

This is one of the most visited attractions in the Canadian Rockies and one of the most reviewed. Plan around the crowds rather than hoping to miss them.

- **Buy timed tickets online before you go.** Walk-up availability can disappear on summer days, and the current price is listed on the official Banff Gondola site.
- **Ride early or late.** Getting there for the first cabins at opening usually puts you ahead of the tour coaches that arrive from mid-morning. The evening slots before closing can be calmer too.
- **Carry a Parks Canada pass.** You're inside Banff National Park, and everyone stopping in the park needs one.
- **Dress for a different climate.** The summit is far cooler and windier than town, even in July, and snow can linger on the boardwalk into early summer.

Staff wave you through by your time slot, so turning up long before yours gains you little. Most people pay by card. Tip in the restaurants as you would anywhere in Canada.

## Getting to the base station

Driving is easy, but the base parking lot fills on busy days. Roam Transit, Banff's public bus system, runs a Sulphur Mountain route from downtown that stops at the gondola, which avoids the parking problem altogether. Taxis and hotel shuttles are other options.

Some visitors hike up instead. The Sulphur Mountain trail starts near the base station and switchbacks about 5.5 km through forest, gaining around 650 metres. Allow two to three hours if you're reasonably fit. Many hikers then ride down, but check the official site for current rules on one-way downhill tickets.

## How long to spend, and when

Give the whole visit two to three hours, including the boardwalk, a look round the museum and a coffee or meal. If you're having a sit-down dinner at the summit, allow longer and book it separately.

At the moment the gondola runs 10am–9pm every day of the week. Those hours shift through the year, though, and the cabins also stop for maintenance and in high winds. Look at the official schedule for your dates. Clear mornings bring the sharpest views of the peaks. Long summer evenings give low, warm light across the valley as the town lights come on.

## Pairing it with the rest of Mountain Avenue

The gondola makes an easy half-day with its neighbours. The Banff Upper Hot Springs is a short walk from the base station, so you can soak after the cold summit. Back down the hill, the Cave and Basin National Historic Site, the Bow Falls viewpoint and the Banff Springs hotel all sit within a few minutes' drive. Banff Avenue's restaurants are a quick bus ride away.
