## One night under the Taipei Dome roof

Stray Kids bring RUN IT to Taipei on December 12, 2026. The venue is Taipei Dome, the city's biggest indoor stage. It opened in late 2023 and was built for baseball, so a stadium-scale K-pop show fits inside easily.

Picture the bowl filled with STAY, the fandom's name, every seat holding a Nachimbong lightstick. The dome's closed roof matters in December. Taipei winters bring drizzle and cool evenings, and none of it reaches the floor.

## Dates, tickets and where the details live

The date is fixed: Saturday, December 12, 2026. This is a single Taipei stop on the RUN IT World Tour. Taipei Dome is the venue.

The tour organiser publishes seating plans, ticket channels and entry rules on its official site and social accounts. Seat prices are posted there as well. Nothing on this page replaces those, and we don't print figures we haven't seen.

For big K-pop dates in Taiwan, sales usually go through one authorised online ticketing platform. Resale outside that platform is where fans most often get burned, through duplicate e-tickets or tickets that never arrive.

## Getting to Taipei Dome by MRT

The MRT is how nearly everyone gets there. The nearest station is **Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall (BL16)** on the Bannan (Blue) Line. Exit 5 leads almost straight to the dome along Zhongxiao East Road.

- **Zhongxiao Dunhua (BL15):** one stop west, a 10–15 minute walk, and a useful escape after the show
- **Taipei City Hall (BL18):** two stops east, near Taipei 101 and the Xinyi hotels
- **YouBike:** docks are spread through the surrounding blocks

An EasyCard handles fares on the MRT, buses and YouBike, and convenience stores top it up. Taxis do clog Zhongxiao East Road at the end of a big show, though.

## Lightsticks, freebies and the dome-side ritual

A Stray Kids night in Taipei starts outside the dome. The plaza and the walk from Songshan Cultural and Creative Park, right next door, fill with fans in member merch. Some wear outfits inspired by the tour.

Taiwanese fan culture has a strong sharing streak. Fans often hand out free photocards, stickers and snacks, which locals call 應援物 or simply "freebies". A thank-you, 謝謝 (xièxie), is the usual reply.

- **Lightstick:** paired with the official app, it syncs to the show's lighting
- **Fan chants:** STAY learn the member-name chants in Korean, and Taiwanese crowds know them well
- **Merch:** official booths draw long lines, so many fans queue during the afternoon
- **Cameras:** large venues commonly ban professional cameras and detachable lenses, and the organiser's entry rules set the limits

Most of the crowd queues in the hours before doors open. A lot of fans fill that time at the park's old tobacco factory buildings or grab a meal in the Eslite and Farglory malls attached to the dome.

## After the encore

When the show ends, tens of thousands of people head for one MRT exit at once. Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall Station handles it with staff and barriers, but expect a long wait.

Plenty of regulars walk west to Zhongxiao Dunhua instead. On the way, the side streets of the East District have late-night noodle shops and bubble tea stands. Fans going east usually walk the twenty-odd minutes to Taipei City Hall and the Xinyi District.

## Staying near the venue

Xinyi and the East District are the obvious bases. Both are within walking distance of the dome, with plenty of food open late.

Fans flying in often stay along the Bannan Line instead, around Taipei Main Station or Ximending. It's cheaper, with a straight ride to BL16 and no transfers. From Taoyuan Airport, the Airport MRT runs to Taipei Main Station, where you switch to the Blue Line.

The next day, Taipei 101 and the Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall are both a short walk from the dome. Elephant Mountain trailhead, the classic skyline viewpoint, is just past Xiangshan Station on the Red Line.
