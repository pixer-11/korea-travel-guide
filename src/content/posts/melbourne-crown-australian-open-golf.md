## Australia's oldest professional title

This is the national championship, and it carries that weight. The Australian Open was first played in 1904 at The Australian Golf Club in Sydney. It has since moved between the country's great courses, with Melbourne hosting many of the strongest editions.

The winner lifts the Stonehaven Cup. Gary Player won it seven times, a record. Jack Nicklaus won six. Greg Norman, Peter Thomson and Adam Scott are all on the honour roll too, so a walk past the trophy feels like a walk through the game's history.

For 2026 the event carries Crown branding and sits on the DP World Tour schedule. That means a field mixing Australian players with international tour professionals, on a course built for a national championship.

## Thursday to Monday: the 2026 dates

The 2026 edition runs from Thursday 3 December to Monday 7 December. That calendar is a little unusual. Most tour events wrap up on a Sunday, but this one concludes on Monday 7 December.

The Monday finish shapes how people plan. Locals with office jobs tend to come on the weekend, while visitors on holiday get a weekday finish that is easier to fit around a trip. The organiser's site carries the daily schedule and tee times for each round.

## The Sandbelt factor

Melbourne golf means the Sandbelt. This strip of sandy heathland in the south-eastern suburbs holds a cluster of the world's most admired courses. The best known are Royal Melbourne, Kingston Heath, Victoria and Metropolitan.

The sandy soil drains fast, so the fairways play firm and the greens run quick. Bunkers are cut right to the edge of the putting surfaces, with steep, sandy faces. Alister MacKenzie's influence shows everywhere, most famously on Royal Melbourne's West Course, which opened in 1926.

What that means for spectators is drama around the greens. A shot that lands a few metres too long can run off into heath or sand. Many regular watchers stand behind a demanding par-three or a short par-four and let the field come to them, instead of trying to follow one group for 18 holes.

## Getting out to the course

Melbourne's Opens have traditionally been played on Sandbelt courses. These sit roughly 20 to 25 kilometres south-east of the CBD, around Cheltenham, Black Rock and Heatherton.

The Frankston line out of Flinders Street serves this corner of the city, with Cheltenham and Mentone stations among the closest stops. Public transport runs on myki cards, which you tap on and off at the gates or readers. The organiser's site sets out any event shuttles from the nearest station, along with the parking plan for that year's venue.

Many visitors stay in the CBD or Southbank and ride out each morning. Others choose bayside suburbs such as Brighton or Sandringham to cut down the trip.

## Walking the ropes without annoying anyone

Watching tournament golf has its own manners, and Australian crowds know them well. Marshals hold up paddles when a player is about to hit. When a paddle goes up, everyone around you stops talking and stands still.

A few habits mark out people who have done this before:

- Phones go on silent, and photos wait until the ball is in the air or play has moved on.
- Nobody walks or unwraps food while a player within sight is over the ball.
- Crossing points are marked on the fairways, and marshals open them between groups.
- Spectators stay behind the ropes, even when a ball lands at their feet.

Seasoned fans usually pick one spot for the morning wave and another for the afternoon. They move during the gaps between groups. Grandstands near the closing holes fill fastest on the final day, as the leaders come through.

## Melbourne in the first week of December

Early December is the start of summer, and Melbourne's weather is famously unpredictable. A warm morning can turn cool and windy by afternoon, and a hot northerly can push temperatures up quickly. Layers and a light rain jacket are common sense on a Sandbelt course, where there is little shelter between holes.

The sun is the bigger risk. UV levels in Australian summer are high, even when it is cloudy. Spectators usually wear broad-brimmed hats and sunscreen, and they carry water for long walks across open heathland.

The title sponsor, Crown, runs Crown Melbourne on the Yarra River at Southbank. It is a busy hub for dinners after play, close to the CBD hotels where many visitors stay. Further south, the bayside stretch from St Kilda to Brighton suits a quiet evening near the water after a long day on the course.
