## Why go

The name translates roughly to "Anthill Cafe," and the moment you duck through the entrance you understand why. The interior is carved and textured to mimic the inside of a termite mound or a limestone cave — undulating plaster walls pocked with irregular hollows, curved archways instead of straight lines, and warm, low light that pools in the recesses rather than flooding the room.

It's the kind of design that photographs better than it can be described, which is exactly why it detonated across Indonesian social media feeds in 2026 and turned a neighborhood coffee shop into a genuine destination. If you're the type of traveler who chases distinctive interiors rather than famous skylines, this is the sort of place that ends up as the single most-screenshotted stop of a Jakarta trip.

## Where it is and how to get there

Cafe Sarang Semut sits in Kebayoran Baru, in the Blok M area of South Jakarta — one of the city's older, leafier commercial districts, known for its mix of shopping arcades, embassies, and a dense scattering of cafes and restaurants that cluster around Jalan Melawai and Jalan Palatehan. Blok M is well served by Jakarta's MRT: Blok M BCA station is the natural landmark to navigate from, and the walk into the surrounding side streets from there is short and manageable on foot, though you should expect Jakarta's humidity to make even a ten-minute walk feel longer than it looks on a map.

Blok M is also a major bus terminal hub (Transjakarta corridor 1 terminates here), so if you're coming from Central Jakarta or the old town, a bus-plus-walk combination is realistic without needing a car.

## What to see (and expect)

There's no getting around the fact that this is primarily a design experience wrapped around a coffee menu. Expect the walls themselves to be the main attraction — the "sarang semut" (anthill) texture extends across most surfaces, giving the whole room a fossilized, organic feel that's unusual for a Jakarta cafe, where minimalist industrial or plant-heavy tropical looks tend to dominate.

Because the space leans so heavily on its visual identity, corners and alcoves function almost like little viewing nooks, and you'll notice most tables angled to make the most of them. Given the viral attention, specifics like the drinks menu, seating capacity, and pricing shift as the cafe matures and responds to demand, so rather than guessing at what's on offer, treat your visit as an on-the-ground discovery — check their current social media presence or call ahead for what's actually being served the day you go.

## When to go

Because this is a newly trending spot rather than an established institution, don't assume it keeps the same hours a typical Jakarta cafe would. Confirm the current opening hours directly (via their Instagram or Google listing) before you leave your hotel, since trending venues in Jakarta are known to adjust hours, add reservation systems, or cap walk-in numbers once crowds pick up.

As a general rule for viral cafes in this city, weekday mid-afternoons (after the lunch rush, before the after-work crowd) tend to be calmer than weekend afternoons and evenings, when young crowds chasing the same photos you are will be lining up.

## How to visit like a local

Jakarta cafe-goers overwhelmingly pay by QRIS (Indonesia's unified QR payment system) or card these days, so don't worry about arriving with a pocket full of rupiah — though carrying some small cash is still sensible in case their system is card/QR-only or briefly down. Tipping isn't customary at Indonesian cafes; rounding up or leaving small change is appreciated but never expected.

Because this is a viral spot, the single biggest mistake visitors make is showing up on a Saturday evening expecting a quiet coffee — go on a weekday, ideally outside the 12–2pm lunch window and before the evening social crowd arrives, and you'll have a much better shot at actually sitting inside rather than photographing the doorway from a queue.

If you want a table rather than a quick look, calling ahead or checking whether they've introduced reservations is worth the two minutes it takes, since fast-trending cafes in Jakarta often move to a booking system within months of going viral. Dress for humidity and bring a portable charger — you'll likely be taking more photos than you planned.

## Nearby, while you're in the area

Blok M rewards lingering. The neighborhood's mix of older shopping arcades, street food stalls along the side streets, and a genuinely walkable grid of leafy roads make it easy to turn a cafe visit into a half-day loop — pair your stop with a wander down Jalan Palatehan or a browse through Blok M Square before heading back to the MRT.
