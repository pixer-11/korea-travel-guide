## Super Show 10 comes to Cotai

Super Junior's SUPER SHOW 10 tour plays Galaxy Arena from January 9 to 11, 2027. The dates fall on a Saturday, Sunday and Monday. That makes it an easy weekend run for fans flying in from across Asia.

The Super Show name goes back to 2008. It is the group's long-running concert brand, and each numbered edition becomes its own world tour. A tenth edition says a lot about a group that debuted under SM Entertainment in 2005. Few K-pop acts are still filling arenas two decades in.

The Macau leg runs across multiple nights rather than a single date. For fans that changes the trip: some come for one show, others stay in Cotai for the whole stretch.

## Inside Galaxy Arena

Galaxy Arena sits inside the Galaxy International Convention Center, part of the Galaxy Macau resort complex in Cotai. It opened in 2023 and holds up to about 16,000 people. That makes it one of the largest indoor venues in the Greater Bay Area built for concerts.

You reach it through the resort itself, so the walk takes you along carpeted corridors and past retail arcades rather than across a stadium car park. The bowl is fully indoor and climate-controlled. A cool January evening outside makes no difference to the show.

Seating wraps around the floor in tiers. Standing or floor sections depend on how the production sets up the stage for each tour.

## Getting to Cotai from Hong Kong, Zhuhai and the airport

Cotai is the reclaimed strip between Taipa and Coloane islands, south of the Macau peninsula. Most visitors arrive through one of these:

- **Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge:** shuttle buses cross from Hong Kong airport and Hong Kong's bridge port to the Macau port.
- **Taipa Ferry Terminal:** ferries from Hong Kong land here, a short drive from Cotai.
- **Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal:** on the peninsula, a longer ride south.
- **Border Gate (Gongbei):** the land crossing from Zhuhai in mainland China.
- **Macau International Airport:** on Taipa, minutes from Galaxy Macau by road.

Galaxy Macau runs complimentary resort shuttle buses from the main entry points, and fans lean on them heavily. The Macau LRT's Taipa Line also runs through Cotai and links it to the Taipa Ferry Terminal and the airport. Taxis work too, but queues at the resort ranks lengthen sharply when 16,000 people leave at once.

## Tickets, seats and where the information lives

The tour's organiser publishes the dates, seating plans and ticket links for each stop. Galaxy Macau's official website also carries the event pages for shows at Galaxy Arena. No ticket prices are given here, because none were supplied to us and any figure would be a guess.

Across a multi-night run, fans who want a particular seat often spread their chances across more than one date. Resale through unofficial sellers is a known risk for K-pop arena shows. Many fans stick to official channels because entry checks can involve the name on the ticket.

## Pearl Sapphire Blue in the arena corridors

Super Junior's fandom is E.L.F., short for Ever Lasting Friends. Its official colour is pearl sapphire blue, and on show nights that blue turns up everywhere: lightsticks, shirts, banners and hand-made slogans.

A few things fans usually do here:

- **Bring the official lightstick.** At SM Entertainment concerts the lightsticks are often synced to the show, so a whole tier changes colour together.
- **Learn the fanchants.** E.L.F. chant members' names over specific songs. Knowing a couple makes the night feel very different.
- **Pay by card or mobile.** Resort shops and restaurants take cards. Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted alongside the Macau pataca, though change often comes back in patacas.
- **Stay seated if your section does.** In tiered seating, standing in front of seated fans causes friction. Watch what your row does.

Fan events, cupsleeve cafés and photo-card trades often appear around tour stops, organised by fans themselves. They tend to circulate on social media rather than through any official channel.

## Eating and staying around Galaxy Macau

The advantage of Galaxy Arena is that dinner, a hotel bed and the show can all sit under one roof. The resort includes several hotel brands, and fans often book there to avoid the post-show transport crush.

Broadway Macau, the street-food strip attached to the resort, is the budget-friendly option. Its open-fronted counters serve Macanese and Cantonese snacks. It is the obvious place to fill up before doors, so it fills with blue-shirted crowds on show evenings.

Taipa Village, with the narrow Rua do Cunha lined with pork-chop-bun stalls and almond-cookie shops, is a short ride away. It is also walkable for those who don't mind the distance. Expect tourists there on any weekend; on a concert weekend, expect more.

## A January weekend in Macau around the show

January is one of the more comfortable months to visit Macau. Days are usually cool and dry, often in the mid-teens Celsius, and a light jacket is enough for walking between resorts at night. Indoors, air conditioning runs hard, so a layer helps in the arena too.

With a Monday date in the run, plenty of fans stay a day either side. The rest of Macau is close:

- **Senado Square and the Ruins of St. Paul's:** the Portuguese-era core of the peninsula and part of the Historic Centre of Macao, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Coloane Village:** quieter lanes south of Cotai, home of the original Lord Stow's Bakery egg tarts.
- **The Cotai Strip:** the neighbouring resorts are walkable from Galaxy Macau through covered links and short crossings.

Entry rules into Macau depend on your passport, and the Macao government's immigration pages set out the current requirements. Visitors crossing via Hong Kong or Zhuhai pass through two sets of immigration, which adds real time to a same-day trip.
