## Why this branch matters

This is Chipotle's first restaurant anywhere in Asia, not just Korea. It landed in Gangnam, Seoul's district of flagship retail, entertainment agencies and expense-account dining, in early September 2026. That location choice is deliberate: Gangnam is where Korean brands test whether an import has staying power before it expands nationally.

The opening is a joint-venture pilot, meaning a Korean partner is running the store under license rather than Chipotle operating it directly. If it performs, expect more locations to follow; if it doesn't, this could stay a one-off. Either way, right now it's the only place in the country to try it.

## What's driving the lines

Korean social media has turned this into an event, not just a lunch option. Diners have posted wait times of up to three hours, which for a fast-casual chain is extraordinary and says more about novelty than capacity.

Part of the pull is simple unfamiliarity: burrito-bowl, build-your-own American fast-casual has no real analogue in mainstream Seoul dining. Part of it is the "first in Asia" bragging rights, which travel and food accounts have leaned into hard.

Expect the crowd to be heavily local and young, drawn by the same curiosity that fuels queues outside newly landed foreign brands in Seoul, from Shake Shack's early days to whatever opens next.

## Finding it and timing your visit

It sits within Gangnam's dense commercial core, walkable from Gangnam Station on Line 2, one of the busiest transfer points in the subway system. The exact address and store hours weren't confirmed at the time of writing, so look up the current listing on Naver Map or Chipotle Korea's own channels before you go.

Given the reported three-hour waits, plan around it rather than against it:

- Aim for an off-peak window: weekday mid-afternoon, well clear of lunch (12-1pm) and dinner (6-8pm) rushes typical of Gangnam eateries.
- Have a backup plan. Gangnam has no shortage of alternatives within a five-minute walk if the line looks unmanageable.
- Don't assume walk-in seating will be fast just because it's fast food; the queue is the whole experience right now, not a side effect.

## Ordering and queue etiquette here

Korean queue culture is orderly and unspoken: line forms, nobody cuts, phones come out to record the wait itself as content. Expect staff or a sign to manage the line rather than a host seating you.

Payment at Korean fast-casual chains is almost always card or mobile pay (Naver Pay, Kakao Pay, Samsung Pay); cash is rarely the default anymore, though it's worth carrying some as backup.

Since this is a new format for Seoul, don't assume the ordering flow matches a US Chipotle exactly. Watch what the person ahead of you does, or ask staff to walk you through the build-your-own line if it's your first time.
