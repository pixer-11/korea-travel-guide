## Getting to Ate along Javier Prado

The Monumental sits off Avenida Javier Prado Este, near Ate's border with La Molina. Javier Prado is Lima's main east–west artery. It is also one of the city's most congested roads, and New Year's Eve is not a quiet night for it.

Most concertgoers come one of three ways:

- **Ride-hailing apps.** Uber, Cabify and InDrive all operate across Lima and are the default for visitors.
- **Licensed taxis.** Hotels in Miraflores, Barranco and San Isidro can call one for you.
- **Organised groups.** Friends often share one car, since the drive from the coastal districts is long.

From Miraflores the trip runs east across the city through San Isidro and Surco. On a normal evening that is a substantial drive. Attendees usually leave themselves generous margin, because the stadium approaches tend to back up as doors open.

## Yellow, grapes and a suitcase at midnight

Peruvians bring their own rituals to the countdown. Even inside a stadium, you'll see them happen around you.

- **Yellow underwear.** Wearing something yellow on New Year's Eve is said to bring luck. Yellow wigs, garlands and glasses are sold on Lima's streets through late December.
- **Twelve grapes.** One grape for each chime at midnight, each with a wish. Smuggling grapes into a stadium is hit and miss, so many people simply mime it.
- **The suitcase lap.** Running around the block with an empty suitcase at midnight is meant to promise travel in the coming year. At a concert it becomes a joke, but someone usually tries.
- **Lentils in a pocket.** A handful, carried for prosperity.

Outside the venue, across the neighbourhoods of Lima, families traditionally burn a *muñeco* of the old year at midnight. Fireworks go up from rooftops all over the city. From the upper tiers of the Monumental, you see some of that glow on the horizon.

## Getting in and out of the Monumental

The stadium works like any big Lima venue on a show night. A few habits make it easier.

**At the gates.** Stadium concerts in Lima usually run bag checks at an outer perimeter before the turnstiles. Regulars travel light: a small bag, a phone, an ID and a payment method. Tickets for big shows are typically scanned from a phone.

**Paying for things.** Peru runs on soles. Cards work at many vendors, and Peruvians lean heavily on the mobile wallets Yape and Plin. Visitors without a Peruvian bank account can't use those, so they carry a card plus some cash in small notes.

**Language.** Stadium staff mostly speak Spanish. A few phrases help: *¿Dónde está la puerta?* (where's the gate?), *salida* (exit) and *baño* (toilet).

**Leaving after midnight.** This is the hardest part of the night. Ride-hailing demand across Lima peaks right after the countdown, and prices on the apps surge. Experienced concertgoers often stay for a while after the show, then walk a few blocks away from the gates before requesting a car. That gets them out of the densest pickup zone.

## Where visitors base themselves

Ate itself is mostly residential and commercial. Few international visitors stay there. The usual bases are on the coast:

- **Miraflores.** The default for first-time visitors, with clifftop parks above the Pacific.
- **Barranco.** Lima's bohemian district, full of bars, so the post-concert night carries on there.
- **San Isidro.** Business hotels, a little closer to the stadium than the coast.

La Molina and Surco sit closer to the Monumental. They suit visitors who would rather cut the journey home at 1am than be near the ocean.

## Turning it into a longer stay

New Year's Day in Lima is slow. Many businesses close or open late on January 1, and the city sleeps off the night before.

The days around it are worth having. Lima's historic centre, with the Plaza Mayor and the Basilica and Convent of San Francisco and its catacombs, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Miraflores has the Huaca Pucllana, an adobe pyramid that predates the Inca. And Lima is the place to eat ceviche: it is a lunch dish here, and cevicherías traditionally serve it at midday.

Late December into January is peak summer on the coast. Limeños head south to the beaches of Punta Hermosa and Asia, so the city has a holiday feel all week.
