## A garden older than most of Hobart

The gardens date from 1818. That makes them the second-oldest botanic garden in Australia, after Sydney's. The 14 hectares slope down the eastern flank of the Queens Domain towards the Derwent River. From most of the lawns you look across the water to the eastern shore, with the Tasman Bridge close by to the north.

The age shows in the walls more than the trees. Convict-built brick walls run through the grounds. The best known is the Arthur Wall, built in 1829 with fireplaces set into it so the warmed bricks could help fruit trees ripen in a cool climate. Look along the base and you can still find the old firebox openings.

## Walking the main collections

The layout is a loose loop rather than a grid, so pick up a map at the visitor centre near the main gate. Most first-timers spend between one and a half and three hours. These are the stops worth planning around:

- **The Japanese Garden**, with its pond, bridge and maples, linked to Hobart's sister-city ties with Yaizu.
- **The Conservatory**, a glasshouse from the late 1930s with rotating seasonal displays.
- **The Tasmanian Community Food Garden**, the vegetable plot formerly known as Pete's Patch from ABC's *Gardening Australia*.
- **The lily pond and lawns**, the flat open ground near the lower end where families spread out.
- **The native Tasmanian plantings**, including the fern collection, if you won't get to the island's rainforests.

Some glasshouses and special houses close for maintenance from time to time. Check the official website before you go if one is your main reason for visiting.

## Getting there from the city

On foot it is roughly a 25 to 30 minute walk from the Hobart waterfront. The usual route climbs past the Cenotaph and follows the Queens Domain paths, passing Government House on the way. The walk is pleasant but has a steady uphill stretch at the city end.

There is a car park beside the main entrance on Lower Domain Road. On busy weekend afternoons it fills, and late arrivals end up circling. Metro Tasmania buses run along the nearby highways, but routes change, so check the Metro journey planner rather than relying on a stop name from memory. A taxi or rideshare from Salamanca Place takes only a few minutes.

## Timing your visit

The gates open at 8am and close at 5pm every day of the week. Last entry isn't the same as closing time, so aim to be heading back towards the gate by about 4:30pm.

Weekdays are quiet from opening to close, which makes any weekday the easy choice. On weekends, the calmest window is 8am to 10am. Try not to arrive between 11am and 4pm on a Saturday or Sunday. That is when the lawns fill, the café queue grows and parking gets tight.

Season changes what you see. Spring, from September to November, brings blossom to the Japanese Garden and bulbs to the beds. Autumn, around April, turns the deciduous trees in the cool-climate plantings red and gold. Hobart winters are cold but rarely harsh, and the gardens stay open year-round.

## Garden manners on the Domain

Hobart treats the gardens as a shared backyard, and a few habits keep it that way:

- **Entry has traditionally been free**, with donation boxes near the entrance. Locals drop something in if they come regularly.
- **Picnics are normal** on the open lawns. Take your rubbish with you, because bins are fewer than you'd expect across the wider grounds.
- **Stay off the planted beds** and leave labels alone. This is a working scientific collection, not just a park.
- **Dogs are generally not allowed** inside the gardens. Dog walkers use the open bushland of the Queens Domain outside the walls.
- **Cards work at the café and shop**, and contactless payment is standard across Hobart.

If you're bringing a wedding party or a large group for photos, contact the gardens first. Group bookings and photography permits are handled through the official site.

## Pairing it with the rest of the Domain

The gardens make an easy half-day when combined with nearby stops. The Cenotaph sits on the hill above, with long views down the Derwent. Government House sits next to the gardens, although you will mostly see it from the paths outside.

From there, walk back down to the waterfront for Salamanca Place and Battery Point in the afternoon. If it's a Saturday, start at the gardens at 8am. You can walk the quiet loop first, then reach Salamanca Market while it's still running.
