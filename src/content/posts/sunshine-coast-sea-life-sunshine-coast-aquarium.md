## Down by the Mooloolah River

This aquarium sits at the edge of the river. You reach it along Parkyn Parade, past moored trawlers, charter boats and the restaurants of The Wharf Mooloolaba. The building faces the Mooloolah River just before it meets the sea. Inside it is bright and colourful, and the displays are pitched squarely at families.

Long-time locals may still call it UnderWater World. That was its name for decades, from its opening in 1989 until it joined the SEA LIFE brand. Many Queenslanders grew up coming here. They now bring their own kids, which explains why the place feels more like a community fixture than a tourist trap.

## The Tunnel Beneath the Wharf

The tunnel is the part everyone remembers. An acrylic tube runs through the main oceanarium tank, so water, sharks and rays surround you on both sides and overhead. Rays slide across the curve above you, and larger sharks cruise past close enough to count their gill slits.

Give it more time than you first plan to. The first walk through is often a rush, especially with excited children pulling ahead. Go through a second time, slowly. The animals circle the tank, so a spot that looks empty one minute usually fills the next.

Children tend to press their faces to the lower curve of the tube. Adults get the better view by stepping back and looking up at the silhouettes against the surface light.

## Seals, Touch Pools and the Talk Schedule

The seal presentations are this aquarium's other big draw. Keepers work with the animals in an open pool area, explaining their training, diet and behaviour while the seals show off. These sessions run at set times. Build your visit around them rather than hoping to wander past at the right moment.

Around the rest of the building you'll find:

- **Touch pools**, where staff help children gently handle hardy rockpool creatures such as sea stars
- **Jellyfish displays**, lit to show off their drifting bells
- **Smaller themed tanks** of reef fish, turtles and other local marine life
- **Keeper talks and feeds** through the day, listed on the schedule at the entrance

The educational programs mentioned in the aquarium's own description are real, not an add-on. Staff at the touch pools and talks know their animals well and answer questions happily.

## Reaching Parkyn Parade

Mooloolaba sits at the centre of the Sunshine Coast. It is roughly 90 minutes' drive north of Brisbane via the Bruce Highway and the Sunshine Motorway. Sunshine Coast Airport at Marcoola is around 20 to 25 minutes away by car.

No train line reaches Mooloolaba itself. If you arrive by rail, local buses connect from the hinterland stations, and Sunbus routes also run along the coast into Mooloolaba. Most visitors drive.

Parking is at The Wharf precinct. It fills up during school holidays, so arrive early or park along the Esplanade and walk. From the beachfront shops, the aquarium is a flat stroll of about 15 minutes along the river.

## Picking Your Moment on the Coast

The aquarium is all indoors, and that makes it the Sunshine Coast's classic rainy-day refuge. When a summer storm rolls in off the Coral Sea, families across Mooloolaba have the same idea at once. Expect it to be busier on wet days and during Queensland school holidays.

On a clear morning the beach empties the aquarium instead. Arriving at opening usually means fewer prams in the tunnel and a front-row spot at the first seal presentation. Weekday visits outside school holidays tend to feel calmer than weekends.

Hours and presentation times change with the season. Check the official SEA LIFE Sunshine Coast website on the day rather than relying on an old listing.

## Tickets, Talks and Moving Through the Building

A little planning makes the visit smoother.

- **Book online.** SEA LIFE sells tickets through its official website, and buying ahead saves you standing at the desk with restless children. The current price is listed there.
- **Photograph the schedule.** When you walk in, take a photo of the board of talk and presentation times. Then plan the tunnel and the smaller galleries around them.
- **Get to the seal area early.** Front positions go to people who wait a few minutes before the start. Smaller children see best from the front rail.
- **No flash.** Flash photography unsettles the animals and bounces off the acrylic anyway. Turn it off and let the tank lighting do the work.
- **Ask about add-on experiences.** SEA LIFE sites often run extra behind-the-scenes or animal encounter sessions with limited places. Book those in advance if you want one.

Cards and contactless payment are the norm in Australia, including at the café and gift shop. Tipping isn't expected.

## After the Aquarium: The Wharf and the Beach

The best part is that you walk straight out onto the waterfront. The Wharf Mooloolaba has cafés and restaurants over the river. Mooloolaba's seafood outlets line Parkyn Parade, and locals buy fresh Mooloolaba prawns there to eat by the water.

Between June and November, humpback whales pass this stretch of coast on their migration. Whale-watching cruises leave from the Mooloolaba harbour area, which makes a fitting follow-up to a morning inside.

From there, the Mooloolaba Spit leads to calm river-mouth swimming spots. Walk the other way along the Esplanade and you reach patrolled Mooloolaba Beach, with Point Cartwright's headland and lighthouse visible across the river mouth. For a family day, that combination of aquarium, lunch and beach is hard to beat on this coast.
