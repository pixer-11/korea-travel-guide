## A steelworks that turned into a park

The smokestacks are still standing. Fundidora Park sits on the site of the Fundidora de Fierro y Acero de Monterrey, founded in 1900. It was the first integrated steel mill in Latin America, and it shut down in 1986.

The city did not tear it down. It kept the blast furnaces, the rusted gantries, the rail lines and the brick industrial halls. Then it planted lawns, trees and jogging paths between them.

That is the experience here. You walk under a canopy of trees and then come out in front of a riveted steel tower several storeys high. Behind it, the saddle-shaped peak of Cerro de la Silla fills the eastern skyline.

## Horno3: up the old blast furnace

Blast Furnace No. 3 is the centrepiece. Its official name is Horno3, the Museo del Acero, and it opened in 2007 inside the shell of the furnace itself.

The exhibits explain how iron ore and coal became steel, and why Monterrey grew into Mexico's industrial city. The halls are built around the furnace's original structure. A glass-and-steel annex sits next to the old metal, so the original equipment stays in view.

The highlight is the ride to the top. An external lift takes you up the furnace to a walkway high above the park. From there you can see:

- Cerro de la Silla to the east
- the Sierra Madre ranges around the city
- the whole park, including the lake and the other furnaces

Ticket prices and the hours for the museum and the summit ride are set separately from the park's. Check the Horno3 official website before you go rather than assuming they match the park gates.

## Arriving along Paseo Santa Lucía

The best way to arrive is on foot along the water. Paseo Santa Lucía is a landscaped artificial river walk that runs for about 2.5 km from the Macroplaza in the city centre and ends at Fundidora.

The path follows the channel past fountains, bridges and planted banks. Boats also run along the same route, which gives you a lazier option.

Walking it one way and taking the Metro back works well. You see the old centre, the Museo de Historia Mexicana and the riverwalk in one long arc, and you finish under the furnaces.

## What's inside the grounds

The park covers roughly 140 hectares, so it helps to know what is where. Inside or along the edges you will find:

- **Parque Plaza Sésamo**, the Sesame Street amusement park, with rides and water attractions for younger children
- **Cintermex**, the convention and exhibition centre
- **Auditorio Citibanamex** and the open-air grounds used for big concerts and the Pa'l Norte music festival
- **Centro de las Artes**, set in former industrial halls, with the Cineteca and Fototeca de Nuevo León
- **Arena Monterrey**, just across the road on the park's edge
- a central lake, play areas and long walking and running trails

Old locomotives and ore wagons are parked along the paths like sculptures. Between 2001 and 2006 the park also hosted a Champ Car street race, the Grand Prix of Monterrey, on a circuit through the grounds.

## Getting to Obrera

The park's address is Adolfo Prieto S/N in the Obrera neighbourhood, east of downtown. Metro is the simplest way in.

Take **Metro Line 1** to **Parque Fundidora** station, which lets you out at the park's northern side. The next stop, **Y Griega**, is also within walking distance of the southern entrances.

Driving is possible, and the park has parking areas. On concert nights and during large events at Cintermex or Arena Monterrey, the surrounding avenues clog up. On those nights the Metro is the better choice. Ride-hailing apps work well in Monterrey and drop off at the main gates.

## Using the park the way Monterrey does

People in Monterrey call it simply "Fundidora," and the park is part of everyday city life. It is very popular, so plan around the crowds rather than hoping to have it to yourself.

**Use the early hours.** The gates open at 6:00 AM every day. Runners and cyclists take over the paths in the cool hours before work. Going early also means you finish your walk before the strongest sun.

**Respect the heat.** Monterrey summers are fierce, and shade between the furnaces is patchy. Carry water, a hat and sunscreen, and save the indoor spaces like Horno3 and Centro de las Artes for the middle of the day.

**Expect families at weekends.** Saturdays and Sundays bring picnics, birthday groups and children heading to Plaza Sésamo. If you want quiet paths, visit on a weekday.

**Bring both card and cash.** Museums and larger venues take cards. Snack vendors and small stands along the paths are often easier to pay in pesos.

**Check the event calendar.** A concert or festival can close off big sections of the grounds. On those days, the area around the stages fills up hours before showtime.

## How long to stay, and when

Two to three hours is enough for a loop of the main paths, the lake and a look up at the furnaces. Add a couple of hours if you're doing Horno3 properly and riding to the top.

A full day works if you combine the Paseo Santa Lucía walk, the museum and a film or exhibition at Centro de las Artes. Families heading to Plaza Sésamo should plan for a full day too.

November to March brings the most comfortable temperatures. The park stays open until 10:00 PM every night. In the evening, the lit-up steel structures look completely different from how they do by day, and the air has cooled enough for a long walk.
