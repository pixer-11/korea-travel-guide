## Twelve lanes, no cars

Sheikh Zayed Road is usually a motorway. It is Dubai's main artery, a multi-lane highway lined with glass towers, carrying traffic between the old city and the Marina at all hours. On Sunday, November 22, 2026, the cars give way to people for Dubai Run.

You walk or jog down the middle of the carriageway, where no pedestrian normally goes. The Emirates Towers, the Museum of the Future and the Burj Khalifa rise beside you in Downtown Dubai. The concrete under your shoes is road surface, painted with lane markings, and the Dubai Metro's elevated Red Line runs alongside.

The event is billed as the world's largest free community fun run. It sits inside the Dubai Fitness Challenge, the city's month-long push to get residents moving. The run comes near the end of that month.

## Choosing between 5km and 10km

There are two routes, and they suit different crowds. The facts are simple:

- **5km:** the family route. It draws strollers, children, walking groups and first-timers.
- **10km:** the longer loop. It attracts regular runners and anyone wanting more of the skyline.
- **Both:** free, untimed in spirit and run on the closed highway.

The 5km is a fun run in the truest sense. Plenty of people walk it start to finish with friends, stopping for photos with the Burj Khalifa behind them. The 10km has more steady runners, though nobody is chasing a podium.

The Dubai Fitness Challenge publishes the route maps, start times and registration on its official site. Registration is free, and participants receive their entry details through it.

## Getting onto Sheikh Zayed Road on run day

The Red Line is the obvious way in. It tracks Sheikh Zayed Road on an elevated viaduct, with stations close to the course:

- **Emirates Towers**
- **Financial Centre**
- **Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall**
- **World Trade Centre**

Driving makes little sense when the city's main road is closed. Most participants ride the metro, take a taxi to the edge of the closure or walk in from Downtown Dubai and DIFC hotels.

Metro fares are paid with a Nol card, tapped at the gates on entry and exit. Visitors usually buy one from the station machines or ticket office. Carriages on run morning fill with people already in their sportswear.

## Start-line manners on Sheikh Zayed Road

This is a community event, and the etiquette reflects that. Faster runners generally move towards the front of their wave. Walkers, families and strollers usually settle towards the back, which keeps the first kilometre from turning into a bottleneck.

Groups walking side by side tend to keep to one part of the road, leaving room for runners to pass. If you stop for a photo, step towards the edge first. Hundreds of people behind you are moving at walking pace.

Standard sportswear is normal here, and the dress code is relaxed for a fitness event. Outside the course, Dubai's malls and metro still expect shoulders and knees broadly covered. Many people pack a light layer for the ride home.

Residents often turn the morning into a social outing with office teams, running clubs and school groups wearing matching shirts. Coming solo is common too. Nobody needs a team to join in.

## What late November feels like

November is the start of Dubai's comfortable season. The searing summer heat has gone, and mornings on the open highway are mild. The sun still comes up strong once it clears the towers.

Participants typically carry little. A phone, a Nol card and a water bottle cover most needs. Sunglasses and a cap help on the open stretches, where the road offers no shade between buildings.

Children in strollers are a familiar sight on the 5km. Parents usually bring snacks and a spare top, since the morning stretches longer than the run itself.

## After the finish

The course leaves you close to Downtown Dubai. Dubai Mall and the Burj Khalifa are a short hop by metro to Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall station. Plenty of finishers end the morning with breakfast there or in the cafés around Boulevard and DIFC.

The Museum of the Future sits beside the Emirates Towers stretch of the road. Its steel shell, covered in Arabic calligraphy, is worth a slow look on foot once the crowds thin.

Dubai Run is one day among many in the Dubai Fitness Challenge. The month also brings other free sessions, rides and classes around the city, all listed by the organiser on the same official site.
