## Twelve lanes, no cars

Sheikh Zayed Road is usually a motorway. It is Dubai's main artery, a multi-lane highway lined with glass towers, carrying traffic between the old city and the Marina at all hours. Dubai Run was set to hand it over from cars to people on Sunday, November 22, 2026.

The course was laid out down the middle of the carriageway, where no pedestrian normally goes. The Emirates Towers, the Museum of the Future and the Burj Khalifa rise beside it in Downtown Dubai. The surface is plain road, painted with lane markings, and the Dubai Metro's elevated Red Line runs alongside.

The event was billed as the world's largest free community fun run. It sat inside the Dubai Fitness Challenge, the city's month-long push to get residents moving. The run was scheduled near the end of that month.

## Choosing between 5km and 10km

There were two routes, each aimed at a different group. The announced format was simple:

- **5km:** the family route, pitched at strollers, children, walking groups and first-timers.
- **10km:** the longer loop, aimed at regular runners and anyone wanting more of the skyline.
- **Both:** free, untimed in spirit and laid out on the closed highway.

The 5km was a fun run in the truest sense. On routes like this, plenty of people walk start to finish with friends, stopping for photos with the Burj Khalifa behind them. The 10km was aimed at steadier runners, though no podium was at stake.

Registration was free. The organiser provided route maps, start times and entry details to registered participants.

## Getting onto Sheikh Zayed Road on run day

The Red Line is the obvious way in. It tracks Sheikh Zayed Road on an elevated viaduct, with stations close to the course:

- **Emirates Towers**
- **Financial Centre**
- **Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall**
- **World Trade Centre**

Driving makes little sense when the city's main road is closed. On run days, most participants typically ride the metro, take a taxi to the edge of the closure or walk in from Downtown Dubai and DIFC hotels.

Metro fares are paid with a Nol card, tapped at the gates on entry and exit. Visitors usually buy one from the station machines or ticket office. On run mornings, carriages typically fill with people already in their sportswear.

## Start-line manners on Sheikh Zayed Road

This is a community event, and the etiquette reflects that. Faster runners generally move towards the front of their wave. Walkers, families and strollers usually settle towards the back, which keeps the first kilometre from turning into a bottleneck.

Groups walking side by side tend to keep to one part of the road, leaving room for runners to pass. People stopping for a photo usually step towards the edge first, since hundreds of others behind them are moving at walking pace.

Standard sportswear is normal here, and the dress code is relaxed for a fitness event. Outside the course, Dubai's malls and metro still expect shoulders and knees broadly covered. Many people pack a light layer for the ride home.

Residents often turn the morning into a social outing with office teams, running clubs and school groups wearing matching shirts. Coming solo is common too. No team was needed to join in.

## What late November feels like

November is the start of Dubai's comfortable season. The searing summer heat has gone, and mornings on the open highway are mild. The sun still comes up strong once it clears the towers.

Participants typically carry little. A phone, a Nol card and a water bottle cover most needs. Sunglasses and a cap help on the open stretches, where the road offers no shade between buildings.

Children in strollers are a familiar sight on the 5km. Parents usually bring snacks and a spare top, since the morning stretches longer than the run itself.

## After the finish

The course was laid out to finish close to Downtown Dubai. Dubai Mall and the Burj Khalifa are a short hop by metro to Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall station. Breakfast there, or in the cafés around Boulevard and DIFC, is a popular way to end a morning run.

The Museum of the Future sits beside the Emirates Towers stretch of the road. Its steel shell, covered in Arabic calligraphy, rewards a slow look on foot.

Dubai Run was one day among many in the Dubai Fitness Challenge. The month's programme also included other free sessions, rides and classes around the city.
