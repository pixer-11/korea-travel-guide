## A French counter on Gray's Inn Road

The name translates as "at Grandpa's breads". It suits the room. Aux pains de papy is a French bakery in a narrow nook on Gray's Inn Road, with a counter of baked goods, sweets and sandwiches and only a handful of seats.

It's budget-friendly, and it is one of the better-reviewed small bakeries around King's Cross. Expect a quick stop, not a long café session. The space is too tight to linger in at busy times.

## Finding 279 Gray's Inn Road

The nearest Tube is **King's Cross St Pancras**, served by these lines:

- Victoria
- Northern
- Piccadilly
- Circle, Hammersmith & City and Metropolitan

From the station, walk south down Gray's Inn Road and away from the King's Cross junction. The walk takes roughly 5–10 minutes, past terraced Georgian and Victorian frontages with small shops at street level. The postcode is WC1X 8QF, on the stretch where King's Cross runs into Bloomsbury.

Coming off a Eurostar at St Pancras International? This is one of the closest places to get a proper French bakery breakfast before heading into the city.

## Ordering at the counter

Seating is limited, so most people order to take away. If you need a seat, take one only once you've ordered, and give it up promptly when there's a queue.

- **Point if unsure.** If you can't name what's in the case, pointing at the counter works fine. You don't need any French.
- **Pay quickly.** Contactless card or phone payment is the norm in London bakeries. If you're carrying only cash, ask at the counter first.
- **No tipping expected.** Tips aren't customary for counter service in the UK. A jar, if there is one, is optional.
- **Go early for choice.** Bakeries bake in batches. The fullest counter is usually earlier in the day, and favourites can sell out by late afternoon.

Sandwiches make this a useful lunch option. In a small room the line can back up near the door, so have your order ready before you reach the counter.

## Where to take your bag

Since you probably won't eat inside, plan a spot nearby. Several are within a short walk:

- **Granary Square and the Regent's Canal**, north of King's Cross station, have wide canal-side steps for sitting outside.
- **Coal Drops Yard**, next to Granary Square, works for browsing after you eat.
- **The British Library** on Euston Road has a forecourt and free exhibition galleries.
- **St George's Gardens and Coram's Fields area**, south-west towards Bloomsbury, are quieter green spaces near the Foundling Museum.
- **Lamb's Conduit Street** suits a slow walk among independent shops.

A visit is 10–15 minutes at the counter, or about half an hour if you get a seat. Pair it with the canal or the British Library for a cheap, easy half-day around King's Cross.
