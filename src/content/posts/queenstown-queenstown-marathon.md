## Saturday 14 November 2026, in late spring

The date is Saturday 14 November 2026. In full it is the New Zealand Sotheby's International Realty Queenstown Marathon, and it bills itself as the world's most beautiful marathon. That is a big claim for a town surrounded by the Remarkables, but most finishers don't argue with it.

Mid-November is late spring in Central Otago. Snow often still sits on the higher peaks while the valley floor turns green. Days are long, and mornings in the basin start cool before the sun gets over the ranges.

## Four distances, one finish town

Each distance is a different day out:

- **Marathon:** the full 42.2km across the Wakatipu Basin
- **Half-marathon:** 21.1km, the most popular choice for runners travelling in
- **10km:** a shorter run that still covers proper trail
- **Kids run:** a short event for younger runners, popular with families

Entries, distance details and race rules are published on the official Queenstown Marathon website. No prices appear here because none were supplied to us. The current entry fees are listed on that site, next to each distance.

## From Arrowtown country to the lakefront

The course is built around the network of the Queenstown Trail. Previous editions have started out near Arrowtown, the old gold-mining town about twenty minutes' drive from Queenstown. From there the route crosses the basin on a mix of sealed road and compacted gravel trail.

The landmarks along the way are the reason the race exists. Runners pass Lake Hayes, which on a still morning mirrors the hills around it. They cross the Kawarau and Shotover rivers on trail bridges, then follow the shore of Lake Wakatipu into town. The gravel sections and short climbs make it slower than a flat city marathon, and experienced runners usually set their time goals with that in mind.

## Getting to the start without the stress

Race morning in Queenstown runs on shuttles. Previous editions have run buses from town to the start area, because there is little parking near the start line and the roads are partly closed. Most runners stay in Queenstown and ride out, then finish on foot at the place they left from.

A few local habits make the weekend easier:

- **Paying:** cards and contactless work nearly everywhere in Queenstown, from cafés to bus fares on the Orbus network.
- **Tipping:** it is not expected in New Zealand. Rounding up for good service is a kindness, not an obligation.
- **Trail manners:** much of the course is shared trail at other times of year. Spectators usually keep to one side and leave the runners' line clear.
- **Layers:** runners commonly wear a throwaway top at the start and drop it, because the basin is cold before the sun reaches it.

Accommodation around race weekend is in heavy demand. Visitors who miss out in town usually stay in Frankton, near Queenstown Airport, or in Arrowtown, which has the advantage of being close to the start.

## Watching from the lake edge

Spectators gather near the finish in central Queenstown, where the town centre meets the waterfront. It is flat, compact and easy on foot. Cafés and bars along the lakefront mean supporters can wait for their runner with a coffee rather than on a kerb.

Supporters with a car or a bike sometimes cheer earlier on the course, around Lake Hayes or the river crossings, then head back into town for the finish. Road closures affect how easily that works, and the organiser's course map shows which sections are reachable.

## The day after the finish line

Queenstown is a good place to recover, partly because there is plenty to do that isn't running. The Skyline Gondola climbs Bob's Peak right above town for a view down the course you have just run. The TSS Earnslaw, a coal-fired steamship dating from 1912, crosses Lake Wakatipu to Walter Peak High Country Farm.

Arrowtown is worth a slower second look once the race traffic has gone. The restored miners' cottages of the Chinese Settlement sit along the Arrow River. For runners with strong legs and a car, the road to Glenorchy follows the lake north for roughly 45 minutes. It is the kind of drive many visitors keep for the final day.
