## A fruit stop on the slow road

The Niagara River Parkway is the scenic way north. It follows the Canadian bank of the river from the Falls, past Queenston and on toward Niagara-on-the-Lake, with orchards and vineyards lining the inland side. Walker's Country Market sits on the stretch between Queenston and Old Town, at 15796 Niagara River Pkwy.

This is a longtime shop, not a weekend pop-up. People come for seasonal fruit, baked goods and sandwiches. Most leave with a jar or two as well, because jellies, jams and gourmet gifts fill the rest of the shelves. It is well known and consistently well rated, so in high season you won't be the only car pulling in.

## What the season puts in the bins

The Niagara Peninsula is Ontario's fruit belt. Lake Ontario softens the climate just enough for tender fruit that struggles elsewhere in the province, and a shop on this road follows that harvest.

What you find depends on the month. Across the region, it usually runs like this:

- **Early summer:** strawberries, then cherries
- **Mid to late summer:** peaches, plums, apricots
- **Late summer into autumn:** pears, grapes, apples

Outside the growing season, expect less fresh fruit and more preserves and baking. Ask at the counter what came in most recently. In a fruit-belt shop, that question gets you the best thing in the room.

## Lunch on the river road

The sandwiches make this a practical place to stop for lunch. At the moment the doors open at 9am and close at 5pm, with the same hours every day this week. Lunchtime fits easily inside that, and so does a mid-afternoon pastry run.

Take your bag south to Queenston Heights Park, where Brock's Monument stands above the gorge and there are lawns for a picnic. Or take it north to the riverside pull-offs along the Parkway, where the far bank is New York State.

## Getting there from the Falls

Driving is the simplest option. Head north on the Niagara Parkway from the Falls tourist strip, carry on past the Floral Clock and Queenston, and keep going toward Niagara-on-the-Lake. The market is on that run, before you reach Old Town.

Cycling is the other good way to do it. The Niagara River Recreation Trail runs alongside the Parkway, and riding it is a popular way to link the Falls, Queenston and Niagara-on-the-Lake. A sandwich and fruit stop halfway is exactly what that ride needs.

## Shopping the counter at Walker's

A few habits make the visit smoother:

- **Don't tip.** Nobody expects a tip when you buy over a market counter in Ontario.
- **Pay by tap if you can.** Contactless cards are the norm across Canada, but ask at the till if you're unsure what's accepted.
- **Check prices on the signs.** Read the shelf labels and bin signs rather than guessing, because seasonal fruit is priced by the day.
- **Get the timing right.** Arriving soon after 9am usually means a fuller bakery case before the midday Parkway traffic. Arriving just before 5pm means the shop is closing and the shelves have been picked over.
- **Buy fruit last.** Ripe Niagara peaches bruise in a hot car. If you're driving in summer, a cooler bag earns its space.

One mistake can actually cost you here. If you're crossing back into the United States, fresh fruit can be restricted or confiscated at the border. Check US Customs and Border Protection rules before you load up on peaches. Sealed jams and baked goods are usually the easier souvenir.

## Building a half day around it

You'll spend 20 to 45 minutes in the market itself, depending on how long the preserves shelves hold you. It works best as one stop in a longer Parkway day.

Stops that pair well with it:

- **The Floral Clock and Queenston Heights**, a short drive south
- **Queenston village**, home of the Laura Secord Homestead
- **Wineries on and around the Parkway**, many with tasting rooms
- **Old Town Niagara-on-the-Lake**, with Queen Street's shops and Fort George at the edge of town

A good order is to start at the Falls in the morning and drive north. Stop at Queenston, pick up lunch here, then finish in Old Town for the late afternoon.
