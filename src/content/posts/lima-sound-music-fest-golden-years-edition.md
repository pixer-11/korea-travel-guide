- **Flo Rida:** the Miami rapper behind "Low", "Right Round", "Whistle" and "Club Can't Handle Me". His set is club-anthem territory, built for jumping.
- **Marshmello:** the DJ-producer in the white helmet, known for "Alone", "Happier" and "Friends". He brings the festival-scale drops.

## Estadio Nacional: the venue

Estadio Nacional is Peru's national stadium and the home ground of the national football team. The current structure dates from 1952. A major renovation finished in 2011 and left it with modern stands holding roughly 50,000 people.

It sits on Avenida José Díaz in Cercado de Lima, on the edge of the Santa Beatriz neighbourhood. Its four stands carry compass names: Oriente, Occidente, Norte and Sur. Your ticket names one of those, or a floor zone if the stage layout includes one.

Next door is the Parque de la Reserva. Its Circuito Mágico del Agua, a park of lit fountains, is one of the city's best-known evening sights. Big concerts here are an easy walk from it.

## Getting to the stadium

Lima's traffic shapes a stadium night more than anything else. Fans usually choose one of three routes.

- **Metropolitano:** the city's bus rapid transit runs down the Vía Expresa (Paseo de la República), right beside the stadium, and has a stop named Estadio Nacional. From Miraflores or Barranco it is a direct ride north along the expressway.
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber, Cabify and inDrive are all widely used in Lima. Most drivers set down a few blocks from the stadium, because the streets close to the gates get sealed off on big event nights.
- **On foot:** from hotels in Santa Beatriz, Lince or the edge of central Lima, walking is often the simplest choice.

Most international visitors stay in Miraflores, San Isidro or Barranco. They have the city's widest choice of hotels and restaurants, and the Vía Expresa links all three to the stadium.

## Leaving after the encore

The real test of the night starts after the last song. With tens of thousands of people leaving at once, the roads around Avenida José Díaz jam fast. Ride-hailing prices climb as the stadium empties.

Experienced concertgoers in Lima usually walk ten or fifteen minutes away from the stadium before requesting a car. Arequipa Avenue and the Lince side work well. Others time their exit with the crowd and take the Metropolitano south, where it runs at night. Groups often agree on a meeting point beforehand, because phone signal around a full stadium can be patchy.

November 21, 2026 is a Saturday. That makes a long Lima weekend easy to plan around the show. Late November is late spring here, and the grey winter blanket of cloud over the coast usually starts to lift around this time.
