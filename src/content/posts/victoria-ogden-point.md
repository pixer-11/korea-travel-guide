## A road of stone into the strait

Ogden Point is mostly a long walk on rock. The breakwater runs roughly 800 metres out from the southwest corner of James Bay, built of huge granite blocks during the First World War years. A small lighthouse sits at the far end.

The deck along the top is flat and wide enough for two-way foot traffic. On one side is the calm water of the harbour approach. On the other the Strait of Juan de Fuca rolls in against the stacked stone. On clear days the Olympic Mountains in Washington State show up across the water.

## Getting to the end of Dallas Road

The address is 211 Dallas Rd, where the shoreline road bends toward the cruise ship terminal. From the Inner Harbour and the Parliament Buildings it's a flat walk of about 25–30 minutes through James Bay. Head south on Belleville or Montreal Street, then follow the water.

- **On foot:** the most pleasant route is the seaside path, with Fisherman's Wharf on the way.
- **By bus:** BC Transit runs routes through James Bay. Check its trip planner for the current stop nearest Dallas Road.
- **By car:** there is paid parking near the terminal. Rates and payment options are posted on the signs at the lot.
- **By bike:** the Dallas Road waterfront has a separated bike path running east toward Beacon Hill Park.

## Becoming the needle on the sundial

Most people walk straight past the sundial. Look down near the path. Installed in 2009, it's an interactive sundial 18 feet wide, and it has no fixed pointer. You are the gnomon.

You stand on the marked spot for the current date and your shadow falls across the hour lines. It only works when the sun is out, which on this coast is never guaranteed. Give it a minute, check your shadow against your phone, and you'll see how close it gets.

It's a good thing to do with kids before the breakwater walk. It also explains why people at Ogden Point sometimes stand very still on the pavement.

## Walking the breakwater

Allow 30–45 minutes for the return walk at an easy pace, longer if you stop for photos. The surface is concrete laid over the granite. Most of it has no railings, and the drop onto the blocks is real.

What to watch for along the way:

- **Divers:** Ogden Point is one of the best-known shore dives in Canada. You'll often see wetsuited groups entering the water on the harbour side.
- **Seabirds:** cormorants and gulls work the rocks, and the outer side catches the swell.
- **Cruise ships:** in cruise season the big ships tie up right beside the breakwater, so they tower over you on the walk out.
- **The lighthouse:** small, squat and functional. It's the turnaround point and the place where everyone takes the same photo back toward the city.

When the wind comes up from the strait, spray can top the outer edge. Stay toward the middle on rough days and turn back if waves are breaking over the deck.

## When the crowds come and go

It's open 24 hours, so timing is entirely up to you. It's calmest between 7am and 9am on weekdays and 7am–8am on weekends. That's when you'll mostly share the breakwater with joggers and dog walkers.

Try not to arrive between 10am and 11pm on weekends. That's the usual peak, when the path fills with families and the sundial gets a queue of shadows. On cruise ship days the area around the terminal fills up with passengers and tour coaches too.

Evenings are popular for watching the sun drop toward Sooke and the Olympics. Bring a layer: the wind off open water drops the temperature fast once the sun is low.

## Breakwater manners

This is a working waterfront, and a few habits keep it pleasant for everyone:

- **Keep to one side.** On busy afternoons the path works like a two-lane road, so walk on the right and let runners through.
- **Stay off the outer blocks.** The granite is slick with algae near the waterline, and the swell can surge without warning.
- **Give divers space.** Don't block the steps where they enter and leave the water, and don't drop anything over the edge.
- **Pay for parking first.** Use the posted machines or app at the lot before you walk out. The return trip is long enough that a ticket is likely if you skip it.
- **Cards are fine.** Canadian businesses around here take tap payment almost everywhere, so cash is rarely needed.

## Carrying on along the shore

Ogden Point is the western start of one of Victoria's best walks. Head east along Dallas Road and the clifftop path runs past an off-leash dog area with views over the strait. After that come Beacon Hill Park and the Mile Zero marker of the Trans-Canada Highway at the foot of Douglas Street.

If you go north instead, Fisherman's Wharf is about a 10–15 minute walk. You'll find colourful float homes and food kiosks on the docks. From there the harbour path continues to the Inner Harbour and the Empress, which makes a neat loop back downtown.

Put together, Ogden Point, the sundial, Dallas Road and Fisherman's Wharf fill an easy half-day on foot. It costs nothing but parking, if you drive.
