## A first for Mongolian esports

The East Asia circuit comes to the steppe. On October 31, 2026, the MLBB Championship Tour East Asia stops in Ulaanbaatar. This is the first time the regional tournament has been held in Mongolia. The local partner is Esports Network (ESN), a Mongolian esports organisation.

Mobile Legends: Bang Bang is Moonton's five-a-side mobile MOBA. Matches are short and fast, and every teamfight is projected on big screens above the players. The format suits a hall full of people who play the game themselves. In Ulaanbaatar, many of them play it daily on their phones.

## Dates, tickets and where to look

The announced date is Saturday, October 31, 2026. ESN and MLBB's official esports channels carry the ticketing and venue information for the Ulaanbaatar stop. This guide has no seat categories, prices or session times from the organisers, so it prints none.

Fans who can't get into the hall usually watch the stream. MLBB tournaments are normally broadcast live on the game's official esports channels, with commentary in several languages.

## Ulaanbaatar at the end of October

It's cold. By late October, daytime highs in Ulaanbaatar hover around freezing and nights drop well below. Inside, the city is heavily heated, so visitors tend to wear layers they can strip off once they're in the arena.

- **Arriving:** Chinggis Khaan International Airport is in the Khöshig Valley, roughly an hour's drive south of the city centre.
- **Traffic:** central Ulaanbaatar is known for gridlock at rush hour. Locals often walk the last stretch along Peace Avenue (Enkh Taivny Örgön Chölöö) rather than sit in it.
- **Staying:** visitors usually base themselves near Sükhbaatar Square, which puts hotels, cafés and the main avenue within walking distance.

## Matchday in the capital

Mongolian crowds are loud for their own teams. When a local roster takes a teamfight, you can expect chanting, banners in Cyrillic script and phone torches raised in the stands. Fans usually turn up wearing team jerseys or cosplay. Halloween falling on the same day adds a few more costumes to the queue.

Paying is easy. Bank cards work in most city businesses, and local mobile banking apps (QPay is the common QR system) handle a lot of small purchases. The currency is the tögrög (MNT). A few Mongolian words go a long way: *sain baina uu* (hello) and *bayarlalaa* (thank you).

Attendees usually spread their sightseeing over the days either side of the event:

- **Gandan Monastery:** the city's main Buddhist monastery, home to a towering gilded Avalokiteshvara (Migjid Janraisig) statue.
- **Sükhbaatar Square:** the government palace and the seated Chinggis Khaan monument face the square.
- **National Museum of Mongolia:** next to the square, covering everything from the Xiongnu to the socialist era.
- **Zaisan Memorial:** a hilltop Soviet-era monument looking out over the city and the Tuul River valley.

Hot *buuz* (steamed mutton dumplings) and *khuushuur* (fried meat pastries) are what Mongolians eat against the cold. Small canteens all over the centre serve them.
