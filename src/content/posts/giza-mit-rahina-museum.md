## What's left of Memphis

You enter a palm-shaded village, not a city. Memphis was Egypt's capital from around 3100 BC, founded according to tradition by Menes. Today most of it lies under fields and houses, and this open-air museum holds the pieces that came back up.

The site is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing "Memphis and its Necropolis", the same listing that covers the pyramid fields from Giza to Dahshur. That gives you the context: this is the city that the pyramids were built to serve.

## The colossus and the sphinx

The main event is indoors. A colossal limestone statue of Ramesses II lies on its back inside a purpose-built hall. A raised gallery runs around it, so you look down on the carved face, the cartouches on the shoulder and the belt, and the dagger at the waist.

Its legs below the knee are missing, yet it still fills the room. Up close you can read the fine detail of the royal headdress and the false beard.

Outside, the garden is laid out as a path between stones:
- **The calcite (alabaster) sphinx**, about 8 m long, the largest sphinx carved from that stone
- **Standing statues of Ramesses II**, some of red granite
- **Stelae, column fragments and sarcophagus lids** set along the walkway

Most visitors are done in 45 minutes to an hour.

## Timing your visit to Mit Rahina

Go early. It's calmest between 8am and 10am on both weekdays and weekends. Avoid weekends from 11am to 3pm: that's when the day-tour minibuses arrive from Saqqara and the Ramesses hall gallery gets crowded.

At the moment the museum opens 8am–4pm every day. Egyptian heritage sites adjust hours by season and for holidays, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities site before you set out. Ending here by early afternoon leaves daylight for Saqqara or Dahshur.

There is no metro. Most people come by taxi, ride-hailing car or a hired driver from Cairo or Giza, usually as one stop on a Memphis, Saqqara and Dahshur loop. Saqqara's Step Pyramid of Djoser is only a few kilometres west.

## Getting in and moving around

Buy your ticket at the office by the entrance. The current price is posted there and on the official ministry site. Ask at the window which payment methods are accepted that day, and carry a backup.

Inside, a few habits help:
- **Start with the Ramesses hall** if you arrive at opening, before groups fill the gallery.
- **Expect guardians** to offer to show you a photo angle or a hidden carving. That help comes with an expected tip, so decline politely if you don't want it.
- **Souvenir stalls** line the exit path. A firm "la, shukran" (no, thank you) works.
- **Shade is limited** in the garden. Bring water and a hat for the outdoor section.

A guide from Cairo will explain how the scattered stones fit into the lost city, which is hard to picture from the labels alone. Booking one through your hotel for the full Memphis and Saqqara day makes the most sense.
