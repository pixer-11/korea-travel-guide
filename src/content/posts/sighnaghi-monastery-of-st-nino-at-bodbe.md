## A nun's garden above the Alazani Valley

The road out of Sighnaghi drops south through cypresses and ends at a stone gateway. Inside, paths cut through clipped hedges and flowerbeds toward a low stone church and a separate bell tower. This is a working convent, and the nuns keep the grounds that way.

The site dates back to the 9th century. Its real draw is far older: St. Nino, the woman credited with converting Georgia to Christianity in the 4th century, is buried in the church. Most of what you see now was rebuilt in later centuries.

## Inside the church and down to the spring

The church is small and dim, lit by candles and hung with icons. St. Nino's tomb lies in a side chapel on the right, and pilgrims queue quietly to kneel and touch it. Photography inside is generally not welcome, so put the phone away at the door.

Outside, the terrace looks straight across the Alazani Valley to the Greater Caucasus. On a clear day the snow line is sharp, and cloud often hides it by afternoon.

Then there is the spring:

- A signed stone stairway leads steeply downhill from the grounds to St. Nino's spring, a small chapel above a holy well.
- Many Georgians bathe there in a simple wooden bathhouse. Bathing shirts are sold on site, and the queue can be long.
- The climb back up is the hard part. Give yourself 40 minutes or more for the round trip, and bring water in summer.

## Dress code and conduct at Bodbe

The rules are enforced at the gate, not just suggested.

- **Women:** cover your head and wear a skirt below the knee. Wraps and scarves usually hang near the entrance for visitors who arrive unprepared.
- **Men:** long trousers, no sleeveless tops, and take off your hat inside the church.
- **Inside:** speak quietly, do not turn your back to the altar for photos, and step aside for worshippers at the tomb.
- **Candles:** thin beeswax candles are sold by the entrance. Light one from an existing flame and set it in the sand tray.

Georgians call it Bodbis Monasteri, or simply Bodbe. A soft *gamarjobat* (hello) to the nuns goes a long way. If a service is running, stand at the back and keep still. Weekends bring large family groups and church tours. Turning up when the gates open on a weekday usually gives you the tomb with only a short wait.

## Getting to Bodbe from Sighnaghi

Most visitors base themselves in Sighnaghi, about two hours' drive east of Tbilisi. Marshrutkas (minibuses) to Sighnaghi run from Tbilisi's Samgori area. From town you can:

- **Walk:** roughly 2 km along the road, mostly downhill on the way out, with a real climb on the way back.
- **Taxi:** drivers wait near Sighnaghi's central square. Agree a return trip and waiting time before you set off.
- **Drive:** there is parking by the gate, which fills up on weekends.

Opening hours are currently 10am–6pm daily, but religious sites change their schedules for feast days and the seasons. Check before you go, especially around Orthodox holidays such as St. Nino's Day in January. Combine the visit with Sighnaghi's 18th-century town walls and a Kakhetian wine cellar to fill the day.
