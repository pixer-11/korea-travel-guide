## Two January nights, one home crowd

AURA plays Hong Kong on Saturday 16 and Sunday 17 January 2027. That's a weekend pair: one night for fans travelling in, one for the city's own diehards.

Joey Yung has been at the top of Cantopop since her debut at the end of the 1990s. A Hong Kong concert from her is a home show in the fullest sense. The crowd knows every word, including the ballads she recorded years before the AURA material existed.

Hong Kong audiences also treat a big concert as a social event. Groups of friends book blocks of seats together. Office colleagues come straight from dinner. Parents bring the children they once sang her songs to.

## Where Hong Kong stages a show like this

The venue is on the ticket itself. Two halls host most of the city's big pop residencies, and both are worth understanding before you look at a seat map.

The **Hong Kong Coliseum** in Hung Hom is the traditional home of Cantopop. It is an inverted-pyramid concrete arena opened in 1983. Its stage sits in the middle of the floor with seating rising steeply on all four sides, so even the upper rows feel close to the performer. Joey Yung has played long runs here across her career.

**AsiaWorld-Arena** sits inside the AsiaWorld-Expo complex on Lantau, next to Hong Kong International Airport. It is a flat-floored hall with an end stage, closer in feel to a European arena. The trade-off is distance: it's roughly half an hour by train from the city centre.

## Reaching Hung Hom or the airport

Hung Hom is easy to reach by train. Hung Hom Station serves the East Rail line and the Tuen Ma line. Covered footbridges run from the station concourse straight to the Coliseum doors, so you never cross a road.

From Tsim Sha Tsui the walk along the waterfront promenade takes about 20 minutes. Taxis line up on the Coliseum's own approach road, but after the encore that queue gets long.

For AsiaWorld-Arena, the Airport Express stops at AsiaWorld-Expo Station, its last station, right at the hall. Concertgoers heading back to Kowloon or Central usually ride the Airport Express. Others take the MTR from Tung Chung, which is a short bus or taxi ride away.

## Flower boards and the encore chant

Some Hong Kong concert habits look strange from outside but are part of the night here.

- **Flower boards (花牌, fa paai):** fan clubs and well-wishers order large floral stands for the venue entrance, printed with messages to the singer. Fans photograph them before going in.
- **Light sticks:** many fans carry official or fan-club light sticks. Whole sections light up in one colour during the ballads.
- **The encore chant:** when the main set ends, the hall shouts "Encore!" in a rhythmic chant. At Hong Kong shows the encore section often runs long, with several songs.
- **Singalongs in Cantonese:** the crowd takes over choruses, so learning the hook lines of her older hits pays off.

## Getting through Hung Hom on a concert night

How it works in practice, from the station to your seat:

- **Pay with Octopus.** The stored-value card covers the MTR, most convenience stores and many food stalls near both venues. Locals rarely pull out cash for small purchases.
- **Eat beforehand nearby.** Concertgoers at the Coliseum usually have dinner in the malls around Hung Hom Station or in Whampoa. Food inside the hall is limited.
- **Read your ticket's gate.** Coliseum entrances are grouped by section around the building. Fans walk the outer concourse to the gate printed on the ticket, not the nearest door.
- **Cameras and phones:** venue rules on recording are printed on the ticket and posted at the doors. Hong Kong ushers do enforce them.
- **Leaving:** most people walk straight back to Hung Hom Station after the show. Fans in no hurry usually wait out the rush in a nearby café, or walk to Tsim Sha Tsui along the harbour.

Useful Cantonese: *hoi chong* (開場) means the show is starting, and *chut hau* (出口) means exit.

## Tickets and seats

Fans usually buy through the organiser's official ticketing partner. Shows at the Coliseum, a government-run venue, are traditionally sold through URBTIX. Other arenas use their own ticketing partners.

At the Coliseum, the higher rows on the long sides give the cleanest view of choreography and lighting. Floor seats put you near the stage but hide the patterns the lights make. Zone prices are set by the ticketing partner and are not given here.

Resale is a sore point in Hong Kong. Tickets for big Cantopop names are widely scalped, and fans warn each other about counterfeit e-tickets. Buying only through the official channel is how regular concertgoers avoid it.

## January in the city

Mid-January is Hong Kong's coolest, driest stretch. Evenings are often in the mid-teens Celsius. Arenas are air-conditioned hard, so locals wear a jacket that comes off easily.

The weekend gives time for more. The Avenue of Stars and the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront are a short walk from Hung Hom. The Star Ferry crosses to Central from Tsim Sha Tsui. Visitors in for both nights usually stay in Tsim Sha Tsui, Hung Hom or Mong Kok, all on train lines that connect directly to either venue.
