## Fifth Avenue, brick by brick

Start under the arch. The lit "Gaslamp Quarter" sign spans Fifth Avenue near L Street and marks the district's southern end. From there the grid runs north toward Broadway, lined with brick and cast-iron storefronts from the 1880s boom.

This was Alonzo Horton's "New Town," the downtown he began laying out in 1867. Its southern blocks became the Stingaree, a red-light district of saloons and gambling halls. Wyatt Earp ran gaming halls here in the 1880s.

The district went onto the National Register of Historic Places in 1980. The gas-style street lamps that gave it the name are replicas from the revival, not originals.

## What to look at, not just where to eat

Most visitors only see restaurant patios. Look up at the second storeys instead. That's where the carved cornices, bay windows and painted dates on the facades are.

- **William Heath Davis House** (Fourth Avenue and Island Avenue): downtown's oldest surviving structure, a prefabricated house shipped around Cape Horn in 1850. It's now a museum and the base for walking tours of the district.
- **Horton Plaza Park** (Broadway and Fourth): the plaza with the restored Broadway Fountain, at the northern edge of the district.
- **Petco Park**: the Padres' ballpark, a block east of Fifth Avenue's southern end.
- **Convention Center and waterfront**: south across Harbor Drive. The USS Midway Museum and Seaport Village are a short walk west along the bay.

Give the architecture an hour on foot. If you're staying for dinner and drinks, plan on most of an evening.

## Getting there and getting around

Don't drive if you can avoid it. Parking is scarce on Fifth Avenue itself, and garages fill up on event nights.

- **Trolley:** Gaslamp Quarter station (Green and Silver Lines) is on Harbor Drive at the southern end. Fifth Avenue station (Blue and Orange Lines) on C Street is just north of Broadway.
- **On foot:** the district is flat and compact. Little Italy is about 20 minutes north, and East Village is right next door.
- **Rideshare:** use the side streets for pickups. Fifth Avenue gets congested at night.

The streets are public and open around the clock. Shops and museums keep their own hours, so check the Davis House's current schedule before planning a tour around it.

## Reading the night on Fifth

The district changes after dark. Weekend nights draw crowds for bars, clubs and rooftop lounges, and some venues have door lines and check IDs. Bring a physical ID, because you must be 21 to enter many places after a certain hour.

Some ways to get the most out of a visit:

- **Know the calendar.** Padres home games and big conventions, especially Comic-Con in July, pack the sidewalks and sell out hotel rooms. Look up the schedules before you book.
- **Book dinner on weekends.** Popular places fill by early evening. Weeknights are more relaxed.
- **Pay by card.** Cards work almost everywhere. Tipping is standard in US restaurants and bars: about 18–20% at a table, and a dollar or so per drink at a bar.
- **Come back in daylight.** Morning is the best time to photograph the facades without the crowds.

The district is a downtown nightlife hub, not a quiet find. Plan around the crowds rather than expecting to dodge them completely.
