## A green front on the quai

Look for the green shopfront and the yellow sign. Shakespeare and Company sits on Rue de la Bûcherie, a short street running along the Seine. Notre-Dame stands directly across the water. Next door is Square René Viviani, home to a propped-up locust tree planted in 1601 and often called the oldest tree in Paris.

Outside, racks and tables of books spill onto the pavement. Inside, the ceilings are low and the shelves reach them. Books are stacked into every corner and alcove. Narrow passages split off in several directions, so you squeeze past other browsers with your elbows tucked in.

## Why this shop matters

George Whitman, an American, opened the shop in 1951 and stocked it with new and used English-language books. In 1964 he renamed it Shakespeare and Company, after Sylvia Beach's original bookshop on Rue de l'Odéon. Hemingway and Fitzgerald were regulars at Beach's shop, and in 1922 she published Joyce's *Ulysses*.

Whitman's shop built its own legend. For decades, travelling writers known as Tumbleweeds have slept on narrow beds tucked between the shelves. In return they help out in the shop, read daily and write a one-page autobiography. His daughter, Sylvia Whitman, now runs the shop and keeps the tradition going.

This is not a quiet find. It is one of the most-reviewed bookshops in the world, and on a busy afternoon you'll feel it.

## Upstairs, where people slow down

A narrow, creaking staircase leads to the upstairs reading library. Those books aren't for sale. You can pull one off the shelf and read it on one of the benches or worn armchairs. Upstairs you'll also find an old piano and a typewriter, plus windows looking out toward the cathedral.

Over one doorway is a line Whitman made the shop's motto: "Be not inhospitable to strangers lest they be angels in disguise." Take your time up here. Downstairs is for browsing, and upstairs is for sitting.

## Timing your visit around the queue

Current hours are 10am–8pm Monday to Saturday. On Sunday the shop opens at 12pm and closes at 7pm. Check the shop's website before you go, because hours can change around holidays.

Crowd forecasts make the timing clear:

- **Weekdays:** quietest between 10am and 12pm, so go right at opening.
- **Saturday:** quietest between 10am and 11am.
- **Weekend afternoons:** busiest from 1pm to 8pm. Avoid this window if you can.
- **Sunday:** doors don't open until noon, so the whole day falls inside the busy weekend stretch.

Allow 30–45 minutes for browsing, longer if you settle in upstairs. In the evenings the shop often hosts readings and author talks, and the website lists them. Popular events can need advance booking.

## Getting through the door, and the stamp

The quickest route is Saint-Michel station on Metro Line 4, or Saint-Michel Notre-Dame on RER B and C. From either one it's a few minutes' walk east along the quai. Maubert–Mutualité on Line 10 is just as close from the south.

How visiting works here:

- **The queue:** when the shop fills up, staff hold people at the door and let them in as others leave. The line moves steadily, so don't give up on it.
- **Bags:** large backpacks are awkward in the passages. Wear yours on your front or travel light.
- **Photos:** signs inside ask visitors not to take pictures. Take your photos of the shopfront outside and keep your phone away among the shelves.
- **The stamp:** ask at the till and they'll stamp your book with the Shakespeare and Company stamp. That's the souvenir most visitors take home, and it's far better than a photo.
- **Paying:** cards are accepted at the till. Staff speak English, though a "bonjour" when you walk in is still polite in Paris.

The best way to support the place is to buy a book. Many people walk out with nothing, so make a point of choosing something.

## Around the corner

You don't have to go far once you're done. Step into Square René Viviani for the old tree and one of the best ground-level views of Notre-Dame. The shop's café is right next door if you want a coffee with your new book.

Cross the Pont au Double and you're on the Île de la Cité, at the cathedral's front. Head east instead and the riverside bouquinistes sell old prints and paperbacks from their green boxes along the parapet. Rue de la Huchette and the Latin Quarter lanes lie just behind you to the west.
