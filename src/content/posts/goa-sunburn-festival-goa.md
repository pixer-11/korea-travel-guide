## A festival built around year's end

Sunburn is a December fixture in Goa. It is billed as Asia's largest EDM festival, and it lands in the stretch of the year when the state is already at its fullest. Christmas lights go up along the beach roads and New Year parties fill the calendar.

The festival began in 2007 on the sand at Candolim. It grew from there into a multi-day event with several stages, big-name international DJs and Indian electronic acts. The format is simple: afternoons warming up, nights at full volume, then repeat.

## The 2026 edition: dates and tickets

Sunburn Festival Goa 2026 takes place in mid-to-late December. That timing matches the festival's habit of closing out the year in the days before Christmas.

The organiser publishes the exact days, the venue, the lineup and the ticket links on Sunburn's official site. Those are the details that decide your trip, so that site is the reference point rather than resale listings or social media reposts. Ticket tiers usually run from general entry to premium and VIP passes, and each one covers different areas of the ground.

## Where the stages have stood

The venue has moved over the years. After the early beach editions at Candolim, Sunburn spent many seasons on the slopes around Vagator in North Goa. The headland there looks down on the Arabian Sea.

The 2024 edition moved inland to Dhargalim, in Pernem taluka, close to the new airport at Mopa. That pattern matters for planning. Whatever the exact site in a given year, the festival has stayed mostly in North Goa, so that is where attendees usually stay.

## Getting to North Goa

Goa has two airports, and for Sunburn the choice is not trivial.

- **Manohar International Airport, Mopa (GOX):** in the far north, near Pernem. It is the closer option for northern venues and beaches like Morjim and Arambol.
- **Dabolim Airport (GOI):** near Vasco da Gama in south-central Goa. Expect a longer road trip north, usually well over an hour in December traffic.
- **By train:** Konkan Railway stops at Thivim, the main station for North Goa. Karmali, near Old Goa, and Madgaon, further south, are the other big stations.

Most visitors get around by taxi or a rented scooter. Goa's own app-based service, GoaMiles, runs alongside the local taxis. Ride-hailing apps that work in other Indian cities have limited reach here, which surprises many first-timers.

## The Christmas-week squeeze

This is what makes Sunburn different from most festivals. It doesn't happen in a quiet month. It runs straight into Goa's busiest fortnight of the year, when domestic tourists arrive for Christmas and New Year's Eve.

Rooms in Anjuna, Vagator, Assagao and Calangute fill early, and rates climb. Attendees typically sort their stay months ahead, often splitting villas or guesthouses in groups. The roads near Baga, Calangute and the bridges over the Mandovi slow to a crawl most evenings.

The weather helps. December is in Goa's dry season, with warm, sunny days and cooler nights. Light layers cover both the afternoon sets and the walk out after midnight.

## Getting through the gates and the long nights

A crowd this size runs on habit, and regulars know the routine.

- **ID:** carry government photo ID. Entry checks are standard, and foreign visitors usually bring their passport or a clear copy.
- **Wristbands and passes:** keep them on and intact. A damaged band can mean a fresh trip through the entry queue.
- **Getting home:** settle the plan before the last set. Taxi demand peaks when the main stage shuts. Many groups pre-arrange a driver or stay within reach of the venue.
- **Scooters:** riding back on dark lanes after a night of drinking is exactly what Goa police check for on festival nights. Helmets are compulsory.
- **Drugs:** India's narcotics law is strict, and it applies to foreigners as fully as to residents. Police presence around Sunburn is heavy every year.

Most food and drink is sold inside, and outside drinks are not allowed in. Indian festivals are mostly cashless now, and UPI payments by phone are everywhere. Foreign cards tend to work at larger stalls, but not always at small vendors in town.

## Before and after the music

Sunburn days leave the mornings free, and North Goa fills them easily.

- **Chapora Fort:** the red laterite ruin above Vagator, with views over the Chapora river mouth. The walk up is short and steep, and late afternoon is the popular time.
- **Vagator and Ozran beaches:** dark rocks frame the sand below the cliffs, with beach shacks along the back.
- **Anjuna Flea Market:** held on Wednesdays through the winter season, selling clothes, jewellery and souvenirs on the open ground near the beach.
- **Assagao and Siolim:** quieter inland villages with Portuguese-era houses and cafés, popular for slow recovery breakfasts.
- **Panaji (Panjim):** the state capital, where the Fontainhas quarter has narrow streets and painted houses. In Christmas week, the white Church of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception is lit up.

Many visitors add a few days after the festival and stay through to New Year. Others head south to Palolem or Agonda, where the beaches are calmer once the bass has stopped.
