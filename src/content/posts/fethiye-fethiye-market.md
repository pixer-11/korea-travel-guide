## A grocery on the Ölüdeniz road

Start with the address. Fethiye Market sits at Ölüdeniz Caddesi No:22 in Taşyaka, the residential district southeast of Fethiye's harbour. Ölüdeniz Caddesi is the main road that carries traffic out of town, uphill towards Hisarönü and the lagoon at Ölüdeniz.

That location is the whole point. If you're renting an apartment or villa along this road, this is a grocery on your way home. You won't need to drive back into the centre for bread and water.

It stays quiet, too. The store has only a modest number of online reviews, and they are mostly favourable. Tour groups don't stop here and nobody sells it as an attraction. It is simply a place to shop.

## Not the Tuesday market

This is the confusion worth clearing up first. Search "Fethiye market" and you'll mostly get the town's famous weekly Tuesday bazaar. That one fills the streets beside the canal in the centre with stalls of produce, textiles and spices.

This listing is something else: a fixed shop in Taşyaka with a street number. In Turkish, *market* is the everyday word for a grocery store or minimarket. That's why so many corner shops across the country have "Market" in their name.

So plan them as two separate outings:

- **Tuesday bazaar:** a half-day browse in central Fethiye, only on Tuesdays.
- **Fethiye Market, Taşyaka:** a quick practical stop, handy any day it's open.

If a taxi driver looks puzzled, show them the full address, Ölüdeniz Cd. No:22, Taşyaka, rather than just the name.

## Getting to Taşyaka

It's easiest with the dolmuş. Fethiye's shared minibuses run along Ölüdeniz Caddesi between the town centre, Hisarönü and Ölüdeniz. Taşyaka is on that route. Flag one down at the roadside and tell the driver where you want to get off.

By car, just follow the Ölüdeniz road out of the centre. The store is on the main road itself, so you won't be threading through back lanes to find it.

From the harbour, walking is possible but it's uphill, along a busy road. In summer heat, and carrying shopping bags back, most people would rather take the minibus one way.

## Stocking a self-catering week

We can't vouch for what this particular shop has on its shelves, so we won't list its stock. What a neighbourhood Turkish *market* usually covers is the daily basics. Knowing what to look for makes the first shop quicker.

Worth putting on your list at any Turkish grocery:

- **Water in large bottles.** Visitors don't usually drink the tap water, so buy multi-litre bottles.
- **Beyaz peynir**, the white brined cheese, and olives, for a Turkish-style breakfast.
- **Ayran**, the salted yogurt drink, which is good cold on a hot afternoon.
- **Bread (*ekmek*)**, which is best bought early and eaten the same day.
- **Sunscreen and bits for the beach** before you head down to Ölüdeniz.

Think of this as a top-up shop. For a week's worth of fresh fruit and vegetables, the Tuesday bazaar in the centre is the traditional place to buy in bulk.

## Paying and packing at the till

It's a quick, low-ceremony transaction. A short *merhaba* (hello) when you walk in and *teşekkürler* (thank you) at the till is all the ritual there is. Tipping isn't expected at a grocery.

Card payment, including contactless, is widely used in Turkish shops. Still, keep some Turkish lira on you in case the card machine plays up.

Plastic bags have been charged for at Turkish tills since 2019. You may hear *poşet ister misiniz?*, meaning "do you want a bag?" Bring a tote from your apartment and you can just shake your head. If you're buying several big water bottles, a sturdy bag or a daypack saves your fingers on the walk back.

## Around Taşyaka

Taşyaka is where people live. Expect apartment blocks and roadside shops rather than sights. The attractions are a short ride away in either direction.

Downhill, central Fethiye has the harbour promenade and the Lycian rock tombs cut into the cliff above town, including the Tomb of Amyntas. The fish market area, where you pick your fish and have it cooked nearby, is a classic Fethiye evening.

Uphill along the same road are Hisarönü and, further on, Ölüdeniz with its lagoon and paragliding landing beach. Stopping at the store on the way back from the beach, with the dolmuş passing along the same road, is the most natural way to fit it into your day.
