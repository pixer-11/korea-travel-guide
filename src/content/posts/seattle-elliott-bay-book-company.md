## A bookstore on several levels on 10th Avenue

The front door opens onto shelves, not a café counter. Elliott Bay Book Company is a full-service bookstore spread over more than one floor, and that layout sets the pace of a visit. The new releases and front tables are on the street level. The deeper stock is further in and on the other levels.

It sits on 10th Avenue just off the Pike/Pine corridor, Capitol Hill's main strip of bars, record shops and restaurants. The store opened in Pioneer Square in 1973 and moved up the hill in 2010. Many Seattleites still talk about the old location, but this is the shop now.

This isn't a quiet find. It's one of the most-reviewed bookstores in the city, and it gets crowded at the weekend.

## What you're actually there for

You come here to browse properly. The selection runs wide, so plan on 45 minutes at least. If you come with a reading list, expect closer to two hours.

What sets it apart is the events programme. The store runs frequent author readings and talks plus regular book clubs. On a good week that means a novelist one night and a poet or historian the next. The calendar on the store's website shows who's coming and whether you need a ticket.

Watch for:
- **Staff picks**, flagged on the shelves, which point you to books you won't see on chain-store tables
- **Signed copies**, which often turn up after a visiting author has read
- **Book clubs**, open to anyone, with dates on the calendar

## Getting to 1521 10th Ave

Capitol Hill light rail station is the easiest way in. Come out onto Broadway and walk south toward Pine Street, then cut east to 10th Avenue. It's under ten minutes on foot. The First Hill Streetcar also runs along Broadway if you're coming from Pioneer Square or the International District.

From downtown you can walk up Pike Street instead. It's a steady uphill climb of roughly 25 minutes from Pike Place Market. Street parking around Pike/Pine is metered and hard to find in the evening, so take transit if you can.

## Browsing etiquette on Capitol Hill

Treat it like a working shop, not a photo stop. Most people pay by card at the till, and nobody expects a tip. If you want something that isn't on the shelf, ask at the desk. The staff can look it up and special-order it.

On a busy reading night, arrive before the start time and expect to stand at the back if you're late. Questions come at the end, and the signing line forms afterwards. Keep phone calls outside, because the event floor is close to the browsing areas.

Locals in Seattle have a particular habit here. Photographing a staff pick and then ordering it online is the one thing that really annoys people. If a card sold you on a book, buy it at the register.

## Picking your hour

Right now the store opens 9am–10pm every day. Hours can change, so check the website before a late visit. Your timing makes a big difference. On weekdays it's quietest from 9am to 12pm, and again from 9pm until closing at 10pm.

Weekends are a different shop. Saturday and Sunday are calm only from 9am to 10am and from 9pm to 10pm. Avoid 11am–8pm, when the aisles fill and the till queue builds.

A late browse after dinner on Pike Street is one of the best ways to see it. The streets outside are at their busiest then, and the shop is at its quietest.

## Around the block

You can easily fill an afternoon nearby:
- **Cal Anderson Park**, a few blocks north, with a reservoir fountain and wide lawns where you can read your new book
- **Broadway**, a short walk west, with cafés and the light rail station
- **Pike/Pine**, right outside the door, for dinner or a drink before an evening reading
- **Volunteer Park and the Seattle Asian Art Museum**, a longer walk north through the residential side of Capitol Hill

Put the bookstore at the start or the end of your walk around the neighbourhood. That way you aren't carrying a bag of hardbacks all day.
