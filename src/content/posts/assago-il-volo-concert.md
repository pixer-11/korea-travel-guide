## Three Voices, One Indoor Arena

Il Volo sing without irony. Piero Barone, Ignazio Boschetto and Gianluca Ginoble met as teenagers on the Italian TV talent show *Ti lascio una canzone* in 2009. They have spent the years since bringing tenor and baritone power to pop arrangements.

The trio won the Sanremo Festival in 2015 with "Grande amore" and finished third at Eurovision that same year. On December 8, 2026 they play Unipol Forum in Assago, the big covered arena that serves as Milan's main indoor concert stage.

## The Date and What It Means in Milan

December 8 is not an ordinary Tuesday in Italy. It is the Festa dell'Immacolata, a national public holiday, and it follows Milan's own feast of Sant'Ambrogio on December 7. Many Milanese take the two days together as a long break.

That shapes the evening around the show:

- Offices and schools are closed, so audiences tend to drift in through the afternoon rather than rushing out of work.
- The city centre usually has its Christmas lights up, and the Duomo area is busy with holiday crowds.
- Some shops and smaller restaurants keep holiday hours, while the big shopping centres generally stay open.

## Inside Unipol Forum

The Forum is a large enclosed arena, home court of the Olimpia Milano basketball team. For concerts it splits into a standing or seated floor in front of the stage and tiered seating rising on the sides and back.

For a vocal act like Il Volo, that bowl shape matters. The upper tiers give a full view of the stage and lighting, while floor seats bring you close enough to watch three faces working through a long held note. Seat categories and the arena map sit with the official ticket sellers for this date.

## Getting to Assago

Assago sits just outside Milan's city limits, roughly southwest of the centre. Getting there is simple:

- **Metro:** line M2 (green), the Assago branch, to **Assago Milanofiori Forum**, the end of the line. The arena is a short walk from the exit.
- **Alternative stop:** **Assago Milanofiori Nord**, one stop back, sits beside the Milanofiori business and shopping district.
- **Car:** the Forum lies close to the Milan ring road (Tangenziale Ovest), with large car parks around the arena.

The M2 branch splits from the main line at Famagosta. Trains heading for Abbiategrasso do not go to Assago, so the destination board on the platform is the thing to watch.

## Forum Night Habits

A few routines make a concert night in Assago smoother. Regulars tend to follow these:

- **The metro fare:** Assago is outside Milan's basic urban fare zone, so a single urban ticket does not cover the trip. Regulars buy a ticket that covers the extra zones, or tap a contactless bank card at the ATM turnstiles.
- **Return trip:** after the encore, thousands of people head for the same platform. Some fans wait out the first surge at the arena bar. Others walk to Milanofiori Nord to board there.
- **Eating:** most fans eat before the show in the Milanofiori area or back in the city, since arena food is snack-level.
- **Bags:** large arenas in Italy run security checks at the gates. Concertgoers usually travel light to get through faster.
- **Singing along:** Italian audiences sing the choruses of the classics out loud. "Grande amore" and "O sole mio" in particular tend to become a room-wide duet.

## Making a Milan Holiday of It

Most visitors stay in Milan proper rather than Assago. The M2 line runs straight to Porta Genova, Cadorna and Centrale, so Navigli or Centrale hotels give a direct ride back after the show.

The holiday timing helps. On December 7 the city celebrates Sant'Ambrogio, and La Scala traditionally opens its opera season that night. The Oh Bej! Oh Bej! Christmas market usually fills the streets around the Castello Sforzesco over the same days. For a fan of operatic pop, an evening of Il Volo after a day of Milan's real opera house makes a neat weekend.
