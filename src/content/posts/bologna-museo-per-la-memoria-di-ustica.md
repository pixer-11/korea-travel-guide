## What's inside

The centerpiece is the actual wreckage of Itavia Flight 870, a DC-9 that fell into the Tyrrhenian Sea on 27 June 1980, killing all 81 aboard.

Bologna-born artist Christian Boltanski and architect Christian Hauvette reassembled the recovered fragments into the full silhouette of the plane, suspended in a dark hall. Around it hang 81 black mirrors, one per victim, and speakers loop fragments of ordinary conversation and music recorded that same evening, meant to evoke the last radio contact.

The crash itself remains one of Italy's great unsolved cases: decades of investigations have pointed toward a missile strike during a NATO-era air battle, but no state has ever admitted responsibility. The museum, opened in 2007, treats the wreckage as memorial rather than forensic exhibit; there's little text, mostly darkness, metal and sound.

## Hours and getting there

This is not a drop-in museum. Opening hours are strictly:

- Friday, Saturday, Sunday: 5:00 – 8:00 PM
- Monday through Thursday: closed

Arrive well before 8pm; last entry is typically earlier than closing, so don't plan a 7:45 arrival. The building sits on Via di Saliceto 3/22 in the Bolognina neighborhood, north of Bologna Centrale station. It's a walkable 15-20 minutes from the station, or a short ride on buses serving Via di Saliceto. There's no dedicated visitor parking lot, so street parking or public transit is the practical option.

## Why it rates so highly

With a 4.8 rating from close to 1,800 reviews, this is one of Bologna's most consistently praised museums, and it's well known well beyond the city, not a niche stop. The reason is the installation itself: nothing about it feels like a standard history exhibit.

Visitors regularly describe it as unexpectedly emotional rather than informative in the usual museum sense. There's no gift shop energy here, no crowds shuffling past display cases. The single room and its silence do the work.

Because it's well-known rather than obscure, the three-day, three-hour weekly window means the small space can fill up on Saturday evenings especially. Fridays right at 5pm tend to be calmer than Sunday early evening, when it's often the last chance of the week for visitors leaving Bologna.

## Visiting with respect

This is a memorial to 81 people who died, not a photo backdrop. Treat the darkened hall accordingly.

- Keep voices low inside the installation room; the audio loop is meant to be heard clearly.
- Photography is generally tolerated but flash and posed shots feel out of place here.
- Read the introductory panels near the entrance before the main hall; they give context the installation itself doesn't spell out.
- Budget the full 45 minutes; rushing through defeats the point of Boltanski's design.

Check the museum's official channels before visiting, since holiday closures or special events can shift the Friday-to-Sunday schedule.
