## A hill, a lawn and a decade and a half of guitars

Fort Canning Green is a sloping lawn, not an arena. It spreads out below Fort Canning Centre, a white colonial-era barracks building with long verandas. Big rain trees ring the edges and the city's office towers rise behind them. On December 4, 2026, the Northern Irish trio Two Door Cinema Club bring their 15th Anniversary Tour to this patch of grass.

The band formed in Bangor, County Down. Their sound is bright and fast: chiming, interlocking guitar lines over dance-floor drums. For a crowd standing on grass in the tropical night, that suits the setting well.

The first four come from the early, guitar-heavy era. "Sun" brings in the warmer, horn-tinged side of the band's second album, *Beacon*.

## Getting to Fort Canning Green

Three MRT stations surround the park, and each one is a short walk uphill.

- **Dhoby Ghaut** (North South, North East and Circle lines): the busiest interchange, at the Orchard Road end of the park.
- **Fort Canning** (Downtown Line): on the River Valley side.
- **Clarke Quay** (North East Line): across the river, handy for dinner beforehand.

The paths through Fort Canning Park are paved but climb steadily, with some stairs. Concertgoers who want to avoid the climb usually take a taxi or ride-hail to the drop-off by Fort Canning Centre. After the encore, a crowd this size heads for the same few exits, so many people walk down to Dhoby Ghaut rather than wait for a car.

## Working the lawn on show night

Outdoor gigs here have their own routine. A few habits make the night easier.

- **Ponchos, not umbrellas.** December falls in Singapore's northeast monsoon season, and evening showers are common. Regulars at outdoor shows bring a light poncho, because open umbrellas block the view behind them.
- **Shoes for grass.** The ground can be damp and uneven after rain. Closed shoes cope better than sandals.
- **Pay by card or phone.** Singapore runs on contactless payment, and festival-style food and drink stalls almost always take it.
- **Pick your slope.** The ground rises away from the stage. Fans who want to dance push towards the front. Those who want a clear view without the crush usually settle further up the incline.

Tipping isn't customary in Singapore, so you won't be expected to tip at the bars. Even after dark the air stays warm and humid, and regulars treat water as part of the plan.

## Before and after the music

The neighbourhood gives the evening some range. Clarke Quay sits just downhill on the Singapore River, a strip of riverside restaurants and bars that stay busy late. Robertson Quay, a quieter stretch further upriver, has cafés and wine bars along the water.

At the other end of the park, Dhoby Ghaut leads straight onto Orchard Road. Its malls have basement food halls for a quick meal. In daylight, the park itself has the Battlebox, a restored underground British command bunker from the Second World War, and the Fort Gate, a remnant of the original nineteenth-century fort.
