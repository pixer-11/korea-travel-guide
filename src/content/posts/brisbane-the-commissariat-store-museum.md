## A convict building among the towers

William Street is mostly glass and concrete. Then you reach a squat, pale stone building with small, deep-set windows, older than everything around it by more than a century. This is the Commissariat Store. Convict labourers of the Moreton Bay penal settlement built it in 1829, and it is now a restored three-floor museum of the city's penal and colonial history.

It is one of only two convict-built structures still standing in Queensland. The other is the Old Windmill on Wickham Terrace. That makes this the closest you can get to the settlement that became Brisbane, without needing any imagination about what a building once looked like.

## What the walls tell you

Start outside. The lower walls are rough, irregular stone, laid by hand by men serving sentences. The building faces the Brisbane River because that is how supplies arrived. Goods came off boats and went straight into storage.

The "commissariat" was the settlement's supply department. Food rations, tools, clothing and stores for convicts and soldiers were kept and issued from here. Control the store and you controlled daily life at Moreton Bay, which explains the thick walls and narrow openings.

Look up and you will see the building is not all one age. The top floor was a later addition, built in the early twentieth century, so the lower and upper levels show two different eras of Brisbane construction stacked on top of each other.

## Three floors, one story

Inside, the museum is arranged over the three levels. The displays follow the arc from penal outpost to free settlement:

- **The penal years**: Moreton Bay opened in 1824 as a place of secondary punishment for convicts who reoffended in other colonies.
- **The commandant era**: the store went up during Captain Patrick Logan's command, remembered for its harshness.
- **The colonial town**: the settlement opened to free settlers in 1842, and Brisbane grew from there.

Expect a compact, text-and-object museum rather than a large interactive gallery. Allow 45 minutes to an hour if you read the panels. History enthusiasts can easily take longer. The building itself is the main exhibit, so pause at the stairs and window recesses as well as the cases.

## The weekday-only door

This is the timing quirk that catches visitors out. The museum opens Tuesday to Friday, 10am to 4pm, and is closed Saturday, Sunday and Monday. A weekend city break will miss it entirely unless you build in a weekday.

Treat 4pm as a hard stop and arrive by 3pm at the latest if you want time on all three floors. A mid-morning visit fits neatly before lunch in the CBD. The store is the home of the Royal Historical Society of Queensland, so check the society's website before you go for public holiday closures and current admission details.

## Getting to William Street

The address is 115 William St, at the river end of the CBD near North Quay. Most visitors arrive on foot from the city centre.

- **Ferry**: North Quay ferry terminal is a short walk away, served by Brisbane's river ferries.
- **On foot from South Bank**: cross Victoria Bridge from the Cultural Centre, then turn along the river towards William Street.
- **Train**: Central Station is a walk of roughly 15 minutes through the CBD.
- **Bus**: Queen Street bus station is close by in the Queen Street Mall precinct.

Parking in the CBD is limited and largely in paid commercial car parks. Public transport is the simpler choice.

## Visiting a volunteer-run museum

This is a small museum looked after by a historical society, and that shapes the visit. Staff at the front are there to help, and many visitors find that a question asked at the desk turns up more than the panels do.

- **Payment**: check the Royal Historical Society of Queensland website for current admission and whether card is accepted, rather than assuming.
- **Pace**: the stairs are part of the building's age. Take them steadily, and ask at the desk about access if stairs are a concern.
- **Groups**: school and tour groups may book weekday visits. If you arrive to a group, start on a different floor and let them move through.
- **Photos**: ask at the front before photographing displays, since policies vary in small collections.
- **Tipping**: none expected. Buying something small or making a donation, where offered, supports the society's work.

Because the museum stays under the radar compared with the galleries across the river, it is usually unhurried. That quiet is a large part of its appeal.

## Pairing it with the old city

The store makes a good starting point for a walk through Brisbane's older layers. Within easy reach on foot:

- **Queens Wharf precinct**: the redeveloped riverside area around the heritage Treasury Building and the Neville Bonner Bridge.
- **Queens Gardens**: a small park beside the old Treasury, with the Queen Victoria statue.
- **South Bank and the Cultural Centre**: across Victoria Bridge, home to the Queensland Museum, the Queensland Art Gallery and GOMA.
- **The Old Windmill**: on Wickham Terrace, the other surviving convict-built structure. Seeing both in one day makes the penal-era story concrete.

See the store first. Once you know what Moreton Bay was, the sandstone public buildings of the later colonial city make more sense.
