## Two nights in Bukit Jalil

Eric Chou brings his world tour to Kuala Lumpur for a weekend. The *Odyssey · Stars* shows run on **October 24 and 25, 2026**, at **Unifi Arena**. It is one of the city's big indoor concert venues, inside the Bukit Jalil National Sports Complex in the south of KL.

Chou is the Taiwanese singer-songwriter behind piano-led Mandopop ballads such as "How Have You Been?" and "The Chaos After You". Malaysia has a large Mandarin-speaking audience, so a crowd that sings along to every chorus is normal here. The tour organiser is the source for seating plans and ticket links.

## Four sides, no back wall

This arena show has no back of the room. The stage is built **360 degrees and four-sided**, standing in the arena floor rather than against one wall. The seating bowl wraps around all of it.

That changes how you pick a seat:

- **Side and end blocks** are not the compromise seats they are at a front-facing show. Each face of the stage plays to its own part of the bowl.
- **Upper tiers** look down onto the whole stage footprint. On a four-sided set, that view often shows the staging better than the floor does.
- **Floor sections** put you closest to the performer. Expect him to face you some of the time, not all of it, because he works every side.

If seeing his face matters most, the lower tiers on any side are a sensible middle ground. If the production is the draw, the higher rows show it whole.

## The arena itself

Unifi Arena opened as the Putra Indoor Stadium. It was built for the 1998 Commonwealth Games, which Kuala Lumpur hosted, and it has had several names since. Many locals and taxi drivers still know it by an older name, Axiata Arena, or simply as "the indoor stadium at Bukit Jalil".

It is a domed, oval sports arena with tiered seating all the way around the floor. That bowl shape suits a centre stage. Next door is the Bukit Jalil National Stadium, the country's main football ground. When both venues host events on the same night, the surrounding roads and the station fill fast.

## Getting to Unifi Arena

The rail line runs almost to the door. **Bukit Jalil LRT station** on the **Sri Petaling Line** sits across from the sports complex. From the station, a covered walkway and footpaths lead you to the arena in a few minutes.

From the city centre, most visitors ride the Sri Petaling Line south from stations such as Hang Tuah, Plaza Rakyat or Masjid Jamek. Change at **Hang Tuah** if you are coming on the Monorail from Bukit Bintang. The ride takes roughly half an hour from central KL.

Driving is the less relaxed option. The complex has large car parks, but leaving them after a sold-out show means a long, slow queue onto the highway. Grab, the ride-hailing app most people use in Malaysia, can drop you near the arena entrance.

## Getting home after the encore

How you leave matters as much as how you arrive. When the house lights come up, thousands of people head for one LRT station at the same moment. The platform queue at Bukit Jalil can stretch back along the walkway.

What concert regulars here usually do:

- **Pay for the LRT with a Touch 'n Go card or a contactless bank card.** The gates accept both, so you skip the token machines, which are where the queue jams after a show.
- **Wait out the surge.** Many fans linger by the arena or walk over to Pavilion Bukit Jalil, the large mall a short walk away, and travel once the platform has cleared.
- **Order a Grab away from the drop-off zone.** Surge pricing and gridlock peak at the arena kerb. Many people walk towards the mall or the main road and order their ride from there, which usually gets a driver through faster.
- **Keep the gear simple.** Fans bring lightsticks, banners and fan-made towels. Large bags slow down security screening at the doors.

Malaysian concert crowds are generally orderly and patient in queues. Singing along loudly is expected, but blocking the view of people behind you with raised phones for whole songs is not well received.

## Before and after: eating near Bukit Jalil

The sports complex has food stalls on show nights, but most people eat nearby. **Pavilion Bukit Jalil** is the obvious choice: a large mall with food courts, chain restaurants and cafés, walkable from the arena. It also gives you air conditioning while you wait for the crowds to thin.

A stop north on the same LRT line, **Sri Petaling** is a long-established, largely Chinese residential neighbourhood with streets of kopitiams and dai chow restaurants. It is a good place for a late plate of noodles, away from the arena traffic.

## Where fans stay

Bukit Jalil itself has few hotels close to the arena. Most out-of-town fans base themselves in the city. **Bukit Bintang** and the **KLCC** area are the usual picks, with easy rail links: the Monorail to Hang Tuah, then the Sri Petaling Line to the arena.

Staying on or near the Sri Petaling Line cuts out a change. That makes the ride home simpler at the end of a long night, especially for anyone attending both shows over the weekend.
