## A bookshop worth slowing down for

Hereford Street is a street people walk along on their way somewhere else. Scorpio Books is a good reason to stop. It is an independent shop selling books and cards, and the selection is chosen by people, not by a head office.

Inside, it works like a good independent should. The tables reward slow browsing and the shelves reward a second lap. The card racks are worth a look too, especially if you want something to post home that didn't come from an airport. Reviewers keep describing it the same way: comfortable, thoughtful, and very well rated for a shop this size.

## Finding 120 Hereford Street

The shop is at 120 Hereford Street, in the grid of the rebuilt central city. From Cathedral Square it's a few minutes' walk. Head along Hereford Street towards the river and look for the number.

Useful landmarks within easy walking distance:

- **Cathedral Square**, with Tūranga, the central library, on its edge
- **The Avon River (Ōtākaro)** and the Bridge of Remembrance off Cashel Street
- **Christchurch Art Gallery Te Puna o Waiwhetū** on Montreal Street
- **The Bus Interchange** on Lichfield Street, for most city services

The central city is flat and compact. Most visitors walk here or take the bus, and the shop fits easily into a loop of the square and the river.

## When to browse in peace

Timing changes the experience here. On weekdays it's usually quietest from 9am–10am, right after opening, and again from 4pm–6pm before the 6pm close. Those are the slots for a long, uninterrupted look at the shelves.

Weekends are different. Hours are shorter, 10am–5pm at the moment, and the shop is typically busiest between 12pm and 4pm. Arrive at 10am–11am, or come in the 4pm–5pm window. Try not to turn up mid-afternoon on a Saturday expecting room to linger.

## How to shop Scorpio's shelves

The best way to use an independent bookshop is to let it steer you. These habits usually work:

- **Ask at the counter.** Staff at shops like this tend to be readers first. Tell them the last book you loved and see what they suggest.
- **Look for New Zealand writing.** A local bookshop is where you find Aotearoa fiction, poetry and nonfiction you won't see at home. It also makes a light souvenir.
- **Check the cards.** They're part of the shop's identity, not an afterthought.
- **Pay by card.** Contactless and EFTPOS payment is the norm across New Zealand, and tipping isn't expected in shops.

If you're travelling with limited luggage space, ask about paperbacks or slimmer editions. Prices are on the books themselves, so you can browse without guessing.

## Building an afternoon around it

Plan on 30 minutes to an hour, longer if you're the type who reads first chapters standing up. Then take your new book somewhere to read it. The riverbank along the Avon is a few blocks away, and the Botanic Gardens are a longer, flat walk west past the Arts Centre.

Rainy day? Pair the shop with Christchurch Art Gallery or Tūranga. Both are close enough that you can keep the day indoors and walk between them in minutes. With a free morning on a weekday, open at 9am here and move on to the gallery after. You'll have beaten the busier part of the day at both.
