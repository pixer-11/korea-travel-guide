## Inside Jakarta International Stadium

JIS opened in 2022 and is the home ground of football club Persija Jakarta. It holds around 82,000 people and has a retractable roof, one of the few stadiums in Southeast Asia built with one. The stands rise steeply on all four sides, so even the upper tiers sit closer to the pitch than in an older athletics bowl.

The stadium sits in Papanggo, Tanjung Priok, a long way from the hotel districts of Sudirman, Thamrin and Kemang. That distance shapes the whole evening. The trip from central Jakarta takes at least an hour in normal traffic, and longer on a Saturday evening. October 10, 2026 fell on a Saturday.

## Getting to Tanjung Priok and back

There are three realistic ways to reach JIS:

- **KRL Commuter Line:** trains run to Tanjung Priok station. From there the trip continues by bus, ride-hail or on foot.
- **TransJakarta:** the city's bus network runs routes toward JIS. Fares are paid with a tap-on card or app, not cash.
- **Ride-hail:** Gojek and Grab cars and motorbike taxis work well on the way in. The return trip is where it gets difficult.

After a full stadium show, thousands of people request rides from the same few streets at once. Prices surge, drivers cancel, and roads lock up. Walking a few blocks away from the stadium before requesting a ride usually gets people picked up faster. Groups that agree a meeting point in advance cope better too, because mobile data slows down when the whole crowd is posting videos at once.

## Buying tickets without getting burned

Resale offers on social media are the most common way fans in Indonesia lose money on big concerts. Tickets bought outside the promoter's own sales channels carry the highest risk of being fake or already used.

Large stadium shows in Indonesia often require a name and ID on each ticket. They also commonly use an exchange step, where an e-ticket is swapped for a wristband ahead of entry. For visitors from abroad, the passport is the usual ID, and its details generally need to match the booking exactly.

## Show-night rules at JIS

A few local habits make the night smoother.

- **Digital payment:** QRIS codes, GoPay, OVO and cards are standard at food stalls and merch stands across Jakarta. Some rupiah in small notes is a useful backup in case signal drops.
- **Heat and rain:** October usually marks the start of Jakarta's rainy season. Afternoons are hot and humid, and evening downpours are common. A light poncho is easier to manage in a crowd than an umbrella, and some stadiums ban umbrellas outright.
- **Bag policy:** stadium security in Indonesia searches bags at the gate, and stadiums keep lists of banned items.
- **Eating:** queues for food inside a full stadium are long. A proper meal near the hotel beats relying on whatever stays stocked late in the night.

Merch lines tend to be shortest once the opening act is on stage, while most people are still finding their seats.

## Building a trip around the concert

Soekarno-Hatta International Airport lies west of the city. The airport rail link runs to central Jakarta, and the stadium is further north from there. Staying in North Jakarta, around Kelapa Gading or Ancol, cuts the post-show journey a lot compared with the southern business districts.

Ancol, on the waterfront east of JIS, has beaches, the Dunia Fantasi theme park and seafood restaurants for the day before or after. Kota Tua, the old Dutch colonial quarter with Fatahillah Square and the Jakarta History Museum, is a short ride southwest. Late-night traffic out of Tanjung Priok means few people get to bed early after a stadium show, so a slow day afterwards suits most visitors.
