## A canyon right off the beach

Most whale-watching needs a long trip out to deep water. In Kaikōura it doesn't. The Kaikōura Canyon drops to depths of more than a kilometre just a short ride from shore. That cold, food-rich water holds male sperm whales all year, and they're the reason this operation exists.

Whale Watch Kaikoura is owned by Ngāti Kurī, the local Māori hapū, and has run since 1987. The skipper finds whales by listening with a hydrophone. A sperm whale lies at the surface for several minutes, breathing in visible blows, then lifts its tail flukes and dives. That fluke-up is the shot everyone waits for, so keep your camera ready once the engines go quiet.

## What else surfaces

Sperm whales are the sure part of the trip. Everything else depends on the season and on luck:

- **Dusky dolphins**: often in large, acrobatic pods
- **New Zealand fur seals**: on the rocks and swimming offshore
- **Humpback whales**: mostly during their winter migration, around June and July
- **Orca**: occasional visitors through the year
- **Albatross and petrels**: following the boat over the canyon

Plan on roughly three and a half hours for the whole trip, from check-in at the station to coming back ashore.

## Booking, boarding and the swell

The base is the old railway station building on Whaleway Station Road, so if you come on the Coastal Pacific train you step off almost at the door. At the moment the office is open 8:30am–5pm most days and closes at 4:30pm on Mondays. Sailing times change with the season, so check the official site for this month's schedule.

- **Book ahead** in summer and school holidays. Departures fill up.
- **Check in early.** Your confirmation gives the exact time; latecomers can lose their seats.
- **Stay seated** in the cabin while the boat is moving. The crew opens the outside decks once the boat stops near a whale.
- **Treat seasickness before you board.** Tablets work best taken in advance, and the canyon swell can be lumpy even on calm-looking days.
- **Layer up.** It's cold on the open water in any month.

Sea conditions decide everything. Trips get cancelled, sometimes on the morning itself. The safest plan is to book your first full day in town, which leaves the next day as a backup. The refund terms for weather cancellations, and for trips where no whale is found, are on the official booking page. Read them before you pay.

## After you're back on land

The Kaikōura Peninsula Walkway starts at Point Kean, a short drive south along the Esplanade. Fur seals often lie on the rocks and the car park edge there. Give them at least 20 metres of space. They move faster than you'd expect.

On the way, Fyffe House is Kaikōura's oldest surviving building, standing on whalebone piles from the town's whaling days. The name Kaikōura roughly means "meal of crayfish". Roadside seafood stalls along State Highway 1 sell crayfish to match, and Ohau Point's seal colony is about 25 minutes north.
