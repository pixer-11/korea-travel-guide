Taipei's Keelung River runs between tall concrete flood walls, and the city keeps a whole second landscape on the river side of them. For one Saturday in November, that flat strip of grass at Dajia Riverside Park turns into a festival ground. ULTRA Taiwan 2026 is the sixth edition of the event, and it runs over a single day.

## What's on the riverbank

There are two stages and one day. The festival is built around the **Main Stage**, ULTRA's signature big-room setup, and the **RESISTANCE Stage**, the brand's stage for techno and underground sounds.

That split shapes your day more than any set time. Main Stage crowds come for the large-format production and the singalong drops. RESISTANCE draws people who want longer, darker sets and less distance between them and the booth.

Because everything happens in one day, there is no second chance to catch an act you missed. Confirm the lineup and running order on the official ULTRA Taiwan site, and plan your stage moves around the two or three sets you would hate to miss.

## Dates and tickets for the 2026 edition

The announced date is **Saturday, November 14, 2026**. The venue is Dajia Riverside Park (大佳河濱公園) in Taipei.

Confirm timing and tickets on the official site. Ticket tiers, gate times and age rules are the details that change between editions. Buy only through the official channel or its named ticketing partner, because resold wristbands for big festivals are a common scam.

Keep in mind that the page you are reading stays online after the event. If you arrive here after November 2026, check the official site for the next edition's details.

## Through the flood gate

This is the part of Taipei most visitors never see. The city's riverside parks sit outside a continuous line of levees. You reach them on foot through **water gates (水門)**, which are openings cut through the wall, or by ramps over it.

Dajia is one of the larger of these parks. It is mostly open lawn, cycle paths and low riverside planting, with almost no permanent shade. That matters for a festival day:

- The ground is grass and paving at river level, so expect mud if it has rained.
- There is little natural shelter from wind off the water.
- Entry and exit funnel through a limited number of gates, so leaving takes time.

On the way out, thousands of people squeeze through those same gaps in the wall. Leaving a few minutes before the final set ends, or lingering a while after, makes the walk back to the MRT far calmer.

## Getting to Dajia Riverside Park

The park sits in Zhongshan District, near Dazhi Bridge. Across the river to the north is the Dazhi neighbourhood, home to the Miramar Entertainment Park Ferris wheel. Upstream to the west, the red-roofed Grand Hotel stands on its hillside.

The nearest metro stops are on the **Wenhu Line (brown line)**:

- **Dazhi Station**, the closest stop on the north bank, a walk over the bridge to the park.
- **Jiannan Road Station**, by the Miramar Ferris wheel, a slightly longer walk.

Expect a walk of around 15 to 20 minutes from either station, plus queue time at the gates. The Wenhu Line runs on elevated tracks with small driverless trains. They fill fast after a large event, so be ready for a wait on the platform.

Taxis and ride-hailing apps work in Taipei, but roads near the park clog at closing. Walking to a main road further from the gates usually gets you a car sooner.

## Dressing for a November night by the river

Mid-November in Taipei is mild by day and cooler after dark. It is also the season of the northeast monsoon, which often brings grey skies and light drizzle to the north of the island.

On an open riverbank, that means planning for both:

- A light waterproof layer or a poncho
- Shoes you can stand in for hours on wet grass
- A thin layer for after sunset, when the air off the river cools
- A portable battery, since you will lean on your phone for maps and the ride home

Check the official site's list of permitted items before packing. Festivals usually restrict bags, umbrellas and outside drinks, and those rules can change between editions.

## Doing festival day the Taipei way

Taipei makes the logistics of a big day out easy if you use what locals use.

- **Get an EasyCard (悠遊卡).** Tap it on the MRT and buses, and use it at convenience stores. Top it up before the event so you skip ticket machines when everyone leaves.
- **Use the convenience stores.** 7-Eleven and FamilyMart branches across Zhongshan and Dazhi sell water, ponchos, snacks and phone chargers. Stock up before you go through the gates.
- **Queue in line.** Taipei commuters stand in marked lines on MRT platforms and keep to the right on escalators. That holds after a festival too, and people notice when visitors push in.
- **Keep the train quiet.** Eating and drinking are banned inside MRT stations and trains, and fines are enforced. Finish your drink before you tap in.
- **Plan the last train.** Check the Wenhu Line's last departures from Dazhi and Jiannan Road on the Taipei Metro site. Decide in advance: either leave in time for the train, or plan on a taxi.

Payment in Taipei is mixed. Cards and mobile wallets are common, but small vendors often take cash only. Tipping is not expected at food stalls or in taxis.

## Before and after the music

The area around the park is easy to fill a day with. Linjiang Street Night Market is in Da'an, so it is too far for a quick bite. Dazhi itself has plenty of casual eateries and convenience stores within walking distance of the stations.

If you arrive early, the Miramar Ferris wheel at Jiannan Road gives you a view over the river and the park before the crowds build. Further west, Yuanshan has the Taipei Fine Arts Museum and the Expo Park, a short MRT ride away. Either makes a gentle way to spend the morning before a long night on your feet.

The day after, take it slowly. A late breakfast of soy milk and egg crepe (蛋餅) at a neighbourhood breakfast shop is what plenty of Taipei residents would choose after a big night out.
