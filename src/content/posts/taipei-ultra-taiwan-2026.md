Taipei's Keelung River runs between tall concrete flood walls, and the city keeps a whole second landscape on the river side of them. For one Saturday in November, that flat strip of grass at Dajia Riverside Park was set to become a festival ground. ULTRA Taiwan 2026 was billed as the sixth edition of the event, on a single day.

## What's on the riverbank

Organisers announced two stages and one day. The festival was built around the **Main Stage**, ULTRA's signature big-room setup, and the **RESISTANCE Stage**, the brand's stage for techno and underground sounds.

At ULTRA events, that split matters more than any set time. Main Stage crowds come for the large-format production and the singalong drops. RESISTANCE draws people who want longer, darker sets and less distance between them and the booth.

One-day festivals leave no second chance to catch a missed act, so the running order carries more weight than it does at a weekend event. Most people build their day around the two or three sets they care about most.

## Dates and tickets for the 2026 edition

The festival was set for **Saturday, November 14, 2026**. The venue, Dajia Riverside Park (大佳河濱公園), is in Taipei.

Ticket tiers, gate times and age rules are the details that usually change between editions. Big festivals sell through an official channel or a named ticketing partner, and resold wristbands are a common scam.

## Through the flood gate

This is the part of Taipei most visitors never see. The city's riverside parks sit outside a continuous line of levees. You reach them on foot through **water gates (水門)**, which are openings cut through the wall, or by ramps over it.

Dajia is one of the larger of these parks. It is mostly open lawn, cycle paths and low riverside planting, with almost no permanent shade. That matters for a festival day:

- The ground is grass and paving at river level, and it turns muddy after rain.
- There is little natural shelter from wind off the water.
- Entry and exit funnel through a limited number of gates, so leaving takes time.

At big riverside events, crowds leave through those same gaps in the wall. The walk back to the MRT is calmest a few minutes before the final set ends, or a while after it.

## Getting to Dajia Riverside Park

The park sits in Zhongshan District, near Dazhi Bridge. Across the river to the north is the Dazhi neighbourhood, home to the Miramar Entertainment Park Ferris wheel. Upstream to the west, the red-roofed Grand Hotel stands on its hillside.

The nearest metro stops are on the **Wenhu Line (brown line)**:

- **Dazhi Station**, the closest stop on the north bank, a walk over the bridge to the park.
- **Jiannan Road Station**, by the Miramar Ferris wheel, a slightly longer walk.

The walk from either station takes around 15 to 20 minutes, plus queue time at the gates on event days. The Wenhu Line runs on elevated tracks with small driverless trains. They fill fast after a large event, and waits on the platform are common.

Taxis and ride-hailing apps work in Taipei, but roads near the park clog at closing. A main road further from the gates usually gets you a car sooner.

## Dressing for a November night by the river

Mid-November in Taipei is mild by day and cooler after dark. It is also the season of the northeast monsoon, which often brings grey skies and light drizzle to the north of the island.

On an open riverbank, the useful kit covers both:

- A light waterproof layer or a poncho
- Shoes you can stand in for hours on wet grass
- A thin layer for after sunset, when the air off the river cools
- A portable battery, since a phone carries the maps and the ride home

Festivals usually restrict bags, umbrellas and outside drinks, and those rules can change between editions.

## Doing festival day the Taipei way

Taipei makes the logistics of a big day out easy if you use what locals use.

- **The EasyCard (悠遊卡).** It works on the MRT and buses and at convenience stores. A topped-up card avoids the ticket machines when a big crowd leaves at once.
- **Convenience stores.** 7-Eleven and FamilyMart branches across Zhongshan and Dazhi sell water, ponchos, snacks and phone chargers.
- **Queueing.** Taipei commuters stand in marked lines on MRT platforms and keep to the right on escalators. That holds after a festival too, and people notice when visitors push in.
- **Quiet trains.** Eating and drinking are banned inside MRT stations and trains, and fines are enforced.
- **The last train.** The Wenhu Line does not run all night, and Taipei Metro publishes the last departures from Dazhi and Jiannan Road. After those, a taxi is the main way home.

Payment in Taipei is mixed. Cards and mobile wallets are common, but small vendors often take cash only. Tipping is not expected at food stalls or in taxis.

## Before and after the music

The area around the park is easy to fill a day with. Linjiang Street Night Market is in Da'an, so it is too far for a quick bite. Dazhi itself has plenty of casual eateries and convenience stores within walking distance of the stations.

Earlier in the day, the Miramar Ferris wheel at Jiannan Road gives a view over the river and the park. Further west, Yuanshan has the Taipei Fine Arts Museum and the Expo Park, a short MRT ride away. Either makes a gentle way to spend a morning ahead of a long night on your feet.

The day after, take it slowly. A late breakfast of soy milk and egg crepe (蛋餅) at a neighbourhood breakfast shop is what plenty of Taipei residents would choose after a big night out.
