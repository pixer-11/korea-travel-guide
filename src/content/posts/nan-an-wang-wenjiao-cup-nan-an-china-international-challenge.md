## Six days of shuttles in southern Fujian

Badminton is a quiet sport until it isn't. Inside the hall you hear the squeak of court shoes, then a smash that sounds like a slammed door. Then comes the umpire's call in English, the working language of every BWF event.

The WANG WENJIAO CUP Nan'an China International Challenge runs from Tuesday 3 November to Sunday 8 November 2026. Badminton Asia and the Badminton World Federation (BWF) sanction it. That puts it on the same international circuit as the big-name Opens, a few tiers below them.

## What an International Challenge actually is

International Challenge events sit on the BWF's development tier. That matters for what you watch. The draws are often full of young national-squad players, juniors moving up, and seasoned pros chasing ranking points between larger tournaments.

Five disciplines run through the week:

- Men's singles
- Women's singles
- Men's doubles
- Women's doubles
- Mixed doubles

The usual shape of a six-day event like this is busy early rounds, with several courts in play at once. The field then narrows day by day until a single show court hosts the finals. Midweek you can drift between courts and see dozens of matches. Sunday is the day for one match at a time, with the hall focused on it.

## Nan'an, and how people get there

Nan'an is a county-level city within Quanzhou prefecture, on Fujian's southeast coast. It sits between Quanzhou city and Xiamen, and most international visitors come in through one of those two.

Typical routes:

- **By air:** Quanzhou Jinjiang International Airport is the nearest. Xiamen Gaoqi International Airport has far more international flights.
- **By rail:** high-speed trains on the Fuzhou–Xiamen corridor serve Quanzhou and Xiamen, and taxis or ride-hailing cover the last stretch.
- **Where people stay:** visiting fans usually split between hotels in Nan'an itself and central Quanzhou. Quanzhou has a wider choice and is a short drive away.

## Courtside manners and paying your way

Badminton halls run on a few unwritten rules. Spectators stay put during rallies and move only between points or at the interval. Flash photography is out, because a burst of light can cost a player a shuttle. Halls are often kept warm with little airflow, since drafts push the shuttle off line. Locals dress in light layers for that reason.

Payment in mainland China is overwhelmingly mobile. Alipay and WeChat Pay handle food stalls, taxis and most hotels. Both apps let foreign visitors link an international card. Ride-hailing through DiDi, which runs inside Alipay, is the usual way to get between hotel and hall.

A little Mandarin goes a long way. Fans cheer with *jiāyóu* (加油), literally "add oil", for whichever player they back. Nan'an and Quanzhou also speak Minnan (Hokkien) on the street, the same language family heard in Taiwan and among Southeast Asian Chinese communities.

## Between sessions: Nan'an and Quanzhou

Nan'an has deep roots overseas. It is the ancestral hometown of a large share of Southeast Asia's Hokkien diaspora. It is also the family home of Zheng Chenggong, better known as Koxinga, and Shijing town has a memorial to him. Shuitou town is one of China's biggest centres for the stone trade, with slab yards stretching along the roads.

Quanzhou is the bigger draw for a free afternoon. It is a UNESCO World Heritage city, listed as an emporium of the Song and Yuan maritime world. Visitors usually head for:

- **Kaiyuan Temple**, with its pair of stone pagodas
- **West Street**, the old lane running beside the temple
- **Qingyuan Mountain**, with its huge stone statue of Laozi

## Eating like a Minnan local

Food here is Minnan cooking: seafood, broths and snacks rather than heavy sauces. Morning or late-night bowls of *miànxiàn hú*, a thick noodle-thread soup, are a Quanzhou staple. Adventurous eaters go looking for *tǔsǔn dòng*, a cold jelly set from sea worms. Oyster omelette turns up at nearly every snack street.

Eating around match times is easy. Breaks between sessions are when fans spill out to the nearest noodle shop, then drift back in for the evening matches.
