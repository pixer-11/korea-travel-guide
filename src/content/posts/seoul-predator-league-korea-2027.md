## Getting Around on Match Days

The subway is the easy answer for almost any Seoul venue. Trains are frequent, stations have English signs, and most large halls are a short walk from an exit.

Visitors usually sort a few things out on arrival:

- **T-money card**: a rechargeable transit card sold at convenience stores and airport counters, used for subway, bus and many taxis

- **Kakao T**: the main app for hailing a taxi, useful after late matches

## Cheering in a Korean Esports Hall

Korean esports crowds are organised and loud. Fans of each team often bring handmade signs, team colours and printed banners, and chants roll through the stands together rather than in scattered bursts.

A few habits keep you in step with the room:

- **Signs stay at chest height** so the row behind can still see the screen
- **Seats are respected**: people stand for big plays and then sit back down
- **Payment is by card**: Korean venues and food stalls take cards almost everywhere, and mobile pay is common
- **Tipping is not expected**, at the venue or anywhere else in Seoul
- **Rubbish goes home with you or into marked bins**: public bins are scarce in Seoul and crowds tend to leave seats clean

A little Korean goes a long way at the merch table. *Annyeonghaseyo* (hello) and *gamsahamnida* (thank you) are enough. Staff at events like this commonly point to a QR code or a signboard for entry, so having a phone with data matters more than paper.

## Late-November Seoul

By the third week of November, Seoul has moved from autumn into early winter. Daytime is crisp. Evenings can drop close to freezing, and wind channels down the wide boulevards.

Visitors usually travel with:

- A warm coat that is easy to carry once you are indoors, since venues are well heated
- Layers rather than one heavy jumper
- A power bank, because ticket QR codes, maps and transit apps all run on your phone

Most of the street ginkgo leaves have dropped by then, but palace grounds such as Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung can still hold late colour. Daylight is short, and darkness falls in the late afternoon.

## Making a Weekend of It

Seoul has more permanent esports venues than any other city, and a Predator League trip slots neatly beside them.

- **LoL Park**: Riot's dedicated League of Legends arena inside the Gran Seoul building in Jongno, by Jonggak Station (Line 1). It hosts LCK matches and is the best-known esports venue in the city.
- **Yongsan Electronics Market**: a sprawl of shops selling components, peripherals and laptops beside Yongsan Station (Line 1). It is where Seoul's PC builders have shopped for decades.
- **Hongdae PC bangs**: the student streets around Hongik University Station (Line 2) are packed with PC bangs. Many have high-refresh monitors and full gaming setups, and you pay by the hour.
- **Gangnam and COEX**: the COEX Mall in Samseong (Line 2) has the Starfield Library, plenty of food courts, and a long run of tech and gaming retail.

PC bangs work simply. You get a seat number at the front desk or a kiosk, log in, and order instant ramyeon or snacks to your desk from an on-screen menu. Many visitors who come for a tournament spend at least one late night this way, playing the same games they watched on stage that afternoon.
