## Four days a week on Allison Street

Allison Street is not a shopping street in the city-centre sense. It runs through Govanhill between rows of four-storey sandstone tenements, with grocers, bakeries and takeaways at ground level and flats stacked above. Category Is Books sits at number 34, a shopfront among the everyday businesses of the street.

That setting is the point. You aren't stepping off Buchanan Street into a chain store. You're in one of Scotland's most mixed neighbourhoods, where signs on the same block can be in several languages, and a small independent bookshop fits that pattern rather than standing apart from it.

Currently the door opens only Thursday to Sunday, 11am–5pm. Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday it is closed. If your Glasgow trip runs midweek only, this one won't work, so plan around it.

## What "Category is" means

The name is a nod to ballroom culture. At drag balls, an MC calls out "Category is…" before announcing the theme the walkers compete in. The documentary *Paris Is Burning* and later *Pose* and *RuPaul's Drag Race* brought the phrase to a wider audience.

Naming a bookshop with it tells you what you're walking into: an LGBTQIA+ bookshop. The focus is queer writing and queer readers, which is a narrower and more deliberate range than a general bookseller's shelf of "LGBT interest" titles.

That narrow focus is why people cross the city for it, and why reviewers keep giving it top marks. It's a small, under-the-radar business, not a landmark. Its reputation comes from what is on the shelves and how the place treats the people browsing them.

## Browsing a shop with one subject

A single-subject bookshop rewards slow browsing in a way a big chain doesn't. Everything on the shelves has been chosen for this shop, so you'll find titles you would have to search for elsewhere sitting face-out next to each other.

Go in with a loose idea rather than a list. Some useful starting points:

- **Fiction by queer authors**, including writers you've not met in mainstream shops
- **Non-fiction** on queer history, gender and identity
- **Scottish and Glasgow writers**, worth asking about specifically since you're here
- **Something for a younger reader**, if you're shopping for a teenager or a child

Allow 30 minutes to an hour. It's a small space, but this is a shop where people tend to read first pages before deciding.

## Getting to Govanhill

The simplest route from the centre is by train. Services on the Cathcart Circle leave from Glasgow Central and reach **Queens Park station** in about ten minutes. From there it is a short walk east into Govanhill and onto Allison Street.

Buses running south along Victoria Road also drop you within a few minutes' walk. Walking from the city centre is possible too: you cross the Clyde and head south through the Gorbals. It takes the best part of an hour and mostly passes housing and main roads.

If you're driving, the surrounding streets are residential and tightly parked. Public transport is the less stressful option.

## Asking, paying and ordering in

Talk to whoever is behind the counter. Recommendations are the main thing a specialist shop does better than an algorithm, so say what you last read and loved and let them work from there. If they don't stock a title, ask whether they can order it in; independent bookshops usually can.

A few habits that go down well:

- **Pay by card or phone.** Contactless is normal across UK shops, though carrying a little cash does no harm.
- **Ask before taking photos** of the interior or of other customers. For some people a queer bookshop is a place they'd rather not be pictured in.
- **Use people's names and pronouns as given.** Many visitors will share theirs, and it's basic courtesy here.
- **Buy something if you browsed for an hour.** Small shops survive on sales, not footfall.

A Saturday or Sunday afternoon is the likeliest time to find the shop lively, simply because those are the free days for most people. If you want a quieter browse and more of the bookseller's attention, a Thursday or Friday visit, when most locals are at work, makes sense.

## The Thursday-to-Sunday window

The short week shapes how you plan. The shop's four open days line up neatly with a weekend break, so a Friday arrival or a Sunday before an evening train both work.

Don't leave it to 4:45pm. With a 5pm close and a shop that invites slow browsing, arriving by mid-afternoon gives you room. Opening hours for small independents also change around holidays and events, so check the shop's own channels before making a special trip.

## Making an afternoon of the Southside

Category Is Books works best as one stop in a wider afternoon south of the river. Within walking distance:

- **Queen's Park**, a large Victorian park with a flagpole hill. On clear days you get one of the best views back across the city.
- **Govanhill Baths** on Calder Street, the Edwardian swimming baths that residents campaigned to save after closure. It is now a community project.
- **Allison Street and Victoria Road** for food: grocers, bakeries, cafés and takeaways that reflect the neighbourhood's South Asian, Eastern European and Roma communities.
- **Shawlands**, a walk or short bus ride west, for more cafés and bars along Kilmarnock Road.

Buy your book, find a café and read the first chapter before heading back to Central. It's a good use of a Glasgow afternoon.
