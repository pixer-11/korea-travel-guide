## Sunday Only, Saunders Street

The calendar matters here. Stockbridge Market trades once a week, on Sunday from 10am to 4pm, and the site on Saunders Street stands empty from Monday to Saturday. If you plan a Saturday for it, you will find nothing there.

It is an outdoor market of covered stalls. Producers selling things to take home share the space with street-food traders cooking dishes from around the world. The format is simple: walk the rows, taste where you can, buy lunch, and carry the rest home in a bag.

It is well known and consistently well rated, and plenty of people already know about it. On a dry Sunday you will be sharing the aisles with families, dog walkers and visitors who planned their weekend around it.

## Reading the Stalls

Two kinds of trader make up the market. The editorial line sums it up: artisanal goods from local producers, plus stalls for global street fare. Plan your loop with both in mind.

On the producer side, look for things that travel well or that you can eat that evening:

- Breads and baked goods for the next morning
- Scottish cheeses and cured goods
- Preserves, chutneys and small-batch condiments
- Seasonal fruit and vegetables

The street-food stalls are the lunch half. Cuisines rotate as traders change, so do one slow lap before you commit to anything. Ordering from two stalls and sharing usually beats one big plate.

Stall line-ups change from week to week, so treat any name you read online as a lead, not a promise.

## Getting Down to Stockbridge

Stockbridge is a neighbourhood just north of the New Town. Saunders Street sits close to the bridge over the Water of Leith where Deanhaugh Street crosses it. The postcode for maps is EH3 6TQ.

From Princes Street it is roughly a 15 to 20 minute walk. You head downhill through the New Town via Frederick Street and Howe Street, or via Dundas Street. The walk back is the reverse, all uphill, which is worth knowing if you are carrying a heavy bag of shopping.

There is no tram stop nearby. Several Lothian Buses routes pass through Stockbridge along Raeburn Place and Deanhaugh Street. Check the Lothian journey planner for current Sunday timings. Street parking around the Stockbridge Colonies and residential streets is tight on weekends, so walking or the bus is the calmer choice.

## Shopping the Stalls Without the Crush

How you move through the stalls shapes the visit. Some habits help:

- **Arrive near the 10am opening** if you want first pick of bread and baked goods. Popular items can sell out later in the day, and the lunch crowd builds once street-food stalls get going.
- **Carry a reusable bag.** You will be juggling food and purchases, and many traders prefer you bring your own.
- **Pay by card or phone, but keep a little cash.** Contactless is normal at UK markets. A smaller trader may still prefer cash, and prices are shown on each stall.
- **Ask before you taste.** Producers often have samples out. Ask before you pick, and never double-dip.
- **Queue at the stall, not in the aisle.** Paths between stalls are narrow, so step to the side of the stall to wait. That keeps the walkway clear for buggies and dogs, and you will see plenty of both.
- **Eat standing or move on.** Seating is limited. Most people take food a few minutes away to the riverside instead of staying near the stalls.

Edinburgh weather is a fact of life here. It is an open-air market, so a waterproof layer will serve you better than an umbrella in the narrow rows.

## Lunch on the Water of Leith

The river is the market's best dining room. The Water of Leith runs right beside Stockbridge, and its walkway is the obvious place to take a street-food lunch.

Head upstream (west) and the path brings you to St Bernard's Well, a small classical temple over an old mineral spring. Keep going and you reach Dean Village. Its old mill buildings and the high Dean Bridge overhead make it one of the city's most photographed corners. The full stroll takes about 20 to 30 minutes at an easy pace.

Downstream, the walkway carries on towards Canonmills and eventually Leith. That direction suits a longer Sunday walk.

## The Rest of a Stockbridge Sunday

The market is the anchor, not the whole day. Stockbridge is compact, and much of it fits around a market morning.

- **Raeburn Place and St Stephen Street:** independent shops, cafés and the neighbourhood's well-known run of charity shops.
- **The Stockbridge Colonies:** parallel rows of 19th-century workers' housing beside the river, quiet residential streets to walk respectfully.
- **Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh:** about a 10 to 15 minute walk north via Inverleith Row. It is a good afternoon once you have eaten.
- **Inverleith Park:** open grass with views back to the city skyline, handy for a picnic of what you bought.

Allow 45 minutes to an hour for the market itself. If you add the river walk to Dean Village and a look round the shops, you have filled most of a Sunday.

## When to Go, and When to Skip It

Timing comes down to the day of the week. Sunday is the only option, between 10am and 4pm. Early arrival suits shoppers after baked goods. Late morning to early afternoon suits anyone who mostly wants lunch, since all the hot-food stalls will be cooking.

Late in the session, a few stalls may start packing down or run short on stock. Do not count on a full spread if you turn up close to 4pm.

Summer Sundays bring longer light and more visitors in town, especially during the August festivals. In colder months the market is shorter on daylight. It is still a good reason to get out to Stockbridge and finish somewhere warm on Raeburn Place.
