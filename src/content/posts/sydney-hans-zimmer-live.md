## Sydney gets the final night

Sydney was the last stop. Hans Zimmer's orchestral live show was set for five Australian cities in October 2026, with Perth, Adelaide, Brisbane and Melbourne first and the finale at Qudos Bank Arena on 28 October.

That made Sydney the last date on the Australian run. For visitors from cities the tour did not include, it was the final chance to see the show in the country.

## What a Zimmer arena show is like

This is film music played as a rock concert. Zimmer's catalogue covers decades of soundtracks: *The Lion King*, *Gladiator*, *Pirates of the Caribbean*, *The Dark Knight*, *Inception*, *Interstellar* and *Dune*. His touring show is built around those scores and played live on stage.

His shows pair orchestral players with amplified instruments, with lighting rigged at arena scale rather than concert-hall scale. The low end of scores like *Inception* and *Dune* fills a room this size in a way headphones never will.

## Inside Qudos Bank Arena

The arena is at Sydney Olympic Park in Homebush, about 15 kilometres west of the CBD. It opened in 1999 as the Sydney SuperDome, built for the 2000 Olympic Games, and later traded as Allphones Arena. It is now Sydney's biggest indoor arena for touring concerts.

The floor sits in a wide bowl, with tiered seating climbing on three sides toward the roof. For a show this cinematic, end-on seats in the lower tier usually give the best view of the whole stage and its lighting. Floor seats are closer, but the screens and rig read better from a little height.

Accor Stadium, the former Olympic stadium, stands a short walk away across Olympic Boulevard. On a night when both venues host events, the precinct gets crowded fast.

## Getting to Olympic Park

The train is the easiest way in. Olympic Park station sits a few minutes' walk from the arena doors, and the walk is signposted through the plaza. Regular trains reach it on the T7 Olympic Park Line, a shuttle from Lidcombe. On major event nights, Sydney Trains often runs extra direct services.

Other options:

- **Ferry:** Parramatta River services stop at Sydney Olympic Park wharf, with a bus link up to the precinct.
- **Car:** Sydney Olympic Park has multi-storey car parks, but leaving after a show is slow.
- **Rideshare:** Pick-ups use the signed zones, not the kerb outside the arena.

## Leaving after the final cue

Most of the evening's stress comes at the end. Thousands of people leave together, and Olympic Park station handles them with controlled queues. Staff hold the crowd in pens on the approach and release groups onto the platform as trains arrive. The line moves steadily along the barriers, and pushing toward the front gains nothing.

A few things make it easier:

- **Opal or a contactless card:** Sydney commuters tap on at the gates, and having a card in hand speeds up the queue for everyone.
- **Food:** Precinct food outlets and arena concessions get busy in the hour before doors.
- **Bags:** Arenas restrict bag sizes, and cloakroom queues build quickly before a show.
- **The last piece:** Zimmer's shows tend to build to the end. Leaving early to beat the train crowd usually means missing the loudest part of the night.

When the train queue is long, twenty minutes at a seat or in the plaza makes a difference. The pens clear surprisingly fast once the first few trains have gone.
