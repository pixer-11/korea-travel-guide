## Sydney gets the final night

The tour ends here. Hans Zimmer brings his orchestral live show to Australia in October 2026 for five cities. It plays Perth, Adelaide, Brisbane and Melbourne first, then finishes at Qudos Bank Arena on 28 October.

That makes Sydney the last date on the Australian run. If the other cities don't suit your travel, this is the date to plan around. Confirm the date, venue and tickets on the official site before you book flights or a hotel.

## What a Zimmer arena show is like

This is film music played as a rock concert. Zimmer's catalogue covers decades of soundtracks: *The Lion King*, *Gladiator*, *Pirates of the Caribbean*, *The Dark Knight*, *Inception*, *Interstellar* and *Dune*. His touring show is built around those scores and played live on stage.

Expect orchestral players alongside amplified instruments, with lighting rigged at arena scale rather than concert-hall scale. The low end of scores like *Inception* and *Dune* fills a room this size in a way your headphones never will.

The setlist and performer lineup are not part of the announced facts. Check the official tour page for who is on stage with him.

## Inside Qudos Bank Arena

You'll find the arena at Sydney Olympic Park in Homebush, about 15 kilometres west of the CBD. It opened in 1999 as the Sydney SuperDome, built for the 2000 Olympic Games, and later traded as Allphones Arena. It is now Sydney's biggest indoor arena for touring concerts.

The floor sits in a wide bowl, with tiered seating climbing on three sides toward the roof. For a show this cinematic, end-on seats in the lower tier usually give you the best view of the whole stage and its lighting. Floor seats put you closer, but the screens and rig read better from a little height.

Accor Stadium, the former Olympic stadium, stands a short walk away across Olympic Boulevard. On a night when both venues host events, the precinct gets crowded fast.

## Getting to Olympic Park

Take the train. Olympic Park station sits a few minutes' walk from the arena doors, and the walk is signposted through the plaza. Regular trains reach it on the T7 Olympic Park Line, a shuttle from Lidcombe. On major event nights, Sydney Trains often runs extra direct services, so check the Transport for NSW trip planner for that evening.

Other options:

- **Ferry:** Parramatta River services stop at Sydney Olympic Park wharf, with a bus link up to the precinct.
- **Car:** Sydney Olympic Park has multi-storey car parks, but leaving after the show is slow. Check the precinct's official site for event parking arrangements.
- **Rideshare:** Use the signed pick-up zones, not the kerb outside the arena.

## Leaving after the final cue

Most of the evening's stress comes at the end. Thousands of people leave together, and Olympic Park station handles them with controlled queues. Staff hold you in pens on the approach and release groups onto the platform as trains arrive. Follow the barriers and the line moves steadily. Pushing toward the front gains you nothing.

A few habits help:

- **Tap on with Opal or a contactless card** at the gates, as Sydney commuters do. Having it in hand speeds up the queue for everyone.
- **Eat before you arrive.** Precinct food outlets and arena concessions get busy in the hour before doors.
- **Check the bag policy** on the arena's site before you leave home. Arenas restrict bag sizes, and a cloakroom queue is not how you want to start the night.
- **Stay seated through the last piece.** Zimmer's shows tend to build to the end. Leaving early to beat the train crowd usually means missing the loudest part of the night.

If the train queue looks long, wait it out at your seat or in the plaza for twenty minutes. The pens clear surprisingly fast once the first few trains have gone.
