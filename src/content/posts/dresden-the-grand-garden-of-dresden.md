## A straight line through the city

The Hauptallee is the place to start. This long, tree-lined axis runs east to west through the Großer Garten, and the park's paths and lawns branch off it. Locals call it the city's green lung, which isn't an exaggeration: the park covers well over a square kilometre on the southeastern edge of Dresden-Altstadt.

At the centre of the axis stands the Palais im Großen Garten. This summer palace, built from 1678, is one of the earliest baroque buildings in Saxony. Its sandstone facade still shows war damage from 1945. Baroque statues line the formal parterre in front of it, and the wide gravel paths are well suited to slow loops.

## What's inside the gates

The palace is the centrepiece, but most visitors also head for the park's edges:

- **Dresdner Parkeisenbahn**: a narrow-gauge park railway that circles the grounds in the warmer months, staffed largely by young volunteers. Check the season schedule before you plan around it.
- **Carolasee**: a lake with rowing boats and the Carolaschlösschen restaurant on its shore.
- **Zoo Dresden**: at the southwest corner of the park.
- **Botanischer Garten**: the university's botanical garden, at the northwest corner.
- **Gläserne Manufaktur**: Volkswagen's glass-walled factory, which faces the park's northwest edge at Straßburger Platz.

For the main axis, the palace and the lake, set aside one to two hours. With the zoo or a full railway loop, plan on half a day.

## Getting in and around

You can reach the park on foot from the Altstadt. From the Frauenkirche, walk southeast along Grunaer Straße or through the Großer Garten's northwest entrance near Straßburger Platz. The walk takes roughly 20 to 25 minutes.

Trams stop at Straßburger Platz and at the zoo, near the park's western corners. The listed address, Hauptallee 10, puts you on the main axis close to the palace.

There are no fixed opening hours, because the park is open 24 hours a day, every day. The railway, the zoo, the botanical garden and the palace's event programme each run on their own schedules, so check them separately. Any ticket prices are posted at their own entrances or on their official sites.

## Beating the weekend crowds

The Großer Garten is one of Dresden's most visited green spaces, and weekends make that obvious. Try not to arrive between 11am and 8pm on Saturday or Sunday. That's when the lawns fill with picnics and the Hauptallee becomes a slow stream of prams, joggers and bikes.

For the quietest visit, come between 7am and 8am on a weekend. At that hour you'll mostly share the paths with runners, and the palace facade catches the low eastern light.

A few habits help you fit in:

- **Stay out of bike lanes.** Cyclists use the main paths heavily and expect pedestrians to keep right.
- **Picnic on the open meadows.** Dresdeners spread blankets there freely. Stay off the formal planted beds around the palace.
- **Carry some cash.** Small kiosks and the railway's station ticket counters don't always take cards.
- **Let the railway decide your route.** Many families ride one stretch and walk back, which is a good way to cover the far eastern end.
