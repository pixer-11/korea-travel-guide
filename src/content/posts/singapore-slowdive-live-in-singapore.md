## A 1930 cinema built for feedback

Slowdive play Capitol Theatre on 12 December 2026. The Reading band formed in 1989, and their records are washes of reverb and blurred guitar with two voices folded together. *Souvlaki* (1993) is the one most fans know by heart. "Alison" and "When the Sun Hits" come from it.

The core members are Rachel Goswell, Neil Halstead, Christian Savill, Nick Chaplin and Simon Scott. They split after *Pygmalion* in 1995 and reunited in 2014. Since then a younger audience has found them, so the crowd mixes original fans from the nineties with people who weren't yet born when the band first played.

## Inside Capitol Theatre

The venue opened in 1930 as a cinema and stage, one of Singapore's grandest at the time. Its restoration kept the domed ceiling painted with signs of the zodiac. Winged-horse sculptures also survive inside the hall.

It's a seated, raked room rather than a warehouse floor. That changes how a shoegaze show feels. You aren't fighting for a sightline, and the sound fills a closed, curved space rather than drifting off into a rooftop or open lawn.

The theatre sits within Capitol Singapore, the restored complex at 17 Stamford Road that also holds a hotel and a shopping arcade. Nearby, all within walking distance:

- **Raffles Hotel**, across the junction on Beach Road
- **CHIJMES**, a former convent full of bars and restaurants
- **National Gallery Singapore** and **St Andrew's Cathedral**, south toward the Padang
- **Funan**, the mall on North Bridge Road with plenty of food options

## Getting to Stamford Road

The easiest route is the MRT. City Hall station serves both the North-South and East-West lines, and Capitol is a few minutes' walk north along Stamford Road. Bras Basah (Circle Line) and Esplanade (Circle Line) are also close.

December falls in the northeast monsoon, the wettest stretch of Singapore's year. Heavy showers often hit in the afternoon or evening. The covered walkways and underground links around City Hall mean you can stay mostly dry between the station and the theatre.

## How the night usually runs

Singapore runs on cards, so cash rarely comes into it. Contactless bank cards pay for the MRT at the gates and for most bars nearby, and tipping isn't part of the culture.

Most fans eat before the show. They usually go to the hawker stalls and restaurants around Bras Basah or to the CHIJMES courtyard, because theatre bar queues build quickly before the set. Capitol's air-conditioning runs cold, like most of Singapore's indoors, and regulars usually bring a light layer for two hours in a seat.

Slowdive are louder live than their records suggest, especially in the long, droning closing songs. Plenty of people in the stalls wear foam or musician's earplugs. Those don't dull the guitars, just the harsh edges.

Phone filming is common at gigs here, though in a seated theatre the people behind you will notice a raised screen. After the encore, fans usually head straight down to City Hall for the MRT home, or across to the bars on Purvis Street and Seah Street for a late drink.
