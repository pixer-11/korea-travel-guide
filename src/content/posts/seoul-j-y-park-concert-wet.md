## Three nights of Christmas with JYP

WET is J.Y. Park's year-end concert. It runs from December 24 to 26, 2026, so the run covers Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and the day after.

Park is the founder of JYP Entertainment, the label behind TWICE and Stray Kids. On stage, though, he is a solo singer and dancer. His back catalogue goes from 1990s hits like "Honey" and "Elevator" to "When We Disco," his 2020 duet with Sunmi. Expect sweat, stretch fabric and a lot of choreography from a performer in his fifties.

Set times and the setlist are not part of the published facts here, so this guide doesn't guess at them.

## Olympic Hall, Songpa-gu

Olympic Hall is a mid-sized, music-focused venue inside Olympic Park. The park was built for the 1988 Summer Olympics. The hall is far smaller than the stadium-scale arenas nearby, which is part of why a year-end solo show feels close-up here.

Getting there:

- **Olympic Park Station (Line 5):** Exit 3 leads into the park. Olympic Hall is a few minutes' walk inside.
- **Mongchontoseong Station (Line 8):** this one serves the park's western side, by the World Peace Gate. The walk to the hall is longer.
- **Jamsil Station (Lines 2 and 8):** Lotte World Tower and the Jamsil hotels are a short ride away.

Late December in Seoul usually sits around or below freezing after dark. The park paths between the station and the hall are wide but have no cover.

## Queueing, merch and the ride home

Korean concerts run on rules that first-time visitors from abroad don't always expect. These are the usual patterns:

- **Ticket pickup:** tickets held at the box office are normally released only against the booker's ID. Foreign fans usually carry their passport.
- **Phones:** most Korean concerts ban filming and photography while the show is on. Staff walk the aisles to enforce it. Fans keep the phone photos for the lobby and the posters outside.
- **Merch:** official goods booths usually open hours before the show. The lines for limited items often form well before doors.
- **Cheering:** Korean audiences sing along loudly and know the call-and-response parts. Hits like "Honey" get the whole room on its feet.
- **Getting around:** a T-money card works on the subway, buses and convenience-store counters. Most concertgoers top one up at any station machine.

After the encore, the crowd heads for Exit 3 at Olympic Park Station all at once. Plenty of people walk on to Mongchontoseong or flag a taxi on Yangjae-daero to avoid the crush. Seoul's last subway trains run around midnight.

## Before and after the show

Olympic Park fills a winter afternoon easily. The **World Peace Gate** stands at the Mongchontoseong entrance, and the **Seoul Olympic Museum** is inside the grounds. A walk along **Mongchontoseong**, an earthen fortress wall from the Baekje era, takes about an hour.

For Christmas lights, Jamsil is two stops away on Line 8. **Lotte World Mall** and the lakeside around **Seokchon Lake** are its seasonal draws. Bangi-dong, just west of the park, has rows of Korean barbecue and chicken-and-beer spots. Groups of concertgoers usually end the night there.
