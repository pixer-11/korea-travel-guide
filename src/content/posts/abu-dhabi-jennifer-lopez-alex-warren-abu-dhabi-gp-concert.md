## The bill: a returning headliner and a breakout voice

Two very different careers meet on one stage. Jennifer Lopez brings decades of chart hits, films and dance-heavy stage shows. This is a return visit to Abu Dhabi for her, not a debut.

Alex Warren comes from a newer route. He built a huge online following as a creator before his song "Ordinary" became one of the biggest hits of 2025. Pairing him with Lopez gives the night two audiences: fans who grew up with "Jenny from the Block" and a younger crowd who found Warren on their phones.

## Etihad Live and the Grand Prix weekend

The concert is part of race weekend, not a separate tour date. Etihad Live is the stage for the Grand Prix's after-race concerts. It sits on Yas Island next to Yas Marina Circuit, so the music follows on from a day at the track.

That shapes the whole evening. A lot of the crowd comes straight from the grandstands, so expect sunscreen, team merchandise and people who have been on their feet since the afternoon. A smaller share comes over from the city just for the music.

In past editions, entry to the after-race concerts came with Grand Prix race tickets rather than a standalone concert ticket. The official Abu Dhabi Grand Prix channels and their ticketing partner publish how access works for this edition.

## Getting onto Yas Island

Yas Island is on the northeastern edge of Abu Dhabi, away from the downtown Corniche. It is one of the closest districts to Zayed International Airport, a short drive away. Central Abu Dhabi takes longer, and a drive from Dubai usually takes over an hour, depending on traffic.

On race weekends, most people use:

- **Ride-hailing:** Uber and Careem both operate in Abu Dhabi.
- **Official shuttles and park-and-ride:** the Grand Prix normally runs these over race weekend.
- **Walking from Yas hotels:** the island's hotels are close to the circuit.

Leaving takes longer than arriving. Thousands of people head out at once and the island has a limited number of road links. Many concertgoers wait for the first wave to clear before calling a car.

## How the night usually works on Yas Island

Most fans make it one long evening. They stay on site from the racing straight through to the encore instead of coming and going. Re-entry rules and bag policies come from the event organiser, and the official Grand Prix information covers them.

A few habits help:

- **Paying:** cards and contactless phone payments are standard at UAE venues. Food and drink stalls rarely need cash.
- **Clothing:** December evenings in Abu Dhabi are mild and can feel cool after sunset. Regulars bring a light layer. Swimwear-style outfits look out of place, even though the venue is relaxed.
- **Alcohol and behaviour:** drinks are served only in licensed areas. Public drunkenness is illegal in the UAE, so most people pace themselves through a long day.
- **Phones:** a charged phone matters for ride-hailing at the end of the night. Many people carry a power bank.

Fans who want a spot near the stage usually move from the circuit to the concert area as the racing winds down. They don't wait for the headliner to be due.

## Making a weekend of it

Yas Island keeps you busy beyond the concert. Ferrari World Abu Dhabi, Warner Bros. World Abu Dhabi and Yas Waterworld are all on the island. Yas Mall covers shopping and casual food.

Yas Marina, along the circuit, is lined with restaurants and moored yachts. The W Abu Dhabi – Yas Island hotel sits right over the track. Its curved, lit-up canopy is the building you see in most race-weekend photos.

To see the city itself, the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and Louvre Abu Dhabi on Saadiyat Island are both a drive away. Many visitors spend the daytime there on non-race days.
