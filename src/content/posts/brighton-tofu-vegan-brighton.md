## A table in the twittens

Meeting House Lane is barely wider than a shopfront. It is one of the old twittens of The Lanes, Brighton's tangle of narrow alleys, and it twists between jewellers' windows and small café doorways. Number 15 is where Tofu Vegan Brighton sits.

The name tells you two things. Everything on the menu is plant-based, and tofu is the point, not a substitute. Brighton has long been one of Britain's most vegan-friendly cities. Even so, a place that puts the bean curd in its own name is making a specific promise.

Its rating is unusually strong for a restaurant with several hundred reviews. Yet it stays a quieter find, tucked off the main shopping streets where few people walking past would spot it by chance.

## Finding 15 Meeting House Lane

The Lanes are easy to get lost in. That's part of the pleasure, but it helps to have an anchor. Meeting House Lane takes its name from the Friends Meeting House, the Quaker building that stands at the lane's western end near Ship Street.

- **From Brighton station:** walk south down Queen's Road and West Street, then turn east into the Lanes. Allow 12–15 minutes, mostly downhill.
- **From the seafront:** head north from the area around Brighton Palace Pier and the Old Steine. You will be in the Lanes in under ten minutes.
- **By bus:** most city routes stop at Churchill Square, Western Road or the Old Steine. All are a short walk away.
- **Driving:** the Lanes are pedestrian alleys. Park in a city-centre car park and walk in.

Paths here are paved and uneven in places. The lanes also narrow enough that two pushchairs passing is a negotiation.

## The Tuesday rule and the late weekends

The schedule is simple once you know it. There is one trap: **Tuesday is closed**. That catches out visitors planning a midweek day trip, so build your plans around it.

The rest of the week looks like this:

- **Monday and Wednesday:** 12pm–10pm
- **Thursday, Friday and Saturday:** 12pm–11pm
- **Sunday:** 12pm–9:30pm, the earliest close of the week

The kitchen opens at noon every open day, so a proper sit-down lunch works on six days out of seven. The later Thursday-to-Saturday hours suit a post-show dinner after something at Brighton Dome, which is a short walk east.

On Sundays, remember the 9:30pm close. Leave enough time to eat without rushing.

## Ordering around the tofu

Arrive hungry and with company if you can. A restaurant built around one ingredient is best explored by ordering several dishes and sharing them, so you can taste tofu in different textures across the table. It can be silken, firm, fried or braised.

The menu changes, so ask your server what they would order. Two questions are worth asking:

- **How spicy is it?** Heat levels on any menu are relative. Asking first saves you from a dish you can't finish.
- **What's in it?** Soy is one of the 14 allergens UK restaurants must declare. Staff are legally required to give allergen information on request. If you are avoiding gluten, sesame or nuts, say so when you order.

Prices sit in the mid-range. The current menu and prices are shown at the restaurant, so check there rather than relying on old screenshots online.

## Settling the bill on Meeting House Lane

Paying works the way it does in most British sit-down restaurants. A few habits help:

- **Card and contactless** are standard in Brighton restaurants. Phone payments are widely accepted too.
- **Check the bill before tipping.** Many UK restaurants add a discretionary service charge, usually shown as a separate line. If it's already there, you don't need to add more.
- **If there's no service charge,** leaving around 10–12.5% for good table service is the normal custom. Tipping is never compulsory.
- **Booking:** a small room in the Lanes can fill up on a Friday or Saturday evening. Check the restaurant's own channels for whether it takes reservations before you set out with a group.

You usually ask for the bill rather than waiting for it. Catch your server's eye, or ask when they next come by. Lingering after your plates are cleared is perfectly normal.

## Before and after: the Lanes on foot

Tofu Vegan Brighton is in the most walkable part of the city, so a meal fits easily into a half-day. Allow 60–90 minutes at the table. Then spend the rest of your time on foot:

- **The Royal Pavilion:** George IV's Indian-style palace with onion domes, about five minutes east past the Old Steine.
- **Brighton Palace Pier and the seafront:** straight south, good for a walk along the shingle after an evening meal.
- **North Laine:** across North Street, a grid of independent shops, record stores and cafés that suits a lunchtime visit.
- **The Lanes themselves:** the jewellers and antique dealers that line the twittens around Meeting House Lane.

Doing lunch? Come on a Monday, Wednesday or Sunday, then walk the Lanes while the shops are open. Doing dinner? Choose Thursday to Saturday for the latest kitchen.
