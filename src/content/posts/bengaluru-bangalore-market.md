Chickpet is Bengaluru's original commercial core, laid out under the Kempegowda-era street grid, and Bangalore Market sits inside its Santhusapet pocket, one lane among wholesale cloth, hardware and provision stores that have traded here for generations. This is not a tourist bazaar built for browsing; it's a working market where shopkeepers move stock in bulk.

## Finding your way into Santhusapet

Chickpet's lanes are narrow, numbered inconsistently, and packed with handcarts. The nearest metro stop is Chickpet Station on the Purple Line, a short walk through Cottonpet Main Road into the market grid.

Auto-rickshaws know "Chickpet Santhusapet" even if they don't know the market by name. Parking a car here is impractical; the lanes weren't built for it. Walk in, and expect to ask a shopkeeper for exact directions once you're close, since address plates are scarce.

## What's actually inside

Bangalore Market functions as a hub for wholesale and semi-wholesale trade, well rated by a large base of reviewers, over 2,000 of them. Expect stalls stacked floor to ceiling with goods sold by the dozen or the bale rather than the single piece.

Come with a specific reason to be there, whether that's fabric, festival decorations, or household goods, rather than expecting a curated shopping experience. Bargaining is standard practice for anything bought in quantity; asking the single-piece price upfront tells you fast whether a stall sells retail at all.

## Timing your visit around the trade

The market runs Monday to Saturday, 10:30am to 8pm on weekdays, 11am start on Saturday, and it's shut entirely on Sunday, so don't plan a Sunday detour here.
Weekends get busy from noon through closing, so a Saturday morning arrival right at 11am buys you the calmest window before the crowd builds.

Weekday mornings, shortly after the 10:30am opening, tend to be quieter too, since much of the real bulk-buying traffic from retailers picks up later in the day. Budget half an hour if you're passing through, longer if you're actually sourcing goods and need to compare stalls.

## Moving through the lanes like a trader

This is a well-known, busy market rather than a quiet find, so the practical skill is navigating the crowd, not avoiding discovery. Cash is still the default currency for small wholesale transactions here; carry small notes since stallholders dealing in bulk goods aren't set up to break large bills quickly.

Dress for heat and foot traffic: closed shoes, not sandals, since the lanes are uneven and carts move fast. Keep bags zipped and in front in the tightest stretches, standard practice in any dense Chickpet lane.

If you're unsure which stall stocks what, ask; shopkeepers direct you to a competitor down the row rather than waste your time, a habit of the wholesale trade here more than of any single business.
