## A streaming giant's year-end party

Scream Night is iQIYI's way of closing the year. The Chinese streaming platform built the event as a celebration of its own stars and shows. Over the years it has grown into one of the more closely followed dates on the Chinese-language entertainment calendar.

For 2026 the show moves to Macau. The Scream Carnival and Scream Night take place at Galaxy Macau on December 4-5, 2026. The bill is a lineup of Chinese pop stars, and iQIYI handles the performer list and ticketing through its own app and official channels.

Because the organiser is a streaming platform, the event is built for cameras as much as for the room. Fans who don't hold tickets have traditionally followed Scream Night on iQIYI itself.

## Two nights, two names

The event's quirk is that it carries two names across two consecutive nights. Scream Carnival and Scream Night share the same December weekend at the same resort, Friday 4th and Saturday 5th.

That matters for travel. Fans who come from Hong Kong or mainland China often treat it as a short Macau weekend rather than a single concert. A Friday arrival and a Sunday departure covers both evenings and leaves Saturday daytime for Taipa or the old city.

Ticket holders should read their ticket for the specific night, because the two names refer to separate evenings. The organiser sets the doors and show times for each night.

## Getting to Galaxy Macau on Cotai

Galaxy Macau stands on Cotai, the strip of reclaimed land that joins Taipa and Coloane. Its gold-topped towers are easy to spot from across the strip. The Venetian and the other big resorts sit on the far side of the main road.

Most visitors reach it in one of these ways:

- **Free resort shuttles.** Galaxy runs complimentary buses to and from the main entry points, including the Taipa Ferry Terminal, the Border Gate with Zhuhai and the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge border post.
- **Ferry from Hong Kong.** Boats from Hong Kong's Sheung Wan and Kowloon terminals dock at Taipa Ferry Terminal, which is a short ride from Cotai.
- **The bridge.** Shuttle buses run across the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge from Hong Kong to the Macau border post, with resort shuttles waiting on the other side.
- **The Light Rapid Transit.** The Taipa Line runs through Cotai and links the ferry terminal and the airport to the resort area.
- **By air.** Macau International Airport is on Taipa, minutes from Cotai by taxi.

Macau has its own immigration controls, separate from Hong Kong and mainland China. Visitors coming from either side pass through a border check. On a concert weekend, the queues at the bridge and the Border Gate are part of the trip.

## Lightsticks, queues and patacas

Chinese fandom brings its own customs to a show like this. Fan clubs often coordinate colours, banners and lightsticks for their idol. Fans commonly gather outside the venue before doors to swap handmade freebies and photo cards. If you're there for one artist, finding their fan club's meet-up is the easiest way into the crowd.

Some practical habits that make the weekend easier:

- **Money.** The local currency is the pataca. Hong Kong dollars are widely accepted, usually at near par, though change often comes back in patacas.
- **Mobile payment.** Alipay and WeChat Pay are common across Galaxy's restaurants and shops, as is Macau's local MPay.
- **Language.** Cantonese and Portuguese are the official languages, but Mandarin is spoken throughout the Cotai resorts, so mainland visitors are rarely stuck.
- **Ticket names.** Large Chinese-language concerts often tie tickets to the buyer's identity. Fans typically carry the same ID document they bought with.
- **Phone signal.** If you're coming from Hong Kong or the mainland, your SIM roams here. Travellers often pick up a Macau data card or turn roaming on before crossing.

After the encore, the shuttle and taxi queues at the resort get long. Fans staying nearby often walk back through the connected resort corridors rather than wait.

## Eating around the show

Galaxy Macau's own dining is spread across its podium levels. For something more local, Broadway Macau is attached to the complex. Its Broadway Food Street gathers casual Macau and Hong Kong eateries under one roof, which makes it useful for a quick bowl before doors.

Taipa Village is a short taxi ride north. Rua do Cunha, its narrow pedestrian lane, is lined with shops selling:

- **Pork chop buns**, Macau's signature sandwich
- **Almond cookies**, pressed and sold in stacked tins
- **Serradura**, the Portuguese-influenced cream and biscuit-crumb pudding
- **Egg tarts**, the Macanese take on the Portuguese pastel de nata

Fans often make Saturday daytime a Taipa trip. The lanes, the pastel Taipa Houses and their small museum fill an easy few hours before the second night.

## December in Macau

Early December is one of Macau's most comfortable stretches of the year. The humid summer and the typhoon season are over, days are usually dry and mild, and evenings turn cool. A light jacket covers the walk between the shuttle stop and the doors.

The city also starts to dress for the holidays around this time. Cotai's resorts put up seasonal displays, and the historic centre around Senado Square and the Ruins of St. Paul's gets its winter lights. Ferries and buses link Cotai to the peninsula for a half-day there.

## Staying on Cotai for the weekend

Most concertgoers sleep on Cotai, and staying inside the Galaxy complex means the venue is an indoor walk away. The hotels clustered around Galaxy Macau share the same podium and corridors. The resorts across the road are a short walk or shuttle hop.

Budget travellers often stay on the Macau peninsula, where older hotels and guesthouses sit near Senado Square. Taxis and buses cross the bridges to Cotai, but late-night queues after the show make the ride home slower.

Fans coming from Hong Kong sometimes don't stay at all and catch a late ferry or bridge bus back. Late departures thin out after midnight, so they usually leave during the final songs or stay over.
