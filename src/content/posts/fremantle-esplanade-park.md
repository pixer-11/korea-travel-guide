## Lawn, pines and a wheel
Esplanade Park is Fremantle's front lawn. Rows of mature Norfolk Island pines tower over flat open grass, their trunks marking out the shade on a hot afternoon. The observation wheel turns at one edge, and you can spot it from the streets around.

Families take over the playground. Couples and backpackers spread towels under the trees. A cafe on the grounds handles coffee and snacks, so nobody needs to leave the grass to refuel.

The park is well known and well used, one of the most-reviewed open spaces in Fremantle. Don't expect solitude on a sunny weekend. What you get is room: the lawn is big enough that a crowd still leaves gaps.

## Finding your way to Marine Terrace
The park fronts Marine Terrace, on the seaward side of Fremantle's old West End.

- **By train:** Fremantle Station, the end of the line from Perth, is roughly a 10–15 minute walk south through the town centre.
- **By free bus:** Fremantle's free CAT buses loop the centre. Check Transperth for current routes and stops.
- **By car:** City of Fremantle runs paid parking around Marine Terrace. Rates and time limits are on the street signs and meters.
- **On foot from the cafe strip:** South Terrace's Cappuccino Strip is a few blocks inland.

## Using the park well
Google currently lists the park as open 24 hours. The wheel, cafe and playground run on their own schedules, so check them before planning a night ride. Ticket prices for the wheel are posted at the booth.

Expect a strong southwesterly sea breeze, the "Fremantle Doctor", on many summer afternoons. Come prepared:

- A blanket you can weigh down; paper plates won't stay put.
- Sunscreen and a hat. The pines shade some of the lawn, not all of it.
- A layer for evenings, when the breeze cools things off fast.

Things in Fremantle sit close together, so you can do a lot in a short day. From the park you can easily walk to:

- **Fishing Boat Harbour** for fish and chips on the boardwalk.
- **Bathers Beach** and the **Round House**, Western Australia's oldest public building.
- **WA Shipwrecks Museum** on Cliff Street.
- **Fremantle Markets** and **Fremantle Prison**, slightly further inland.

## How Freo uses its front lawn
Locals call the town "Freo", and this lawn is where the town gathers. Big community events, Fremantle festivals and public celebrations often take over the grass. Check the City of Fremantle events calendar before you go, both to catch something and to avoid finding the lawn fenced off.

Picnic etiquette is easy. Claim a patch of shade, keep the space around the playground clear, and take your rubbish to the bins. Local rules on alcohol in public places can apply, so read the park signage instead of guessing.

The trick here is to treat the park as your base. Get a coffee at the cafe, take your cup to the shade, and walk out to the harbour or the beach from there. In summer, sunset sends you west towards Bathers Beach a few minutes away. Come back through the park as the pines go dark.
