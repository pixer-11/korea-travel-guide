## A village on the reservoir

You step off the main gate path into the year 1910. Heritage Park sits at 1900 Heritage Dr SW, on the east shore of the Glenmore Reservoir. It is one of the most-reviewed attractions in Calgary and consistently well rated, and it calls itself Canada's largest living history museum.

The grounds move forward in time as you walk:

- **An 1860s Hudson's Bay Company fur-trading fort** with a First Nations encampment beside it
- **A 1910 prairie town** with a working blacksmith, a general store and a bakery, staffed by interpreters in period dress
- **Gasoline Alley Museum**, a hall of vintage cars, trucks and old gas pumps
- **Heritage Town Square**, a 1930s–50s streetscape near the entrance

## What actually moves

The working antiques are the draw. A steam locomotive pulls carriages on a loop around the village, and you can board it at the station platforms. On the water, the S.S. Moyie, a sternwheeler paddleboat, takes passengers out onto the reservoir.

There is also an antique midway with a carousel and a Ferris wheel. Rides can need their own pass on top of admission, so check the ticket options at the gate or on the official site. Current prices are posted there. Kids gravitate to the midway. Adults tend to linger in Gasoline Alley.

## Getting in without the weekend crush

The busiest window is weekends from 10am–5pm, which covers nearly the whole operating day. If your dates allow it, go on a weekday. If you can only do a Saturday or Sunday, accept the crowds and plan around them:

- **Buy tickets online** before you leave so you skip the gate line.
- **Walk to the far end first.** Most visitors work outward from the entrance, so start at the fort and the reservoir and come back toward Town Square.
- **Time the train.** It runs on a loop, so ride it as transport across the grounds, not as a separate queue.
- **Ask the interpreters questions.** They stay in character and will demonstrate their trade, which is the point of the place.

The full historical village is seasonal. It runs daily in summer and on reduced days into autumn, while Gasoline Alley and Town Square stay open longer. Hours shift through the year and around holiday events, so the official site has this month's schedule.

## Reaching the park

Driving is simplest, and parking is on site off Heritage Drive. On transit, take the CTrain Red Line to Heritage Station. In season, a Calgary Transit bus connects the station to the park gate. The walk from the station is long, so confirm the bus route before relying on it.

Plan on four hours for the main village. A full day is easy with kids. Afterwards, Glenmore Park and the pathways around the reservoir are close by. They are good for a walk along the water once the gates close.
