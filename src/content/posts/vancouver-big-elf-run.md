## Four distances, one Saturday

You choose the distance; everyone gets the holiday theme. The Big Elf Run is an annual festive fun run in Vancouver, and the 2026 edition takes place on Saturday, December 12. It offers four routes:

- **1K:** short enough for small children, strollers and walkers
- **5K:** the classic fun-run distance, roughly 25–45 minutes for most people
- **10K:** a proper race distance for regular runners
- **15K:** an unusual length that rewards people training through winter

At a steady 6 minutes per kilometre, the 15K takes about 90 minutes. Most entrants pick the distance their slowest group member can finish comfortably, then stay to see the longer runners come in.

## Registration and the start area

Tickets for this event are entries for one distance. The organiser runs registration and publishes the start and finish location on its official event page. This guide does not name a venue, because none is part of our verified facts.

Families usually register children for the 1K and adults for a longer route, which spreads finishing times across the morning. Groups who run in matching festive outfits tend to register together under one team name, if the entry form allows it.

## What December in Vancouver means for runners

The weather comes into it more than the course does. December is one of Vancouver's wettest months, with daytime temperatures usually a few degrees above freezing and steady drizzle more common than snow. The sun rises around 8am and sets close to 4:15pm, so a morning start runs in flat, grey light.

That shapes what experienced local runners wear:

- a light waterproof shell rather than a heavy jacket
- a thin hat or headband and light gloves, taken off after the first kilometre
- shoes with grip, since wet paths and painted lines get slick
- a dry layer and a towel left in a bag for after the finish

Costumes go over the running kit, not instead of it. A felt elf hat soaks through in Pacific drizzle and sits heavy by the 10K mark.

## Getting there on Compass and tap

Vancouverites use transit to get to race mornings. The SkyTrain's Expo, Millennium and Canada lines connect downtown with Burnaby, Richmond and the airport. You can tap a credit card, phone or Compass Card at the fare gates, so you don't need a paper ticket. Weekend service starts later than on weekdays, which matters for an early start.

Runners who drive usually face Saturday downtown parking, which is metered and tightly enforced. Car-share and cycling are common local alternatives. Vancouver has protected bike lanes across downtown, though a wet saddle after a 15K is its own small penance.

Afterwards, most groups head for brunch. Table service in Canada normally comes with a tip of 15–20 percent, added on the card terminal the server brings to the table. Kitchens fill quickly after a mass-participation event, so big groups often split across several cafés rather than wait for one long table.
