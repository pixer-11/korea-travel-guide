## Booking, paying and getting home

A newly reopened spot with a viral team behind it tends to fill up fast. Reserve ahead through the venue's official channels, and confirm the address and opening hours when you do. Schedules at new openings often change in the first months.

Payment in Kuala Lumpur bars is simple:

- **Cards** are the norm at sit-down venues. QR e-wallets like DuitNow and Touch 'n Go are common too.
- **Read the bill.** Many KL venues add a service charge plus government tax, so you don't need to tip on top. Leaving a little extra for good service is welcome but not expected.
- **Bring ID.** The legal drinking age in Malaysia is 21, and some door staff check.

Alcohol in Malaysia is heavily taxed. Drinks at a venue like this sit on the pricier side, so check the menu before you order a round for the table.

## Planning the night around it

Treat Suzie Wong as a whole evening. If you're there to eat, give it two to three hours. If you're staying for the dancing, plan for longer.

Getting home:

- **Grab** is how Kuala Lumpur gets around after dark. It's the regional ride-hailing app, and it's reliable for late pickups.
- **Trains stop early.** The LRT and MRT close around midnight, before a dancing crowd usually leaves.
- **Book your ride from inside.** Pickups outside nightlife venues get slow at closing time, so order before you reach the kerb.

Malaysia is a majority-Muslim country, but bars and nightlife operate openly in Kuala Lumpur. Behaviour that suits the dance floor is best left there. Keep it low-key on the street outside, especially in mixed residential areas.

Your first stop is the venue's official social media. It's the most reliable place to confirm the location, any dress code, reservation rules and the current menu before you head out.
