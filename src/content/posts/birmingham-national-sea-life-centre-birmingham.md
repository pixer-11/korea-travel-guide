## A glass box on the canal

The building is the first exhibit. Foster and Partners designed this angular block of glass and steel, and it opened in 1996 as part of the Brindleyplace canalside regeneration. It sits right on the towpath at The Water's Edge. Narrowboats tie up outside, and the Utilita Arena faces it across the water.

Inside, the route winds downwards and in a loop. Low light, blue-lit tanks and signs at child height set the tone early. This is a family attraction first. The layout keeps pushchairs moving, and small play areas break up the walk for younger visitors.

## What you'll see on the route

The main event is the ocean tunnel. It is a full 360-degree acrylic tube, and sharks and rays glide over your head and along both sides. Most people slow right down here. The best views come from standing to one side and letting the crowd pass.

Around it, the displays move from shallow British waters to tropical reefs. Headliners worth planning around:

- **Gentoo penguins** in a chilled enclosure, best at feeding time
- **Asian short-clawed otters**, busiest and loudest when they're fed
- **Sea turtles** in the large ocean display
- **Rockpool and touch-style areas** where staff explain what's in front of you
- **Jellyfish tanks**, backlit so the bells glow against the dark

Talks and feeds run through the day. Their times are posted near the entrance, and checking them first shapes the rest of your visit.

## Getting to The Water's Edge

The address is The Water's Edge, Birmingham B1 2HL, in the Brindleyplace development just off Broad Street. From Birmingham New Street station it is roughly a 15 to 20 minute walk. Head west past Victoria Square and Centenary Square, then drop down to the canal.

The West Midlands Metro is quicker for tired legs. Its Brindleyplace stop on Broad Street is a few minutes' walk from the door. Driving works too. There are multi-storey car parks around Brindleyplace and the ICC, though city-centre parking fills up on event nights at the arena.

## Booking, timing and the tunnel shuffle

This is one of Birmingham's most visited attractions, so timing matters more than anything else. It's calmest between 10am and 11am on weekdays. On weekdays it opens at 10am, so arriving at opening puts you inside the quiet hour. Weekends are a different story. Avoid 12pm to 5pm on Saturdays and Sundays, when it's at its busiest.

On Saturday the doors open earlier, at 9:30am, and that head start is worth taking if a weekend is your only option. Sunday opens at 10am. Opening hours run:

- **Monday to Friday:** 10am to 4pm
- **Saturday:** 9:30am to 5pm
- **Sunday:** 10am to 4:30pm

Book online in advance. Entry works on timed slots, and the current prices are on the official SEA LIFE website. Card and contactless payment is standard in the UK, and tipping isn't expected here. In the tunnel, the unspoken rule is to take your photo and step aside so the next family can reach the glass.

## How long to stay and what it suits

Most visits take about two hours. It stretches towards three if you catch several talks or have young children who linger at the touch pools. Weekday hours close at 4pm, so a mid-afternoon arrival leaves you rushing. Starting in the morning is the better plan.

The draw is at its strongest for families with children up to about ten. The centre also offers group packages for schools and parties. Adults who come on their own tend to get the most from the tunnel and the penguins, and less from the smaller themed tanks.

## Around Brindleyplace afterwards

You come out straight onto the canal network, and the area rewards an hour's wander. The Ikon Gallery is a few minutes away, in a converted Victorian school with free contemporary art. Gas Street Basin is a short walk along the towpath. Its narrowboats and old wharf buildings give you a feel for the canals that powered industrial Birmingham.

Brindleyplace and Broad Street have plenty of chain and independent restaurants for lunch. The Library of Birmingham on Centenary Square is on your walk back towards New Street. Its free rooftop garden gives wide views over the city.
