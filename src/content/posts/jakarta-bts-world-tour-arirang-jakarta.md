## Two nights between Christmas and New Year

BTS plays Jakarta on December 26 and 27, 2026. These are two concerts on the ARIRANG world tour, the group's first headline tour since the members came back together after military service. For many Indonesian fans it is the first chance in years to see all seven on one stage.

The tour takes its name from "Arirang", the Korean folk song that most Koreans can sing from memory. Calling a comeback tour after it tells you the group is leaning into where it comes from. Expect that idea to carry through the show's look and the set.

The timing matters as much as the music. The shows fall on the long Christmas to New Year holiday, when Jakarta fills with travellers and the roads slow down. That holiday calendar affects nearly every practical choice below.

## Where the tickets and dates live

BTS tour information comes from BigHit Music, the group's label under HYBE. Fan-facing announcements usually go out on Weverse, the group's official fan platform. That is where fans normally go for show dates, ticketing partners and entry rules.

Some things are not covered here:

- **Prices:** the official ticketing page carries them, so this guide prints no figures.
- **Start times and set length:** nothing verified, so none are given.
- **Resale:** fans often warn each other about fake tickets passed around on social media and in chat groups. Indonesian promoters usually tie tickets to the buyer's name and ID.

## Jakarta's stadium-sized stages

Only a handful of Jakarta venues can hold a crowd this size. This guide does not name a stadium for these dates. Here are the venues the city normally uses for stadium-scale concerts, so you can follow the details on the ticket.

- **Gelora Bung Karno Main Stadium (GBK), Senayan, Central Jakarta.** This is the big Soviet-aid bowl built for the 1962 Asian Games. It sits inside a large sports complex with tree-lined walkways and food stalls all round it.
- **Jakarta International Stadium (JIS), Tanjung Priok, North Jakarta.** A newer stadium near the port, with a roof over the stands and a closed modern shell.
- **Indonesia Arena, inside the GBK complex.** An indoor arena, smaller than either stadium.

## Getting across the city on show night

Your route depends on which part of Jakarta the venue is in. For anything at Senayan, the MRT Jakarta North–South line is the easiest way in. Istora Mandiri and Senayan stations sit along Jalan Sudirman right by the complex, and the TransJakarta busway stops at GBK on Corridor 1.

North Jakarta is harder. Concertgoers at JIS mostly use Grab or Gojek, private cars, or shuttles that promoters sometimes run. When a whole stadium leaves at once, ride-hail pickup points get crowded. Many fans walk a few streets away before ordering a car.

Holiday traffic stacks on top of the concert crowd. Jakarta's evening rush is slow on a normal weekday. During the year-end break, toll roads and the main arteries out of the centre stay busy late into the night.

## Rain season and the December show

Late December falls in the middle of Jakarta's wet season. Afternoon and evening downpours are normal from November to March. They can arrive fast and hard, then stop within the hour.

At an outdoor stadium that changes what you pack. Stadiums often restrict umbrellas in the stands, so fans usually carry a thin plastic poncho instead. Other common items:

- A waterproof pouch or zip bag for your phone and ID
- Shoes that cope with wet concrete and flooded kerbs
- A small towel, because the humidity stays high even when it isn't raining
- A power bank for the long queue and the ride home

Rain doesn't usually stop a Jakarta concert. It does slow the walk out and push up ride-hail demand.

## Inside the ARMY crowd: how a Jakarta show works

Indonesia has one of the world's largest BTS fanbases, and its concert habits are well organised. Most fans carry the ARMY Bomb, the official lightstick. Paired to your seat through the official app, it changes colour with the show, so the whole stadium becomes one programmed light display.

Some local customs to know:

- **Ticket redemption.** Indonesian promoters often run a step called *penukaran tiket*, where you swap your e-ticket for a wristband or physical ticket. It usually needs the ID that matches the ticket name.
- **Freebies.** Fans hand out free photocards, stickers and snacks in the queue, often from small tables set up by fan groups. Many attendees bring a small stack of their own to swap.
- **Fan chants.** The crowd shouts the members' names in a set order during certain songs. Fan accounts share chant guides before tour stops.
- **Paying.** Food and merch stalls around big venues mostly take QRIS, Indonesia's standard QR payment, as well as e-wallets like GoPay and OVO. Some cash is still useful for street vendors outside the gates.

Queues for official merchandise often start well before the show, and fans usually go on a separate trip to buy it. A few Indonesian phrases also help: *terima kasih* (thank you) and *permisi* (excuse me) when you squeeze past someone in a packed row.

## Staying and eating around the shows

Where you sleep decides how bad the ride home is. For Senayan, hotels along Sudirman, around Senayan City and Plaza Senayan, or in SCBD are close enough that some fans just walk back. For North Jakarta, places near Kelapa Gading or Ancol are a shorter ride than the central business district.

Rooms in late December fill with holiday travellers as well as concertgoers. Fans travelling from other islands usually sort out flights and rooms as soon as they have tickets.

The food around Jakarta venues is quick and cheap. You'll find carts selling *bakso* (meatball soup), *sate*, and sweet iced tea in plastic cups. Senayan's malls also have food courts with air conditioning, which helps on a humid evening.
