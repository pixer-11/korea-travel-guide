## One night, one name on the poster

This is a solo night. Park Jinyoung, born in 1994, spent years in GOT7, the JYP Entertainment boy group whose members all left the agency together in 2021. He has since built a second career as an actor and a soloist, and CODE# is the concert carrying his own name.

Manila has long been one of GOT7's warmest markets. The crowd at a show like this is usually a mix of longtime Ahgase, the fandom name GOT7 fans have used since the early days, and people who know him first from his television dramas.

## Who you are actually going to see

It helps to know his range. Fans of the group remember him from his early stage name, Jr., before he switched to performing under his own name. His acting work includes the drama *He Is Psychometric* and the second season of *Yumi's Cells*.

Between those roles he completed his mandatory military service in South Korea. A concert after that kind of break tends to feel like a reunion as much as a performance. That is part of why the floor at a show like CODE# usually sings along to everything.

If you are coming as a plus-one, a few evenings with his solo releases and some GOT7 favourites will cover the songs most likely to get the loudest response. The exact setlist is his team's business, and no guide can promise it.

## Tickets and where the details live

Money questions have one honest answer here. Seating sections, ticket tiers and prices are set by the concert's promoter and sit on its own ticketing page, and this guide prints no figure for any of them.

Philippine K-pop shows commonly split the room into tiers, with perks such as soundchecks or send-offs attached to some of them. Whether CODE# has any of these is the promoter's call. A screenshot of the seat map is something fans usually keep on their phone, because it makes settling a seating argument in the queue far quicker.

Resale is where fans most often get burned. Tickets bought through strangers on social media carry a real risk of duplicates or fakes. Experienced concertgoers in Manila tend to stick to the official channel and any exchange the promoter itself runs.

## A December Saturday in the Christmas rush

The date matters more than it looks. December 5 falls deep in the Filipino Christmas season, which famously starts in September. By early December the malls are full of shoppers and the roads are full of people heading to Christmas parties.

A Saturday night in that stretch is among the heaviest for traffic across Metro Manila. A drive that takes twenty minutes at dawn can take well over an hour in the evening. Fans coming from Makati, BGC, Pasig or the southern suburbs usually allow far more time than a map app suggests.

The weather is on your side, at least. December sits in the dry season, so you are less likely to meet a typhoon-season downpour than at a mid-year show. Evenings run slightly cooler than the rest of the year, though a packed queue still gets warm.

Most attendees get to the venue by Grab, the dominant ride-hailing app in the Philippines, or by a pre-arranged car. Surge pricing and long waits are normal straight after a concert lets out. Many groups walk a few blocks away from the venue and only then request a ride, to escape the pickup crush.

## Freebies, banners and the queue outside

Manila's fan culture has its own rituals. Long before the doors open, fan clubs and individual fans usually set up outside to hand out "freebies": photocards, stickers, slogans, fans and small printed goods with the artist's face on them. Swapping and collecting these is half the social life of the evening.

A few habits help you fit in:

- **Take freebies politely and one at a time.** Many are made by fans at their own expense, and a quick "thank you" goes a long way.
- **Bring a small tote or folder.** Photocards bend in pockets, and fans tend to carry sleeves to protect them.
- **Follow the banner project.** Fan groups often hand out banners or slogans with a cue for a specific song. When the arena lifts them together, join in.
- **Charge your phone.** Digital tickets, Grab and meet-up messages all run off one battery. A power bank is the most borrowed item in any K-pop queue.
- **Respect the photo rules.** Policies on cameras and filming vary by promoter and are enforced at the door. Professional lenses are commonly refused at concerts here.

Filipino fans are known for singing loudly, cheering in fan chants and screaming between songs. Learning the fan chant for one or two songs will make you feel part of the room rather than an observer. Even then, people usually keep their phones low enough that the row behind can still see.

## After the encore

The crowd does not scatter straight away. Fans usually linger outside to trade the last freebies, take group photos and find friends. Food stalls and nearby convenience stores fill up quickly with people who skipped dinner to hold their place in line.

For visitors staying overnight, picking a hotel close to the venue spares you a post-concert crawl across the city. Late-night traffic eases after the immediate exodus. A short wait over a meal often gets you home sooner than joining the first wave of cars.
