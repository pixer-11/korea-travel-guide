## How to visit like a local

Treat a tasting-menu dinner in Rome as an evening commitment, not a quick stop — locals dining at this level of restaurant typically block out two to three hours and don't rush between courses. Dress smart-casual to elegant; Rome's fine-dining rooms skew a notch more polished than its trattorie, and a hotel-attached restaurant with this much buzz will likely lean that way too. Payment is almost universally by card in Roman restaurants at this tier, though it's worth carrying some cash for anything ambiguous. Tipping in Italy is not obligatory the way it is in the US — a service charge is often built in, and locals typically leave only a small additional amount, if any, for exceptional service, so don't over-tip out of habit. The most common mistake visitors make with a buzzy new opening like this is walking in without a reservation expecting a table; call ahead, and if you're told the tasting menu is the only format offered, don't ask for substitutions or an à la carte option — let the kitchen set the pace, which is the whole point of the format. Arriving on time (not early, not late) for your reservation slot is also the norm, since tasting-menu kitchens often fire courses for the whole room in sync.
