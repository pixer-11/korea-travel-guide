## What you're walking into

Dong Ba Market is not staged for visitors. It's the main supply market for Hue, a low multi-story block on Trần Hưng Đạo, right on the north bank of the Perfume River near the Citadel walls.

Inside, the layout runs by trade rather than by aisle number. Fabric and tailoring stalls cluster on one level, dried seafood and spices in another stretch, fresh produce and meat closer to the river-facing entrances. Nobody has translated the signage for you, and that's the point.

## The clock tower and the river side

The market's clock tower facing the river is the easiest landmark if you get turned around inside. Use it as your reset point; the stalls behind it thin out into the quieter fabric and household-goods section.

Cross the road toward the river and you get a clear view back at the building's long, arcaded front, which is a decent spot to get your bearings before diving back in.

## What to look for on the food row

The market's cooked-food stalls are where Hue's specific dishes show up in their working-class form, not the restaurant plating:

- **Bún bò Huế** – the city's spicy beef noodle soup, sold by the bowl at stalls with a pot already going
- **Bánh bèo, bánh nậm, bánh lọc** – steamed rice cakes wrapped in banana leaf, sold in stacks
- **Mè xửng** – Hue's sesame candy, good to buy in bags to take home
- **Nón lá** – the conical hats, sold nearby if you want one that isn't a souvenir-shop version

Eat where a stall has a cluster of locals already seated on low stools; that's a better signal than any sign.

## Bargaining without the friction

Prices on food are usually fixed or close to it. On souvenirs, textiles and non-food goods, an opening price is an opening price, not the price.

- Ask "how much" and expect the first number to be padded for a foreigner
- Counter roughly half and move up from there, without treating it as a fight
- Walking away calmly if you're not happy is normal and won't cause offense
- Small notes help; nobody at a produce stall wants to make change for a large bill

Cash is what actually moves here. Don't expect cards at food stalls or most of the smaller vendors.

## Getting there and timing your visit

The market runs 7am to 7pm every day, which covers most of what you'd want to do here. Because it's a well-known stop on the Hue circuit rather than a quiet local secret, midday tour groups do pass through, so the crowd thickens noticeably by late morning.

Going soon after opening, while stallholders are still setting up produce and the aisles are wider, is the easiest way to see it working rather than jammed. It's a five-to-ten minute walk from the Citadel's Thuong Tu Gate, or a short cyclo or taxi ride from most riverside hotels.

## Moving through without getting lost

The building reads as confusing on a first pass because it isn't organized for outsiders. Give yourself permission to just follow one aisle to its end rather than trying to map the whole thing.

Keep valuables zipped and worn in front; it's a dense, narrow-aisled market, not a place for a loose bag. If you want a specific item, a hat, a length of fabric, a bag of mè xửng, ask a few stalls before buying at the first one, since prices vary stall to stall for the same goods.
