## A palace that still does its job

Most palaces open to the public are retired. This one isn't. Qasr Al Watan sits inside the UAE Presidential Palace compound, where state visits and cabinet meetings still happen, and it opened to visitors in 2019.

From the approach you see a long white facade of granite and limestone under a central dome. The detailing borrows from Arabian and Islamic architecture. It is one of the most-reviewed attractions in the capital, and it gets busy.

## Inside the halls

You don't walk up to the front door. Visits start at a visitor centre, where you go through airport-style security. A shuttle bus then drives you through landscaped gardens to the palace.

Once you're through the doors, these are the rooms that hold people longest:

- **The Great Hall**: a domed space whose central dome is among the largest in the world, with geometric patterns climbing the walls and ceiling.
- **The Spirit of Collaboration**: a circular chamber built for councils such as Gulf Cooperation Council meetings.
- **The Presidential Banquet Hall**: where state dinners are held.
- **The Hall of Presidential Gifts**: diplomatic gifts presented to UAE leaders by visiting heads of state.
- **The library**: the Qasr Al Watan collection of books and manuscripts on the country's history and governance.

The floors are polished marble and you'll cover a lot of ground, so allow at least two hours. Three is better if you read the exhibit panels on Emirati governance and craft.

## Getting in smoothly

The palace keeps a working compound's rules, and they're enforced at the door.

- **Dress:** cover your shoulders and knees, men included. Tight or sheer clothing can get you turned away or handed a cover-up.
- **Timing:** it opens daily at 11am and closes at 6pm, including weekends. Arriving at opening usually puts you ahead of the midday coach tours coming from Dubai.
- **Tickets:** book on the official Qasr Al Watan website to skip the ticket desk. Current prices and combined options with the gardens are posted there, not here.
- **Bags:** bring only what you need, since everything goes through the scanner.
- **Photography:** phones and cameras are fine in the main halls. Watch for signs in restricted areas, and don't film security staff or the compound gates.
- **Manners:** keep your voice down in the Great Hall. It's a ceremonial space, and staff will remind you.

## Getting there and what's next door

Abu Dhabi has no metro, so a taxi or ride-hailing app is the easy option. Ask for Qasr Al Watan by name, because drivers know it better as that than as the Presidential Palace. Coming from downtown, it's a short drive west along the Corniche to Al Ras Al Akhdar.

If you're coming from Dubai, the drive takes roughly one and a half to two hours. That's why the tour buses reach the palace after 11am, not at opening.

Emirates Palace hotel is right next door, and a coffee in its lobby pairs naturally with the visit. The Etihad Towers are also nearby, with an observation deck on the upper floors. Head back along the Corniche and you reach the public beach and promenade, or take a longer taxi ride out to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque. That makes for a full day of Abu Dhabi's formal architecture.
