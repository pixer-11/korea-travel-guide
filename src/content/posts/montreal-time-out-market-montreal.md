## A food court with a downtown address

Forget the mall-food-court picture. Time Out Market Montréal sits inside Centre Eaton on Rue Sainte-Catherine Ouest, downtown's main shopping street. The counters belong to established Montreal restaurants, and Time Out's editors chose which ones got in. Long communal tables fill the middle, with bars between the food stalls.

Prices are mid-range. That's higher than a standard food court but lower than sitting down at the restaurants the vendors come from. The line-up of counters changes over time, so look at the market's own site before you go if you want one particular kitchen. Plan on 45 minutes to an hour and a half.

## Reaching Centre Eaton

McGill station on the Green Line is the closest metro. Its underground passages lead into Centre Eaton, so in January you can arrive without ever going outside. That link is part of RÉSO, the city's underground network. Once inside the mall, follow the market signs up to the food hall.

If you'd like to make a half day of it, these are all close by:

- **Christ Church Cathedral**, on Sainte-Catherine right next to the mall
- **Place Ville Marie** and its plaza, a short walk south
- **McGill University** campus and the **McCord Stewart Museum**, up the hill on Sherbrooke
- **Quartier des Spectacles**, a walk east along Sainte-Catherine

## Timing your table

At the moment the market opens every day at 11:30am. Monday and Tuesday it closes at 9pm. Wednesday through Saturday it stays open until 10pm. Sunday is the short day, with doors shutting at 6pm, so don't plan a Sunday dinner here.

Weekends are when it gets hard. From 12pm to 8pm on Saturday and Sunday, the seating fills and the lines at popular counters stretch out. On weekdays it's usually calmest between 12pm and 4pm. Late evenings from 9pm to 10pm are quiet too, but only Wednesday to Friday, since Monday and Tuesday close at 9.

## Ordering the Montreal way

Every counter takes its own orders and payments, so there isn't one shared bill. That makes splitting up easy:

- **Claim seats first.** On a busy day, one person holds a table while the rest go and order.
- **Spread out.** Each person gets in a different line, and you share plates at the table.
- **Pay by card or phone.** Tap payment is normal across Quebec.
- **Expect a tip screen.** Card terminals usually suggest a percentage. At a counter it's up to you, and you can type in your own amount.
- **Get drinks at the bar**, not from the food counters.

Greet staff with "Bonjour" or "Bonjour, hi." Downtown staff move easily between French and English, and opening in French is a courtesy people notice. Menus are usually in both languages. Words like *poutine*, *tourtière* and *smoked meat* need no translation.

Because it's a food hall, you don't book. You just turn up. That's why the weekday afternoon window matters so much. If you come on a Saturday, arrive at 11:30am opening and you'll be seated and eating before the 12pm rush.
