## A 70-metre drop into a basalt bowl

Brandywine Creek falls 70 metres over a cliff. The cliff is stacked layers of old lava flows, and at the bottom the water has carved out a round, dark bowl. From the main platform you look straight across at the rock wall. Its banded, column-like volcanic structure is the reason geology classes stop here.

It's a quick stop, not a hike. Most visitors spend 30 to 60 minutes here, including the walk, the photos and a sandwich at the picnic tables near the lot.

## From Highway 99 to the platform

The park entrance is on BC-99, the Sea-to-Sky Highway, between Squamish and Whistler. It's on the east side of the road if you're coming from Whistler Village, a drive of roughly 15 minutes south. Look for the BC Parks sign. The turn comes up fast at highway speed.

From the parking lot, the trail runs about 1 km through second-growth forest to the falls. Along the way you'll:

- cross a bridge over Brandywine Creek
- cross an active rail line on foot (look both ways, because freight trains do use it)
- pass the main falls platform, then continue a few minutes to a second lookout over Daisy Lake and the Cheakamus valley

The path is mostly level gravel and fine for kids. Wheelchairs and strollers can manage it with some effort. If you want a longer outing, the Sea to Sky Trail runs through the park and links north towards the Cal-Cheak suspension bridge.

## Beating the tour-bus hours

This is a well-known roadside stop on every Vancouver–Whistler day trip, so timing matters more than anything else. Weekends from 9am to 6pm are the busiest stretch. That's when the lot fills, people double-park along the entrance road and you wait your turn at the platform railing.

The quietest window is 7am to 8am, on weekdays and weekends alike. Early light also falls well on the east-facing wall. The data also shows 9pm to 11pm as quiet. Check BC Parks for gate and access times before planning an evening visit, and never walk the rail crossing after dark.

- Stay behind the fences. The cliff edges are unstable, and people have died going past the barriers for a better photo.
- Keep dogs on a leash, as in all BC provincial parks.
- Pack out everything you bring. The picnic areas have no staff clearing up after you.
- Drones and off-trail scrambling to the base are not the done thing here. The view from above is the view.

## Seasons and what's nearby

Snowmelt makes the falls loudest and fullest in late spring and early summer. By late summer the flow thins to a ribbon. In winter the spray can freeze into an ice cone at the base. Winter access and parking change with conditions, so check BC Parks' current notices before you drive out in snow.

Brandywine pairs easily with other Highway 99 stops. Squamish's Sea to Sky Gondola and Shannon Falls lie south. Whistler Village and Function Junction are a short hop north, so you can fit the falls into a morning drive without giving up the whole day.
