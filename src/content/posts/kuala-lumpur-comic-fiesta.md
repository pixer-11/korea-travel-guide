## Two days under the Twin Towers

Comic Fiesta is Malaysia's largest anime, comics and games convention. The 2026 edition runs on Saturday 19 and Sunday 20 December at the Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre.

The setting does half the work. Across one weekend, a glass-fronted convention hall at the foot of the Petronas Twin Towers fills with cosplayers, sketchbook artists and game fans. Plenty of them travel in from across Asia. At lunchtime, foam armour and hand-sewn costumes are in the food court queues beside office workers.

## Reaching the Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre

The centre sits on the edge of KLCC Park, and most attendees come by train. The usual routes:

- **KLCC LRT station (Kelana Jaya Line):** the nearest stop. Follow the underground link through Suria KLCC mall, then cross toward the park side and the convention centre.
- **From Bukit Bintang:** an air-conditioned elevated walkway runs from the Pavilion area to KLCC. It keeps you out of the traffic and out of any rain.
- **Grab:** ride-hailing drops off at the convention centre entrance. Roads around KLCC are slow on a busy weekend.

Mid-December falls in the northeast monsoon season, and afternoon downpours are common in Kuala Lumpur. Cosplayers with paper or foam props tend to choose the covered routes for exactly that reason.

## What fills the halls

The convention runs on a few overlapping worlds. Exhibitor booths sell official merchandise and figures and demo games. Stage programmes bring panels and performances. The artist area is where many regulars spend most of their two days.

That artist area is the heart of the fandom side. Rows of tables carry original zines, prints, acrylic charms and stickers from independent creators, many of them Malaysian. Stock at the popular tables is finite, so serious collectors usually make that area their first stop each morning.

Cosplay is everywhere: in the halls, on the stairs and outdoors in the park. Photographers gather wherever there is good light and room to step back. For a lot of attendees, the costumes are the whole reason to come.

## Cosplay manners and paying at the tables

The etiquette follows a few long-settled norms.

- **Ask before you photograph.** A cosplayer will usually stop and pose if asked. Candid close-ups, or shots taken mid-meal or while they are fixing a costume, are considered bad form.
- **Costume is not consent.** Touching props, wigs or the person without permission is off-limits, however casual it seems.
- **Pay the way Malaysians pay.** Many artist tables take e-wallet QR codes such as DuitNow and Touch 'n Go eWallet, and plenty also take cash. Card readers are less common at small tables than at the big commercial booths.
- **Carry your own bag.** Merch hauls add up fast, and regulars bring a sturdy tote or a flat folder for prints.

The convention sets its own rules on prop weapons, costume changes and photography areas. They sit with the event information on Comic Fiesta's official site, and serious cosplayers treat them as part of building a costume.

## Dates, tickets and the official details

The dates are fixed: 19–20 December 2026, across a Saturday and Sunday. Comic Fiesta's official site carries the ticket links, the admission categories and the venue information for this edition.

Day passes and full-weekend access are the formats attendees usually weigh. Many first-timers choose one day and spend it mostly in the artist area and on the cosplay grounds. Returning fans more often take both days, saving the second for panels and anything they missed.

Food inside the centre is limited. Most people head to Suria KLCC next door, which has a food court and a long row of restaurants.

## When the halls close around KLCC

The convention sits in one of the easiest parts of Kuala Lumpur to keep exploring. KLCC Park wraps around the back of the centre, with a jogging track, a large fountain lake and lawns. By evening, cosplayers are often still doing shoots by the water.

The Petronas Twin Towers rise directly above Suria KLCC, and Aquaria KLCC sits inside the convention centre complex itself. For dinner, Bukit Bintang and the Jalan Alor food street are a walk away along that covered walkway. They are where many out-of-town fans end up on Saturday night.

Visitors from abroad usually stay around KLCC or Bukit Bintang. Both are a walk or one LRT stop from the halls, which matters with a costume and a bag of merch to carry home.
