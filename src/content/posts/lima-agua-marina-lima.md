## Cumbia from Sechura, in a stadium

Agua Marina is Peruvian cumbia at its most singable. The band formed in the 1970s around the Quiroga family in Sechura, a fishing town on the Piura coast. Its sound is the northern style: bright electric guitar runs, steady percussion, and lyrics about heartbreak that whole crowds know by heart.

On November 28, 2026, the band plays Estadio San Marcos in Lima. This is a family-friendly show as much as a party. Grandparents, couples and groups of coworkers usually end up dancing in the same row.

## Estadio San Marcos and the route in

The stadium belongs to Universidad Nacional Mayor de San Marcos, the oldest university in the Americas. It stands inside the Ciudad Universitaria campus between Avenida Venezuela and Avenida Universitaria, in Cercado de Lima close to the Callao boundary.

It is an open bowl with concrete stands, so you see the sky above the stage. Getting there:

- **From Miraflores or Barranco:** a ride-hail car (Uber, Cabify, InDrive) is the usual choice. Lima traffic can stretch the trip well past half an hour on a Saturday evening.
- **From the historic centre:** it is a short drive west along Avenida Venezuela.
- **Leaving:** thousands of people want a car at the same moment, so many attendees walk a few blocks along Avenida Universitaria before requesting a pickup.

Late November is late spring in Lima. The winter garúa drizzle has mostly lifted, but evenings by the coast still cool down, and a light layer is useful in the stands.

## Tickets and zones

Stadium concerts in Peru are sold by zone. Pitch areas sit closest to the stage, and the stands give you a seat and a wider view. Each zone has its own price on the official seller's page, so this guide prints no figures.

Buy only from the official seller. Resold paper tickets and screenshots outside the gates are a known risk at big Lima shows. Entry is usually by QR code scanned at the turnstile, along with a bag search.

## How a cumbia night here works

Nobody stands still at a cumbia concert. The pitch becomes a dance floor, couples pair off for the slower numbers, and the whole stadium shouts the choruses back at the stage. People usually dance in pairs or small circles, so leave a little room around you.

Some practical habits:

- **Paying:** Yape and Plin, Peru's mobile wallets, are everywhere, but street vendors on the surrounding avenues mostly take small-note soles.
- **Bags:** security searches are routine, so attendees tend to bring a small bag and leave umbrellas, bottles and anything sharp at home.
- **Timing:** fans of Peruvian cumbia usually come for the full set. Support acts and the headliner can run long, so a late finish is normal.
- **Phones:** keep yours zipped away inside the crowd, where pickpockets work the densest areas.

Before or after, Plaza San Miguel is a short ride west for food. The Parque de las Leyendas zoo also sits near the campus, though it is a daytime outing rather than a pre-show stop.
