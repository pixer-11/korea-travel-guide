## Why Tadhanà is worth planning around

"Tadhanà" means "fate" or "destiny" in Filipino, and the name is the whole pitch: this is chef Francis Tariga's return home after building her reputation in New York's demanding fine-dining scene. The restaurant has quickly become one of Manila's most talked-about openings, built around a ten-course tasting menu that reframes Filipino flavors — the sourness, funk, char, and layered sweetness that define the cuisine — through the technique and plating language of contemporary global fine dining. It's less a place to grab dinner than an event to build an evening around, closer in spirit to a degustation night out than a casual meal.

## What makes it stand out

What separates Tadhanà from Manila's broader wave of new Filipino-fusion restaurants is the pedigree and intent behind it: a chef who spent roughly a decade abroad absorbing kitchen discipline and technique, then chose to bring that back specifically to reinterpret her own culinary heritage rather than open a generic international concept. The ten-course format signals a chef-driven, narrative meal — expect the kitchen to walk diners through a sequence of ideas rather than a static menu of interchangeable mains and appetizers. Because the specific dishes rotate with seasonality and the chef's evolving story, it's best to go in without fixed expectations about what will land on the table and instead trust the format: a long, paced, multi-sensory dinner rather than a quick bite.

## Getting there and what to expect

Tadhanà sits within Manila's dense, ever-shifting restaurant landscape, where buzzy openings like this one tend to cluster in the city's established dining districts. Given its tasting-menu format, treat it like any serious degustation restaurant abroad: arrive on time (tasting menus are timed and courses are often released together for the room), expect the meal to run a couple of hours or more, and don't plan anything immediately afterward. Because exact address details, opening days, and service times can shift in a restaurant's first months of operation, confirm the current location, hours, and whether lunch or only dinner service is offered directly through the restaurant's official channels — Instagram and a reservation platform are typically the fastest-updated sources for a new opening like this.

## Booking and cost expectations

As a ten-course tasting menu from a chef with international fine-dining credentials, Tadhanà should be budgeted as a premium, special-occasion meal rather than an everyday dinner — likely priced well above typical Manila restaurant fare, possibly with an optional wine or beverage pairing add-on, as is standard for this format elsewhere. Because pricing and package structure (a la carte vs. tasting-only, pairing costs) can change as a new restaurant settles in, don't rely on secondhand price reports online — call or message ahead, or check the reservation page, before you commit a night to it. ## When to go

If flexibility allows, aim for a weeknight seating rather than a Friday or Saturday — tasting menu kitchens typically run a tighter, less rushed service on quieter nights, which usually translates to more attentive pacing between courses. Early evening reservations (rather than the last seating) also tend to get more of the kitchen's full attention before a long night of back-to-back service. Since Tadhanà is still new, its popularity and booking difficulty may keep shifting — check availability a few weeks out and again close to your date in case tables open up from cancellations.

## How to visit like a local

Book directly through whatever official reservation channel the restaurant lists (many new Manila tasting-menu spots use Instagram DMs or a booking platform rather than phone-only reservations) and confirm dietary restrictions when you book, since tasting menus are hard to modify last-minute. Dress a notch above casual — this isn't a t-shirt-and-shorts kind of dinner. Payment at this level of restaurant is typically by card, though it's worth having some cash on hand for tipping, which in Manila's upscale dining scene is appreciated but not automatically mandatory unless a service charge is already added to the bill — check your receipt before tipping twice. The biggest mistake visitors make with tasting-menu restaurants like this is treating the booking casually: cancel or reschedule with real notice if your plans change, since a no-show costs the kitchen a seat it could have given to someone else, and repeat no-shows can affect your ability to rebook at in-demand restaurants across the city.
