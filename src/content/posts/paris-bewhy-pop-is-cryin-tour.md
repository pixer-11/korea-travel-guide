## A Korean rapper under a pagoda roof

The facade is what you see first. On boulevard Voltaire, between the Haussmann apartment blocks, the Bataclan's painted front looks like a Chinese pavilion. It has a curved, pagoda-style cornice and bright trim, and a vertical sign hangs over the doors. On October 22, 2026, that sign hosts the Paris date of BewhY's POP IS CRYIN' Tour.

BewhY (Lee Byung-yoon) broke through nationally in 2016 when he won the fifth season of *Show Me the Money*, Korea's televised rap competition. "Forever" came out of that run and is still the track most international fans know him by. His style is dense, technical and openly devotional, and it suits a room this size better than an arena.

## Inside the Bataclan

The hall is compact. Most of the space is a flat standing floor that faces a raised stage, and a horseshoe balcony runs around three sides above it. Capacity is roughly 1,500, so even near the back you're closer to the stage than at most Paris venues of the same fame.

The building opened in 1865, designed by the architect Charles Duval. Its name comes from *Ba-ta-clan*, a Jacques Offenbach operetta. It started as a café-concert, went through cinema and music-hall phases, and has been one of the city's main rock and hip-hop rooms for decades.

It is also where 90 people were killed in the attacks of 13 November 2015. The venue reopened in November 2016, and a memorial plaque sits on the boulevard outside. Most concertgoers pass it quietly. Some stop for a moment, which is entirely normal here.

## Getting to boulevard Voltaire

Getting there by Métro is simple. **Oberkampf** (Lines 5 and 9) is the closest station, and its exits put you on boulevard Voltaire a minute's walk from the doors.

Other options if your line doesn't reach Oberkampf:

- **Saint-Ambroise** (Line 9): one stop east, a short walk back up the boulevard
- **Filles du Calvaire** (Line 8): about five minutes west, past the Cirque d'Hiver
- **République** (Lines 3, 5, 8, 9, 11): a ten-minute walk down boulevard Voltaire, useful if you're coming from the north or west of the city

Taxis and ride-hails can drop off on the boulevard, but traffic slows around show time. Most people take the Métro.

## Oberkampf before doors, Voltaire after the encore

Being in the 11th arrondissement is half the appeal of a Bataclan night. The neighbourhood is built for drinking and eating at street level, and concertgoers have used it that way for years.

Before the show, most people head up **rue Oberkampf** and the parallel **rue Jean-Pierre Timbaud**. Both are lined with bars, natural-wine counters and cheap eats, and the terraces fill on weeknights. The **Cirque d'Hiver**, a polygonal 1852 circus building on rue Amelot, is two minutes away and worth a look on your walk over.

After the encore, the crowd spills onto boulevard Voltaire. Some walk south toward **rue de la Roquette** and the late bars around Bastille. Others head north to **Canal Saint-Martin**, about fifteen minutes on foot.

## Getting home on a Thursday night

October 22, 2026 falls on a weeknight, and that matters. The Paris Métro stops around 1am on weeknights, later than that only on Fridays and Saturdays. A Bataclan show that finishes at a normal hour leaves plenty of time for the last trains from Oberkampf.
