## Sheep, a summit and a city all around

Sheep graze a few minutes from Auckland's motorways. Cornwall Park is still a working farm, so its paddocks of sheep and cattle run right up the slopes of Maungakiekie / One Tree Hill. That is a dormant volcanic cone, and its terraced flanks are the earthworks of a large Māori pā.

You walk on tree-lined avenues, past stone walls and open grass, with the summit obelisk always in view above. The park was gifted to the people of New Zealand by Sir John Logan Campbell in 1901. He named it after the Duke and Duchess of Cornwall and York, who visited that year.

## Up the cone and around the farm

Most visits are a loop. Here is what to aim for:

- **The summit of Maungakiekie.** It is a short, steady climb to the obelisk and Campbell's grave. From the top you get a 360-degree view across the isthmus to both harbours.
- **The crater rims and pā terraces.** You can still read the ridges and storage pits in the grass.
- **The tree on the summit.** The lone Monterey pine that gave One Tree Hill its English name was attacked in the 1990s and removed in 2000. Native trees were planted on the top in 2016.
- **Acacia Cottage.** Built in 1841, it is often called Auckland's oldest surviving wooden building. It was moved into the park in 1920.
- **Huia Lodge.** This is the park's information centre, with displays on the farm and its history.

Give it two to three hours if you want the summit, the cottage and a coffee. A fast runner's lap of the roads takes far less.

## Café, creamery and picnic lawns

The café and creamery are the park's natural pause point. Expect a queue at the counter on busy afternoons, since walkers, cyclists and families all end up there. Check the menu board on site for what's on and current prices.

Picnics are the other option. The flat lawns under the old oaks and pōhutukawa fill up first on sunny weekends. The higher slopes stay quieter but catch more wind.

## Sharing the paddocks with the farm

Remember that the animals are livestock, not a petting zoo. Visitors here tend to follow a few habits:

- **Leave the stock alone.** Don't feed or chase the sheep, and give cattle a wide berth.
- **Shut every gate you open.** Some paddocks are closed during lambing in late winter and spring. Signs at the fences tell you which ones.
- **Keep dogs on a lead** and follow the posted rules at the paddock entrances. Rules can differ between paddocks.
- **Respect the maunga.** Maungakiekie is a sacred site for Māori. Stay on the formed tracks and off the terraces, and don't climb on the summit structures.
- **Drive slowly.** The internal roads are shared with runners, cyclists and prams. Park only in the marked bays.

## Beating the crowds on One Tree Hill

Timing makes a big difference here. On weekdays it is quietest between 7am and 9am, and again from 6pm to 8pm. At weekends the calm windows are shorter: 7am to 8am and 6pm to 8pm.

Try not to arrive on a Saturday or Sunday between 8am and 5pm. That is when parking bays fill, the summit gets crowded and the café queue grows. An early weekday start gets you the cone with mostly runners and sheep for company.

At the moment the park is open 7am to 8pm daily. Park hours shift through the year, though, so check the official Cornwall Park website for this month's times before planning an evening walk.

## Getting to Epsom

The park sits on Green Lane West in Epsom, about 5km south of the CBD. Driving in from Green Lane West or Manukau Road is the easiest way, and there are parking bays along the internal roads.

By train, take the Southern Line to Greenlane Station. From there it is roughly a 15–20 minute walk west along Green Lane to the park gates. Buses along Manukau Road stop near the western entrance.

The Stardome Observatory is in the adjoining One Tree Hill Domain, so you can pair the park with an evening show. Ellerslie Racecourse is just across Green Lane.
