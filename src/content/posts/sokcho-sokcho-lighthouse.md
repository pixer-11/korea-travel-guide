## Up the Rock, One Step at a Time

The staircase decides your visit. It climbs straight up the rock from Yeonggeumjeong-ro, steep enough that most people stop at least once on the way. You come out on a clifftop beside a working lighthouse, with nothing between you and the East Sea.

This is a small site in the north of Sokcho, and it doesn't get the crowds that pack Sokcho Beach or the central market. That's most of the appeal. You get a sea-and-mountain panorama without the tour-bus scrum that tends to come with views this good.

## Two Horizons From One Deck

Turn east and you face open water. The coastline runs north towards Goseong, with fishing boats working out of Dongmyeong Port just below. The rocks of Yeonggeumjeong break the surf at the base of the cliff.

Turn west and the city drops away to the Seoraksan range. On a clear day you can pick out the granite ridges of the national park above the low rooftops of Sokcho. Few spots in town let you take in both horizons without moving your feet.

What you'll see up there:

- The East Sea, with the waves breaking white on the rocks below
- Dongmyeong Port and its fishing fleet
- Yeonggeumjeong's pavilion and footbridge reaching out over the water
- The Seoraksan skyline behind the city, sharpest in clear, dry air

## Sunrise Below, Panorama After Nine

The lighthouse keeps strict hours, and they shape your plan. It's open from 9am to 6pm every day of the week, with no shorter days listed. So the famous East Sea sunrise can't be watched from the top.

The fix is right at the foot of the hill. The pavilion at Yeonggeumjeong sits on the shore rocks, and its footbridge goes out over the sea. It's one of Sokcho's best-known sunrise spots. Watch the sun come up from there, get breakfast near the port, then climb the stairs once the gate opens at 9am.

At the other end of the day, aim to be up top well before 6pm. Late afternoon light falls from the west across the sea, while Seoraksan behind you turns into a silhouette. Leave enough time to come back down the stairs at a careful pace.

## Finding Yeongnang-dong

The address is 8-28 Yeonggeumjeong-ro 5-gil, in the Yeongnang-dong neighbourhood. That's the northern coastal stretch of the city, between Dongmyeong Port and Yeongnangho lake.

Getting there:

- **From Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal:** this is the nearer terminal, close to the port. From there it's a walk north along the waterfront.
- **From Sokcho Express Bus Terminal:** this one is farther south near Sokcho Beach, so a short taxi ride makes more sense.
- **By taxi:** show the driver 속초등대전망대 (Sokcho Lighthouse Observatory) or 영금정 (Yeonggeumjeong).
- **On foot from the port:** follow the coast road north and the lighthouse hill is easy to spot.

City buses take T-money cards. Taxis can be hailed on the street or called through the Kakao T app.

## On the Staircase and the Deck

A few habits make the climb easier for you and everyone else.

- **Keep to one side.** The stairs are narrow in places. Let people coming down pass, and step aside to rest rather than stopping in the middle.
- **Use the handrail.** Coming down is harder on the knees than going up, especially when the steps are damp from sea spray.
- **Travel light.** Leave the suitcase at your guesthouse or a station locker. Hauling it up the stairs is miserable.
- **Remember it's a working lighthouse.** Stay in the visitor areas and keep away from anything fenced or marked off.
- **Take your rubbish with you.** Korean public spaces often have few bins, and the usual practice is to carry your trash until you find one.

Check the signs at the bottom of the stairs for any rules or visitor information. Things may have changed since this guide was written.

## The Shoreline Loop Around the Hill

The lighthouse works best as one stop on a short coastal walk. Everything below is within easy reach of the stairs.

**Yeonggeumjeong.** The name refers to an old story that waves crashing on these rocks sounded like a geomungo, a Korean zither. Today you'll find a pavilion on the rocks and another reached by a footbridge over the water. It's the best place to see the surf up close.

**Dongmyeong Port.** This is a working harbour with seafood stalls and restaurants along the waterfront. It's the obvious place to eat before or after the climb. Prices are posted at each stall or on the menu.

**Deungdae Beach.** The name means "lighthouse beach". It's a small strip of sand north of the hill and much calmer than Sokcho Beach to the south.

**Yeongnangho.** This lagoon lake sits a short ride inland and has a walking path around its shore. On clear days you get views back towards Seoraksan.

**Further afield.** Sokcho Jungang Market and the Abai Village ferry crossing are south along the coast. They're an easy taxi hop if you want to make a day of the city.

## Planning Your Hour

For the lighthouse itself, allow about an hour. That covers the climb, a slow look from the deck and the walk back down. Add Yeonggeumjeong and a meal at the port, and you've got a comfortable half day.

Clear, dry days give you the best Seoraksan views, and in Korea that often means autumn and winter. Haze and sea mist can hide the mountains completely. If the inland skyline is your main reason for going, check the forecast first. Wear shoes with good grip, because the steps are steep.
