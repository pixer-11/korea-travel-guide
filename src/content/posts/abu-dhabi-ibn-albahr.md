Reem Island has been filling in fast, and Makers District is the part of it built specifically for people to walk along the water, sit outside, and eat well. Ibn AlBahr is one of the names now anchoring that stretch: a Lebanese seafood restaurant that already has a following across the bridge in Dubai, and has now planted a second flag here, facing straight out at the Arabian Gulf.

## What the place is built around

The idea is simple and it's the same idea that made the original popular. You walk past a seafood counter laid out like a market stall before you sit down, pick your fish, and it goes to the grill.

Char-grilled red snapper is the dish people mention first. Expect the wider format to lean into whole fish and shared plates, in the tradition of Lebanese seafood houses rather than a plated tasting menu.

## Why Reem Island, why now

Makers District was designed as a waterfront leisure strip, mixing restaurants, cafes and gallery-style spaces along a promenade. A Dubai favourite choosing to open here says something about where Abu Dhabi's dining scene is pulling energy toward right now.

For visitors, that means you get a Gulf-facing terrace setting without the older, more built-up waterfronts of Al Maryah or the Corniche. It's newer, lower-rise, and still filling out its identity.

## Getting there and settling in

Reem Island sits just across the Maqta and Mina bridges from central Abu Dhabi, a short taxi or ride-share from downtown. Makers District itself is walkable once you arrive, so it's worth parking or being dropped once and strolling the promenade rather than moving the car between stops.

- Aim for a taxi or ride-share rather than driving yourself if you want to linger over drinks
- Arrive with some daylight left if you want to see the water before dark
- Treat it as part of an evening on the promenade, not a single isolated stop

## Reading the room at the seafood counter

The counter is the part of the experience that rewards a little confidence. Walk up, look at what's laid out on ice, and ask the staff what's freshest that day rather than defaulting to a name you recognise from home.

This is standard practice at Lebanese seafood restaurants generally: you're choosing your own fish and portion, and staff expect questions about size, cut and preparation. If you're new to the format, say so, they'll talk you through it.

Because this is a newly landed, trending branch, the practical details are still the ones that shift fastest: opening hours, whether they take walk-ins or expect a booking, and how busy a given evening runs. Call ahead or check their current listing before you go, especially for weekend dinner.

## Making an evening of it

Given the waterfront setting, this reads as a sit-down dinner destination rather than a quick bite. Plan for a proper meal length: ordering from the counter, waiting on the grill, and eating unhurried with a Gulf view.

Pair it with a walk along the rest of the Makers District promenade before or after, since the area is built for exactly that kind of evening. If you're coming from central Abu Dhabi, treat the crossing onto Reem Island as part of the trip rather than a detour, the bridges themselves give you a first look at the water you'll be sitting beside later.
