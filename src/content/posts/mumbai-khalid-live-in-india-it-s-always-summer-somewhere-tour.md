## Getting in and getting home on a Mumbai weeknight

Most concertgoers here pay for everything on their phone. UPI apps such as Google Pay, PhonePe and Paytm work at nearly every food stall and bar counter. International visitors without an Indian bank account usually carry a card and some rupees as a fallback.

Ride-hailing through Uber and Ola is the default way home. When thousands of people leave at once, surge pricing and pickup chaos are normal. Regulars usually walk ten minutes away from the gates before calling a cab, or pick a meeting point with their friends.

The suburban rail network is the cheapest way across the city, on the Western, Central and Harbour lines. Late at night it is crowded and confusing for first-timers. Auto-rickshaws run in the suburbs but are not allowed into South Mumbai, so a cab is the only door-to-door option from Worli southward.

At big Indian shows, security usually checks bags and allows only small ones. Outside food and drink are usually confiscated. Fans also tend to carry photo ID, since organisers often ask for it at entry or at the bar.
