## A long rectangle of shade

Oaxacans call it El Llano, "the plain." On maps it appears as Parque Juárez. It is a long, flat block of walking paths, flower beds and tall palms in Centro, a few streets east of Avenida Macedonio Alcalá and the Santo Domingo complex.

At its centre stands a monument to Benito Juárez. He was Mexico's first Indigenous president, born in the Oaxacan highland village of Guelatao. That makes this park a civic point of pride as well as a place to sit. The Templo de Guadalupe faces the park, and it anchors the large Virgin of Guadalupe celebrations every 12 December.

The park is open around the clock. It is one of the most-reviewed outdoor spaces in the city, so expect company.

## Getting there on foot

Oaxaca has no metro, and nobody needs one for this. From the Zócalo:

- Walk north up the pedestrian Alcalá towards Santo Domingo.
- Turn east and continue a few blocks to Avenida Juárez, which runs along the park's edge.
- Allow about 15 minutes at an easy pace.

Taxis know it as "El Llano," and that name works better than the formal one. Street parking around the centre fills quickly. Walking is the sensible choice if you are staying anywhere in Centro.

## Timing your visit

Crowd data for weekends is clear. The quietest window is 7am to 8am, when runners and dog-walkers have the paths almost to themselves. Try not to arrive between 2pm and 11pm on a Saturday or Sunday. Through those hours families, vendors and couples fill the benches, and finding a shaded seat gets hard.

Mid-morning on a weekday makes a calmer middle ground for photographing the Juárez monument without people in the frame. Early December is the exception to every rule. Around the 12th, the Guadalupe festivities pack the surrounding streets, and children are often dressed in traditional clothing for blessings at the church.

Thirty to forty-five minutes covers a slow lap. Stay longer if you are people-watching.

## Using the park as Oaxacans do

Treat El Llano as a living room, not a sight. Locals use it for:

- an early jog or walk before the heat builds
- a bench break between errands in Centro
- an evening stroll with a snack bought from a cart

Carry small notes and coins. Street vendors rarely take cards, and breaking a large bill for a single snack is awkward. Say "buenas tardes" when you sit down near someone; a greeting is ordinary courtesy here.

The monument is a civic memorial, so climbing it for photos draws looks. Late at night the park stays open, but the busy centre thins out after the weekend rush. Keep to the lit main paths and walk back towards Alcalá in a group if you can.

Combine the visit with Santo Domingo and the Jardín Etnobotánico, both a short walk west. The park then works as the shaded end point of a morning on foot through Centro.
