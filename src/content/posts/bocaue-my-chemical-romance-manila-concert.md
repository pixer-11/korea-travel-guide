## Twenty years of waiting, one night

This is the band's first Philippine show. My Chemical Romance formed in New Jersey in 2001. They broke up in 2013, reunited in 2019, and have toured on and off since. Across all of that, they never once played the Philippines.

That gap matters here. Filipino fans grew up on *Three Cheers for Sweet Revenge* and *The Black Parade* the same as everyone else. Their only way to see the band was a flight to Singapore, Bangkok or further. On November 14, 2026, the band comes to them instead, at the largest indoor arena in the country.

## Inside the Philippine Arena

The Philippine Arena sits inside Ciudad de Victoria, a large planned complex in Bocaue, Bulacan. It was built by the Iglesia ni Cristo and opened in 2014. It is widely cited as the largest indoor arena in the world, with a capacity of around 55,000.

The roof is a single enormous dome. The upper tiers rise steeply, so even the far seats look down onto the stage rather than across at it. Because it is enclosed, a November rain shower outside will not touch the show. Big sound in a room this size does echo, though. Seats facing the stage head-on usually hear the mix most cleanly.

## Getting to Ciudad de Victoria

Bocaue is in Bulacan province, north of Metro Manila. The Philippine Arena has its own interchange on the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX). For most visitors, the trip is a road trip, not a quick metro ride. No rail line runs to the venue.

Your realistic options:

- **Grab or a private car** up NLEX to the Ciudad de Victoria exit. This is flexible, but expect a slow crawl in and out.
- **Organised concert shuttles** from Manila malls and hubs, often arranged for major Arena shows. Check the official announcement and the organiser's channels for what runs on this date.
- **Self-drive.** Parking is on site, but leaving after 50,000 people is a long, patient process.

Build in far more time than the map suggests. Friday and Saturday evening traffic out of Manila on EDSA and NLEX can double the journey.

## Dates and tickets

The announced date is **Saturday, November 14, 2026**, at the Philippine Arena. This guide does not list the ticket tiers, prices or door times. Those belong to the promoter. Confirm timing and tickets on the official site, and check the seat map there before you choose a section.

Buy only through the authorised ticketing partner named in the official announcement. Large Manila concerts attract fake resale listings on Facebook groups and marketplace apps. A screenshot of a QR code proves nothing. If a reseller won't use the official transfer system, walk away.

## Arena-night rules Manila fans already know

Filipino concert crowds sing every word, loudly. Expect full-arena singalongs on "Welcome to the Black Parade", "Helena" and "I'm Not Okay (I Promise)", and expect people near you to have learned the deep cuts too. Black clothing and marching-band jackets are the unofficial dress code, and nobody will look twice at heavy eyeliner.

A few habits that make the night easier:

- **Hit the merch booth early.** Queues for tour shirts at big Arena shows build fast. They often sell out of popular sizes before the headliner starts.
- **Carry a charged phone and some cash.** GCash and cards are common in Manila, but signal inside a packed dome can falter.
- **Read the venue's bag policy first.** Large Arena events usually limit bag sizes and ban outside food, umbrellas and power banks above set limits. Check the official advisory rather than guessing.
- **Agree on a meeting point outside.** Once the show ends, finding friends in the exit flow without signal is harder than you'd think.
- **Pre-plan your ride home.** Ride-hail surge and waits after a sell-out are routine. Many fans walk toward the edge of the complex before booking, or simply wait out the first rush.

## Making a day of Bulacan

If you're coming from abroad or from the south of Manila, consider staying north. Malolos, the provincial capital, is a short drive from Bocaue. Its Barasoain Church is where the Malolos Congress convened in 1898 and drafted the constitution of the First Philippine Republic. It's a calm, worthwhile stop on the afternoon of the show.

Bocaue itself is best known in the Philippines for its fireworks makers and the roadside stalls that sell their stock around New Year. In mid-November you'll mostly see the town as you pass through. Eat before you enter the complex. A full stomach makes the long queue in and the long wait out much easier.
