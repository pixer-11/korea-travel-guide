## Twenty years of waiting, one night

This was the band's first Philippine show. My Chemical Romance formed in New Jersey in 2001. They broke up in 2013, reunited in 2019, and have toured on and off since. Across all of that, they had never once played the Philippines.

That gap mattered. Filipino fans grew up on *Three Cheers for Sweet Revenge* and *The Black Parade* the same as everyone else. Their only way to see the band was a flight to Singapore, Bangkok or further. The November 14, 2026 date brought the band to them instead, at the largest indoor arena in the country.

## Inside the Philippine Arena

The Philippine Arena sits inside Ciudad de Victoria, a large planned complex in Bocaue, Bulacan. It was built by the Iglesia ni Cristo and opened in 2014. It is widely cited as the largest indoor arena in the world, with a capacity of around 55,000.

The roof is a single enormous dome. The upper tiers rise steeply, so even the far seats look down onto the stage rather than across at it. Because the arena is enclosed, rain outside does not reach the show. Big sound in a room this size does echo, though. Seats facing the stage head-on usually hear the mix most cleanly.

## Getting to Ciudad de Victoria

Bocaue is in Bulacan province, north of Metro Manila. The Philippine Arena has its own interchange on the North Luzon Expressway (NLEX). For most visitors, the trip is a road trip, not a quick metro ride. No rail line runs to the venue.

The realistic options:

- **Grab or a private car** up NLEX to the Ciudad de Victoria exit. This is flexible, but traffic in and out is usually a slow crawl.
- **Organised concert shuttles** from Manila malls and hubs, often arranged for major Arena shows.
- **Self-drive.** Parking is on site, but leaving alongside 50,000 people is a long, patient process.

Journeys here usually take far longer than the map suggests. Friday and Saturday evening traffic out of Manila on EDSA and NLEX can double the travel time.

## Dates and tickets

The concert was set for **Saturday, November 14, 2026**, at the Philippine Arena. Ticket tiers, prices and door times came from the promoter.

Tickets were sold through the authorised ticketing partner named in the official announcement. Large Manila concerts attract fake resale listings on Facebook groups and marketplace apps. A screenshot of a QR code proves nothing, and a reseller who won't use the official transfer system is the usual warning sign.

## Arena-night rules Manila fans already know

Filipino concert crowds sing every word, loudly. Songs like "Welcome to the Black Parade", "Helena" and "I'm Not Okay (I Promise)" are made for full-arena singalongs, and local fans tend to know the deep cuts too. Black clothing and marching-band jackets are the unofficial dress code, and nobody looks twice at heavy eyeliner.

A few things shape a big night at the Arena:

- **Merch queues.** Lines for tour shirts at big Arena shows build fast, and popular sizes often sell out before the headliner starts.
- **Phones and cash.** GCash and cards are common in Manila, but signal inside a packed dome can falter, so cash is a useful backup.
- **Bag rules.** Large Arena events usually limit bag sizes and ban outside food, umbrellas and power banks above set limits.
- **Meeting points.** Finding friends in the exit flow without signal is harder than most people think, so groups often pick a spot outside.
- **The ride home.** Ride-hail surge pricing and long waits after big shows are routine. Many fans walk toward the edge of the complex before ordering a car, or simply wait out the first rush.

## Making a day of Bulacan

For visitors from abroad or from the south of Manila, staying north is a practical option. Malolos, the provincial capital, is a short drive from Bocaue. Its Barasoain Church is where the Malolos Congress convened in 1898 and drafted the constitution of the First Philippine Republic. It is a calm, worthwhile stop for an afternoon.

Bocaue itself is best known in the Philippines for its fireworks makers and the roadside stalls that sell their stock around New Year. In mid-November the town is mostly something you pass through. A meal before entering the complex makes the long queue in and the long wait out much easier.
