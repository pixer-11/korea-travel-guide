## Two days in the dry season

Mid-December is the right moment for this. Big Mountain Music Village runs on December 12 and 13, 2026, in the border country around Sisophon, the capital of Banteay Meanchey province in northwest Cambodia. December falls in the cool, dry season, when the rice fields have been harvested and the plains turn straw-coloured and dusty.

The festival is a regional one. It belongs to a cluster of cross-border festival tourism events that draw visitors to the Cambodia–Thailand frontier in December. Expect a crowd that mixes Cambodians, Thais and travellers who have come overland. It does not feel like a stadium show flown in from abroad.

## Where Sisophon sits

Sisophon is a crossroads town, and its geography explains the festival's draw. National Road 5 and National Road 6 meet here. One runs west to the Thai border, the other east toward Angkor.

Rough distances by road:

- **Poipet border crossing** (to Aranyaprathet, Thailand): about 50 km west
- **Battambang**: about 70 km south on National Road 5
- **Siem Reap**: about 100 km east on National Road 6
- **Phnom Penh**: roughly 360 km southeast

The town itself is low-rise and practical. Its central market, petrol stations and roadside rice shops serve truckers and travellers heading toward the border. Locals also call it Serei Saophoan, and you will see that name on signs and maps.

## Getting there from Bangkok or Siem Reap

Most festival-goers arrive by road. From Thailand, the usual route is to Aranyaprathet, then across on foot at Poipet. From there, shared taxis and minibuses cover the short run east to Sisophon. Cambodia issues e-visas, and Poipet also handles visas on arrival.

From inside Cambodia, buses on the Phnom Penh–Poipet and Siem Reap–Poipet routes pass through Sisophon. Many visitors base themselves in Siem Reap or Battambang and do the drive as a day trip. Others take a guesthouse in Sisophon itself, which keeps the late-night journey home short.

Tuk-tuks and motodops (motorbike taxis) cover the last stretch from town to rural sites. Drivers usually wait outside large events for the return trip.

## Riel, dollars and the sampeah

Cambodia runs on two currencies at once. US dollars and Khmer riel circulate side by side, and change often arrives as a mix of both. This close to the border, Thai baht also turns up in markets and at some stalls. Cash handles most small purchases. Mobile payment by KHQR code (the ABA app is the common one) is now everywhere in Cambodian towns.

A few habits that go down well:

- **Greeting:** the *sampeah*, palms pressed together at chest height with a slight bow, answers anyone who greets you that way.
- **Thank you:** *aw kun* (or *aw kun charan* for "thank you very much").
- **Haggling:** for tuk-tuk rides, attendees usually agree the fare before getting in. A smile helps more than hard bargaining.
- **Tipping:** not expected, though rounding up for a driver who waited is appreciated.

Dress at an outdoor show is relaxed. Covered shoulders matter only at the temples you might add on either side of the weekend.

## What the nights feel like

December nights on the northwestern plains are noticeably cooler than the afternoons. Attendees at outdoor events here typically bring a light layer for after dark and something to keep the dust off. That means a scarf or a *krama*, the checked Cambodian cotton cloth sold in every market.

Roadside food in Banteay Meanchey leans on grilled meats, rice dishes and noodle soups. Outdoor festivals in the region usually have food and drink stalls alongside the stages. The organiser handles the lineup, set times and the venue map, and this guide does not repeat any of them.

## Temples and border towns nearby

The days either side of the festival are easy to fill. The pick for most visitors is **Banteay Chhmar**, a vast 12th–13th century temple complex about 60 km north of Sisophon. It is known for its carved bas-reliefs and sees few visitors compared with Angkor.

Other stops on the same roads:

- **Battambang**: colonial-era shophouses, the bamboo train area and Phnom Sampeau's bat cave at dusk
- **Siem Reap and Angkor Wat**: about two hours east
- **Aranyaprathet**: Rong Kluea market on the Thai side, a wholesale market for cross-border goods

Many travellers run the festival into a loop: Bangkok, Poipet, Sisophon, then on to Siem Reap or Battambang before heading home.
