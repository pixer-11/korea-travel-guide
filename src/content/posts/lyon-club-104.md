Lyon's 6th arrondissement is mostly known for its wide Haussmann-style boulevards, the Parc de la Tête d'Or, and a residential calm that doesn't usually produce nightlife buzz. Club 104 is the exception: a single address that functions as three different venues depending on when you walk in.

## What Club 104 actually is

By late morning it reads as a serious coffee shop. Espresso and filter drinks come from well-sourced beans, the kind of sourcing that gets discussed on the shop's own social channels rather than buried on a menu. Laptops are out, tables are shared, and the pace is unhurried.

By early evening the same room shifts register. Lighting drops, the counter starts pouring cocktails and natural wine alongside the coffee, and conversation gets louder. Later still, a DJ takes over and the workspace crowd gives way to a bar crowd dancing between the same tables they were typing at hours earlier.

## Why it's become the address everyone mentions

Lyon has plenty of good coffee shops and plenty of good bars, but few places run both formats in the same four walls without feeling like a compromise. Club 104 has become a reference point precisely because of that hybrid format: it's a legitimate place to work at 10am and a legitimate place to dance at midnight.

That range is also why it fills up fast. Word travels quickly in a city where genuinely new concepts are rare, and Club 104 has been the subject of exactly that kind of word-of-mouth since it opened.

## Finding it in the 6th

The 6th arrondissement sits just east of the Rhône, north of Part-Dieu and bordering the Parc de la Tête d'Or. It's a quieter, more residential district than the Presqu'île or Vieux Lyon, which makes a spot like this stand out more than it would in a nightlife-heavy neighbourhood.

A hybrid venue like this is also the type to adjust its daytime/evening split seasonally, so don't assume the schedule that worked last month still holds.

## Ordering and settling in

The rhythm of the day dictates how you should approach the counter.

- **Morning to afternoon:** order coffee at the counter, grab a table, expect a work-friendly crowd and laptop use to be normal.
- **Early evening:** the menu widens to cocktails and wine; this is the transition window, often quieter than what comes after.
- **Night:** a cover charge or guestlist may apply once DJ sets start, and tables get repurposed as standing space. Confirm this detail on the night, since door policy at trending bars shifts often.

Cash is rarely essential in Lyon's newer venues, but carrying a card that works contactless is the safer default anywhere in the city.

## Timing your visit

If you want the coffee-shop version of Club 104, come on the earlier side, since a hybrid venue like this tends to fill with laptop workers first and then gradually tip toward a drinks crowd as the afternoon wears on. If you're after the DJ nights, arriving right as the music starts usually beats arriving an hour in, when a small, buzzy room reaches capacity quickly.

Reservations, where offered, are worth using for evening drinks or a table during a known DJ set. Walk-ins are more realistic for daytime coffee.

## Making it part of a 6th arrondissement outing

Pair a visit with a walk through the Parc de la Tête d'Or, one of the largest urban parks in France and the district's main daytime draw, complete with a free zoo and botanical garden. Club 104 works well as a coffee stop before the park or a nightcap after dinner elsewhere in the 6th, bridging the arrondissement's quiet daytime identity with a livelier evening one that's relatively new to the neighbourhood.
