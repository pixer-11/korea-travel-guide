## An address that keeps long hours

Market Jaipur rarely leaves you checking the clock. Google lists it at D-13 Sansar Chandra Road, in the Jalupura area just outside the walled city. It's open from 11am to 10:30pm, seven days a week, with no short day and no closing day.

That schedule does a lot of the planning for you. You can stop in late morning before heading into the old city, or leave it until after dark when Jaipur's streets start to cool.

It's consistently well rated, with a few thousand Google reviews behind it. This isn't an undiscovered corner. Plenty of people have found it already, and it sits on a road that visitors pass along all the time.

## What the listing does and doesn't tell you

Google files this address as a market. The listing doesn't include a stall-by-stall inventory, so this guide won't make one up.

If you're after something specific, look through the recent photos and reviews on the listing before you go. They'll show you what's there right now much better than any description written once and left alone. Go with that in mind and the visit won't catch you out.

## Sansar Chandra Road, between the station and the walls

The street explains why this address is so handy. Sansar Chandra Road is one of the roads feeding MI Road, the long commercial strip that runs along the southern side of the walled city from Ajmeri Gate. It's a working street of hotels, offices and shopfronts, with traffic all day.

A lot of Jaipur's transport sits close by:

- **Sindhi Camp bus stand**, where most intercity buses arrive
- **Jaipur Junction**, the main railway station
- **Sindhi Camp metro station** on the Pink Line, which also runs to Chandpole

Because it's this central, the market works well as a stop between other plans rather than a trip of its own.

## Getting to D-13

Auto-rickshaws and e-rickshaws cover this part of town constantly. Uber and Ola also operate across Jaipur.

When you give directions, say "Sansar Chandra Road" and add "near MI Road" as a landmark. Drivers know the road, but a named house number like D-13 means less to them than a well-known junction nearby. If you take a street auto, agree the fare before you get in.

The Jaipur Metro Pink Line is an easy option if you're staying along the corridor. Get off at Sindhi Camp and do the last stretch by rickshaw or on foot. Crossing the traffic on MI Road on foot takes patience, so leave a little time for it.

## The 10:30pm close and Jaipur's heat

The late closing time suits this city. From April to June, Jaipur afternoons are fiercely hot, and walking between shopfronts in the midday sun is hard work. An evening visit is simply more comfortable.

The late hours make an easy pairing possible. Watch the sunset from Nahargarh Fort on the ridge above the city, then come down and head to Sansar Chandra Road with time to spare before closing. From October to March the days are milder, and a late-morning visit just after the 11am opening is pleasant too.

Whatever the season, the schedule doesn't change by day. Weekends and weekdays run the same hours, so pick your day around the rest of your itinerary.

## Paying and haggling on Sansar Chandra Road

Paying for things in Jaipur has its own habits. It helps to know them before you reach the counter.

- **UPI QR codes are everywhere.** Indians pay by scanning with apps like Google Pay, PhonePe or Paytm. Most of these need an Indian bank account, so many international visitors can't use them.
- **Carry small rupee notes.** Change for large notes can be hard to find, especially at smaller counters.
- **Cards work at larger shops**, but don't count on them everywhere. Ask before you pick something out.
- **Haggling depends on the label.** Where prices aren't marked, asking politely for a better price is normal in Jaipur's bazaars. Where they're printed, they usually stand.

A few words of Hindi go a long way. "Namaste" to open, "kitna hai?" for "how much is it?", and "bhaiya" (brother) to address a male shopkeeper warmly. Shoulders-and-knees-covered clothing is the norm in commercial Jaipur and draws less attention.

## Adding the walled-city bazaars

The famous bazaars are a short ride away. Once you're done on Sansar Chandra Road, the old city is close enough to make it a half-day loop.

- **Bapu Bazaar**, just inside the southern wall, for block-printed textiles and embroidered *mojari* slippers
- **Johari Bazaar**, the old jewellers' street, running up towards Badi Chaupar
- **Hawa Mahal**, the pink sandstone facade of latticed windows at Badi Chaupar
- **City Palace and Jantar Mantar**, side by side in the centre of the walled city

Coming from Sansar Chandra Road, Chandpole Gate on the western wall is the nearest way into the Pink City. The Pink Line's Chandpole stop sits right beside it. From there, the old city's straight, grid-planned streets make it hard to get badly lost.

A sensible order: the walled-city sights in the morning, a rest through the hottest hours, then Market Jaipur in the evening. It stays open well after most sightseeing closes.
