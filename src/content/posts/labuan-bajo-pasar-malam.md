Labuan Bajo's tourist strip runs along the harbour: dive shops, sunset bars, restaurants pricing menus in three currencies. Pasar Malam sits a few streets back on Jl. Soekarno Hatta, and it's where the town actually eats dinner.

## What Pasar Malam actually is

"Pasar malam" just means night market in Indonesian, and that's exactly what this is: a stretch of stalls that sets up in the evening and packs down by morning. No storefronts, no signage in English, just folding tables, gas burners and plastic stools.

Expect grilled fish and chicken (ikan bakar, ayam bakar), fried noodles, satay skewers, and whatever vegetables and fruit came in that day. Portions are simple, cooked to order, and served on paper plates or banana leaf rather than at a sit-down restaurant setup.

## Why it's worth the detour

At 4.2 stars from a modest number of reviews, this isn't a market that shows up on every Labuan Bajo itinerary, and that's the appeal. Most visitors stick to the harbour road for dinner before an early Komodo boat departure, so the night market stays a genuinely local scene rather than a staged one.

You won't find English menus or card machines here. What you will find is a cheaper, more direct version of Flores home cooking than most of the restaurants built for the dive-tour crowd.

## Finding it on Jl. Soekarno Hatta

The market address puts it on Jl. Soekarno Hatta, one of the main roads running through central Labuan Bajo, inland from the harbour and marina area where most hotels and tour operators cluster.

- Walk or take a short ojek (motorbike taxi) ride from the harbour front; it's not a long trip from most guesthouses in town.
- Look for the cluster of lit stalls and parked motorbikes rather than a formal market building.
- Go on foot once you arrive: it's a walk-between-stalls setup, not a sit-down restaurant.

## Eating like it's just another Tuesday

Order by pointing at what's grilling or asking the vendor what's fresh that night; a little basic Indonesian ("boleh minta...", "berapa ini?") goes a long way since English is limited.

Cash is what moves here, in small denominations since change can be tight at a food stall. Nobody expects a tip, and haggling isn't really the custom for food prices the way it might be for souvenirs elsewhere in Indonesia.

Eat where you buy: pull up a plastic stool at the stall itself rather than expecting table service or a shared seating area. Portions are sized for one, so it's normal to order from two or three stalls across the evening rather than one big meal.

## When to show up

Night markets in Indonesia run on their own schedule tied to when vendors decide to open and close, so there's no fixed opening hour to plan around here. Aim for early evening once the sun is down and stalls have had time to set up and start cooking; arriving too early means half the stalls aren't fired up yet.

Since this stays under most tourists' radar, you're unlikely to be fighting a crowd even at a reasonable dinner hour. It's a good option on a night when you want food and don't want to plan around a boat schedule or a sunset-bar reservation.

## Pairing it with the rest of your evening

Pasar Malam works well as a first stop before or after wandering back toward the harbour, where the sunset-facing bars along the waterfront pick up later in the evening. Since it's inland from the marina, it also makes sense on a night you're staying closer to the town center rather than right on the water.

Keep expectations calibrated to what it is: a functional, local food market, not a tourist attraction dressed up as one. That's precisely why it's worth the walk.
