## Three Crosses on a Crooked Lane

It's a street corner, not a monument. Calle Cruces opens into a small square where the lanes of Barrio Santa Cruz meet, and in the middle stand old stone columns, each topped with a wrought-iron cross. Those crosses gave the street its name. You don't walk up to them; you come round a bend and find them.

The houses around the square follow the barrio's pattern: whitewashed walls with ochre trim, iron window grilles (*rejas*) and balconies, and potted plants hung on the facades. It's a pause in the maze rather than a sight you tour. Most people who arrive here meant to go somewhere else.

## Finding Calle Cruces

Santa Cruz was built to confuse, so follow the street names on the corner tiles and don't trust your phone's blue dot.

- **Metro:** Line 1 to Puerta de Jerez or Prado de San Sebastián, then walk 10 to 15 minutes northeast into the barrio.
- **Tram:** the Metrocentro line also stops at Puerta de Jerez and Prado de San Sebastián.
- **On foot from the Alcázar:** go through Patio de Banderas into Calle Judería, or skirt the Jardines de Murillo, then head up into the lanes toward Calle Cruces.
- **Address to give a taxi:** C. Cruces, 10, 41004 Sevilla. Cars can't reach most of the barrio, so expect to walk the last stretch.

## Pairing It with the Barrio

On its own, the square fills ten minutes. As one stop on a slow loop through Santa Cruz, it earns its place. Everything below is a short walk away:

- **Plaza de Santa Cruz**, a garden square with its own ornate iron cross, where the church that named the barrio once stood.
- **Plaza de Doña Elvira**, a tiled square shaded by orange trees and a favourite of painters.
- **Callejón del Agua**, a lane that runs along the Alcázar's garden wall.
- **Jardines de Murillo**, the palm-lined gardens on the barrio's southern edge.
- **Real Alcázar and Seville Cathedral**, both just west of the barrio.

Allow an hour or two for the whole loop if you stop in the squares and don't rush the lanes.

## Keeping the Quiet in Las Cruces

It's a residential street before it's an attraction. Those balconies belong to people's homes, so keep your voice down after dark and don't block doorways for photos. Sevillanos eat and go out late. People will still be walking home through here near midnight.

Walking tours come through Santa Cruz, so early morning is the obvious time to have the square to yourself, before the Alcázar queue builds. Because Las Cruces sits a few turns away from the main walking routes, it's often empty even when the lanes nearby are packed.

- **Light:** the lanes are narrow and the walls are tall, so the square spends much of the day in shade. That's a real relief in a Seville summer, when afternoon heat drives locals indoors.
- **Sound:** in the narrow lanes, voices and rolling suitcases carry. Lift your bag over the cobbles.
- **Paying for drinks nearby:** tapas bars in the barrio usually take cards. Tipping is modest, often just rounding up or leaving some small change.

There's no gate or ticket and no closing time, because it's simply part of the street.
