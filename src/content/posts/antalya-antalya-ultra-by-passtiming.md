## A winter ultra on the Konyaaltı shore

Konyaaltı is a long shingle beach, and the course sits along it. The Mediterranean is on one side. On the other, the Beydağları range climbs steeply behind the city's western apartment blocks, often snow-capped by late December.

Antalya Ultra by PassTiming takes place on **December 26, 2026**. PassTiming organises the race, which runs along this coast. It falls in the gap between Christmas and New Year, when Antalya's beach hotels are quiet and the promenade belongs mostly to walkers, cyclists and fishermen.

Running here in December is a different thing from the summer package-holiday version of Antalya. Daytime temperatures are usually mild, often in the mid-teens Celsius. December is also among the wettest months, so mud on trail sections and wet pebbles near the shoreline are normal conditions.

## Getting to Konyaaltı and staying nearby

Antalya Airport (AYT) is east of the city. Konyaaltı is on the far western side, typically a 30–45 minute drive depending on traffic. Taxis and the Havaş airport buses connect the two. Many runners take a taxi straight to their hotel.

Inside the city, the nostalgic tram runs along the coast road toward the Antalya Museum at the eastern end of Konyaaltı. City buses run along Konyaaltı Caddesi too. Public transport uses the Antalyakart, which is sold at kiosks and machines.

Runners usually stay in the Konyaaltı hotel strip or in Kaleiçi, the walled old town with its Ottoman houses and Roman harbour. Konyaaltı makes race morning simpler. Kaleiçi has more restaurants open in winter.

## Race-day habits on the Konyaaltı front

Supporters tend to gather where the course meets the promenade, since the flat seafront path makes it easy to move between viewpoints. The Tünektepe cable car at the western end of the bay lifts visitors to a hilltop with a view across the whole coastline.

A few practical customs help:
- Card payment is standard in hotels, cafés and supermarkets. Small simit stalls and kiosks often prefer cash in Turkish lira.
- Restaurants usually get a tip of about ten percent, left in cash on the table.
- Tea (çay) arrives in small tulip glasses at almost every café. Runners often warm up on it after finishing.
- "Teşekkürler" (thank you) gets used constantly with aid-station volunteers.

After the race, many runners walk the Konyaaltı promenade to loosen their legs. Others head into Kaleiçi for a slow dinner near Hadrian's Gate. The Antalya Museum is right by the beach, with its Roman statuary from Perge, and it suits a rest day before flying home.
