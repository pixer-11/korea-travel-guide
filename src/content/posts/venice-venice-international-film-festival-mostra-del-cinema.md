## Why go

This is the oldest film festival on the planet, running since 1932, and it still feels like an event built for cinema rather than for Instagram. Unlike Cannes, the Mostra unfolds on a low-rise sandbar of a barrier island rather than a glossy boulevard, the Lido di Venezia, a strip of Adriatic beach town with Art Deco hotels, bicycle rental stands, and a slower pace than Venice's tourist-choked calli.

The 83rd edition was set for September 2–12, 2026, with the Golden Lion competition and a jury headed by Maggie Gyllenhaal. The announced programme covered premieres and press conferences across the island, plus the kind of low-key celebrity-spotting that happens on a boardwalk rather than behind velvet ropes.

## Getting there

The Lido is reached only by water. From Piazza San Marco or the train station (Venezia Santa Lucia), vaporetto lines run directly to Lido S.M.E.. The trip from San Marco takes roughly 15–20 minutes, longer from the station. For the festival, extra ACTV boat services and shuttle buses were planned to supplement the regular routes between the Lido waterfront and the festival venues; exact schedules differ from year to year.

Once on the Lido, the festival hub, the Palazzo del Cinema and its Sala Grande, sits a short walk from the vaporetto stop, along with the Palazzo del Casinò and the Sala Darsena, the venues that were scheduled to host most competition screenings and premieres.

## What to see and do

Most festivalgoers without accreditation come for the atmosphere rather than the screenings themselves: the red-carpet arrivals outside Sala Grande in early evening, the paparazzi scrums along Lungomare Marconi, and the buzz around the Excelsior and Des Bains hotels, where cast and crews are based. The Mostra announced public tickets for many screenings, separate from the industry and press accreditation system, with allocations, prices, and the booking window changing annually.

Beyond the red carpet, the Lido rewards wandering: renting a bike, walking the beach promenade, or ducking into one of the small trattorias a few streets back from the festival buildings, where the pace stays unhurried even with a festival in full swing.

## When to go

The eleven days from September 2 to 12, 2026 spanned the full arc from opening ceremony to Golden Lion award night. The atmosphere shifts across a Mostra: the opening weekend and the final two or three days cluster the major premieres and the awards ceremony, drawing the thickest crowds and the most photographers, while the middle weekdays are calmer, with shorter lines at public screenings and more breathing room along the boardwalk.

## How to visit like a local

Venetians and Lido residents treat the festival as a normal part of late summer, not a novelty, so the etiquette is casual: dressing reasonably well suits anyone loitering near the red carpet, while gown-level formality belongs to those holding an actual premiere ticket. Payment on the Lido is overwhelmingly by card or phone tap, same as the rest of Venice, though some cash is useful at small bars and gelato stands that prefer it.

The accredited press and industry lines outside Sala Grande operate as separate systems from public ticketing. The most common visitor mistake is assuming the festival happens in central Venice: it doesn't, and travelers who skip the vaporetto ride to the Lido end up nowhere near the actual action.

A vaporetto multi-day pass (a standard ACTV tourist pass) beats single tickets for anyone commuting from the city center daily, and the last services back from the Lido fill up with the after-screening crowd.
