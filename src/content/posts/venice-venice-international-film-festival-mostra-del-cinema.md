## Why go

This is the oldest film festival on the planet, running since 1932, and it still feels like an event built for cinema rather than for Instagram. Unlike Cannes, the Mostra unfolds on a low-rise sandbar of a barrier island rather than a glossy boulevard — the Lido di Venezia, a strip of Adriatic beach town with Art Deco hotels, bicycle rental stands, and a slower pace than Venice's tourist-choked calli.

For the 83rd edition, September 2–12, 2026, the Golden Lion competition returns with a jury headed by Maggie Gyllenhaal, and the island fills with premieres, press conferences, and the kind of low-key celebrity-spotting that happens on a boardwalk rather than behind velvet ropes.

## Getting there

You reach the Lido only by water. From Piazza San Marco or the train station (Venezia Santa Lucia), vaporetto lines run directly to Lido S.M.E. — the trip from San Marco takes roughly 15–20 minutes, longer from the station. During the festival, extra ACTV boat services and shuttle buses typically supplement the regular routes to move the crowds between the Lido waterfront and the festival venues, though exact schedules shift year to year, so check ACTV's site closer to the date.

Once on the Lido, the festival hub — the Palazzo del Cinema and its Sala Grande — sits a short walk from the vaporetto stop, along with the Palazzo del Casinò and the Sala Darsena, the venues where most competition screenings and premieres take place.

## What to see and do

Most festivalgoers without accreditation come for the atmosphere rather than the screenings themselves: the red-carpet arrivals outside Sala Grande in early evening, the paparazzi scrums along Lungomare Marconi, and the buzz around the Excelsior and Des Bains hotels where cast and crews are based. If you do want to see films, the Mostra sells tickets to the general public for many screenings each edition, separate from the industry and press accreditation system — but allocations, prices, and the booking window change annually, so treat any number you read elsewhere as unconfirmed until you check labiennale.org directly.

Beyond the red carpet, wandering the Lido itself is worthwhile: rent a bike, walk the beach promenade, or duck into one of the small trattorias a few streets back from the festival buildings, where the pace stays unhurried even with a festival in full swing.

## When to go

The eleven days from September 2 to 12, 2026 cover the full arc from opening ceremony to Golden Lion award night. The atmosphere shifts noticeably across the run: the opening weekend and the final two or three days (when major premieres and the awards ceremony cluster) draw the thickest crowds and the most photographers, while the middle weekdays are calmer, with shorter lines at the public screenings and more breathing room along the boardwalk.

If your goal is soaking up the atmosphere without elbowing through a crowd, aim for a Tuesday-to-Thursday stretch mid-festival rather than the first or last weekend.

## How to visit like a local

Venetians and Lido residents treat the festival as a normal part of late summer, not a novelty, so the etiquette is casual: dress reasonably well if you're hoping to loiter near the red carpet, but nobody expects gown-level formality unless you actually hold a premiere ticket. Payment on the Lido is overwhelmingly by card or phone tap, same as the rest of Venice, though it's worth carrying some cash for small bars and gelato stands that prefer it.

Don't bother trying to "queue-jump" the accredited press and industry lines outside Sala Grande — they're separate systems from public ticketing, and locals know to simply pick a spot along the barriers early rather than push toward the front. The most common visitor mistake is assuming the festival happens in central Venice: it doesn't, and travelers who skip the vaporetto ride to the Lido end up nowhere near the actual action.

Buy your vaporetto multi-day pass (a standard ACTV tourist pass) rather than single tickets if you're commuting from the city center daily, and plan your return boat before dark, since the last services back from the Lido can queue up with the after-screening crowd. Always double-check the current year's exact venue list, screening schedule, and ticket-release dates on the official Biennale Cinema site before finalizing your trip, since these details are set and updated by the festival itself each year.
