## Why go

You don't need to be in New Orleans during Carnival season to understand what Mardi Gras actually looks like — you just need to walk into this cavernous warehouse on the river's edge. Mardi Gras World is Blaine Kern Studios' working production facility, where the floats for Krewe of Bacchus, Endymion, and dozens of other parades get sculpted, painted, and stored between years.

Step through the doors and the scale hits you first: 400,000 square feet of workshop space crowded with papier-mâché giants — a two-story genie, a grinning jester head taller than a bus, gold-leafed dragons mid-roar. Some are half-finished, foam ribs showing before the fiberglass skin goes on. It's part art studio, part storage yard, part theater set, and it smells faintly of fresh paint and river air.

## Getting there

The address is 1380 Port of New Orleans Place, tucked behind the Ernest N. Morial Convention Center on the Mississippi's edge — an easy 10 to 15-minute walk from the Warehouse District or a short ride from the French Quarter. Most visitors either walk along the riverfront path from the Convention Center (following the levee, you'll see the building's signage before you reach it) or take the free shuttle that Mardi Gras World runs from a pickup point near Riverwalk and the Spanish Plaza — check with your hotel concierge or the venue for the current shuttle schedule, since these details shift.

If you'd rather not walk, a short rideshare from the Quarter or the Garden District runs only a few minutes given the light traffic along the riverfront.

## What to see and do

Tours leave roughly every hour throughout the day and start with a short video on Carnival history before turning you loose in the working den itself. You'll walk past sculptors' benches thick with paint splatter, foam-carving stations, and rows of finished float sections waiting for their season. Look for the recurring cast of characters — Bacchus's giant head, the Baby New Year, camels and kings from the Twelfth Night traditions — many of which appear again and again in different parades because the studio reuses and repaints float bodies year after year.

Photo ops are everywhere: you're encouraged to climb onto float steps, pose with oversized props, even try on sequined Carnival costumes in a dress-up area. King cake, the purple-green-gold iced pastry that's a Carnival season staple, embodies that same spirit, giving you a taste of the culture along with the visuals.

## When to go

The building is open daily, so there's flexibility in planning around it. Because it draws heavy tour-bus and cruise-ship traffic (many cruise passengers stop here before boarding ships docked nearby), late morning through early afternoon tends to be the busiest stretch. Arriving at or shortly after the 9am opening, or catching one of the last couple of tours before 5:30pm closing, generally means smaller groups and more elbow room among the floats.

## How to visit like a local

This is a well-known, well-visited attraction rather than a quiet find, so the real skill is timing and logistics rather than discovery. Book your tour slot in advance online if you can — walk-ups are accepted, but pre-booking guarantees you a spot on the hour you want and skips any line at the desk. Tours run on a fixed schedule, so ask what time the next one departs when you arrive rather than assuming you can just wander in; entry is tour-guided, not self-directed.

Most visitors pay by card at the counter, and there's no tipping expectation for the tour guides beyond the usual U.S. norm of a few dollars if a guide goes out of their way. The mistake first-timers make is treating this as a quick photo stop — the tour itself, workshop floor included, easily fills an hour, and if you linger in the gift shop and costume area afterward you can stretch a visit past ninety minutes.

Combine it with a riverfront walk toward the Convention Center or Spanish Plaza afterward, since you're already down by the water and the walk back into the Warehouse District's galleries and restaurants is flat and scenic.
