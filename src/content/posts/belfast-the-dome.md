## A glass lid over the shopping centre

Victoria Square is a covered shopping centre that opened in 2008. The Dome is its roof: a glass and steel hemisphere above the atrium with a viewing gallery inside it. You ride up through the shop floors, and at the top the city opens out on every side.

You don't need a ticket desk or a guide. The gallery is a ring of walkway pressed against the glass, so you walk the full circle and Belfast turns past you like a slow panorama. It's a short visit with a big payoff, and it suits a rainy day because you're under glass the whole time.

## What you can pick out from the top

Belfast is low and compact, so most of its landmarks show up from this height. Walk the circle and look for:

- **Samson and Goliath**, the two yellow Harland & Wolff gantry cranes, out east beyond the River Lagan
- **Cave Hill** to the north, the basalt ridge locals call Napoleon's Nose for its profile
- **Black Mountain** and the Belfast Hills along the western skyline
- **Belfast City Hall** and its green copper dome to the south-west
- **The Albert Memorial Clock**, Belfast's leaning clock tower, close by towards the river

Clear days show you the most of Belfast Lough. When the cloud is low the hills disappear, but the rooftops of the centre are still close enough to read.

## Timing the climb around the opening hours

Hours are the same Monday to Saturday, 10am to 6pm. Sunday is shorter: nothing opens before 1pm, so a Sunday-morning plan won't work here. Check the Victoria Square website before you go, because hours can change around holidays and events.

The light is part of the reason to time it well. In winter Belfast gets dark mid-afternoon, so a visit just before 6pm lets you watch the city lights come on. In summer the sun is still high at closing, and morning visits avoid looking straight into it from the western side of the walkway.

Victoria Square sits between Chichester Street and Ann Street. From Lanyon Place station it's roughly a ten-minute walk west, and the Glider bus routes stop on the streets around the city centre.

## Doing the Dome the Belfast way

Treat it as a pause in a longer walk, not the whole outing. A loop most people manage in a morning:

- **St George's Market** on May Street, a Victorian covered market busiest from Friday to Sunday
- **The Albert Memorial Clock** in Queen's Square, a few minutes north-east
- **The Cathedral Quarter**, with its pubs and murals around Commercial Court, just beyond

Inside the centre, follow the signs for The Dome from the upper levels. Shop staff will point you up if you lose your way. Keep your voice down near the glass, because the gallery is narrow and carries sound. Lean in towards the panes rather than standing back from them, which keeps reflections out of your photos. Northern Irish shops take contactless payment almost everywhere, so you won't need cash for a coffee in the food court after.

A lot of visitors see the Lagan from the Titanic Quarter side and never get the view back across the city. Up here you can follow the river from the cranes to the centre and work out the walk you just did.
