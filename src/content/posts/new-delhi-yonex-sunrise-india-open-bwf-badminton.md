## Several courts at once, then just one

Early in the week, the arena floor holds several courts at once. Shuttles go up from three or four rallies side by side. Line judges sit on low chairs at the edges, and a scoreboard hangs over each court.

By the weekend the floor has been cleared down to a single show court. The same bowl that felt like a trade fair on day one now points every seat at one net. That shift in mood is the reason many fans come for both ends of the week.

This is a BWF World Tour Super 750 event, one tier below the season's top tournaments. That ranking draws top international players. It also brings strong groups of travelling fans from East and Southeast Asia, so you will see flags and chants from several countries in the stands.

## Dates and how the week runs

The 2027 edition takes place in mid-January, in its usual slot early in the badminton calendar. The Badminton Association of India hosts it, and the organiser's official site carries the day-by-day dates and the ticket links.

Super 750 tournaments typically follow the same pattern:

- **Early rounds:** a full day on several courts, with men's and women's singles plus men's, women's and mixed doubles all running together.
- **Quarter-finals:** fewer matches, and the crowd builds.
- **Semi-finals and finals:** one show court, with all five titles decided on the last day.

Many visitors buy early-round sessions because they can watch several matches at once, and finals day for the atmosphere. On the early days, a ticket often gets you an afternoon of court-hopping without leaving your seat.

## Getting to Indira Gandhi Arena

The arena sits inside the Indira Gandhi Sports Complex, near ITO in central-east Delhi. It was built for the 1982 Asian Games and is one of the largest indoor arenas in India. From the outside it is a broad concrete drum.

**Indraprastha Metro station (Blue Line)** is the closest station, about a 10–15 minute walk away. ITO station on the Violet Line also works if you are coming from the south. The Delhi Metro is how most spectators arrive, because traffic around the ITO junction is heavy in the evening.

Auto-rickshaws and app cabs drop off along the Ring Road side. After the night sessions, the crowd walking back to Indraprastha thins out quickly, and lines at the metro gates move fast.

## Between rallies: crowd etiquette at the arena

Badminton crowds follow a rhythm, and the regulars keep to it. Everyone goes quiet the moment a player sets up to serve. The noise only starts once the shuttle hits the floor.

- **Moving seats:** people wait for the end of a rally, or better, the change of ends, before walking along a row.
- **Photography:** flash is off for the whole match. The players are tracking a white shuttle under bright lights.
- **Cheering:** Indian players get the loudest roars. Visiting fan groups answer with their own chants, and good rallies get applause from both sides.
- **Security:** a bag check runs at the entrance, so fans tend to bring a small bag and keep power banks to a minimum.

Phones held up for long rally videos block the rows behind. Most people film the final points of a game, not the whole match.

## Delhi in January

Mid-January is the cold heart of Delhi's winter. Mornings are often foggy, and that fog regularly delays flights and long-distance trains into the city. Many visitors build in a spare day for this reason.

Inside, the arena is a sheltered place to spend a winter afternoon. Outside, evenings turn cold quickly, so regulars carry a jacket for the walk back to the metro. Air quality can be poor at this time of year, and some travellers pack a well-fitted mask.

## Around ITO between sessions

The arena sits close to several landmarks of central Delhi, which helps fill the gaps between sessions.

- **Raj Ghat**, the memorial to Mahatma Gandhi, is a short ride south-east along the Ring Road.
- **Purana Qila** and **Pragati Maidan** lie a little further south, toward India Gate.
- **Daryaganj** and **Old Delhi**, with Jama Masjid and the lanes of Chandni Chowk, are north of ITO.

Visitors usually stay around **Connaught Place**, which is a few metro stops away and has the widest choice of hotels and restaurants. From there, a ride on the Blue Line puts you at Indraprastha without changing lines.
