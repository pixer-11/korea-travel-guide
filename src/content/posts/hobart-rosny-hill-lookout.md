## The city, seen from across the water

Most Hobart views look out from the city. This one looks back at it. From the top of Rosny Hill on the eastern shore, the whole western bank is laid out in one frame: the Derwent River, the long curve of the Tasman Bridge, the CBD and Sullivans Cove, and kunanyi/Mount Wellington rising behind everything.

That layering is why the lookout rates so consistently well. The mountain fills the back of the picture, the city sits at its foot, and the water runs through the foreground. You get the scale of Hobart in a few seconds, which is harder to do from the summit of kunanyi, where the city shrinks to a map below you.

The hill is a bushland reserve on the edge of the suburbs, so this is not a built-up attraction. It's a car park, a viewing area and the open hillside around it. Come for the view and the air, not for facilities.

## Getting up Rosny Hill

The lookout sits just over the Tasman Bridge from central Hobart. The address Google gives is 49 Leura St, Rosny TAS 7018. By car it's a short drive from the CBD: cross the bridge eastbound on the Tasman Highway and follow the signs up the hill.

Without a car, take a Metro Tasmania bus to the Rosny Park interchange beside Eastlands shopping centre. From there it's an uphill walk to the top. The climb is steady rather than steep, but leave time for it and bring water.

- **Drive:** Tasman Bridge, then up from the Rosny side
- **Bus:** Metro to Rosny Park interchange, then walk
- **Time on site:** 20–30 minutes for the view, longer with a walk

## Choosing your light

Timing changes this view more than anything else. The lookout faces west across the river, so mornings put the sun behind you. Low light falls straight onto the city and the mountain, and photos come out sharp and evenly lit.

In the evening the sun drops behind kunanyi. The mountain turns into a dark outline and the sky behind it takes the colour. After dark the city lights and the lit-up bridge show across the water. Google currently lists the lookout as open 24 hours, but check conditions before a night visit, and remember the hill can be windy and cold at any time of year.

## The hill's quiet hours

It's an under-the-radar spot by Hobart standards, but it still has a rhythm. BestTime's forecast of typical foot traffic puts the calmest windows at **7am–8am on weekdays** and **7am–11am on weekends**.

Try not to arrive after 11am on a weekend. Weekends usually stay busy from then until 11pm, so the car park fills and the viewing area gets crowded. Weekend sunsets fall inside that stretch. If you want that light with fewer people, a weekday evening is the better bet.

## Up the hill without the weekend crowd

A few habits make the visit smoother:

- **Pull in tidily.** The car park is small. Leave space for others and don't idle across the turning area while you take one photo.
- **Stay on the tracks.** The reserve is native bushland, so keep to the formed paths and take your rubbish down with you.
- **Pack a layer.** The wind comes straight off the Derwent. Even a mild day in the city can feel sharp at the top.
- **Use the place name in two languages.** Tasmanians increasingly say kunanyi for Mount Wellington, and signs often show both.
- **Don't rush the frame.** Give the view a few minutes. Ferries crossing, traffic on the bridge and cloud moving over the mountain all change the picture.

People here come up for a quiet look rather than a spectacle. Keep your voice and your music low. That's especially true early in the morning, when walkers and dog owners have the hill to themselves.

## Making a half-day of the eastern shore

The lookout fits easily into a wider loop of Hobart's eastern shore. Bellerive is a short drive or a longish walk south. Its waterfront boardwalk looks back toward the city at water level, and Bellerive Oval, Tasmania's main cricket and football ground, is nearby.

Above Bellerive, the Kangaroo Bluff Historic Site preserves a nineteenth-century coastal fort built to guard the river. Back in Rosny, Eastlands is handy for supplies or a coffee before you head up. Pair a morning on the hill with lunch in Bellerive and you'll have seen Hobart from the side most visitors skip.
