## Over the ridge instead of around it

Most traffic between Tbilisi and Kakheti takes the lowland highway through Sagarejo and Gurjaani. The Gombori road cuts straight over the range instead, and it is the noticeably shorter route to Telavi. Your price for that is hairpins.

The road climbs from the Iori valley through thick beech and hornbeam forest. Near the top there are wide gravel shoulders where drivers pull over. On the far side the land falls away to the Alazani Valley, with vineyards and villages on the valley floor. On a clear day the white wall of the Greater Caucasus rises behind them.

## What to stop for along the road

The pass makes sense as a chain of short stops, not as a single destination.

- **Ujarma Fortress**: a restored medieval stronghold above the Iori River, on the Tbilisi side of the climb.
- **The summit pull-offs**: cooler air, forest views, and the occasional roadside seller in season.
- **The descent toward Telavi**: the long valley views open up here. Use a proper pull-off; never stop on a bend.
- **Telavi itself**: the Batonis Tsikhe palace complex and the old centre are at the bottom of the road.

Leave Tbilisi in the morning and you can cross the pass, stop at Ujarma and be in Telavi for lunch. You still have the afternoon for wineries in the Alazani Valley.

## Driving the Gombori road

There is no gate, no ticket and no closing time. It is a road, open around the clock. That doesn't make every hour a good one. The curves are tight and some stretches are unlit, and Georgian overtaking can be bold. Plan to cross in daylight.

- **Fog** often settles over the top even when Tbilisi and Telavi are clear. Slow down and put your lights on.
- **Winter snow and ice** can make the pass hard going or shut it for a while. Check local road reports or ask your guesthouse before you set off.
- **Livestock** wanders onto the road, mostly cows on the open stretches.
- **Fuel** up before the climb. Don't count on stations at the top.

Shared minibuses (marshrutkas) between Tbilisi and Telavi often take this route. If you don't want to drive, a hired driver is the easy way to make stops wherever you like.

## Timing the views and the quiet

The pass is still quiet. It's a road people drive through, not a coach-tour stop, so the pull-offs are rarely crowded. What decides your visit is the light, not the crowds.

- **Late spring** brings green forest and snow still on the Caucasus peaks.
- **Autumn** turns the beeches copper and coincides with the Kakheti grape harvest (*rtveli*) below.
- **Morning** air tends to be clearer for the long mountain views. Haze builds as the day warms.
- **Summer** is a few degrees cooler at the top than in the valleys. Bring a layer.

Treat the forest edge with respect. Take your rubbish with you, because the pull-offs have few bins. If you share a pull-off with Georgian families picnicking, a nod and a *gamarjoba* (hello) is normal. Their offer of a glass of something homemade is generous, but the driver should turn it down. Georgia's drink-driving rules are strict.
