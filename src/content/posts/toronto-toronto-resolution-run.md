## Five kilometres before the year gets going

The Toronto Resolution Run starts 2027 at a jog. It is a 5K road race held at Exhibition Place on January 1, 2027. That's the morning most of the city is still asleep, and it's a deliberate way to turn a resolution into a finish time before lunch.

A 5K suits the date. Experienced runners can push for a time, and newer runners can complete it at an easy pace. Walkers get round too. Distance and date are the fixed points. The organiser publishes registration, start times and course details on its official race page, and this guide sticks to what holds true either way.

## Exhibition Place in midwinter

In January, Exhibition Place doesn't look like the end-of-summer fairground of the CNE. The grounds cover a large stretch of land between Lake Shore Boulevard West and the Gardiner Expressway. The roads are wide and flat, and in winter they're mostly empty, which is exactly why a road race works here.

You'll pass landmarks most Torontonians know by sight:

- **Princes' Gates**, the triumphal arch at the eastern entrance, finished in 1927
- **BMO Field**, home of Toronto FC and the Argonauts
- **Enercare Centre** and **Beanfield Centre**, the big exhibition halls
- **Coca-Cola Coliseum**, the arena where the Toronto Marlies play

Lake Ontario sits just south across Lake Shore Boulevard. That's the source of the wind you'll feel on any exposed stretch.

## Reaching the grounds on a holiday morning

New Year's Day transit in Toronto runs on a holiday schedule, which affects how runners plan their trip. There are three main options:

- **TTC streetcar:** the 509 Harbourfront runs west from Union Station to Exhibition Loop. The 511 Bathurst comes down from Bathurst station on Line 2 to the same loop.
- **GO Transit:** Exhibition station on the Lakeshore West line sits at the north edge of the grounds, one stop from Union.
- **Driving:** Exhibition Place has its own paid surface lots and garages, with access from Lake Shore Boulevard West and Strachan Avenue.

Runners from out of town often stay downtown near Union Station. From there, the streetcar or GO train covers the trip in minutes.

## Dressing for a lakefront 5K in January

January in Toronto usually means below-freezing temperatures. A breeze off the lake makes it feel colder still. Ice and packed snow are common on sidewalks, and race-day roads are often patchy even after salting.

Experienced winter runners tend to dress for the second kilometre, not the start line. Here's what they usually wear:

- a moisture-wicking base layer and a light windproof shell
- tights or lined running pants
- gloves or mittens, plus a hat or headband that covers the ears
- a buff or neck gaiter to pull over the face into the wind
- shoes with decent grip, and sometimes traction spikes when the forecast mentions ice

A dry layer and warm socks waiting in a bag make the walk back to transit far more pleasant.

## Race-morning habits at Exhibition Place

Holiday races have their own rhythm. Runners usually leave themselves a cushion for the slower holiday transit and for finding their way across the grounds, because the venue is large. The walk from Princes' Gates to the western end takes longer than first-timers expect.

Toronto runners tend to warm up indoors or in short loops nearby, then shed layers at the last minute. Corral etiquette is the same as at any Canadian road race. Faster runners line up near the front, walkers and families stay towards the back, and people step to the side before stopping on the course.

Paying for transit is straightforward. Most riders tap a PRESTO card, or a credit or debit card, on TTC and GO readers. New Year's Day is a statutory holiday in Ontario, so many shops and cafés near the grounds keep shorter hours or stay closed.

## After the finish line

A 5K at Exhibition Place leaves the rest of the day free, and the waterfront is right there. The Martin Goodman Trail runs along the lake just south of the grounds. It makes an easy cool-down walk with open views over the water.

Liberty Village is just north of the Gardiner, across the rail corridor, a short walk from the grounds. Its converted industrial blocks hold plenty of brunch spots, though holiday hours vary from place to place. Further east, Fort York National Historic Site and Harbourfront are an easy streetcar ride back towards Union Station. Many runners spend the afternoon resting their legs at home, which counts as a successful first day of the year too.
