Beytepe isn't where you'd expect Ankara's next big dining story to break. It's a quieter district best known for Hacettepe University's campus and its proximity to Bilkent, more residential than nightlife hub. That's part of what makes Botka Meyhane's sudden buzz notable: it's pulling people out of the usual Kızılay and Çankaya circuits to a part of town that hasn't historically been a destination for a night out.

## What makes Botka stand out

A meyhane, in Turkish tradition, is a tavern built around meze, rakı, and long, unhurried tables shared with friends. Botka takes that structure and gives it a contemporary shell. The building itself is part of the draw: reports describe a stylish, architecturally distinct space that reads more like a design project than a typical tavern conversion.

Inside that frame, the kitchen leans into fusion, reworking classic meze plates rather than replicating them exactly. If you know meyhane culture from Istanbul or the older spots in Ankara's center, expect the vocabulary to be familiar but the execution to feel newer, plated with more attention to presentation than tradition usually calls for.

## The garden and the fire

The other reason Botka keeps coming up is its garden. It's spacious enough to function as a genuine outdoor dining area rather than a token patio, and in winter the seating shifts toward fire, with fireside tables that let you sit outside even once temperatures drop. Ankara winters are dry and cold, so a well-run fireside setup is a real amenity, not just atmosphere.

That combination, indoor architecture worth noticing plus an outdoor space that works across seasons, is unusual enough locally that it's driving word of mouth on its own.

## Getting to Beytepe

Beytepe sits near Bilkent University and is most easily reached by car or taxi from central Ankara; expect a drive of roughly 20-30 minutes from Kızılay depending on traffic. If you're relying on public transport, check current EGO bus routes serving Bilkent and Hacettepe's Beytepe campus, since a meyhane in a university-adjacent neighborhood will likely sit close to those lines. Given it's a newer opening, confirm the exact address with a map app or by calling ahead before you set out.

## Ordering and sitting like a regular

Meyhane etiquette in Turkey has its own rhythm, and it's worth knowing before you sit down:

- Meze arrive first and are meant to be shared across the table, not ordered as individual plates
- Rakı, diluted with water and often ice, is the traditional pairing, though wine and beer are common alternatives
- A meal is expected to stretch over a couple of hours; nobody rushes courses
- Tipping is customary, generally rounding up or leaving a modest percentage in cash

Given the fusion menu, ask your server which meze are the reworked versions versus the closer-to-classic ones if you want a sense of what's traditional and what's Botka's own spin.

## Before you go

Because Botka is a recent opening, published hours, exact prices, and reservation policies weren't independently confirmed for this guide. Meyhanes in Turkey typically run dinner and late-evening hours rather than lunch, but don't assume that pattern holds here without checking. Call ahead or check the restaurant's current listing to confirm opening hours and whether a reservation is needed, especially for a fireside garden table on a cold evening when demand for that seating will be highest.

If you're combining the trip with other Beytepe or Bilkent errands, plan to arrive in early evening so you're not guessing at a closing time.
