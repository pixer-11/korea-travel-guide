## A sailors' church on the peninsula's last hill

Penha Hill is the final rise before the Macau peninsula drops into the water at Barra. The chapel stands on its crown, a pale stone church with a single bell tower. Its terrace looks out over the harbour that its first congregation sailed from.

The original chapel dates to 1622. Crew from a ship that slipped past the Dutch are said to have founded it, and it became the place where seafarers prayed before heading out. The building you see now is the 1935 rebuild, small enough that you can take in the whole nave from the door.

Next door is the Bishop's Residence, whose walls frame the courtyard. Below the terrace you'll find a statue of Our Lady of Lourdes set in a grotto.

## What you see from the top

The terrace is why most people make the climb. From the railings the view runs:

- **North-east:** Nam Van Lake and the Macau Tower
- **South:** the Sai Van and Governador Nobre de Carvalho bridges arching across to Taipa
- **West:** the Inner Harbour, with Zhuhai close enough to make out its apartment blocks

Inside, the chapel is plain and quiet: wooden pews, an altar to Our Lady of Penha, and model ships that nod to the sailors' devotion. For most visitors, ten minutes in the nave and half an hour on the terrace is enough.

## Getting up Penha Hill

The hill is a walk, not a ride. Most routes start in the old Lilau quarter, near Lilau Square and the Mandarin's House. From there, Calçada da Penha and the lanes off it climb steeply past colonial villas for about 10 to 15 minutes.

You can also go up from A-Ma Temple at the foot of Barra. Visit the temple first, then climb, and it makes a tidy half-day. Taxis can drop you near the top if the gradient is a problem.

The doors open at 10am and close at 4pm every day of the week, with no day off. Plan around that 4pm close. A late-afternoon walk up can leave you on the terrace after the chapel has shut, and the courtyard may close with it.

## Visiting a working chapel

This is an active place of worship, not a museum, so a few habits make the visit go smoothly.

- **Dress:** cover your shoulders before you go inside, even in Macau's summer heat.
- **Voices and cameras:** keep your voice low in the nave and follow any photography signs at the door. Save the wide shots for the terrace.
- **Mass and services:** if a service is on, stand at the back or come back later rather than walk the aisle.
- **13 May:** on this date the Our Lady of Fátima procession winds from St Dominic's Church up to Penha by candlelight. The hilltop fills that evening, so pick another day if you want quiet. Go on the day if you want to see the procession.

The hill doesn't get the cruise-group crowds of Senado Square. On an ordinary weekday you may share the terrace with a few joggers and a couple of photographers. Arriving soon after 10am gives you the softest morning light over the lake. Bring water: there are no shops on the summit.
