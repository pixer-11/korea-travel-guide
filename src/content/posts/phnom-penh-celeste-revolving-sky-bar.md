## What makes it the first of its kind

Celeste isn't just another rooftop with a view. It occupies the 43rd floor of The Penthouse Residence, one of Phnom Penh's taller towers, and its claim to fame is mechanical, not just architectural: the terrace itself turns.

Two levels rotate slowly, so you don't need to walk the rail to see the whole city. Sit in one spot for an hour and you'll pass over the Mekong, the Tonle Sap confluence, and the low-rise sprawl toward the airport in sequence.

That's a genuine engineering rarity. Revolving restaurants exist elsewhere, but a revolving open-air sky bar, built specifically for outdoor drinking and viewing rather than a sealed dining room, is a first.

## Getting the angle right

The dual-level design matters for how you plan your visit. The upper terrace and lower terrace rotate at different points, so ask staff which level is turning when you arrive if a specific view matters to you.

Phnom Penh has no other building close to this footprint downtown, so the sightlines are unobstructed in most directions. That's rare in a city where riverside towers tend to block each other.

- Riverside and Tonle Sap confluence: best on the side facing the water
- Diamond Island (Koh Pich) towers: visible on the loop toward the southeast
- Sunset light: strongest in the 20-30 minutes before full dark

Because the whole point is the rotation, arriving right at the start of your reservation window gets you a full cycle rather than a partial one if you're on a schedule.

## Booking and arrival, before you go

This is a recently opened venue, and details like hours, dress code, and whether walk-ins are accepted are the kind of thing that shift in the first months of operation. Confirm current opening hours and reservation policy directly before heading over, ideally the same day.

Being 43 floors up in a residential tower means there's a lobby check-in and elevator process, not a street-level walk-up. Build in extra time versus a ground-floor bar, especially if there's a guest list or security check tied to reservations.

If you're coming for sunset, that's the highest-demand slot in any rooftop bar in the city, so book ahead rather than assume you'll get a table on arrival.

## Fitting it into a Phnom Penh evening

Celeste rewards being the anchor of your evening rather than a quick stop. The rotation means a single seat gives you variety, so there's little reason to rush.

Pair it with a riverside walk near Sisowath Quay beforehand, since that's the closest historic waterfront strip and a natural staging point before heading to a high-rise bar. Dress code at towers like this tends to skew smart-casual rather than beachwear, though confirm specifics when you book.

Because it's a newly trending spot, expect other visitors to be there specifically for the novelty of the rotation, not just the skyline. That means a slightly more photo-heavy, first-time-visitor crowd than an old-guard expat bar, which changes the energy compared to Phnom Penh's longer-running rooftops.
