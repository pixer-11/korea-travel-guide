Jl. Pantai Pererenan has turned into one of the busiest restaurant strips near Canggu, and Cocomo sits right in the middle of that energy. You'll know you've found it before you read the sign: a flamingo statue several times taller than a person stands out front, pink against the palm trees and motorbike traffic.

That statue isn't just a photo prop. It sets the tone for the whole place, which leans into an island-holiday theme rather than the minimalist concrete-and-rattan look that dominates so much of Canggu's dining scene.

## What the room feels like

Inside, Cocomo goes for playful over polished. Expect bold colour, tropical motifs and a layout built for groups rather than solo diners hunched over laptops.

Tables are arranged for sharing, and the noise level reflects that: this is a place that fills with conversation and clinking glasses once the sun goes down, not a quiet corner for a working lunch. If you want a mellow, subdued meal, this isn't it. If you want somewhere that photographs well and feels like a night out, it delivers.

## The food: built for sharing

The signature move here is the butter-aged Angus tenderloin, served as a centrepiece for the table rather than a single-plate main. That framing runs through the menu: dishes are designed to land in the middle of the table and get passed around, not eaten solo.

- Come with at least three or four people if you want to try a real spread
- Order in rounds rather than all at once, since sharing plates work best staggered
- Ask staff which dishes are meant to be split versus individual, as the format can vary by course

Menus and pricing at newer Canggu restaurants change often as kitchens settle in, so treat any specific dish list you see online as a starting point and confirm with staff on the day.

## Finding it on Jl. Pantai Pererenan

Jl. Pantai Pererenan runs from the main Canggu bypass road down toward Pererenan Beach, and it's now dense with restaurants, beach clubs and boutique shops on both sides. Cocomo's flamingo makes it one of the easier spots on the street to spot, even in patchy phone signal or after dark.

Traffic on this road backs up badly in the late afternoon and early evening as beachgoers head to sunset spots nearby. If you're driving a scooter or booking a car, build in extra time, and consider walking the last stretch if your accommodation is within Canggu or Pererenan itself.

## Booking a table

Given how much foot traffic this part of Pererenan sees, and how central sharing plates like the tenderloin are to the concept, reserving ahead is the safer move rather than a formality. A table booked for a specific time also means your shared dishes can be timed properly instead of arriving in a rush.

Contact details and current reservation channels change, so check Cocomo's own listing or a recent booking platform for the live phone number or online form before you go. Confirm opening hours the same way, since venues on this strip sometimes shift their schedule seasonally.

## Eating like you belong on Pererenan

Dinner on this street runs late by most visitors' home-country standards; locals and long-term expats tend to eat closer to 8 or 9pm rather than at 6.

Splitting the bill is normal practice among groups here, and most restaurants on this stretch of Canggu accept cards alongside cash, though it's worth carrying some rupiah as backup in case of connectivity issues with card machines. Tipping isn't obligatory in Bali, but rounding up or leaving a small amount for good service is appreciated and increasingly expected at restaurants aimed at international visitors.

If you're arriving by scooter, parking along Jl. Pantai Pererenan can be tight once the evening crowd sets in; arriving before the dinner rush usually means an easier spot right outside rather than a longer walk from further down the road.
