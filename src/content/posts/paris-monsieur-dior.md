## Lunch at a couture address

Your meal starts at the front door of a fashion house. 30 Avenue Montaigne is the address Christian Dior chose for his house in 1946. His first collection, the one the press called the New Look, was shown there in February 1947. Monsieur Dior sits inside that same building.

This is a fashion flagship with a serious kitchen inside it. Yannick Alléno runs the food. He is the chef behind Pavillon Ledoyen, one of the most decorated kitchens in the city, and at Dior he serves a tasting menu.

## What the Michelin star actually says

The star came fast. Monsieur Dior earned its first Michelin star in 2026, seven months after opening. That is quick for any restaurant, and quicker still for one that lives inside a shop.

The inspectors' citation is worth reading closely. They recognised "the fusion of the creative universe of the House of Dior and the sublime cuisine of Chef Yannick Alléno." They mention the house and the chef together, on purpose. The star rewards how the two fit together, so the setting is part of what you are booking.

## A tasting menu cut like a collection

Monsieur Dior describes its menu as a collection. Expect a sequence of dishes, presented in order the way looks come down a runway. The idea is to mix haute couture showmanship with precise, technical French cooking.

We are not going to list the dishes. The menu has not been published in a form we can confirm, and a menu built as a collection is likely to change. What you can count on:

- **Format:** a tasting menu, not à la carte grazing
- **Style:** high-precision French cooking from a multi-starred chef
- **Pace:** a multi-course meal, so leave the afternoon or evening open
- **Concept:** fashion is part of the plating and storytelling, not just the room

Ask about dietary needs when you book, not at the table. Tasting menus are planned in advance, so the kitchen needs the warning.

## Avenue Montaigne around the meal

The street is a good reason to come early. Avenue Montaigne runs from the Rond-Point des Champs-Élysées down to Place de l'Alma. It is lined with couture flagships, with tall Haussmann stone façades and wrought-iron balconies above the shop windows.

Along the avenue and nearby:

- **La Galerie Dior**, the house's museum, is in the same complex, with its entrance on Rue François 1er. It makes sense to visit before or after you eat.
- **Théâtre des Champs-Élysées**, at 15 Avenue Montaigne, has an Art Deco façade with reliefs by Antoine Bourdelle.
- **Pont de l'Alma and the Seine** are at the bottom of the avenue, with the Eiffel Tower across the river.
- **Grand Palais and Petit Palais** are a short walk east, across Avenue Winston Churchill.

## Getting to 30 Montaigne

The Métro is the easiest way in. Two stations are within a few minutes' walk:

- **Franklin D. Roosevelt** (Lines 1 and 9): walk down Avenue Montaigne from the Rond-Point end.
- **Alma-Marceau** (Line 9): come up from Place de l'Alma at the Seine end.

From either one the walk is flat and easy, past the shop windows. Taxis and ride-hails can drop you on the avenue. Street parking in the Golden Triangle is scarce, so don't count on finding a space.

## Booking a table at Dior

Treat this as a reservation-only meal until Dior tells you otherwise. A brand-new restaurant with a Michelin star and a famous name gets booked up. Reserve through Dior's official channels as early as you can, and confirm the opening days and service times when you do. We have not been given hours, and a restaurant this new may still change its schedule.

A few Paris habits help here:

- **Arrive on time.** At tasting-menu restaurants the meal is paced, and arriving late squeezes every course after it.
- **Greet the staff with "Bonjour" first**, then switch to English if you need to. It sets the tone in any Paris dining room.
- **Service is included in the bill** in France (*service compris*). If you want to tip, leave a little extra for good service. It is a thank-you, not an obligation.
- **Cards are the norm** at this level. Check the total on the terminal before you tap.
- **Ask for prices when you book.** We have no published figures to give you. Dior's official site or the reservation confirmation is where to find the current price of the menu.

On dress, the address speaks for itself. Nobody has published a dress code we can confirm, but you are eating inside a couture house. Polished clothes will fit the room better than sightseeing gear.

## Is it worth building a day around?

It depends on what you want from the meal. If you want a famous chef's tasting menu in a setting that is part of fashion history, this is one of the most interesting new tables in Paris. The star came quickly, and the citation praises the setting and the cooking together.

If you just want a good bistro lunch near the Champs-Élysées, it is the wrong fit. This is a long, planned meal. Pair it with La Galerie Dior and a walk down to the Seine and you have a full half day on one street.
