## Nine days, dozens of small stories

A short film festival works on a different clock. One sitting can take you from a documentary to an animation to a five-minute comedy before the lights come up. For nine days in December, Macau gives that format a full festival.

The Macau International Short Film Festival runs from **4 to 12 December 2026**. Public screenings, masterclasses and a gala awards ceremony take place at venues spread across the city. It is a compact festival in a compact place, and for a visitor that is the appeal.

## What the programme holds

The festival has three strands, and each one suits a different kind of visitor.

- **Public screenings:** the main event. Shorts are usually grouped into themed or competition blocks, so you watch several films in one sitting.
- **Masterclasses:** sessions where people from the industry talk about their craft. These suit anyone who makes films or studies them.
- **Gala awards ceremony:** the night the competition ends and prizes are handed out.

The organiser publishes the full programme, the venue for each session and the ticket links. The festival covers several venues, so the schedule matters as much as the film titles. A block in one district and a masterclass in another can be a short taxi ride apart or a bus across the bridge to Taipa.

## Reaching Macau in December

December is one of Macau's most comfortable months. The air is drier and cooler than during the humid summer, which makes walking between venues easy. Evenings can feel cool by the water, so festivalgoers usually carry a light layer.

Most international visitors come through Hong Kong or Zhuhai:

- **By ferry:** boats from the Hong Kong–Macau Ferry Terminal in Sheung Wan arrive at the Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal on the peninsula or the Taipa Ferry Terminal.
- **By road:** shuttle buses cross the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge from the Hong Kong port to the Macau port.
- **From mainland China:** the Gongbei–Border Gate crossing links Zhuhai with northern Macau.
- **By air:** Macau International Airport on Taipa has regional connections around Asia.

Once you are in Macau, the Light Rail Transit's Taipa line serves Taipa and Cotai. Public buses cover the peninsula.

## Sitting through a block of shorts

Short film audiences behave a little differently from a multiplex crowd. Because several films play back to back, many people stay seated through the brief gaps between titles. Walking out halfway through a block disturbs the next film, not just the one you left. Festivalgoers who only want one particular film usually take a seat near the aisle.

Getting around the city, most locals tap a **Macau Pass** card on buses, and mobile payment is common in shops and cafés. Hotel and casino shuttle buses run from the ferry terminals and border crossings. Visitors often ride these free shuttles into Cotai or the peninsula and walk on from there.

Street signs in Macau are bilingual, in Chinese and Portuguese. Taxi drivers usually respond best to a venue name written in Chinese, so attendees tend to keep the Chinese address saved on their phone. Cantonese is the everyday language, and English is understood at most festival and hotel desks.

Masterclasses and the gala usually draw industry guests and filmmakers. Smart-casual clothes fit the awards night. Jeans are fine for daytime screenings.

## Between screenings: the peninsula and Taipa

The gaps in a festival schedule are where Macau rewards you. On the peninsula, **Senado Square**'s wave-patterned Portuguese pavement leads uphill to the stone facade of the **Ruins of St. Paul's**, all that survives of a 17th-century Jesuit church. Both belong to the Historic Centre of Macau, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Film lovers usually find their way to **Cinematheque·Passion** as well. This arthouse cinema sits on Travessa da Paixão, a lane just below the Ruins.

Across the bridges, **Taipa Village** has narrow lanes around Rua do Cunha, lined with bakeries selling almond cookies and pork-chop buns. The colonial **Taipa Houses** stand nearby along the old waterfront. Most visitors find two or three full days enough for a festival block or two plus the main sights. Staying on the peninsula keeps you closest to the old town.

## Making a short trip of it

The festival's nine-day run spans a weekend at each end. Visitors from Hong Kong or Guangdong often come over for a single day of screenings and leave by the evening ferry. Others stay through to the awards ceremony. Either way, the format suits a short stay: you see a lot of cinema in a few hours, and the old town is close by when the lights come up.
