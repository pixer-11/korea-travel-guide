Farglory Ocean Park sits on the coast road between Hualien City and Hualien Airport, close enough to the sea that the theme park's Victorian-pier design reads as a deliberate joke on English seaside resorts, transplanted to a stretch of Taiwan's east coast backed by the Coastal Mountain Range. It's a full day out, not a quick stop: the park combines an aquarium, a gondola ride up the hillside, animal shows and a handful of rides, and 28,000-plus reviews put it among the most visited attractions on this coast.

## Getting there and getting in

The park sits in Yanliao Village, Shoufeng Township, about a 20-minute drive north of central Hualien City. Most visitors arrive by rental car, scooter or a pre-booked shuttle/tour bus, since there's no train station within walking distance.

- Address: 福德189號, Shoufeng Township, Hualien County
- Opens daily 9:30am, closes 5:00pm, every day of the week
- Budget the full day: gondola, aquarium halls and shows together easily fill 5-6 hours
- Parking is on-site for those driving themselves

Because the whole park runs on one continuous schedule with no early or late exception days, there's no trick to catching shorter hours. The trick is which day you pick, not what time you arrive.

## The gondola climb

The park's signature feature is a cable-car ride that lifts you from the pier-front entrance up the hillside, giving a wide view back over Hualien's coastline and the Pacific beyond the mock-Victorian rooftops below. It's included in general admission and is usually the first thing arriving crowds queue for, so riding it early or deliberately later in the afternoon avoids the worst of the midday bunching.

The ride itself is short, more a scenic transfer between park zones than a standalone attraction, but it's the best vantage point in the park for photos of the coast and the layout of the grounds below.

## Inside the aquarium halls

The aquarium is built around large tanks and themed galleries rather than a single showcase tank, with the animal shows, dolphin and sea lion performances among them, run on a fixed daily timetable that's posted at the entrance and on park signage. Check that schedule as soon as you arrive so you can plan your route around show times instead of backtracking across the park.

Expect the usual aquarium mix of reef tanks, touch pools and larger marine mammal enclosures, laid out so the walking route naturally loops you from the entrance pier zone toward the show stadiums and back past the gondola base.

## Beating the weekend crush

This is a very popular park, not a quiet local secret, and the crowd pattern reflects that plainly. Weekends are consistently the busiest stretch, with the heaviest crowds falling in the core 10am to 5pm window, essentially the whole time the park is open.

Weekdays are the quieter option, with that same 10am–5pm window being the calmest time to be inside. If your schedule allows any flexibility at all, shifting a visit from Saturday or Sunday to a weekday is the single biggest crowd-avoidance move available here.

- Quietest window: weekdays, 10am–5pm
- Avoid: weekends, when the same hours turn into the busiest stretch of the week
- Arrive close to the 9:30am opening on a weekday if you want the gondola and first show slot with minimal queuing

## Eating and resting between zones

Like most large theme parks, Farglory spreads food stalls and sit-down options across the grounds rather than concentrating them in one area, so you're rarely far from a place to refuel between the gondola ride and the next animal show. Bring a refillable water bottle if you're visiting in Hualien's warmer months, since a lot of the walking route is outdoors and exposed along the pier-front sections.

Benches and covered rest areas are dotted along the main walkways between the aquarium halls and show stadiums, useful if you're pacing a full day with kids or older family members in the group.

## Paying, tipping and moving through the park

Ticketing works like any major Taiwanese theme park: buy a single admission ticket that covers the aquarium, gondola and shows, either at the gate or in advance through official ticket vendors. Tipping isn't expected anywhere inside, in line with general practice across Taiwan.

- Have ticket and any bag contents ready for a quick bag check at the gate
- Mobile payment and cards are widely accepted at food stalls and shops inside
- Follow the posted show schedule rather than the park map's suggested route if your priority is catching a specific animal show
- Photography is generally fine throughout, but flash and close approach are usually restricted near the marine mammal pools, as posted on signage

Because this is a well-established, heavily reviewed park rather than an under-the-radar find, the practical edge for visitors is less about discovering some quiet corner and more about timing: pick a weekday, arrive at opening, and check the show schedule before you start walking.

## Planning the rest of your day

Farglory Ocean Park's coastal location means it pairs naturally with a broader loop along Hualien's eastern coast road, particularly if you're already driving north toward the airport or south back into Hualien City. Given the 9:30am to 5:00pm window, most visitors treat it as a standalone full-day trip rather than one stop among several, simply because the gondola, aquarium and show schedule together take up most of the available hours.

If you're staying in Hualien City, factor in the roughly 20-minute drive each way on top of your park time, and confirm current opening hours and ticket prices on the park's official channels before you go, since seasonal adjustments do happen at Taiwanese theme parks around public holidays.
