## One address, two rooms

Atelier Kau Lou opened alongside Opia. Opia is its sister cocktail bar, and the two share the same Jing'an space. That makes one booking a whole evening: a drink at the bar on one side, then a Cantonese meal on the other. Few Shanghai openings pair the two this closely.

In 2026 it quickly became one of the most talked-about Cantonese showpieces in the city. That is not a small claim in Shanghai. Cantonese cooking already has a strong following here, from dim sum halls to hotel dining rooms. A new place has to stand out to get people talking.

The word "showpiece" matters. This is a destination dinner rather than a quick bowl of wonton noodles. Plan on a sit-down meal, not a snack between sights.

## Getting to Jing'an

Jing'an is one of Shanghai's central districts. It is crowded with office towers, malls and old lane houses around Jing'an Temple. Most of the city's newest restaurants and cocktail bars open in this district.

- **Metro:** Jing'an Temple Station sits on Lines 2, 7 and 14. It is the district's main interchange, but confirm the restaurant's exact street address before choosing an exit.
- **Taxi:** DiDi has an English interface and runs constantly through Jing'an. Ask the restaurant for its address in Chinese characters and paste that into the app.
- **On foot:** if you are staying near People's Square or the former French Concession, Jing'an is close enough for an evening walk.

## Getting a table and settling the bill

A buzzy 2026 opening fills up, so don't count on walking in. Most Shanghai restaurants take reservations through WeChat or the Dianping app. Some higher-end rooms also answer messages through their social accounts. Book a few days ahead for weekends.

Paying works differently from most countries:

- **Mobile payment is standard.** Alipay and WeChat Pay both let foreign visitors link an international card. Set this up before you land.
- **Carry a backup.** Upscale places usually take foreign cards too, but have a payment app ready.
- **No tipping.** Tipping is not customary in mainland China. Some upscale restaurants add a service charge, so read the bill rather than leaving extra.

Cantonese meals are made for sharing. Dishes come to the middle of the table, and everyone serves themselves. Order across the menu rather than one plate per person. Ask the staff which dishes the kitchen is proudest of. The menu is new, and that is the quickest way to find what made the place's reputation.

Hours for new restaurants often change in their first year. Check the current schedule before you set off.

## Planning the rest of the evening

Opia is the obvious first stop: have a drink before dinner, or come back for a nightcap after the last dish. Since both run from the same space, you don't need a taxi between them.

For an earlier start, Jing'an Temple's gilded halls stand beside the station of the same name. Jing'an Park is just across Nanjing West Road. Nanjing West Road is also the district's main shopping street, lined with flagship malls. Any of them fills the hours before a dinner booking.
