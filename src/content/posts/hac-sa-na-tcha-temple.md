## Beside the façade

The temple is tucked behind the left shoulder of the Ruins of St. Paul's. If you are on the steps of St. Paul's, you are about 30 seconds away.

## A single room for a child god

The shrine is tiny. It is one low chamber of grey brick and granite, built in 1888 and dedicated to Na Tcha (Nezha). In Chinese folk religion he is a boy warrior, shown with a spear, a fire-tipped ring and wheels of wind and fire under his feet. Local accounts say the temple went up to ward off a plague.

The contrast is the point. A Jesuit church front looms over it while incense smoke drifts from a Chinese altar a few metres away. That pairing is part of why the temple is listed in the Historic Centre of Macao, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2005.

Next to the temple you will find:
- **A surviving section of the Old City Walls**, built from chunam, a compacted mix of earth, sand, straw and oyster shells
- **The Na Tcha Exhibition Hall**, which displays ritual objects and explains the festival
- **The Museum of Sacred Art and Crypt**, behind the St. Paul's façade
- **Mount Fortress and the Macau Museum**, up the stairs to the east

## Getting there on foot

Walk it. From Senado Square, follow the tiled lanes of Rua de São Domingos and Rua de São Paulo uphill, past the almond-cookie and jerky shops, to the foot of the Ruins steps. Climb them, then bear left around the façade.

The whole loop takes about an hour, including the temple, the wall, the exhibition hall and St. Paul's. Hours for the temple and hall are not fixed in our data, so confirm on site or with Macao Government Tourism Office listings before you build a day around them.

## Stepping inside a working shrine

Locals still worship here, so it is not a museum. It is a quieter find than the crowded steps it sits beside, and most visitors pass it without stopping.

- Step over the raised threshold. Do not stand on it.
- Keep to the side of the altar if someone is praying or lighting incense.
- Photograph the building freely, but ask before you photograph worshippers.
- Do not point at the deity's statue, and keep your voice down inside.
- If you want to give something, use the offering box rather than handing money to anyone.

Time a visit for Na Tcha's birthday on the 18th day of the fifth lunar month, usually in June. Look up the exact date for the year you travel. Around then, the neighbourhood holds processions, lion dances and Cantonese opera, and the lane is busier than usual. On ordinary days, come before the tour groups reach St. Paul's, or late in the afternoon.
