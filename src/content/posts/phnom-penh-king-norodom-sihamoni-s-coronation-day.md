## A city dressed in blue, red and gold

Phnom Penh puts on its formal clothes for this one. The day marks the coronation of King Norodom Sihamoni, crowned on 29 October 2004 after the abdication of his father, King Norodom Sihanouk. In 2026 it falls on October 29, and the capital marks it with flags, portraits and evening celebrations in its public squares.

Expect the national flag, with its blue bands and white Angkor Wat on red, strung along the main boulevards. Framed portraits of the King go up on government buildings, gateways and shopfronts. After dark, the squares fill for concerts and fireworks.

## Where the celebrations gather

The obvious focus is the Royal Palace on Sothearos Boulevard. Its gold-tipped spires face the riverside park where the Tonle Sap meets the Mekong. Sisowath Quay, the long promenade running north from the palace, is where Phnom Penh walks in the evening on any day of the year. On a national celebration it becomes the city's front row.

Other spots worth walking:

- **Independence Monument**, the lotus-shaped tower at the junction of Norodom and Sihanouk boulevards
- **The park strip south of the monument**, toward the statue of King Father Norodom Sihanouk
- **Wat Phnom**, the hilltop temple at the north end of the riverside, for decorated approach roads

Specific concert stages and fireworks locations vary. Confirm the venue for each event on official government and Ministry of Information channels before you set out.

## Tickets, timing and what is open

The decorations and public squares are open spaces you simply walk into. For any staged concert, check the official announcement to see whether entry is open or needs a ticket. No lineup or performance times are published in what we can verify, so treat any schedule as something to confirm.

Coronation Day is a Cambodian public holiday. Ministries, banks and many offices close, while markets, cafés and riverside restaurants generally keep trading. The Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda can close to visitors around royal ceremonies. Check the palace's status for the day before planning a visit inside its walls.

## Joining the evening crowds respectfully

This is a royal occasion, and Cambodians treat the King's image seriously. Behave around portraits as you would around a national flag. Do not pose jokingly beside them, and never touch or deface them.

A few habits that help:

- **Dress for the palace even if you're only passing it.** Shoulders and knees covered is the Royal Palace standard, and it reads as respect near any official site.
- **Stand still during the anthem.** If "Nokor Reach" plays at a ceremony, stop walking and stay quiet until it ends.
- **Carry small notes.** US dollars and Cambodian riel circulate side by side. Street vendors often give change in riel, and many stalls also accept KHQR codes through local banking apps.
- **Book rides by app.** PassApp and Grab work across the city and avoid haggling with tuk-tuk drivers in a crowd.
- **Pick a meeting point.** Mobile signal can strain when the quay fills, so agree on a landmark, such as the Chaktomuk Conference Hall, beforehand.

Roads around the palace and the riverfront may close to traffic on celebration nights. Arriving early in the evening and walking the last stretch is usually easier than taking a tuk-tuk to the barriers.

## Making a day of it

Spend the daylight hours on the sights the holiday is built around. The National Museum of Cambodia sits beside the Royal Palace in a red sandstone-coloured Khmer-style building, holding Angkorian sculpture. Check whether it is open on the holiday. Wat Ounalom, the seat of Cambodian Buddhism, faces the quay a short walk north.

Late October is the tail of the rainy season. Afternoon downpours are still possible, so a compact umbrella beats a sunhat for the evening.

## Other dates to know around it

October and November are busy months on Cambodia's calendar. King Father Norodom Sihanouk's Commemoration Day falls on October 15, and Independence Day is November 9, centred on Independence Monument. The Water Festival, Bon Om Touk, follows the lunar calendar and can land near these dates, bringing boat races to the same riverfront. Check the official public holiday list for the year if you are planning a trip around several celebrations.
