## A church that is in use every day

Hurghada's resorts face the sea. This church faces its congregation.

Saint Shenouda is a working parish of the Coptic Orthodox Church, Egypt's ancient Christian community. You visit as a guest in a place where people come to pray, light candles and attend liturgy. That is the reason to come: you see a side of Hurghada that the hotel strip doesn't show.

It is well known and consistently well rated by visitors. It is also a regular stop on half-day city tours. Don't expect to have it to yourself, and don't treat it as a museum.

## Who Saint Shenouda was

The name tells you something about Upper Egypt. Saint Shenouda the Archimandrite led the White Monastery near Sohag in the 4th and 5th centuries. He is one of the most revered figures in Coptic tradition and wrote in Coptic, the late form of the ancient Egyptian language.

Inside, look for what every Coptic church shares:

- **The iconostasis**, a screen of icons in front of the sanctuary (the *haikal*), where the priests serve the liturgy
- **Icons of saints and the Holy Family**, often including the Flight into Egypt, which Copts hold dear
- **Candle stands** where worshippers light tapers and stop to pray
- **Coptic and Arabic script** side by side, since the liturgy uses both languages

Spend a few minutes just watching how people move through the space. Many touch or kiss an icon, cross themselves and step back.

## Getting there from the resorts

There is no metro in Hurghada, so you'll arrive by road. The church is at 7R38+P98 on El-Lewaa Salman Mazhar street, in the Hurghada 2 postal district. Paste that plus code into your map app so the driver goes to the right spot.

From the hotels along Sheraton Road or further south, a local taxi or a ride-hailing app is easiest. Agree the fare before you get into a street taxi. If you're on an organised city tour, the church usually comes with Al Mina Mosque and a stop in the older shopping streets. That's an efficient way to fit it into a beach holiday.

## Timing your visit around services and hours

Check today's hours first. They are short on one day of the week.

According to the schedule Google currently shows, it opens 7am to midnight Friday through Wednesday. On Thursdays it closes at 12pm (noon). Hours can change around major feasts, Holy Week and Christmas (7 January in the Coptic calendar), so check on the day if your dates fall near one.

In Egypt, Friday and Sunday are the main liturgy days, with services in the morning. If you'd like to look around rather than worship, a weekday morning or late afternoon usually gives you more room. If you'd like to experience a liturgy, a Friday or Sunday morning is when one is most likely. Stand quietly at the back and stay as long as you like.

## Being a good guest at Saint Shenouda

Dress and behaviour count for more here than anywhere else on your trip.

- **Cover shoulders and knees.** Beachwear won't do. A light scarf in your bag solves most problems, and women may choose to cover their hair, though visitors aren't required to.
- **Expect a security check at the gate.** Egyptian churches are guarded. Have a bag you can open and be patient with the staff.
- **Ask before photographing people**, and never use flash during a service. Some parishes ask that you take no photos while liturgy is under way.
- **Don't step into the sanctuary** behind the icon screen. It is reserved for clergy, and Copts take their shoes off before entering it.
- **Keep your voice low and your phone silent.** Tour groups that talk loudly are the most common complaint.
- **Leave a donation if you want to.** Use the offering box; nobody will ask.

If a parishioner or deacon offers to explain the icons, take them up on it. Copts are often glad to talk about their faith and the saints of their church to curious, respectful visitors.

## Pairing it with the rest of town

The church fits easily into half a day in Hurghada's older districts. Combine it with:

- **Al Mina Mosque**, the large waterfront mosque that city tours usually visit alongside the church
- **The marina promenade**, for a walk and a meal by the boats
- **A local market**, for spices and everyday shopping away from the resort boutiques

Seeing a mosque and a church in the same morning shows how Muslims and Christians share daily life in Egyptian towns. Go to the church first if it's a Thursday, given the noon close.
