Khalid's tour is called It's Always Summer Somewhere, and the Paris stop lands at Salle Pleyel on October 18, 2026. That title is a deliberate contrast to the room he's playing: Salle Pleyel is one of the city's grand old concert halls, not a beach.

## Why Salle Pleyel is the interesting part of this show

Salle Pleyel opened in 1927 and has spent most of its life hosting the Orchestre de Paris and classical recitals, not R&B tours. Its Art Deco auditorium is known for tight, precise acoustics built for orchestras.

A Khalid show here means his falsetto and low-end production will land in a room designed for clarity, not the muddier boom of an arena. Seats fan out steeply around the stage, so even upper levels get a clean sightline.

## Finding Salle Pleyel

The hall sits at 252 Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré, in the 8th arrondissement, a short walk from Parc Monceau. It's an easy stroll from the Champs-Élysées but quieter, lined with embassies and townhouses rather than shops.

- **Metro:** Ternes or Courcelles (Line 2), both about 10 minutes on foot
- **Other option:** Miromesnil (Line 9 or 13) also works if you're coming from the Opéra side
- **Landmarks nearby:** Parc Monceau, Musée Jacquemart-André, Arc de Triomphe (about 20 minutes' walk)

Street parking in this part of the 8th is scarce on show nights, and the neighbourhood empties out early since it's mostly residential and diplomatic offices. Public transport is the simpler route in.

## What a night at Salle Pleyel actually feels like

Unlike a standing arena show, Salle Pleyel is a seated hall, so expect assigned seats rather than a general-admission crush toward the stage. The lobby is compact marble and brass, built for pre-concert conversation rather than long bar queues.

Doors typically open well before an evening downbeat at Paris concert halls, giving you time to find your row without rushing. Because it's a working classical venue most nights of the year, security and bag checks tend to be brisk rather than festival-style.

## Getting tickets

Tickets for the October 18 date are sold through Salle Pleyel's own box office and standard European ticketing platforms. Because it's a single date in a mid-sized hall rather than a multi-night arena run, seats in a good sightline can move faster than you'd expect for a pop or R&B act.

Confirm timing and tickets on the official site, and double-check the specific seating category before you buy, since a hall built for orchestras sells sections (stalls, first balcony, upper balcony) rather than a flat general-admission floor.

## Around Faubourg Saint-Honoré before the show

The stretch of Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré near the hall is mostly antique dealers, embassies, and small bistros that close early, so this isn't a neighbourhood for a long pre-show bar crawl. If you want a proper dinner, aim for somewhere near Parc Monceau or head toward Ternes, where there are more casual brasseries with later kitchen hours.

Parc Monceau itself is worth a short walk if you arrive with daylight to spare: its rotunda gates and faux-ruins were laid out in the 1770s, and it closes at dusk, so it's really a pre-show rather than post-show stop.

## Getting home after the encore

Salle Pleyel doesn't sit on a major nightlife strip, so the crowd disperses quickly rather than spilling into late bars. Ternes and Courcelles metro stations are the fastest way out; both lines run until around 1am, later on Friday and Saturday nights, though October 18, 2026 falls on a Sunday, so check the RATP schedule for that night specifically since Sunday service can wind down earlier.

Taxi and ride-share pickup works best a block or two away from the hall's main entrance rather than right outside it, since the immediate street narrows and fills with departing concertgoers.

## Practical notes for international visitors

France uses standard Schengen entry rules for most visitors, and Salle Pleyel, like most French concert halls, will list its own bag and camera policy on official ticket pages rather than a blanket national rule. Given the venue's classical-hall background, expect a stricter no-recording culture at the door than you'd meet at an outdoor festival.
