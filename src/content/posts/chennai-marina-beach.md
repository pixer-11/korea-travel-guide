## Sand the width of a city block

The sand comes first, and lots of it. Between Kamarajar Salai and the waterline at Marina Beach lies a broad, flat apron of pale sand. You walk across it for several minutes before your feet reach wet ground. It is one of the longest urban beaches in the world, running south from near Fort St. George towards San Thome.

The beach faces due east onto the Bay of Bengal, so the morning light falls straight across it. Fishing boats are often pulled up at the southern end. Behind you, the road carries a steady line of buses and autorickshaws past some of Chennai's grandest colonial-era buildings.

## What lines Kamarajar Salai

The promenade has as much to see as the shore. Walk the paved path beside the road and you pass a run of landmarks:

- **Anna Square and the memorials** at the northern end, honouring former Tamil Nadu chief ministers C. N. Annadurai, M. G. Ramachandran and J. Jayalalithaa
- **Presidency College** and the **University of Madras**, both red-brick Indo-Saracenic buildings facing the sea
- **Vivekanandar Illam**, the old Ice House, where Swami Vivekananda stayed in 1897
- **The Chennai Lighthouse**, a tall striped tower near the southern end
- **The statues along the median**, including the Triumph of Labour sculpture

At the far southern end, San Thome Basilica stands a short walk inland. It is built over the traditional tomb of St. Thomas the Apostle.

## Eating from the carts

The snacks are half the reason people come. The sand nearest the road is crowded with stalls and pushcarts. At several of them, oil hisses in wide iron kadais.

Look for:

- **Sundal**, spiced chickpeas or peanuts tossed with coconut, served in a paper cone
- **Molaga bajji**, long green chillies dipped in gram-flour batter and fried
- **Raw mango slices** dusted with chilli powder and salt
- **Roasted or boiled corn** brushed with lime and spice
- **Murukku**, the crunchy spiral of rice flour

Sundal sellers often walk the beach with a tin or basket. Many will come to you if you sit down. Pick stalls that are frying fresh, with a fast turnover of customers.

## Doing the Marina the Chennai way

The water is for looking at, not for swimming. Strong rip currents run along this coast and drownings happen regularly, so bathing is prohibited. Lifeguards and police patrol to enforce it. Locals paddle at the edge with their trousers rolled up and go no further.

A few habits will help you fit in:

- **Carry small notes.** Cart vendors and pony-ride handlers work in cash, and many will not have change for large notes. UPI QR codes are common on stalls but not universal.
- **Agree on prices before horse rides** or toy-stall games. Negotiating is normal, so do it politely.
- **Dress modestly.** Most visitors here are families in everyday clothes. Swimwear draws stares.
- **Leave your shoes on** until you reach firm sand. The dry sand near the road heats up fast by midday.

Weekend and holiday evenings are when families arrive in large numbers. Come on a weekday if you want room to walk.

## Getting to Marina Beach

The MRTS suburban railway runs parallel to the beach, a few blocks inland. Its stops include:

- **Chepauk**, best for the northern memorials and Chepauk Stadium
- **Thiruvallikeni (Triplicane)**, for the middle stretch and Vivekanandar Illam
- **Light House**, for the southern end

From each station it is a 10–15 minute walk east to the sand. Autorickshaws and app cabs can drop you anywhere along Kamarajar Salai. Name a landmark such as "Lighthouse" or "Anna Samadhi" so the driver knows where to stop. The road is busy and wide, so cross at the signals.

Combine the trip with the Parthasarathy Temple in Triplicane, a short ride inland. It is one of the city's oldest Vishnu temples.

## Timing your visit

Google currently lists the beach as open 7am–7pm every day. Public spaces like this can change their hours, so check locally before planning around the edges. Within those hours, the early morning is the coolest. That is when joggers and walkers use the promenade before the heat sets in.

Late afternoon brings the sea breeze and the snack carts at full strength. Between April and June, the hottest months, midday on the open sand is punishing. The northeast monsoon, roughly October to December, can bring heavy rain and rough seas.

Allow one to two hours for a walk and a snack. Add more if you are climbing the lighthouse or visiting the memorials.
