## What the festival actually is

The Fête des Vendanges marks the yearly harvest of the Clos Montmartre, the small vineyard tucked behind Sacré-Coeur on the Butte. It's the neighbourhood's biggest annual event, and the 2026 edition was set for October 7-11, 2026. The programme announced five days of wine tastings, concerts, street theatre, artisan food stalls and a costumed grand parade through the hilltop streets.

The vineyard itself dates back to the 1930s and produces only a token amount of wine each year. The harvest is largely ceremonial, an excuse for the whole quartier to throw a party. A fireworks display typically closes out the festivities. The event spills across Montmartre's steep lanes rather than sitting in one enclosed venue.

## Where it happens

The action centres on Rue des Saules and Rue Saint-Vincent, where the Clos Montmartre vineyard sits. It is a short walk from Sacré-Coeur basilica and Place du Tertre. Stalls, tasting tents and stages typically spread through the surrounding streets of the 18th arrondissement.

- Nearest metro: Lamarck-Caulaincourt (Line 12) or Abbesses (Line 12)
- Alternative: Anvers (Line 2), then walk up through the streets below Sacré-Coeur
- The grand parade route usually winds through the historic core of the Butte

The festival is spread across a hillside neighbourhood rather than a single fenced site, so there is no single ticket gate. Stage schedules, tasting fees and parade timing are set independently each year.

## Getting the timing right

Montmartre's cobbled streets are narrow and already busy with regular tourist traffic. Festival days add costumed marchers, stalls and crowds on top of that. The grand parade and evening concerts usually draw the thickest crowds. Daytime hours earlier in the five-day run are typically calmer for seeing the vineyard or browsing stalls than the final weekend.

Sacré-Coeur's steps and Place du Tertre are busy with regular sightseers regardless of the festival. The climb up the Butte, on foot or by the funicular from Square Louise Michel, takes longer on busy days.

## Tasting, buying and moving through the crowd

Wine tastings at the festival usually involve a token fee for a glass or tasting card, paid on the spot at the relevant stand. Card acceptance varies from stall to stall at a street event like this, and cash is the most widely accepted.

- Street food and wine stalls: pay per item, cash is safest
- Concerts and parade viewing: generally free and open along the public street
- Any ticketed tastings or special events: priced separately, with no fixed cost

Locals treat this as a neighbourhood fair, not a formal ticketed festival. The streets mean walking on slopes and cobblestones, and there is more standing room than seating. Exact daily schedules and any ticketed elements change year to year.
