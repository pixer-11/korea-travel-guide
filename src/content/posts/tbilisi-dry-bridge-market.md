## Medals, cameras and canvases on the railings

Picture a bridge with nothing beneath it. The Dry Bridge once crossed a channel of the Mtkvari River, and the channel was filled in long ago. Today the bridge and the park beside it carry one of the most-reviewed markets in the country.

The goods sit on blankets, folding tables and upturned crates. Painters hang their canvases along the park railings. Expect to find:

- Soviet badges, medals, uniforms and Lenin busts
- Film cameras, lenses, watches and radios
- Vinyl records and old postcards
- Georgian enamel jewellery, silverware, horns for drinking and daggers
- Paintings, from Old Town street scenes to abstracts

It is a flea market, not a curated shop. Real antiques sit next to reproductions, and part of the fun is learning to tell them apart.

## Reaching the bridge from Liberty Square

The nearest metro is Liberty Square (Tavisupleba) on Line 1. From there, walk down toward the river along the old streets below Rustaveli Avenue. It takes about 15 minutes. You can also come up from the Bridge of Peace and Old Tbilisi along the embankment and make it part of a riverside walk.

The address shown on maps is the plus code PR23+96X. The sellers spread over the bridge deck and into Dedaena Park, so walk both. Give it 1–2 hours if you plan to browse properly. Serious collectors can fill a whole morning.

## Bargaining at the blankets

Haggling is expected, but keep it friendly. Ask the price, offer lower, and let it go back and forth a couple of times. Sellers often start higher for visitors. Nobody prints prices, so every figure you hear is an opening offer.

- **Pay in lari cash.** Most stalls are just a blanket, and few take cards. There are ATMs up the hill near Liberty Square.
- **A few Georgian words help.** *Gamarjoba* means hello, *ramdeni?* means how much, and *madloba* means thank you. Plenty of sellers also speak Russian.
- **Ask before you photograph** a stall or a painter. Many are happy to say yes. Some are not.
- **Handle with care.** Ask before you pick up glass, cameras or old paper.
- **Ask about export paperwork.** Georgia restricts the export of genuine antiques and cultural items. If something is truly old, ask the seller what documents you need before you pay.

## Beating the afternoon crowd

Time it well. The market currently opens 11:00 AM–6:00 PM every day. It is an outdoor market, though, so weather and the season can thin out the stalls or send sellers home early. On a cold or rainy day, check before you go.

The calm window is 11am–12pm on both weekdays and weekends. That hour gives you room at the tables and sellers who have time to talk. Try not to arrive between 1pm and 6pm on a weekend. That is when the bridge gets crowded and the best pieces have usually been picked over.

It is a well-known stop, not a secret, so an early start is the only way to beat the crowd. Arrive at eleven, browse the bridge first, then the park. Afterwards, walk on to Dedaena Park's river edge or the Bridge of Peace.
