## What you're looking at

Wat Chalong is Phuket's biggest and most revered temple complex, founded in the early 1800s and rebuilt many times since. The centrepiece is a soaring chedi housing a fragment of Buddha relic bone, its exterior covered in gold mosaic tile that catches full sun by mid-morning.

Inside, spiral stairs climb past three levels of murals depicting the Buddha's life, painted in vivid reds and golds. Ground-floor halls hold gilded Buddha statues, some life-size, draped in saffron cloth left by worshippers. Outside, statues of Luang Pho Chaem and Luang Pho Chuang, the monks credited with saving the area during an 1876 tin-miner rebellion, stand under separate pavilions.

## Getting there and getting in

Wat Chalong sits on Thanon Chao Fah Tawan Tok in Tambon Chalong, about 8km south of Phuket Town and minutes from Chalong Circle, the roundabout most Phuket road trips pass through. Grab, taxi or rented scooter are the practical options; there's no rail or fixed bus route serving it directly.

- Opening hours: daily, 8:00am–5:00pm
- Entry: free, donation boxes throughout
- Parking: free lots on-site, fill fast after 10am
- Time needed: 45 minutes to 1.5 hours

## Dodging the tour groups

This is one of Phuket's most-visited sites, and coach tours from Patong and Kata start rolling in mid-morning. Arriving near opening at 8am gets you the grounds before the buses, with soft early light on the chedi's gold tiles.

Midday brings the heaviest crush, especially in high season (November–March). If you can't go early, a late-afternoon visit after 3:30pm often thins the crowd again before closing.

## Temple etiquette that actually matters here

Wat Chalong enforces dress code strictly: covered shoulders and knees for everyone, no exceptions. Sarongs are usually available to borrow or rent near the entrance if you arrive underdressed.

- Remove shoes before entering any building with a Buddha image
- Keep voices low near the chedi and prayer halls
- Women should avoid touching monks or handing them objects directly
- Small notes for donation boxes are appreciated over asking for change
- Photography is fine in most areas, but check posted signs inside inner shrine rooms

A small market stall area near the car park sells amulets, incense and cold drinks, useful before or after the walk around the grounds.
