Pali Hill has no shortage of cafés chasing the "aesthetic" crowd, but Mokai is doing something stranger and more specific: it isn't sure if it's a coffee shop or a gallery, and it doesn't seem to want to decide.

Walk in and the room announces itself with a nine-foot origami wave, folded and suspended so it seems to curl over the seating below. It's the kind of installation you'd expect in a design museum, not hovering above someone's oat-milk latte. The scale is the point: it dwarfs the tables underneath it and makes the whole space feel like it's mid-motion.

## The floating matcha bar

The other reason Mokai is getting attention is its matcha bar, described as the first of its kind in India built on a "floating" design. Rather than a standard counter, the setup appears to hover, turning the ritual of whisking matcha into something closer to a performance piece than a drink order.

Matcha bars have become a genre unto themselves in cities like Tokyo and Seoul; Mokai is importing that idea and giving it a distinctly sculptural, Instagram-ready spin for Mumbai. Expect the counter itself to be as much a draw as whatever ends up in your cup.

## Behind the concept

Mokai is the work of Karreena Bulchandani, who has framed the space as part café, part art gallery rather than a straightforward restaurant launch. That dual identity shows in how deliberately the room is staged.

This isn't a café with some art on the walls; it's built around the idea that the installation and the beverage program are equally the reason to show up. If you're the type who reads gallery wall text, expect Mokai to reward that same attention here.

## Finding it in Pali Hill

Pali Hill is one of Mumbai's most design-conscious neighbourhoods, tucked between Bandra West's shopping lanes and the older bungalow streets that give the area its quieter, leafy character. Mokai fits right into that pattern of boutique openings that treat the space itself as the attraction.

Because it's a new opening, exact hours and whether reservations are required weren't confirmed at the time of writing. Before heading over:

- Check Mokai's Instagram or Google listing for current opening hours
- Call ahead if you're planning a specific time, especially weekend afternoons
- Ask whether the matcha bar operates on a walk-in or booking basis

## Getting the light and the room to yourself

New openings like this tend to draw dense crowds in the first weeks, especially once word spreads on social media. If you want photos of the origami wave without a queue of other phones in frame, arriving right as the doors open on a weekday is usually the smarter move than a weekend afternoon.

Order at the counter if that's how the space is set up, and let the staff walk you through the matcha bar if you haven't seen one in this format before; part of the appeal is watching how the drink is made, not just receiving it. Treat your visit as unhurried. This is a space designed to be looked at as much as sat in, and rushing through misses half of what Mokai is trying to do.

## Nearby, if you're making a day of it

Pali Hill and neighbouring Bandra West are dense with cafés, concept stores, and street-side landmarks like Mount Mary Church and Bandra Fort. Mokai works well as one stop on a slower afternoon of wandering the area's tree-lined lanes rather than a standalone destination trip.

Given how recently it's opened, details like pricing and full hours are still settling. Confirm directly with Mokai before you go, and treat any menu specifics you read elsewhere online as provisional until you're there.
