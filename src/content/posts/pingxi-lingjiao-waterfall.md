Pingxi is known for sky lanterns and a slow branch-line railway, but Lingjiao Waterfall is the district's answer for anyone who wants water instead of paper lanterns overhead. It drops in two separate spouts down a mossy rock face into a pool the color of green glass bottles, the kind of spot locals swam in long before it showed up on travel blogs.

## Finding the trail

The waterfall sits back from the main road through Pingxi, accessed by a marked path that cuts through subtropical forest before opening onto the rocks at the base. The trail is short, generally under 15 minutes each way, but it's uneven in sections with tree roots and wet stone, so flat-soled sandals are a bad idea here.

Most visitors arrive by combining it with the Pingxi Branch Railway, riding to Pingxi or Jingtong Station and then walking or taking a short taxi to the trailhead. There's no single official parking lot at the falls, so drivers typically leave cars along the main road nearby and walk in.

## What you'll find at the base

The draw is the two-tiered drop and the pool it feeds, deep and still enough in the dry season for wading and swimming. Water clarity depends heavily on recent rain: after a storm the pool runs cloudy and the current picks up, while a dry stretch brings out the green that gives the spot its reputation.

There's no facility infrastructure at the falls themselves, no changing rooms, no lifeguard, no snack stand. Bring your own water, a towel, and grippy footwear, and treat the rocks around the pool as slick even when they look dry.

## When to go

Lingjiao is open 24 hours with no gate, but that doesn't mean it's ever truly empty. On weekends the busiest stretch runs from 9am until as late as 11pm, when the trail and the pool's edge fill with visitors making a day of it alongside the nearby railway towns.

If you want the place closer to how it looks in photos, minus the crowd, weekends between 7am and 8am are the quiet window. Weekday mornings tend to be calmer too, simply because the railway crowds haven't arrived yet from Taipei.

## Swimming and safety at the pool

Swimming here is informal and unsupervised, which is part of the appeal and also the thing to respect. Water levels and current strength shift fast after rain upstream, even if it's dry where you're standing, so check recent weather before you plan on getting in.

- Wear water shoes or trail sandals with grip, not flip-flops
- Skip swimming after heavy rain, when current and debris increase
- Keep valuables in a dry bag; there's nowhere to lock things up
- Don't jump from rocks you haven't scouted from below first

## Getting the etiquette right

This is a well-known stop now, not a quiet local secret, so expect other visitors, especially on weekend afternoons. The unwritten rule locals and regulars follow is simple: pack out everything you bring in, keep noise down since sound carries across the pool, and don't block the narrow trail sections while taking photos.

Cash isn't relevant here since there's no vendor or entry fee, but courtesy at the water's edge matters more than most first-timers expect. Give swimmers space, don't dangle feet or drop gear where others are trying to get in the pool, and let people pass on the single-file stretches of trail.

## Making it part of a Pingxi day

Lingjiao works well paired with Pingxi Old Street and the railway itself, since the branch line stations sit within a short ride or walk of the trailhead. Many visitors do the waterfall in the morning before the old street's crowds and lantern shops fill up by midday.

Budget half a day if you're combining both, less if the waterfall is a standalone stop. Check the weather forecast the night before, since a rainy trail changes both the footing and the water clarity at the base.
