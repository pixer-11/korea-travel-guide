## Why Homigot exists on every Pohang itinerary

Korea has a lot of sunrise spots, but Homigot has the geography to back up the hype: it's the easternmost point on the Korean mainland, the place where the sun is said to strike land first each morning. Locals and the tourism board have leaned into that fact hard, building an entire plaza around the moment of daybreak. The result is the "Sangsaengui Son" — the Hand of Harmony — a pair of matching bronze hands, one planted in the shallow sea about 100 meters offshore, the other set into the stone plaza on land. At sunrise, the sky behind the sea-hand turns from grey to pink to a hard orange, and the fingers of the bronze hand go black against it. It's a genuinely striking, photogenic thing to watch happen, not just a monument to walk past.

## Getting there

Homigot sits in Homigot-myeon, Nam-gu, on a peninsula jutting east from Pohang city. There's no direct train or subway link — this is a car, taxi, or intercity-bus destination. From central Pohang (near the KTX station or the old downtown around Jukdo Market), a taxi takes roughly 40–50 minutes depending on traffic. Local city buses run out to Homigot from Pohang's bus terminal area, though schedules thin out early and late, which matters if you're chasing sunrise — check current bus times the night before rather than relying on the first departure being early enough. If you're driving, there's a paved parking area right by the plaza, and the coastal road out to the point is scenic in its own right, running along fishing villages and breakwaters.

## What to see once you're there

The plaza itself centers on the land-hand sculpture, flanked by an eternal flame installation and a row of stone markers. Walk the boardwalk toward the water and you'll get the classic photo angle: both hands in one frame with the sun rising between them. The Homigot Lighthouse, a working aid to navigation with a small attached maritime museum, sits a short walk from the plaza and gives you context on the peninsula's fishing history and the lighthouse's own role guiding ships along this stretch of the East Sea. Along the promenade, stalls sell grilled and dried squid and other seafood snacks — smoke curling off the grills is as much a part of the Homigot morning as the sunrise itself. Budget 20–30 minutes for the plaza and hands, another 20–30 for the lighthouse and museum, and leave room to just sit on the sea wall and watch the water — that's really the point of the place.

## When to go

Sunrise is the whole reason most people make the trip, and Homigot times its biggest annual celebration around New Year's Day, when crowds gather in the dark and cheer as the first sun of the year clears the horizon — book accommodation in Pohang well ahead if you're aiming for that date specifically. Outside of New Year's, any clear morning works, and the plaza is far calmer. Rated 4.4 from over 400 reviews, Homigot has a loyal following but isn't yet swamped the way some of Korea's better-known sunrise spots are, so a random Tuesday sunrise here still feels unhurried. Midday is the least interesting time to visit — the light goes flat and the atmosphere that makes the hands sculpture work simply isn't there.

## How to visit like a local

Arrive in darkness, not at "sunrise time" — locals get there 30–40 minutes before the posted sunrise to claim a spot near the water's edge and watch the sky change color, which is half the experience. Dress warmer than you think you need to; the wind off the East Sea at dawn cuts through jackets that felt fine in town. Pay for squid and street snacks in cash where possible — small seaside stalls here are less consistent with card readers than city restaurants. There's no ticket or admission gate to the plaza itself, so there's no queue to plan around — the only "booking" that matters is arranging your taxi or checking bus times back into Pohang, since options thin out fast once you're done. The mistake most visitors make is treating Homigot as a quick photo-stop on the way somewhere else; give it the full hour before and after sunrise instead of rushing back to the car the moment the sun clears the water.
