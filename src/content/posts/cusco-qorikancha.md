## Two empires, one wall

You walk into a Spanish cloister and find Inca stone holding it up. Qorikancha means "golden enclosure" in Quechua. It was the most sacred temple of the Inca state and was dedicated to Inti, the sun. Chroniclers described its walls sheathed in gold sheet. The conquistadors stripped the gold and melted it down, then the Dominicans raised the Convent of Santo Domingo on the foundations.

The 1950 earthquake brought down much of the colonial church. The Inca walls underneath survived almost untouched. That contrast is what you're here to see:

- **Inca chambers** off the cloister, traditionally linked to the Moon, Stars, Rainbow and Thunder
- **Trapezoidal niches and doorways**, the Inca signature shape that holds up in earthquakes
- **Mortarless ashlar**, cut so tightly that the joints are barely visible
- **The curved wall** below the church, best seen from Avenida El Sol

## Finding the entrance

The address is Santo Domingo s/n, where Calle Loreto meets Avenida El Sol. From the Plaza de Armas, walk down Calle Loreto. It takes about five to ten minutes on foot, and the lane runs between original Inca walls, including those of the Acllawasi, the house of the chosen women.

Down the slope, the terraced lawns below the church once held the sacred garden. Chroniclers wrote of life-size plants and animals made of gold there. The separate Museo de Sitio del Qorikancha sits under these gardens on Avenida El Sol. It belongs to the city's boleto turístico. The temple and convent are not on that pass, so you buy their ticket at the door, where the current price is posted.

## Timing it around the crowds

Our crowd data has a clear message: come early. It is quietest from 9am to 10am on weekdays, and the same holds on Saturdays. At that hour you can stand at the niches without a tour group behind you.

Try not to arrive between 12pm and 3pm on weekends. Those are the busiest hours, and the narrow chamber doorways back up fast. The current schedule is 8:30am–5:30pm Monday to Saturday. Sundays are shorter, 2pm–5pm only, so a Sunday visit means arriving into the weekend peak. Hours change through the year and on religious holidays, so check the official listing for this month before you go.

## Reading the stones properly

This is one of Cusco's most-visited sites, and without context it can feel like a row of empty rooms. Licensed guides wait near the entrance and work in English and Spanish. Agree on the fee and the length of the tour before you start. If you'd rather go alone, read about Inca stonework the night before.

Some habits worth keeping:

- **Dress for a church.** Santo Domingo is still a working Dominican priory. Cover your shoulders and lower your voice in the nave.
- **Ask before you photograph.** Some interior areas restrict photos or flash, and staff will tell you.
- **Look at the joins.** Run your eye along the seams and compare the Inca base with the rougher colonial masonry above it.
- **Bring water and pace yourself.** Cusco sits above 3,300m, and the lower terraces mean steps on the way back up.

Afterwards, head back up Loreto to the Plaza de Armas and the Cathedral. Or walk about 15 minutes west to Mercado San Pedro for a juice-stall lunch.
