You smell the space before you fully understand it — something between warm ceramic glaze and the faint smoke of a kitchen that treats French technique as a language for Cantonese memory. That's TATE Dining Room, Chef Vicky Lau's two-Michelin-starred project, now settled into a new permanent home in Sheung Wan.

## Why TATE stands out

Vicky Lau built her name on a simple but rare idea: French fine dining fluent in Chinese flavor, not just borrowing from it. Ingredients like fermented bean curd, shaoxing wine, or dried scallop show up plated with the precision of a Parisian tasting menu.

The result reads as translation, not fusion. Every course carries a small story about where East and West actually meet on the plate.

## The new Sheung Wan space

The move into this permanent address is the headline for 2026. It's a two-story build with interiors by designer Chris Shao, a deliberate departure from a typical white-tablecloth dining room.

Look up and around as you're seated — the space is dotted with bespoke ceramics commissioned from more than 20 different artisans. No two settings feel mass-produced, which mirrors the kitchen's own refusal to repeat itself course after course.

Downstairs (or tucked into the build, depending on the night) sits a 1,200-bottle French wine cellar. It's built for pairing menus that lean into Burgundy and beyond, matched against dishes designed to hold their own against serious wine.

## What a meal here feels like

Expect a multi-course tasting format rather than an à la carte scan-and-order. Courses arrive slowly, plated like small ceramic still lifes — the kind of food photographed before it's eaten.

Because the menu changes with the seasons and the chef's own instincts, don't go in expecting specific dishes. Go in expecting a narrative: something that moves from delicate raw elements toward richer, wine-friendly mains, then a dessert built more like a final sentence than an afterthought.

## Getting there and booking

TATE sits in Sheung Wan, one of Hong Kong Island's oldest districts, walkable from Sheung Wan MTR station on the Island Line. The neighborhood itself rewards a slow arrival — dried seafood shops, antique dealers, and small galleries line the surrounding streets.

Because this is a newly opened permanent location, treat every practical detail as provisional until you check directly:

- Confirm current opening hours before booking
- Reserve through TATE's official website or listed booking platform, ideally several weeks out
- Ask about dietary accommodations when you book, not on arrival
- Confirm dress code expectations, as fine dining rooms in Hong Kong often lean smart-casual minimum

Two Michelin stars and a fixed tasting format mean tables turn over slowly and demand is high. Last-minute walk-ins are not a realistic plan here.

## How to visit like a local

Book the moment a reservation window opens, and treat weekend evenings as the hardest tables to land — locals celebrating anniversaries and business dinners fill them fast. Weeknight seatings, especially earlier in the evening, tend to move a little easier.

Payment is by card, as with virtually all fine dining in Hong Kong; cash isn't the expectation here. Tipping isn't obligatory since a service charge is typically added automatically to the bill, so there's no need to calculate extra.

Dress smart but not stiff — think tailored-casual rather than black tie. The most common visitor mistake is arriving with only a vague sense of timing; tasting menus here run long, so keep the rest of your evening open.

If you're curious about the room itself, arrive a few minutes early rather than rushing straight to the table. Take a moment in the entry to look at the ceramics — each artisan's piece is worth noticing before the food arrives and pulls your attention elsewhere.

## Nearby to pair with your visit

Sheung Wan rewards lingering before or after your meal. Wander toward Tai Ping Shan Street for small galleries and cafés, or walk down to Man Mo Temple, one of the district's oldest and most atmospheric sites, easily folded into an evening out.
