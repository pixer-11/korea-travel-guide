You find it almost by accident. Walking along North Michigan Avenue near the river, you spot a small, easy-to-miss stairwell sign pointing down into the lower level of the street — the famous "Lower Wacker" tier of Chicago's double-decked roads. Descend, and the light changes: fluorescent tubes instead of daylight, exposed pipes overhead, the low rumble of traffic passing on the level above. This is Billy Goat Tavern, the original location, tucked into Lower 430 North Michigan Avenue since the 1960s, and it looks like it hasn't redecorated since.

## Why go

This is the birthplace of the "cheezborger, cheezborger, no fries, cheeps" chant immortalized by the old *Saturday Night Live* sketch, and the grill men behind the counter still bark orders with the same rapid-fire cadence today. It's a no-frills burger counter — vinyl-topped tables, newspaper clippings and framed photos crowding every inch of wall space, a countertop grill hissing with double-patty cheeseburgers pressed thin and charred at the edges. The menu is short: cheeseburgers, hot dogs, chips, maybe a bowl of chili. Nobody comes for subtlety. They come for the char, the onions griddled alongside the patties, and the sense that you're eating inside a piece of Chicago media history — this was long the watering hole of newspaper reporters from the nearby Tribune and Sun-Times offices.

## Getting there

The lower-level entrance sits beneath N Michigan Avenue near the Wrigley Building and Tribune Tower, a short walk from the Michigan Avenue Bridge. If you're on the CTA, the Grand Red Line stop or the Chicago Brown/Purple Line stop each leave you a 10–12 minute walk away; several Michigan Avenue bus routes stop closer. Look for stairwells marked "Lower Michigan Avenue" — there are a few access points, and first-timers often circle the block once before spotting the right staircase down.

## What to eat

Order the cheeseburger doubled up, with cheese, and don't ask for lettuce, tomato, or mayo — the counter is famous for saying no to add-ons that slow down the line. A side of chips (not fries — there are none) and a canned or fountain soda round out the classic order. It's fast food in the truest sense: your burger is often on the tray before you've finished paying. Portions are modest and prices stay budget-friendly, in keeping with its reputation as a cheap, fast, unpretentious meal rather than a destination dinner.

## When to go

Billy Goat is open long hours — from 7am on weekdays (9am weekends) until midnight most nights, and until 1:30am Thursday through Saturday — so it works equally well as a breakfast burger stop or a late-night bite after a show. Measured foot-traffic shows the calmest window is weekday mornings between 7am and 10am, before the lunch office crowd and tour groups arrive. Weekends stay busy nearly all day, with the heaviest crush from noon to 10pm — worth avoiding if you want to actually get a seat rather than eat standing at the counter. If you can only come on a weekend, aim for the morning hours before 11am.

## How to visit like a local

This is a well-known stop on the tourist circuit now, not a hidden find, so the trick isn't discovery — it's timing. Go early. The weekday morning lull is your best shot at ordering without a line snaking toward the stairs. Cash still moves fastest here, though cards are accepted; either way, have your order ready before you reach the counter, because the grill staff move quickly and appreciate quick, direct answers ("cheeseburger, cheese, no onions" — said plainly, no hesitation). Don't expect table service: you order at the counter, take a number or wait for your name, and bus your own tray after. The classic rookie mistake is treating it like a sit-down burger joint and asking for substitutions or a menu explanation — you'll get a good-natured but firm "no" and hold up the line behind you. Budget 30–45 minutes total, snap your photo of the wall of press clippings and Bill Murray-era memorabilia, then free up your table for the next round of hungry office workers and tourists filing down the stairs.
