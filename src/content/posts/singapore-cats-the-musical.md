## A junkyard full of cats

The stage looks like a rubbish tip. CATS has always been set in a junkyard: tyres, bent cans and oversized household debris piled high, so the performers look cat-sized against them. The 2026 production was planned as a limited run at Sands Theatre at Marina Bay Sands.

Andrew Lloyd Webber wrote the score around T.S. Eliot's 1939 poems, *Old Possum's Book of Practical Cats*. The show opened in London's West End in 1981. It is almost entirely sung and danced, with hardly any spoken dialogue, so it suits anyone whose English is shaky.

The story is thin on purpose. On one night a year, the Jellicle cats gather and introduce themselves in song. Old Deuteronomy then chooses one of them to be reborn at the Heaviside Layer.

## Getting to Sands Theatre

Sands Theatre is inside The Shoppes at Marina Bay Sands, the mall at the foot of the three hotel towers. The MRT is the simplest way there:

- **Bayfront Station**, on the Circle Line (CE1) and Downtown Line (DT16), connects underground to The Shoppes.
- Signs lead to The Shoppes, then theatre signage inside the mall leads to the auditorium.
- Taxis and Grab cars drop off at the Marina Bay Sands driveways. The resort has parking, with rates posted at the car park entrance.

The mall is large and the floors are confusing at first. The walk from the station to a seat takes around 15 minutes, longer with a stop for merchandise.

## Choosing seats and buying tickets

Where you sit changes the show. CATS is a dance show first. The big ensemble number, the Jellicle Ball, makes the most sense from a little height, where the full stage patterns are visible. Stalls seats put you closer to the faces and makeup. Productions have also traditionally sent cats prowling through the aisles, so the seats near those aisles can feel very close to the action.

## Curtain-up etiquette at Sands Theatre

A few local habits shape an evening at the theatre:

- **Layers help.** Singapore keeps indoor spaces heavily air-conditioned. Three hours sitting still in a cold auditorium catches out visitors in beach clothes.
- **Smart casual is the norm.** Most of the audience comes from dinner in the mall, so neat clothes are usual. Nobody expects black tie.
- **Phones stay away.** Photographing or filming during the performance is not allowed. Ushers spot a lit screen in the dark and ask for it to be put away.
- **Latecomers wait.** Staff usually hold late arrivals until a suitable break. Missing the opening number of CATS means missing the cats' first entrance.
- **Paying is easy.** Card and contactless payment work almost everywhere in the complex, including the bars and the merchandise stands.

Sands Theatre sets age guidance for each production. CATS is long, with an interval, and the second act is slower and more reflective than the first, which matters for small children.

## Making a night of it at Marina Bay

The theatre sits in one of the busiest leisure districts in the city, with plenty to fill the hours around a show. Within a short walk are:

- **ArtScience Museum**, the lotus-shaped building on the waterfront beside the mall.
- **The Helix Bridge**, the double-helix footbridge leading towards the Esplanade.
- **Spectra**, the free outdoor light and water show at the Event Plaza on the bay.
- **Gardens by the Bay**, reached by the footbridge behind the hotel towers, where the Supertrees are lit after dark.
- **Merlion Park**, across the water, at the far side of the bay promenade loop.

The Shoppes has food courts and restaurants at every price level, but the hour before curtain-up is when everyone eats at once, and sit-down tables fill quickly. After the show, the bayfront is cooler and the promenade is lit.

For anyone who knows the music only from "Memory", Grizabella's big number, a cast recording is the easiest introduction. Knowing Rum Tum Tugger, Mr. Mistoffelees and Macavity by name makes the first act easier to follow.
