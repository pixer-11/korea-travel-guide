## Reaching Senayan without sitting in traffic

Driving into Senayan on a concert night is the slow option. Jalan Sudirman is Jakarta's main business corridor, and it is congested on Saturday evenings even without a stadium show. Rail is the better choice here.

- **MRT Jakarta (North–South Line):** Istora Mandiri and Senayan stations are both on Jalan Sudirman, a short walk from the complex. The line runs south to Blok M and Lebak Bulus and north to Bundaran HI.
- **TransJakarta Corridor 1:** the Blok M–Kota busway runs along Sudirman with stops beside the complex.
- **KRL commuter rail:** Palmerah station, on the Rangkasbitung line, is on the western edge of the complex. It's useful for anyone staying out towards Tangerang.
- **Ride-hailing:** Gojek and Grab cars and motorbike taxis drop at the edge of the complex. Inner roads are often closed on event days.

From hotels around Thamrin, Kuningan or Blok M, Senayan is well under half an hour away by MRT.

## November means rain at GBK

The show was set in Jakarta's rainy season. Heavy afternoon and evening downpours are common in November. They can be short and violent, and they turn the walkways round the stadium into shallow streams. The roof covers the seating tiers but not the pitch, so festival-floor standing tickets leave fans under open sky.

The usual kit for a November show:

- a light poncho rather than an umbrella, since stadiums commonly ban umbrellas from the stands
- a zip bag for a phone and passport
- shoes that can get wet
- a small towel, because humidity stays high after dark

Search policies at Indonesian stadium gigs are often strict about food, drink and power banks.

## Paying, queuing and getting through the GBK gates

The crowd sorts itself out by gate, and each ticket names one. The complex is big enough that walking from the wrong side can take a while.

A few things Jakarta concert-goers do as a matter of habit:

- **QRIS or card.** Indonesia's national QR payment standard is accepted by most vendors in and around GBK. Some rupiah in cash helps with street sellers on Jalan Sudirman.
- **No tipping.** Tipping isn't expected at food stalls or by ride-hailing drivers, though rounding up in the app is appreciated.
- **Authorised sellers only.** Scalped e-tickets that don't match the holder's ID are a real risk at name-checked entry.
- **Eating first.** The malls next door have large food courts, and queues for stadium food grow as a show approaches.
- **Two useful words.** *Pintu* means gate and *antre* means queue. Both show up on signs and in shouted instructions from marshals.

## Senayan before the show

Senayan is one of the easier parts of Jakarta to spend a few hours in. Plaza Senayan and Senayan City face each other across Jalan Asia Afrika, right beside the complex. Both have air conditioning, food courts and plenty of places to sit out a storm.

FX Sudirman is a short walk up the main road. Inside the complex, the jogging paths and Hutan Kota GBK, the city forest on the stadium's edge, offer shade and open space. On stadium nights they fill with fans in band T-shirts through the afternoon. Eating, finding a gate and settling in without rushing usually takes two to three hours.

## The midnight walk back to Sudirman

The real challenge at a big GBK show comes after the last song. Tens of thousands of people leave together, and every ride-hailing app in Senayan surges at the same moment. Pickup points jam up, and drivers often can't get near the stadium.

The calmer way out:

- The MRT is usually quicker than waiting for a car. Its last-train time varies, and extended service for big events isn't guaranteed.
- Walking 15 to 20 minutes away from the complex, towards Senayan City or further up Sudirman, makes it far easier to get a car.
- A meeting point agreed in advance matters. Mobile data often slows to a crawl when a full stadium tries to upload videos at once.
