## Concert-night etiquette in a Toronto K-pop crowd

The night starts well before the lights drop. Merch tables open before doors at most Toronto venues, and those lines are often longer than the entry queue. Fans typically arrive an hour or more before doors for that reason alone.

What the crowd usually brings and does:

- **Lightstick:** the official one, fully charged. Venues sometimes sync them to the stage show.
- **Fan chants:** shouted during the intros and rap breaks of the bigger songs, learned from fan-made chant guides online.
- **Freebies:** fans often swap handmade photocards and stickers in the queue, and a few spares to trade are expected.
- **Payment:** Toronto venues are almost entirely card and tap. Bar staff are usually tipped 15–20%, often added on the card machine.

Phone filming is common, but holding a screen up for an entire song blocks the row behind. Most fans film a clip or two and put the phone away.

## Getting around Toronto in December

December in Toronto is properly cold, often below freezing after dark. That matters because the entry queue is usually outdoors. Fans dress for standing still for an hour, then check heavy coats where the venue offers it.

Getting there and away:

- **TTC subway:** most downtown venues sit within walking distance of Line 1 (Yonge–University). Union Station is the main hub.
- **Fares:** the TTC accepts PRESTO cards and tap-to-pay with a credit or debit card at the fare gates.
- **After the show:** subway platforms near downtown venues fill quickly at the end, so many attendees walk a stop or two before boarding.
- **Rideshare:** surge pricing around venue exits is normal after a big show. Walking a few blocks before ordering a car usually helps.

Out-of-town fans tend to stay downtown, around Union Station or the Entertainment District. Both are close to most large venues and on the subway line home.

For food, Toronto's Koreatown on Bloor Street West, around Christie Station on Line 2, is the usual pre- or post-show stop for K-pop fans. It has Korean barbecue, fried chicken and late-opening cafés.
