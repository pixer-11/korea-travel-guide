## Three days on a bay that grows its own reef

Pemuteran's coral reef is the reason this festival exists. For more than two decades the village has rebuilt its damaged reef with Biorock: steel frames sunk in the shallows and fed a low-voltage current. Limestone builds up on the metal, and coral grows on it.

The Pemuteran Bay Festival takes place from November 6 to 8, 2026. It celebrates that restoration work with cultural performances and community activities. The festival is about the reef first, with a stage and food stalls added on, so the water is part of the venue.

## The shoreline that serves as the venue

The festival is held along Pemuteran Bay, in the Gerokgak district of Buleleng regency. The beach here is dark volcanic sand, and the water inside the reef is usually calm and shallow. Dry hills come almost down to the coast behind it.

Village life runs along the coast road and the beach paths. Fishing boats (jukung, with their bamboo outriggers) sit pulled up on the sand. Dive shops and small hotels line the shore. On festival days the community activities and performances share this same strip of sand with the people who live and work here.

## Reaching Bali's quiet northwest

Pemuteran is a long way from the south of Bali. From Ngurah Rai International Airport the drive usually takes around four hours or more, and most visitors hire a private driver. There are two routes:

- **North route:** over the mountains through Bedugul, then west along the coast past Singaraja and Lovina.
- **West route:** along the southwest coast towards Gilimanuk, then northeast to Pemuteran.

Travellers coming from Java cross on the ferry from Ketapang to Gilimanuk, which is a short drive west of the bay. Lovina is the nearest larger resort area, roughly an hour or more east by road.

## Reef manners and temple manners in Pemuteran

Most visitors spend the festival in or near the water, and the village expects some care in return. The Biorock frames are fragile and easy to reach from the beach. Snorkellers here stay floating, never stand on the structures and keep their fins clear of the coral.

- **Sunscreen:** reef-safe sunscreen or a rash guard is the norm among divers here.
- **Offerings:** small palm-leaf canang sari sit on steps and pavements. People step around them.
- **Temples:** a sarong and sash are needed to enter, and many temples lend or rent them at the gate.
- **Paying:** rupiah cash is still the easy option at warungs and craft stalls. Larger places often take cards or QRIS phone payments.
- **Tipping:** it is not expected, but rounding up for a driver or dive guide is welcome.

When a ceremony or dance is under way, watch from the edges and leave the front to the people taking part. It is polite to ask before photographing performers up close.

## Around the bay: Menjangan, Pulaki and the national park

Most people who come this far stay a few extra days. These are the usual add-ons:

- **Menjangan Island:** inside West Bali National Park (Taman Nasional Bali Barat). Its wall dives and snorkelling are among the best known on the island, and boats leave from Labuhan Lalang, a short drive west.
- **Pura Pulaki:** a seaside temple just east of the village, set against cliffs and known for its resident macaque monkeys.
- **Banyuwedang hot springs:** natural hot pools a few minutes west.
- **West Bali National Park:** savanna, mangroves and forest trails, home to the Bali starling conservation programme.

## How long people stay, and where

Pemuteran is too far for a day trip from the south. Festival-goers usually stay in the village for the three festival days, often adding a day or two for Menjangan. Accommodation runs from simple homestays on the back lanes to dive resorts on the beach. Lovina is an option for people who want more choice, though it means a drive each day.

November falls at the start of Bali's wet season. The northwest is generally one of the drier parts of the island, but afternoon showers are possible, so a light rain layer and a dry bag for phones are sensible to pack.
