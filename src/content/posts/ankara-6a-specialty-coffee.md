Ankara runs on tea. It comes in tulip glasses on saucers and costs almost nothing, and it's poured all day in every office and shop. 6A Specialty Coffee is on the other side of that habit. It's a coffee-first room in Aziziye that takes its cues from Berlin rather than Istanbul.

It's recently opened, and it has built a following among the city's diplomats. That makes sense when you look at the map: Çankaya is where Ankara keeps its embassies.

## A Berlin habit in Çankaya

The idea is simple. Berlin's third-wave cafés are known for spare rooms, short menus and beans whose origin someone behind the bar can tell you about. 6A brings that approach to a city still largely loyal to çay and Türk kahvesi.

The interior is the first thing you'll take in. It's concrete and wood, with hard grey surfaces softened by timber. That pared-back look is what most clearly sets it apart from the tasselled, carpeted cafés of the old town around Ankara Castle.

## What changes in the cup

The beans change, and that's the reason to come back. 6A works with rotating guest roasters, so the coffee on the bar isn't fixed. A bag you liked last month may be gone, replaced by a roaster you've never heard of.

What that means for you:

- **Ask what's on now.** The guest roaster is the headline, so the staff are the best guide to it.
- **Order something plain on your first visit.** A black coffee shows off a new roaster better than a milk drink does.
- **Come back.** A second visit a few weeks later is effectively a different café.

We haven't been given a menu, so we won't guess at one. Expect the menu to follow the coffee rather than the other way round.

## Finding your way to Aziziye

Aziziye is a residential neighbourhood in Çankaya, the hilly southern district that rises above the city centre. It's within easy reach of Kavaklıdere and Tunalı Hilmi Caddesi, Ankara's best-known shopping and café street. Kuğulu Park, the small swan park at the southern end of Tunalı, is a useful reference point.

Kızılay is the main transit hub, served by the M1 metro and the Ankaray line. From there, the simplest option is a taxi up the hill. Locals mostly book one through the BiTaksi app. City buses also run through Çankaya, and you pay for them with a rechargeable Ankarakart rather than cash.

## Ordering at the bar

Turkish specialty cafés mostly work the same way. You order and pay at the counter, take a seat, and your drink is brought to you or your name is called. Cards and contactless payment are standard in Ankara, so you won't need lira in your pocket for a coffee.

A few habits worth copying:

- **Tipping is optional.** Rounding up or leaving small change at the counter is common and appreciated. Nobody expects a percentage.
- **Learn two words.** *Filtre kahve* is filter coffee. *Teşekkürler* is thank you.
- **Check before you travel.** We don't have confirmed opening hours, and the place is new, so check its current listing or social accounts first, along with whether it takes reservations.
- **Laptops are a judgement call.** With a diplomatic and professional crowd, quiet laptop use is likely to fit in. Taking over a big table for hours at a busy moment probably won't.

Working weeks in Çankaya follow office and embassy schedules. Weekday mornings and early lunch are likely to be the room's most businesslike stretch, and weekends are likely to be more relaxed. That's a guess from how the district works, not a measured pattern.

## Making a half-day of it

On its own, 6A fills 45 minutes to an hour. It works better as the start of a walk through upper Ankara.

From Aziziye you can head down towards Tunalı Hilmi Caddesi for its shops and side-street restaurants, and stop at Kuğulu Park on the way. Further south, Seğmenler Park offers lawns and city views. Atakule, the tower overlooking Çankaya, is a short ride away if you want the panorama.

Ankara has hot, dry summers and cold winters with snow on the hills. A concrete-and-wood room with a good filter is welcome in both. In July, come for the coffee and the shade. In January, come for something to warm your hands.
