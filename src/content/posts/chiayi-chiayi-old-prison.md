## Behind the Wall on Weixin Road

From the street you see a long, high perimeter wall with a gatehouse set into it. What it hides is a complete prison compound. Chiayi Old Prison was finished in 1922, under Japanese rule, and kept holding inmates for most of the twentieth century. The prisoners were moved to a new facility in the 1990s, and the old site opened as a museum in 2011.

It is widely described as the only Japanese-era prison in Taiwan that still stands close to its original form. That is why it is a protected national monument. It is also why it is one of Chiayi's best-known sights, so don't expect it to yourself.

It isn't a place for hands-on exhibits. The appeal is being in a working institution that has barely been dressed up. You get the corridors, the cell doors and the sightlines exactly as the guards had them.

## The Fan and the Watchtower

The layout is the part people come to see. Three cellblock wings spread out in a fan from one central observation post. From that hub, a single guard could look down every corridor at once. It follows the radial surveillance plan used in prisons across the West and Japan in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Once you stand at the hub, the logic is obvious. Each wing runs away from you in a straight line, and cell doors face each other across a narrow passage. Timber is everywhere: in the doors, the roof framing and the small hatches. That fits Chiayi, which grew rich on cypress from Alishan.

Things to look for as you walk:

- **The central hub**, where the three wings meet.
- **Cell doors and hatches**, set low for passing food and watching inmates.
- **Roof ventilation**, built to cope with Chiayi's humid summers.
- **The perimeter wall**, which feels different once you know what it enclosed.

## Joining the Docent Tour at the Gate

Visits here have long been built around docent-led walks. Guides take small groups through the wings, and most tours are in Mandarin. The guides are the reason the place makes sense. They explain what each room was for, how the daily routine ran, and stories the signs leave out.

How the visit works now:

- **Check the format before you go.** Tour times, group sizes and whether you can wander alone have changed over the years. The Chiayi Prison Museum's official page and the notice at the gate carry the current arrangements.
- **Ask about English tours in advance.** If your Mandarin is shaky, ask ahead whether an English-language tour can be booked. Don't count on one appearing on the day.
- **Check the admission policy at the gate.** Don't rely on a figure from an old blog.
- **Keep your voice down in the wings.** People are there to listen to the guide, and the corridors carry sound.
- **Ask before photographing people.** The architecture is fair game. Guides and other visitors are a courtesy question.

Tours run to a schedule, so arriving early in the day usually gives you more slots to pick from. It also gives you more time for the neighbourhood afterwards.

## Getting to Weixin Road

The prison is in the East District, north-east of the city centre. It is an easy trip from the main rail station.

- **From Chiayi Station (TRA):** it's roughly a 20 to 25 minute walk north-east, or a short taxi ride.
- **From Chiayi HSR Station:** the high-speed rail stop is outside the city. Take the free shuttle bus or the BRT into the centre, then walk or take a taxi.
- **Via the Alishan Forest Railway:** Beimen Station, the first stop out of Chiayi on the mountain line, is near Hinoki Village and within walking distance of the prison.

The streets around it are flat and quiet, lined with low-rise houses. Walking between the station, Beimen and the prison is easy on foot.

## Timing a Tuesday-to-Sunday Visit

The one rule to remember: **it is closed on Mondays.** Every other day it opens 9am to 5pm, and weekend hours are the same as weekday hours.

Allow 1 to 2 hours inside, depending on the tour and how long you stay at the hub. Arriving after 3:30pm leaves little time for a full walk before the 5pm close.

Weekends and Taiwanese public holidays bring domestic visitors and families. If your trip allows it, a weekday gives you more room in those narrow corridors. Chiayi summers are hot and sticky, and the wings aren't air-conditioned, so a morning visit tends to be more comfortable.

## The Walk from Cellblock to Cypress Village

The prison sits in a cluster of Japanese-era sites, so plan to keep walking afterwards.

- **The former prison staff dormitories:** these wooden houses near the compound once housed the officers who worked inside. They show the other side of the same institution.
- **Hinoki Village:** a group of restored Japanese-style timber houses built for forestry workers. It's a short walk away and full of small shops and cafés.
- **Beimen Station:** the small wooden station building on the Alishan Forest Railway, and a good stop for railway fans.
- **Chiayi Forestry Culture Park:** old sawmill and rail yard sites that tell the story of the cypress trade.

To finish, head back toward the centre for a bowl of **turkey rice** (huǒjī ròu fàn). It's the dish Chiayi is known for across Taiwan. Shredded turkey is laid over rice and dressed with fat and shallot oil, and you'll find it at counters all over the old downtown.
