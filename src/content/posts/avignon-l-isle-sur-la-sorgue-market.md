## Stalls along the river

At this market, you're shopping beside water. L'Isle-sur-la-Sorgue is built on branches of the Sorgue, which split and wrap around the old town. The market follows the channels along Quai Jean Jaurès and into the streets that run off it.

Awnings line the quays. The river runs clear and fast a few steps from the tables, and at the bridges the old wooden waterwheels turn, furred green with moss. Lanes branch off toward the town centre and fill with more stalls. The place is well known and well visited, so plan your morning around the crowds rather than hoping to miss them.

What you'll find on a full market day:

- Fruit and vegetables from the Vaucluse farms around the town
- Cheese, charcuterie, olives and bread
- Cut flowers and potted herbs
- Artwork, from framed canvases to painters selling their own work
- Antiques and brocante: furniture, linen, old signs, glassware

## Thursday or Sunday: pick your morning

The market runs on only two mornings. It opens at 7am on Thursday and packs up at 1:30pm. On Sunday it opens at 7am and runs until 2pm. For the other five days of the week, the quays are simply quays.

Sunday is the big one. That's the day the antiques and brocante dealers spread out beside the food stalls, and when the town fills most. Thursday is smaller and centred on food, closer to a weekly shop than a spectacle. If antiques are your reason for the trip, it has to be Sunday.

## Reaching the quays from Avignon

The town is an easy half-day from Avignon. Regional TER trains run from Avignon Centre to L'Isle-Fontaine-de-Vaucluse station, a ride of roughly half an hour. From the station it's a short walk into the centre. Sunday services are thinner than weekday ones, so check the SNCF timetable the night before.

Driving takes a similar time, but Sunday traffic backs up on the way into town. The car parks nearest the centre fill early. It usually works better to park on the edge of town and walk in along the river.

## Working the stalls without looking lost

A few habits make the morning smoother:

- **Say "bonjour" first.** Greet the vendor before you ask for anything, and say "merci, au revoir" when you leave. Skipping it comes across as rude.
- **Let the vendor choose.** At produce stalls you don't handle the fruit yourself. Point, name a quantity ("un kilo", "une barquette"), and the stallholder bags it.
- **Carry coins and small notes.** Some bigger stalls take cards, but plenty of small producers prefer cash.
- **Haggle only at the brocante.** A polite counter-offer is normal with antiques dealers. At food stalls the price is the price.
- **Bring a basket or tote.** You'll need it for cheese, bread and flowers.
- **Ask about shipping.** Before you fall for a sideboard, ask the dealer whether they can arrange delivery abroad.

Timing comes down to how the day fills up. Stalls are set up by opening, but visitors from Avignon and the coach tours tend to arrive mid-morning. Being on the quays soon after 7am gets you the best produce and room to browse. Don't aim for the last hour, because many stalls start packing away before the official close.

## After the last awning comes down

The antiques trade here goes well beyond market day. L'Isle-sur-la-Sorgue is known as one of France's great antiques towns. Permanent dealers and antiques "villages" cluster around the town, especially toward the station. Many are open at weekends, so a Sunday visit can run on into the afternoon.

In the old centre, the Collégiale Notre-Dame-des-Anges is worth stepping into. Its plain exterior hides a heavily gilded Baroque interior. The town also holds large international antiques fairs around Easter and in mid-August, when the dealer count swells well beyond a normal Sunday.

For the source of all that water, drive about 7 km upstream to Fontaine-de-Vaucluse. There the Sorgue rises from a spring at the foot of a cliff. The riverside restaurants back in town fill up quickly once the market winds down, so book a table if you want lunch by the water.
