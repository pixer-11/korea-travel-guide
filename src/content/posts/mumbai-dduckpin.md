Duckpin bowling is a different sport from the ten-pin lanes most Mumbai malls already have. The balls are smaller, palm-sized rather than finger-holed, and the pins are shorter and squatter, so the game plays faster and rounds turn over quicker between groups. DDUCKPIN is the first place in India built around this format, and it's tucked inside Infiniti Mall in Malad West, a part of the city better known for multiplex screens and food courts than for imported bar concepts.

## What makes it a genuine first for Mumbai

Duckpin bowling started in the northeastern United States over a century ago and stayed mostly a regional curiosity there, never spreading the way ten-pin did. Its arrival in Malad means Mumbai now has a bowling format nobody else in the country offers.

That novelty is the draw as much as the bowling itself. Shorter pins and smaller balls make it easier for people who've never bowled to knock a few down, which matters if you're bringing a mixed group of confident bowlers and total beginners.

## The bistro half of the name

DDUCKPIN isn't a bowling alley with a snack counter bolted on; the food and drink side is built to run alongside the lanes as its own draw. The menu leans into globally inspired comfort food, and there's a full bar, so the plan is to bowl a round, then hang back at the table rather than leave once the game ends.

Karaoke rooms round out the format, which pushes this firmly into group-outing territory: birthdays, work leaving-dos, reunions of college friends who want three different activities under one roof instead of hopping venues across the city.

## Finding it inside Infiniti Mall Malad

Infiniti Mall Malad sits on Link Road in Malad West, one of two Infiniti Mall properties in the city (the other is in Andheri, so double-check you've got the right one on maps). The mall is a known entertainment anchor for the western suburbs already, with a multiplex and a wide food court, so DDUCKPIN slots into an ecosystem people already visit on weekends.

Because it's a recent opening, exact floor and unit numbers can shift as mall directories update; ask at the mall's help desk or check DDUCKPIN's own listing when you arrive rather than relying on an old map.

## Booking a lane without the wasted trip

Group-format venues like this usually run on reservations for lanes and karaoke rooms, especially on weekend evenings when a leaving-do or birthday group can occupy a lane for a couple of hours.

- Call ahead or book online if a reservation system is listed, particularly for Friday and Saturday nights
- Bring your group size and rough headcount when booking, since duckpin lanes and karaoke rooms are typically sized for a set number of people
- Ask about minimum spend or time-slot limits when you book, since bowling-bistro formats elsewhere often run in fixed blocks
- Confirm current opening hours directly with the venue or mall before setting out, as these can change in the first months after launch

## What to actually do once you're there

Split the outing rather than treating it as bowling-then-leave. Play a round or two of duckpin first while everyone's fresh, then move into the bistro side for food and drinks once the competitive edge wears off.

If your group is large enough to want both a lane and a karaoke room, ask at booking whether they can be reserved back-to-back or run in parallel, since that affects how you plan the rest of the evening. Given the format is new to India, staff are likely used to walking first-timers through the rules, so don't hesitate to ask for a quick explainer on scoring before your first frame.
