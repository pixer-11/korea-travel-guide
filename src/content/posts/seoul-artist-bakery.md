## Why this bakery, and why now

Seoul's bakery scene has a hierarchy, and at the top of it sits the team behind London Bagel Museum — the bagel shop that turned queuing into a civic pastime in Anguk. Artist Bakery is their follow-up, and it inherited the same phenomenon almost overnight: a bakery-café that draws lines out the door on weekdays, not just weekends. You don't stumble onto this place by accident anymore; you go because you've heard about the salt bread, the baguette sandwiches, and the fact that showing up "sometime in the afternoon" usually means showing up to a closed case of empty trays.

The pull is simple to understand once you're standing in front of the counter. Salt bread — that glossy, butter-laminated roll with a savory core — has become something of a signature category in Seoul's bakery boom, and Artist Bakery's version is part of why. Alongside it sit baguette sandwiches, the kind built for eating on a bench nearby rather than at a table inside, given how tight seating can get.

## Getting there

The bakery sits in the Anguk neighborhood of Jongno-gu, the same pocket of central Seoul that holds Bukchon Hanok Village, Jogyesa Temple, and the palace-adjacent lanes around Insadong. Anguk Station on Line 3 is your anchor — from there it's a short walk into the surrounding streets of low-rise cafés, galleries, and hanok-style buildings that make this district one of the most walkable in the city. Because the area is already a magnet for Bukchon's photogenic alleys, a stop at Artist Bakery slots naturally into a morning spent wandering the hanok village before the afternoon crowds arrive there too.

## What to expect once you're in line

This is not a grab-and-go transaction. Expect a queue-management system of some kind — historically these Anguk bakeries have used sign-up sheets, numbered tickets, or phone-based waitlist apps to handle demand, and Artist Bakery is no exception. Because systems like this change without much notice, check recent visitor reports or the bakery's own social channels the morning you plan to go, rather than assuming last month's process still applies. Bring a payment method that works for both counter service and any pre-order kiosk, since Korean bakeries increasingly mix cash, card, and mobile pay depending on the till.

## What makes the bread worth the wait

Salt bread rewards eating fresh — the exterior should still have a faint crackle and the butter inside should be soft, not congealed, which is the entire reason people don't buy a bag for later. If you're building a small picnic, pair the salt bread with one of the baguette sandwiches for contrast: crisp crust against a softer, more yielding roll. Because this is a café as much as a takeaway bakery, there's usually the option to sit with a coffee, though seats are the scarcest resource in the building — don't expect to camp with a laptop.

## How to visit like a local

Locals and repeat visitors treat the opening hour as sacred: get there before the doors open, not after, because the most-wanted items — salt bread chief among them — sell out well before midday on busy stretches. Given that this is a very-popular destination rather than a quiet neighborhood find, the smart move is to avoid the mistake most first-time tourists make: strolling up mid-afternoon expecting café-style availability. Instead, treat it like a morning errand — arrive early, take your number or sign up, then use the wait to circle Bukchon's nearby lanes for twenty minutes rather than standing at the door. Order in Korean if you can manage basics ("소금빵" for salt bread), though staff in this heavily touristed pocket of Jongno-gu are used to English orders too. Pay by card or mobile pay if the line moves fast — fumbling for exact cash is the easiest way to hold up a queue that doesn't have slack in it.

## Before you go

Because this is a recently trending spot rather than a fixed institution, hours, queue systems, and even the sandwich lineup are the kind of details that shift month to month. Confirm current opening hours and whether same-day reservations are possible before you leave your hotel — a wasted trip to a shuttered storefront is the one outcome worth actively planning around.
