## A pyramid that changes its mind

You can see the kink from the car park. Partway up, the faces of Sneferu's pyramid drop from a steep angle of about 54 degrees to a shallower 43 degrees. That gives it the profile its name describes. Egyptologists generally read the change as a mid-build correction, since the steeper slope was proving unstable.

It is one of the earliest attempts at a true smooth-sided pyramid, from the Fourth Dynasty, around 2600 BC. Much of its original polished limestone casing is still in place, which almost no other pyramid can claim. Up close, the lower courses show the finish the Giza pyramids lost long ago.

## Inside the chambers

The interior is open to visitors. That is rarer than you might expect at Egypt's pyramids. From the north face you go down a long, low descending passage, bent over, and then it opens into corbelled chambers with stepped stone ceilings rising overhead.

What to expect:

- A cramped, steep descent with a handrail and wooden slats underfoot
- Stale, warm air at the bottom
- Narrow sections where people coming up have to pass you
- Not ideal if you are claustrophobic or have knee trouble

Allow about 1–1.5 hours for the pyramid, the passage and a walk round the exterior. That includes the small satellite pyramid on its south side.

## Getting to Dahshur

There is no metro or reliable public transport. Most visitors hire a driver or use a ride-hailing app from Cairo or Giza, and the trip takes roughly an hour depending on traffic. Ride-hail pickups for the return are unreliable out here. Agree a round trip, including waiting time, before you leave.

Dahshur pairs naturally with nearby sites:

- **Red Pyramid**: Sneferu's next attempt, the first successful smooth-sided pyramid, a short drive north on the same site
- **Saqqara**: the Step Pyramid of Djoser, north toward Cairo
- **Memphis**: the open-air museum with the colossal Ramesses II statue
- **Black Pyramid**: Amenemhat III's eroded mudbrick pyramid, visible across the desert

## Timing the desert visit

Arrive at 9am, when the gates open. It is quietest 9am–10am and again 3pm–4pm on weekdays, and 9am–10am and 2pm–4pm at weekends. Avoid 11am–2pm on Saturdays and Sundays, when tour vans from Saqqara arrive.

Hours currently run 9am–4pm. Egyptian antiquities sites adjust their schedules through the year and around holidays, so check the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities site before you go. The last hour is the coolest and the quietest, but get down the passage well before closing.

Practical habits here:

- Buy your ticket at the site entrance. Prices are posted at the office and on the official site.
- Carry small notes for your driver and for site guards. Guards may offer to show you a corner or take a photo and will expect a tip; a polite "la, shukran" (no, thank you) declines.
- There is no shade on the plateau. Bring water, a hat and sunscreen.
- Leave large bags in the car. You will want both hands free in the passage.
