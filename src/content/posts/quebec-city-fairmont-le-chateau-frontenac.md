## A railway hotel built to look like a castle

Copper roofs gone green, steep turrets and a brick tower rise above the St. Lawrence. The Canadian Pacific Railway opened Le Château Frontenac in 1893. American architect Bruce Price designed it in the château style, and that look went on to shape railway hotels across Canada.

The central tower that dominates the skyline came later, in the 1920s. The hotel takes its name from the Comte de Frontenac, a governor of New France. It is a National Historic Site of Canada. Guinness has also listed it as the most photographed hotel in the world, which is easy to believe once you see where it sits.

In 1943 and 1944 it hosted the Quebec Conferences. Churchill, Roosevelt and Mackenzie King met there to plan the Allied war effort. Today it is a high-end Fairmont hotel with fine dining, a spa, a wine bar and river-view rooms. It is also one of the most-reviewed landmarks in the city, so expect company.

## Where to stand for the photograph

The best views of the château are from outside it. These are the angles worth the walk:

- **Dufferin Terrace:** the wide wooden boardwalk at its feet, with the river below and the turrets right above you.
- **Rue du Petit-Champlain, Lower Town:** look up from the narrow lanes and the hotel fills the top of the cliff.
- **The Québec–Lévis ferry:** the short crossing gives you the full skyline, with the château sitting on top of the old walled city.
- **Governors' Promenade:** the cliffside stairway toward the Plains of Abraham looks back along the cliff.

## Walking in without a room key

It is a working hotel, but the public areas are generally open to visitors. The front entrance faces a courtyard off Rue Saint-Louis. Go in calmly and keep to the lobby and corridors. Don't wander guest floors or block the front desk for a photo. Staff are used to visitors, and they are kinder to people who act like guests.

To see more than the lobby, book a table at one of the restaurants or the wine bar. Reserving ahead matters most on weekends and in summer. The hotel has run guided history tours in the past. Check the official Fairmont site for current tours and booking, and for any prices.

Start with "Bonjour." Quebec City works in French first, though hotel staff switch to English easily. Cards are accepted almost everywhere. In restaurants and bars, a tip of 15–20 percent on top of the bill is standard in Canada. Dress smart-casual for dinner.

## Getting up to Upper Town

The hotel sits at the top of the cliff, and most routes involve going uphill. From Lower Town you have two choices:

- **Old Québec Funicular:** it runs from Rue du Petit-Champlain and comes out on Dufferin Terrace beside the hotel. The fare is posted at the station.
- **Breakneck Steps (Escalier Casse-Cou):** the steep public stairway nearby, free and tiring.

From Gare du Palais train station, the walk climbs through the old streets and takes about 15–20 minutes. Jean Lesage International Airport is roughly a 25–30 minute drive away. Driving inside the walls means narrow one-way streets, so walking is the easiest way to get around once you arrive.

## Timing it through the year

Every season changes how the place looks. In summer, buskers play on Dufferin Terrace and the boardwalk stays busy late into the evening. Early morning is the realistic way to get the railings to yourself, before the walking tours and cruise passengers arrive.

In winter, snow covers the roofs and a toboggan slide usually goes up at the end of the terrace. In early February, the Carnaval de Québec fills Upper Town. Book rooms and dinners well ahead for festival weeks and autumn colour season. At dusk the château is lit from below, and the Lévis side of the river is the best place to see it.

## Around Place d'Armes

Several things are within a few minutes' walk:

- **Place d'Armes:** the square in front of the hotel, a starting point for many walking tours.
- **Saint-Louis Forts and Châteaux National Historic Site:** the excavated remains of the governors' residences, under the boardwalk of Dufferin Terrace.
- **Rue du Trésor:** a narrow lane where artists hang their prints.
- **Notre-Dame de Québec Basilica-Cathedral:** a short walk up Rue de Buade.
- **The Citadelle and the Plains of Abraham:** reached along Governors' Promenade.

Together they make an easy half-day loop with the château in the middle.
