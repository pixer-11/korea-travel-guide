## Christmas Day, Filipino films only

On December 25, Manila's cinema schedules change overnight. For the length of the Metro Manila Film Festival, Hollywood releases come off the screens. Every auditorium shows the festival's official Filipino entries, and nothing else.

The 2026 edition is the 52nd. It begins on Christmas Day and runs into early January 2027. The Metro Manila Development Authority (MMDA) runs the festival, which dates back to the mid-1970s. Releases are usually a mix of family comedies, horror, drama and the odd prestige period film, and the box-office race between them makes the news every day of the festival.

## Parade of Stars: December 18

The festival starts a week before opening day, out on the street. The Parade of Stars takes place on December 18, 2026, and this year its route runs through San Juan and Mandaluyong. Each official entry gets a float decorated around its film, carrying the cast past crowds along the roadside.

- **Where:** San Juan and Mandaluyong, two neighbouring cities on the east side of Metro Manila
- **What to expect:** themed floats, actors waving from the decks, and road closures along the route
- **Getting close:** spectators usually reach the route on foot, because traffic on parade day is gridlocked

## Where the films screen

The festival has no single venue. It takes over commercial cinemas across Metro Manila, most of them inside malls. The big chains are SM Cinema and Ayala Malls Cinemas. SM Megamall in Mandaluyong, a short walk from Ortigas Station on MRT-3, is one of the largest multiplexes in the city. In San Juan, the Greenhills shopping area also has screens.

Tickets are sold one screening at a time, at the box office or through each chain's app and website. Price levels match regular Manila cinema tickets, and every operator posts its fares at the counter. The awards night, the Gabi ng Parangal, traditionally takes place in the festival's first week. Winners tend to pick up extra showtimes afterwards.

## Watching a festival film in a Manila mall

For many Filipino families, going to an MMFF film is part of Christmas Day itself. Lines at the mall box offices are at their longest on December 25 and New Year's Day. Plenty of moviegoers buy seats through the cinema apps so they can skip the counter queue.

- **Seating:** most mall cinemas sell reserved seats, so the seat printed on your ticket is the one you sit in
- **Payment:** cards and e-wallets such as GCash are widely accepted alongside cash
- **Ratings:** the MTRCB classifies every film, and some cinemas check ID for R-13 and R-16 titles
- **Subtitles:** many entries are in Filipino or Taglish. English subtitles vary by film, and the posters and cinema listings usually say whether a print has them.

Weekday screenings in the second week usually have the most empty seats. That's also when word of mouth reshuffles the schedules, and the less popular titles lose showtimes to the hits.
