## A stadium-sized night in Saigon's dry season

8Wonder is Vingroup's festival, and it is built big. The idea is simple: one or more international headliners share a bill with Vietnamese artists. That mix explains the crowd, with K-pop and Western pop fans standing beside people who came for the local acts.

The winter edition returns to Ho Chi Minh City in December 2026. Earlier winter editions here drew crowds in the tens of thousands, so the format is a large outdoor show, not a club night.

December falls in the city's dry season. Evenings are warm and rain is unlikely, which suits an open-air field. The late-afternoon heat still builds before sunset, and most of the crowd arrives in light clothing.

## Dates, venue and tickets for 2026

This edition is held in December 2026. Vingroup, as organiser, controls the dates, the venue and the ticket links. Its official 8Wonder website and social channels are where that information lives, along with the lineup.

On venue, history is the best guide. The December 2023 winter edition was staged at Vinhomes Grand Park, Vingroup's large township in Thu Duc City on the city's eastern side. It is open, flat ground far from the old centre, and the ride out is a long one.

Ticketing for Vietnamese mega-concerts typically splits into tiers by distance from the stage. Entry usually works with a QR code e-ticket on your phone, scanned at the gate. Prices sit with the organiser's ticket pages, and this guide does not repeat them.

## Getting in and out like Saigon does

Leaving the site is the hard part. A few habits help locals handle a crowd this size:

- **Ride-hailing is the default.** Grab and Xanh SM, Vingroup's own electric taxi fleet, carry most attendees. Phones crowd the pickup points at the end of the night, so many people walk a few blocks away from the gates and request their ride from there.
- **Motorbikes are everywhere.** Many Vietnamese fans ride in and leave the bike at paid parking near the site. Visitors usually take a GrabBike instead of renting one.
- **Phones carry everything.** E-tickets, ride-hailing and mobile payments all run through them, so a power bank is the most common accessory in the queue.
- **Small cash still matters.** Snack and drink vendors outside the gates often take Vietnamese dong in cash only.
- **Bags stay small.** Big events here usually run bag checks at entry, and light, compact bags move through faster.

Fans of the headliners tend to arrive by mid-afternoon to claim a spot near the barrier. Casual attendees usually turn up around sunset, when the main acts start to draw closer.

## Where to base yourself

District 1 is the usual base for international visitors. It has the hotels, the Ben Thanh Market area and the riverfront along Ton Duc Thang. Getting to and from Thu Duc City is a longer ride across the river, and it slows badly in evening traffic.

People who only care about the show sometimes stay in Thu Duc City itself, near Vinhomes Grand Park. The trade-off is fewer bars and restaurants within walking distance.

Many visitors spend the day before or after on Nguyen Hue walking street, at the Saigon Central Post Office and around the Notre-Dame Cathedral area, all in District 1. A two- or three-night stay covers the festival and the centre without rushing.
