## A football ground for a piano man

Suphachalasai is an old concrete bowl. Its tiered stands curve around a running track and pitch on Rama I Road, a few minutes' walk from the shopping malls of Siam. It is Thailand's original national stadium, built in the late 1930s. It takes its name from Luang Suphachalasai, who headed the country's physical education department in its early years.

For decades it has hosted football internationals and athletics meets. Now it hosts a Japanese singer who first became known for sitting alone at a piano and posting covers to YouTube. The scale is the point. On December 26, 2026, Fujii Kaze plays an open-air stadium in the middle of the city to close out his year.

## Who you're hearing

Fujii Kaze grew up in Satoshō, a small town in Okayama Prefecture. As a teenager he posted piano covers online, mixing jazz, soul, pop and classical pieces. His debut album *Help Ever Hurt Never* came out in 2020, followed by *Love All Serve All* in 2022.

Outside Japan, one song did most of the work. "Shinunoga E-Wa" spread across TikTok and streaming playlists worldwide, and its title comes from Okayama dialect. Plenty of Thai fans who can't speak Japanese know every line of it anyway.

Other songs that people tend to know by heart:

- "Shinunoga E-Wa", the viral one
- "Kirari", the upbeat single that went out everywhere in 2021
- "Matsuri", built around a festival rhythm
- "Nan-Nan", the early song that first got people talking about him

His live sets usually move between a full band and quieter stretches where he plays the keys himself. In a stadium, those quiet stretches pull the whole crowd in.

## The Boxing Day slot

The date matters in Bangkok. December 26 falls in the cool, dry season, when evenings are about as comfortable as the city gets. Rain is unusual at this time of year. That counts for a lot in an open-air venue with no roof over the field.

It also falls in the busy stretch between Christmas and New Year. Hotels around Siam and Pathum Wan fill up with year-end travellers, and the malls along Rama I Road stay lit and crowded late into the night. Visiting fans from Japan and elsewhere in Asia usually combine the show with a few days of holiday in the city.

## Reaching Suphachalasai on Rama I Road

The Skytrain is the obvious way to get there. BTS National Stadium station is the end stop of the Silom Line, and it sits on Rama I Road right next to the stadium. Siam station, the interchange with the Sukhumvit Line, is one stop away. So from almost anywhere on the BTS network you are one change at most from the gate.

If you're staying further out:

- **Sukhumvit hotels:** ride the Sukhumvit Line to Siam, then change to the Silom Line for one stop
- **Silom and Sathorn:** stay on the Silom Line all the way to National Stadium
- **Riverside:** take the Silom Line from Saphan Taksin, which connects to Sathorn Pier
- **MRT users:** change to the BTS at Sala Daeng (Silom MRT) or Asok (Sukhumvit MRT)

Taxis and Grab cars do reach the stadium. The catch is that Rama I Road and the Pathumwan intersection jam up on ordinary evenings, never mind with a stadium crowd leaving at once.

## How the night moves around the stadium

Stadium shows in Bangkok follow a pattern, and fans who know it get a smoother night. Most people come by BTS, so the platforms at National Stadium and Siam fill up fast right after the encore. Plenty of concertgoers walk to Siam station instead, or wait a while in a mall or noodle shop until the first rush clears.

A few habits help on the night:

- **Rabbit card or contactless:** BTS gates take stored-value Rabbit cards, and many fans load theirs beforehand so they skip the ticket machines at the end of the night
- **Cash for street food:** the vendors around the stadium and on Banthat Thong Road mostly take cash or Thai QR payments
- **Merch lines:** at Japanese artists' shows, fans often queue at merchandise stalls well before the music begins
- **Heat and hydration:** even a cool-season evening is warm by most standards, and standing on a field gets hot

Thai crowds at concerts are generally relaxed and good-humoured. People sing along loudly, film the big songs and then put their phones away. Keeping a phone at eye level for an entire set tends to annoy whoever is behind you, especially on a flat field.

Ticket details for Fujii Kaze Bangkok sit with the concert organiser, and that is where seating zones, entry rules and bag policies are set out.

## Before and after in Pathum Wan

You don't need to go far to fill the hours either side of the show. The neighbourhood is one of the densest in Bangkok.

- **Banthat Thong Road:** runs behind the stadium and has become one of the city's best-known street-food strips, with stalls and shophouse restaurants that stay busy into the night
- **MBK Center:** across Rama I Road, connected to the station by skybridge, packed with phone stalls, souvenirs and a large food court
- **Bangkok Art and Culture Centre:** the white spiral gallery on the Pathumwan intersection, opposite MBK, with free-to-browse exhibitions
- **Jim Thompson House:** the teak house museum of the American silk trader, down a lane near the National Stadium BTS station
- **Siam Square and Siam Paragon:** one stop east, for shopping, cinemas and late dinners

For a slower evening, Chulalongkorn University's leafy campus sits just south of the stadium area. The canal-side lanes near Jim Thompson House are quieter than the main road.
