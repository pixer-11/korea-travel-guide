## Why this one stands out

Jakarta has no shortage of hotel restaurants chasing a skyline view. What sets The Crown apart is the name behind the pass: Kirk Westaway, the British chef known for earning a Michelin star at J Sheekey Atlantic Room in Hong Kong before turning his attention to Southeast Asia.

Here he's applying that same technical precision to modern British cooking, reimagined for a Jakarta dining scene more used to French tasting menus and Japanese omakase counters.

## Where to find it

The restaurant occupies the 22nd floor of Fairmont Jakarta, in the Senayan/SCBD corridor — the same stretch of the city that holds Senayan City mall and the Gelora Bung Karno stadium complex.

That elevation matters. At this height, Jakarta's usual ground-level chaos — the motorbikes, the honking, the heat rising off the asphalt — disappears entirely, replaced by a wide, glassed-in view over the city's rooftops toward the haze on the horizon.

## What the room feels like

Expect the polish of a five-star hotel flagship: white tablecloths, soft ambient lighting, a kitchen visibly running with the calm choreography of a chef who's cooked at the highest level.

The menu leans into modern British classics — think elevated, technique-driven versions of dishes that reward slow courses rather than rushed plates. Because the concept and menu are still fresh to Jakarta, treat any specific dish names you read elsewhere as provisional until you're seated.

## Getting there and settling in

Fairmont Jakarta is easily reached by taxi or ride-hailing app from most of the CBD; ask your driver for the Fairmont on Jl. Asia Afrika if the Senayan reference doesn't land.

A few practical notes:

- Book a table well in advance — new fine-dining openings in Jakarta tend to fill quickly with both expats and local diners curious about the chef's pedigree
- Request a window-side table if the skyline view matters to you
- Confirm current opening hours, dress code, and whether it's à la carte or tasting-menu-only when you call, since these details can change in a restaurant's first months
- Set aside at least two hours — this isn't a quick-bite stop given the floor it's on and the kitchen's reputation

## When to go

Sunset is the obvious draw here. Arriving as the light turns gold over the city, before Jakarta's evening haze thickens, gives you the best version of that 22nd-floor view.

If you're after a quieter, more intimate room rather than a lively one, an early dinner reservation — right as service opens for the evening — will typically feel calmer than a Friday or Saturday peak slot.

## How to visit like a local

Jakarta's fine-dining regulars treat a place like this as an event, not a casual dinner — dress smartly, and expect to linger over courses rather than rush through them.

Reservations are essential, not optional; call or book online ahead rather than walking in, especially on weekends when hotel restaurants across SCBD fill with both business dinners and celebrations.

Payment is by card as standard at this level — cash isn't the norm. A service charge and tax are typically added to the bill automatically at Jakarta's five-star hotels, so tipping beyond that is appreciated but not expected.

The mistake first-time visitors make is treating the view as the whole point and rushing the menu. Slow down, let the kitchen show you what a modern British menu looks like through a Michelin-trained lens, and ask your server about the story behind the space — staff at a new flagship like this are usually well briefed and glad to talk through the chef's background.

Before you finalize plans, call Fairmont Jakarta directly or check their official channels for current hours, reservation policy, and menu format. As a recently opened venue, these specifics are still settling and worth confirming close to your visit date.
