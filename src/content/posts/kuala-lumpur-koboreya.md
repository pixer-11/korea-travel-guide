## A Tokyo backstreet dish, now in Bukit Bintang

Most of Kuala Lumpur's Japanese food is ramen, sushi and yakiniku. Koboreya does something else. It is billed as the city's first restaurant dedicated to monjayaki, the loose, savory batter dish that Tokyo associates with Tsukishima, the island district whose "Monja Street" is lined with griddle restaurants.

The draw is that you do the cooking. Monjayaki is a table dish by design. The batter is meant to be stirred, spread and scraped while you talk, so a meal here is closer to a shared activity than to dinner served to you.

## What lands on the griddle

Monjayaki gets compared to okonomiyaki. The two are cousins, but they don't behave the same way. Okonomiyaki sets into a thick pancake that you slice. Monja stays runny. It's a thin, broth-based batter that cooks down into something molten in the middle and crisp at the edges.

In the Tokyo tradition, a bowl arrives with chopped cabbage and fillings piled on top of the liquid batter. Typical Japanese fillings include:

- Mentaiko (spicy cod roe) with mochi and cheese, the Tsukishima classic
- Seafood such as squid and shrimp
- Pork, corn or a crunch of tempura bits

Koboreya's own menu may differ, so read it at the table rather than expecting a fixed Tokyo lineup.

## Cooking it yourself, step by step

The traditional method takes a few minutes to learn and is part of the fun. Staff at monja restaurants usually walk first-timers through it, so ask if you're unsure.

1. Tip the solid fillings onto the hot griddle and chop them with the spatulas while they fry.
2. Push them into a ring, a "dike" (*dote* in Japanese), with a hollow in the middle.
3. Pour the liquid batter into the centre and let it thicken before you mix everything together.
4. Spread it flat and wait for it to bubble and brown.

You don't plate it. Each diner takes a tiny spatula (a *hagashi*) and presses a bite against the griddle so it crisps, then eats straight off the blade. The browned layer that sticks to the iron, the *okoge*, is the bit regulars fight over. Scrape for it.

## Finding it in Bukit Bintang

Bukit Bintang is KL's main shopping and nightlife district, so getting there is easy. Two rail lines stop at Bukit Bintang:

- **MRT Kajang Line:** Bukit Bintang station, under the Pavilion Kuala Lumpur side of Jalan Bukit Bintang
- **KL Monorail:** Bukit Bintang station, on the elevated track above Jalan Sultan Ismail

Grab rides are cheap and simple here, but evening traffic around Jalan Bukit Bintang and Jalan Sultan Ismail crawls. The train plus a short walk is usually faster. Before you set out, check the exact address and entrance on Koboreya's official listing, because the street-level layout around here can be confusing.

## Booking a seat and sitting down to cook

Griddle tables are sociable, so come in a group. Two people works fine. Three or four lets you order different bowls and swap bites across the griddle. A new restaurant with a novelty format tends to fill up first with groups and birthday tables, so book if Koboreya takes reservations, especially on Friday and Saturday nights.

A few practical habits:

- **Paying:** cards and QR e-wallets such as DuitNow and Touch 'n Go are standard at sit-down places in Bukit Bintang. Check the bill for a service charge before deciding whether to add anything; tipping isn't expected in Malaysia.
- **Dress for the grill:** cooking oil and batter spit a little. Leave the white silk shirt at the hotel.
- **Pace yourself:** monja is eaten slowly, a bite at a time. Rushing it means scraping off burnt batter.
- **Ask before ordering pork:** Malaysia has a large Muslim population, and halal status varies between Japanese restaurants. If it matters to your group, confirm with staff.

## Before and after

You're in the middle of KL's best walking district, so the meal fits easily into an evening. Jalan Alor, the hawker street one block over, wakes up at dusk with satay smoke and plastic stools along both sides. Go there for durian or grilled stingray if monja has only whetted your appetite.

For drinks, Changkat Bukit Bintang's bar strip is a short walk uphill. During the day, pair lunch with Pavilion Kuala Lumpur or Lot 10, whose basement food hall gathers well-known local hawker names under one roof.
