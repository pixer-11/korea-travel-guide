## What this place actually is

La Taperia Martínez isn't a quiet neighbourhood tapas bar with three items on a chalkboard. It's a full-scale Spanish restaurant built around a 45-item menu, which is enormous by tapas standards. The chef behind it is Valencian, already known in Manila for Terraza Martinez and Alma, and this new venture reads like the fuller, more ambitious version of what those two places started.

That scale changes how you should plan a visit. This isn't a stop-in-for-two-plates kind of night. It's built for grazing across hot and cold sections, sharing dishes across a table, and staying long enough to work through several rounds.

## The dishes that set the tone

Two items on the menu signal what the kitchen is going for. Charcoal-grilled Black Onyx beef anchors the meat side, a premium cut cooked over live fire rather than a flat-top, which is a different cooking philosophy from most tapas menus in the city.

On the seafood side, there's ravioli finished in Carabinero prawn oil, made from the intensely flavoured red prawns prized in Spanish cooking for the oil rendered from their heads. Pairing an Italian pasta format with a distinctly Spanish seafood technique tells you this menu isn't chasing tapas cliché.

## Where it sits in Manila's Spanish food scene

La Taperia Martínez arrives in a city that already has a serious appetite for Spanish and Basque cooking, much of it shaped by the same chef's earlier restaurants. Coming from the team behind Terraza Martinez and Alma gives it an established reputation to live up to rather than a blank slate.

Because it's newly opened, it's also generating the kind of buzz that fills tables fast. Expect it to be busy on weekend evenings especially, simply because Manila's fine-dining crowd tends to descend on anything new from a chef with a track record.

## Ordering strategy at the table

With 45 dishes on offer, the biggest mistake is over-ordering cold items and running out of room for the charcoal-grilled course, which needs time on the fire and is worth pacing your meal around.

A workable approach: start with two or three cold tapas to open the palate, move into a hot dish or two, then let the Black Onyx beef anchor the middle of the meal before finishing with something lighter like the seafood ravioli. Sharing plates family-style, Spanish tapas custom, works naturally here given the menu's breadth.

Ask your server which dishes are new additions versus signature holdovers from the chef's earlier restaurants; with a menu this size, staff recommendations are more useful than trying to read all 45 items cold.

Treat your first trip as reconnaissance rather than a one-and-done. A menu this large rewards repeat visits more than a single marathon sitting, since no group can reasonably taste all 45 dishes in one meal.
