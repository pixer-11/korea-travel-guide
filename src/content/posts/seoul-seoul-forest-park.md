## What Seoul Forest actually is

This is a 120-hectare public park in Seongdong-gu, built on former military and golf-course land along the Jungnangcheon stream near the Han River. Five zones make up the park: Culture and Arts Park, Ecological Forest, Nature Experience Learning Center, Han River Waterside Park, and a wetland zone with an observation deck. The Ecological Forest is where the deer live, roaming a fenced meadow you can view up close along a boardwalk.

A butterfly and insect house sits nearby, along with a small deer-feeding area (feed sold on-site in past years, but confirm availability before going). The rose garden blooms in a curved bed near the central plaza, peaking in late May and June.

## Getting there and getting around

Take Seoul Subway Line 2 to Ttukseom Station, Exit 8, then walk north about 10 minutes. The Bundang Line's Seoul Forest Station, Exit 3, drops you even closer to the main entrance. Bike-share docks (Ttareungyi) sit at multiple park entrances if you'd rather cycle in along the riverside path. The park is open 24 hours daily and entry is free, though individual attractions like the insect house may run shorter hours.

Inside, wide paved paths loop through each zone, and a small train-like tram sometimes operates on weekends for a fee. Bring your own bike or rent one nearby if you want to ride the full riverside stretch toward the Han River. Public restrooms and convenience kiosks are scattered near the main plaza and the deer zone.

## When to go

As one of Seoul's most visited parks, weekends and holiday afternoons draw heavy crowds, especially near the deer enclosure and rose garden in bloom season. Weekday mornings are markedly quieter and the best window for photos without crowds. Spring (April cherry blossoms, late May roses) and autumn (October foliage along the stream) are the two peak visual seasons. Summer evenings bring locals out for picnics on the lawns once the heat breaks after sunset.

## Picnic etiquette and getting around like a regular

Seoul Forest runs on the same unspoken rules as most Korean parks: pack out your own trash, since bins are sparse, and lay down a mat rather than sitting directly on the grass in the main lawn areas. Convenience stores just outside the park entrances sell instant picnic kits, mats, and drinks if you didn't bring your own. Cyclists and pedestrians share paths, so keep to the marked lanes near the river stretch.

If you're heading to the deer or butterfly zones on a weekend, arriving right after opening hours reset each day (effectively early morning, since the park never fully closes) beats the midday tour groups and school trips.

Confirm current hours for the insect house, deer-feeding program, and any seasonal exhibits on the official Seoul Forest website before visiting, since these vary by season independent of the park's 24-hour access.
