Beijing is a city that runs on duck fat, lamb skewers and cumin smoke. A restaurant that cooks without meat and still holds two Michelin stars says a lot about how the capital eats now. Lamdre does exactly that, in Sanlitun, and it is one of the most closely watched tables in China.

## Why Lamdre matters

Lamdre's reputation rests on two recent results. The 2026 MICHELIN Guide promoted it to Two Stars. It also ranked No. 17 on Asia's 50 Best Restaurants, which puts it among the most decorated plant-based kitchens on the continent.

The restaurant is owned by Zhao Jia, and chef Dai Jun runs the kitchen. Their stated aim goes further than vegetarian cooking. They frame the food around health and sustainability, so vegetables are the point of the meal, not a stand-in for meat.

That matters to you as a diner. Come expecting cooking that treats plants as the main subject, and judge it the way you would judge any other two-star kitchen.

## Sanlitun, around the table

Sanlitun sits in Chaoyang District, east of the old city. It is Beijing's embassy and nightlife quarter. The open-air Sanlitun Taikoo Li complex anchors it, and its glass-fronted flagship stores and wide pedestrian lanes fill up every evening.

Lamdre is an easy match with the rest of the neighbourhood. Nearby you'll find:

- **Sanlitun Taikoo Li**, for a walk before dinner or a drink after
- **Workers' Stadium (Gongti)**, rebuilt and reopened, a short walk south-west
- **Chaoyang Park**, further east, if you want greenery by day
- **Ritan Park**, south toward the CBD, with its Ming-era altar grounds

## Getting there without fuss

Don't rely on a rough pin. Take the exact street address from your booking confirmation, and keep a copy in Chinese characters to show a driver.

Tuanjiehu Station on Line 10 is the usual subway stop for the Sanlitun area. Depending on where the restaurant sits, the walk can be fifteen minutes or more. For dinner, most visitors find a DiDi car simpler. The app has an English interface, and the address you paste in is the address you get.

## Booking and paying at a two-star table

Behaving well here mostly comes down to planning ahead. A newly promoted two-star restaurant does not take casual walk-ins. These steps make the night go smoothly:

- **Reserve early.** Bookings tighten after every awards announcement, and this restaurant just had two.
- **Confirm the format.** Ask about menu structure, how long the meal runs and the current hours when you book, as these change.
- **Flag dietary needs up front.** The kitchen is plant-based already, but allergies and things like avoiding garlic or onion are best raised at booking, not at the table.
- **Set up mobile payment.** Mainland China pays by Alipay and WeChat Pay. Both now accept foreign cards once linked, so do it before you fly and carry a physical card as backup.
- **Skip the tip.** Tipping is not customary in mainland Chinese restaurants. Check the bill for any service charge before adding anything.

Arrive on time. Fine-dining kitchens pace each table, and a late start can push back every course after it.

## Who it suits, and who should think twice

Lamdre suits diners who want to see where Chinese fine dining is heading. It is also an obvious choice for vegetarians and vegans, who rarely get a meal at this level built entirely around them.

If your one Beijing dinner has to be roast duck, keep that plan and book Lamdre on another night. Treat it as a long, deliberate meal and keep the rest of the evening light. A stroll through Taikoo Li afterwards works better than racing to another reservation.
