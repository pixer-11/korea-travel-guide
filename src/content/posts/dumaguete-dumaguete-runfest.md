## A Sunday morning on the city's streets

Dumaguete is a good size for a road race. The city is compact and mostly flat along the coast, with tricycles, a university campus and a seafront promenade. Dumaguete Runfest takes place on Sunday, December 13, 2026, and it brings runners of all levels onto those streets.

It is a community run, not an elite event. First-timers line up next to club runners in matching singlets. Families wait at the finish, and some students from Silliman University usually turn out too.

December sits at the start of the cooler months in the Visayas. "Cooler" here still means tropical heat once the sun is up. That is why Philippine road races traditionally start around dawn, while the streets are empty and the air is at its mildest.

## Robinsons Dumaguete as race base

The event is based at Robinsons Dumaguete, the Robinsons mall in Barangay Calindagan, south of the downtown grid. A mall base is practical on race morning, and runners lean on it for a few things:

- **Space:** the open parking area holds a start-and-finish setup and a crowd of runners.
- **Landmark:** every tricycle driver in the city knows it, so you only need to say "Robinsons".
- **Afterwards:** once the mall opens for the day, there are food courts, a supermarket and air-conditioning.

Race categories, distances, registration and the route are all handled by the event organiser. This guide doesn't list them, since those details weren't confirmed for us.

## Reaching Dumaguete for the weekend

Most out-of-town runners arrive on Friday or Saturday. That leaves time to collect race kits and get a full night's sleep before a dawn start.

- **By air:** Dumaguete–Sibulan Airport is a short drive north of the city. It has regular flights from Manila and Cebu.
- **By sea:** fast ferries link Dumaguete port with Cebu, Tagbilaran in Bohol and Siquijor. The port sits right beside Rizal Boulevard.
- **By road:** buses come down the coast from Bacolod and other Negros towns. From Cebu there are bus-and-ferry combinations via Liloan port in Santander.

Runners usually stay downtown near Rizal Boulevard or close to Robinsons in Calindagan. From either area, a tricycle ride to the venue is short.

## Race-morning manners in Dumaguete

Race morning follows a familiar Filipino fun-run rhythm. Here is how it usually goes in Dumaguete.

- **Kit claiming:** Philippine races usually hand out the bib and singlet on set days before the race, not at the start line. Runners who leave it to the last minute often end up queueing.
- **Getting there in the dark:** tricycles are the main way around town, and fares are paid in cash. Keep small peso notes with you, because drivers rarely have change for big bills before sunrise. Many runners agree a pickup with their driver the night before.
- **At the start:** runners usually arrive well before the gun to warm up and find their corral. They also pose for the group photos that every Filipino running club takes.
- **On course:** keep to the side of the road you are told to use. Walkers stay to the right so faster runners can pass. Water stations are staffed by volunteers, so a quick "salamat" (thank you) goes a long way.
- **At the finish:** medals and finisher food are usually collected in a line, not grabbed. Bags and phones go home with family, or into the organiser's baggage area if there is one.

## After the finish: recovery, food and side trips

Most runners finish before mid-morning, which leaves the rest of Sunday free. Rizal Boulevard is the city's seafront promenade. It is lined with acacia trees and benches facing the Tañon Strait toward Cebu, and it makes a good place for an easy cooldown walk.

Food is part of the reason people come. Dumaguete is known for **silvanas**, the frozen cashew-meringue sandwiches sold by Sans Rival Cakes and Pastries, a long-running local bakery. Look out too for **budbud kabog**, a sticky millet cake from nearby Tanjay.

If you are staying an extra day, these trips are close by:

- **Silliman University:** the campus has old acacia-shaded grounds and sits right in town.
- **The Dumaguete Cathedral bell tower:** an old stone belfry beside Quezon Park.
- **Apo Island:** snorkelling with sea turtles, reached by boat from Malatapay in Zamboanguita.
- **Twin Lakes Balinsasayao and Casaroro Falls:** forest and waterfall trips up in the hills toward Valencia.

## Practical notes for visiting runners

Pack for heat, not cold. Most runners wear light, quick-drying kit with a cap. They bring electrolytes, sunscreen and a dry shirt for after the race.

December falls in Christmas season in the Philippines. Malls run long festive hours, and the city fills up with parols, the star-shaped lanterns. Flights and hotels get busy with holiday travel and students heading home, so out-of-town runners usually sort out where they are staying well before the weekend. If you have family or friends coming to watch, the finish area at Robinsons Dumaguete is the easiest place to meet.
