## A border you cross on a walkway

The national border runs through the treetops here. At Walderlebniszentrum Ziegelwies, the Baumkronenweg treetop walk leaves Bavaria on a steel-and-timber walkway and comes down in Tyrol. Nobody checks your passport, and the only marker is a sign at the line.

Below the walkway, the Lech river runs green and quick through a protected stretch of floodplain and mountain forest. The centre is there to explain that landscape: why the trees grow the way they do on these slopes, and what lives along the gravel banks. It's well known in the Füssen area and gets a lot of families, so treat it as a popular stop rather than a quiet retreat.

## What's on the grounds

The site is laid out in layers, from riverbank to canopy. Here's what you'll find:

- **The exhibition centre**, with displays on mountain forest, the Lech river habitat and forest management in the Alpine foothills.
- **Adventure and nature trails** at ground level, built for children to touch, climb and test things along the way.
- **The Baumkronenweg**, the treetop walk, roughly 480 metres long and up to about 21 metres above the forest floor at its highest point.
- **The Lech riverbank**, with gravel bars and floodplain woodland you can reach on foot.

The treetop walk is the part most people come for. It's built as a loop of platforms and bridges, and it sways slightly underfoot. It runs on its own seasonal schedule and has separate admission, so check the official Ziegelwies website before you set your day around it.

## Getting to Tiroler Straße 10

The centre sits on Tiroler Straße, the main road south out of Füssen towards Reutte in Austria. From the old town it's a flat walk of about 30 minutes. The nicest route goes past the **Lechfall**, the stepped weir and waterfall on the Lech, and over the Maxsteg footbridge.

From Füssen station, allow a little longer on foot. Regional buses heading for Reutte also run along this road, so a local timetable is worth a look if you'd rather not walk.

Drivers will find parking near the entrance. Charges and rules are posted on site. In summer the space fills with day-trippers who are also doing the Neuschwanstein run, so coming early helps.

## Timing your visit

The grounds are open **every day, 9:00 AM–7:30 PM**, which gives you long summer evenings to work with. The gate opens later than the castle ticket centres in nearby Hohenschwangau. That makes Ziegelwies a good afternoon stop once the Neuschwanstein tour is done.

Plan on two to three hours if you do the exhibition, one trail and the treetop walk. Families with young children often stay longer on the play trails. Autumn is a strong season here: the beech and larch on the slopes above the Lech change colour, and you see it from canopy height.

## Walking the canopy without the crowds

A few habits make the visit smoother.

- **Start with the treetop walk**, then do the exhibition. Coach groups and families tend to work the other way round, starting indoors.
- **Arriving near opening usually beats the day-trip traffic** from the castles, which builds through the late morning.
- **Wear shoes with grip.** The walkway grating and riverbank gravel can both be slick after rain.
- **Keep to the marked paths on the riverbank.** This is a protected habitat, and the gravel bars are breeding and feeding ground for birds.
- **Greet staff with "Grüß Gott"**, the standard hello in Bavaria and Tyrol alike.
- **Carry some euros in cash** for smaller purchases. Cards are widely accepted in Füssen, but smaller kiosks don't always take them.

People with a fear of heights should know the walkway is open-sided and moves a little. Most visitors find it steady enough, but if you're unsure, take the first section slowly before committing to the full loop.

## Pairing it with the rest of Füssen

Ziegelwies fits easily into a half-day loop on foot. Head back north along the river to the Lechfall, then up into the old town. There you'll find the **Hohes Schloss**, the late-Gothic castle above the town, and the former **St. Mang Abbey**, now home to the town museum.

With a car or bike, the Alpsee and Schwansee lakes and the Hohenschwangau castles are a short ride east. If you want more forest, the trails along the Lech towards Pinswang carry on from the Austrian end of the treetop walk.
