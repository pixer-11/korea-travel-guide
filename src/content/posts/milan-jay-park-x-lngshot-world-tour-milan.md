## One Night on Via Fantoli

The date is Saturday, October 17, 2026. That night Jay Park brings his LNGSHOT World Tour to Fabrique, Milan's mid-sized rock and hip-hop club. The shows are for fans who have followed him since the b-boy videos, and for newer fans who found him through LNGSHOT.

Fabrique works as a general-admission room. The floor is open, the stage sits at one end, and the bar runs along the side. Nobody is assigned a seat. At a show like this, you spend the night standing shoulder to shoulder with people who know every word.

## Who Is on Stage

Jay Park grew up in Seattle and was a b-boy with the Art of Movement crew. He first became famous as leader of 2PM, then rebuilt his career as a solo rapper and singer. He went on to found two labels, AOMG and H1GHR MUSIC.

Fans at his shows tend to wait for a few particular songs:

- "Mommae", the club hit that made his solo name
- "All I Wanna Do", the smoother R&B side of his catalogue
- "SOJU", his collaboration with 2 Chainz
- "Ganjiswaeh" and "McNasty", for the faster rap sections

The tour title pairs him with LNGSHOT, the boy group from his newer label, More Vision. The organiser decides the setlist and running order. We don't know them, so this guide doesn't guess.

## Getting to Fabrique

Fabrique is at Via Gaudenzio Fantoli 9. That's in the Mecenate area east of the centre, well outside the old city walls. You won't find it by wandering from the Duomo. It sits among warehouses and offices, and the streets around it go quiet once the working day ends.

Most concertgoers get there by ATM public transport. The M4 metro line runs from San Babila out towards Linate and serves this side of the city. Trams and buses cover the last stretch. A lot of fans skip the whole connection and take a taxi or ride-hail straight to the door.

Going home is the harder part. When the show ends, crowds pour out at once. Taxis are scarce on Via Fantoli, so groups often walk to a main road before calling a car. ATM's night services and journey planner show what is still running late on a Saturday.

## Inside the Room

The building is a former industrial space turned into a concert club. It feels like one: high ceilings, a hard floor, and sightlines that favour anyone near the middle of the floor. If you're shorter, the slightly raised areas towards the back usually give a clearer view than the crush near the barrier.

Concerts here usually end with a sweaty floor. Milan in mid-October is mild outside, but a packed club stays warm. Attendees dress in layers they can strip down to.

## Doing the Night Properly

Italian club shows have their own routine, and it helps to know it.

- **Cloakroom first.** Fabrique runs a *guardaroba* (cloakroom). Regulars drop their jackets there so they aren't holding a coat for two hours.
- **Paying.** Contactless card payment is normal at Milan venues, and most people tap at the bar.
- **Lightsticks and chants.** K-pop and K-hip-hop crowds bring lightsticks and banners, and they learn the fan chants. Joining in is the whole point, and nobody minds a beginner.
- **Queue manners.** Dedicated fans form a line outside well before doors, wanting a spot on the barrier. It's self-organised and taken seriously, so people who arrive later join at the back.
- **A little Italian.** "*Permesso*" ("excuse me, coming through") gets you through a crowd far more politely than pushing.

Fabrique and the tour's official ticket partner publish the ticketing details, entry policy and bag rules. Those terms apply at the door. Resale sites outside that channel are where fans most often run into trouble.

## Before and After the Show

The area around the venue has very little in the way of dinner. Most visitors eat in the city first. Porta Romana and the streets around Corso Lodi are on the way out from the centre, and both have trattorias and pizzerias that fill up with Saturday crowds. Many people do *aperitivo* there before heading out.

Out-of-town fans usually stay somewhere central with a direct metro link, such as San Babila or the Porta Venezia area. Then the trip home is one short ride. If you're flying in, Linate is the closest airport to Fabrique. Malpensa and Bergamo are much further out.

For the day before, the Duomo, the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II and the Navigli canals are all easy to reach on the metro. Plenty of K-pop fans turn a Milan concert into a full weekend.
