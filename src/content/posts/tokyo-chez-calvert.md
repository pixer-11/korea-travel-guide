## A chef leaves the hotel tower

For years, Daniel Calvert cooked inside a hotel. Chez Calvert is the first restaurant that carries his own name.

He made his reputation as executive chef of Sézanne, inside the Four Seasons Hotel Tokyo at Marunouchi. Under him it earned three Michelin stars, and in 2024 it topped Asia's 50 Best Restaurants. Chez Calvert is his move from that hotel dining room to a place of his own. It sits in Azabudai Hills, one of the newest addresses in central Tokyo.

That background explains the attention around the opening. Tokyo diners who could never get a table at Sézanne now have a second chance to eat his cooking. Anyone who followed his career will be watching what he does when nobody else's brand hangs over the door.

## What the kitchen is built on

The name is French, the chef is British and the produce is Japanese. The restaurant is built around seasonal Japanese ingredients, which also defined his cooking at Sézanne.

In Japan, "seasonal" means something narrow and specific. Ingredients like bamboo shoots, ayu sweetfish and matsutake mushrooms appear for a few weeks and then disappear. Kitchens that cook this way change their dishes to follow those windows. A visit in spring and another in autumn can feel like two different restaurants.

We are not printing a menu here, because one has not been confirmed to us. Check the restaurant's own channels for the current format and dishes. Expect it to change as the seasons do.

## Getting to Azabudai Hills

Azabudai Hills is a Mori Building development in Minato ward. It opened in late 2023 around Mori JP Tower, one of the tallest buildings in Japan. The complex mixes offices, residences, shops and restaurants around a landscaped central garden.

Getting there:

- **Kamiyacho Station (Tokyo Metro Hibiya Line):** the closest stop, with an underground connection into the complex.
- **Roppongi-itchome Station (Tokyo Metro Namboku Line):** a short walk away, and handy if you're coming from Roppongi.
- **On foot from Tokyo Tower:** the tower is close by, an easy walk downhill.

The complex is big and laid out on several levels. Give yourself extra time to find the restaurant inside it, especially for a dinner booking.

## Booking and behaving at a Tokyo chef's table

High-end Tokyo restaurants have their own etiquette, and a new opening from a chef this well known follows it. A little preparation keeps the evening smooth.

- **Book early.** Tables at chef-led Tokyo restaurants often go weeks ahead. Book through the restaurant's official channels, not resellers.
- **Declare allergies and dietary needs when you book.** Kitchens buy for each service in advance, so tell them then, not at the table.
- **Read the cancellation policy.** Fine-dining places in Tokyo commonly charge for late cancellations and no-shows.
- **Be on time.** A set-time seating means arriving late can cut into your meal and the people booked after you.
- **Don't tip.** Japan has no tipping culture, and leaving cash on the table can cause confusion.
- **Ask about payment when you reserve.** Upscale places usually take cards, but confirm it rather than assume.

Phones on the table and loud photo sessions sit badly in small Tokyo dining rooms. A quick, quiet picture of a dish is usually fine. A tripod is not.

## Turning dinner into an evening

Azabudai Hills gives you enough to fill the hours before your reservation. The neighbourhood around it adds more.

- **teamLab Borderless:** the digital art museum moved into Azabudai Hills, so you can book it before an evening seating.
- **Tokyo Tower:** close by, and lit up after dark.
- **Zojo-ji Temple:** a large Buddhist temple at the foot of Tokyo Tower, good for a walk before dinner.
- **Roppongi Hills and the Mori Art Museum:** one stop or a short walk toward Roppongi.

If your booking is in the evening, a late-afternoon walk from Zojo-ji past Tokyo Tower and up into Azabudai Hills shows you the area before you sit down.
