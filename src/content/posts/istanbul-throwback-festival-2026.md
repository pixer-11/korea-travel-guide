## Tickets without the headache

Concert tickets in Turkey are usually sold through national platforms such as Biletix and Passo, often as e-tickets linked to the buyer's name. Screenshots resold through social media carry a real risk, since a QR code can only be scanned once.

Foreign visitors can usually register on these sites with a passport number in place of a Turkish ID number. Some doors check names against tickets, so the passport matters on the night itself. On these platforms, the price is shown at checkout.

## Getting across the city on a Friday

Istanbul's Friday evening traffic is heavy. The bridges over the Bosphorus clog first, so a taxi ride that looks like 20 minutes on the map can take an hour. Rail is the safer bet whenever a venue sits near a line.

The main lines for reaching venues across the city:

- **M2 metro**, the north–south spine of the European side through Taksim, Şişli and Levent
- **Marmaray**, the undersea rail tunnel linking the European and Asian shores
- **T1 tram** through the historic peninsula, Sultanahmet to Kabataş
- **Ferries** between Kadıköy, Üsküdar, Karaköy, Eminönü and Beşiktaş

One rechargeable **Istanbulkart** works on all of these. It can be topped up at the yellow machines in stations, and several friends can share one card by tapping once each.

## Getting home after the last chorus

The rhythm of an Istanbul concert night has its own logic. Doors typically open well before the headliner, the bar queue builds between acts, and the real crush comes at the exit as everyone heads for transport at once. A few local norms:

- **Last services vary.** Metro and ferry schedules differ by line, and last departures are not the same across the network.
- **Taxi apps help.** BiTaksi and Uber both hail licensed yellow cabs, which avoids fare arguments at a busy kerb.
- **Tipping is simple.** Taxi drivers don't expect a percentage tip; rounding the fare up is normal.
- **Cards and cash both matter.** Bars at large venues generally take cards, but street vendors outside often don't.
- **Pickups are easier a block away.** Cabs drop off and pick up more easily one street away from a main exit.

Locals often stretch the night out with a late plate of köfte or a bowl of tripe soup (işkembe çorbası). Both are traditional end-of-night food in Istanbul.

## Making a weekend of it

Mid-November in Istanbul is cool and often damp. Daylight fades around 5pm. A jacket that can be tied round the waist indoors, or checked at a cloakroom, suits the season.

Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque are quieter in the morning, before the crowds build. On Sunday, the Kadıköy market streets on the Asian side are full of fishmongers, pickle shops and meyhanes (taverns).
