## Getting across the city on a Friday

Istanbul's Friday evening traffic is heavy. The bridges over the Bosphorus clog first, so a taxi that looks like 20 minutes on the map can take an hour. Rail is the safer bet whenever your venue sits near a line.

Check the venue address on the official listing, then look for the nearest stop on these lines:

- **M2 metro**, the north–south spine of the European side through Taksim, Şişli and Levent
- **Marmaray**, the undersea rail tunnel linking the European and Asian shores
- **T1 tram** through the historic peninsula, Sultanahmet to Kabataş
- **Ferries** between Kadıköy, Üsküdar, Karaköy, Eminönü and Beşiktaş

One rechargeable **Istanbulkart** works on all of these. You can top it up at the yellow machines in stations, and several friends can use the same card by tapping once each.

## Getting home after the last chorus

The rhythm of an Istanbul concert night has its own logic. Doors open well before the headliner, the bar queue builds between acts, and the real crush comes at the exit as everyone heads for transport at once. A few habits make it easier:

- **Check last services in advance.** Metro and ferry schedules vary by line, so check the return trip before you leave your hotel.
- **Use an app for taxis.** BiTaksi and Uber both hail licensed yellow cabs, which avoids fare arguments at a busy kerb.
- **Round up, don't calculate.** Taxi drivers don't expect a percentage tip; rounding the fare up is normal.
- **Carry a card and a little lira.** Bars at large venues generally take cards, but street vendors outside often don't.
- **Walk a block before hailing.** Cabs drop off and pick up more easily one street away from the main exit.

Locals often stretch the night out with a late plate of köfte or a bowl of tripe soup (işkembe çorbası). Both are traditional end-of-night food in Istanbul.

## Making a weekend of it

Mid-November in Istanbul is cool and often damp. Daylight fades around 5pm. Bring a jacket you can tie round your waist indoors, or check it at a cloakroom if the venue has one.

The festival falls on a Friday, so you have the weekend free. Saturday is good for a slow ferry across the Bosphorus, with simit (sesame bread rings) from a deck-side vendor. You could also spend a morning at Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque before the crowds build. On Sunday, the Kadıköy market streets on the Asian side are full of fishmongers, pickle shops and meyhanes (taverns).
