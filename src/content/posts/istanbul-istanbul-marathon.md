## A race that starts on one continent

The start line is in Asia. The 2026 Istanbul Marathon, the event's 48th edition, takes place on November 1, 2026. The field gathers near the 15 July Martyrs Bridge, the suspension bridge across the Bosphorus that most people still call the first bridge.

The opening kilometres are on the bridge deck. On every other day it carries motorway traffic only, and walkers aren't allowed on it. On race morning it's handed over to runners. Below you are the water, the ferries and the minarets of the European shore, and you reach Europe before most races have finished their warm-up loop.

## The route from bridge to Sultanahmet

Once across, the course follows the European side of the Bosphorus and works its way into the old city. Two landmarks on the official course description are good orientation points:

- **Dolmabahçe Palace**, the long white Ottoman palace on the shore at Beşiktaş
- **Galata Bridge**, the low two-level bridge over the Golden Horn between Karaköy and Eminönü, usually lined with fishermen's rods

The finish is in **Sultanahmet**. That's the historic peninsula with the Blue Mosque, Hagia Sophia and the old Hippodrome square.

The bridge is the only real climb at the start. The approach rises from the Asian shore before levelling out over the water. Pace yourself on it and don't spend your legs early. The full course map and elevation profile are on the official site.

## Registering and confirming the details

Everything official goes through one place. Start times, distances, entry categories, bib collection and the race expo are all on the official Istanbul Marathon website (maratonistanbul.com). Check timing and registration there rather than on reseller pages.

International entrants usually collect race packs in person in the days before the race. Build that into your arrival plan, and bring the ID you registered with. Shorter distances and a non-competitive public run have traditionally been held alongside the marathon. Check the current programme on the official site before choosing your event.

## Race-day logistics, Istanbul style

The city closes major roads for this race. That changes how everyone gets around, so plan your morning around it.

- **Getting an İstanbulkart.** This rechargeable transit card works on the metro, Marmaray, trams, buses and ferries. Buy it before race weekend at a machine in any major station.
- **Crossing to the start.** The Marmaray rail tunnel under the Bosphorus links the European side to Üsküdar on the Asian side. It's the most reliable way to reach the start area when bridges and coast roads are shut.
- **Forget taxis.** With roads closed, a car can leave you stuck in traffic while your wave sets off.
- **Expect tram changes.** The T1 tram line runs through Sultanahmet, but parts of the finish area are taken over by the race. Check the transport operator's race-day notices.

In Turkish the race is the İstanbul Maratonu, and you'll see that name on signs. Crowds along the route shout "Haydi!" (come on!). Shouting it back at the people cheering you on goes down well.

## Watching without running

Spectators get the best scenery. Galata Bridge is an easy place to stand: it has a pedestrian level, tram links on both ends and views of the Golden Horn. The waterfront near Dolmabahçe Palace is another good stretch, close to the Kabataş tram and ferry terminals.

To see the start, take a ferry or the Marmaray across to the Asian side early. The bridge is closed to traffic, so the water and the tunnel are the ways across. Near the finish in Sultanahmet, crowds build up around the Hippodrome. Settle on a meeting point with your runner beforehand, as phone networks can be slow when thousands of people are in one square.

## After the finish line

You finish in the middle of Istanbul's best-known sights, so your recovery walk can be a sightseeing walk too. The Blue Mosque and Hagia Sophia are both working mosques. Visitors need to cover their shoulders and knees, women cover their hair, and everyone takes their shoes off at the entrance. A finisher's foil blanket won't count as covering up, so pack a light layer in your bag-drop.

Mosques close to visitors during the five daily prayers. If you plan to go in, check the prayer schedule on the day. Basilica Cistern and the Grand Bazaar are within walking distance, though tired legs may not agree. Most cafés and shops around Sultanahmet take cards. Small change is handy for simit (the sesame bread rings sold from street carts) and for public toilets.

November in Istanbul is usually cool, and showers are common, so pack a dry layer for after the race. Check the race-day forecast and the official guidance before you set off. Then cross the Bosphorus on foot, which very few visitors ever get to do.
