## Through the farm gate, by coach

You don't walk into the Shire. You get driven there. Tours board a shuttle coach at the visitor base, and the coach runs along a farm road between paddocks of grazing sheep before it drops you at the top of the set.

The set belongs to the Alexander family farm. Peter Jackson's location scouts spotted it from the air in the late 1990s, drawn by a large pine beside a lake and the rolling grass with no roads or power lines in view. The set built for *The Lord of the Rings* was mostly temporary. It was rebuilt in permanent materials for *The Hobbit* trilogy, which is why there is something to tour today.

## What the guided walk covers

The route is set, and a guide leads it. You go downhill on gravel paths, through gardens that are kept in season, past round doors cut into the hillsides. There are 44 hobbit holes, each with its own painted door, and many have tiny props in the front garden: washing on a line, a wheelbarrow, jars on a windowsill.

The stops most people come for:

- **Bag End**, Bilbo and Frodo's home, at the top of the hill under its oak
- **The Party Tree**, the big pine above the field where Bilbo's birthday party was filmed
- **The lake**, the double-arched bridge and the mill
- **Hobbit holes built at different scales**, a trick that made actors look larger or smaller on camera, and your guide explains how it worked

Most of the holes are facades with shallow spaces behind the doors. Guides fill the walk with filming stories, and they will stop for photos at the classic angles.

## A drink at the Green Dragon

The tour ends at the Green Dragon Inn, across the bridge from the mill. It is a working pub built to the film's design, with low beams, a fire and wooden tables, and the standard tour includes a drink there.

The house range is brewed specially for the set under the Southfarthing name. It usually includes ales, a cider and a non-alcoholic ginger beer, served in earthenware mugs. Take your time with it. The inn and its outdoor tables are a good spot to look back across the lake at the hill you just walked down.

## Getting to Buckland Road

You need your own wheels or a booked coach. The set is in farmland near Hinuera, between Matamata and Cambridge, with no public bus that reaches it.

Rough driving times:

- **Auckland**: about 2 hours south via State Highway 1 and SH27
- **Hamilton**: under an hour
- **Rotorua**: around an hour
- **Tauranga**: around an hour

Many travellers fit it into a day between Auckland and Rotorua. Parking is at the visitor base, The Shire's Rest. Some tours also leave from the Matamata i-SITE on Broadway in town, a visitor centre styled like a gatehouse from the films, so check which departure point your ticket is for.

## Booking a tour and riding the shuttle

Book ahead. Departure slots sell out, especially in the New Zealand summer and school holidays, and walk-up places are never guaranteed. Booking is done online through the official Hobbiton Tours site. Prices and the full list of tour types are posted there, so check them rather than relying on old figures.

How the day works:

- **Arrive early** for your slot. You check in at The Shire's Rest or the i-SITE, then board the coach at your printed time.
- **Stay with your group.** The set is guided only, and you can't wander off the path or open doors yourself.
- **Card is fine** at the café and gift shop on site. New Zealand doesn't expect tipping, so thank your guide and leave it there.
- **Ask questions.** Guides know the filming detail well, and asking is how you hear the better stories.

There is also an evening banquet tour. It ends with a feast in the Green Dragon and a lantern-lit walk back. It books out early, so plan it a long way ahead if you want it.

## Season, light and weather

This is a garden as much as a film set. In spring and summer the vegetable plots and flower beds are full, while the autumn and winter months are greener and quieter. The paths are outdoors and gravel, so the Waikato's rain comes with you; the site usually has umbrellas, but a rain jacket is easier to carry.

Bad weather rarely cancels a walking tour, though it changes how the photos look. Morning light on Bag End's hillside and late-afternoon light over the lake both flatter the set. Departure times change through the year, so the official site has the current schedule.

## Staying on around Matamata

Matamata is a small dairy town with a café-lined main street and the i-SITE as its landmark. It's an easy stop for lunch either side of the tour.

If you have half a day spare:

- **Wairere Falls**, in the Kaimai Range east of town, is among the highest waterfalls in the North Island. The track to the lookout is steep and takes most walkers around 45 minutes each way.
- **Cambridge**, to the west, is a tree-lined town known for horse breeding.
- **Rotorua** is a short drive further for geothermal parks and Māori cultural evenings, which makes it a natural place to spend the night after the set.
