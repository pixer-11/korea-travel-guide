## A geothermal valley you reach by boat

The park starts at a jetty. The visitor centre and riverside café face Lake Ohakuri, and across the water a hillside steams in white, orange and green. The only way over is the park's own ferry, a crossing of a couple of minutes.

That boat ride matters more than its length suggests. With no road on the far side, the thermal area is free of car parks and tour-coach bays. Once you step off, the sound around you comes from the ground.

The valley's old name is the Hidden Valley. Don't read that as a secret, though. It's a well-known stop between Taupō and Rotorua, and on a busy afternoon you'll notice it.

## Terraces, the Golden Fleece and Artist's Palette

Start with the colours. A boardwalk loop of around 2.5 km climbs from the landing past silica sinter terraces. Mineral-loving bacteria stain them in bands of rust, ochre, cream and algae green.

The Emerald Terrace sits close to the water, with hot water spreading over it in a thin sheet. Higher up, the Great Golden Fleece is a huge sloping face of sinter, left by an ancient fault. It's one of the largest silica terraces in New Zealand.

Artist's Palette is a wide, flat area dotted with small boiling pools and vents. Expect these sights along the loop:

- Geysers that erupt on their own schedule, not on cue
- Mud pools blipping and spitting grey clay
- Hot springs too acidic and too hot to touch
- Steam vents near the path, with that unmistakable sulphur smell

## Down into Ruatapu Cave

The cave gives the park its name. Ruatapu is one of only a few geothermal caves in the world. Steps lead down into its mouth, where ferns grow on the damp rock walls.

At the bottom lies a small, clear green pool. Its traditional name, Waiwhakaata, is usually translated as "pool of mirrors". The light drops off quickly as you go down, and the steps can be slick with condensation. Take them slowly.

Allow about an hour to an hour and a half for the full loop with the cave. Add time for the café before or after.

## Beating the 11am rush

Timing changes this place more than most. It's calmest between 8am and 10am, both on weekdays and at weekends. Arrive then and you'll often get the cave steps and the Golden Fleece viewpoints mostly to yourself.

Try not to arrive between 11am and 3pm on a weekend. That's when the ferry fills, and the narrow boardwalk becomes a slow single file behind groups stopping for photos.

At the moment the park opens 8am–4pm, seven days a week. Hours can shift through the year, and the last boat across leaves before closing. Check the official site for this month's times and the final crossing if you're coming late.

## Boardwalk rules on the far shore

The far side has its own etiquette, and some of it keeps you safe:

- **Pay at the visitor centre first.** Your ticket covers the crossing. You buy it on the café side before going down to the jetty, and the current price is posted there and on the official site.
- **Stay on the boardwalk, always.** The sinter crust next to the path can be thin over boiling water. People have been badly burned in New Zealand's geothermal areas after stepping off the track.
- **Keep children's hands in yours.** Some barriers are low, and the pools are scalding.
- **Leave the formations alone.** Don't throw coins or stones into the pools, and don't touch the terraces. Silica builds up over centuries and is easily damaged.
- **Dress for steps and steam.** You'll want closed shoes for the climb and the wet cave stairs. Your sunglasses may fog near the vents.

The riverside café is a good place to wait out a busy ferry. It's also the place to rest your legs after the climb back down.

## Getting there from Taupō

You'll need your own car or a tour, as public transport doesn't run here. From Taupō, head north on State Highway 1 towards Rotorua and Hamilton. Then turn off and follow the signs along Orākei Kōrako Road to number 494. The drive takes about 30 to 40 minutes.

Many visitors pair the park with Huka Falls and Craters of the Moon on Taupō's northern edge, which are on the way. Others continue north to Wai-O-Tapu and Rotorua the same day. If you leave Taupō at opening time and do Orakei Korako first, you get the quiet window. You'll then be back near town while the weekend crowds are crossing the lake.
