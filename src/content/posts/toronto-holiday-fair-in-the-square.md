## Eleven days at Yonge and Dundas

Holiday Fair in the Square is a free holiday market. It runs from Thursday, December 10 to Sunday, December 20, 2026. It takes place on Sankofa Square, the paved public plaza at the corner of Yonge Street and Dundas Street in downtown Toronto.

The event is pitched as nostalgic and festive. Expect an old-fashioned seasonal fair set down at one of the city's busiest crossroads, not a ticketed attraction. Entry costs nothing. Anything you buy is priced at the stall, and the organiser's own listing holds the daily opening times and the vendor details.

The calendar gives you two full weekends: December 12–13 and December 19–20. Those fall in the middle of the Christmas shopping rush on Yonge Street. The weekdays in between are the fair's other half, shaped by office workers and commuters instead of weekend shoppers.

## Lanterns under the billboards

What sets this market apart is the place it occupies. Sankofa Square is a hard-paved, open plaza with tall buildings on its edges. Big digital screens and advertising signs wrap the intersection, much like a smaller Times Square. A holiday fair here sits under that glow, not under a canopy of trees.

The contrast is the draw. Wooden stalls and seasonal decorations sit a few steps from one of Canada's largest downtown malls. Crowds pour out of the subway and streetcars beside them. After dark, the screens light the whole square, so evening visits feel very different from a grey midafternoon.

The square has no roof. On a December evening, wind runs straight down Yonge Street and across the open paving. Snow or sleet falls straight onto the stalls. The fair's cosy, nostalgic mood is partly about huddling against that, so it helps to dress for it.

## Getting to the square

Transit beats driving here. The TTC makes it simple:

- **Subway:** Dundas station on Line 1 (Yonge–University) opens directly onto the square. Queen station, one stop south, is a short walk away.
- **Streetcar:** the 505 Dundas streetcar stops at Yonge Street, beside the square.
- **Payment:** TTC fare readers take PRESTO cards, as well as tap with a contactless credit or debit card or a phone. You don't need to buy a separate ticket.
- **Union Station:** visitors coming in on GO Transit or VIA Rail ride Line 1 north to Dundas, a few minutes away.

Driving downtown in December means slow traffic and costly garage parking around the Eaton Centre. Most people treat the fair as a transit trip and walk the last few metres from the station exit.

## Dressing for a plaza with no shelter

Toronto in mid-December is usually at or below freezing, especially once the sun sets in the late afternoon. The square gives you no shelter while you browse. People who stay longest tend to wear:

- An insulated coat, plus a hat that covers the ears
- Gloves thin enough to work a phone or a tap-to-pay terminal
- Waterproof boots, because slush collects on the paving after any snowfall
- A tote or backpack, which keeps your hands free for a hot drink

When the cold wins, the Toronto Eaton Centre is directly across Yonge Street. It's the obvious place to warm up before heading back out to the stalls.

## Moving through the fair like a Torontonian

Paying is easy in Toronto. Tap-to-pay with a card or phone is the norm at most counters, and the Canadian debit network, Interac, is widely accepted. Small market vendors sometimes prefer cash. A few bills in your pocket covers you at any stall without a card reader.

Tipping works the usual Canadian way. Card terminals at food and drink counters often show tip prompts. Choosing a tip at a self-serve market stall is up to you, but people often add one where someone makes a drink or cooks to order.

On foot, Torontonians keep right on crowded sidewalks and stand right on escalators so others can pass on the left. Dundas station's exits fill up at rush hour. Weekday evening visitors usually let the commuter wave clear before stopping to look around the square's edge.

Timing follows the city's rhythm, not hard data. Weekday afternoons fall between the lunch crowd and the after-work rush, so they're usually the easiest time to browse. Saturday afternoons combine fair visitors with Yonge Street shoppers, and the square fills accordingly.

## How long to give it

The market sits inside a single city square, so most people need an hour or so to walk every stall, eat something and take photos under the screens. Making a fuller downtown outing of it, as many visitors do, fills a whole evening.

The best plan is to pair the fair with other things nearby. One visit after dark lets you see the square lit up, and the walk to Nathan Phillips Square takes about ten minutes.

## A holiday loop from the square

Several December fixtures sit within a short walk of the square:

- **Toronto Eaton Centre:** the long glass-roofed mall across Yonge Street, with holiday decorations and a direct indoor link to both Dundas and Queen stations.
- **Nathan Phillips Square:** in front of City Hall, a few blocks southwest along Queen Street West. It has the outdoor skating rink and the large TORONTO sign.
- **Elgin and Winter Garden Theatre:** the stacked pair of historic theatres on Yonge Street near Queen, both dating from 1913.
- **Massey Hall:** the concert hall on Shuter Street, a couple of blocks southeast, known for December concerts.
- **Ed Mirvish Theatre:** on Yonge Street just south of the square, home to touring musicals.

A common evening has three stops. People browse the fair first, walk down to Nathan Phillips Square for skating or photos, and finish with dinner or a show along Yonge Street. Everything stays within a short walk, and Line 1 gets you home from Dundas, Queen or Osgoode stations.
