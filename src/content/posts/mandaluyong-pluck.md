## What Pluck actually is

Pluck is not another fast-casual rotisserie counter. It's a sit-down restaurant in NBS Park, Mandaluyong, built around a single technique done well: koji-marinated chicken, roasted until the skin turns deep gold.

Koji, the fermented rice culture used in miso and sake production, breaks down the meat's proteins slowly. The result is a bird that's noticeably more tender than a standard rotisserie chicken, with a savory depth that plain salt-and-herb marinades don't reach.

The kitchen pairs that chicken with a curated wine list, a detail that signals intent. This is a restaurant angling for a relaxed-but-elevated dinner crowd, not a grab-and-go stop.

## Ordering like it's a wine dinner, not a chicken shop

Treat the pairing as the point. Pluck's wine list is curated, meaning the by-the-glass selection is likely chosen specifically to cut through the richness of koji-roasted skin and fat.

Ask your server for their pairing suggestion rather than defaulting to a familiar label. A curated list at a rotisserie-focused restaurant usually reflects deliberate choices by someone who tasted the chicken against each bottle first.

Come hungry enough to share a whole bird if the format allows it. Rotisserie chicken restaurants built around a single centerpiece protein tend to reward ordering the full portion rather than splitting attention across too many small plates.
