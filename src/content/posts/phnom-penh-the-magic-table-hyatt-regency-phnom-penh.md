Most hotel dinner shows put the trick on a stage across the room and hope you can see it past someone's shoulder. The Magic Table flips that: the illusion happens on the white tablecloth in front of you, projected from a rig in the ceiling, so the "stage" is wherever your plate is sitting.

## What actually happens at your table

The concept comes from a partnership with The Clairvoyants, the mentalist-magician duo who placed second on America's Got Talent with an act built on rapid-fire mind-reading and sleight of hand. Their visual language, cards, ink, light, shifting shapes, gets adapted here into projection mapping beamed down onto each table.

Practically, that means:

- Animated effects appear to spill across your tablecloth, react to plates and glasses, and sync with a live performer working the room
- The illusion is timed to hit during specific courses rather than running as background visuals throughout
- Because the trick depends on precise alignment between projector and table, seating is arranged to specific spots rather than open picnic-style seating

It's closer to dinner theatre than ambient lounge music, so expect the room's attention to swing toward the show at intervals rather than a constant murmur of conversation.

## Where to find it inside the Hyatt

The experience runs inside Hyatt Regency Phnom Penh, the international five-star property on the Cambodian capital's riverside side of town. As a hotel-run concept dining event rather than a standalone restaurant, it typically operates as a special seating or ticketed evening rather than a walk-in dinner service.

Ask the hotel concierge or check Hyatt Regency Phnom Penh's own events page for which restaurant or function space hosts it on a given night, since a concept like this can rotate rooms as the projection setup is installed and struck.

## Why a magic duo and a hotel restaurant paired up

Hotel dinner shows usually lean on a single magician doing tableside card tricks between courses, which is a low-tech format that's existed for decades. This one is different because the show is engineered into the room itself: the projection mapping is the trick, not a garnish on top of one.

Pairing with The Clairvoyants specifically borrows a recognizable act with actual television pedigree rather than an unnamed in-house performer, which is likely the draw for anyone who caught their AGT run and wants to see the visual style up close, at a table, in Phnom Penh rather than on a screen.

## Reading the room before you book

Because this is a recently launched concept, the operating rhythm (which nights it runs, how many seatings per evening, whether it's a set menu or à la carte) is still something you should verify directly with the hotel rather than assume from any listing, including this one.

Treat any show time, price, or menu you see online as provisional until the hotel confirms it. A concept this technical, tied to projector calibration and a touring-style act, is more likely than a fixed restaurant to shift its schedule as it beds in.

## Getting a table without the projector cutting out

Because the illusion depends on your table sitting in exact alignment under the ceiling rig, where you're seated isn't a preference here, it's part of the mechanics. That likely means:

- Reservations matter more than for a normal hotel restaurant meal, since only tables within the projection zone can host the effect
- Arriving on time counts for more than usual; a table-based show synced to courses is harder to slot latecomers into mid-sequence
- If you're booking for a special occasion (anniversary, birthday), mention it when reserving, since concept dining events at international hotels often build small personalizations into a themed evening

Dress the way you would for any five-star hotel dining room in Phnom Penh: smart-casual is the safe default, and it's worth checking if the venue enforces anything stricter for this particular event.

## Getting to Hyatt Regency Phnom Penh

The hotel sits on the riverside side of the city, within the stretch of Phnom Penh that also holds the Royal Palace, the National Museum and the promenade along the Tonle Sap and Mekong confluence. Most visitors arrive by tuk-tuk or app-based ride rather than public transit, since Phnom Penh has no metro system.

If you're combining the show with sightseeing, the riverside location makes it easy to pair with a sunset walk along Sisowath Quay beforehand, since that stretch gets its own golden light as boats and street vendors set up for the evening.

## Before you lock in a reservation

Confirm three things directly with Hyatt Regency Phnom Penh before finalizing plans: the exact evenings The Magic Table runs, whether it's a fixed-price set menu or à la carte, and how far in advance tables need booking. A concept this new can also sell out its limited projection-aligned seating faster than a standard restaurant reservation would suggest.
