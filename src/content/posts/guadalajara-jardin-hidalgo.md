## A plaza between two churches

Jardín Hidalgo works like a living room for Tlaquepaque. It's a square of clipped trees and planted beds, with paths leading in to a central fountain. Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla, the priest who started Mexico's independence war in 1810, stands on his statue among the benches.

The square sits on Guillermo Prieto in Tlaquepaque's Centro, with a church on each side. The Parroquia de San Pedro Apóstol faces one edge and the Santuario de Nuestra Señora de la Soledad faces another. Both are free to step into. Sunday mass spills families out into the garden afterwards.

## Getting to Tlaquepaque Centro

Tlaquepaque is its own municipality, roughly 7 km southeast of Guadalajara's cathedral. Your options:

- **Light rail:** Line 3 of Guadalajara's SITEUR system stops at Tlaquepaque Centro station. From there it's a short walk through town to the plaza.
- **Rideshare or taxi:** ask for "Jardín Hidalgo, Tlaquepaque Centro". Some streets near the square are pedestrian-only, so expect to be dropped a block or two away.
- **Driving:** weekend parking near the centre fills up. Use one of the paid lots at the edge of the historic core.

## What surrounds the garden

On its own the garden takes 20 minutes. Most visitors make it the anchor of a half-day loop, and these are all within a few minutes' walk:

- **El Parián:** a large enclosed square of restaurants and bars ringed by arches, right beside the garden. It's known for mariachi bands playing at the tables.
- **Calle Independencia:** a pedestrian street full of galleries, ceramics and blown-glass shops. Tlaquepaque's pottery tradition is the reason most people make the trip.
- **Museo Regional de la Cerámica:** on Independencia, with a collection of Jalisco pottery styles.

Allow two to three hours if you plan to browse the shops and eat.

## Claiming a bench before the crowds

The garden never closes, but crowds hit hard at fixed times. Weekends from 11am to 11pm are its busiest stretch. That's when the benches fill and the paths into El Parián get congested. Weekdays, or weekend mornings before 11am, are your best chance at a seat in the shade by the fountain. The only measured peak is that weekend block, so anything outside it should feel calmer.

How it works on the ground:

- **Mariachis** in El Parián charge per song. Settle the price with the band before they play, not after.
- **Street vendors** around the plaza usually want cash in small pesos. Card readers are uncommon at carts, though more common in the shops on Independencia.
- **Church etiquette:** keep your voice down and your shoulders covered in San Pedro and La Soledad. Don't take photos during mass.
- **Evenings** are when families come out to stroll. Weekday evenings give you that atmosphere without the full weekend crowd.

This is one of the most visited squares in the Guadalajara area and it's consistently well rated. You won't have it to yourself. Timing your visit is the only way to get some breathing room.
