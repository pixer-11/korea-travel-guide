Nearly everyone's time on Koh Tao begins and ends on this concrete jetty. It pushes out from Mae Haad village into the Gulf of Thailand, and big catamarans pull in alongside it. Passengers come down the gangway two at a time while crew pass bags hand to hand onto the boards. Behind you there's a short strip of ticket offices, dive shops and pickup taxis, all squeezed between the water and the island's hills.

It's a well-known place and busy most of the day, so don't expect a quiet corner. What it gives you is the island's working rhythm up close: arrivals, departures, fishing boats, and the west-facing water at the end of the day.

## What you're standing on

Mae Haad Pier is a working port first. It's where Koh Tao connects to the mainland and to the rest of the Samui archipelago. The high-speed catamarans run the popular routes:

- **Chumphon**, the usual mainland link for travellers coming by train or bus from Bangkok
- **Koh Phangan**, a short hop south
- **Koh Samui**, for its airport and onward connections
- **Surat Thani**, including the slower overnight boats

Lomprayah and Seatran Discovery are the names you'll see most often on the offices facing the pier. Their timetables change with the season and the sea state. Check the current schedule with the operator or at their counter, not a departure time you found on an old forum.

## The west-coast light

The pier faces west, and that shapes how it feels as the day goes on. Late in the afternoon the sun drops over the open water beyond the boats. Once the day's last big departures have gone, the jetty becomes a reasonable place to stand with a drink from a nearby shop and watch it.

This isn't a viewpoint with a railing and benches. Treat it as a bonus you get for being there. For a proper sunset, most people walk north to Sairee Beach, the island's long western strand.

## Arrivals: the ten-minute scramble

When a full catamaran docks, the next ten minutes run to the same routine. Passengers come off first. Then luggage is unloaded in a pile or a line on the jetty, and everyone crowds in to find their own bag.

At the landward end, taxi drivers and resort staff hold up signs and call out destinations. Koh Tao's taxis are mostly pickup trucks with bench seats in the back. Each boat arrival brings a wave of them.

A few things make it easier:

- **Tag your bag with something bright.** Dozens of near-identical backpacks come off each boat.
- **Know your accommodation's area name**, such as Sairee, Chalok Baan Kao or Tanote Bay, before you get off.
- **Agree the taxi fare before you climb in.** Drivers usually set fares by destination, so ask first.
- **If your resort offers a pickup, book it ahead** and look for your name on a sign.

If you're staying in Mae Haad itself, you can simply walk off the pier with your bag and skip all of this.

## Catching your boat without the crush

Leaving is where planning pays off. Weekends from 8am to 9pm are the pier's busiest hours. If you're departing on a Saturday or Sunday in that window, expect lines at the offices, a packed waiting area and slow luggage drop-off.

How departures usually work here:

- **Buy tickets ahead** from the operator's website, a travel agent in Sairee, or the company office by the pier. Leave it to the morning of departure and you risk a sold-out boat in high season.
- **Arrive well before departure.** Operators ask passengers to check in in advance, and at busy times the check-in line itself takes a while.
- **Exchange your voucher for a boarding pass or sticker** at the counter. Some operators give you colour-coded stickers for your shirt and bag that match your onward destination, so the crew know where you and your luggage get off.
- **Keep valuables and seasickness tablets in a day bag.** Big bags go into a luggage area, and you won't reach them during the crossing.

On a rough-sea day, the outer decks get spray and the lower cabins get stuffy. Sit near the back and low down if you're prone to motion sickness.

Etiquette on the jetty is simple. Queue where the staff point you, keep the gangway clear, and let crew handle the heavy lifting of bags onto the boat. Payment at the pier offices and for taxis is still mostly cash in Thai baht. ATMs and convenience stores sit along the road behind the pier.

## Mae Haad village around the pier

The streets directly behind the pier are compact and practical. You'll find ferry offices, dive schools, small restaurants, convenience stores, pharmacies and ATMs within a few minutes' walk. That makes it an easy place to sort out cash, a SIM card and snacks before a crossing.

Mae Haad is a sensible base if you have an early boat. It's also good if you'd rather be central than on a beach. The island's main road runs north and south from here, so both ends of Koh Tao are a short ride away.

## Getting to and from the pier on land

Koh Tao is small, and the pier sits roughly in the middle of the west coast. Your options:

- **On foot:** Sairee Beach is north of the village, walkable with a light bag along the road.
- **Pickup taxi:** the standard way to reach Chalok Baan Kao in the south, or the east-coast bays such as Tanote. Some of those roads are steep.
- **Scooter:** widely rented on the island. The hills and loose surfaces on the smaller roads are a known hazard for inexperienced riders, so be honest about your skill.

Boat trips to Koh Nang Yuan, the three linked islets off the northwest coast, can also be arranged from the Mae Haad area. Ask at the ferry and tour offices near the pier.

## Timing your visit

The pier never closes, but it's only really active around boat schedules. Early morning and late afternoon departures bring rushes of people. The gaps in between are much calmer. At night, the jetty is mostly used by overnight boat passengers and fishing crews.

Weekends are the time to plan around. The 8am to 9pm stretch on Saturdays and Sundays is when the pier is busiest. On weekdays the same boat schedule applies, but with less of a crowd at the counters.

Most visitors spend only as long as a ferry takes: an hour either side of a crossing. If you're simply curious, twenty minutes on the jetty around sunset is enough to take in the boats and the light.
