## Green ground at Giza Square

Orman sits right where Giza traffic is thickest. Cairo University's domed hall rises on one side, the Giza Zoo is across the road, and microbuses pull in and out along Ahmed Zewail Street. Once you're through the gate, the noise drops behind hedges and old trees, and the paths split off into flower beds and shaded lawns.

The garden goes back to the 1870s. It was laid out for Khedive Ismail as part of his palace grounds, during the years he was remaking Cairo along European lines. The name comes from the Turkish word for forest. Its job then was to supply plants and shade for the palace, and that history still shows in how mature the trees are.

## What grows where

The garden is big, so choose a route. The main sections are:

- **Rose patches**: formal beds that are at their fullest in late winter and spring.
- **Cactus and succulent area**: rows of spiny shapes in sandy beds, a reminder that the desert starts not far from here.
- **Papyrus**: the reed of ancient Egyptian paper, growing in and around water features.
- **The herbarium**: Orman's scientific side, a collection of preserved plant specimens.
- **Lawns and tree-lined walks**: where most Cairenes actually spend their time.

The garden is sometimes described as a plain park, which undersells it. Many of the plants are labelled, and the mix of species is broader than in Cairo's other green spaces.

## Spring, and the flower show

Spring is when Orman matters most to the city. The garden traditionally hosts Cairo's annual spring flower exhibition, with nurseries and growers setting up stalls of plants, seedlings and pots. It's usually held around March and April, but dates change every year, so check local listings before you plan around it.

Winter, from November to February, is the most comfortable time for a long walk. Summer afternoons are hot even under the trees. In those months, come early in the day or as the light fades.

## Reaching the gate

The Cairo Metro is the simplest way in. Take **Line 2** to **Cairo University station**. From there it's a short walk east through Giza Square towards the zoo and the university gates, and Orman's entrance faces the square.

A taxi or ride-hailing app works well too. Ask for "Hadiqat al-Orman, Giza" and the driver will know it. The Pyramids of Giza are a separate trip further west, so don't expect to walk between them. A morning here does pair easily with the zoo across the road, or with a walk along the Nile corniche nearby.

## Doing Orman the Cairene way

Orman is one of Giza's best-known parks, and families treat it as their own. That shapes how you should plan your visit.

- **Pay at the gate in Egyptian pounds.** Tickets are sold at the entrance, and the current price is posted there. Carry small notes, because change can be short.
- **Avoid Fridays and public holidays if you want quiet.** Families arrive with picnic blankets and children. Sham el-Nessim, the spring holiday after Coptic Easter, is famously packed.
- **Come early on a weekday.** Before the midday heat and the after-school rush, you'll mostly share the paths with walkers and gardeners.
- **Dress modestly.** It's a family space. Covered shoulders and knees fit in, and you'll want them for the sun anyway.
- **Ask before photographing people.** Couples on benches and families on the lawns come here for privacy as much as for the greenery.
- **Stay on the paths around the beds.** The rose and cactus sections are working collections, not lawns.

You'll find drinks and snacks for sale near the gates. Still, bring water on warm days, because the garden is bigger than it looks from outside.

## Hours and timing

Google currently lists Orman as open 3:30am–11:00pm every day of the week. As a botanical garden, though, its hours can change with the season and on holidays, so confirm on the day before going early or late. For the light, the hour after opening and the late afternoon are best: the low sun on the trees and beds makes the walk cooler and the photos softer.

Plan **1–2 hours** for a proper loop. Allow longer if the flower show is on or you want to see the herbarium.
