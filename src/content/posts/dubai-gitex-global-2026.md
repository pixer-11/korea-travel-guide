## Five days of tech in December

GITEX Global 2026 runs from Monday, December 7 to Friday, December 11. For decades the show was an October fixture, so a December edition is a real change for regular visitors. It's one of the largest technology exhibitions anywhere, and exhibitors and delegates fly in from across the world.

Expect hall after hall of stands. Telecoms operators, cloud providers, AI startups, cybersecurity firms and government digital agencies sit side by side. Conference stages run talks alongside the show floor. Startup activity usually has its own zone, separate from the large corporate pavilions.

## Finding the halls

Dubai World Trade Centre organises GITEX. The organiser publishes the venue, hall map and ticket links on the official GITEX Global site. Earlier editions filled the halls of Dubai World Trade Centre on Sheikh Zayed Road.

Both of Dubai's big exhibition grounds sit on the Metro Red Line:

- **Dubai World Trade Centre:** World Trade Centre station, with a covered walkway into the complex.
- **Dubai Exhibition Centre, Expo City Dubai:** Expo 2020 station, the Red Line's southern terminus branch.

Taxis and ride-hailing queue at the main gates, but Sheikh Zayed Road jams at rush hour during big shows. That's why many delegates take the Metro. A Nol card covers Metro, buses and trams.

## Passes and badges

GITEX sells several visitor and conference pass tiers. Each one gives different access to the show floor and conference stages. The organiser publishes pass names, inclusions and prices on its official site, and this guide quotes no figure.

Registration is online. Visitors normally collect a printed badge or show a QR code at entry. Security screening sits at the hall entrances, so morning lines form around the opening hour. The organiser publishes each day's opening times in the official event app and on its website.

## Working the show floor

GITEX rewards a focused route. The floor is vast and crossing it end to end takes real time. Seasoned attendees use the official app to map the pavilions they care about by hall number, then cluster their meetings within one or two halls each day.

- **Dress:** business or smart-casual is the norm. Indoor halls run heavily air-conditioned, so a light jacket helps.
- **Networking:** most people swap details by scanning LinkedIn or badge QR codes. Some delegates still carry printed cards, especially for government and Gulf corporate contacts.
- **Weather outside:** December is Dubai's mild season, with warm, dry days and cooler evenings. Walking between buildings is comfortable.
- **Friday:** the UAE weekend is Saturday and Sunday, so Friday is a working day. Midday Friday prayers do shorten some local schedules, and visitors often keep that window free of meetings.

Visitors usually stay near the venue they're attending. That means Downtown and Trade Centre hotels for DWTC, and Jebel Ali or Dubai South for Expo City. Hotel rates across the city typically climb sharply during GITEX week, and repeat attendees treat this as a given.

Evenings tend to fill with side events and receptions. Those not on the circuit head for Dubai Mall and the Burj Khalifa fountain shows in Downtown, a few Metro stops from World Trade Centre.
