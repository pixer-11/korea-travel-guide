## Three days on Al Ain Road

The 2026 edition runs from November 27 to 29. It returns to The Sevens Stadium, a purpose-built rugby complex beside Al Ain Road (E66) on Dubai's inland desert fringe. Dubai has staged a sevens tournament since 1970, and the event moved to this site in 2008.

The main stadium carries the international matches. Dubai has traditionally hosted a round of the World Rugby Sevens Series. Across the surrounding grass pitches, club, school and invitational teams play their own tournaments at the same time. You can drift between elite rugby and amateur matches within a few minutes' walk.

Late November is the start of Dubai's comfortable season. Days are warm and sunny and evenings turn cool, which suits a full day outdoors in the stands.

## Getting out to The Sevens

The Dubai Metro does not reach the stadium. Allow roughly 30 to 40 minutes by road from Downtown Dubai or Dubai Marina, depending on traffic on the E66 and Emirates Road.

Fans usually get there in one of these ways:

- **Ride-hailing:** Careem and Uber both operate in Dubai, alongside metered RTA taxis.
- **Organised coaches and shuttles:** these have run from central points in previous years. The organiser lists the transport options for each edition.
- **Driving:** the complex has large surface car parks, but leaving after the final match is slow.

The organiser publishes dates, venue details and ticket links on the official Emirates Dubai 7s website. Ticket types differ in which days and seating areas they cover.

## Reading the stands like a regular

Two groups fill the stands. One is there for the rugby. The other is there for the costumes, and the weekend is famous for fancy dress. Groups of friends arrive as matching superheroes, astronauts or national mascots, and the costumes get bigger as the weekend goes on.

The final day is when the knockout matches decide the titles. That makes it the fullest day in the main stadium. Earlier days are better for wandering the outer pitches and catching local club sides.

- **Seats:** many fans go for the open terraces and move between stands. Others stay put in one block with their group for the whole day.
- **Sun:** the stands are mostly uncovered, so regulars wear hats and sunscreen even in November.
- **Dress code:** costumes are fine inside the venue. Outside it, Dubai's public decency norms still apply on the trip home.
- **Leaving:** taxi and ride-hailing queues build fast after the last whistle. Groups often stay for the evening entertainment or walk to a pickup point away from the main gate.

## Making a weekend of it

A rugby day takes up most of the daylight, so attendees tend to spend the evenings back in the city. Downtown Dubai, with the Burj Khalifa and Dubai Mall, is the nearest big cluster of restaurants to the E66 approach.

Dubai Marina and Jumeirah Beach Residence are further from the stadium. Many visiting fans stay there anyway for the beach and the nightlife, and get a ride-hail to the ground each morning.

Al Qudra, further inland along the desert roads, is the place for an off-day bike ride or sunset drive. It is closer to The Sevens than most of the coast.
