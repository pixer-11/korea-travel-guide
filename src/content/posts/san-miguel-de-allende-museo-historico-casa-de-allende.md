## The house the town is named for

Ignacio Allende was born here in 1769. The town took his surname in 1826, adding it to San Miguel to honour a man executed for starting Mexico's independence war. Few birthplaces explain a place's name so directly.

The house stands at Cuna de Allende 1, on the corner of the Jardín, the main square. The pink sandstone spires of the Parroquia de San Miguel Arcángel rise next door. Look up at the baroque stone facade for the Latin inscription *Hic natus ubique notus*: "Here born, everywhere known."

Despite the address, the museum stays fairly quiet. Most visitors head for the Parroquia and walk straight past the door.

## Inside: two floors around a courtyard

The rooms are arranged around a central patio, with history galleries on one level and furnished domestic rooms on the other. The house belonged to a wealthy criollo family, and the furnishings show how a prosperous household lived in 18th-century New Spain.

The galleries cover more than Allende. They trace the region's story from pre-Hispanic settlement through the colonial silver economy to the 1810 uprising. Allende's part in it is the thread that ties the rooms together:

- He served as a captain in the Queen's Dragoons regiment, quartered in San Miguel.
- He conspired with the priest Miguel Hidalgo, whose Grito de Dolores in September 1810 opened the revolt.
- He was captured and executed in Chihuahua in 1811.
- His head hung in a cage at the Alhóndiga de Granaditas in Guanajuato until 1821.

## Getting to Cuna de Allende 1

Once you are in the historic centre, you walk. Every street in Zona Centro leads to the Jardín, and the museum sits on its corner by the Parroquia. You cannot miss it.

The nearest airports are Bajío International in León and Querétaro International, roughly 1.5 hours away by shuttle or taxi. Long-distance buses arrive at the Central de Autobuses west of the centre. A short taxi ride brings you to the square.

The centre is all steep cobblestones, so parking is scarce. If you are driving in for the day, leave the car in a paid lot near the edge of the centre.

## Planning around the closed days

The museum is closed Monday and Tuesday. If you are only in town early in the week, you will miss it, so plan around that. Wednesday to Sunday the doors open at 9am and close at 4:15pm, earlier than many visitors expect. Don't leave it for a late-afternoon slot.

- **Go early.** A 9am start puts you inside before the day-trippers from Querétaro and Mexico City fill the Jardín.
- **Expect Spanish.** Much of the labelling is in Spanish. A translation app with camera mode makes the history galleries far more rewarding.
- **Tickets.** The museum is run by INAH, Mexico's national heritage institute. Buy your ticket at the desk inside. Current admission is posted there and on INAH's official site.
- **Pace yourself.** The house has a staircase and the historic floors are uneven. Take the upper level slowly.

Afterwards, cross to the Parroquia, then sit on a wrought-iron bench in the Jardín. Allende's sword, his execution and his renamed town will make more sense from the square he grew up on. To follow the story further, Dolores Hidalgo is under an hour's drive north. Guanajuato's Alhóndiga, where his head was displayed, is about 1.5 hours west.
