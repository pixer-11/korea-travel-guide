## A city that gets three New Years

Cambodians have several chances to start the year. Khmer New Year, Chaul Chnam Thmey, falls in mid-April and is the big family holiday. Chinese New Year brings red lanterns to Phnom Penh's shophouse streets in January or February. December 31 is the international one, and Celebrating Cambodia is how the capital marks it.

The festival is an annual year-end event of festivities and performances. It sits on Cambodia's official December events calendar. This edition takes place on **December 31, 2026**, so it is an evening into midnight occasion, not a daytime fair.

## What the night is built around

The event is about performance and celebration. The facts for this guide do not include a lineup, so no acts are named here. Think of the night as a public send-off for the year, with a stage as its focus, not a ticketed concert by one headliner.

The December setting matters too. This is the dry season, and evenings are the coolest and least humid of the Phnom Penh year. Standing outdoors at night is comfortable in a way it is not in April or during the summer monsoon. Light cotton clothing and a thin layer for after midnight suit most visitors.

## Moving through a Phnom Penh crowd on December 31

Getting around on the night works the way it does on any busy evening here, only more so. Most visitors travel by tuk-tuk (the Cambodian remork, a motorbike pulling a covered carriage) or by ride-hailing app. **PassApp** and **Grab** both operate in the city and let you see the fare before you ride. That avoids haggling at a kerb full of other people trying to leave too.

Some habits that make the night smoother:

- **Paying:** US dollars and Cambodian riel both circulate. Change for small purchases often comes back in riel, so small notes of both are useful at street stalls.
- **Pickup points:** After a big event, drivers usually wait a few streets back from the crowd. Walking two blocks out before requesting a ride usually gets you picked up faster.
- **Greetings:** The sampeah, palms pressed together at chest height with a slight bow, is the polite way to thank someone. A smile and "arkun" (thank you) go a long way.
- **Monks:** Monks often walk through public spaces. Women traditionally avoid touching them or handing items to them directly.

Phnom Penh crowds are generally good-natured, but pickpocketing and snatch theft from passing motorbikes do happen. Phones carried on the side away from the road and bags worn across the body are the local norm.

## Folding the night into a Phnom Penh stay

Most visitors base themselves near the riverside or in **BKK1**, the leafy district south of Independence Monument packed with guesthouses and cafés. Both are a short tuk-tuk ride from the central landmarks.

The days around New Year are a good time to see the city's major sights in cooler weather:

- **The Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda**, where shoulders and knees must be covered
- **The National Museum**, a red Khmer-style pavilion holding Angkorian sculpture
- **Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum** and the **Choeung Ek** killing fields, sobering visits that take most of a morning
- **Wat Phnom**, the hilltop temple that gives the city its name
- **Central Market (Phsar Thmei)**, the yellow Art Deco dome

New Year's Day is a public holiday in Cambodia. Some offices and smaller shops keep shorter hours on January 1, while markets and tourist-facing businesses mostly stay open.

## Who the festival suits

Celebrating Cambodia suits travellers who are already in Phnom Penh at the turn of the year and want to spend midnight with the city, not in a hotel bar. It is a public celebration from the national events calendar, not a club night. Families, groups of friends and solo travellers tend to mix together in the crowd.

If you care most about Khmer tradition, the April New Year is the deeper cultural experience. December 31 is the capital looking outward, with music, crowds and a shared countdown.
