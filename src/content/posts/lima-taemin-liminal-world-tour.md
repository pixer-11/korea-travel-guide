## Concert-night habits in Lima's K-pop crowd

Peru has one of Latin America's most organised K-pop fandoms, and its customs shape the night.

- **Fan projects:** coordinated banners or lightstick colours are often organised for specific songs. Fan accounts on X and Instagram usually spread the instructions, and joining in is welcome.
- **Lightsticks:** fans who own the official lightstick bring it. Paper slogans are the cheaper common alternative.
- **Paying:** cards are widely accepted in Miraflores and San Isidro. Locals pay small vendors with soles in cash or with the mobile wallets Yape and Plin, which visitors without a Peruvian bank account usually cannot use.
- **Language:** a few words of Spanish go a long way with vendors and drivers. "¿Cuánto es?" (how much is it?) and "gracias" cover most exchanges.

Phone filming rules differ from tour to tour, and venue staff enforce whatever the production sets. Fans in front sections usually keep phones low out of courtesy to the people behind them.

## Lima beyond the arena

Lima in early November is in late spring. Skies are often grey and overcast, rain is rare, and evenings turn cool enough that a light layer is the usual choice for a night outdoors or in transit.

Travelling fans often add a day or two for the city itself:

- **Barranco:** street murals, the Puente de los Suspiros and bars in old mansions.
- **Historic centre:** the Plaza Mayor, the Cathedral and the Monastery of San Francisco with its catacombs, part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Food:** ceviche at lunchtime, which is when Limeños traditionally eat it, plus lomo saltado and anticuchos.
- **Huaca Pucllana:** an adobe pyramid that predates the Inca, standing among Miraflores apartment blocks.

For a first solo show in a new country, the city around it is a good part of the trip.
