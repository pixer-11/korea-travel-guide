## A market with a food street wrapped around it

YongLe Market (永樂市場) is two places at once. Inside is a working neighbourhood market. Outside, along Section 3 of Guohua Street, the market's frontage and the shopfronts facing it form one of Tainan's densest runs of snack stalls.

The building is an older multi-storey block with flats stacked above the market floor. At street level you get low stools, steel counters and handwritten menus, with scooters parked in every gap. The draw here is regional specialties: noodle and rice dishes in small portions, made to be eaten quickly and followed by the next stall.

This is not a quiet find. It is one of the most-reviewed food spots in Tainan, and on weekends Guohua Street fills with people walking and eating.

## What to eat on Guohua Street

Tainan food runs to small, slightly sweet dishes, and this block covers most of the classics. Stalls to look for, on the market side and across the road:

- **Wa gui (碗粿):** savoury rice pudding steamed in its own bowl, topped with a thick sauce. Fu Sheng Hao (富盛號) is the long-running name here.
- **Spring rolls (春捲):** soft wrappers rolled around vegetables, meat and peanut powder. Jin De (金得) is the stall people queue for.
- **Squid rice noodles (小卷米粉):** thin rice noodles in clear broth with chunks of squid. Several stalls on Guohua Street sell them.
- **Fried and braised snacks:** fish-paste items, oyster dishes and pork rice bowls are sold along the same stretch.

Portions are small on purpose. Four or five stops make a full meal, and the whole loop takes about an hour to 90 minutes.

## Grazing without the crush

Most stalls take cash only, and there is no tipping in Taiwan. Keep coins and small notes ready so the line keeps moving.

At busier stalls you tick a paper order sheet, hand it over and find a stool. At others you just point and say the dish name. Either way, seating is shared: take any free stool and give it up once you've finished. Don't hold a table while a friend queues somewhere else.

Share every dish between two or three people so you have room for more stalls. Hours vary a lot from stall to stall, and many shut on a weekday or once they sell out, so check what's open before you commit to a single famous name. Arriving soon after the morning market opens, or on a weekday, usually beats the weekend crowds on Guohua Street.

## Getting there and what's nearby

The address is No. 123, Section 3, Guohua Street, in the West Central District's old core. From Tainan Railway Station it is roughly a 25-minute walk west, or a short taxi or city-bus ride. Scooter parking is easy. Car parking is scarce, so leave the car at a paid lot further out.

The market also makes a good first stop on a walk through old Tainan:

- **Shennong Street (神農街):** restored shophouses and lanterns, a short walk west. Best in the evening.
- **Haian Road:** murals and bars that fill up after dark, just beyond Guohua Street.
- **Chihkan Tower (赤崁樓):** the Dutch-era fort site, walkable to the east along Minzu Road.

Eat here first, then walk it off among the temples and lanes of the West Central District.
