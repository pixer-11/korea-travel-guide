## A new grill with a known team

Ashe brings two well-known names together. The Bentley Group built its reputation on serious, wine-led dining in Sydney, with Bentley Restaurant + Bar as its best-known room. Ashe takes that group into new territory: a grill built around Southeast Asian cooking.

Khanh Nguyen leads the kitchen. Food-minded readers will know his name from Melbourne, where Sunda and Aru made him one of the country's most closely watched chefs. Those restaurants drew on Southeast Asian flavours with modern Australian technique. That background is the main reason Ashe made so many lists of Sydney's best new openings of 2026.

## What to expect at the table

You are booking a grill, so plan around smoke, char and heat. Across Southeast Asia, grilling means skewers, marinated proteins and charred vegetables, served with sharp, sour and fragrant sauces. That is the tradition Ashe's name and format point to.

We have not been given the menu, and a new kitchen changes it often. Don't go in with a fixed dish in mind. Read the menu on the day, and ask the floor staff:

- which dishes come straight off the grill
- how many plates suit your group
- what changes with the season or the week

With the Bentley Group behind it, the wine list is worth asking about. Ask for a pairing suggestion, especially with spicy or sour plates.

## Booking a table at a new opening

Ashe is new and in demand, so tables go quickly. Book online through the restaurant's or the Bentley Group's official site as early as you can, especially for Friday and Saturday dinner. Confirm the hours before you travel. New openings often add or drop lunch service and closing days in their first year.

If you can't get a booking, check whether the restaurant keeps seats for walk-ins. Many Sydney restaurants hold a few bar seats back, and an early dinner gives you the best chance. Treat that as something to ask about, not a promise.

- **Check first:** current address, hours and walk-in policy, on the official site
- **Book:** online, as far ahead as possible
- **Group dinners:** ask about set menus or minimum spends when you book

## Paying and tipping in Sydney

Sydney dining runs on cards and phones. Contactless is the default, and some restaurants add a small card surcharge, which the menu or bill will show. Many places also add a surcharge on Sundays and public holidays. It should be printed on the menu, so read the fine print before you order.

Tipping is not expected in Australia, because hospitality wages are set by award rates. At a restaurant of this level, many diners round up or leave around ten percent when service is good. Leaving nothing is not rude. If the bill already includes a service charge for a large group, you don't need to tip on top.

Smart casual clothing suits a Bentley Group room. Nobody will check your shoes, but most diners dress up a little for dinner.

For a full evening, pair Ashe with a drink nearby beforehand, and allow about two hours at the table. A grill menu meant for sharing is a slow, many-plate meal, not a quick dinner. Booking any later than mid-evening can shorten your meal.
