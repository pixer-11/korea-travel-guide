Out on the planks, the boats line up along both sides. Catamarans with tour company names on their hulls, the smaller Russell passenger ferry, water taxis tied up between them. Paihia Wharf is less a sight than the Bay of Islands' front door. Nearly every trip onto the water starts with a walk down this pier.

It is one of the best-known spots in Northland, and it is consistently well rated. It does not offer quiet, though. It works like a small harbour terminal with a sea view.

## A pier that works like a terminal

Most of the wharf is moving people. Booking offices for the bigger operators sit at the shore end, close to the Bay of Islands i-SITE visitor centre on Marsden Road. That is the place to compare trips, pick up timetables and ask about the weather out at Cape Brett.

Beyond the offices, the pier runs straight out into the bay with berths on either side. From the end you look across the water to Russell and the islands beyond. Between departures you may see people fishing off the sides. Plenty of visitors walk out simply for the view.

## The boats worth catching

Your plans will decide which berth you need. The main departures are:

- **Hole in the Rock cruises**: out to Motukōkako, the pierced island off Cape Brett, sea conditions permitting.
- **Dolphin and wildlife trips**: the bay is known for bottlenose and common dolphins.
- **Urupukapuka Island**: the largest island in the bay, with walking tracks, beaches and a café near Otehei Bay.
- **Sailing and fishing charters**: often smaller boats with smaller groups.
- **Water taxis**: for island drop-offs on your own schedule.

Half-day cruises suit most first visits. Budget a full day if you plan to combine a cruise with an island stop.

## Crossing to Russell

The short passenger ferry ride across to Russell is the simplest trip of all. It takes only a few minutes and lands you on The Strand. That is Russell's waterfront, home to Christ Church, the oldest surviving church in New Zealand, and Pompallier Mission.

Note that this boat carries foot passengers only. Drivers who want their car in Russell take the vehicle ferry from Opua, south of Paihia, to Okiato. Fares and sailing times are posted at the wharf and on the operators' sites.

## Getting here and parking

Paihia is roughly a three-hour drive north of Auckland, following State Highway 1 and then State Highway 11. The nearest airport is Bay of Islands (Kerikeri), about half an hour's drive away. Intercity coaches stop in central Paihia, a short walk from the pier.

The wharf sits right on Marsden Road, along the town's main waterfront strip. You can reach most accommodation in Paihia on foot. In peak season, street parking near the wharf fills up early. If your hotel is within walking distance, leave the car there.

## Boarding without the scramble

The wharf has no single queue. Every operator boards its own boat, and staff at the shore-end offices tell you which side to walk to. Get your berth number when you collect or check your booking. That way you won't be wandering along the pier five minutes before departure.

- **Book ahead in summer.** From Christmas to late January Kiwi holidaymakers fill the boats, and popular cruises can sell out.
- **Pay by card.** Booking offices accept cards, and many trips can be paid for online.
- **Ask about weather calls.** Trips to Hole in the Rock can change route or be cancelled when the sea is rough. Operators usually rebook or refund.
- **Bring a layer.** Even on a warm day it gets breezy out past the heads, and the top deck catches the wind.

Since this is a working pier, keep out of the boarding lanes when you're not boarding. Waiting groups stand clear of the gangways while crew handle the lines.

## Along the waterfront

Walk away from the pier in either direction and you're on the beach. The Waitangi Treaty Grounds sit about 2 km north and are an easy walk along the shore and over the Waitangi bridge. The Treaty of Waitangi was signed there in 1840, and the grounds hold the carved meeting house and a large waka. Allow at least half a day.

Head south and the coastal track leads on toward Opua. Back on Marsden Road, cafés, ice-cream shops and takeaways face the water. The pier is the classic spot for an evening walk, since the 24-hour listing means nothing locks you out after the last boat has gone.
