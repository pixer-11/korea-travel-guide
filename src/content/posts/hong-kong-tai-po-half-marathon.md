## A race on the edge of Tolo Harbour

Tai Po is a different Hong Kong. The district sits at the head of Tolo Harbour in the New Territories, where the city thins into low hills, river mouths and long cycle tracks along the water. It is a residential town with an old market core, so the towers stand further apart than they do in Kowloon.

The Tai Po Half Marathon takes place here on December 27, 2026. The race offers four distances: 1K, 3K, 10K and the half marathon (21.1K). The spread means the whole field is mixed, from children doing a single kilometre to club runners racing the full 21.1.

## Four distances, one morning

Four distances change who turns up. The 1K and 3K are short enough for kids, older relatives and first-timers. Those starts usually look more like a family outing than a race.

The 10K is the step-up distance for newer runners and a fast tempo effort for regulars. The half marathon is the main event.

Which distance suits you:

- **1K:** children and families, or anyone who has never pinned on a bib
- **3K:** casual joggers and walkers who want a finish line without training
- **10K:** regular runners and people building towards longer races
- **Half marathon:** trained runners looking for a late-season result

The organiser sets the course, start area and wave times for each distance and publishes them on its official site. This guide does not guess at the route.

## Late-December running weather

The date is part of the appeal. Late December falls in Hong Kong's dry season, after the typhoon months and well before the humid spring. Mornings in the New Territories are often noticeably cooler than downtown, because the district is greener and less built up.

That suits distance running. Runners who know Hong Kong only through its sticky summers tend to be surprised how comfortable a December race feels. Dawn can still be chilly near the water, though. A throwaway layer for the wait at the start is common kit.

The timing also fits the local racing calendar. Hong Kong's biggest road race, the Standard Chartered Hong Kong Marathon, is usually held in the first months of the year. A December half is a natural tune-up for runners aiming at it.

## Getting to Tai Po on race morning

The MTR is the simplest way in. Tai Po is on the **East Rail line**, which runs north from Admiralty on Hong Kong Island through Hung Hom, Kowloon Tong and Sha Tin towards the border at Lo Wu and Lok Ma Chau.

The two stations serving the town:

- **Tai Po Market station:** the main interchange, with a bus terminus beside it
- **Tai Wo station:** one stop north, closer to the northern housing estates

From Admiralty the ride to Tai Po Market takes roughly 40 minutes. From Kowloon Tong it is closer to 20. Visitors usually stay in Kowloon or Sha Tin and come up by train rather than overnighting in Tai Po, which has few hotels.

The start area is set by the organiser. On race mornings a lot of participants travel together, so the trains towards Tai Po tend to fill with people already in running kit.

## Race-day habits in Hong Kong

The Octopus card does most of the work. One tap covers the MTR, buses and green minibuses, and it pays at convenience stores like 7-Eleven and Circle K. Many visitors now tap a contactless bank card or phone at MTR gates instead.

Runners here usually keep things simple on the morning:

- **Pre-race food:** convenience stores near the stations sell water, sports drinks, bananas and rice balls
- **Bag drop:** many local runners skip it and arrive in race kit with only a phone and an Octopus card
- **Family support:** supporters tend to gather near the finish rather than along the course

No tipping is expected at cha chaan teng or cooked food stalls. Sit-down restaurants usually add a 10% service charge to the bill. Small family places and market stalls often prefer cash or Octopus, so carrying both saves an awkward moment at the till.

The place name is 大埔 in Chinese, pronounced roughly "dai-bo" in Cantonese. It means "big port", which makes more sense once you see the harbour.

## After the finish line: old Tai Po

The streets around Tai Po Market station hold the district's history. They are an easy walk for loosening tired legs.

- **Hong Kong Railway Museum:** set in the old Tai Po Market station building from 1913, with a Chinese-style roof and vintage carriages on the tracks outside
- **Man Mo Temple on Fu Shin Street:** a late-19th-century temple at the end of a narrow market street lined with dried goods, fruit and herbal shops
- **Tai Po Hui Market:** the municipal market complex, with a cooked food centre upstairs serving cheap Cantonese plates and milk tea
- **Tai Po Waterfront Park:** a long harbourside park with a lookout tower and wide lawns facing Tolo Harbour

Opening hours for the museum and park change, so their official pages carry the current times.

## Making a day of the New Territories

Tai Po is a base for the wider north-east. The Lam Tsuen Wishing Trees, where people throw paper offerings into the branches at Lunar New Year, are a short bus ride west of town.

To the east, a cycle track follows the shore towards Tai Mei Tuk and Plover Cove Reservoir. Bike rental shops cluster near Tai Wo station and Tai Mei Tuk. Riding an easy flat loop the day after the race is a popular way to spin out stiff legs.

Heading south, the same cycle track runs along the Shing Mun River to Sha Tin. There, the Hong Kong Heritage Museum and the Ten Thousand Buddhas Monastery turn race weekend into a fuller trip.
