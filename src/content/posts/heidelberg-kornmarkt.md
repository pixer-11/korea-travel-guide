## A square tilted toward the castle

Look up from Kornmarkt and Heidelberg Castle fills the frame. The red sandstone ruin sits on the wooded Königstuhl slope directly above, near enough to pick out its windows. Most visitors take in this view from the Old Bridge or the Philosophenweg. From here it is shorter, steeper and much more personal.

The square itself is compact and cobbled, and it rises gently toward the hill. Old townhouses line its edges. In the middle stands the Kornmarkt Madonna, an early-18th-century Baroque statue of Mary on a tall column above a fountain basin. The name means "grain market": this is where corn was once traded.

## Getting to Kornmarkt

The square is easy to find. It sits one short block east of the Marktplatz, the square with the Heiliggeistkirche (Church of the Holy Spirit) and the Rathaus.

- **On foot:** walk the Hauptstraße to the Marktplatz, then continue east past the Rathaus. The Old Bridge (Alte Brücke) is about five minutes north.
- **By bus:** the nearest stop is Rathaus/Bergbahn, on the Neckar side of the old town.
- **To the castle:** the Bergbahn's Kornmarkt station is on the square's edge. The funicular climbs to the castle and continues to Molkenkur and the Königstuhl summit.
- **Walking up:** the Burgweg footpath starts close by. It is a short, steep climb on cobbles to the castle gardens.

Plan on 15–30 minutes for the square on its own. It makes a natural stop between the Marktplatz and the ride or walk up to the castle.

## When the light works

The square is open 24 hours, so the time you choose comes down to light and people. The castle faces west over the old town. Late afternoon sun warms the sandstone, and in the morning the facade sits in shade.

After dark the castle is usually floodlit. With the column in the foreground, that makes a good night shot without a crowd elbowing in. Early morning works too: delivery vans come and go, and you get the Madonna and the castle with almost nobody else in the picture.

Because most tour groups head straight for the Bergbahn or the Marktplatz, many people pass through without stopping. That is the square's quiet advantage.

## Lingering at Kornmarkt properly

The old town is mostly pedestrian, but the area still gets cyclists, delivery vehicles and people dragging suitcases. Stand on the uphill side of the column for photos. It keeps you out of the main walking line and gives the best angle for fitting both the statue and the castle in.

- **Bergbahn tickets:** buy at the Kornmarkt station machines or counter before you queue. Check the current fares and timetable on the official Bergbahn site, as they change with the season.
- **The fountain:** treat it as a monument. Sitting on the basin rim is fine, but climbing the column is not.
- **Paying nearby:** card payment is common in German cafés now, but small bakeries and kiosks still sometimes prefer cash.
- **Sundays:** shops on the Hauptstraße close, so the old town is calmer, and the square is quieter still.

If you are tackling the Burgweg, start your climb from here rather than doubling back from the Marktplatz. And save Kornmarkt for the end of your castle visit, when the view from below makes sense of everything you have just walked through.
