## Granite at eye level

From down in Plaza del Azoguejo, you crane your neck to see the aqueduct. From this viewpoint on Calle San Juan, you look along it. The arches are dry-stacked granite blocks with no mortar, a two-tier Roman structure that has carried its channel through Segovia for nearly two thousand years.

At the Azoguejo the arches reach their tallest, at roughly 28 metres. From here you look down the line of the double tier into the square. The aqueduct and the old town have been on UNESCO's World Heritage list together since 1985.

The view is from an open street spot, not a building, so you won't find a ticket desk or a gate. It gets far fewer reviews than the crowded square below, though the ones it has rate it highly. That gives it a quieter feel than anywhere else this close to the arches.

## Getting to Calle San Juan

Every route into Segovia ends at the Azoguejo, and the viewpoint is a short uphill walk from there.

- **From Madrid by train:** high-speed services run to Segovia-Guiomar station, outside the centre. City buses connect it to the Azoguejo.
- **From Madrid by bus:** intercity coaches leave from the Moncloa interchange and arrive at Segovia's bus station. From there you walk to the aqueduct.
- **On foot in Segovia:** from Plaza del Azoguejo, take Calle San Juan uphill beside the arches. The address is number 12.

Allow 15–20 minutes for the viewpoint itself. It's one stop on a walk through the old town, not a half-day outing.

## Timing the light and the people

BestTime's forecast shows weekdays are usually calmest from 8pm to 9pm. That hour also catches evening light on the stone, depending on the season.

Weekends are a different story. Try not to arrive between 8am and 9am, 10am and 12pm, or 3pm and 9pm. Those windows cover most of a Saturday or Sunday, so if a weekend is your only option, aim between them. Better still, come on a weekday evening.

Google lists the spot as open 24 hours. Because it's public street space, you can come back after dark to see the arches under their floodlights.

## Sharing a viewpoint on a residential street

Calle San Juan is a lived-in street, not a plaza. Here's how to fit in:

- **Keep to the edge.** The space is small. Take your photo and step aside rather than setting up for long stretches.
- **Leave the tripod folded at busy times.** It blocks the narrow pavement. Save long exposures for the quiet weekday evening hour.
- **Lower your voice late.** People live in the houses here, and sound carries between the stone walls.
- **Don't climb the aqueduct.** Sitting on the base blocks or scrambling on the stonework damages a monument held together by nothing but its own weight.

Afterwards, walk up into the old town. Calle Real climbs past the faceted granite front of the Casa de los Picos and the arcaded Romanesque church of San Martín. It continues to the Plaza Mayor and the Gothic cathedral, and the Alcázar sits at the far end of the ridge.

If you're hungry, Segovia's signature dish is cochinillo asado (roast suckling pig). The Mesón de Cándido, at the foot of the aqueduct, is known for its show of carving the pig with the edge of a plate. Menus with current prices are posted at the door.
