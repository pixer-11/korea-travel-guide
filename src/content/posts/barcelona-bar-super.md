El Born has no shortage of wine bars, but Bar Super earns its name by doing one thing with real focus: natural wine, poured by people who clearly taste everything before it goes on the list. It sits close enough to the Santa Caterina market that the kitchen leans on what the stalls are selling that week, which shows up on the plate rather than on some laminated menu.

The location matters. Santa Caterina, with its wavy tiled roof by architects Enric Miralles and Benedetta Tagliabue, anchors this stretch of El Born and pulls a mix of market shoppers, market workers on break, and locals who've made the short walk from the Gothic Quarter or the Born's own tangle of medieval lanes. Bar Super sits in that current, close enough to duck into after browsing the market stalls, far enough from Passeig del Born to avoid the tourist-menu crowd.

## What's actually on the table

The kitchen works Italian-Catalan, which in practice means market vegetables treated simply, cured meats and cheeses that need no dressing up, and pasta or small plates built to match a glass rather than fill a table. Expect the menu to shift with what the market had that morning rather than staying fixed season to season.

- Low-intervention, natural wines by the glass and bottle, with staff who'll talk you through what's pouring
- Italian-Catalan small plates meant for sharing, not individual mains
- A menu that leans on Santa Caterina's produce and changes often

Don't go in expecting a printed wine list you can study in advance online. Part of the appeal here is asking what's open and letting the person behind the bar steer you.

## Finding it near the market

Bar Super sits in El Born, within easy walking distance of Santa Caterina market itself. The closest metro stops are Jaume I (Line 4) and Urquinaona (Lines 1 and 4), both a short walk through the Born's narrow streets. If you're coming from the Gothic Quarter or the beach end of Barceloneta, it's an easy add-on to a wander rather than a destination you need to plan a whole evening around.

Because it's a newer opening riding a wave of local word-of-mouth, treat any hours or contact details you find online as provisional. Confirm the day's opening hours and whether they're taking reservations before you set out, especially if you're coming from across town.

## Ordering and settling the bill

This isn't a place with table service and a laminated wine list dropped in front of you. At bars like this in Barcelona, you typically order at the bar or flag down staff for recommendations, then run a tab that gets settled at the end.

- Ask what's being poured that day rather than requesting a specific bottle by name
- Small plates are for sharing: order two or three and let the table graze
- Card payment is standard in Barcelona bars now, but carrying some cash is still a good habit
- Tipping isn't expected the way it is in the US; rounding up or leaving small change is plenty

Because the space is small and the crowd is genuinely local, arriving right at opening or going for a late-evening glass rather than mid-dinner rush usually gets you a spot without a wait.

## Why it stands out from other Born wine bars

El Born has plenty of wine bars trading on exposed brick and a vague "natural wine" label without much behind it. What separates Bar Super is the pairing: a kitchen that treats Italian and Catalan market cooking as one continuous idea, next to a wine list that isn't just trendy orange wine for its own sake.

The market proximity does real work here too. A lot of Barcelona's wine bars import their identity from somewhere else; this one is anchored to Santa Caterina's produce, which gives the food a reason to change and a reason to taste fresh.

## Making it part of a Born evening

Pair a stop here with an actual walk through Santa Caterina market if you haven't seen the roof and stalls yet, then let Bar Super be the wind-down rather than the main event. The surrounding streets, Carrer de la Princesa, the Picasso Museum's stretch of Carrer Montcada, are all close enough to fold into the same evening without much backtracking.

Keep the visit loose. Part of what makes a spot like this work is not overplanning it: show up hungry-ish, thirsty, and ready to order whatever the person pouring tells you to.
