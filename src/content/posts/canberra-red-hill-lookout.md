## A city laid out like a drawing

Canberra makes sense from up here. Walter and Marion Griffin designed the capital around hills, lake and long sightlines. Red Hill is one of the summits where that plan reads clearly, the way it does on paper.

Look north and Parliament House sits in the foreground with its flagmast. Behind it, Lake Burley Griffin cuts across the city, and Mount Ainslie closes off the far end. Turn west and Black Mountain rises with Telstra Tower on top. Further out, the Brindabella Ranges fold into blue along the horizon.

## What's at the summit

The top is a ridge of dry eucalypt woodland with a road ending in a car park. Viewing areas face several directions, so you can walk a short loop and take in the whole basin. It's an open, exposed hilltop. Wind comes straight across it, even when the suburbs below feel still.

Red Hill Nature Reserve covers the slopes. It's grassy woodland with scattered gums, and eastern grey kangaroos graze there, most visibly around dawn and dusk. Stay well back if you meet them on a track. Bird life is constant: magpies, cockatoos and parrots work the trees around the car park.

## Driving or walking up

Most visitors drive. Red Hill Drive branches off Mugga Way and climbs to the summit car park. The suburb of Red Hill and the Manuka and Kingston shops are a few minutes downhill, so it pairs easily with a coffee or a meal in the inner south.

Walking is the better way to earn the view. Several tracks climb the reserve from its edges. The Canberra Centenary Trail also crosses the hill, so you can fold the lookout into a longer walk. Expect a steady uphill on gravel and dirt. Allow:

- **30–60 minutes** at the top if you drive
- **2–3 hours** for a walk up and back from the base
- **Half a day** if you link it into the Centenary Trail

Cyclists use Red Hill Drive as a training climb. Drivers need to give them room on the bends.

## Beating the weekend rush

This is a quieter lookout than Mount Ainslie, which gets most of the tour traffic, and it still feels like a find. Weekends are a different story. Crowd forecasts show the summit usually busy from 8am–10pm on Saturdays and Sundays. The car park fills and the viewing areas get crowded.

The fix is simple: arrive between 7am and 8am, which is the quietest weekend window. Early light also comes in low from the east across the lake, and kangaroos are more likely to be out on the slopes then. If you can only come later, park lower down and walk up. The tracks are far emptier than the summit.

## Doing it the Canberra way

Canberrans treat Red Hill as a backyard hill, not a sight. It's a workout first and a view second. A few habits are worth copying:

- **Bring a layer.** Canberra winters are cold, frosty mornings are common, and the summit is windier than town.
- **Carry water** on the walk up. The tracks are exposed, with little shade on hot summer afternoons.
- **Keep to marked tracks** in the reserve. The woodland is protected habitat.
- **Watch for kangaroos on the road** when you drive at dawn or dusk. That's when they cross.
- **Take your rubbish down with you.** This is a nature reserve, not a park with staff clearing up.

Google currently lists the lookout as open 24 hours. Road access and conditions can still change, so check the signs at the bottom of Red Hill Drive, especially after dark.

## When the light is best

Sunrise and sunset are the obvious picks. In the morning the lake and Parliament House light up from the east. In the evening the sun drops behind the Brindabellas and the city lights come on below. Clear winter days give the sharpest long views. Morning fog sometimes fills the valleys and leaves the hilltops sitting above it.

Spring adds wildflowers in the grassy woodland, and autumn turns the exotic trees in the inner-south streets gold below you. For the full picture, combine Red Hill with Mount Ainslie on the same day. You get the city from the south and then from the north, along the line Griffin drew between them.
