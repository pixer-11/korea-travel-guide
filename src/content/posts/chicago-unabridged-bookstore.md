Broadway curves gently through Lakeview here, past nail salons and diners, and Unabridged sits at street level with its name painted across the front window in plain block letters. No café bolted on, no merch wall by the door. Just a bookstore, floor to ceiling, the way the neighborhood has known it for decades.

## What the shelves actually hold

The store is compact from the sidewalk but the inventory runs deep once you're inside. General fiction and nonfiction fill the main aisles, but two sections are the real reason people cross the city for this address.

- LGBT literature: one of the most substantial sections you'll find in a Chicago bookstore, spanning fiction, memoir, history and poetry
- Children's books: a full room's worth, handled by staff who clearly read the stock, not just shelve it
- Staff picks tucked into most sections, handwritten and specific rather than generic "bestseller" tags

Lakeview has long been a center of Chicago's LGBTQ community, and the store's catalog reflects that history rather than treating it as a token shelf near the register.

## Finding it off the Belmont stop

The store sits at 3251 N Broadway, about a ten-minute walk east from the Belmont station, where the Red, Brown and Purple Line trains all stop. Buses along Broadway and Halsted also pass close by. There's no dedicated lot, so most visitors arrive on foot or by transit rather than driving in and hunting for parking on a residential side street.

## A shop that never varies its hours

Unabridged keeps the same schedule seven days a week: 10am to 7pm, no early closing, no Sunday shrink. That consistency is unusual for an independent bookstore and makes it one of the easier stops to fold into a Lakeview afternoon without checking a clock first. Because the hours never dip, there's no "off day" to avoid; the only real planning question is how much of Broadway you want to walk afterward.

## Browsing without a plan

This isn't a store built for a five-minute grab-and-go. The aisles are narrow enough that you'll end up shoulder to shoulder with another browser at some point, and that's normal here, not a sign to leave.

- Ask staff directly if you're looking for something specific; the store rewards a conversation more than a self-guided search
- Check the staff-pick cards before you check your phone for reviews, they're usually more useful
- If you're buying for a kid, say the age out loud to whoever's at the counter; the children's section is organized in a way that makes sense once someone walks you through it
- Bring a tote or bag of your own if you're planning to leave with more than one book, the aisles get tight to maneuver with an armful

## The children's room as its own destination

Plenty of general bookstores carry a children's shelf. Unabridged carries a children's section big enough that parents come specifically for it, independent of anything else in the store. It's worth budgeting real time here rather than treating it as a side stop on the way to fiction, especially if you're picking something out with a kid in tow rather than for one.

## Paying and leaving

Like most independent bookstores of this size, expect a straightforward counter checkout, card or cash, no loyalty app to download before you leave. If you want a specific title and aren't sure it's in stock, calling ahead or asking staff directly is more reliable than assuming the shelf will have it; a store this well-curated turns over stock rather than warehousing everything in the back.

Because it's well-known rather than obscure, don't expect an empty store on a Saturday afternoon. Weekday mornings shortly after the 10am opening tend to be the calmest stretch, before the after-work and weekend browsing picks up.

## Nearby if you want to make an afternoon of it

Lakeview rewards a slow walk. Broadway and Halsted both have diners, coffee shops and bars within a few blocks, and Wrigley Field is close enough for a detour if baseball season lines up with your visit. Boystown's rainbow-lit pylons run along Halsted just west of the store, tying the neighborhood's LGBTQ history to what you'll find on Unabridged's own shelves.
