## A mountain in the middle of the city

Montrealers just call it "the mountain." Frederick Law Olmsted, who also designed New York's Central Park, laid out the park, and it opened in 1876. Instead of a flat lawn, you get a wooded hill with downtown's towers below it on one side and the Plateau's rooftops on the other.

The main route is Chemin Olmsted, a wide gravel road that switchbacks gently through maples and oaks. Narrower dirt paths cut off it into the woods. At the summit sits the Chalet du Mont-Royal, a big stone pavilion. In front of it is the Kondiaronk Belvedere, a semicircular terrace where the city opens out toward the St. Lawrence River.

## Where to go once you're up

- **Kondiaronk Belvedere:** the classic downtown panorama, right beside the Chalet.
- **Mount Royal Cross:** the steel cross on the eastern summit, lit up at night and visible from much of the city.
- **Beaver Lake (Lac aux Castors):** a shallow pond with lawns around it. Boats are for hire in the warm months, and in winter it becomes a skating spot.
- **Smith House:** the stone visitor centre run by Les amis de la montagne, with maps and trail information.
- **George-Étienne Cartier Monument:** on Avenue du Parc, at the park's eastern foot.

Give the walk up and the lookout 2–3 hours. If you want the lake and the cross as well, plan for half a day.

## Getting up the mountain

From downtown, walk north on Peel Street until it ends at Avenue des Pins. Wooden stairs climb from there to Chemin Olmsted, and from the top of the stairs it's a steep 20–30 minutes to the Chalet. If you'd rather not climb, take the Orange Line to Mont-Royal station. Bus 11 runs from there across the mountain on Chemin Remembrance and stops near Beaver Lake and Smith House.

There are pay parking lots near Beaver Lake and Smith House. Rates are posted at the pay stations. On busy weekends the lots fill up, and public transit is the easier choice.

At the moment the park is open 6am to midnight. Park hours and services can change with the season, so check the city's official park page before a late visit.

## Doing the mountain on Montreal's terms

Locals treat the mountain as a daily habit, not a sight. They come early, they come often, and they come in every season.

- **Go at 7am–9am.** This is the quietest window on weekdays and weekends alike, so you get the belvedere railing mostly to yourself.
- **Avoid weekends from 11am to 9pm.** That's when the lookout terraces, the Chalet and the lawns at Beaver Lake are at their fullest.
- **Find the Tam-Tams.** On summer Sundays, drummers and dancers gather at the Cartier Monument on Avenue du Parc. Combine it with an afternoon climb from the east side.
- **Keep right on Chemin Olmsted.** Runners and walkers share the road, and in winter people bring cross-country skis and snowshoes.
- **Say "bonjour" first.** Staff at the Chalet and Smith House answer in English or French, and a French greeting is the local courtesy.
- **Pack layers.** The summit is often windier than downtown. From late fall, the shaded paths can be icy long after the streets are clear.

For a full loop, climb the Peel stairs, take in the view, walk along to the cross, and come down the east side to Avenue du Parc. From there it's a short walk into the Plateau.
