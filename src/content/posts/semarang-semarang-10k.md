## Race morning on Jalan Pemuda

Jalan Pemuda is usually a long, honking boulevard of motorbikes. On race morning the traffic is gone and runners in matching bibs fill the road outside City Hall instead.

The Semarang 10K takes place on Sunday, December 13, 2026, with the start and finish at Balai Kota Semarang. It is a city road race over 10 kilometres. That is long enough to need proper training, and short enough that plenty of first-timers line up next to club runners.

## Balai Kota and the streets around it

Balai Kota Semarang is the city government's headquarters. It faces Jalan Pemuda, the old colonial-era main road connecting the Tugu Muda roundabout with the Kota Lama (Old Town) district. Most of what a visitor wants to see is within a few kilometres of the start line.

- **Tugu Muda and Lawang Sewu**: the independence monument and the "thousand doors" Dutch railway building, roughly a kilometre west along Jalan Pemuda.
- **Kota Lama**: the restored Dutch quarter around the domed Gereja Blenduk, a short ride east.
- **Simpang Lima**: the city's main five-way square, with malls, hotels and night food stalls.

The facts we have cover only the start and finish. The organiser sets the course itself, so we don't describe the route here.

## Dates, registration and where details live

The race date is December 13, 2026. The organiser publishes registration, entry categories, start time and race-pack collection details on its official channels. That is the place to look for anything this page leaves out, including entry fees, which we don't print.

City road races in Indonesia usually include a race-pack collection day before the event. Runners pick up their bib, timing chip and race shirt there, and the bib has to be worn on race day. In Indonesia this is called *pengambilan race pack*, and it is often held at a mall or hotel near the venue.

## Running 10K in a Java December

December falls in Central Java's rainy season. Semarang sits on the north coast, so it is hot and humid even before sunrise. Downpours usually come in the afternoon and evening, but an early-morning shower is perfectly possible.

That climate explains why Indonesian road races usually start around dawn, while the air is still at its coolest. Experienced runners on this coast tend to:

- carry a light cap rather than a heavy one, because it gets soaked either way
- drink steadily the day before, not just at water stations
- run with a slower target pace than they would in a cooler city
- pack a dry shirt and a plastic bag for the post-race wet kit

## Race-day habits in Semarang

Getting to the start line works differently on race morning. Roads around Balai Kota are usually closed for the race, so ride-hailing drivers on Grab and Gojek drop runners at the edge of the closure. Many runners book hotels within walking distance of Jalan Pemuda to skip the drop-off scramble altogether.

Paying is easy. QRIS, Indonesia's standard QR payment, is accepted by most food stalls and small shops, and plenty of street vendors also take cash. Vendors with coffee, bottled water and *nasi bungkus* (rice wrapped in paper) usually set up near big start areas.

A few local courtesies are worth knowing:

- Say *permisi* ("excuse me") when you squeeze past people in the starting pen.
- Thank volunteers at water stations with *terima kasih*.
- Many runners are Muslim, and modest race kit is common. Nobody expects visitors to match it, but very skimpy outfits will stand out.

## After the finish: lumpia and old Semarang

The run is over before mid-morning, which leaves the rest of the day for eating. Semarang is known for *lumpia Semarang*, spring rolls filled with bamboo shoots, and many shops along Jalan Pandanaran sell them along with *bandeng presto* (pressure-cooked milkfish).

Other local dishes to try:

- **Tahu gimbal**: fried tofu and shrimp fritters in peanut sauce.
- **Wingko babat**: a dense coconut cake, the classic edible souvenir.

Lawang Sewu and Kota Lama both make easy afternoon walks on tired legs. Kota Lama is flat and compact, and its cafés fill the old warehouses around Gereja Blenduk.

Most visitors fly into Jenderal Ahmad Yani International Airport or come by train to Semarang Tawang or Semarang Poncol station. Both stations are a short ride from City Hall.
