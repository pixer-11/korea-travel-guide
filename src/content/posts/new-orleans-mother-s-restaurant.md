## Why go

Mother's has been dishing out roast beef po' boys and red beans since 1938, and the room still feels like it hasn't updated its playbook since — in a good way. You order at a steam-table counter under a wall of yellowed newspaper clippings and photos of regulars, then carry your own tray to a Formica table while a cafeteria line of staff calls out orders behind glass.

It's loud, bright, and unapologetically old-school, the kind of place where the smell of frying seafood and simmering gravy hits you before you're even through the door.

## Getting there

The address is 401 Poydras St, right at the edge of the Central Business District, a few blocks from the Mississippi River and an easy walk from the Riverfront streetcar line or the Poydras Street stops on the Canal Streetcar. If you're coming from the French Quarter, it's a flat 15–20 minute walk down Poydras or a short cab/rideshare ride.

Because it sits so close to the convention center and hotel row, it also fills up fast with conference crowds and tourists heading to or from the Quarter — factor that into your timing.

## What to eat

Order at the counter the way regulars do: the "Ferdi Special" is the signature move, a po' boy stacked with debris — the shredded roast beef and drippings left over from slow-roasting — plus baked ham, gravy, and dressed with lettuce, tomato, and mayo. The baked ham itself, cured and slow-cooked in-house, is what put Mother's on the map decades ago and still shows up in half the dishes on the board.

For breakfast, the biscuits and grits or a plate of eggs with that same debris gravy are the move, especially since the doors open at 7am daily and breakfast service runs the full length of the morning. Sides worth grabbing include red beans and rice and the mac and cheese, both steam-table classics that taste like they've been made the same way for generations.

## When to go

Mother's opens at 7am and stays open until 10pm every day of the week, so there's real flexibility here — this isn't a lunch-only spot. The measured foot-traffic pattern shows the calmest stretches are early mornings and the off-peak weekday hours; weekends are a different story; from 11am to 9pm on Saturday and Sunday the line can snake toward the door as brunch crowds, tourists, and conventioneers all converge at once.

If you can manage an early breakfast or a late-morning visit before 11, or swing by mid-afternoon on a weekday, you'll skip the worst of it.

## How to visit like a local

This is a cafeteria-counter operation, not table service — walk up, scan the handwritten and posted menu boards above the steam table, and have your order ready before you reach the register, because the line moves fast and staff expect decisiveness. Payment is by card or cash at the counter, and since it's counter service there's no need to tip the same way you would at a sit-down restaurant, though a jar for counter staff is common practice around town.

The single biggest mistake visitors make is showing up right at the start of the weekend lunch rush expecting a quick in-and-out; because this spot is genuinely one of the most visited restaurants in the city rather than a quiet local secret, the smart move is timing your visit for opening hour or the mid-afternoon gap between lunch and dinner crowds.

Grab a tray, don't wait for a host, and expect to share table space during peak hours — it's a communal, elbow-to-elbow kind of room. If you want the classic experience without the wait, come hungry right when the doors open and order the debris-and-gravy breakfast plate while the dining room is still half empty.

## Nearby

Once you've eaten, Mother's location puts you within walking distance of the National WWII Museum, the Warehouse Arts District's gallery row, and the riverfront path along the Mississippi — all reasonable ways to walk off a heavy po' boy before heading back toward the Quarter.
