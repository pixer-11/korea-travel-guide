## A record bar on the business island

Abu Dhabi's newest late-night room is all about sound. Saikindō sits inside the Four Seasons Hotel Abu Dhabi, on Al Maryah Island, the financial district across a short channel from the main island. By day this is a place of glass towers and lanyards. After dark, Saikindō turns part of the hotel into a listening bar.

The idea comes from Japan. There, small bars and jazz cafés built their reputation on high-end speakers and record collections, where the music came first and the room was tuned around it. Saikindō takes that approach and adds two more things: cocktails built around musical themes, and a robata dining space for a proper dinner.

## What the listening-bar format means for your night

A listening bar is not a club. The sound system is the centrepiece, and vinyl sets the pace of the evening. Expect a room designed for hearing records clearly rather than shouting over them.

That shapes how the evening goes. People come to talk at a low volume, sip something considered and let a record play out. If you want a dance floor, this is the wrong address. If you want a drink where the music actually gets heard, it is the right one.

The cocktail list leans into the music theme. We have not been given the menu, so ask the bartender to walk you through it. In a bar like this, that conversation is part of the point.

## Dinner at the robata

The second half of Saikindō is a small robata dining room. Robata is the Japanese style of grilling over charcoal, traditionally with the cooks working in front of guests. It suits a long evening: small plates arrive as they come off the grill, and you order a few at a time.

The setting is described as intimate, so think of a compact room rather than a hotel ballroom restaurant. That makes booking more important, especially for dinner. A sensible plan is to eat at the robata first, then move to the bar for records and a final drink.

## Getting to Al Maryah Island

Abu Dhabi has no metro, so you will arrive by car. The usual options are:

- **Taxi:** the official cream-coloured Abu Dhabi taxis are easy to find, and hotel staff can call one for the ride home.
- **Ride-hailing:** Careem and Uber both operate in the city; enter "Four Seasons Hotel Abu Dhabi at Al Maryah Island".
- **Driving:** the hotel has valet at the entrance.

The hotel stands next to The Galleria Al Maryah Island mall and the waterfront promenade. That makes an early-evening walk along the water, or dinner beforehand at the Galleria, an easy warm-up. Ask the hotel lobby team to point you to Saikindō once you arrive.

## Booking, dress and paying at Saikindō

Treat this as a reserved evening, not a walk-in. Saikindō is newly opened, and hours and booking policies at new venues often shift in the first months. Check the Four Seasons Abu Dhabi website or call the hotel before you go, and book the robata if you want dinner.

A few practical points for a hotel bar in Abu Dhabi:

- **Age:** the legal drinking age in Abu Dhabi is 21. Carry your passport or Emirates ID, because staff can ask.
- **Dress:** smart casual is the norm at five-star hotel bars here. Collared shirts and closed shoes are the safe call; beachwear is not.
- **Paying:** cards and contactless payment are accepted almost everywhere in the city's hotels.
- **Tipping:** many UAE hotel bills already include a service charge. Read the bill first, and add a little extra only if service stood out.
- **Volume:** in a listening bar, keep phone calls outside and conversation low while the record plays.

Do not drive after drinking. The UAE has a zero-tolerance limit for drink-driving, and penalties are severe. Order a taxi or Careem instead.

## Who it suits

Saikindō is aimed at people who care about the details of an evening. Music lovers will want to see how the room is tuned. Couples will appreciate a small dining space and a bar where you can hear each other. Business travellers staying on Al Maryah Island get a destination without leaving the building.

Come later in the evening, when the island quiets down and the bar is doing what it was built to do. Allow two to three hours for dinner and drinks. If you are only stopping in, a single drink and one full side of a record takes about an hour.
