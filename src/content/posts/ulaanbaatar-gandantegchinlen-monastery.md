## Up the hill west of the centre

Gandan is a working monastery and not a museum. Maroon-robed monks cross the courtyards between classes, and rows of prayer wheels line the walkways.

The complex stands in Bayangol district, roughly 2km west of Sükhbaatar Square. Ulaanbaatar has no metro, so you either walk or take a taxi. The walk from the square takes about 25–30 minutes through Soviet-era blocks, with a gentle climb at the end. A taxi drops you at the main gate in a few minutes when traffic allows.

Gandan dates from the 19th century. It survived the communist purges of the late 1930s, which destroyed most of Mongolia's monasteries. During the socialist decades it was the only monastery allowed to stay open, so it carries more weight here than its size would suggest.

## Inside the Janraisig temple

The tall white temple at the back of the main courtyard holds Gandan's centrepiece. Megjid Janraisig is a gilded standing figure of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion, rising 25 metres through the full height of the hall.

The original statue was taken away by the Soviets in 1938. The one you see now was built after independence and consecrated in the 1990s. Hundreds of small Buddha figures sit in niches along the walls around it.

Elsewhere in the complex:

- **Prayer halls** where monks chant from long Tibetan-script texts, often in the morning
- **Prayer-wheel corridors** around the temple walls
- **The open courtyard**, where pigeons gather and some visitors buy grain to feed them

## Moving through Gandan the right way

Respect matters more here than at a tourist site, because services are going on around you.

- **Walk clockwise** around temples and stupas, and spin prayer wheels clockwise with your right hand.
- **Don't step on the threshold** of a temple door. In Mongolian custom that's bad luck, so step over it.
- **Cover your shoulders and knees**, and take off your hat inside the halls.
- **Don't point your feet** at altars or monks. If you sit at the back during chanting, tuck them under you.
- **Look for photo rules at each hall's entrance.** Photography inside is restricted, and any permit or charge is posted there. Never photograph a monk mid-prayer without asking.

You can stand quietly at the back while chanting is underway. Many locals come in, make an offering and leave in a few minutes, so keep the doorways clear.

## Timing your visit

Arrive early. At the moment Gandan opens 9am–5pm every day, but temple hours shift with the season and around religious holidays, so confirm on the official site before you go.

It's calmest between 9am and 11am on weekdays, and on weekends between 9am and 10am. Try not to arrive after midday on a Saturday or Sunday: from 12pm to 5pm the Janraisig hall fills up, and getting near the statue's base turns into a slow shuffle.

Arriving early has another advantage: morning is when you're most likely to hear chanting in the prayer halls. In Ulaanbaatar's winter, mornings are bitterly cold and the courtyards are exposed, so dress for the walk between buildings.

Afterwards, the walk back east takes you toward Sükhbaatar Square and the National Museum of Mongolia. Together they make a half-day route on foot.
