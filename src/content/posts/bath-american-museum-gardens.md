## A Bath stone house full of America

Bath stone outside, New England inside. Claverton Manor is a pale, columned house designed by Sir Jeffry Wyatville in the 1820s. It stands on a ridge above the Avon valley, well away from the Georgian crescents. Since 1961 it has been the American Museum, the first museum of American decorative arts to open outside the United States.

Its founders, Dallas Pratt and John Judkyn, wanted British visitors to understand how Americans actually lived. So the house is arranged as a run of rooms rather than glass cases. You step from one century into the next through a series of doorways.

## Room by room through three centuries

The period rooms are the core of the visit. Each one is a recreated American interior, from a plain, low-beamed 17th-century room to grand early 19th-century parlours. They are furnished with original pieces, so the chairs, hearths and panelling are the evidence, not the backdrop.

Look out for:

- **The quilts and textiles**, one of the best-known collections of American quilts outside the US
- **Folk art**: painted furniture, weathervanes and portraits by self-taught artists
- **Native American material** in the galleries beyond the period rooms
- **Special exhibitions**, which change and often take up a whole floor

The house has one more footnote. A young Winston Churchill gave his first political speech in its grounds in 1897.

## Gardens with a view of the valley

Outside, the land falls away steeply into the Limpley Stoke valley. The lawns look across woodland to the hills on the far side. You won't get a view like it from anywhere in Bath's city centre.

The gardens are a reason to come in their own right. The best-known part is a replica of George Washington's flower garden at Mount Vernon, with box-edged beds and a white picket fence. Beyond it, paths run through an arboretum of American trees. Wear shoes you can manage a slope in.

Families get a play area as well as the café, the two things the museum itself lists. That makes it an easy half day with children who have had enough of Roman baths.

## Getting up Claverton Down

The museum is about 2.5 miles southeast of Bath Spa station, uphill most of the way. Driving is the simplest option, and the museum has a car park. Approaching from the city, you climb Bathwick Hill and North Road towards the University of Bath campus, then follow the signs along Claverton Down.

Buses to the University of Bath run up the same hill from the city centre and leave you within walking distance. Check the current route and stop before you set off.

If you're walking, the Kennet and Avon Canal towpath runs along the valley floor below. Claverton Pumping Station is down there too. The climb back up to the manor is steep.

## Making the most of a day at Claverton

The museum keeps a season. It is not open all year, and its hours and closed periods shift, with a separate programme around Christmas. Currently it opens 10am–5pm, but the official website has this month's dates. Check it before you plan a trip around a visit.

A few things make the day go better:

- **Do the house first.** The period rooms feel cramped once a group is in them. Arriving near opening usually puts you ahead of the coach parties and school groups.
- **Then go outside.** The gardens and views reward an unhurried walk, and the light across the valley is softer later in the afternoon.
- **Ask in the rooms.** Volunteer guides often stand in the period rooms, and they know the stories behind individual objects.
- **Book exhibitions ahead.** For major special exhibitions, buying tickets online can save time at the entrance. Prices are on the official site.

Most visitors pay by card at the entrance and in the café. Photography rules can differ between the permanent rooms and loaned exhibitions, so check the signs as you go in.

## Why it feels like a quieter find

Compared with the Roman Baths or the Royal Crescent, Claverton is still a quiet find. Its position up on the down keeps it off most walking-tour routes. On many days you will share the Mount Vernon garden with a handful of people rather than a crowd.

Pair it with the canal below, or with Prior Park Landscape Garden on the next hillside. Together they make a full day of green Bath away from the city centre.
