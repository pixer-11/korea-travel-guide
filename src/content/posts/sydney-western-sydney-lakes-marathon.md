## A marathon on Olympic water

Picture a long, straight sheet of water with the Blue Mountains as a low blue wall to the west. That is the Sydney International Regatta Centre. The Western Sydney Lakes Marathon is held here on Saturday, November 21, 2026.

The venue was purpose-built for the rowing and canoe sprint events of the Sydney 2000 Olympics. It sits on reclaimed quarry land in the Penrith Lakes area, near the Nepean River on the city's western edge. The result is a rare thing for Sydney running: wide, open and almost dead flat.

## Dates, tickets and where the details live

The race date is November 21, 2026. That is a Saturday, so most interstate and regional entrants treat it as a one-night trip.

The organiser publishes entry links, start times, any shorter distances and the course map on its official race site. This guide deliberately gives no entry fees or start times. Those details belong to the organiser, and the official page is where they are kept current.

## Running the lakes: what the ground gives you

Regatta courses have to be straight and sheltered, and the land around them follows suit. The paths beside the water are broad, sealed and close to level. That is why the venue draws runners who are chasing a personal best.

A few things are reliably true of the site:

- **Flat profile.** There are no real climbs, so it rewards steady pacing more than hill strength.
- **Open exposure.** There is little tree cover along the water, which means sun and wind hit you directly.
- **Mountain views.** The Blue Mountains escarpment fills the western horizon for much of the day.
- **Spectator sightlines.** On a long, straight course, supporters can watch runners from a long way off.

A marathon held inside an enclosed venue usually means repeated loops rather than a point-to-point route. The official course map shows exactly how this one is laid out.

## The Penrith heat factor

Penrith is regularly one of the hottest places in the Sydney basin. Late November is already well into the warm season out here, and a clear morning can climb quickly once the sun is up.

That shapes how experienced entrants approach the day. Runners usually start their drinking the evening before, wear a cap and sunscreen, and let the aid stations set their rhythm rather than skipping them on the cool early kilometres. With so little shade by the water, supporters typically bring a hat, folding chairs and their own water.

## Getting to the Regatta Centre

The venue is on Old Castlereagh Road, north of Penrith's town centre. Sydney's CBD is roughly 55 km east.

- **By train:** The T1 Western Line and the Blue Mountains Line both stop at Penrith Station. From Central the trip takes around an hour. The station is still several kilometres from the lake, so runners usually finish the journey by taxi, rideshare or a lift.
- **By car:** The M4 Motorway runs straight from the city to Penrith. Take the Penrith exits and head north toward Castlereagh.
- **Where people stay:** Visiting runners usually book in Penrith itself so the drive on race morning is short. Some stay in Parramatta, which sits halfway along the M4.

Race-day parking and any shuttle arrangements are set by the organiser and appear in its event information.

## Race morning at the lake

Mass-start events at large venues tend to follow a rhythm. Runners usually arrive with time to spare to park, collect anything outstanding and queue for the toilets, because those queues are the slowest part of the morning. Bag drop and corrals follow the organiser's printed instructions.

Out on the course, the habits of Australian road racing apply:

- **Keep left** to let faster runners pass. Signal before you stop at an aid station.
- **Drop cups in the bins** or at the volunteer zone, not on the path. This is a working sports venue beside protected water.
- **Thank the volunteers.** Australian runners say "cheers" to them almost by reflex.
- **Supporters stay off the running line.** On a lap course that matters even more, since runners come past again and again.

Paying for things around the venue is easy. Tap-to-pay card and phone payments are standard across Penrith, and tipping is not expected at cafés.

## After the finish: Penrith and the mountains

The Regatta Centre is part of a larger Olympic precinct. Next door is the Penrith Whitewater Stadium, the artificial slalom course from the 2000 Games, where you can sometimes see paddlers practising. The Nepean River foreshore runs close by and has easy walking paths for stiff legs.

The town centre of Penrith has plenty of places for a post-race meal. Many visitors make a weekend of it and drive west into the Blue Mountains on the Sunday. Katoomba and the Three Sisters lookout at Echo Point are about an hour up the Great Western Highway. It is a calm way to spend the day after a marathon.
