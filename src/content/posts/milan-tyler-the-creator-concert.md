## Getting to Fiera Milano Live

The venue sits in Rho, on Milan's northwestern edge, inside the Fiera Milano exhibition complex — not to be confused with the older Fieramilanocity site nearer the centre. The most reliable way in is the M1 (red) metro line to Rho Fiera station, which drops you right at the exhibition centre's entrance; from central Milan (Duomo or Cadorna) that's roughly a 30–40 minute ride.

Suburban rail (the S-lines/Malpensa Express corridor) also stops at Rho Fiera, useful from Malpensa airport or towns further out. For drivers, the complex has large paid car parks, though a summer stadium-scale show means traffic bottlenecks on the surrounding roads for at least an hour after the set ends.

## How to visit like a local

Milanese concertgoers rarely show up right at doors — they eat first. Rho itself is light on restaurants, so the local move is dinner in the city centre, then the M1 out with a bit of a buffer before the set, rather than hours spent hungry near the venue. On the metro and at the gates, cashless payment (contactless card or phone) is standard; bars and merch stands rarely run cash-only.

Queue discipline at Italian arena shows is looser than in northern Europe: people cluster rather than form a strict line, so spots near the front go to whoever turns up well before doors, not to whoever has been waiting longest. The mistake most first-time visitors make is booking a hotel near the Duomo assuming it's "close enough" — it's a solid 40-minute trip each way by metro plus a walk from the station, and the return leg is the crowded one, with M1 trains absorbing the entire audience at once.

The platforms thin out about 15–20 minutes after the last song, which makes for a quieter exit.
