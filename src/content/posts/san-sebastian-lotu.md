## Mugaritz, minus the drive

Mugaritz sits in the green hills above Errenteria, outside the city. For years, eating with Andoni Luis Aduriz's team meant a taxi ride into Basque farmland and an afternoon booked out for the meal.

Lotu brings that team into San Sebastián itself. It's inside the Hotel Palacio Bellas Artes, which has only just opened. The name is a Basque word meaning "to tie" or "to connect". That fits a restaurant trying to bring a country table into a city hotel.

## What the table is built around

The food is organised around the seasons. Expect the menu to change as Gipuzkoa's produce changes, so don't arrive set on a dish you saw online. Nobody has published a fixed menu, and any list you find may already be out of date.

What stays the same is the pace. Lotu describes its dining as leisurely and countryside-style. That makes it a long sit-down meal, not a quick stop on a pintxos crawl. Three things set it apart:

- **The lineage:** it comes from the kitchen team behind one of Spain's best-known experimental restaurants.
- **The setting:** a dining room in a brand-new hotel, not a bar counter.
- **The terrace:** DJ sessions turn the outdoor space into somewhere to stay after you've eaten.

## Booking a table at Lotu

Lotu opened recently, so nothing about it has settled yet. Hours, terrace dates and how bookings work could all change in the first few months. Check the hotel's official website or contact the restaurant directly before you plan an evening around it.

Some San Sebastián habits help here:

- **Book ahead.** The best tables in this city fill early, and a new opening with the Mugaritz name will draw curious diners.
- **Eat on Spanish time.** Locally, lunch usually starts around 2pm and dinner rarely before 9pm. An early table often means a half-empty room.
- **Tip lightly.** Service is built into how Spain prices meals. Rounding up or leaving a little extra for good service is normal, and big percentage tips are not expected.
- **Use a few words of Basque.** *Kaixo* (hello) and *eskerrik asko* (thank you) are noticed in Gipuzkoa, even though staff will switch to Spanish or English.
- **Ask about the terrace.** DJ sessions run on their own schedule, which isn't published here. Confirm the date when you book if the music is the reason you're going.

## Around the evening

Lotu works well as the slow, seated part of a bigger San Sebastián day. Before you eat, walk the curve of La Concha bay to Monte Urgull. After a long lunch, the pintxos bars of the Parte Vieja are close by for a last *txakoli*, the sharp local white poured from a height.

If Lotu gives you a taste for the full experience, book Mugaritz itself well ahead. Its reservations open far in advance and go fast. Lotu is the lighter, easier way into the same kitchen's thinking.
