## Edinburgh opens the run

Edinburgh opens the tour. The Britpop Tour starts here, ahead of every other UK date, and it is the first live outing for songs from Britpop. Opening nights usually show the setlist and staging before anyone has seen them, which is part of the appeal for fans who travel up for the first show.

From Scotland the tour plays on across the UK. It then heads abroad and closes in Lima, Peru, on 12 December 2026, so the Edinburgh date opens a run lasting most of the year.

## What the Britpop album brings to the stage

The album's title points back to the mid-1990s. That is when Williams left Take That in 1995 and started his solo career. Expect the new material alongside the catalogue that made him one of Britain's biggest live acts.

- **"Angels"**: the closing singalong at his shows for decades
- **"Let Me Entertain You"**: his long-time opener
- **"Rock DJ"**, **"Feel"** and **"She's the One"**: staples of his stadium years

His stadium pedigree is long. In 2003 he played three nights at Knebworth to some 375,000 people, still one of the largest UK concert runs ever staged. The setlist for this tour isn't public, so the songs above show what he usually plays rather than a promise.

## Moving around Edinburgh on gig night

Most visitors arrive by train. Edinburgh Waverley, in the valley between the Old Town and the New Town, takes trains from London King's Cross in about four and a half hours. Haymarket, one stop west, sits closer to the West End.

- **From the airport**: the Edinburgh Trams line runs through Haymarket and along Princes Street to the city centre
- **Buses**: Lothian Buses cover the city, and riders tap a contactless card or phone on board
- **Staying over**: visitors usually base themselves in the New Town or the West End, within walking distance of both stations

After a big concert, taxi ranks clear slowly. Many concertgoers walk back toward Princes Street or Haymarket rather than wait.

## Doing an Edinburgh gig the way regulars do

Edinburgh is almost entirely cashless at venues and bars. Card and phone payments are standard at merch stands and food stalls. Tram tickets come from the platform machines or the Transport for Edinburgh app before boarding, and inspectors issue penalty fares to anyone without one.

Large concerts here run under strict bag rules. Regular gig-goers usually bring only a small clear bag. Scottish crowds sing loudly and often, and big Edinburgh shows usually end with thousands of people singing on the walk out.

Fans coming for the opening night usually turn the trip into a weekend:

- **Saturday morning**: the Royal Mile up to Edinburgh Castle
- **Afternoon**: Calton Hill for the view over Princes Street
- **Sunday**: Arthur's Seat, an easy climb if your legs survived the night

Robbie Williams' official website carries the tour schedule, venue information and authorised ticket links. Fans typically buy through those links rather than resale sites.
