## Before the Loop, there's the market

Most visitors pass through Hà Giang city fast. They come off a night sleeper bus from Hanoi, pick up a motorbike and head north toward Quản Bạ and the Đồng Văn Karst Plateau. Hà Giang Market, on Trần Phú street, is where the town buys its food, household goods and everything else it needs.

It gives you a look at the province going about its normal business. You won't find a show put on for visitors here. The market is consistently well rated and has close to a thousand reviews, most of them short and practical. That tells you what it is: a working market that visitors find useful, with no ambitions to be a tourist attraction.

## A daily market, not a Sunday one

This matters when you plan your route. The highland markets that feature in Loop itineraries, such as Đồng Văn and Mèo Vạc, are best known for their weekly Sunday gatherings. Hmong, Dao and other ethnic minority communities walk or ride in from the surrounding villages for those.

Hà Giang Market is a different kind of place. It's the town's everyday market, open seven days a week. If your Loop dates don't line up with a Sunday in the mountains, this is the market you can count on. Treat it as the provincial capital's pantry, not a substitute for a highland market day.

## When to walk in

The hours are long but uneven, so check the day before you go:

- **Tuesday to Sunday:** 5:00am to 7:00pm
- **Monday:** 5:00am to 11:30am only

Monday is the one that catches people out. If you arrive on the afternoon bus from Hanoi on a Monday, the market will already be closed.

For space to look around, go between 1pm and 2pm, Tuesday to Friday. That's the quietest measured window on weekdays, when the morning shopping is finished and nobody has started buying for dinner yet. Avoid weekends if you can: the aisles stay busy from 8am right through to the 7pm close.

For the fullest spread of fresh produce and meat, the early hours after the 5am opening are the obvious choice. Vietnamese markets do most of their fresh trade before breakfast. You'll share the aisles with serious shoppers, not browsers.

## What to look for on the stalls

The fixed facts about this market stop at its address and hours, so treat what follows as a regional shopping list. These are Hà Giang specialities, and it pays to ask for them by name:

- **Thịt trâu gác bếp:** buffalo meat smoked and dried over a kitchen fire, a staple of the northern mountains
- **Rượu ngô:** corn liquor, the drink you'll be offered at homestays along the Loop
- **Bánh tam giác mạch:** buckwheat cake, tied to the pink buckwheat flowers that bloom on the plateau in autumn
- **Chè Shan Tuyết:** tea from old, tall Shan Tuyết trees grown at altitude in the province
- **Cháo ấu tẩu:** a bitter, warming rice porridge the town is known for, usually eaten in the evening

It's also a good place to stock up on the practical things before a multi-day ride. Look for rain ponchos, snacks, fruit and water, and buy them here, not at a roadside stop in the hills.

## Buying without fumbling

Knowing a few habits makes the whole visit easier. In Vietnamese a market is **chợ**, and "Chợ Hà Giang" is enough for any taxi or motorbike driver.

- **Pay in cash, in đồng.** Carry small notes, since stallholders selling a bag of fruit rarely have change for a large one.
- **Point, then ask.** "Bao nhiêu?" (how much?) works everywhere. Many vendors will type the number into a phone or calculator for you.
- **Haggle lightly, and only on goods.** Some friendly back-and-forth is normal for clothes, fabric or souvenirs. Produce and cooked food usually sell at the going rate, so don't push.
- **Ask before you photograph people.** This matters most with ethnic minority vendors. Gesture at your camera and wait for a nod, and if the answer is no, accept it.
- **Watch for motorbikes.** In Vietnamese town markets, scooters often roll right up to the stalls to load. Keep to the side of the aisle.
- **Don't tip.** You pay the price and that's the end of it.

## Getting to Trần Phú street

Hà Giang city is roughly 300 km north of Hanoi. Most people arrive on an overnight or daytime sleeper bus, which takes around six to seven hours depending on the road. The market's address is on Đ. Trần Phú, one of the town's main streets. Type the plus code **RXJP+XFV** into Google Maps to go straight to it.

A note on names: after Vietnam's 2025 provincial mergers, the address now reads Tuyên Quang province, not Hà Giang province. The town, the market and the Loop haven't changed. Only the paperwork has.

The town is compact. From most central guesthouses a short walk or a quick Grab ride will get you there. If you've already rented a bike for the Loop, park where the stallholders park and lock it.

## How long, and what comes after

Plan on 45 minutes to an hour. That's enough to walk the aisles, buy supplies and try something to eat. Stay longer only if you're hunting for a particular item, like good tea or a bottle of corn liquor to share at a homestay.

Most visitors fit the market into the day they arrive or the morning they leave. From Hà Giang city, the road north climbs toward the Quản Bạ Heaven Gate and on through Yên Minh to Đồng Văn and the Mã Pí Lèng Pass. Filling your bags on Trần Phú first means one less thing to look for once you're on the mountain roads.
