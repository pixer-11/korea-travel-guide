## A concrete lotus over the Nile

Look up from the Gezira riverbank and the shaft rises in a tight concrete lattice. The diamond pattern is said to echo the stem of a lotus, and the top flares open like the flower. Up close, the surface is covered in mosaic: around 8 million small lozenges set into the concrete.

The tower went up between 1956 and 1961, under Gamal Abdel Nasser. Architect Naoum Shebib designed it, and at 187 metres it was a statement of post-revolution confidence. It is still the high point of central Cairo. It stands on the island in the Nile between downtown and Giza, so the view from the top covers both banks.

## Reaching Gezira Island

The tower's address is Kasr Al Nile, Zamalek, on the southern half of Gezira Island. The nearest metro is **Opera station on Line 2**. Come out toward the Cairo Opera House, then walk north through the island's gardens. It takes roughly 10–15 minutes and the tower is in view the whole way.

From Tahrir Square, you can also walk west across **Qasr al-Nil Bridge** with its four bronze lions. It's a good approach, because the tower fills more of the skyline with every step. Ride-hailing apps work well here too. Ask to be dropped at the gate on the river side rather than somewhere near the sporting club.

## Inside the tower

At ground level you buy a ticket, pass a security scanner and join the line for the lift. The ride is quick. It lets you out near the top, where a ring-shaped open deck runs all the way around the crown behind a protective barrier.

Walk the full circle slowly. Here's what to look for:

- **East:** downtown, Tahrir Square and the Egyptian Museum's terracotta block, with the Citadel and the minarets of Islamic Cairo beyond.
- **South:** the Opera House complex and the river bending toward Old Cairo.
- **North:** the green lawns of the Gezira Sporting Club and the leafy streets of Zamalek.
- **West:** the Giza sprawl. The Pyramids appear on the horizon when the air is clear.

Plan on 45 minutes to an hour, longer if you stay to eat.

## Beating the haze and the crowds

Cairo's air decides how much you see. Dust and smog often blur the far horizon. Clearer days are more common in the cooler months and after a strong wind, and that's when the Pyramids stand out on the western skyline.

The lift line decides how long you wait. The tower is calmest from **9am–11am on weekdays** and **9am–12pm on weekends**. Try not to arrive on a weekend between **4pm and 11pm**. That's when Cairo families come for sunset and the city lights, and the wait for the lift grows long.

Google currently lists 9am–11pm for all seven days. Hours can change for holidays and events, so check the official listing before you go late in the evening.

## Ticket window and lift-line etiquette

There are two prices at the ticket window: one for Egyptians and a higher one for foreign visitors. That's normal at Egyptian landmarks. The current rates are posted at the window, so look there rather than trusting anyone who offers to buy tickets for you outside.

Carry Egyptian pounds in small notes, in case the card machine isn't working.

- **Security:** bags go through a scanner, so pack light. A large tripod may cause delays.
- **The lift:** it's small, and staff fill it to capacity. Expect to stand close to strangers for the short ride.
- **On the deck:** people take turns at the best spots on the rail. Take your photos, then step back.
- **Restaurant:** the revolving restaurant sits just below the deck. Check the bill for a service charge. Rounding up or leaving a little extra for the waiter is customary.

## Combining it with Zamalek

The tower pairs easily with the rest of the island. The **Cairo Opera House** is a few minutes south, with the Museum of Modern Egyptian Art on the same grounds. North of the tower, Zamalek's tree-lined streets have galleries, cafés and the faded villas of 26th of July Street.

For a full half day, cross Qasr al-Nil Bridge to the **Egyptian Museum** in the morning. Then walk over to Gezira and go up the tower for the early-evening light. On a weekday, that timing usually avoids the worst of the weekend sunset rush.
