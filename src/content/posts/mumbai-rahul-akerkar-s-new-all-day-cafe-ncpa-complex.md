## What's actually happening at NCPA

Rahul Akerkar needs no introduction to anyone who's eaten their way through Mumbai's fine-dining decades; he built Indigo into a landmark and has spent years since chasing his next big idea. That idea, with restaurateur Jaydeep Mukherjee, has landed inside the NCPA complex in Nariman Point: an all-day café built around live fire.

Everything reportedly moves through open flame and embers here, which is a different proposition from the usual Mumbai café script of espresso machines and glass display cases. The fire is the point, not a garnish.

## The dishes people are talking about

Two dishes keep coming up in early word of mouth. The Flint Eggs Benny takes the classic brunch order and runs it through a wood-fired treatment, poached eggs and hollandaise landing on something smokier than the hotel-brunch version you've had before.

Later in the day, the black truffle butter spaghetti is the dish diners mention when the conversation turns to dinner. Between the two, the café is positioned to work as a genuine all-day address rather than a breakfast-only or dinner-only room, which is rarer in this part of South Mumbai than it should be.

- Flint Eggs Benny: fire-cooked take on eggs Benedict, best ordered at breakfast
- Black truffle butter spaghetti: the dish people return for at lunch or dinner
- Menu built around live-fire cooking rather than a standard café setup

## Why the NCPA setting matters

NCPA, the National Centre for the Performing Arts, is Mumbai's main venue for theatre, classical music and dance, spread across a campus on the reclaimed land at the tip of Nariman Point. A café inside that complex has a built-in rhythm: pre-show dinners, post-concert late bites, and daytime visitors cutting through between galleries and auditoriums.

That means the crowd here isn't purely a restaurant crowd. Expect performance-goers dressed for an evening out mixed with people who've simply come for the food, which changes the pace of service compared to a standalone restaurant elsewhere in the city.

## Getting there and getting a table

Nariman Point sits at the southern tip of Mumbai, an easy taxi or auto ride from Churchgate railway station, which is the nearest major suburban rail stop. From Churchgate, it's a short drive down Marine Drive or through the business district to reach the NCPA campus.

Because this is a newly opened, buzzed-about restaurant from a chef with a serious following, walk-ins at peak breakfast and dinner hours are a gamble. Call ahead or book through whatever reservation channel the café has set up, and reconfirm the day's opening hours before you leave home, since early-stage restaurants sometimes adjust their schedule in the first months.

## Timing your visit around NCPA's own schedule

If you're combining the café with a show, eat before rather than after if you want a relaxed pace; post-performance crowds tend to arrive in one wave and can stretch kitchen timing. A daytime visit, outside of a performance day entirely, is likely your calmest option for a slower meal.

Weekday mornings are worth trying if you want the Flint Eggs Benny without a wait, since NCPA's own footfall builds later in the day as rehearsals, matinees and evening shows bring people onto the campus.

## Making the most of the NCPA complex

Treat this as a half-day out rather than a quick meal. NCPA's campus includes multiple performance halls and gallery spaces, so check what's on before or after your booking; a concert or exhibition can turn a café visit into a proper outing.

- Confirm the café's current hours and booking method before you go, since details for new openings shift fast
- Pair your meal with a same-day NCPA show or gallery visit for the full campus experience
- Come hungry enough to try both a fire-cooked breakfast dish and something from the pasta side of the menu if your visit stretches across meal times
- Ask staff which dishes are cooked directly over embers versus wood-fired oven, since the technique varies across the menu

Marine Drive is a five-minute stroll away if you want a post-meal walk along the promenade, making this an easy anchor for a Nariman Point afternoon or evening.
