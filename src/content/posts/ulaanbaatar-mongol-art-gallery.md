## A gallery in the eastern district

Most visitors stay near Sükhbaatar Square. This gallery isn't there. Its address is BZD - 26 khoroo, which places it in Bayanzürkh (БЗД), the large district east of the centre. The khoroo is its numbered subdistrict.

It doesn't get many reviews, but most of them are positive. That makes it a small, focused stop, not a museum where you need half a day. The public collections across town are bigger. You come here to see work in a gallery setting, with time to look at it properly.

## Getting across Ulaanbaatar

Ulaanbaatar has no metro, and traffic on Peace Avenue (Enkh Taivny Örgön Chölöö) is heavy at rush hour. From the centre, the realistic options are:

- **Taxi or ride-hail.** UBCab is the app most visitors use. Show the driver the address in Mongolian, "БЗД 26-р хороо", plus a pinned map location.
- **Bus.** City buses run east along Peace Avenue. You'll need a U Money transit card, and the last stretch is easier on a map app than by asking.
- **Timing.** Avoid the morning and early-evening commute, when an east–west crossing can take twice as long.

## Looking, buying, and the export paperwork

Hours are currently 10am–6pm on weekdays and 10am–5pm on Saturday and Sunday, so weekend visits end an hour earlier. These are this week's times, and small galleries change them around holidays and exhibition changeovers. Tsagaan Sar and Naadam (mid-July) are the most likely closures, so check the Google listing before you travel across town.

Some practical habits help here:

- **Ask before photographing.** Many galleries in the city allow phone photos. Some don't, and some only restrict certain works.
- **Ask about prices.** Questions are welcome at a gallery that sells work. Card payment is common in Ulaanbaatar, and QPay mobile payment is widespread. Confirm how you'll pay before you commit.
- **Get documents for anything you buy.** Mongolia controls the export of cultural items. Ask for a dated receipt and any certificate the gallery can provide, and keep them with the piece through customs at Chinggis Khaan International Airport.
- **Small shopping.** If a framed painting is too big for your luggage, ask whether unframed or rolled works are available.

## Pairing it with the city's art collections

A single gallery visit pairs well with the city's major art collections. Going back to the centre, the Zanabazar Fine Arts Museum has Buddhist sculpture by the 17th-century artist-monk Zanabazar, plus classical *Mongol zurag* painting. The Mongolian National Modern Art Gallery, in the Palace of Culture on Sükhbaatar Square, covers 20th-century and contemporary Mongolian work.

One sequence works well. See the historical collections in the morning, eat lunch in the centre, then take a taxi east to this gallery in the afternoon. Arrive by 4pm on a weekend, since closing is 5pm, or by 5pm on a weekday. That gives you an unhurried hour before closing, and you can compare the classical tradition with what is being shown and sold now.
