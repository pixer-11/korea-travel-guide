## Ulaanbaatar at the end of October

Late October is the start of winter here. ## Sitting with a Mongolian Counter-Strike crowd

The home crowd is loud and well informed. People cheer clutch rounds by name, and chants for local teams start up during tense buy rounds. Joining in is welcome. Booing a player's mistake is not the done thing.

A few habits that help:

- **Paying:** cards work in most cafés, malls and hotels, and locals lean heavily on QPay, the QR payment app. A little cash in tögrög helps at small kiosks.
- **Getting around:** the UBCab app is the easiest way to book a car. Plenty of people also flag down ordinary cars as unofficial taxis, which visitors without Mongolian usually avoid.
- **Language:** "Sain baina uu" is hello, and "bayarlalaa" is thank you. Younger fans often speak some English, and gaming vocabulary is universal.
- **Seats:** at LAN events, regulars tend to claim spots near the front during the gap between maps, when seats free up.

## Between match days

The city centre is compact enough to fill a free morning. Sükhbaatar Square, also called Chinggis Square, is the formal heart of the capital. The Government Palace faces it, with its huge seated statue of Chinggis Khaan.

A short ride west takes you to Gandan Monastery, the country's most important Buddhist monastery. Its main temple houses a towering standing statue of Avalokiteshvara, known locally as Migjid Janraisig. The National Museum of Mongolia, near the square, gives a quick run through the steppe empires.

For a view over the whole valley, the Zaisan Memorial sits on a hill at the southern edge of town. In the evenings, the restaurants around Seoul Street and the State Department Store area cover everything from buuz dumplings to Korean barbecue. That makes them a natural spot for a post-match meal.

International visitors arrive at Chinggis Khaan International Airport, about 50 km south of the centre. The ride into the city usually takes around an hour, depending on traffic.
