## Two nights in Bukit Jalil

BTS brings the ARIRANG World Tour to Kuala Lumpur on December 12 and 13, 2026. The tour shares its name with Arirang, Korea's best-known folk song. Both shows take place at Stadium Nasional Bukit Jalil, Malaysia's national stadium and the largest venue in the country.

The stadium opened in 1998 for the Commonwealth Games and holds around 87,000 people. A roof canopy covers the seating bowl, but the pitch, where floor sections usually go for concerts, is open to the sky. The bowl is steep and oval, so upper-tier seats look down on the stage from a long way off. On stadium tours, fans in those tiers watch mostly through the big screens.

## Reaching the stadium

The train is the simplest way in. Bukit Jalil LRT station, on the Sri Petaling Line, is beside the National Sports Complex, and a covered walkway takes you to the stadium grounds.

- **From KL Sentral or Masjid Jamek:** take the LRT to Chan Sow Lin and change to the Sri Petaling Line heading south. Without delays, the ride is roughly half an hour.
- **From KLIA:** the KLIA Ekspres goes to KL Sentral, and the LRT takes you on from there.
- **By car or Grab:** Bukit Jalil sits off the MEX and KESAS highways. Exit roads usually take a long time to clear once a full stadium lets out.

Pavilion Bukit Jalil, a large shopping mall across from the sports complex, is where many fans eat and wait before the doors open.

## Getting through a stadium night

After a sold-out show, the crowd leaving matters more than the crowd arriving. Platforms at Bukit Jalil station fill up straight after the encore. Station staff usually control how many people go down at once, so a wait of 30 minutes or more is normal. Fans who are not in a hurry often walk over to Pavilion Bukit Jalil and let the queues shrink first.

- **Payment:** Malaysia's transit runs on Touch 'n Go cards, contactless bank cards and QR-code fares. Locals top up their cards before a big night to skip the ticket-machine lines.
- **Fan culture:** ARMY in Malaysia trade freebies, photocards and handmade slogans along the stadium concourse before the shows. People usually swap, not sell.
- **Lightsticks:** the official ARMY Bomb links up with the show's lighting. Fans pair it to their seat in the official app before the concert starts.
- **Dress:** venues in Malaysia are relaxed about clothing. Light, breathable fabric suits the stadium's humid air.

## December weather and staying nearby

December falls in Malaysia's wet season. Heavy evening downpours are common in Kuala Lumpur. Fans in floor sections usually carry a light poncho, because the field has no cover. Big stadiums often ban umbrellas inside the bowl.

The organiser and its ticketing partner publish the show times, seat maps and entry rules on their official channels. That is where to find the official version of anything this page doesn't cover.

As for hotels, visitors usually stay either right by the stadium or along the train line:

- **Bukit Jalil and Sri Petaling:** you can walk back after the show, and the neighbourhood is quieter.
- **Bukit Bintang:** more food and nightlife, about 40 minutes away on two LRT lines.
- **KL Sentral:** the easiest base for airport transfers and a direct link to the Sri Petaling Line via Masjid Jamek.

Fans in town for both nights often spend the day in between at Petaling Street, Jalan Alor or the Petronas Towers. All three are easy to reach by train.
