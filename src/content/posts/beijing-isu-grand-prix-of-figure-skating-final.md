## Six skaters per discipline, one weekend

Most of the season is about qualifying for this. The Grand Prix Final closes out the autumn Grand Prix series. Its field is the top six entries in each discipline: men, women, pairs and ice dance, ranked on points from the series events.

For 2026–27, that field meets in Beijing. A small field changes the experience in the stands. Nobody is filler. Each warm-up group is packed with the skaters you have been watching on streams since October.

Traditionally the Junior Grand Prix Final shares the same rink and the same days. That puts the next generation of quad jumpers on the ice between the senior sessions.

## How the four days usually unfold

The 2026 edition runs from Thursday, December 10 to Sunday, December 13. The format is a fixed one. Each discipline skates two segments:

- **Short program** (men, women, pairs)
- **Rhythm dance** (ice dance)
- **Free skate** (men, women, pairs)
- **Free dance** (ice dance)

Short programs and rhythm dances normally come first. Free segments, where the medals are decided, fill the later days. An exhibition gala usually closes the event. It is looser and more theatrical, with costumes and choreography that would never get past the judges.

The ISU publishes the exact session order. Many fans who can only manage one day choose a free-skate day, because that is when results are settled on the spot.

## Tickets and the venue

The International Skating Union is the organiser. Its official site is where the venue name, session timetable and ticket links for this Final live.

Beijing already has serious skating pedigree. The Capital Indoor Stadium hosted figure skating and short track at the 2022 Winter Olympics, and the city has staged ISU events before. Do not assume any venue from that history, though. The ISU's event page names the rink.

Large sporting events in China commonly use real-name ticketing linked to an ID document. International visitors usually register with their passport number and carry the same passport through the entrance checks. Security screening at Chinese arenas is thorough, and bags are searched. Lighters, aerosols and large banners on poles are the items most often turned away.

## Beijing in mid-December

December in Beijing is cold and very dry. Daytime is usually around freezing and nights well below, with sharp wind along the wide avenues. The arena will be warm. The walk from the subway exit and the queue outside are not.

The Beijing Subway is the practical way to cross the city. It is cheap and signed in English. Travellers usually rely on:

- **Alipay or WeChat Pay**, both of which accept linked foreign cards, for transit, food and taxis
- **DiDi** for ride-hailing after evening sessions, when queues at the nearest station are long
- **Layers and lip balm**, because the heating indoors and the dry air outside crack skin quickly

Visitors often stay near a subway line that connects directly to the venue. Between sessions, the long gap is easy to fill with lunch in a nearby hotpot or roast duck restaurant.

## Rinkside manners at a skating final

Figure skating crowds follow a few unwritten rules. Locals and regulars keep to them closely.

- **Stay seated while a skater is on the ice.** Ushers hold people at the aisle until the program ends and the scores come up. Moving between skaters is the accepted window.
- **Clap during the program, not just after.** Applause after a clean jump or a fast spin is normal, and so is a rhythmic clap-along during the step sequence.
- **Wrap thrown gifts.** Soft toys and flowers tossed onto the ice go in sealed plastic so nothing loose reaches the surface. Young sweepers skate out to collect them.
- **Flags stay small.** Hand-held national flags and fan banners are common. They come down when they block the row behind.

Chinese fans are knowledgeable and loud for home skaters. They are also generous to everyone else. Expect a full-arena roar for a home pair team and warm applause for a clean skate from any country.

## Making a trip of it

Four days of skating still leaves mornings free. December is the quiet season at the big sights, and frozen lakes add something you don't see in summer.

- **Shichahai (Houhai) lakes**: when the ice is thick enough, they become a public skating and ice-cycling ground. It is a fun contrast to the competition.
- **The Forbidden City and Jingshan Park**: the climb up Jingshan gives the classic view over the palace roofs. Winter air is often at its clearest.
- **Temple of Heaven**: early mornings bring locals doing tai chi, cards and group exercise in the long corridor.
- **Mutianyu Great Wall**: a day trip that is far less crowded in winter than in October.

Skaters themselves usually leave right after the gala. For fans, the Monday after the Final is often the best sightseeing day of the trip.
