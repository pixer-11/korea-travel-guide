## A lotus tower and a lit flame

The ceremony centres on a sandstone-coloured tower shaped like a lotus. The Independence Monument, known in Khmer as Vimean Ekareach, was built in 1958 and designed by the architect Vann Molyvann. It marks 9 November 1953, when Cambodia gained full independence from French colonial rule under King Norodom Sihanouk.

On November 9, 2026, the monument was set to host the national ceremony, followed by parades and fireworks. Its tiers carry rows of carved naga heads, and the structure stands in a traffic circle. Flag-lined boulevards radiate out from it. The day is a public holiday, so offices and many government services close.

## Getting to Vimean Ekareach

The monument sits at the junction of Norodom Boulevard and Sihanouk Boulevard in central Phnom Penh. Road closures and security checkpoints usually surround it on Independence Day, and the last stretch is on foot.

- **Tuk-tuk or ride-hailing:** PassApp and Grab both work in Phnom Penh. Drivers usually drop passengers a few blocks out, since vehicles are turned back near the cordon.
- **From the riverside:** Sisowath Quay is roughly a 20 to 25 minute walk, passing close to the Royal Palace.
- **Nearby landmarks:** The bronze statue of King Father Norodom Sihanouk stands just east of the monument. The long park strip beyond it runs toward Wat Botum and gives room to stand back.

## Watching from the cordon line

This is a state occasion first and a spectacle second. The inner circle is reserved for the royal family, officials and invited guests. Public viewing happens from the edges of the boulevards and the park.

- **Tickets:** We know of no general ticketed admission to the 2026 ceremony.
- **Dress:** Covered shoulders and knees are the norm. The audience usually includes officials, monks and uniformed units, and modest clothing fits in.
- **Anthem and flag:** People stand still and stay quiet during the national anthem and flag moments.
- **Photos:** Photographing the parade is generally fine. Pointing cameras at security staff, or trying to get past barriers for a closer shot of royalty, is not.
- **Money:** US dollars and Cambodian riel are both used for street snacks and tuk-tuks. Small notes in both currencies speed things up.

## Fitting it into a Phnom Penh day

On Independence Day the ceremony and parades usually fill half a day around the monument, with the fireworks after dark. With the city on holiday, families typically fill the parks along Sihanouk Boulevard, and food carts set up wherever the crowds settle.

Sights within walking distance include:

- **Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda:** about a kilometre north-east, though closures are possible on a state holiday.
- **National Museum of Cambodia:** next to the palace, in a red pavilion-style building holding Angkorian sculpture.
- **Wat Botum:** a working pagoda in the park south of the palace.

November is the start of Cambodia's dry season, the daytime sun is strong, and there is little shade on the open boulevards. Ceremony arrangements are set by the state each year.
