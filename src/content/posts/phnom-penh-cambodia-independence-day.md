## A lotus tower and a lit flame

The ceremony centres on a sandstone-coloured tower shaped like a lotus. The Independence Monument, known in Khmer as Vimean Ekareach, was built in 1958 and designed by the architect Vann Molyvann. It marks 9 November 1953, when Cambodia gained full independence from French colonial rule under King Norodom Sihanouk.

On November 9, 2026, the monument hosts the national ceremony, followed by parades and fireworks. Its tiers carry rows of carved naga heads, and the structure stands in a traffic circle. Flag-lined boulevards radiate out from it. The day is a public holiday, so offices and many government services close.

## Getting to Vimean Ekareach

The monument sits at the junction of Norodom Boulevard and Sihanouk Boulevard in central Phnom Penh. Expect road closures and security checkpoints around it on the day. Plan to arrive on foot for the last stretch.

- **Tuk-tuk or ride-hailing:** PassApp and Grab both work in Phnom Penh. Ask to be dropped a few blocks out, since vehicles are turned back near the cordon.
- **From the riverside:** Sisowath Quay is roughly a 20 to 25 minute walk, passing close to the Royal Palace.
- **Nearby landmarks:** The bronze statue of King Father Norodom Sihanouk stands just east of the monument. The long park strip beyond it runs toward Wat Botum and gives you room to stand back.

## Watching from the cordon line

Treat this as a state occasion first and a spectacle second. The inner circle is reserved for the royal family, officials and invited guests. Public viewing happens from the edges of the boulevards and the park.

- **Tickets:** We know of no general ticketed admission to the ceremony. Confirm access rules and timing on the official government source before you go.
- **Dress:** Cover shoulders and knees. Crowds include officials, monks and uniformed units, and modest clothing fits in.
- **Anthem and flag:** Stand still and stay quiet during the national anthem and flag moments. Locals do.
- **Photos:** Photograph the parade freely. Do not point cameras at security staff, and do not try to get past barriers for a closer shot of royalty.
- **Money:** US dollars and Cambodian riel are both used for street snacks and tuk-tuks. Small notes in both currencies speed things up.

## Fitting it into a Phnom Penh day

Give the monument area half a day if you want the ceremony and the parades. Come back after dark for the fireworks. With the city on holiday, families fill the parks along Sihanouk Boulevard, and food carts set up wherever the crowds settle.

Combine it with sights within walking distance:

- **Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda:** about a kilometre north-east, though closures are possible on a state holiday.
- **National Museum of Cambodia:** next to the palace, in a red pavilion-style building holding Angkorian sculpture.
- **Wat Botum:** a working pagoda in the park south of the palace.

Bring water and a hat for the daytime part. November is the start of Cambodia's dry season, and there is little shade on the open boulevards. Confirm timing, venue and access on the official site, because ceremony arrangements are set by the state each year.
