## Where the street circuit keeps its archive

Macau has held a road race on its own streets every November since 1954. The track is the Guia Circuit, about 6.2 kilometres of public road with barriers bolted along it, and it's famous for the hairpin at Melco and the run through Lisboa bend. This museum is where that history goes for the rest of the year.

It stands on the NAPE reclaimed land, on Rua de Luís Gonzaga Gomes. You're only a few blocks from the grandstand area on Avenida da Amizade, where the start-finish straight runs. That puts a race car on a plinth very close to the tarmac it once ran on.

## On the floors: cars, bikes and a roll of names

The museum reopened in 2021 after a large expansion. It now fills several floors where it used to be squeezed into one basement hall. Most of the space goes to machines, set out so you can walk all the way around them:

- **Single-seaters** from the Formula 3 race, which has been the Grand Prix's headline event since 1983
- **Motorcycles** from the Macau Motorcycle Grand Prix, run on the same walled circuit since 1967
- **Touring and GT cars** from the support races
- **Memorabilia**: helmets, race suits, trophies, posters and programmes going back to the early amateur years

Look for the winners' lists. Ayrton Senna won the first Formula 3 race here in 1983. Michael Schumacher won in 1990, and plenty of later Formula 1 drivers came through Macau on the way up. If you're not a racing fan, those lists show quickly why a city this small matters so much to the sport.

## Behind the wheel: the simulators

The museum's best-known hands-on exhibit is its race simulator. It puts you in a cockpit seat in front of a screen showing the Guia Circuit. Real drivers find the narrow, unforgiving street layout hard, and the simulator lets you find out why.

How simulator sessions are booked, and any age or height limits, can change. Ask at the front desk as soon as you arrive. If you're travelling with children, put this first, before you start the slow walk past the display cases.

## Getting to Rua de Luís Gonzaga Gomes

The museum is easy to reach on foot from most of the Macau peninsula's arrival points.

- **From the Outer Harbour Ferry Terminal**: walk south through the Fisherman's Wharf area toward the NAPE grid. It takes about 15 minutes.
- **By taxi**: show the driver the Chinese name, 澳門大賽車博物館. The Portuguese name, Museu do Grande Prémio de Macau, also works.
- **From Senado Square or the Lisboa area**: the walk takes 20 to 25 minutes along Avenida da Amizade, which is part of the circuit itself.

The free casino shuttle buses run between the ferry terminal and the big NAPE hotels. Plenty of visitors use them to cut down the walk.

## November: when the exhibits move outside

The museum's timing quirk is the Grand Prix itself. The race weekend falls in November. In the days before it, roads around NAPE and the outer harbour are fenced off, crash barriers go up, and pedestrian bridges get added over the track.

During race week the museum makes much more sense, because the real thing is running a few streets away. Getting around is harder, though. Taxis detour, bus routes change, and crossing the circuit means using the temporary footbridges. Check the Grand Prix's official road-closure notices before you travel that month. Any other time of year, the streets are back to normal traffic and the museum is the only sign of the race.

## Making the most of a visit

Getting the timing right matters most here, because it's the easiest thing to get wrong.

- **Tuesday is the closed day.** The museum opens 10am to 6pm Monday and Wednesday through Sunday, with no shorter day in the week.
- **Arrive by 4pm** if you want to try the simulator and still read the displays. An hour on the cars alone goes quickly.
- **Allow 1.5 to 2 hours** for a full visit. Racing fans can easily spend longer.
- **Labels** are in Chinese, Portuguese and English, as at most of Macau's public museums.

For tickets and current entry rules, check the posted information at the entrance or on the Macao Government Tourism Office site before you go. In Macau, both the pataca and the Hong Kong dollar are widely used, and tipping isn't expected at a museum desk. This place doesn't get the crowds the casino resorts do, so you can usually take your time and look at a car from every angle without being moved along.

## Around the block in NAPE

NAPE is a grid of wide avenues on reclaimed land, and the museum fits easily into a half-day on foot.

- **Macau Fisherman's Wharf**: the themed waterfront complex just north, toward the ferry terminal
- **Golden Lotus Square**: the bronze lotus sculpture gifted for the 1999 handover, a short walk inland
- **Kun Iam Statue**: the bronze statue of the goddess of mercy on the waterfront, with views across to Taipa
- **Macao Science Center**: the angled cone-shaped building designed by I. M. Pei, further along the same shoreline

A good loop is the museum in the morning, then a walk along the waterfront to Kun Iam and the Science Center. Finish with a walk back up Avenida da Amizade, which in November is a racetrack.
